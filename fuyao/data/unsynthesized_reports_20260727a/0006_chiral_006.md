## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull and passed the cited no-imaginary-frequency screening, providing strong direct evidence for a thermodynamically and dynamically viable phase. CS-4 and TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, and TS-5 contain zero-hull analogues, showing that stable inorganic nitrogen frameworks and halide-containing phases are represented in the reference data. However, CS/SS/TS do not directly calculate the target, so their evidence is supportive rather than determinative; direct target validation comes from the supplied source-paper data.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses room-temperature or mildly heated solution processing, consistent with CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, and TS-3. Its main deductions arise from the likely multistep isolation procedure and the possible use of a toxic hydrazine-derived N–N precursor, which is **[Reasonable Inference]**. No high-pressure or high-temperature penalty is applied.

### F3 — Route 1

Solution transport gives high precursor activity and low diffusion resistance, as shown by precipitation and slow-evaporation procedures in CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, and TS-3. The principal limitation is structural reconstruction: the target is a chiral Cu–Br–N framework with N–N units, while the analogues are predominantly molecular, one-dimensional, or coordination-network materials with different bonding motifs. This mismatch justifies a moderate F3 rather than a high score.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is based on the closest chemical precedent, CuPN₂, which required 5 GPa and 1000 °C, and on Zn₂PN₃, which required 8 GPa and 1200 °C (CS-1, CS-4). The route therefore requires specialized equipment and controlled precursor handling, with deductions for reactive atmosphere, sealed operation, multistep processing, and air-sensitive Cu₃N. The initial 5 GPa condition avoids the explicit “>5 GPa” deduction, but escalation to 8 GPa would lower the score further.

### F3 — Route 2

Cu₃N-based HPHT synthesis is directly supported by CS-1, and the target shares a Cu/N framework character and corner-sharing tetrahedral motifs with CuPN₂. High pressure can increase the likelihood of retaining a dense inorganic framework and suppressing decomposition, as demonstrated by CS-1 and CS-4. Uncertainty remains high for bromide incorporation, N–N bond formation, and preservation of the chiral P3₁ structure.

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 follows the thermal processing logic of TS-5, including vacuum drying, pelletization, Ar sealing, long annealing, and slow cooling. These operations are substantially more demanding than solution growth but less extreme than the HPHT routes in CS-1 and CS-4. The proposed use of Cu₃N and an N–N reagent introduces additional handling hazards that are partly extrapolated and labeled **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### F3 — Route 3

Long annealing can improve ordering, as demonstrated by TS-5, which used a 150 h hold and slow cooling. Nevertheless, solid-state diffusion is slower than solution transport, and the target requires a substantial structural rearrangement relative to the ZnCN₂ analogue in TS-5. The absence of a direct Cu–Br–N solid-state analogue limits confidence.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one chemical cation, Cu, despite having two inequivalent crystallographic Cu sites, so the cation-size-mismatch criterion is not triggered. The supplied structural description emphasizes corner-sharing CuBr₃N tetrahedra and does not report simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity. Exact unit-cell atom count and direct amorphization data are unavailable from CS/SS/TS, so this factor is scored optimistically but with substantial uncertainty.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and it passed dynamic-stability screening, although experimental realization remains unconfirmed. The strongest chemical precedent is CuPN₂, which demonstrates high-pressure formation of a Cu/N framework from nitride precursors (CS-1), while solution-growth analogues support practical crystallization under mild conditions but do not reproduce the target topology (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). The most enabling factor is the target’s calculated thermodynamic and dynamic stability together with the existence of related stable nitrogen frameworks. The most limiting factor is the unproven formation and retention of N–N units together with bromide in the chiral P3₁ Cu framework. Consequently, solution crystallization is the best initial screening route, while HPHT synthesis is the best chemically grounded fallback route.
