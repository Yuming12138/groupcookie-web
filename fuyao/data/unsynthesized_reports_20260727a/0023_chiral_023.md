## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0500\) eV/atom lies exactly at the highest-value boundary of the supplied 90–100 scoring bin, and the target validation reports no imaginary phonon modes. This supports a high but not maximal score because the target remains theory-only and the hull value is not zero (target record). CS contains several stable fluoride/oxyfluoride analogues at or near the ground state, whereas TS includes compounds at 0.053–0.1037 eV/atom, showing that modest metastability can still be compatible with synthesis (CS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is whether the predicted P2₁ phase is kinetically accessible rather than replaced by Tl–S, Tl–F, or sulfur-fluoride competing phases (CS/SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]).

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives 65 because its temperature is mild relative to all CS solid-state examples, which use 140–250 °C, but it requires dry sealed handling and contains a toxic thallium precursor (CS; target safety record). The sealed-tube approach is operationally established for fluoride and oxyfluoride synthesis (CS). The sulfur volatility and need for exact stoichiometry are extrapolations from the sulfur/halogen chemistry and are marked [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 1

The route benefits from low diffusion demand because related fluoride phases form at 140–250 °C, far below the melting point of Tl metal, suggesting that reaction may proceed through defect-mediated or partially mobile intermediates (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). However, the required conversion to an ordered S₃ substructure is more substantial than the oxide/fluoride substitutions in the CS examples (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for the direct reactivity of TlF₃ with sulfur, so the precursor-activity estimate remains uncertain.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives 70 because solution crystallization avoids severe thermal treatment and is supported by the Sn₂F₃I cooling-crystallization method and liquid-SO₂ sulfur/halogen crystallization (CS/SS/TS). Deductions are applied for volatile solvent handling, toxic Tl chemistry, and the multistep crystallization sequence. The principal uncertainty is solubility and chemical compatibility of TlF₃ with anhydrous sulfur-containing solvents, for which there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**.

### F3 — Route 2

A liquid medium should improve contact and mass transport compared with a conventional solid-state reaction, and the SS/TS example demonstrates that controlled solvent removal can produce large sulfur/halogen crystals. The score is reduced because the structural rearrangement needed to form TlSF₅ pyramids connected through an S₃ unit is not represented directly in the references (SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]). Slow crystallization may be particularly useful for selecting the chiral P2₁ arrangement, but this is an extrapolation and is labeled [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives 55 because it combines dry controlled-atmosphere processing, sealed annealing, toxic Tl chemistry, and multiple operations. The grinding-plus-tempering sequence has precedent in the Cu–Te–S–Cl synthesis (TS), but that precedent uses much higher temperatures and a different composition. There is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** that mechanochemistry improves phase selectivity for Tl–S–F materials.

### F3 — Route 3

Mechanical activation should increase surface area and defect density, which can improve precursor activity [Reasonable Inference]. The low annealing temperature is consistent with CS fluoride synthesis, but the ordered S₃ chain and chiral framework may require a substantial reconstructive step. Repeated grinding and annealing could also increase the number of competing local arrangements, so the structural-reconstruction score is lower despite good nominal mass transport (CS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]).

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

No cation-radius mismatch is apparent because the target contains one principal cation, Tl, and the supplied structure describes a single Tl coordination environment. The target summary does not give the unit-cell atom count, so there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** to resolve the >40-atom criterion; this was treated conservatively as an unresolved neutral factor. The described corner-sharing TlSF₅ pyramids do not demonstrate simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, although the complete CIF would be needed for a definitive connectivity analysis (target structure summary; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**).

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best regarded as a low-hull, potentially metastable-to-near-ground-state phase rather than an experimentally established ground-state material because its calculated hull distance is 0.050 eV/atom and it remains theory-only (target record). CS provides useful support for low-temperature fluoride solid-state chemistry, while SS/TS support sulfur-rich halide crystallization and chalcogen-chain structural motifs; however, none directly validates Tl–S–F formation or chirality transfer (CS/SS/TS). The most important enabling factor is the combination of favorable thermodynamics and potentially effective low-temperature crystallization or flux-mediated mass transport (target record; CS/SS/TS). The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control in the presence of toxic Tl chemistry and potentially volatile sulfur/fluorine-containing species (target safety record; SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]).
