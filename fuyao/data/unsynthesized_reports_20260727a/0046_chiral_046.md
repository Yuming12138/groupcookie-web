## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target energy above hull is 0.090 eV/atom, which falls in the moderately metastable 0.05–0.20 eV/atom interval. Related CS compounds include several phases at 0–0.001 eV/atom, while structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfur-rich layered materials range from approximately 0.007 to 0.129 eV/atom [CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1, TS-4, TS-5]. Therefore, the target is less thermodynamically favored than the best analogues but remains within a range commonly accessible through kinetic synthesis.

### F2: Route 1

The direct route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but requires vacuum or sealed conditions because sulfur, bromine, and ZrBr₄ can be volatile [CS-2, CS-3, SS-1]. The closest S/Br analogue was obtained by sealed-tube heating at 500 °C, supporting practical feasibility [CS-2]. The main penalty is the need to control volatile halogen chemistry and possible competing phases.

### F2: Route 2

CVT requires an evacuated sealed ampoule, a temperature gradient, a transport agent, and a long reaction period, as shown for CrPS₄ and Nb chalcogenide halides [CS-3, SS-1, TS-1]. These requirements make the route more operationally demanding than a simple one-zone solid-state reaction. Its advantage is that vapor transport can compensate for slow solid-state diffusion.

### F2: Route 3

The flux route is speculative because no CS/SS/TS example directly uses an alkali-halide flux for an analogous Zr–S–Br phase. A sealed inert environment remains necessary because the starting sulfide and halide components can be volatile or air-sensitive [CS-2, CS-3]. Flux removal and possible incorporation of alkali ions add additional uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

### F3: Route 1

The precursor set is more reactive than dense elemental Zr, but formation of the target still requires major rearrangement into a mixed ribbon/sheet structure [Reasonable Inference]. Related phases require from 2 days to several weeks, and poor phase control or side-product formation is reported for some analogues [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-4]. Consequently, diffusion and structural-ordering scores are moderate rather than high.

### F3: Route 2

CVT improves effective mass transport because related compounds crystallize through halide-assisted vapor transport in evacuated ampoules [SS-1, SS-5, TS-1]. The method is particularly favorable for obtaining ordered low-dimensional crystals, but the target’s two distinct structural motifs and chiral P2₁ symmetry create a selective nucleation problem [Target structure; SS-1, TS-1]. The route therefore has better kinetics than direct sintering but greater process sensitivity.

### F3: Route 3

A molten KBr/CsBr medium could enhance diffusion and reduce the need for long-range solid-state transport [Reasonable Inference]. Nevertheless, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the flux will preserve the desired Zr:S:Br ratio or prevent alkali-containing impurity phases. The score is therefore conservative.

Mixed edge-sharing connectivity is documented, but simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra are not established, and the unit-cell atom count is not supplied; there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2.
