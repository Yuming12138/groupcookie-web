### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.137 eV/atom places it in the metastable but often experimentally accessible 0.05–0.20 eV/atom range; this comes from the provided target data. TS2 provides a closely thermodynamic analogue with E_hull ≈0.136 eV/atom that is experimentally accessed, supporting plausibility. CS1/SS1 show an extremely close Cu–Pt–B analogue on the hull, but this does not prove the target is a ground state. Remaining uncertainty is substantial because no CS/SS/TS source reports Pt3CuB2 itself.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is moderately severe because it requires arc melting, Ar protection, vacuum annealing, Ta wrapping, and a 240 h anneal. CS1/SS1 directly support those conditions. The main reason the score is not lower is that no high pressure, reactive gas chemistry, or toxic/air-sensitive precursor is required. Insufficient evidence from TS for Route 1-specific operating conditions, so TS is neutral for this route severity.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to dry solid-state synthesis because arc melting homogenizes Pt/Cu/B before long annealing. CS1/SS1 directly demonstrate that a very similar Cu–Pt–B phase can form by this approach. The remaining uncertainty is whether the orthorhombic chiral ordering of Pt3CuB2 is selected instead of the known hexagonal Cu–Pt–B analogue. TS gives insufficient route-specific kinetic evidence for this exact Cu–Pt–B arc-anneal route and is treated as neutral.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has less severe nominal conditions than arc melting because it avoids a melt step, but it still requires high temperature, sealed Ta/BN containment, and long anneals. These requirements are supported by CS2–CS5 and SS2–SS3. No source requires high pressure or toxic/air-sensitive Pt/Cu/B precursors. TS provides only partial support through analogous high-temperature boride synthesis, so it is secondary for this score.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are the main limitation because Pt and B are refractory elemental solids and full structural reconstruction from elemental powders is required. CS4/CS5 and SS2/SS3 show that related systems often need long holds, off-stoichiometry, or phase optimization. Cu melting near the proposed temperature may help diffusion [Reasonable Inference], but this is not directly shown for Pt3CuB2. Insufficient direct evidence from TS for Pt–Cu–B solid-state diffusion, so TS is neutral to weakly supportive.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is challenging because it adds off-stoichiometric screening, sealed containment, possible volatile/composition control, and post-growth separation. Off-stoichiometry and phase-window concerns are supported by SS2/SS3, CS5, and TS2. The Cu-flux concept itself is an extrapolation, not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. Therefore, CS/SS/TS provide indirect but not definitive support.

**F3 — Route 3**  
A Cu-rich liquid medium may improve transport and crystallization relative to dry powders [Reasonable Inference]. However, related systems with flux/off-stoichiometry show homogeneity ranges, poor crystal quality, and impurity phases, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score. SS2/SS3, CS5, and TS2 support these phase-control risks. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for a successful Cu-rich flux synthesis of Pt3CuB2.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because the system is a dense metallic boride with known crystalline analogues. CS1/SS1 strongly support crystallization in the same Cu–Pt–B chemistry. A conservative mixed-connectivity trigger is assigned because related borides show complex polyhedral sharing in CS2, SS1, and TS1. Unit-cell atom count and exact target polyhedral connectivity are not fully established by CS/SS/TS, so those parts include [Reasonable Inference] and remaining uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt3CuB2 is most likely a metastable phase rather than a true ground-state compound because the target E_hull is 0.137 eV/atom, although its reported dynamic stability supports local structural viability. The CS/SS evidence is strong because the closest analogue Pt9Cu3B5 has the same Cu–Pt–B chemistry, similar local bonding, high similarity, and an experimentally demonstrated arc-melt/anneal synthesis. TS evidence supports metastable accessibility because thermodynamically similar borides around 0.136–0.186 eV/atom have experimental synthesis precedent, especially TS2 and TS3. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated Cu–Pt–B arc-melting route from CS1/SS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering into the lower-symmetry chiral P2₁2₁2₁ structure rather than known nearby Cu–Pt–B or Pt–B phases.
