### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull of 0.090 eV/atom places it in the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom scoring band, so 78/100 is appropriate from the provided target data. SS gives strong support because structurally similar Zr-fluorides are synthesized and have low E_hull values of 0–0.028 eV/atom in SS1–SS5. TS gives moderate support because thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.098–0.200 eV/atom are experimentally accessed in TS1–TS4, while CS is mixed because CS1 is a high-hull outlier but CS2–CS5 are closer/stable fluorides.

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Direct sealed solid-state.** The condition severity is moderate because CS5 and SS2–SS3 support sealed, dry, high-temperature fluoride synthesis below 1000°C, but controlled atmosphere and sealed volatile fluoride handling still trigger deductions. Kinetics are moderate because binary fluoride frameworks form directly in SS2–SS3, but the ZrS₂/S conversion to a chiral Zr–S–F sheet is a **[Reasonable Inference]** not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for this route and is treated as neutral 50 for Route 1.

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Flux growth.** F2 is lower because TS3–TS4 require flux drying, vacuum sealing, long heating, cooling, and washing, and SS1/SS5 also show complex sealed crystal-growth logistics. F3 improves relative to direct solid-state because TS1 and TS3–TS4 support flux/liquid transport, but structural selection remains uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source crystallizes ZrSF₃. CS provides insufficient evidence for flux growth and is treated as neutral 50 for Route 2.

**Route 3 F2/F3 — HF/solvothermal.** F2 is relatively favorable because CS1 and TS5 show low-temperature Teflon/HF or hydrothermal conditions, but HF and sulfide chemistry still produce toxicity and sealed-vessel deductions. F3 is limited by structural reconstruction because CS1 only shows fluorometallate crystallization and TS5 only shows titanate hydrothermal chemistry, while SS provides insufficient solvothermal evidence and is neutral 50. The route is kinetically attractive but chemically least validated for Zr–S–F.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered from the target description. The described inequivalent sites do not support a >40 atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing trigger, so the formal amorphization score is high at 92/100. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphous byproducts for ZrSF₃-like chemistry, so each source is neutral 50 for direct F4 evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrSF₃ is best treated as a metastable but plausible target because its E_hull is 0.090 eV/atom, while TS1–TS4 show that nearby thermodynamic analogues can be synthesized even around 0.098–0.200 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS2–SS3, where ZrF₄ reacts directly into crystalline Zr-fluoride frameworks, and from CS2–CS5, where sealed fluoride solid-state reactions are established. The most important enabling factor is the accessibility of Zr–F coordination networks under sealed fluoride conditions, supported by SS2–SS5. The most important limiting factor is the absence of any direct CS/SS/TS Zr–S–F analogue, making S–S/disulfide stabilization and volatile F/S stoichiometry the key uncertainty. Overall, the target is moderately synthesizable, with Route 1 recommended first and Route 2 as the best crystal-growth fallback.
