## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0600 eV atom⁻¹ in the supplied target record, placing it in the moderately metastable but potentially synthesizable 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ interval. Related Pd–Se–Br materials span 0.0035–0.0767 eV atom⁻¹ in CS/TS, while a related Pd–Se–Cl phase is higher at approximately 0.0993 eV atom⁻¹ (CS-1; CS-2; CS-3; TS-1; TS-5). Thus, the target is thermodynamically plausible but not as secure as a phase at or very near the convex hull. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the target-specific hull energy; the target record is required for the numerical score.**

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, but it requires a controlled N₂/vacuum environment, sealed ampoules, volatile bromine, and multiphase crystal separation (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1; CS-3). These factors justify deductions despite the relatively mild 180–200 °C temperature. The main uncertainty is whether a sulfur analogue will remain stable in the same solvent/halide medium, because the direct analogue contains Se rather than S (CS-1; CS-3).

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids autoclave pressure but requires vacuum sealing and extended thermal treatment, as seen in the Re–Te–Br and Cu–Te–S–Cl examples (SS-4; TS-4). Volatile Br₂ and sulfur species make a sealed system necessary [Reasonable Inference]. The route receives the same nominal F2 as Route 1 because its lower equipment complexity is offset by higher-temperature operation and slower solid-state processing.

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest temperature requirement and directly benefits from a demonstrated hydrothermal Pd–Se–Br precedent at 453 K for five days (CS-2). Concentrated HBr is corrosive and volatile, and the process must be sealed; these hazards justify deductions (CS-2; CS-4; SS-5). The exact sulfur source and water activity are not established by the supplied references, so the score contains [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 1

The Pd–Se–Br analogue demonstrates that Pd, chalcogen, and bromine can react under low-temperature sealed solvothermal conditions (CS-1; TS-1). This improves transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, but the closest analogues are zero-dimensional or one-dimensional rather than the target’s extended chiral framework (CS-1; CS-2; CS-3). Therefore, the kinetics are assessed as intermediate rather than highly favorable.

### F3 — Route 2

Direct reaction of Pd, S, and Br₂ is kinetically limited by the refractory nature of Pd and by the need for long-range structural ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Related solid-state syntheses required 48 h at 550 °C with controlled slow cooling or 6 h at 600 °C followed by two weeks at 400 °C (SS-4; TS-4). These long treatments support a low-to-moderate F3 score.

### F3 — Route 3

The acidic HBr environment also provides a chemically relevant bromide medium (CS-2; CS-4; SS-5). The target is best regarded as a moderately metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase because its supplied \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹, although it reportedly passed dynamic-stability screening in the target record. Route 3 is therefore the preferred first screen, with Routes 1 and 2 useful as complementary phase-discovery approaches.
