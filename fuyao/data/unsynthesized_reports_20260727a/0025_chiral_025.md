# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹ according to the supplied target data, placing it near the low-energy boundary of the requested 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ category. Thermodynamic analogues show similar values of 0.0627–0.0852 eV atom⁻¹ [TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. This is favorable for synthesis but does not establish that LaS₃F is the ground state; it may still be metastable relative to competing La–S–F phases. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a complete La–S–F phase diagram; this uncertainty is treated neutrally.**

## F2 — Route 1

Direct solid-state synthesis is penalized because the proposed upper temperature is near or slightly above 1000 °C, the reaction requires vacuum sealing, sulfur is volatile, and multiple operations are needed [SS-1; SS-4; TS-1; TS-2]. The long high-temperature treatments used for rare-earth sulfides and quaternary sulfides indicate that diffusion and phase equilibration can be slow [SS-4; TS-1; TS-2]. The resulting score is moderate rather than low because these sealed-tube conditions are experimentally established for closely related sulfides [SS-4; TS-1; TS-2].

## F2 — Route 2

The flux route avoids the most severe temperature penalty because related polychalcogenide flux growth has been conducted at approximately 650 °C [SS-2]. It still requires inert handling, vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur management, and slow cooling [SS-2; SS-5]. The main uncertainty is that a sulfur/polysulfide flux for LaS₃F is an extrapolation and could introduce alkali-containing competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

## F2 — Route 3

Mechanochemical preactivation lowers the expected diffusion burden but does not remove the need for inert handling, sulfur containment, or post-milling annealing [TS-1; TS-2; SS-4]. The route is therefore less severe than a very high-temperature direct reaction but remains operationally multi-step. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct mechanochemical synthesis of a chiral mixed-anion La–S–F phase**, so its condition score is less certain.

## F3 — Route 1

The precursor activity is limited because La₂S₃ and LaF₃ are refractory binary compounds [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is scored moderately because US₃ contains the relevant S–S and metal–sulfide motifs [SS-4], but the fluoride-containing chiral topology is not directly reproduced by the analogue. The principal kinetic risk is incomplete reaction or formation of separate La₂S₃/LaF₃-derived phases [CS-2; SS-4; Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 2

The strongest feature of the flux route is enhanced mass transport through a sulfur-rich liquid or partially molten medium, as demonstrated by polychalcogenide flux growth [SS-2]. Slow cooling is also compatible with crystal growth and ordering of complex chalcogenide frameworks [SS-2]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the supplied data do not demonstrate that fluoride remains quantitatively incorporated during sulfur-flux growth [CS/SS/TS: insufficient evidence for fluoride retention in a sulfur/polysulfide flux].

## F3 — Route 3

Mechanical activation should increase contact area and shorten diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s S–S-containing framework has a relevant structural analogue in US₃ [SS-4]. However, the lack of direct mechanochemical precedent for this mixed-anion chiral phase reduces confidence in the structural-reconstruction score [CS/SS/TS: insufficient evidence for mechanochemical formation of LaS₃F].

The target description reports corner- and edge-sharing tetrahedral connectivity but not simultaneous face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not confirmed [Target structure]. # SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LaS₃F is best regarded as a **low-energy but potentially metastable phase**, because its reported Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹ rather than zero, although this is close to the thermodynamic analogue range [TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. The strongest structural support comes from monoclinic US₃, which contains three inequivalent sulfur sites, S–S bonds, and related metal–sulfide polyhedral connectivity [SS-4]. The strongest enabling factor is the availability of sealed, sulfur-rich synthesis environments that can preserve volatile sulfur and permit S–S framework formation [SS-2; SS-4]. The main limiting factor is selective fluoride incorporation without separation into La₂S₃-, LaF₃-, or other La–S–F competing phases [CS-2; SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, flux-assisted sealed-tube growth is the preferred first experiment, followed by direct solid-state annealing as a phase-purity route [SS-2; SS-4; CS-2].
