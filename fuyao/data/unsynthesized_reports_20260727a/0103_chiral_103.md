### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.099 eV/atom places it in the metastable-but-plausible 65–85 score band [Target data]. TS analogues show several synthesized or reported materials with comparable positive hull energies, supporting that this energy scale is not automatically prohibitive [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5]. CS and SS do not provide the target’s hull directly, so direct thermodynamic evidence is insufficient from CS = 50 and insufficient from SS = 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C operation, controlled Ar/sealed-tube handling, volatile Tl containment, and Tl toxicity [SS2, SS3, Target safety note]. Its kinetic score is moderate because CS4/SS3 validate Pt–B formation from elements, while SS2 validates sealed synthesis of Tl intermetallics; the remaining uncertainty is whether Tl3BPt2 orders cleanly rather than forming binary Tl–Pt or Pt–B phases [CS4, SS2, SS3; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids the highest temperatures but requires repeated grinding/annealing and sealed atmosphere control [SS3, SS4, TS4]. Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because solid-state Pt/B diffusion is slow, and CS5/SS3 both show boride phase control can require long anneals or non-stoichiometric optimization [CS5, SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether a mostly solid-state path can equilibrate the Tl–Pt–B sheet before Tl segregation or loss occurs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has harsher operational severity because excess Tl flux increases toxicity, volatility, and post-growth handling burden [Target safety note; Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetic score is the best because CS2/CS3 demonstrate that metal flux can grow chemically similar 2D intermetallics through slow cooling, but SS provides insufficient direct Tl–Pt–B flux evidence and is treated neutral at 50 for direct support. The largest uncertainty is whether B dissolves sufficiently in Tl-rich flux to form Tl3BPt2 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 benefits from low temperature but is penalized for vacuum processing, multistep deposition/annealing, and Tl volatility/toxicity [TS2; Target safety note]. TS2 gives only weak support for vapor deposition because its formula/precursor details are internally inconsistent, while CS and SS provide insufficient thin-film evidence and are scored neutral at 50. The main uncertainty is whether the chiral P2₁ phase can crystallize in a film before Tl evaporates or amorphous B persists [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is applied: a large Tl/Pt size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target appears to have a relatively small formula/cell and the description does not show explicit mixed vertex–edge–face polyhedral sharing, while CS5 and TS5 show that some analogues with complex connectivity have phase-control issues [Target data, CS5, TS5]. Exact target atom count and polyhedral-sharing evidence are insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so those source signals are neutral at 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl3BPt2 is most likely a metastable but accessible phase rather than a robust ground state, because its target E_hull is 0.099 eV/atom while the source validation reports dynamic stability [Target data]. The analogue support is moderate: CS4 and SS3 support Pt–B boride formation, SS2 supports sealed synthesis of Tl intermetallics, and TS analogues show similar positive-hull materials can be experimentally realized [CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed, inert, high-temperature processing that keeps Tl in the reaction while allowing Pt/B bonding [SS2, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control under Tl volatility/toxicity, especially because the target is theory-only and not an experimentally known composition [Target data; SS2; CS5]. Overall, Route 1 is the best first attempt, with Route 3 better suited if single crystals are required.
