## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target Eₕᵤₗₗ of 0.090 eV atom⁻¹ lies in the supplied moderate-metastability interval of 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹, corresponding to a nominal score of 65–85. Related mixed chalcogen–halide and ternary chalcogenides have comparable hull energies, including Sn₂SbS₂I₃ at 0.0889 eV atom⁻¹ and AgInS₂ at 0.0897 eV atom⁻¹ (TS). This supports a score of 75 rather than a low score, but the target remains above the convex hull and is therefore more likely to require kinetic control than a robust equilibrium synthesis (TS; target thermodynamic record). The target is theory-only, with no experimental procedure reported, so experimental accessibility remains unverified (source-paper validation).

### F2 — Route 1: CVT

The CVT route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, which is favorable compared with high-pressure ZnS₂/CdS₂ and AgInS₂/AgInSe₂ syntheses (SS/TS). However, vacuum operation, iodine volatility, sealed-tube requirements, and precursor preparation impose deductions; vacuum-sealed iodine transport is directly documented for Sn₂SbS₂I₃ (TS). The final score is 55 because the chemical environment is controlled rather than intrinsically severe, but iodine-containing feedstocks and phase selectivity increase operational risk **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### F2 — Route 2: Direct Vacuum Solid State

Direct solid-state synthesis is supported by elemental chalcogenide examples using evacuated silica or quartz tubes, prolonged heating, and slow cooling (CS/TS). The route receives deductions for vacuum, volatile sulfur/iodine, multiple thermal and grinding operations, and hazardous or air-sensitive iodine-containing materials; the latter hazard assessment is **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is therefore feasible in conventional materials laboratories but less forgiving than CVT or flux growth.

### F2 — Route 3: Iodide/Sulfur Flux

The flux route has the lowest nominal temperature among the proposed approaches because Cs₂Sb₄S₈ was grown at 280 °C using a sulfide flux and slow cooling (SS). It still requires vacuum or glovebox handling, sealed containment, volatile iodide components, and several preparation/post-treatment steps (SS; **[Reasonable Inference]**). The resulting score of 55 reflects moderate equipment and temperature demands but substantial chemical-potential sensitivity.

### F3 — Route 1: CVT

The target is more likely to be a kinetically accessible metastable phase than a robust ground-state phase because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.090 eV atom⁻¹, although this value is comparable to several synthesized or structurally characterized analogues in TS. CS/SS/TS provide useful support for In–S frameworks, S–S-containing networks, iodine-assisted transport, and low-temperature flux growth, but none directly demonstrates experimental InS₂I formation. The most critical limiting factor is iodine chemical-potential control, because iodine loss or excess iodine could produce iodine-deficient indium sulfides or iodine-rich indium iodides **[Reasonable Inference]**. Formation of the chiral P4₃ polymorph may also require controlled nucleation or spontaneous enantiomorphic symmetry breaking, for which there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.
