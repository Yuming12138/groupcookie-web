### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.011 eV/atom is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score window from the prompt’s target data. CS/SS/TS support low-energy Pt-rich analogues: CS1/SS1/TS5 are on-hull, TS1 is ~0.006 eV/atom, and TS3 is ~0.014 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not prove the exact cubic P2₁3 GeBPt3 phase experimentally.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 loses points because arc melting implies very high local temperature and controlled Ar/vacuum handling, as used in CS1, SS2, SS4, and TS1. It is still practical because those same sources show arc melting and sealed annealing are standard for Pt-rich borides/germanides. The main uncertainty is Ge/B stoichiometry retention during melting, which is [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because arc melting improves mixing, and CS1/TS1 show annealing can complete ordering. Pt–B bond lengths in CS1/SS2 and Pt–Ge coordination in TS1 transfer well to the target local chemistry. The uncertainty is that the target P2₁3 topology is not directly matched by any CS/SS/TS analogue.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids arc-melting temperatures but still requires >1000 °C and sealed inert reaction conditions, as shown in CS2 and CS3. It also requires homogenization/reannealing, directly supported by CS3. SS gives insufficient evidence for this non-arc route and is treated as neutral.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because elemental Pt/B/Ge must interdiffuse without an initial melt, and CS2/CS3 use long 50 h scale reactions. Intermediate grinding should help by [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates direct solid-state Pt–Ge–B P2₁3 formation. This route is therefore plausible but not preferred.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has moderate condition severity because TS3 uses 1200 °C annealing and Ar handling, but not high pressure. It is penalized for multi-step rapid solidification plus annealing, as in TS3. CS and SS provide insufficient melt-spinning support for borides and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Rapid solidification can improve mixing for Pt-rich intermetallics, as TS3 shows for Pt3Ga. However, no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates B incorporation or P2₁3 ordering by melt-spinning, so structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic term. This route is exploratory and most useful if arc melting gives competing phases.

**F2 — Route 4**  
CVT uses lower temperatures, but SS5/TS2/TS4 require evacuated sealed ampoules and iodine transport. Iodine introduces volatility and toxicity concerns, and phase selection is sensitive to transport chemistry in those references. CS provides insufficient CVT evidence and is neutral.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Vapor availability is the weak point because SS5/TS2/TS4 only show Pt–Al transport, not Pt–Ge–B or boron transport. Composition control is also poor because the Pt–Al CVT references produce different phases depending on iodine chemistry. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth follow-up after bulk synthesis, not a primary synthesis path.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target appears low-risk for amorphization because its described BPt6 network is corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. CS1/CS2/CS3 contain more complex mixed polyhedral motifs, but those are analogue structures and do not override the target description. Ionic-radius and exact unit-cell-count checks have insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, so those are treated conservatively with [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GeBPt3 is best interpreted as a **near-hull metastable or nearly ground-state** phase because its E_hull is 0.011 eV/atom rather than exactly 0.0. The analogue support is strong for chemistry: Pt–B borides form by arc melting/annealing in CS1/SS1/TS5, RPt3B phases form by arc melting in SS2, and Pt–Ge ternaries form by arc melting/annealing in TS1. The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for **elemental Pt–B and Pt–Ge intermetallic synthesis under Ar followed by annealing**. The most critical limiting factor is **phase selection into the exact chiral P2₁3 GeBPt3 topology**, which no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates. Overall, the target is experimentally credible but likely requires careful annealing and phase-purity screening.
