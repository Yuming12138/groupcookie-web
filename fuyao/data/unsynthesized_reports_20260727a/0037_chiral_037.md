## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) of 0.0800 eV/atom lies in the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom interval, corresponding to moderate predicted stability. TS-1 and TS-2 provide close thermodynamic analogues with \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) values of 0.0767 and 0.0889 eV/atom, respectively, supporting a score near 75 rather than either a highly stable or strongly unstable classification. The remaining uncertainty is that an above-hull phase may require kinetic trapping or a narrow synthesis window.

### F2: Route 1

Route 1 requires evacuated sealed ampoules and long annealing, directly supported by CS-1 and TS-5. The target’s bromine component adds volatility and handling severity, which is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Because the route uses staged reaction, grinding, and annealing, the multi-step deduction is justified by TS-5 and the prolonged treatment in CS-1.

### F3: Route 1

CS-1 demonstrates that Ge–S solid-state chemistry can proceed in evacuated quartz at 500–600 °C, while TS-5 demonstrates staged high-temperature homogenization followed by grinding and tempering. These analogues indicate that diffusion is feasible but that ordering may be slow, leading to a moderate F3 score. Direct evidence for formation of GeBr₃ clusters and S ribbons from the selected precursor combination is absent, so the structural-reconstruction component remains uncertain.

### F2: Route 2

CS-2 and TS-2 establish that sealed quartz CVT with iodine and temperature gradients is experimentally practical for chalcogen-containing solids. Vacuum, volatile transport chemistry, and hazardous halogen reagents justify the deductions. The exact bromine transport behavior of the target composition is not established, so the severity estimate is moderately uncertain.

### F3: Route 2

The vapor-transport score benefits from the demonstrated use of iodine transport agents and hot/cold gradients in CS-2 and TS-2. However, composition control is penalized because sulfur and bromine activities can vary along the tube, and the target’s 1:3 bromine stoichiometry may be sensitive. Direct evidence for the target phase is absent, so the crystallization score is based on analogy rather than confirmation.

### F2: Route 3

SS-2 and TS-1 support sealed N₂-protected solvothermal synthesis of chalcogen/halide clusters at 180–200 °C, while CS-3 supports low-temperature crystallization from a sulfur-compatible solvent. The proposed GeBr₄/CS₂ combination introduces volatility, toxicity, and moisture sensitivity as **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is less thermally severe than the solid-state routes but still requires controlled containment.

### F3: Route 3

Dissolved or molecular precursors should provide higher chemical activity and shorter diffusion lengths than dense elemental powders **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS-3 demonstrates that sulfur-containing halide species can crystallize from solution, but its product is zero-dimensional, not a one-dimensional ribbon. Consequently, Route 3 receives the highest provisional F3 score but has the greatest structural-transfer uncertainty.

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target description does not provide evidence for a large cation-size mismatch, a unit cell containing more than 40 atoms, or simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity. Therefore, C1–C3 are not confirmed, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is scored neutral for each check. ## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is more likely to be a kinetically accessible or metastable phase than a strict ground-state phase because its \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0800 eV/atom, although TS-1 and TS-2 show that close-hull chalcogen-halide materials can be synthesized. CS-1 provides the strongest chemical precedent through Ge–S solid-state synthesis, while SS-2, TS-1, and TS-3 support the feasibility of volatile chalcogen-halide cluster and ribbon chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated ability of sealed quartz or silica systems to retain volatile chalcogen/halogen components during extended thermal treatment (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target requires simultaneous formation of GeBr₃ clusters and ordered S ribbons, whereas several analogues show multiphase products or strong dependence on gradients and annealing (SS-2, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3).
