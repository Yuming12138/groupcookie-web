# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported energy above hull is 0.070 eV atom⁻¹, and the supplied validation reports successful dynamic-stability screening, supporting a thermodynamically accessible metastable phase rather than an intrinsically implausible structure (target validation data). TS-1, TS-3, and TS-4 have similarly modest hull energies of approximately 0.067–0.099 eV atom⁻¹, while CS-3 and SS-3 include near-ground-state chalcogenide analogues (TS-1; TS-3; TS-4; CS-3; SS-3). The principal uncertainty is that low calculated hull energy does not guarantee that the Ge–Se–Cl phase will outcompete elemental Se, GeSe₂, or other chlorides during synthesis.

## F2: Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because TS-1 and TS-3 establish that selenium/halide products can be prepared at only 180–200 °C, avoiding extreme thermal requirements. The score is reduced by the need for sealed handling, volatile Se/SeCl₄, inert or vacuum conditions, and long reaction times (TS-1; TS-3). The target safety note further supports a deduction for toxic selenium-containing materials.

## F3: Route 1

Route 1 has the highest F3 score because TS-3 provides both chemical and dimensional precedent: a selenium-rich chloride compound forms a one-dimensional ribbon under sealed solvothermal conditions. The target differs in cation identity, coordination geometry, and chirality, so the analogy supports partial rather than exact structural reconstruction (TS-3). The low-temperature reactive medium should improve diffusion relative to a dry solid-state mixture [Reasonable Inference].

## F2: Route 2

Route 2 uses temperatures comparable to those reported for CS-3 and TS-4, but it requires vacuum ampoules, grinding/resealing, and long annealing periods (CS-3; TS-4). Volatile selenium and chlorine species make an open solid-state synthesis unsuitable [Reasonable Inference]. Consequently, the route is feasible but operationally demanding.

## F3: Route 2

CS-3 directly supports the formation of tetrahedral Ge–chalcogen connectivity by sealed solid-state synthesis, which is favorable for the Ge-centered part of the target. However, the target also requires Se–Se connectivity and chlorine incorporation, and no CS/SS/TS analogue demonstrates all of these features together. The long treatments used in CS-3 and TS-4 indicate that ordering and homogenization may be slow.

## F2: Route 3

CVT requires an evacuated sealed tube and precise thermal-gradient control, as shown by CS-4 and TS-2. These references do not require high pressure, but they do require volatile transport agents and prolonged crystal-growth periods. The use of I₂, SeCl₄, and selenium creates additional volatility and contamination concerns [Reasonable Inference].

## F3: Route 3

CS-4 and TS-2 demonstrate that CVT can produce well-crystallized chalcogenide single crystals, giving Route 3 a favorable crystallization score. However, the supplied CVT examples do not contain a Ge–Se–Cl composition, so the transport coefficients and chlorine incorporation window are unknown. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for reliable prediction of target stoichiometry control in CVT.

## F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target is a small, one-cation framework with a one-dimensional ribbon rather than a large multication network, which argues against severe amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. The structural description does not explicitly report the coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, and the target formula suggests a small structural repeat. Because CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization measurements for this target, there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**; the score is therefore kept conservative at 90 rather than 100.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a **metastable but synthesizable phase**, because its calculated hull distance is 0.070 eV atom⁻¹ while dynamic-stability screening was passed (target validation data). The strongest analogue support comes from TS-3, which combines selenium-rich chemistry, chlorine, and a one-dimensional ribbon produced under sealed low-temperature conditions. CS-3 independently supports the feasibility of tetrahedral Ge–chalcogen framework formation, whereas SS and CVT analogues mainly support crystal-growth and sealed-tube processing rather than the exact composition (CS-3; TS-3; CS-4; TS-2). The most important enabling factor is the availability of a reactive selenium/halide medium that can promote Se–Se ribbon formation at moderate temperature (TS-3). The main limiting factor is the absence of a direct precedent for simultaneously controlling Ge tetrahedral coordination, chlorine retention, Se–Se polymerization, and P4₃ chiral ordering.
