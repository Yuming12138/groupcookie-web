### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull = 0.170 eV/atom places it in the 65–85 range, so 75 is a middle-high metastable score from the provided target metadata. TS supports that phases with comparable or even higher E_hull values can be experimentally accessed, including TS3 at ~0.121 eV/atom, TS4 at ~0.142 eV/atom, TS5 at ~0.193 eV/atom, and TS1/TS2 above 0.20 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). CS and SS analogues are mostly near the hull, so they support Li/Zn intermetallic chemistry but do not by themselves prove this 0.17 eV/atom target is easy to isolate (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, consistent with Li–Zn solid-state analogues at 600–800 °C (CS1, CS3, SS4). The severity deductions come from Ar/glovebox handling, sealed volatile Li/Zn systems, repeated heat/grind steps, and Li air sensitivity (CS1, CS3, CS4, TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether B reacts fully under these sub-melting or partially molten conditions, because direct Li–Zn–B evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are helped by Li/Zn mobility and successful Li–Zn analogue formation at moderate temperatures (CS1, CS3, SS4). Kinetics are penalized because B is likely refractory and because the target P4₃ phase has no exact structural analogue, implying ordering/reconstruction challenges [Reasonable Inference] (CS3, SS4). Repeated heating/homogenization in CS3 supports using cycling rather than a one-shot anneal (CS3).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has similar atmosphere and volatility penalties to Route 1, but no high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty because the best analogue melt route uses 900 °C (CS4). The sealed Ta/high-vacuum or Ar setup is directly supported by Li–Zn–Ge and Zn-rich melt syntheses (CS4, SS3, SS5). The major practical uncertainty is managing Li/Zn vapor pressure without shifting the final Li₃Zn₂B stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference] supported by Zn evaporation issues in TS1.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Melt/self-flux processing receives the highest kinetic score because liquid-mediated transport and slow cooling are directly demonstrated for Li–Zn–Ge and Zn-rich analogues (CS4, SS3, SS5). This route most plausibly overcomes slow B incorporation by using a Li/Zn-rich liquid phase [Reasonable Inference], although direct B solubility evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutral 50. Structural selection remains uncertain because the target chiral P4₃ structure is not an exact match to any analogue (CS4, SS3, SS5).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is heavily penalized for >5 GPa high pressure, specialized capsule assembly, and multi-step HP operation, all directly supported by SS2, TS2, and TS4. It avoids a >1000 °C deduction in the recommended window, but HP analogues show narrow processing windows and impurity sensitivity (SS2, TS2, TS4). CS gives insufficient evidence for HP necessity in Li–Zn chemistry, so CS is neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 3**  
HP processing can improve densification and trap metastable phases, which is favorable for a positive-hull target [Reasonable Inference] based on HP analogues (SS2, TS2, TS4). The kinetic score is not higher because HP analogues report impurity phases, decomposition windows, or difficult crystal growth, indicating phase selection remains challenging (SS2, TS2, TS4). This route is a fallback rather than best route because it is less chemically matched to the Li–Zn analogues than sealed melt processing (CS4, SS3).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target appears more likely to crystallize than amorphize because dense metallic/intermetallic analogues with high coordination are experimentally crystalline under sealed heating or melt cooling (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS3). One amorphization trigger is assigned for likely mixed polyhedral connectivity, inferred from analogous edge/corner/face-sharing networks and target high coordination (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2, SS3). Unit-cell size and cation-radius checks lack direct CS/SS/TS data, so those source signals are neutral 50 and interpreted as non-triggers [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₃Zn₂B is more likely a metastable phase than a ground-state phase because its provided E_hull is 0.170 eV/atom, although the retained no-imaginary-frequency phonon screening supports local dynamic stability from the target metadata. CS and SS analogues strongly support sealed Li/Zn intermetallic synthesis using elemental precursors, Ta containment, Ar/vacuum sealing, and controlled cooling, especially for Li–Zn–Ge and Li₂ZnSn-like systems (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS4). TS analogues support the broader idea that positive-hull metallic phases can be isolated by sintering, melt/anneal, or high-pressure routes (TS1–TS5). The key enabling factor is Li/Zn liquid or high-mobility transport in a sealed Ta system, which should help crystallization and homogenization [Reasonable Inference] (CS4, SS3, SS5). The key limiting factor is the absence of direct Li–Zn–B experimental precedent plus uncertain B dissolution/reaction kinetics and Li/Zn volatility control; direct Li–Zn–B evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS and is treated neutral 50.
