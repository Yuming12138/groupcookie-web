### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s 0.088 eV/atom hull distance falls in the metastable-but-plausible 65–85 band, and TS includes synthesized analogues at comparable or higher E_hull values. (TS1, TS2, TS5) CS and SS analogues are mostly ground-state or near-ground-state compounds, so they support accessible chemistry but do not prove Ta₃Al₂B is itself a ground-state phase. (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5) Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not identify an experimentally known Ta₃Al₂B phase, so the score is capped below the top of the band. (CS/SS/TS)

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Arc melting has strong chemical precedent because multiple chemically similar borides are made by arc melting under gettered Ar with 1250–1400°C anneals. (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5) The penalty is driven by high temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, and multistep remelting/annealing. (CS1, CS2, CS5) Kinetics are moderate rather than easy because Ta–B systems show slow equilibration and difficult phase windows, although melt homogenization helps. (SS4; TS4; CS3)

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state synthesis is plausible because close structural and Ta–B analogues are made by sintering/annealing, but the sources show long anneals and sometimes very high temperatures. (SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5; TS4) Kinetics score low because Ta and B are refractory solid precursors, and Ta₂B analogues require 2000–2190°C or repeated treatments. (SS4; TS4) The main uncertainty is whether the target chiral Ta₃Al₂B topology can form before decomposing into Ta borides and Ta–Al binaries. (SS3, SS4; TS4)

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Al-rich melt growth is supported by a Ta–Al–C structural analogue grown from metallic melt at 1500°C with controlled slow cooling. (SS3) This route improves mass transport versus dry solid-state synthesis, but SS3 also reports impurity challenges and Ta–B analogues show competing boride stability. (SS3; SS4/TS4) Its score is close to Route 1, but it is less directly supported for borides because the exact Ta–Al–B target has no reported melt-growth analogue in CS/SS/TS. (CS/SS/TS)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The strongest amorphization flag is mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which appears in the closest structural analogue. (SS1) The inferred unit cell is not large because SS1 has 24 atoms per cell, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius evidence for the >15% radius mismatch check. (SS1; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for ionic radii) The resulting F4 score is low–medium risk rather than high risk. (SS1, SS3)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃Al₂B is best treated as a metastable but plausible phase rather than a likely ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.088 eV/atom. (TS1, TS2, TS5 for metastable synthesized analogues) The structural case is strong because Ta₃Al₂C is nearly isostructural in the Ta–Al framework and polyhedral connectivity, but the chemical case is less exact because no CS/SS/TS entry reports Ta₃Al₂B directly. (SS1; CS1–CS5) The most important enabling factor is melt homogenization under inert conditions, which is repeatedly successful for chemically similar borides. (CS1–CS5) The main limiting factor is phase competition/slow ordering in Ta–B/Ta–Al systems, evidenced by high-T Ta₂B equilibration and impurity formation in Ta–Al–C melt growth. (SS3, SS4; TS4)
