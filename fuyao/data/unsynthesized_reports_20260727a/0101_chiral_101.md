### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability
The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.080 eV/atom, placing it in the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom metastable window. CS and SS are favorable because most chemically and structurally similar Pt–B or noble-metal borides are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, while TS is mixed because it includes comparable metastable analogues at ~0.071–0.092 eV/atom and a less favorable RhB analogue at 0.214 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not prove Pt3B2Pb2 is experimentally isolable; they only indicate that nearby intermetallic/boride chemistry is often accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1
Route 1 receives moderate severity because CS/SS/TS repeatedly use arc melting, Ar, sealed tubes, Ta foil, and long anneals rather than exotic high-pressure synthesis. The major deductions come from vacuum/inert handling, multiple steps, and Pb toxicity/volatility **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Pb vapor pressure behavior in Pt3B2Pb2, so that part is extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 1
Kinetics are moderate because arc melting improves homogenization, and SS/CS Pt–B analogues successfully form after annealing. However, Pt and B are refractory, and many analogues require long anneals, remelting, or homogenization. The target’s exact tetragonal chiral P4₂ structure is not directly represented in CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2
Route 2 avoids sustained >1000 °C treatment, which helps severity, and CS provides a direct excess-Pb flux precedent at 800 °C. The route still requires sealed vacuum conditions, slow cooling, flux removal, and Pb handling. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for Pb-flux specifically, so their route-specific signal is neutral for flux feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 2
Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because molten Pb can plausibly improve mass transport and crystal growth **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS demonstrates excess Pb flux synthesis for a Pb-containing intermetallic/pnictide. The main uncertainty is B incorporation into a Pb-rich melt, since CS Pb-flux evidence involves P rather than B and SS Pt–B analogues mostly use arc melting/annealing. Composition control may be narrow because excess Pb can stabilize competing Pb-rich phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3
Direct sealed-tube solid-state synthesis has similar atmosphere and toxicity penalties to Route 1/2 but lacks the homogenizing benefit of arc melting or flux. CS and SS show sealed-tube elemental reactions can work for related borides, but they also require high temperatures and homogenization. TS provides insufficient route-specific evidence for direct Pt–Pb–B sealed-tube reaction, so it is neutral for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 3
The kinetic score is lowest because dense Pt and refractory B are expected to react slowly without melt homogenization **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS/SS analogues often use arc melting, repeated heating, or long anneals, implying direct diffusion-only synthesis is less favorable. The route remains plausible but would likely require repeated regrinding and extended annealing.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency
F4 is medium risk because the target combines very different bonding/size components, and SS analogues show complex mixed Pt–B polyhedral environments. CS/SS/TS do not provide exact target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered. The main uncertainty is whether the chiral P4₂ ordering is robust during cooling or competes with simpler Pt–B/Pb-rich intermetallic phases.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt3B2Pb2 is most likely a **metastable but accessible** phase rather than a clear ground state because the supplied target E_hull is 0.080 eV/atom and TS includes experimentally synthesized analogues in a similar metastability range. CS and SS give strong qualitative support because Pt–B frameworks and noble-metal borides are repeatedly synthesized by arc melting, sealed-tube annealing, or flux-like routes. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of liquid/excess Pb or melt processing to overcome Pt/B diffusion limits **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under Pb volatility and competition from simpler Pt–B or Pb-rich phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the candidate is experimentally plausible but likely requires careful sealed synthesis and iterative phase-purity optimization.
