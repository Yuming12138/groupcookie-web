## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**
The target E_hull of 0.130 eV/atom falls in the metastable-but-accessible 65–85 rubric band from the supplied target data. TS gives the strongest analog support because TS1, TS4, and TS5 are synthesized phases with E_hull values near 0.106–0.190 eV/atom. CS and SS do not provide the exact target hull, so exact-target F1 evidence is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS** and neutral at source level. Remaining uncertainty is whether SbP2Cl has a narrow decomposition pathway into Sb–P binaries plus chlorides **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 1**
Route 1 benefits from moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule precedents in CS1, SS3, and SS4, but vacuum sealing and volatile P/Cl control drive F2 down. Kinetics are acceptable because SS3 forms a P-rich Sn phosphide at 575 °C, but long anneals in CS1 and SS4 imply slow ordering and possible incomplete conversion. The main uncertainty is whether SbCl3 reacts productively rather than forming volatile side products **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 2**
Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1, but F3 is higher because CS4 and TS4 demonstrate vapor-mediated phosphide crystal growth. CVT should help overcome slow bulk diffusion and improve crystal quality **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is phase selectivity, because TS4 explicitly shows phosphide polymorph ratios depend on P pressure.

**F2/F3 — Route 3**
High-pressure synthesis is supported by SS1, SS2, SS5, and TS5 for structurally similar P-rich frameworks, but it is penalized by high temperature, capsule complexity, and volatile chloride retention. Kinetics under pressure are favorable for P-network formation, but no CS/SS/TS example proves chloride-containing high-pressure Sb phosphide synthesis. The method is a useful fallback for metastable stabilization rather than the first-choice route **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 4**
TS1 supports vacuum deposition for a thermodynamically similar GaP phase, so thin-film screening is plausible. F3 is limited because P and Cl fluxes must be controlled simultaneously, and TS4 shows phosphide phases are pressure-sensitive. CS/SS give insufficient evidence for thin-film SbP2Cl crystallization, so this route is mainly exploratory.

**F2/F3 — Route 5**
CS2 and CS5 support low-temperature sealed solution chemistry, but they do not directly support an anhydrous extended Sb–P–Cl framework. Solvothermal transport improves precursor contact, yet structural reconstruction is poor because CS5 products are molecular chloride clusters rather than extended phosphide-halides. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route, so it is lower-confidence despite mild temperatures.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**
No F4 trigger is clearly established: the target does not have an evident multication radius mismatch, no evidence indicates >40 atoms per unit cell, and the described target connectivity does not show simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS/SS/TS are insufficient for exact target C1/C2 validation and are treated as neutral 50 at source level. The F4 score is high but not perfect because low-symmetry chiral P21 ordering may still be sensitive to kinetics **[Reasonable Inference]**.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SbP2Cl is most likely a **metastable** rather than true ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.130 eV/atom, while TS1/TS4/TS5 show that phases in this metastability range can still be synthesized. The analogues support synthesis moderately well: CS4 and TS4 strongly support sealed vapor/transport handling of P-rich volatile systems, while SS1–SS5 support P–P framework formation. The most important enabling factor is vapor-mediated transport, which should improve crystallization and composition control relative to purely diffusive annealing **[Reasonable Inference based on CS4, TS4]**. The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of P and Cl chemical potentials, because TS4 shows phosphide phase selectivity depends on P pressure and the target additionally requires retained Cl. Overall, the target is credible but not routine; CVT is the preferred first experimental route.
