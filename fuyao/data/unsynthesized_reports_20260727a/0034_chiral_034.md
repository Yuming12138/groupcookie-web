## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.080 eV atom⁻¹, which falls within the supplied 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ moderate-stability interval and supports a score of 78/100. **[Target data]** The closest CS/SS analogue Ba₅P₅I₃ is on the calculated ground state, and Ba₂P₇I is also reported at E_hull = 0, indicating that Ba–P–I compositions can be thermodynamically viable. **[CS, SS]** The target remains above the hull, so competing phases and metastability cannot be excluded. **[Target data; CS, SS]**

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the closest chemical and structural analogue and therefore avoids the most severe temperature penalty. **[CS, SS]** Nevertheless, the Ba-phosphide precursor is air-sensitive, I₂ is volatile, and the process requires glovebox preparation, evacuation, sealing, and controlled heating/cooling. **[CS, SS]** These factors justify the 55/100 score. **[CS, SS]**

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is based on directly documented Ba–P and Ba–P–I ampoule reactions. **[SS]** Its main disadvantage is that elemental Ba, P, and I₂ must react simultaneously, increasing the likelihood of incomplete conversion and competing binary phosphides. **[SS; Reasonable Inference]** The conditions remain below 1000 °C but require a sealed inert system, so the same 55/100 condition-severity score is assigned. **[SS]**

### F2 — Route 3

The flux route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, consistent with the moderate-temperature flux and cooling-crystallization examples in TS. **[TS]** However, a dry inert or vacuum-sealed system is still necessary because BaI₂/phosphides are moisture-sensitive and iodine-containing species can be volatile. **[CS, SS; Reasonable Inference]** The lack of a directly demonstrated Ba–P–I flux synthesis prevents a higher score. **[TS; Reasonable Inference]**

### F3 — Route 1

The phosphide route benefits from chemically activated Ba–P precursors and a close Ba–P–I structural analogue, giving relatively favorable precursor activity and reconstruction scores. **[CS, SS]** Ion transport is acceptable at the demonstrated 777 °C reaction temperature, but the target must reorganize from the Ba₅P₅I₃-type chemistry into a chiral P3₂ structure. **[CS, SS]** The resulting F3 score is 70/100. **[CS, SS; Reasonable Inference]**

### F3 — Route 2

The elemental route has favorable elemental reactivity for Ba but suffers from poorer precursor preorganization and greater diffusion/reaction heterogeneity than Route 1. **[SS; Reasonable Inference]** Ba₅P₉ and Ba₂P₇I demonstrate that direct sealed-tube reactions can form Ba–P and Ba–P–I frameworks, but their structural similarity to BaPI is only moderate. **[SS]** This supports an F3 score of 65/100. **[SS]**

### F3 — Route 3

A halide flux can provide liquid-assisted transport and increase the effective precursor activity, which is the primary kinetic advantage of Route 3. **[TS; Reasonable Inference]** Slow cooling in TS flux syntheses is consistent with promoting crystal growth and ordering. **[TS]** However, no directly chemically similar Ba–P–I flux reaction is supplied, so the structural-reconstruction score remains modest and the overall F3 is limited to 75/100. **[TS, SS; Reasonable Inference]**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius-mismatch trigger is not applicable. **[Target data]** The structure description confirms corner- and edge-sharing connectivity but does not report face sharing, so the mixed vertex/edge/face trigger is not established. **[Target data]** Unit-cell atom count is unavailable; therefore, **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for C2, which is scored neutral at 50/100 and lowers confidence in the F4 estimate. **[CS, SS, TS]**

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaPI is more plausibly a metastable or weakly metastable phase than a robust ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.080 eV atom⁻¹, although its reported dynamic-stability screening was passed. **[Target data]** CS and SS provide strong support for chemical accessibility because Ba–P–I compounds, including Ba₅P₅I₃ and Ba₂P₇I, have been synthesized by sealed solid-state methods. **[CS, SS]** TS supports the use of flux-assisted transport and slow cooling, but its chemical similarity is limited and therefore provides weaker direct evidence. **[TS]** The most important enabling factor is the close Ba–P–I phosphide-iodination precedent. **[CS, SS]** The most critical limitation is phase selectivity: the target must compete with iodine-poor Ba–P–I phases and binary barium phosphides while preserving its chiral P3₂ ordering. **[CS, SS; Reasonable Inference]**
