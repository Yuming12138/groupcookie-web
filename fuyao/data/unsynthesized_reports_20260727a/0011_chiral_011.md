# SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target is a selenium-rich titanium bromoselenide with a two-dimensional monoclinic \(P2_1\) structure. The closest chemical precedent is the CVT synthesis of Nb–Se–Br ribbons from elemental metal, Se, and Br\(_2\) at 500–460 °C under vacuum (CS). Structurally and thermodynamically, Cu–Se–Br compounds provide additional precedent for both CVT and sealed-ampoule melt growth, including a two-dimensional monoclinic phase (SS, TS). Direct solid-state synthesis is also plausible by analogy with Fe–Ge–Se and Cr–Sb–Se systems prepared from elements in evacuated quartz ampoules, although Ti and Br introduce greater volatility and reactivity concerns (CS, TS).

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The CS, SS, and TS datasets provide low-hull analogues, including NbSeBr\(_3\), CuBrSe\(_3\), CuSe\(_2\)Br, and CrSbSe\(_3\), which support the general thermodynamic plausibility of Se/Br transition-metal frameworks (CS, SS, TS). **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an independent target hull calculation; those sources are neutral at 50 for that direct factor.** The remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated zero-hull phase is experimentally accessible under the required bromine chemical potential and whether competing polymorphs intervene [Reasonable Inference; CS, SS, TS].

## F2: Route 1 — CVT

The Nb–Se–Br precedent directly demonstrates synthesis from elemental metal, Se, and Br\(_2\) in a vacuum temperature gradient at 500–460 °C (CS). Cu–Se–Br CVT further supports the use of a sealed transport tube and moderate temperature gradient (SS, TS). The deductions arise mainly from vacuum operation, volatile Se/Br, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents; no high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is required (CS, SS, TS).

## F3: Route 1 — CVT

CVT improves transport relative to a purely solid-state reaction, as shown by the successful growth of Nb–Se–Br and Cu–Se–Br crystals from sealed tubes and temperature gradients (CS, SS, TS). Nevertheless, Ti metal is expected to be less kinetically active than a molecular halide precursor, and the target’s two-dimensional chiral structure is more complex than the closest one-dimensional CVT analogue [Reasonable Inference; CS, SS]. The main uncertainty is whether the transport species and bromine activity will selectively produce TiSe\(_3\)Br\(_2\) rather than other Ti–Se–Br compositions [Reasonable Inference; CS, SS, TS].

## F2: Route 2 — Direct Solid-State Reaction

Direct fusion in evacuated quartz ampoules is established for Fe\(_2\)GeSe\(_4\), and repeated sintering in evacuated tubes is established for CrSbSe\(_3\) (CS, TS). The target route is penalized for vacuum containment, volatile Se/Br, and the likely need for multiple grinding/annealing cycles. The direct examples do not include a bromine-rich Ti compound, so the severity estimate includes an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference; CS, TS].

## F3: Route 2 — Direct Solid-State Reaction

The Fe–Ge–Se synthesis and especially the Cr–Sb–Se synthesis show that chalcogen-rich solid-state frameworks can require prolonged heating and repeated annealing for phase formation (CS, TS). Ti metal is a relatively refractory starting material, making diffusion and reaction completion more difficult than for molecular or molten precursors [Reasonable Inference; CS, TS]. The major uncertainty is whether a high-temperature treatment would improve ordering or instead cause bromine loss and decomposition [Reasonable Inference; CS, TS].

## F2: Route 3 — Bromide/Alkali-Halide Flux

InBr\(_3\)/metal bromide syntheses at 450 °C demonstrate that bromide-rich reactions can proceed at moderate temperature in evacuated ampoules with controlled cooling (CS). The proposed flux route still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed handling of volatile or moisture-sensitive bromides, and several operational steps [Reasonable Inference; CS].
