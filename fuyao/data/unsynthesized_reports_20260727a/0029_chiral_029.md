# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target Eₕᵤₗₗ of 0.060 eV/atom falls just above the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability boundary in the supplied rubric, giving an F1 score in the low-80s. **[Target validation data]** CS and SS analogues are generally at or below approximately 0.05 eV/atom, while TS analogues include Ba₂S₃ and BaThBr₆ near 0.06 eV/atom, supporting the plausibility of a low-energy metastable phase. **[CS: mp-28049, mp-27985; SS: mp-32711, mp-28021; TS: mp-28978, mp-570615]** The main uncertainty is whether the computed chiral ordering is kinetically accessible and whether competing Sr–S or Sr–Br phases are lower in energy under experimental conditions.

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because related Sr halide syntheses use 650–860 °C, sealed or evacuated ampoules, controlled cooling, and annealing. **[SS: mp-32711, mp-28021]** Vacuum/inert handling and possible bromide volatility impose deductions, while no high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C are required. **[SS: mp-32711; CS: mp-28049]** The principal uncertainty is whether SrS can react completely with SrBr₂ without formation of binary Sr sulfide/bromide impurities. **[Reasonable Inference]**

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the same nominal F2 score as Route 1 because it remains below 1000 °C but requires sealed containment and controlled atmosphere. **[CS: mp-28049; SS: mp-32711]** Its advantage is that melting or partial melting can improve contact and diffusion, but the liquid phase may also increase bromide volatility and compositional drift. **[SS: mp-32711; Reasonable Inference]** The route is therefore conditionally favorable rather than unambiguously easier.

## F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives the lowest F2 score because it adds a separate milling operation and still requires inert or vacuum handling, sealed annealing, and air-sensitive precursors. **[Reasonable Inference; SS: mp-28021]** Mechanical activation may reduce the thermal burden, but no direct CS/SS/TS precedent establishes that it will preserve the target’s sulfur and bromine stoichiometry. **[CS/SS/TS: insufficient direct mechanochemical evidence]**

## F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate because SrBr₂-based mixtures have demonstrated successful reaction under high-temperature, near-melting conditions with controlled cooling. **[SS: mp-32711, mp-28021]** The Sr–Br portion should therefore be kinetically accessible, but formation of the S–S motif and chiral P2₁2₁2₁ ordering requires additional structural reconstruction. **[TS: mp-28978; Target structure; Reasonable Inference]** Long annealing may be necessary if ordering is slow, as seen in related ordered halide systems. **[SS: mp-28021; TS: mp-570615]**

## F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the highest F3 score because above-melting treatment and controlled cooling are directly supported by BaBrCl and Sr mixed-halide procedures. **[CS: mp-28049; SS: mp-32711]** A bromide-rich liquid should improve ion transport and reduce solid-state diffusion limitations. **[Reasonable Inference]** The uncertainty is that the target is not merely a mixed halide: its S–S bond and chiral ordering may not nucleate from the same liquid pathway. **[TS: mp-28978; Reasonable Inference]**

## F3 — Route 3

Mechanical activation improves the precursor-contact component of kinetics by increasing defects and reducing diffusion lengths, but this benefit is inferred rather than directly demonstrated in the supplied data. **[Reasonable Inference]** The route still requires thermal reconstruction into the target structure, and no mechanochemical analogue containing the same Sr–S–Br combination is supplied. **[CS/SS/TS: insufficient direct evidence]** Consequently, its kinetic score is comparable to Route 2 but with lower confidence.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. **[Target structure]** The unit-cell atom count is not specified sufficiently to trigger C2; therefore this factor is neutral because of insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS. **[CS/SS/TS: insufficient evidence; neutral 50]** Mixed coordination and connectivity are supported by the target description and related Sr bromide/Ba sulfide structures, giving one trigger and a low–medium amorphization risk. **[SS: mp-32711, mp-567744; TS: mp-28978]**

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrSBr is most likely a **low-energy metastable phase**, not a confirmed ground-state compound, because its calculated Eₕᵤₗₗ is positive at 0.060 eV/atom despite reported dynamic stability. **[Target validation data]** CS, SS, and TS analogues collectively support the chemical plausibility of combining Sr/alkaline-earth cation environments with sulfide, S–S, and bromide substructures. **[CS: mp-28049; SS: mp-32711, mp-28021; TS: mp-28978, mp-570615]** The most important enabling factor is a bromide-rich molten or partially molten medium that can accelerate diffusion and permit framework reconstruction. **[SS: mp-32711, mp-28021; CS: mp-28049; Reasonable Inference]** The main limiting factor is selective formation of the S–S-containing chiral phase without bromide loss or competing SrS/SrBr₂-derived phases. **[Reasonable Inference]** Overall, a sealed-ampoule melt/controlled-cooling experiment is justified as the first experimental test.
