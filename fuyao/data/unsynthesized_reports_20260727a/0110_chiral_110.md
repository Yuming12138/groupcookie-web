### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Target E_hull = 0.145 eV/atom is not ground-state but falls in the moderate metastability window, so a mid-to-upper score within 65–85 is appropriate [Target]. TS analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally known or reported candidates, but several require high pressure or show metastability, limiting confidence [TS1–TS5]. CS and SS analogues are mostly 0-hull stable phases and therefore support chemical/structural accessibility but are less direct for target metastability scoring [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 conditions are moderate in temperature because CS boride and intermetallic analogues use 900–950 °C sealed reactions rather than extreme temperatures [CS2, CS5]. The severity score is reduced by controlled atmosphere, multi-step sealing/annealing, and Be toxicity [CS1, CS2, Target]. Kinetics are only moderate because elemental powders/chunks and refractory B require long diffusion times, although molten Al at the reaction temperature may improve transport **[Reasonable Inference]** [CS2, CS5]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for ambient sealed elemental Be–Al–B synthesis is treated as neutral 50 for this route-specific condition signal.

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Arc melting is strongly supported by SS analogues and Be-rich TS5, but the high local melt temperature and Be fume hazard make condition severity worse than Route 1 [SS1–SS5, TS5, Target]. Kinetics improve because melt homogenization shortens diffusion length, but SS analogues report long annealing, impurity phases, and equilibrium challenges [SS2–SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS for an exact Be–Al–B arc route is neutral 50, although CS3/CS4 broadly support melt-processing of intermetallics [CS3, CS4].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
TS analogues provide the strongest support for high-pressure access to metastable phases near the target hull, so Route 3 is chemically plausible for metastability control [TS1–TS4, Target]. The F2 score is low because >5 GPa pressure, capsule handling, high temperature, multiple steps, and toxic Be all apply [TS1–TS4, Target]. Kinetics are moderate because HP treatment can stabilize dense metastable phases but uses compact pellets and short holds, so complete reconstruction remains uncertain [TS1–TS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for high-pressure BeAl₃B synthesis is neutral 50 for Route 3.

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
SS analogues support Al-rich melt/anneal crystallization, but a true Al self-flux route for BeAl₃B is an extrapolation rather than directly demonstrated [SS2–SS5, Reasonable Inference]. F2 is better than arc melting because the route avoids extreme arc temperatures, but sealed processing, slow cooling, and Be toxicity still apply [SS5, CS2, Target]. F3 is relatively favorable because liquid Al should improve transport, but B solubility, Be retention, and narrow phase windows remain uncertain **[Reasonable Inference]** [SS2–SS5]. Insufficient evidence from TS for Al-flux BeAl₃B growth is neutral 50.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is assigned because Be/Al size mismatch is likely >15%, while the target does not appear to exceed 40 atoms per unit cell and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing target network is reported **[Reasonable Inference]** [Target]. SS analogues show complex crystalline Al-rich frameworks can form, which lowers concern that structural complexity alone forces amorphization [SS3–SS5]. CS and TS provide insufficient target-specific evidence for unit-cell size and polyhedral sharing, so those source signals are neutral 50 for F4-specific checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeAl₃B is best viewed as a **metastable but potentially accessible** phase rather than a likely ground state, because its E_hull is 0.145 eV/atom while the supplied candidate passed dynamic stability screening [Target]. CS analogues support direct elemental synthesis of ternary borides/intermetallics, SS analogues support Al-rich melt/anneal crystallization, and TS analogues show that similar-hull phases can be accessed, sometimes only by high pressure [CS2, CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the Al-rich composition, because molten Al can plausibly aid transport and crystallization during sealed annealing or flux-like growth **[Reasonable Inference]** [Target, SS2–SS5]. The main limiting factor is not basic chemistry but phase selectivity: target metastability, possible competing Al/Be/B phases, and Be toxicity all raise experimental difficulty [Target, SS2–SS5, TS1–TS5]. Overall, synthesis is feasible enough to attempt, but it should be treated as a controlled exploratory solid-state campaign rather than a routine preparation [CS2, CS5, Target].
