# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The supplied target \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.030\) eV/atom places it in the most favorable scoring bin, giving F1 = 95/100. This is compatible with TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, and TS-5, whose thermodynamically similar materials have hull energies near 0.029–0.049 eV/atom. The target’s low hull energy suggests that bulk formation is plausible, but the value is not zero, so the target may still be metastable relative to competing phases.

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures and no external pressure, which is favorable relative to the high-pressure method in TS-3. However, controlled Ar/vacuum conditions and sealed tubes are required, as in CS-3, SS-1, SS-3, and TS-2. The principal uncertainty is fluorine retention; CS/SS/TS contain no directly comparable Al–S–F synthesis, so this limitation is [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 1

The solid-state route is kinetically plausible because CS-3 and SS-1 demonstrate sulfide formation around 623 K–700 °C, while SS-3 and TS-2 demonstrate higher-temperature sealed-tube synthesis. The target requires greater structural reorganization than a simple binary sulfide because it contains both Al–F and S–S linkages, so structural-reconstruction scoring is only moderate. CS-5 also indicates that Al–S materials can require long thermal treatments and careful phase control.

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 requires vacuum sealing, iodine transport, a thermal gradient, and a multiday growth period. These requirements are directly represented in CS-4 and TS-5, while TS-2 also documents iodine-assisted chalcogenide transport. The possibility of volatile fluorine-containing transport species is not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS and is therefore [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 2

CVT receives the strongest kinetic score because a vapor-mediated transport mechanism can bypass slow bulk diffusion. CS-4 is particularly relevant because it produced an Al–S material by iodine-assisted CVT at 740 °C, and TS-2/TS-5 show related transport strategies for sulfur-rich materials. The main uncertainty is whether the transport chemistry deposits AlS₃F rather than Al₂S₃, AlF₃, sulfur-rich phases, or iodine-containing impurities.

## F2 — Route 3

The precursor-assisted fluorination route avoids very high temperatures but introduces a highly reactive fluorination step. The solid-state precursor stage is supported by CS-4, CS-5, and SS-3, whereas the fluorination stage has no direct CS/SS/TS precedent. Consequently, the atmosphere, toxicity, and multistep deductions are partly [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 3

Preforming an Al–S framework may improve local structural organization before fluorine incorporation, but it could also make F diffusion the rate-limiting step. CS/SS/TS provide useful evidence for Al–S precursor formation and sulfur-rich structures, particularly CS-5 and SS-5, but insufficient evidence for post-synthetic fluorination. The structural-reconstruction score is therefore lower than for CVT.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target description suggests a framework dominated by corner-sharing AlS₃F₂ units with S–S linkages, rather than the strongly mixed edge/face-sharing networks present in some analogues such as CS-4 and SS-3. The unit-cell atom count is not supplied, so CS/SS/TS are neutral for that check at 50 as required. Overall, the target is expected to have low-to-medium amorphization risk, but this estimate remains less certain than F1.
