### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.150 eV/atom is metastable but inside the 65–85 scoring band. [Target] Chemically and structurally close Nb–Se–Cl and Nb–S–Br analogues are reported at or near the hull, which supports the broader stability of Nb/chalcogen/halide frameworks. [CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5, SS1–SS4] Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.13–0.18 eV/atom have reported syntheses, but they are not exact Nb–S–Cl analogues. [TS1–TS4]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires sealed evacuated tubes and volatile/air-sensitive chloride precursors. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4] The one-pot solid-state route is operationally simpler than CVT, but the sealed halide system creates safety and stoichiometry constraints. [CS1, CS2, Reasonable Inference] TS gives only indirect support for sealed halide ampoules, so the most relevant F2 evidence is weighted toward CS/SS. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4; insufficient direct TS evidence = 50 neutral]

**F3 — Route 1**  
The strongest kinetic advantage is structural transfer: the closest analogues are 1D Nb–Se–Cl ribbons with similar Nb–chalcogen/halide coordination. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4] The limiting factor is slow ordering and phase selection, because analogues require one to several weeks and report low reproducibility or side-product formation. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4] Sulfur substitution for selenium is chemically reasonable but not directly demonstrated for NbS₂Cl₃. [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS2, CS3]

**F2 — Route 2**  
CVT uses moderate temperatures but imposes vacuum sealing, volatile halide transport, and multi-step crystal-growth handling. [CS3, SS3] The route is more severe than direct sealed-tube reaction because successful growth depends on a controlled gradient over several weeks. [CS3, SS3] TS1 supports gradient ampoule synthesis generally, but not exact Nb–S–Cl CVT. [TS1]

**F3 — Route 2**  
CVT improves mobility through vapor transport, and NbX₂Y₂ analogues with S/Se and Cl/Br/I support halide-mediated crystal growth. [CS3, SS3] The main uncertainty is that the demonstrated CVT analogues are layered NbX₂Y₂ phases, whereas the target is a 1D NbS₂Cl₃ ribbon. [CS3, SS3, Target] Phase purity risk is elevated because the same evidence reports polymorphism and stoichiometry sensitivity. [CS3, SS3]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Elemental halogen-rich ampoule synthesis can be short and moderate-temperature, as shown by Nb–S–Br and Ta–S–Br analogues. [CS5, SS1, TS1] The penalty comes from sealed volatile halogen donors and air-/moisture-sensitive chloride chemistry. [CS5, SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference] It is less controlled than Route 1 because chlorine fugacity must be generated directly from aggressive halogen donors. [Reasonable Inference from CS5, TS1]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Halogen-rich media should enhance transport, but Nb metal remains a refractory kinetic bottleneck. [CS5, SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference] Structural similarity is moderate because Nb(SBr)₂ contains Nb–S and S–S motifs similar to the target, but it is 2D and bromide-based rather than 1D chloride-based. [CS5, SS1, Target] TS1 supports halogenated Ta–S cluster formation, but the cation and dimensionality differ from NbS₂Cl₃. [TS1, Target]

**F2 — Route 4**  
The solution/solvothermal route is low-temperature but uses highly reactive chlorosulfide/chloride reagents and controlled dry/sealed handling. [CS4, TS4, SS5] Multi-step isolation and crystallization are expected from the cluster analogues. [CS4, TS4, SS5] CS/TS/SS support chlorosulfide formation but not a simple benign synthesis environment. [CS4, TS4, SS5]

**F3 — Route 4**  
Molecular reagents give high precursor activity, as shown by Mo and Ru chlorosulfide cluster syntheses. [CS4, TS4, SS5] The major kinetic/structural weakness is that those analogues crystallize as 0D clusters, not extended 1D ribbons. [CS4, TS4, SS5, Target] Therefore this route is exploratory rather than recommended for first-pass synthesis. [Reasonable Inference from CS4, TS4, SS5]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. [Target, Reasonable Inference] The formula is small and the described motif is an ordered 1D ribbon; exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so those sources are neutral for C2. [Target, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS = 50 neutral] CS/SS analogues repeatedly form crystalline 1D/2D low-dimensional halochalcogenides, which argues against a high intrinsic amorphization tendency, although target-specific polyhedral connectivity is not fully specified. [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS4, Target]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbS₂Cl₃ is most likely a metastable but potentially isolable phase rather than a ground-state phase, because its target E_hull is 0.150 eV/atom. [Target] The synthesis case is supported most strongly by chemically and structurally close Nb–Se–Cl ribbons and Nb–S–Br analogues made in sealed halide-containing tubes. [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2, SS4] The enabling factor is halide-mediated transport/crystallization, which allows low-temperature ordering of refractory Nb compounds far below Nb’s melting point. [CS1, CS2, CS3, TS1, Reasonable Inference] The limiting factor is phase selectivity: close analogues show poor reproducibility, side-product formation, polymorphism, and inhomogeneous products. [CS1, CS2, CS3, SS2–SS4] Overall, the target is moderately synthesizable, with the best attempt being a carefully controlled sealed-tube NbS₂/Nb chloride reaction. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4, Reasonable Inference]
