### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZnSe₃Cl₂ is more likely a **metastable crystalline phase** than a ground-state phase because its provided E_hull is 0.180 eV/atom, while TS shows experimentally realized analogues in a similar metastability range. [TS1, TS2] The strongest support comes from SS because Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ and related chalcogen-halides demonstrate that Se–Se/halide frameworks can crystallize under low-temperature sealed or hydrothermal conditions. [SS1, SS4] CS supports sealed-tube/CVT Se-rich halide growth through CuBrSe₃, but CS does not provide a direct Zn–Se–Cl example. [CS4] The key enabling factor is low-temperature solution/melt/vapor transport that preserves volatile Se/Cl species. [SS1, SS4, CS4] The key limiting factor is phase purity, because SS/TS analogues frequently report multiphase products, impurities, or narrow cooling/stoichiometry windows. [SS1, SS2, SS3, TS2]

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile Se/Cl loss during heating or transport. | High | Use sealed quartz/Vycor ampoules or sealed quartz tubes in autoclaves, as used for CVT and hydrothermal analogues. [CS4, SS4, SS5, TS2] |
| 2 | All | Competing phase formation and multiphase products. | High | Screen Zn:Se:Cl ratio, avoid uncontrolled excess Se, and use slow cooling or mechanical separation when needed. [SS1, SS3, TS2] |
| 3 | Route 1 | SeCl₄ toxicity, moisture sensitivity, and over-chlorination risk. | High | Use dry inert loading, small charges, sealed secondary containment, and Se-rich but Cl-controlled compositions. [SS1, Reasonable Inference] |
| 4 | Route 2 | Corrosive acid pressure and possible mixed hydrothermal products. | Med–High | Use sealed quartz tube inside steel autoclave, slow cooling near 2 °C/h, and post-growth crystal selection. [SS4, TS2] |
| 5 | Route 3 | Narrow transport/crystallization window. | Medium | Apply a controlled temperature gradient similar to 340→290 °C CuBrSe₃ CVT and vary hot/cold zones systematically. [CS4, SS5] |
| 6 | Route 4 | Cooling-rate sensitivity and metastable decomposition. | High | Screen quench vs. slow cool because CuSe₂Br formation depended critically on rapid cooling. [SS2] |
| 7 | Route 4 | Slow ordering kinetics in bulk sealed ampoules. | Medium | Use regrinding/annealing cycles or long anneals, as used for solid-state chalcogenide analogues. [CS2, TS3, TS5] |
| 8 | Route 5 | Flux contamination or alkali incorporation. | Medium | Use low-temperature flux, slow cooling, and inert flux removal; verify product by PXRD/EDS. [TS1, Reasonable Inference] |
| 9 | All | No exact ZnSe₃Cl₂ experimental analogue in CS/SS/TS. | Medium | Start with the structurally closest low-temperature SS routes and confirm phase identity before scaling. [SS1, SS4, Reasonable Inference] |
