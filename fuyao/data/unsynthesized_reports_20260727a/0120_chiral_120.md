CS supports the chemical feasibility of Nb-rich intermetallic and Nb–B formation, especially by arc melting and high-temperature annealing [CS1–CS5]. SS supports structural transferability most strongly through Nb–In/Nb–Sn A15-like analogues and flux-grown Nb-rich intermetallics, but none is the exact chiral boride topology [SS1–SS5]. The key enabling factor is access to non-equilibrium or liquid-mediated pathways that can bypass slow Nb/B diffusion [SS1; SS3; SS5; CS3]. The key limiting factor is maintaining precise Nb:In:B stoichiometry while avoiding In volatility and competing Nb–B or Nb–In phases [SS5; TS1; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | In volatility and stoichiometry drift | High | Use sealed ampoules, In excess/vapor buffer, or rate-controlled film deposition [SS5; TS1; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | All | Competing Nb–B and Nb–In phases | High | Use phase screening, slight composition gradients, and repeated homogenization/annealing [CS1; CS2–CS5; SS5]. |
| 3 | Routes 1–2 | Slow Nb/B diffusion and incomplete reaction | High | Use arc remelting, long anneals, regrinding, or mechanochemical activation [CS1; CS2–CS5; CS3; SS2]. |
| 4 | Route 3 | Poor B solubility in In/Sn flux | Medium–High | Test NbB2 or pre-reacted Nb–B source charge and slow-cool flux compositions [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 5 | Route 4 | Narrow composition and substrate-temperature window | High | Calibrate independent Nb/In/B fluxes and use composition-spread thin films [SS5; TS1; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Route 5 | Amorphous product or secondary phases after milling | Medium | Use controlled annealing near 900 °C and monitor crystallization/impurity formation [CS3]. |
| 7 | Route 6 | Halide over-transport or boron immobility | High | Use CVT only after pre-reacting a source powder and keep I2 loading low [TS2; TS3; TS5; Reasonable Inference]. |
