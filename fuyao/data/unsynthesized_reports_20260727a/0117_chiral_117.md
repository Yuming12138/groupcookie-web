### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The target E_hull of 0.166 eV/atom places Be3BSb in the metastable but potentially accessible 65–85 scoring band (Target). TS analogues near or above ~0.20 eV/atom often require high pressure or harsh thermal control, while SS Be-rich analogues around ~0.11 eV/atom are experimentally known but phase-sensitive, so 75/100 is appropriate rather than a ground-state score (TS1–TS4, SS2–SS4). Remaining uncertainty is that finite-temperature entropy, defects, and experimental competing Be–B/Sb phases are not resolved by CS/SS/TS data (CS2, CS5, SS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 1:** Route 1 is strongly supported by CS/SS precedent for direct elemental boride and Be–B synthesis in sealed/inert systems, but it carries deductions for >1000 °C operation, sealed volatile control, multistep annealing, and Be toxicity (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, Target). Kinetically, direct elements are only moderately active, and long anneals plus impurity reports imply slow diffusion and difficult phase selection (CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1). CS3, TS2, and SS5 provide insufficient useful signal for this route and are treated as neutral 50 evidence.

**F2/F3 — Route 2:** Arc melting is supported for Be-rich intermetallic analogues and generic thermodynamic analogues, but it is penalized for very high local temperature, inert-atmosphere control, volatility, multistep remelting/annealing, and Be toxicity (SS2–SS4, TS5, Target). Kinetically, the melt improves transport, but SS2–SS4 report phase impurity, inhomogeneity, and contamination risks, so structural-selection kinetics remain weak (SS2–SS4). CS3 and TS2 are neutral 50 because solution/hydrogenation routes do not inform arc-melt Be3BSb synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 3:** High pressure is justified by TS analogues where metastable phases are accessed at 1–7.7 GPa and 500–1000 °C, but pressure equipment and capsule workflow impose major severity deductions (TS3, TS4). Kinetic gains are only moderate because powdered-element reactions still need structural reconstruction, and TS3/TS4 report narrow phase windows, impurity phases, or decomposition sensitivity (TS3, TS4). CS evidence is insufficient for pressure stabilization of Be3BSb and is neutral 50 for the pressure-specific factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 4:** Sb flux is the highest-scoring route because it plausibly lowers reaction temperature and improves mass transport [Reasonable Inference], while sealed-system handling is supported by CS/SS volatile-component precedents (CS1, CS2, SS1). The route remains uncertain because CS/SS/TS contain no direct Sb-flux analogue for Be3BSb, so the direct route-transfer evidence is neutral 50 and the expert adjustment is negative. Its kinetic score is higher than direct solid-state because a liquid transport medium should reduce diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference], but phase selection remains uncertain due to analogue multiphase behavior (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2–SS4).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** One amorphization trigger is assigned for large Be/B/Sb size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], while the target appears structurally compact and not obviously >40 atoms/cell (Target). Mixed polyhedral sharing is present in several SS Be-rich analogues, but it is not explicitly proven for Be3BSb, so C3 is not triggered and CS/SS/TS are neutral 50 for that specific check (SS2–SS4, Target). The resulting 78/100 reflects low–medium amorphization risk rather than high glass-forming risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Be3BSb is more likely a metastable phase than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.166 eV/atom, even though the target data reports dynamic stability with no imaginary phonon modes (Target). CS and SS analogues support synthesis in sealed high-temperature environments, especially because ternary borides and Be–B phases can be made from elemental powders under Ar/vacuum conditions (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1). TS analogues support contingency use of high pressure for above-hull phases, but they also imply narrower phase windows and more difficult optimization (TS3, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is sealed, composition-retentive synthesis that allows Be/B/Sb to react without losing volatile components [Reasonable Inference] (CS1, CS2, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is Be toxicity combined with volatile/phase-control risk, especially because Be–B and Be-rich analogues report sublimation, impurity, and inhomogeneity concerns (Target, SS1, SS2–SS4).
