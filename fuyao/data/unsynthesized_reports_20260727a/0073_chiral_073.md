### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s 0.160 eV/atom hull energy falls in the metastable 65–85 scoring band, and TS provides useful support because TS1, TS3, and TS5 include synthesized analogues around 0.147–0.176 eV/atom. SS provides favorable structural evidence because several close chalcogen-halide analogues are near the hull, including SS1 and SS3 at 0.0 eV/atom and SS2/SS5 near 0.006/0.0035 eV/atom. CS is mixed: CS1 is stable and CS3 is moderately metastable, but CS5 has very high E_hull and is therefore a weaker stability analogue.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-Ampoule Direct Solid-State

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because CS1/TS5 use evacuated ampoules and long heat treatments, while the target also requires volatile iodine/selenium control. Kinetics are acceptable because Ga is not refractory and sealed-tube selenide synthesis is known from CS1/TS5, but complete reconstruction into a chiral Ga–Se–I chain/network is a significant uncertainty. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct solid-state Ga–Se–I synthesis is scored neutral where direct-route transferability is concerned.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Chemical Vapor Transport

Condition severity is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile iodine/selenium chemistry, multistep gradient growth, and toxic/corrosive precursors, all consistent with CS2/CS3/SS4 practice. Kinetics are strong because CS2/CS3/SS4 show that vapor/halide transport enables crystalline chalcogenide or chalcogen-halide products at accessible temperatures. This is the most transferable route because SS4 contains metal–Se–halide tetrahedra and Se-chain bonding similar to the target, while CS3 gives an iodide-containing CVT precedent.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Acidic Hydrothermal / HI

Condition severity is lower by the rubric because temperatures are only ~180–250 °C and pressure is autoclave rather than >5 GPa, as shown by SS5 and TS1. Kinetics may be good because solution transport is fast, and SS1–SS3/TS1 strongly support chalcogen–iodide hydrothermal crystallization. The uncertainty is high because Ga–Se–I speciation is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS, and TS1 reports multiphase products requiring separation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Flux Growth

TS2 supports low-temperature liquid-mediated synthesis, which gives favorable diffusion and precursor mobility. The condition penalty remains meaningful because TS2 uses vacuum sealing, glovebox/N₂ handling, slow cooling, and air-sensitive flux chemistry. The main uncertainty is transferability: TS2 is a polychalcogenide flux system, while CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for neutral Ga(SeI)₃ flux crystallization.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target’s formula and described site count imply a small-to-moderate low-dimensional unit rather than a >40-atom complex cell, and SS analogues show crystalline low-dimensional chalcogen-halide frameworks. Mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity is not evident from the target description, so amorphization risk is low, though selenium/iodine-rich materials can still form glassy or multiphase products if quenched too fast. [Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ga(SeI)₃ is more likely a metastable synthesizable phase than a true ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.160 eV/atom, while TS includes experimentally realized analogues in a similar metastability range. The strongest support comes from SS4 and CS3, which demonstrate crystallization of related chalcogen-halide frameworks by temperature-gradient transport. The most critical enabling factor is controlled iodine/selenium chemical potential in a sealed gradient, because CS2/CS3/SS4 show that halogen transport can overcome kinetic limitations. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against Ga₂Se₃, GaI₃, elemental Se/I₂ condensates, and other Ga–Se–I stoichiometries. Hydrothermal HI chemistry is plausible from SS1–SS3/TS1, but it is less preferred because Ga speciation is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS and TS1 warns of multiphase products.
