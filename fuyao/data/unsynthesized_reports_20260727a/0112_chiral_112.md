### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.151 eV/atom places it in the metastable but potentially accessible 65–85 scoring interval [Target]. TS is strongly supportive because all five thermodynamic analogues are reported as synthesized despite E_hull values around 0.17–0.22 eV/atom for several entries [TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. CS and SS are also supportive because the closest Pt–B analogue is on the hull and experimentally synthesized [CS-1; SS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and OQMD absence is not an exhaustive novelty or experimental proof [Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
The route is conditionally severe because it requires arc melting, Ar/vacuum control, and long sealed annealing, but it avoids high pressure and radioactive precursors [CS-1; SS-1; TS-2]. Kinetics are moderate because arc melting improves initial mixing, while long 600 °C annealing indicates ordering or diffusion remains slow [CS-1; SS-1]. The strongest support is that the closest CS/SS analogue is synthesized by the same route [CS-1; SS-1].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
The sealed solid-state route has similar atmosphere and multi-step penalties to Route 1, but it lacks the strong liquid mixing step from arc melting [CS-2; TS-3]. Kinetic risk is higher because Pt/B diffusion through solids is slow by [Reasonable Inference], and analogues require 50–120 h at 1100–1230 °C [CS-2; TS-3]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for this ambient sealed direct-reaction route and is neutral 50 for that route-specific factor [SS].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
The controlled-cooling melt route improves kinetics and crystallinity relative to simple annealing, as shown by LaIr4B4 requiring high-T treatment after poorly crystallized arc products [CS-5]. Its F2 score is lower because it adds >1400 °C processing, sealed containment, and tighter cooling control [CS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether Pt3AlB2 has a compatible melt/crystallization window; TS and SS provide only indirect support for high-temperature bulk processing [TS-1; SS-4].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
High pressure is justified only as a contingency because structurally similar Rh borides required 5–9 GPa, 1200 °C, and quenching [SS-3; SS-5]. The route receives the lowest F2 score due to pressure, encapsulation, and multi-step handling [SS-3; SS-5]. CS and TS provide insufficient high-pressure signal for this target route and are neutral 50 for the route-specific high-pressure factor [CS; TS].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
No F4 trigger is strongly supported: the target description gives a crystalline ordered P3₁ structure and corner-sharing pyramids rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not directly provide target ionic radii or unit-cell atom count, so those source signals are neutral 50 for C1 and C2 [CS; SS; TS]. The low amorphization risk is partly supported indirectly because multiple complex ternary borides crystallize under bulk conditions [CS-1; CS-2; SS-1; TS-1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt3AlB2 is most likely a **metastable** rather than true ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.151 eV/atom, although the reported no-imaginary-frequency screening supports local dynamic stability [Target]. The CS/SS evidence is favorable because the closest analogue, Pt9Cu3B5, is experimentally accessible by elemental arc melting plus long annealing [CS-1; SS-1]. The TS evidence is also favorable because several thermodynamically similar borides are experimentally reported at comparable or higher hull energies [TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. The key enabling factor is the established ability of Pt/PGM–B networks to form crystalline borides under arc-melt/anneal conditions [CS-1; TS-1]. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity, because analogous boride systems report impurities, narrow stoichiometry windows, peritectic behavior, or poor crystallinity before optimized heat treatment [CS-5; TS-1; TS-3].
