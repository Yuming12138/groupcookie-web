### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
GaS₂I is likely metastable rather than ground-state because its E_hull is 0.120 eV/atom, which falls in the 65–85 scoring band [Target]. TS provides strong support because several thermodynamic analogues are synthesized or considered near similar metastability, including FeBi₄S₇, CrPS₄, and ZnS₂ [TS1, TS2, TS3]. CS is contradictory because CS1 is experimentally synthesized despite high computed E_hull, while CS2–CS5 are stable or near stable; I therefore weight target E_hull plus TS/SS trends more heavily than CS1 alone [CS1–CS5, TS1–TS3, SS1, SS4, SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C, but it requires vacuum sealing, volatile S/I containment, and dry handling [CS2, SS1, SS4, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetics are the best among routes because halide/chalcogenide melts or vapors can assist transport and because Ga–S tetrahedral analogues exist [CS2, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is phase competition, since related chalcohalides show sensitivity to stoichiometry and some failed members [SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 because evacuated ampoules, iodine transport, volatile components, and multi-step growth are required [TS3, TS4]. Kinetics are favorable for crystal growth because iodine-assisted CVT produced CrPS₄ and FeGa₂S₄ crystals [TS3, TS4]. The limiting uncertainty is composition control because target iodine is both a lattice component and potential transport agent [Target, TS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has milder condition severity because solvothermal/hydrothermal analogues run at 185–220 °C rather than high temperature [CS1, TS5]. Precursor mobility is good in solution, but F3 is penalized because no CS/SS/TS source directly shows Ga–S–I crystallization from solution and TS5 reports multiphase iodochalcogenide products [CS1, TS5]. The main uncertainty is gallium hydrolysis or amorphous gallium sulfide formation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaS₂I is most likely a **metastable but potentially isolable** phase rather than a ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.120 eV/atom and the target is theory-only [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from sealed-tube chalcohalide and Ga–chalcogenide analogues, especially CS2 and SS1/SS4/SS5 [CS2, SS1, SS4, SS5]. The most important enabling factor is closed-system control of sulfur and iodine chemical potentials, because the target contains both volatile iodine and an S–S unit [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition under narrow stoichiometry windows, which is reported in related chalcohalide and iodochalcogenide systems [SS5, TS5]. Dynamic stability improves the outlook because the target passed the no-imaginary-frequency screening, but no CS/SS/TS source verifies experimental GaS₂I synthesis [Target].
