### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull = 0.161 eV/atom indicates a metastable rather than ground-state compound under the provided MP-reference thermodynamics [Target Data]. TS supports feasibility because synthesized analogues appear at comparable hull energies, including TS1 at 0.186 eV/atom and TS5 at 0.214 eV/atom [TS1, TS5]. CS/SS support nearby stable Zn–Rh–B compounds but do not prove the target phase is a ground state [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 gets moderate condition severity because it avoids high pressure and reactive gases but requires 1200–1230 °C, sealed Ta/quartz containment, and multi-day processing [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Its kinetic score is limited by refractory Rh/B and reported long reaction times, but molten Zn and very close Zn–Rh–B analogues improve transferability [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity, because CS1/TS1 explicitly report mixture formation and narrow compositional windows [CS1, TS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has better kinetic potential because flux/metal-rich conditions can improve transport, and Zn5Rh8B4 plus Li2Rh3B2 show Rh borides crystallize from sealed metal-rich environments [CS2, SS2, SS3]. Its condition score is worse when Li flux is included because Li requires inert handling and vacuum distillation [SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether a Zn-rich flux stabilizes Zn2BRh3 or instead favors known Zn–Rh–B competitors such as Zn5Rh8B4/Rh7Zn5B3 [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is synthetically plausible because Rh-boride/intermetallic analogues are reported by arc melting and annealing [CS3, CS5, TS5]. It scores poorly on severity because arc melting is very high temperature and Zn retention is problematic by [Reasonable Inference], while closest Zn–Rh–B syntheses instead use sealed ampoules [CS1, TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether the desired chiral phase can survive melt processing rather than forming competing RhB or Zn–Rh–B phases [CS5, TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The main amorphization flag is mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which is explicitly present in the closest cubic chiral analogue Li2Pt3B and in the target topology [SS5, Target Data]. The unit-cell-size check is not triggered because the closest cubic analogue has 24 atoms per cell rather than >40 [SS5]. CS/SS/TS do not directly provide ionic radii, so C1 is treated by [Reasonable Inference] and source-specific ionic-radius evidence is neutral where absent.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zn2BRh3 is most likely a metastable chiral boride rather than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.161 eV/atom [Target Data]. The synthesis case is supported by very close Zn–Rh–B chemical/structural analogues and by thermodynamic analogues that are experimentally known at similar hull energies [CS1, SS1, TS1, TS5]. The strongest enabling factor is the existence of same-element Zn–Rh–B borides made from elements in sealed Ta/quartz ampoules [CS1, SS1]. The strongest limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry sensitivity, because the closest Zn–Rh–B/Zn–Ir–B sources report impurities, excess reactant needs, and narrow phase windows [CS1, TS1]. The recommended first attempt is therefore sealed-Ta direct reaction with controlled Zn excess and cooling-rate screening [CS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference].
