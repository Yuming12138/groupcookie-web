## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0100 eV atom⁻¹, which lies in the highest scoring bin specified in the rubric; this value comes from the target record, not from CS/SS/TS. The supplied analogue set also contains low-hull materials, including AgBi₂Se₃Cl at 0.0041 eV atom⁻¹ by SS/TS, BiSeI at 0.0128 eV atom⁻¹ by TS, and Bi₃Se₄Br at 0.0276 eV atom⁻¹ by SS/TS. These analogues support, but do not prove, favorable phase formation for BiAs₂Cl. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** for the target’s own exact hull value; the target-record value is therefore used directly.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because related sealed solid-state reactions occur at approximately 350–600 °C by CS and at 623 K for Bi₃Se₄Br by SS. The deductions arise from the required vacuum or sealed atmosphere, volatile BiCl₃/arsenic components, toxic handling, and likely annealing or homogenization steps. The exact volatility behavior of the Bi–As–Cl target is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so the deductions are conservative **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses temperatures below 1000 °C and ambient pressure, which avoids the severe penalties associated with very high temperature or high pressure. However, SS/TS show that the chemically related AgBi₂Se₃Cl synthesis requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and controlled slow cooling, while the target additionally contains toxic arsenic. The score therefore remains limited by containment and composition-control demands.

### F2 — Route 3

The CVT route avoids high external pressure and can operate below 1000 °C, as shown by the Bi₃Se₄Br CVT route in SS and the BiSeI transport route in TS. Nevertheless, transport requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and volatile chemical species; arsenic-containing vapor chemistry is an additional concern not directly represented in the supplied analogues. The multi-stage nature of source preparation, transport, and crystal recovery also supports the deductions.

### F3 — Route 1

Elemental precursors generally have lower activity than nitrate, acetate, or sol-gel precursors, so precursor activity was scored conservatively. The ion-diffusion score is relatively favorable because the relevant reaction temperatures are below the highest-cation-melting-point penalty range, and CS/SS provide successful lower-temperature sealed reactions. Structural reconstruction is only partial: Bi square-pyramidal motifs are supported by SS/TS, but the exact As–As and Bi–As network is not directly reproduced.

### F3 — Route 2

The melt route receives the highest F3 because melting or partial melting substantially accelerates mass transport. The AgBi₂Se₃Cl melt-growth precedent at 790 K from SS/TS is especially relevant because it combines bismuth, a chalcogen/pnictogen-like anion sublattice, and chlorine. The remaining uncertainty is whether BiAs₂Cl melts congruently or instead decomposes into Bi–As and Bi–Cl phases; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is available on this specific phase diagram.

### F3 — Route 3

The CVT route benefits from direct evidence that Bi–chalcogen–halide materials can crystallize in sealed temperature gradients: Bi₃Se₄Br by SS and BiSeI by TS. Its lower score reflects uncertainty in the relative volatility and transport rates of Bi, As, and Cl, which can produce nonstoichiometric deposits. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for a direct Bi–As–Cl transport-agent system, so the composition-control score is intentionally low.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains chemically dissimilar Bi and As sites, so a cation-size/bonding mismatch trigger is assigned as a **[Reasonable Inference]**. The unit-cell atom count cannot be determined from the supplied structural description, and there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** to activate C2. The described target connectivity is mainly corner-sharing rather than the simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing criterion, so C3 is not triggered.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiAs₂Cl is most plausibly a low-energy, potentially ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its target-record \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is only 0.0100 eV atom⁻¹; the supplied data do not independently establish experimental ground-state status. CS, SS, and TS provide meaningful support through Bi–halide square-pyramidal coordination, sealed-ampoule reactions, melt cooling, and chemical transport of related compounds. The most important enabling factor is the combination of low calculated hull energy and a chemically close Ag–Bi–Se–Cl melt-growth precedent from SS/TS. The principal limiting factor is simultaneous control of arsenic and chlorine volatility, for which direct Bi–As–Cl experimental evidence is absent. A sealed, slow-cooled melt reaction is therefore the best initial route, with direct solid-state synthesis as a lower-complexity backup.
