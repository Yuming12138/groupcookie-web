# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.080 eV/atom, which falls in the supplied moderate-stability interval of 0.05–0.20 eV/atom and supports an F1 score of 80. This target-specific value is not supplied by CS, SS, or TS; therefore, **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for the absolute target F1 value, and those sources are neutral for this factor. The analogue set is nevertheless compatible with synthesis of weakly metastable phases: TS-1 and TS-5 have E_hull values of 0.129 and 0.119 eV/atom, while CS-2, CS-3, and SS-3 are calculated at or near the ground state.

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives 65 because it requires vacuum and a sealed system, deductions supported by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-1. Iodine and sulfur volatility make containment important, and iodine-assisted transport adds handling risk; the hazard deduction is **[Reasonable Inference]** because explicit safety data are incomplete. The route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, which prevents additional deductions.

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the same nominal F2 score because it also uses evacuated sealed tubes and volatile iodine/sulfur. CS-2, SS-3, and TS-3 show that this type of apparatus is practical for related chalcogen-halides, but SS-3 reports difficulty obtaining phase-pure material. The proposed reaction temperature remains below 1000 °C, so the principal penalty arises from atmosphere control and volatile components rather than thermal severity.

## F2 — Route 3

Route 3 scores lower because it adds a precursor-preparation stage to the vacuum/volatile-component requirements. CS-2 and TS-2 support the use of preformed chalcogenide precursors, but the exact staged Zr–S–I process is not represented in the data; **insufficient evidence from SS** exists for this route. The multi-step penalty is therefore a conservative **[Reasonable Inference]**.

## F3 — Route 1

CVT receives the highest kinetics score because vapor-mediated transport can overcome slow bulk diffusion, as demonstrated by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-1. The structural reconstruction score is intermediate rather than high because the target has a chiral P3₁ framework and mixed S–S/S–I connectivity, whereas the closest analogues are mostly lower-symmetry Nb, W, or Te systems in CS-2/CS-3 and SS-3. The score remains uncertain because no direct ZrS₃I₂ growth experiment is supplied.

## F3 — Route 2

Direct solid-state kinetics are less favorable because dense Zr and refractory Zr–S bonding may limit diffusion at the proposed temperature; this is **[Reasonable Inference]** and is not directly quantified by CS/SS/TS. Related sealed-tube reactions do demonstrate that chalcogen-halide phases can form at moderate-to-high temperatures, including CS-2, CS-5, SS-3, and TS-3. However, SS-3 reports phase-pure synthesis difficulties and CS-4 describes a low-yield side product, supporting a lower structural-reconstruction score.

## F3 — Route 3

The preformed Zr–S precursor should increase precursor activity relative to elemental Zr, an extrapolation from CS-2 and TS-2 where preformed chalcogenides are used. The CVT second stage retains the transport advantage shown by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-1. Its main uncertainty is whether the Zr–S precursor will transform into the iodinated chiral phase or simply remain as ZrS₂/ZrS₃; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** directly resolves this competition.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

No cation-size-mismatch trigger is apparent because the target has only one cation species. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is available for the target unit-cell atom count and for simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so these checks are not penalized. The target’s mixed coordination and chalcogen–halogen contacts may complicate ordering, but they do not automatically satisfy the specified amorphization criteria.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a weakly metastable phase rather than a strongly ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.080 eV/atom, although this target-specific conclusion is not independently supplied by CS/SS/TS. The analogue evidence supports synthesis of mixed chalcogen–halide frameworks by CVT or sealed solid-state reaction: CS-2/CS-3, SS-3, and TS-1 provide particularly relevant metal–chalcogen–halide precedents. The most important enabling factor is iodine-assisted vapor transport, which can improve mass transfer and crystal growth as shown by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-1. The main limitation is phase selectivity, since SS-3 reports difficulty obtaining phase-pure Nb–Se–I material and CS-4 reports a related compound only as a low-yield side product. Overall, synthesis is plausible but should be approached as a condition-screening problem rather than a routine one-shot reaction.
