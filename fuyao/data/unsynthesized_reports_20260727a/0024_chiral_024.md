## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary of the supplied ≤0.05 eV/atom category. TS-2 provides a close thermodynamic comparison at 0.05077 eV/atom, while TS-1 and TS-3 show that chalcogen–halide materials with E_hull values around 0.06–0.08 eV/atom can still be considered plausible synthetic targets. The target’s reported dynamic-stability pass strengthens the case, but the absence of experimental confirmation limits confidence.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate-to-good F2 score because it avoids high pressure and extreme temperature, unlike TS-2, which requires 15–89 kbar and up to 1600 °C. However, sealed handling is required because SS-2, SS-4, and TS-1 demonstrate that chalcogen–halide systems are commonly processed in evacuated or inert sealed ampoules. ZnCl₂ moisture sensitivity and sulfur volatility justify additional deductions. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more operationally demanding because it adds a sealed solvent or reactive-melt environment, extended reaction times, and potentially autoclave operation. SS-2, SS-4, and TS-1 support the feasibility of such equipment and conditions, but they also show that these syntheses can require one to two weeks and careful pressure management. The optional CCl₄ solvent is particularly uncertain because it is not directly validated for ZnCl₂/S chemistry; this part of the route is therefore only a screening extrapolation. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F3 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route benefits from the relatively mobile, low-melting ZnCl₂ component and sulfur, giving favorable precursor activity and diffusion estimates. CS-3 demonstrates successful formation of a Cu–P–S framework from solid precursors at 623 K, and CS-5 demonstrates that Zn–S tetrahedral frameworks can be obtained by solid-state treatment. Nevertheless, the target requires substantial framework reconstruction and includes an unusual combination of Zn–Cl ribbons, Zn–S coordination, and S–S bonds, so structural-reconstruction kinetics remain the primary uncertainty.

### F3 — Route 2

The sulfur-rich route receives a higher F3 score because a liquid or solvothermal medium should improve diffusion and facilitate crystal growth. SS-4 and TS-1 show that chalcogen-rich metal–halide compounds can form in sealed, moderate-temperature media, while SS-2 demonstrates related solvothermal preparation with sealed ampoules and extended heating. The limitation is that none of these examples contains the exact ZnS₃Cl₂ composition or target topology; consequently, the score depends strongly on [Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is not obviously penalized by cation-size mismatch because only Zn is present, but complete unit-cell atom count and detailed polyhedral-sharing statistics are not provided. Therefore, there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** for C2 and a neutral treatment is used for those missing factors. CS-3 and CS-5 demonstrate that related sulfide frameworks can crystallize, whereas SS-3 indicates that chalcogen-containing framework synthesis may suffer from competing phases and narrow processing windows.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a **metastable or near-ground-state phase**, rather than an unequivocally stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and it has no experimental precedent in the supplied validation. CS/SS/TS analogues provide meaningful support for sealed-tube chalcogen–halide chemistry, Zn–S tetrahedral frameworks, and S–S-containing structures, but none directly reproduces ZnS₃Cl₂. The most important enabling factor is the favorable thermodynamic position combined with the high mobility of ZnCl₂ and sulfur under sealed conditions. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: sulfur/ZnCl₂ volatility and competing Zn–S, Zn–Cl, and polysulfide phases may prevent formation of the chiral framework. **[Reasonable Inference]**
