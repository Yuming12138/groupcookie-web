### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
GeSBr is not predicted as a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.170 eV/atom, but it remains within the common metastable synthesis window used for the 65–85 scoring band (Target data). TS is the strongest support because TS1–TS3 include experimentally synthesized analogues with E_hull values near 0.17–0.18 eV/atom, while CS/SS analogues are mostly more stable and therefore only indirectly supportive (CS1–CS5, SS2–SS5, TS1–TS3). Remaining uncertainty is high because exact GeSBr is absent from the provided experimental analogues, so exact-composition evidence from CS/SS/TS is neutral 50.

**Route 1 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
The solid-state ampoule route has moderate severity because Ge–S analogues use standard sealed silica/quartz ampoules at 500–600 °C, but vacuum, long anneals, and volatile bromide control add deductions (SS4, SS5, TS5). Kinetics are acceptable because Ge is the only cation and Ge–S tetrahedra are already present in GeS2-like feeds, but chain formation from GeS2/GeBr2 requires partial structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS4, SS5]. The main uncertainty is whether GeBr2 remains available without disproportionating or transporting away [Reasonable Inference from SS3, Target data].

**Route 2 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
CVT has the best kinetics because chemical and thermodynamic analogues demonstrate crystal growth by halogen transport, reducing the need for long solid-state diffusion (CS1, TS2). Its severity remains moderate because vacuum ampoules, iodine/bromide transport, and dry handling are required (CS1, SS3, TS2). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS1 and TS2 support CVT generally, but no exact GeSBr CVT analogue is provided, so exact-composition evidence from CS/SS/TS is neutral 50.

**Route 3 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
Flux growth is kinetically attractive because TS1 shows a thermodynamically similar sulfide crystallized from NaI–CsI flux, and flux should improve transport of soft Ge–S–Br species [Reasonable Inference from TS1]. The route is penalized for vacuum ampoule operation, flux separation, possible halide exchange, and difficult phase control reported in TS1/TS5. **Route 4 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
High pressure is credible only as a metastability-trapping route because SS2 demonstrates high-pressure GeS2 polymorph synthesis and CS3/CS4 demonstrate 60 kbar chalcogenide synthesis. Its F2 is low because pressure above 5 GPa, specialized hardware, sealed capsules, and dry volatile-bromide containment all apply (SS2, CS3, CS4; [Reasonable Inference from SS3]). **Route 5 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
The solution route is low-temperature but chemically uncertain because SS1 supports halide cluster crystallization rather than extended GeSBr chains, and SS3 supports low-temperature bromide ampoule crystallization but not solution growth of Ge–S–Br. Molecular precursors should be highly reactive, but that same reactivity may favor amorphous Ge–S or molecular bromogermanium sulfides rather than the target ribbon [Reasonable Inference from SS1, SS3]. **F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The formal F4 checks give zero clear triggers: the target has one cation, no reported >40-atom cell, and corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing tetrahedra (Target data; SS2, SS4, SS5). The score is kept at the low end of the zero-trigger range because SS2 explicitly uses GeS2 glass, so Ge–S chemistry can access amorphous states even when crystalline phases exist (SS2). Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so that source-specific check is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GeSBr is best interpreted as a metastable, dynamically stable target rather than a ground-state material because its E_hull is 0.170 eV/atom (Target data). The analogue support is meaningful: CS supports CVT/ampoule chemistry for related chalcogenides, SS supports Ge–S tetrahedral frameworks and bromide handling, and TS shows that materials at similar thermodynamic distance can be synthesized (CS1, SS2–SS5, TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is controlled halide chemical potential in a sealed CVT or self-transport ampoule, which can grow crystals while compensating bromide volatility (CS1, SS3, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition against GeS2 plus Ge bromide phases, because the target is metastable and exact GeSBr experimental precedent is absent from CS/SS/TS (Target data; SS2, TS5). Overall, the best route is plausible but will likely require small gradient, Br activity, and feed-stoichiometry screens rather than a single deterministic recipe [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS3, TS2, TS5].
