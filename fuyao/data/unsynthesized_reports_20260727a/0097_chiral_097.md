### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, which falls in the highest rubric band and indicates near-hull metastability rather than a confirmed ground state. [Target data] TS analogues cluster near similar energies, including ~0.043–0.071 eV/atom Pt–Si phases and RhGe at ~0.066 eV/atom, so TS provides useful support rather than neutral evidence. [TS1–TS5] CS and SS analogues include several stable or nearly stable intermetallic borides/germanides, supporting a high F1 but not proving the target is already experimentally known. [CS1–CS5; SS2; SS3]

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Arc Melting + Annealing.** F2 is moderate because arc melting plus sealed Ar/vacuum annealing requires controlled atmosphere and multiple operations, but no high pressure or toxic target precursors are required. [CS1; SS2; SS3; TS1–TS4] F3 is the best among bulk routes because the liquid/arc step mitigates slow Pt/B/Ge diffusion, and SS2/SS3 demonstrate Pt–Ge phases can be homogenized by arc melting plus week-long annealing. [SS2; SS3; Reasonable Inference] Remaining uncertainty is whether Pt₃BGe₂ forms instead of nearby Pt–Ge or Pt–B binaries/ternaries. [CS1; SS2; SS3; Reasonable Inference]

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Direct Solid-State Sintering.** F2 matches Route 1 because the proposed conditions still exceed 1000 °C, use sealed/inert atmosphere, and require multiple grinding/annealing steps. [CS2; CS3; SS5] F3 is lower because elemental Pt and B are low-activity dense/refractory precursors, and CS2/CS3 show high-temperature direct reactions are feasible but not kinetically effortless. [CS2; CS3; CS5] TS gives insufficient evidence for this route and is treated as neutral 50. [TS1–TS5]

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Flux Growth.** F2 is moderate because CS4 uses sealed high-vacuum slow cooling near 1050 °C and requires flux removal or decanting. [CS4] F3 is moderate because flux improves transport, but the exact Pt–Ge–B target has no direct flux analogue and CS3 suggests off-stoichiometry may be needed for crystal growth. [CS3; CS4; Reasonable Inference] TS gives insufficient evidence for flux growth and is treated as neutral 50. [TS1–TS5]

**Route 4 F2/F3 — Thin Film.** F2 is high because SS1 uses only 200–750 °C annealing under vacuum and avoids high pressure. [SS1] F3 is low because the target requires ternary Pt/Ge/B composition control and there is no direct CS or TS thin-film analogue, so CS and TS are neutral 50. [SS1; CS1–CS5; TS1–TS5; Reasonable Inference] The main uncertainty is whether the bulk Pnma phase can crystallize from a thin-film diffusion couple rather than forming competing Pt–Ge or Pt–B phases. [SS1; Reasonable Inference]

**Route 5 F2/F3 — HPHT.** F2 is penalized by >5 GPa pressure and multistep HPHT processing, directly based on TS5. [TS5] F3 is moderate-low because high pressure/temperature can access metastable germanides, but short reaction times and unverified Pt–B–Ge chemistry limit confidence. [TS5; Reasonable Inference] CS and SS give insufficient evidence for HPHT synthesis and are treated as neutral 50. [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has a compact ordered intermetallic/boride description and only edge-sharing BPt₅ units, so it does not trigger the mixed-connectivity amorphization check. [Target data] CS and SS show some analogues with complex mixed sharing, but that complexity is not reported for the target, so it is not counted as a target trigger. [CS1–CS3; SS1] TS gives insufficient structural amorphization evidence and is treated as neutral 50. [TS1–TS5]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt₃BGe₂ is most likely a **near-hull metastable phase**, not a confirmed ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom rather than 0.000 eV/atom. [Target data] The analogue support is fairly strong for bulk synthesis because CS1 supports Pt–B arc-melt chemistry, while SS2/SS3 support Pt–Ge arc-melt plus anneal chemistry. [CS1; SS2; SS3] TS1–TS4 further support that near-hull Pt-containing intermetallics can be accessed by arc melting and sealed-tube heat treatment. [TS1–TS4] The most critical enabling factor is melt homogenization followed by long annealing, which addresses slow diffusion and ordering. [CS1; SS2; SS3] The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Pt–Ge, Pt–B, and ternary products because several analogues report phase-window or impurity-control challenges. [CS3; SS1; SS2; TS1–TS4]
