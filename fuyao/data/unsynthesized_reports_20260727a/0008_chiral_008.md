## SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target is a chiral, one-dimensional In–S–Br ribbon with tetrahedral In coordination and S–S connectivity. The closest structural evidence is the S–Br molecular solid in SS/TS, while the closest topology/coordination evidence is the one-dimensional indium selenide ribbon in CS. The mixed chalcogen–halide solvothermal compounds in SS/TS support sealed, moisture-free synthesis but are mostly zero-dimensional, so topology transfer remains uncertain. **[Reasonable Inference]**

## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a reported DFT energy above hull of 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ and passed the stated no-imaginary-frequency screening, giving a maximum thermodynamic score. **[Target validation]** TS contains stable zero-hull SBr and SCl analogues, which supports the broader stability of sulfur–halogen bonding, but these analogues do not validate the exact In(SBr)₃ phase or its chirality. **[TS #1–#2]** The principal uncertainty is that the target remains theory-only and its experimental decomposition pathway is unknown. **[Target validation]**

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because the temperature is only approximately 150–180 °C, consistent with the 140–200 °C mixed chalcogen–halide syntheses in SS/TS. **[SS #2; TS #3]** The main penalties arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed handling of volatile bromine, multiple operations, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. **[SS/TS]** There is insufficient evidence from CS for bromine-specific precursor hazards, so the hazard deduction is based on the supplied bromine chemistry and the sealed bromide syntheses in SS/TS. **[CS]**

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 scores higher because SS #5 reports a controlled temperature-gradient ampoule synthesis yielding a bromide/chalcogenide crystal quantitatively. **[SS #5]** The route still requires sealed handling and careful precursor preparation, particularly if Br₂ or moisture-sensitive InBr₃ is used. **[SS/TS]** There is insufficient evidence from CS for CVT specifically, so CS is neutral for this route’s F2 assessment. **[CS]**

### F2 — Route 3

The direct solid-state route is penalized for the same bromine-related containment and atmosphere requirements as Route 1. **[SS/TS]** CS #4 supports elemental solid-state synthesis but reports long reaction times and phase-homogeneity problems in related chalcogenide frameworks, so a long anneal or repeat cycle may be necessary. **[CS #4]** The exact low-temperature reaction window for In–S–Br is not established. **[CS/SS/TS]**

### F3 — Route 1

CS #1 supports indium chalcogenide ribbon formation and demonstrates solvothermal conditions that can overcome ordinary solid-state diffusion limitations. **[CS #1]** SS #2 and TS #3 demonstrate that sealed mixed chalcogen–halide systems can crystallize at moderate temperatures, supporting the high diffusion component. **[SS #2; TS #3]** The structural-reconstruction score is lower because the SBr analogue is zero-dimensional while the target is a one-dimensional chiral ribbon. **[SS/TS #1; target structure]**

### F3 — Route 2

CVT receives the highest F3 score because volatile halide-containing precursors can provide a transport pathway and the target contains a potentially mobile bromine component. **[SS #5; Reasonable Inference]** The temperature-gradient method is known to produce ordered bromide/chalcogenide crystals, but SS #5 concerns Al–Se–Br rather than In–S–Br. **[SS #5]** There is insufficient evidence from TS for direct CVT kinetics of the target, so the CVT advantage is an extrapolation rather than a demonstrated target-specific result. **[TS]**

### F3 — Route 3

Elemental powders are chemically accessible, and CS #4 demonstrates that extended chalcogenide structures can be produced by powder heating. **[CS #4]** Nevertheless, the target’s S–Br bonds and one-dimensional chiral ordering require substantial reconstruction from the starting mixture, while bromine retention may be difficult. **[SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]** The absence of direct In–S–Br solid-state precedent limits confidence in the kinetics. **[CS/SS/TS]**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains one cation species and a small apparent structural unit, so the cation-size and unit-cell-size triggers are not clearly activated. **[Target structure]** The supplied connectivity description does not report the simultaneous coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, so C3 is also not triggered. **[Target structure]** CS/SS/TS provide no direct amorphization data for this target; therefore, **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**, and the high score should be treated as a structural-risk estimate rather than an experimental prediction. **[CS/SS/TS]**
