## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s supplied energy above hull is 0.030 eV atom⁻¹, placing it in the highest stability band specified by the rubric [target validation]. TS analogues independently cluster near the hull, including CuBiSCl₂ at 0.037 eV atom⁻¹, Bi₃(TeCl₅)₂ at 0.0285 eV atom⁻¹, WTeCl₉ at 0.0227 eV atom⁻¹, and Mo(SeCl₆)₂ at 0.0339 eV atom⁻¹ [TS]. The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull DFT stability does not establish the target’s experimental kinetic accessibility or resistance to decomposition during heating [TS/Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because the temperatures are not extreme, but dry vacuum or Ar handling, volatile InCl₃/sulfur, and multistep pelletization/sealing are required [SS/TS]. SS1/SS3 and TS1 confirm that evacuated ampoules and controlled cooling are practical for related mixed chalcogen/halide reactions [SS/TS]. The main uncertainty is whether InCl₃ volatility will produce a sufficiently homogeneous reaction rather than an In-rich or sulfur-deficient assemblage [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 1

The elemental and halide precursors should be reasonably reactive after fine grinding, but solid-state formation still requires local diffusion and structural reconstruction [CS/SS]. The target’s mixture of InSCl₄ square pyramids, InS₂Cl₄ octahedra, and S–S connectivity is more complex than the simple starting materials [target structure; SS/TS]. Long annealing and slow cooling are therefore justified by the multiday schedules used for SS1/SS3 and TS1 analogues [SS/TS].

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because CVT requires dry vacuum or inert handling, volatile precursors, sealed ampoules, and several days of controlled growth [SS/TS]. InBr₃ sublimation and TS2–TS4 show that the equipment and temperature-gradient strategy are experimentally established for indium-halide and chalcogen-halide systems [SS/TS]. The uncertainty is the absence of a direct In–S–Cl transport precedent [SS/TS].

### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport is kinetically favorable when the feed contains volatile halides, as shown for InBr₃ and the TS CVT compounds [SS/TS]. However, composition control is penalized because sulfur and chloride may have different transport rates, and TS2–TS4 show that related CVT reactions can exhibit by-products or condition-dependent phases [TS]. Thus, Route 2 is better suited to single-crystal growth after phase formation has been established than to initial bulk-phase discovery [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3

The solvothermal route uses moderate temperatures similar to CS1 and avoids high-temperature solid-state diffusion [CS]. It nevertheless requires sealed autoclave operation, moisture control, and management of volatile or hydrolyzable indium chloride species [CS/Reasonable Inference]. CS2–CS5 establish that mild chloride crystallization is feasible, but they do not directly validate this sulfur-containing target [CS].

### F3 — Route 3

CS1 provides the strongest kinetic support for Route 3 because indium and a chalcogen powder were converted solvothermally at 185–200 °C over several days [CS]. Solution transport should improve precursor mixing and ion mobility relative to a dry powder reaction [CS/Reasonable Inference]. However, CS2–CS5 predominantly form discrete chloride or coordination compounds, so the probability of obtaining the target’s extended mixed-connectivity framework is uncertain [CS].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one principal cation, so the cation-size-mismatch criterion is not clearly activated [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The supplied description does not indicate a unit cell exceeding 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered on the available evidence [target structure]. C3 is triggered because both corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra coexist, although SS1, SS3, and TS1 show that related mixed-connectivity frameworks can crystallize rather than necessarily amorphize [SS/TS].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best regarded as a **near-hull, potentially metastable or weakly stable phase**, because its supplied energy above hull is only 0.030 eV atom⁻¹ but the provided validation is theoretical rather than experimental [target validation; TS]. SS and TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of ordered mixed sulfur/chloride frameworks produced in sealed ampoules, while CS1 supports moderate-temperature solvothermal indium–chalcogen chemistry [CS/SS/TS]. The most important enabling factor is the target’s favorable near-hull thermodynamics combined with the demonstrated crystallization of related mixed chalcogen/halide networks [TS/SS]. The main limiting factor is composition and structural control: volatile chloride/sulfur species and the unusual S–S-linked mixed-connectivity framework may favor competing phases [SS/TS/Reasonable Inference]. Route 1 should therefore be attempted first for phase discovery, with Route 3 as a complementary low-temperature screen and Route 2 reserved for crystal growth [SS/TS/CS/Reasonable Inference].
