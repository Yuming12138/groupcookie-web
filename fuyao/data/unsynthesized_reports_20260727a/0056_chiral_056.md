### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.120 eV/atom places it in the metastable but commonly synthetically accessible 0.05–0.20 eV/atom band [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues at similar E_hull values, especially FeBi₄S₇ at 0.1188 eV/atom and MoTe₄Br at 0.1072 eV/atom, show that comparable metastability can be experimentally realized [TS1, TS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and OQMD absence is not exhaustive experimental novelty proof [Target].

**F2 — Route 1 CVT**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, matching BiSeI and Sn₂SbS₂I₃ CVT conditions below 600 °C [CS1, SS3, TS2]. The deductions come from the evacuated sealed ampoule, volatile iodine, likely multistep prereaction/growth workflow, and iodide handling risk [CS1, SS3, SS4, TS5]. The condition severity is moderate rather than easy because iodine activity must be high enough for transport but not so high that iodine-rich phases dominate [SS3, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 CVT**  
CVT receives the best kinetics score because iodine transport bypasses long-range solid diffusion and directly matches the top BiSeI analogue [CS1, SS1, SS3]. Structural reconstruction is only partial because Bi–chalcogen–halide motifs transfer well, but S–S chains and chiral P2₁ ordering add complexity [Target, CS1, SS1, Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is the absence of a known Bi–S–I experimental analogue in the supplied data [CS/SS/TS overall].

**F2 — Route 2 Solid-State**  
Route 2 uses standard sealed-tube conditions similar to SiPb₂S₄ and FeBi₄S₇, so it avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature [CS4, TS1]. It still incurs deductions for vacuum sealing, volatile S/I control, possible regrinding/reannealing, and iodide handling [CS4, TS1, TS5]. The route is operationally simple but less selective than CVT [TS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 Solid-State**  
Solid-state kinetics are adequate because Bi and iodide components can melt or transport at moderate temperature, but dense Bi₂S₃-like phases may react slowly [CS4, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction into a chiral Bi–S–I framework with S–S chains is more demanding than forming conventional ternary sulfides [Target, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Repeated grinding and annealing may be required, as reported for related solid-state products [TS1, TS5].

**F2 — Route 3 Melt / Bridgman / Self-Flux**  
Route 3 uses moderate temperatures consistent with AgBi₂Se₃Cl melt cooling and TlFeI₃/TlMnI₃ iodide growth [SS2, TS5]. It receives the same main deductions as sealed routes because volatile iodide chemistry, controlled atmosphere, multistep growth/annealing, and dry handling remain necessary [SS2, TS5]. CS5 mentions Bridgman growth but gives insufficient condition details from CS5, so that condition-specific signal is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3 Melt / Bridgman / Self-Flux**  
Melt or flux growth improves kinetics by enabling liquid-mediated diffusion and slow crystallization [SS2, TS5]. The composition window may be narrow because BiI₃-rich flux can favor iodine-rich or binary side phases, while sulfur activity must preserve S–S bonding [TS5, TS4, Target, Reasonable Inference]. This route is promising for crystals but less chemically targeted than CVT [SS2, TS5, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 High Pressure**  
Route 4 is penalized strongly because analogous high-pressure syntheses use ~5.5 GPa to 60–89 kbar and specialized presses [SS5, TS4]. The route also requires closed capsules and multistep recovery, and iodide volatility remains a risk [SS5, TS4, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. It avoids the highest-temperature deductions only because the proposed Bi–S–I screen would deliberately stay below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference from SS5, TS4].

**F3 — Route 4 High Pressure**  
High pressure can overcome diffusion and stabilize S–S or metastable chalcogenide phases, as shown by BiSe₂ and ZnS₂ analogues [SS5, TS4]. The structural reconstruction score is low because high-pressure quenching often creates phases far from the starting topology and recovery can be difficult [SS5, TS4, CS3]. No direct Bi–S–I high-pressure precedent is provided, so transferability is uncertain [SS5, TS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has one cation type, an inferred unit cell below 40 atoms, and no explicit face-sharing coexistence, so none of the three amorphization triggers is activated [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The closest analogues are crystalline single crystals or phase-controlled powders rather than amorphous products, indirectly supporting low amorphization tendency [CS1, SS1, SS2, SS3, TS1]. Direct amorphization data are not provided, so measured amorphization evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50 as a direct-source signal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Bi(SI)₂ is best interpreted as a metastable but plausible phase rather than a likely ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.120 eV/atom while phonons are reported dynamically stable [Target, TS1–TS5]. The analogue support is strongest for CVT because BiSeI is both the top chemical and structural analogue and was made as single crystals by chemical transport [CS1, SS1]. The enabling factor is volatile halide-assisted mass transport, which can form low-dimensional chalcogenide-halide crystals at moderate temperature [CS1, SS3, SS4, TS3]. The limiting factor is maintaining the Bi:S:I ratio and S–S bonding while avoiding stable Bi–S, Bi–I, or iodine-rich competing phases [Target, TS4, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is experimentally worth attempting first by iodine-assisted CVT, with melt/self-flux as the next-best crystal-growth route [CS1, SS1, SS2, SS3, TS5].
