### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is 0.140 eV/atom, which places it in the metastable but accessible 65–85 rubric band (Target record). TS gives the strongest support because several highly thermodynamically similar materials are synthesized despite E_hull values of ~0.129–0.183 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). CS is mixed because CS1/CS3 are ground-state analogues but CS5 has very high E_hull, so CS is only weakly supportive rather than decisive.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Direct sealed-tube synthesis has moderate severity because CS1/CS3/SS4/SS5/TS1 use standard sealed tubes or ampoules below 1000 °C, but vacuum/inert handling and volatile S/F control are required (CS1, CS3, SS4, SS5, TS1; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics are only moderate because the target requires forming both mixed Ga–S/F coordination and S–S bonding, while analogous sulfides report long holds or phase-control issues (SS1, SS4, TS1; Target record).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth has greater procedural severity than direct heating because it likely requires prereaction, flux growth, and flux removal, as in CS3 and TS1. Its kinetics are better because halide fluxes explicitly enable single-crystal growth in CS3 and TS1, and flux transport should reduce diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference from CS3/TS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT is supported by TS2 and TS5, which show iodine-assisted transport of Ga sulfide/chalcogenide single crystals in evacuated ampoules. Its main kinetic weakness is composition control, because no CS/SS/TS source confirms that Ga–S–F stoichiometry survives iodine transport without F loss or I/F exchange; CS and SS are insufficient for this route and are neutral = 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly relevant to the S–S motif because ZnS₂ has S–S = 2.06 Å and is made at 60–89 kbar, close to the target’s S–S = 2.04 Å (SS2/TS4; Target record). The route is penalized heavily for severity because it requires >5 GPa pressure, high temperature, specialized equipment, and quench recovery (SS2/TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 5.**  
Solution chemistry has low thermal severity and high intrinsic molecular mobility, as CS4/CS5 show low-temperature crystallization routes. It scores poorly on structural reconstruction because CS4/CS5 are molecular or zero-dimensional analogues, and SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for solution synthesis of an extended GaS₂F framework, so SS and TS are neutral = 50 for this route.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk (Target record). The target cell is likely not very large and no vertex+edge+face coexistence is established from the supplied structure, while SS/TS only show some corner/edge-sharing analogues rather than direct target connectivity (Target record; SS1, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is the exact unit-cell atom count and polyhedral-sharing topology, which are not fully specified in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaS₂F is most likely a **metastable** rather than ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.140 eV/atom, although the target passed the supplied dynamic-stability screening (Target record). The best support comes from TS, where thermodynamically similar chalcogenides with comparable metastability are experimentally synthesized by flux, CVT, or high-pressure methods (TS1–TS5). CS supports the use of sealed halide-rich environments, especially excess metal halide flux for crystal growth, but it does not directly prove Ga–S–F formation (CS1, CS3). The most critical enabling factor is a sealed flux environment that maintains sulfur and fluoride activities while enabling crystal growth (CS3, TS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control of volatile/reactive S and F in a novel metastable mixed-anion phase with no direct experimental analogue in the provided data (Target record; [Reasonable Inference]).
