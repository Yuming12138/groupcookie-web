# SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target has a fully stable calculated thermodynamic position, with \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom, but it remains theory-only and has no reported experimental procedure [Target/source-paper data]. The closest structural analogue is CuBrSe\(_3\), which contains bent halogen bridges, chalcogen–chalcogen bonds, and corner-sharing metal–chalcogen–halogen polyhedra and is made by CVT [SS: mp-28741]. A second relevant analogue is Pd(Se\(_3\)Br)\(_2\), which contains Se–Se bonding and metal–halide/chalcogen coordination, although its metal coordination and dimensionality differ [SS: mp-568146]. The chemical analogues support sulfide/halide solid-state, CVT, and flux approaches, but none directly demonstrates synthesis of CoS\(_2\)Br [CS/SS/TS].

# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom and passed the reported dynamic-stability screening [Target/source-paper data]. This places it in the highest stability category under the supplied rubric. The CS/SS/TS records provide supporting precedent because several chemically or structurally related chalcogenide-halide materials also have zero or near-zero hull energies, but they do not replace the target-specific calculation [CS/SS/TS].

## F2 — Route 1: CVT

CVT requires a controlled vacuum and a sealed vessel, as demonstrated for CuBrSe\(_3\) and Fe\(_2\)Ga\(_2\)S\(_5\) [SS: mp-28741; CS: mp-608823]. Sulfur, iodine, and potentially bromine-bearing species introduce volatility and handling concerns [CS: mp-608823; Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids high pressure and extreme temperatures, so the principal penalties arise from atmosphere control, volatile components, and precursor sensitivity [SS: mp-28741; Reasonable Inference].

## F2 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Reaction

Sealed-ampoule solid-state reactions are supported by CuSe\(_2\) and CuPS\(_3\), which use moderate temperatures and vacuum or inert conditions [TS: mp-2000; CS: mp-1105187]. CoBr\(_2\) and sulfur are easier to weigh than elemental bromine, but CoBr\(_2\) is moisture-sensitive and sulfur can volatilize [Reasonable Inference]. The route therefore receives the same nominal condition score as CVT, but its practical phase-control burden is higher because it lacks the vapor-phase separation and crystal-growth advantage of CVT [Reasonable Inference].

## F2 — Route 3: Halide Flux

Flux routes in the supplied data use sealed ampoules, inert or vacuum conditions, multistage heating, and controlled cooling [CS: mp-1079795; SS: mp-30151; TS: mp-30151]. The flux improves transport but adds a separation step and introduces possible alkali-halide contamination [CS: mp-1079795; Reasonable Inference]. Consequently, the route receives a larger multi-step deduction despite its favorable kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 1: CVT

The CVT analogues demonstrate that chalcogenide and halide components can be transported and crystallized under temperature gradients [SS: mp-28741; CS: mp-608823]. This directly improves the estimated diffusion score relative to ordinary solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction remains only moderate because the target has CoS\(_4\)Br\(_2\) octahedra and P6\(_5\)22 chirality, whereas the closest structural analogue has CuSe\(_2\)Br\(_2\) tetrahedra and orthorhombic symmetry [Target data; SS: mp-28741].

## F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Reaction

Elemental Cu–Se and binary Cu–P–S reactions demonstrate that sealed solid-state processing can generate chalcogenide frameworks [TS: mp-2000; CS: mp-1105187]. However, direct solid–solid diffusion is slower than flux or vapor transport, particularly when dense metal particles are used [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s mixed corner-/edge-sharing octahedral framework and S–S bonding require substantial structural organization, reducing the structural-reconstruction score [Target data].

## F3 — Route 3: Halide Flux

The NaI–CsI eutectic example and the K\(_2\)Se flux synthesis show that molten salts can improve crystal growth and support controlled cooling of chalcogenide materials [CS: mp-1079795; SS: mp-30151; TS: mp-30151]. The liquid-mediated mechanism justifies the highest diffusion score among the proposed routes [Reasonable Inference]. The score is not higher because the target’s phase field, alkali-halide compatibility, and chiral selection are unknown [Target/source-paper data; CS/SS/TS].

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one specified cation and therefore does not present a cation-size mismatch between multiple crystallographic cation species [Target data]. The supplied summaries do not provide the target unit-cell atom count, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2 and that component is treated as neutral [CS/SS/TS]. The target description reports corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but not face-sharing octahedra, so C3 is not triggered [Target data]. The resulting F4 estimate is favorable but provisional [Reasonable Inference].

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is more likely to be a ground-state phase than a metastable phase because its calculated \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is exactly 0 eV/atom and it passed the reported dynamic-stability screening [Target/source-paper data]. The CS/SS/TS analogues provide meaningful support for chalcogenide–halide synthesis, particularly vacuum CVT of CuBrSe\(_3\) and iodine-assisted CVT of Fe\(_2\)Ga\(_2\)S\(_5\) [SS: mp-28741; CS: mp-608823]. The most important enabling factor is the thermodynamic stability combined with the ability of CVT to separate transport and crystallization zones [Target/source-paper data; SS: mp-28741]. The most important limiting factor is the absence of direct CoS\(_2\)Br experimental precedent and the need to select the correct bromine chemical potential while obtaining the chiral P6\(_5\)22 ordering [Target/source-paper data; CS/SS/TS]. Overall, CVT is the most credible first experiment, with flux growth as a useful second-line strategy [Reasonable Inference].
