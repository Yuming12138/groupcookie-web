**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.130 eV/atom places it in the 65–85 range, so 75/100 is appropriate for a metastable but potentially isolable phase (Target). TS is the strongest support because several thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are reported as synthesized, while CS and SS show related chemistries and structures at or near the hull (CS; SS; TS). The main uncertainty is that OQMD absence and local formula novelty do not prove absence from all experimental databases, and the target is theory-only in the source paper (Target).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/Ar sealed-tube control, volatile Se/Br chemistry, and toxic/air-sensitive handling, matching sealed bromide and selenide analogues (CS1; TS3; Target). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure in the proposed screen, so it remains moderate rather than severe [Reasonable Inference; CS1; TS3].

**F3 — Route 1**

The limiting kinetic factor is reconstruction into the target edge-sharing Sn–Se/Br octahedral network, because analogues often require annealing, repeated processing, or controlled stoichiometry (CS1; TS2; TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SnSeBr is most likely a metastable phase rather than a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.130 eV/atom, although it passed dynamic-stability screening (Target). The strongest synthesis support comes from sealed-tube CS/TS analogues and Sn–chalcogen/halide SS analogues, which show that related chemistries can crystallize under vacuum/Ar ampoule conditions (CS1; SS1; SS3; TS3). The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive binary halide/chalcogenide pathways, especially a proposed SnSe₂ + SnBr₂ route [Reasonable Inference; CS1; SS4; TS3]. The main limiting factor is maintaining Br/Se chemical potentials while avoiding competing Sn–Se, Sn–Br, or multiphase products, because analogues show stoichiometry sensitivity, repeated annealing needs, and multiphase outcomes (CS1; CS2; TS4; TS5). Overall, Route 1 is the most practical first attempt because it balances transferability and manageable severity better than CVT, melt growth, or hydrothermal screening (CS1; SS4; TS3).
