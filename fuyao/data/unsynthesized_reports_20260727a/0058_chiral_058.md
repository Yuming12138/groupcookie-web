### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.120 eV/atom places it in the metastable-but-plausible 65–85 band `[Target]`. The closest Ta–S–halide analogue Ta4S9Br8 is synthesized despite E_hull ≈ 0.132 eV/atom, which strongly supports a score near the upper-middle of that band `[CS3/SS3/TS1]`. SS analogues include hull-stable Nb chalcogen chlorides, further supporting feasibility `[SS1/SS2/SS4]`. The main uncertainty is that CS contains high-E_hull molecular compounds that are synthesized, but those are less transferable to extended TaS2Cl3 because they are nitrido or complex-salt systems `[CS2/CS5]`.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, matching Ta4S9Br8 and Nb(SBr)2 precedents below 500 °C `[CS3/SS5]`. The deductions come from vacuum/sealed operation, volatile halogen chemistry, and toxic chlorinating reagents `[CS3/SS5; Reasonable Inference]`. The condition severity is therefore moderate rather than extreme `[CS3/SS5]`.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The main kinetic penalty is the use of elemental Ta, which is refractory and slow to react `[Reasonable Inference]`. Halogen-assisted sealed ampoule chemistry partially offsets this penalty because Ta4S9Br8 crystallizes at 400 °C from elemental Ta/S/Br2 `[CS3/SS3/TS1]`. Structural reconstruction remains substantial because the target 1D ribbon must form from elements `[Target; Reasonable Inference]`.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 uses the same general temperature and sealed-tube environment as Nb3Se5Cl7 and NbSeCl3, so it receives no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty `[SS1/SS2]`. The score is reduced for vacuum sealing, volatile metal chlorides, and air-sensitive chlorides `[SS1/SS2/CS2]`. TS gives indirect support through Ta4S9Br8 but no unique chloride-exchange condition, so TS is not used to increase Route 2 F2 beyond the SS/CS basis `[TS1]`.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Preformed TaS2 should reduce reconstruction relative to elemental Ta because NbSe2 + NbCl4 directly gives a 1D chalcogen chloride analogue `[SS1; Reasonable Inference]`. Kinetics remain only moderate because long holds of one to three weeks are reported for related Nb–Se–Cl phases `[SS1/SS2]`. This balance makes Route 2 the best compromise between transferability and manageable reaction severity `[SS1/SS2/CS3]`.

**F2 — Route 3**  
CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile transport agents, and often feed preparation plus crystal-growth separation `[SS4/TS5]`. These features make the route more operationally severe than Route 2 despite good crystal-growth precedent `[SS4]`. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact TaS2Cl3 CVT exists beyond the natural-gradient Ta–S–Br analogue, so CS is only indirect for Route 3 `[CS3]`.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Vapor transport improves precursor activity by forming mobile halide species `[SS4/TS5; Reasonable Inference]`. The kinetic uncertainty is phase-selective crystallization rather than simple diffusion, because SS4 reports narrow purity windows and contamination issues in chalcogen-halide CVT `[SS4]`. Route 3 is therefore attractive for single crystals but slightly less robust as a first synthesis attempt `[SS4/TS5]`.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Solution crystallization has mild temperature and pressure requirements, as shown by CH2Cl2 reactions at room temperature `[CS1/TS4]`. The score is reduced for dry inert handling and hazardous volatile chlorosulfur reagents `[CS2; Reasonable Inference]`. Insufficient evidence from SS supports this route class, so SS is neutral 50 for Route 4 condition evidence.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Solution mobility is favorable, but the target extended Ta–S–Cl ribbon is not demonstrated by the CS/TS solution analogues `[CS1/TS4/Target]`. The closest solution precedents form W–N–Cl ribbons or Mo–N–S–Cl clusters, so the structural reconstruction/selectivity score is low `[CS1/TS4]`. This route is exploratory rather than recommended as the primary bulk synthesis route `[Reasonable Inference]`.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a single cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger C1 `[Target]`. The likely unit cell is below 40 atoms based on the reported inequivalent sites and P3₁ symmetry, but CS/SS/TS do not directly validate the target atom count, so they are neutral 50 for C2 `[Target; Reasonable Inference]`. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaS2Cl3 is most likely a metastable but synthetically accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.120 eV/atom rather than zero, while the target also passes dynamic-stability screening `[Target]`. The strongest support comes from Ta4S9Br8, which is a synthesized Ta–S–halide analogue with very similar thermodynamic stability and bonding motifs `[CS3/SS3/TS1]`. The structurally similar Nb chalcogen chlorides show that 1D chalcogen-halide ribbons can be made in sealed tubes or transport setups `[SS1/SS2/SS4]`. The key enabling factor is volatile halide transport/chloride exchange, which permits crystallization far below the refractory Ta diffusion scale `[CS3/SS1/SS4; Reasonable Inference]`. The key limiting factor is phase control under volatile sulfur/chlorine chemical potentials, because related systems report byproducts, poor reproducibility, and inhomogeneous products `[CS3/SS1/SS2/SS4]`.
