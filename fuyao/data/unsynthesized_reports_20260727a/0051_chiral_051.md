### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull = 0.100 eV/atom falls in the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom band, so the assigned 78/100 reflects moderate metastability rather than ground-state certainty (Target data). CS analogues are strong chemical positives because ErSeI, LaSeCl, HoSI, and DySI are reported synthesized and several are at E_hull = 0 (CS1–CS5). TS analogues show that synthesized halide/chalcogenide-related phases can occur around E_hull ≈ 0.11–0.15 eV/atom, which supports accessibility of a 0.100 eV/atom phase (TS1, TS3, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and MP-referenced hull stability is not an exhaustive experimental phase-diagram proof (Target data).

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids >1000 °C and high pressure but still requires sealed vacuum/inert handling and volatile Se/I control (CS2, CS3, CS5, TS5). Its kinetics are acceptable because Ca-based diffusion should be easier than refractory cation systems [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s trigonal P3₂ Se–Se-containing framework is not identical to the CS orthorhombic LnSeX/LnSX analogues (Target data; CS1–CS5). The main uncertainty is whether CaSe + CaI₂ + Se equilibrates into CaSeI rather than stable binaries [Reasonable Inference; phase competition issues appear in CS2 and TS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is penalized for I₂/Se volatility, Ca reactivity, and staged heating, even though direct element routes work for HoSI/DySI analogues (CS3, CS5). Kinetics are relatively favorable because elemental reactants are active, but direct reactions also increase pressure and side-reaction risk [Reasonable Inference from CS5/TS3]. The largest uncertainty is safe control of the Ca–I₂ and Ca–Se reaction front [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has the best total because flux or sealed melt conditions should improve mass transport and crystallization while keeping temperatures below ~800 °C [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS2, SS5]. SS5 specifically supports slow-cooled iodide melt crystallization under dry Ar, and CS1 supports halide flux/transport chemistry for a chemically similar ErSeI system. The limiting uncertainty is composition control, because no CS/SS/TS source gives an exact CaSeI flux window.

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is plausible because CS1 directly demonstrates CVT for ErSeI-type chemistry, but it is less certain for Ca because Ca–Se–I vapor speciation is not provided by CS/SS/TS. Its condition severity is similar to other sealed routes, but its kinetics/composition score is lower because vapor transport can fractionate Se and I [Reasonable Inference; CS1 reports phase-control difficulty across halide variants]. SS/TS provide only indirect support for iodide crystal-growth handling, not a direct CaSeI CVT route, so that evidence is weak-to-neutral for transport chemistry (SS1, TS1).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation type, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent (Target data; [Reasonable Inference]). The described P3₂ cell likely contains fewer than 40 atoms, and the target explicitly reports corner/edge sharing but not face sharing, so no F4 triggers are activated (Target data; [Reasonable Inference]). CS/SS/TS are insufficient direct evidence for the target’s F4 checks and are treated as neutral 50 for source-specific F4 signal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaSeI is more likely a metastable, dynamically stable phase than a ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.100 eV/atom but it passed the no-imaginary-frequency screening (Target data). The CS set gives the strongest support because multiple chalcogenide-halide analogues are synthesized by sealed ampoule, direct solid-state, flux, or CVT methods (CS1–CS5). The SS set supports iodide-rich coordination environments, sealed halide handling, and slow-cooling crystallization, but it is chemically less direct because most SS examples lack Se and Ca together (SS2, SS4, SS5). The TS set supports thermodynamic accessibility of related metastable halides/selenides but includes chemically diverse cases, so it is mainly contextual rather than prescriptive (TS1, TS3, TS5). The critical enabling factor is use of sealed halide/iodide flux or melt conditions to preserve Se/I activity and accelerate ordering [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS5]. The critical limiting factor is maintaining Ca–Se–I stoichiometry against volatile Se/I loss and competing CaSe/CaI₂-type binaries [Reasonable Inference from CS2/CS5/TS5].
