### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamics**  
PAuBr₂ is not predicted as a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.170 eV/atom, but it lies inside the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom scoring window where metastable synthesis can be plausible (Target). TS analogues with similar E_hull values, including AgCrSe₂ at 0.1685 eV/atom and AgTe₃I at 0.1757 eV/atom, have reported synthesis routes, supporting a mid-high F1 score rather than rejection (TS2; TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that OQMD absence and theory-only status do not prove experimental accessibility (Target).

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for vacuum ampoules, volatile Br/P species, multi-step handling, and toxic halogen chemistry (CS1; TS1; SS1). Its kinetics are only moderate because direct reaction from dense Au metal requires major reconstruction, even though vapor-phase Br/P species can partially improve transport [Reasonable Inference; CS1; TS1]. Phase control remains uncertain because sealed P systems can show pressure-dependent polymorph outcomes (TS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has low nominal temperature but still requires sealed, inert, Br₂-containing handling, so F2 remains modest (SS1; SS5). F3 is better than Route 1 because solvothermal Br₂ chemistry should improve precursor activity and bypass bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference; SS1]. The main uncertainty is phase purity, since SS1 reports multiphase products and manual separation (SS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has the same sealed-volatile penalties as Route 1, but vapor transport improves kinetics relative to static solid-state reaction (TS1; SS5). F3 is relatively high because halide/P vapor species can transport material below Au’s melting point [Reasonable Inference; TS1; SS5]. The limiting issue is stoichiometry control because vapor pressure can steer product ratios and polymorphs (TS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 receives the best F3 because flux/liquid growth provides high mobility at 250–350 °C and has direct Au-containing precedent (TS5). Its F2 remains limited by vacuum/inert handling, air-sensitive fluxes, and volatile bromide/P reagents [Reasonable Inference; TS5; SS5]. The main uncertainty is that bromide flux transfer to a PAuBr₂ framework is inferred rather than directly demonstrated [Reasonable Inference; Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 5**  
Route 5 has mild temperature and no vacuum requirement, so F2 is the highest among routes (TS3; SS2). Its F3 is low because aqueous hydrothermal conditions may hydrolyze P–Br species and TS3 reports multiphase products requiring separation [Reasonable Inference; TS3]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PAuBr₂ is most likely a metastable phase rather than a ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.170 eV/atom, although its reported no-imaginary-frequency phonon screening supports local dynamic stability (Target). The strongest synthesis support comes from thermodynamic analogues near the same hull range and from low-temperature halide/flux routes that can access metastable crystals (TS1–TS5; SS5). The most critical enabling factor is halide-mediated mobility, because Br-rich vapor, solvothermal, CVT, or flux environments can bypass slow Au solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference; SS1; SS5; TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry/phase control under volatile Br and P chemical potentials, which is consistent with pressure-sensitive polymorph formation and multiphase halide products in analogues (TS1; SS1; TS3). Overall, the target is moderately synthesizable, with the best chance coming from dry bromide flux or CVT screening rather than aqueous hydrothermal synthesis (TS5; SS5; [Reasonable Inference]).
