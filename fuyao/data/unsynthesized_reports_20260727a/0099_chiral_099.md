### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s 0.073 eV/atom hull distance places it in the metastable-but-plausible 65–85 score band, and TS neighbors occupy a similar 0.0668–0.136 eV/atom thermodynamic range [TS1–TS4]. CS and SS analogues are mostly on the hull, showing that nearby Pt/Ir/Pd boride frameworks can be true ground states even if the target is above hull [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not prove the exact Au–Pt–B composition is experimentally stable; exact Au transferability is therefore neutral 50 where source-specific evidence is absent.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for high local melt temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum atmosphere, and a melt/remelt/anneal sequence [CS2, CS4, SS1]. It avoids high pressure and avoids known toxic/air-sensitive precursors based on CS/SS/TS evidence, but Au-specific volatility evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Arc melting improves kinetics by bypassing long-range solid-state diffusion, and this is strongly supported by RPt₃B and Pt–Cu–B analogues [CS2, CS4, SS1]. The long anneals and repeated remelts indicate that ordering and phase purity are still nontrivial [CS2, CS4, SS1]. The main uncertainty is whether Au stabilizes the same ordered chiral Pt–B network; direct Au–Pt–B evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has milder nominal temperature severity than arc melting but requires sealed Ar/vacuum handling and multiple heat/grind/anneal steps [CS1, SS2, SS3]. Optional 1100 °C treatment would worsen severity, as seen in the Sn–Ir–B analogue [CS1]. Volatility-specific evidence for Pt/Au/B sealing is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state kinetics are weak because dense Pt/Au and B must interdiffuse without a full liquid pathway [SS2, SS3; [Reasonable Inference]]. SS analogues report poor crystal quality, homogeneity ranges, and off-stoichiometric requirements, so reconstruction and phase control are difficult [SS2, SS3]. CS1 also uses long high-temperature reaction plus homogenization, supporting a low kinetics score [CS1].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Melt growth has the same high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere penalties as arc melting, plus annealing/slow-cooling complexity [CS3]. It avoids high pressure and highly reactive gas chemistry [CS3]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for true Pt–Au–B melt growth and are treated neutral 50 for route-specific transfer.

**F3 — Route 3**  
The liquid growth pathway should improve diffusion, but CS3 reports a secondary phase in CePt₃B, showing that melt processing does not guarantee phase purity [CS3]. Long annealing is still needed, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score [CS3]. The route is best viewed as a crystal-growth route after powder synthesis is validated [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
HPHT is strongly penalized because TS1 uses high pressure up to 60 kb and high temperature near 1200 °C or above [TS1]. The route also requires capsule handling, quenching, and specialized equipment [TS1]. CS and SS provide insufficient high-pressure evidence for this target and are scored neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Pressure may help access metastable regions, but TS1 is not a direct boride or Au-containing analogue, so transfer is weak [TS1]. Short HPHT holds may not fully order a multi-element Pt–Au–B network without a pre-alloyed feedstock [Reasonable Inference]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is more likely a metastable phase than a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.073 eV/atom, although TS analogues with comparable hull distances are known in the reference set [TS1–TS4]. The strongest support comes from CS and SS analogues showing that Pt-rich borides and Pt₃B-like frameworks can be made from elemental precursors by arc melting or sealed-tube reaction [CS2, CS4, SS1, SS2, SS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a liquid/homogenized arc-melt pathway that can overcome slow Pt/B diffusion [CS2, CS4, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: several analogues show secondary phases, homogeneity ranges, poor crystal quality, or long annealing requirements [CS3, SS2, SS3, SS4, TS4]. Direct Au–Pt–B precedent is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and is the largest extrapolation risk, scored neutral 50 where route transfer depends on Au.
