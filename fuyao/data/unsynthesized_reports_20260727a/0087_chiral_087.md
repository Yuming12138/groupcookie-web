### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull of 0.180 eV/atom places SnPBr in the metastable but potentially accessible 65–85 band rather than the ground-state band (Target). TS provides the strongest support because TS1 and TS3 include synthesized analogues at similar E_hull values near 0.17–0.19 eV/atom. SS supports local structural plausibility through near-hull SnP3/SnPSe3 analogues, while CS is mixed because it includes both stable Sn–halide chemistry and chemically similar high-hull molecular halide entries (SS1, SS5, CS1, CS3).

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized for sealed vacuum/dry handling, volatile P/Br containment, multi-step annealing, and hazardous SnBr2/P handling (CS2, SS1, TS1). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, so severity remains moderate rather than extreme (SS1, CS4). CS, SS, and TS all provide useful condition signals for this route through sealed-tube or direct solid-state analogues (CS2, CS4, SS1, TS3).

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory oxide synthesis because Sn-containing precursors are non-refractory and SnBr2 can act as a reactive halide source [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction from Sn–P motifs into a Br-containing chiral framework, and analogues show repeated heating/grinding or residual P impurity can occur (SS1, CS2).

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 receives the same severity deductions as Route 1 for evacuated sealed operation, volatile halogen/P species, multi-step feed/transport/recovery, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents (SS2, SS5, TS1). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, which keeps it more practical than the high-pressure route (SS2, SS5). CS gives insufficient evidence for the CVT process itself, so CS route-process source score is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2.** CVT gives the best kinetic score because vapor transport reduces bulk diffusion limitations and is directly demonstrated for SnPSe3 and iodine-containing Sn–Sb–S–I analogues (SS5, SS2). The uncertainty is composition control: TS1 shows phosphorus pressure changes phase selection, and the target also requires Br chemical-potential control (TS1, Target). Structural reconstruction remains moderate because no exact SnPBr CVT analogue is provided, so the final F3 is high but not near-perfect (SS2, SS5).

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux growth has moderate condition severity because it uses sealed tubes, volatile halide/P chemistry, multiple growth/separation steps, and moisture-sensitive halides [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 supports flux synthesis as accessible without high pressure, but CS and SS give insufficient evidence for Sn–P–Br flux-specific conditions, so CS and SS source scores are neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3.** Flux should improve precursor activity and diffusion by providing a liquid medium [Reasonable Inference], and TS5 supports flux-enabled crystallization of difficult layered phases. The structural-reconstruction score is reduced because SnBr2 self-flux for SnPBr is extrapolated rather than directly reported, and TS5 also flags phase-control difficulty for flux-grown analogues.

**F2 — Route 4.** High-pressure synthesis is the most severe route because it can require >5 GPa, near-1000 °C operation, sealed volatile precursors, and specialized anvil/capsule hardware (SS3, SS4, TS4). CS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure Sn–P–Br behavior, so CS source score is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 4.** High pressure can accelerate densification and stabilize P-rich frameworks, as shown by InP3 and ZnP4 analogues (SS3, SS4). The route is penalized because Br incorporation under high pressure is chemically less supported, and phase retention after decompression is uncertain for high-pressure-derived analogues (SS3, TS4).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has no clear multi-cation radius mismatch and no evidence for a >40-atom cell or vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence, so no F4 triggers are assigned [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives the most useful structural signal because SnP3 has closely matching Sn–P and P–P local bonding, while CS and TS are insufficient for exact target amorphization checks and are treated as neutral 50 for this factor (SS1, CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SnPBr is more likely a **metastable phase** than a ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.180 eV/atom, although this still lies inside the moderate metastability range and the target passed the no-imaginary-frequency screening (Target). The structural evidence is encouraging because SnP3 has very similar Sn–P and P–P bonding motifs, and SnPSe3 demonstrates that Sn–P–chalcogenide frameworks can be grown by CVT (SS1, SS5). The thermodynamic evidence is also supportive because TS1 and TS3 show synthesized analogues with comparable E_hull values near the target’s stability range. The most critical enabling factor is controlled sealed-tube vapor chemistry, which can manage P/halogen transport and crystal growth (SS2, SS5, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry drift from volatile P/Br species and competing Sn–P or Sn–halide phases, as indicated by phase sensitivity in SnICl and ZnP2 analogues (CS1, TS1).
