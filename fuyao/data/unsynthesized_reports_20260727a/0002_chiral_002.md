## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ above hull**, which places it in the highest stability category. The source-paper validation additionally reports no imaginary phonons, supporting dynamic stability. SS-1–SS-5 and TS-1–TS-5 all have \(E_\text{hull}=0\), while CS-5 is also on hull; however, CS-1–CS-4 are less stable analogues, so the CS signal is mixed. The remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or experimental phase selectivity.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives deductions for vacuum/sealed operation, volatile sulfur/halide chemistry, multiple handling steps, and toxic or air-sensitive precursors. These requirements are supported by CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5, which use evacuated or vacuum-sealed ampoules, and by the halide/sulfur analogues in SS-2 and SS-5. The proposed temperature is moderate relative to CS-5, so the route avoids the >1000 °C deduction. The principal uncertainty is the unverified thermal stability and vapor pressure of the BeCl₂/S mixture under the proposed program [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is based most directly on SS-5/TS-5, where closed ampoules, vacuum, moderate temperatures, and temperature gradients are used. These conditions justify the vacuum, volatile precursor, and multistep deductions. The absence of externally applied high pressure is supported by SS-5/TS-5. The major uncertainty is whether BeCl₂ and sulfur will establish a useful reversible transport cycle rather than deposit competing chloride or sulfur-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the closed-vessel solvothermal conditions demonstrated for RuS₃Cl₈ in SS-2/TS-2 and the cooling-crystallization logic of SS-1/TS-1. It is penalized for sealed operation, volatile/reactive precursors, and multistep handling, but not for high temperature or extreme pressure. The route could be experimentally accessible at approximately 80–140 °C [Reasonable Inference]. Its uncertainty is high because the solution speciation of Be–S–Cl species is not represented directly in the supplied analogues.

### F3 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route has moderate precursor activity because it combines reactive BeCl₂ with comparatively less mobile elemental sulfur [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 shows that sulfur solid-state reactions can require 750 °C, one week, slow cooling, and careful handling, with reported impurity or inhomogeneity concerns. Structural reconstruction is scored low because no CS analogue demonstrates the same chiral 2D Be–S–Cl connectivity. The route may still work because the target is calculated to be stable, but the kinetic pathway is uncertain.

### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because SS-5/TS-5 demonstrate crystal growth at only 140–155 °C under a temperature gradient, indicating vapor-assisted mass transport. The target’s orthorhombic structure is also directionally compatible with the orthorhombic Fdd2 CVT analogue in SS-5, although the space groups are not identical. Reconstruction remains only moderate because SS-5 concerns an Hf–Se–Cl coordination framework rather than a Be–S–Cl sheet. The dominant uncertainty is the absence of a direct Be-containing CVT precedent [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 3

Liquid- or vapor-assisted transport should improve precursor mixing and diffusion relative to dense powders [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the low-temperature solvothermal precedent in SS-2/TS-2. Nevertheless, SS-1/TS-1 and SS-2/TS-2 produce molecular or zero-dimensional products, not extended 2D sheets. Therefore, precursor activity and diffusion are favorable, but structural reconstruction is the limiting component. The target may crystallize only after a thermal post-treatment [Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a single cation species, so a large cation-radius mismatch is not indicated. Its description emphasizes corner-sharing BeSCl₃ tetrahedra and sulfur connectivity, without reporting the simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral network that would trigger C3. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for the exact unit-cell atom count, so C2 is neutral at 50 rather than treated as a trigger. The resulting low amorphization risk is therefore provisional and based primarily on the supplied target structural description [Reasonable Inference].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamically favored phase rather than an intentionally metastable phase because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ and the source-paper validation reports dynamic stability; the analogue thermodynamic set is also uniformly favorable in TS-1–TS-5. The strongest synthetic support comes from SS-5/TS-5, which demonstrate moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule vapor transport for an orthorhombic chalcogen/halide material, while CS-4 supports sulfur-rich sealed solid-state processing. The most important enabling factor is the combination of favorable thermodynamics with volatile sulfur/halide chemistry that may permit vapor-assisted crystal growth. The main limiting factor is structural selectivity: the supplied analogues are predominantly molecular, zero-dimensional, or chemically dissimilar, and none directly demonstrates formation of a chiral two-dimensional Be–S–Cl sheet. Consequently, Route 2 is preferred for obtaining ordered crystals, but phase competition and BeCl₂/S transport chemistry must be experimentally established [Reasonable Inference].
