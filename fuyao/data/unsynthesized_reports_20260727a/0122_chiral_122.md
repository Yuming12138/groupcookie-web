### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.177 eV/atom places it in the 65–85 range and makes it more likely metastable than ground-state. [Target] TS analogues include synthesized phases at similar or higher E_hull values, such as TS2 at 0.161 eV/atom, TS1 at 0.206 eV/atom, and TS5 at 0.258 eV/atom, supporting a score above the midpoint of the band. [TS2, TS1, TS5] CS and SS analogues are mostly hull-stable or near-stable, which supports chemical plausibility but does not remove the metastability penalty for the target. [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C synthesis but still needs sealed vacuum/Ar containment because Mg-containing analogues explicitly require sealed systems. [CS2, TS5] The main severity deductions are Mg volatility, controlled atmosphere, and multi-step sealing/annealing. [CS2, CS1, CS3] There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that MgAl₃B can be made phase-pure in a single direct firing, so no extra credit is applied. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics benefit from low-melting Mg/Al cations, but B is refractory and must be incorporated from powder. [CS2, Reasonable Inference] MgNi₃B₂ required 900–950 °C for 1 week, indicating that B incorporation and ordering are not instantaneous. [CS2] No same-structure precursor or exact analogue is provided, so structural reconstruction is scored conservatively. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Arc/levitation melting adds a high-temperature severity penalty, even though the subsequent anneal is moderate. [CS3, TS1] Mg volatility is especially limiting during melt processing, and analogues use extra Mg or careful heating to control loss. [CS3, TS1] The route remains plausible but operationally more severe than sealed solid-state or flux growth. [CS3, TS2]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Melt processing improves initial mixing and cation diffusion, which raises precursor activity and diffusion scores. [CS3, TS1] Long post-melt anneals of 400 h in related systems show that crystallographic ordering can still be slow. [CS3, TS2] There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that arc melting preserves MgAl₃B stoichiometry without Mg loss, so composition risk limits the score. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth avoids >1000 °C and uses the low-melting Al-rich matrix to lower condition severity. [SS4] It still requires inert/sealed handling and Mg excess because Mg volatility is repeatedly flagged in Mg-containing analogues. [CS2, CS3] Multi-step flux separation and long annealing keep the route from being easy. [SS4]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Al-rich flux improves B transport and phase ordering compared with dry powders. [SS4, Reasonable Inference] The diffusion score is high because Mg/Al are low-melting and analogous flux/direct routes operate at 650–900 °C. [SS4, TS5, CS2] The structural reconstruction score is moderate because SS4 reports residual Al/Al₂Ca and peritectic-control issues, implying possible competing phases. [SS4]

**F2 — Route 4**  
Thin-film synthesis has no direct CS/SS/TS precedent, so the source-specific evidence is neutral 50. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS] Vacuum processing and Mg volatility impose moderate condition severity. [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference] The route is non-equilibrium and potentially useful for metastability, but this is not demonstrated by the supplied analogues. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 4**  
Vapor availability is limited mainly by Mg volatility and B incorporation difficulty. [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference] Crystallization likely needs post-deposition annealing because no analogue shows direct thin-film crystallization. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, Reasonable Inference] Composition control is scored low because MgAl₃B stoichiometry may be narrow and Mg loss can shift the film off target. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgAl₃B is more likely a **metastable but dynamically viable** phase than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.177 eV/atom while the supplied validation says it passed the no-imaginary-phonon screening. [Target] The CS evidence strongly supports sealed elemental Mg-containing synthesis, the SS evidence supports Al-rich melt/flux crystallization, and the TS evidence shows experimentally accessed analogues at comparable metastability. [CS2, CS3, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS5] The most critical enabling factor is the low-melting Mg/Al matrix, which can provide liquid-assisted transport for B under sealed conditions. [CS2, SS4, TS5, Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Mg stoichiometry while avoiding competing Al-rich or Mg–B phases. [CS2, CS3, SS4] Overall, the target is **moderately synthesizable**, with Route 3 preferred for balancing phase ordering and Mg retention. [SS4, CS2, CS3]
