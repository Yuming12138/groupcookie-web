# SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target is a one-dimensional Al–S–Br ribbon with mixed corner/edge-sharing AlS\(_2\)Br\(_4\) octahedra and an S–S bond. The closest structural analogue is CuBiSCl\(_2\), which contains mixed edge/corner-sharing metal–chalcogen–halogen octahedra and is made by sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction (SS-4). Thermodynamic analogues ZnAl\(_2\)S\(_4\) and MnAl\(_2\)S\(_4\) demonstrate that Al–S frameworks can be obtained by high-temperature solid-state or pressure-assisted methods (TS-2, TS-3). AlBr\(_3\)-containing molecular halide chemistry is demonstrated by SS-2 and SS-3.

# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) of 0.090 eV atom\(^{-1}\) lies in the moderately metastable 0.05–0.20 eV atom\(^{-1}\) interval, supporting a score of 78/100 (target validation). Thermodynamic analogues include materials near 0.10–0.12 eV atom\(^{-1}\), such as FeGa\(_2\)S\(_4\), ZnAl\(_2\)S\(_4\), MnAl\(_2\)S\(_4\), and FeBi\(_4\)S\(_7\), showing that related chalcogenide frameworks can be synthesized despite nonzero hull energy (TS-1–TS-4). The principal uncertainty is whether the target’s calculated metastability corresponds to a sufficiently deep kinetic basin under experimental conditions.

## F2 — Route 1

The sealed solid-state route receives 55/100 because related products are formed in evacuated quartz ampoules at moderate-to-high temperatures (SS-4, SS-5, TS-2), but the route requires controlled atmosphere, sealing, volatile AlBr\(_3\), and potentially extended heating. AlBr\(_3\) is explicitly associated with moisture-sensitive halide chemistry (SS-2). The main uncertainty is whether dry powder contact is sufficient to form the unusual one-dimensional mixed-connectivity ribbon rather than competing Al–Br/S phases.

## F3 — Route 1

The route has reasonable diffusion kinetics because the proposed temperature is below the melting point of aluminum and related sulfide frameworks are accessible by solid-state reaction (TS-2, TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). However, precursor activity is only moderate and structural reconstruction is substantial because the target is not a simple derivative of the molecular AlBr\(_4\) units in SS-2. Long reaction times and repeated treatments are reported for related chalcogenides, indicating that ordering and phase homogenization may be slow (CS-5, TS-4).

## F2 — Route 2

CVT receives 55/100 because sealed ampoules and temperature gradients are established methods for chalcogenide and halide crystal growth (SS-2, TS-1). The deductions arise from volatile AlBr\(_3\), sulfur, and halogen transport agents, together with the need for controlled atmosphere and multiple operations. Exact transport chemistry for AlSBr\(_2\) is unavailable, so the severity estimate is uncertain.

## F3 — Route 2

CVT offers good vapor availability for AlBr\(_3\) and iodine/chlorine transport agents, as demonstrated by SS-2 and TS-1. Nevertheless, composition control is penalized because a one-dimensional Al–S–Br phase may compete with simple molecular halides or sulfur bromides, and the appropriate transport-agent concentration is unknown [Reasonable Inference]. CVT may improve crystal size but does not necessarily improve bulk phase yield.

## F2 — Route 3

The ionothermal route receives the highest F2 score because SS-1 demonstrates crystalline framework formation at only 165 °C in an ionic-liquid medium. The deductions reflect the need for glovebox loading, moisture exclusion, sealing, and handling of corrosive halide chemistry (SS-1, SS-2). No high pressure or extreme temperature is required.

## F3 — Route 3

The flux route is favored kinetically because a liquid ionic medium should increase precursor contact and ion mobility relative to dry solid-state powders [Reasonable Inference], consistent with successful ionothermal synthesis in SS-1. The principal penalty is structural uncertainty: no provided analogue forms the exact AlSBr\(_2\) ribbon, so nucleation and reconstruction remain unverified. The 70/100 value therefore reflects improved transport kinetics but incomplete evidence for phase selectivity.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only the mixed-connectivity criterion is clearly triggered because the target contains both corner- and edge-sharing octahedra (target structure), a motif also present in SS-4 and TS-1. There is no mixed-cation radius mismatch because only Al is present, while the target unit-cell atom count is not provided; therefore **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for C1 and C2. The resulting low–medium amorphization risk is appropriate, although the actual crystallization tendency may be reduced by the target’s chirality and one-dimensionality.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is more likely to be a metastable or kinetically stabilized phase than a strict ground-state phase because its \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.090 eV atom\(^{-1}\), although its reported phonon screening passed (target validation). CS/SS/TS analogues provide meaningful support: Al–halide chemistry, mixed metal–chalcogen–halide connectivity, one-dimensional chalcogenide ribbons, and near-metastable sulfides are all represented (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, TS-1–TS-3). The most important enabling factor is enhanced precursor mobility in a Lewis-acidic bromide flux (SS-1; [Reasonable Inference]). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity, especially competition from molecular aluminum bromides, sulfur bromides, and other Al–S–Br compositions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is a credible experimental target, but synthesis should begin as a broad phase-screening study rather than a single fixed recipe.
