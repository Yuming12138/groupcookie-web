### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull = 0.197 eV/atom places it just below the 0.20 eV/atom threshold, so it scores in the lower part of the 65–85 rubric band [Target data]. TS analogues show that phases with comparable or higher hull energies can still be synthesized, including LiSi at 0.218 eV/atom and boron-rich analogues above 0.29 eV/atom [TS3–TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and not experimentally validated in the provided data [Target data].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is conditionally severe because it requires inert handling, sealed refractory-metal containment, Li excess, and flux removal [CS1, CS4, SS1/TS1]. Its kinetics are favorable because Li-rich flux routes directly form crystalline Li–B/metal borides and reduce diffusion barriers [CS1, SS1/TS1]. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates Li–In–B phase formation, so In transferability is a [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has moderate condition severity because direct Li intermetallic analogues form below or near 1000 °C in sealed/inert setups [CS2, CS3, CS5]. Its kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because B incorporation into a chiral boride framework likely requires more reconstruction than Li–Si or Li–Zn–Si analogues [CS2, CS5, SS4, Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is phase purity, since repeated heating and composition control are often needed in related systems [CS2, CS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has the harshest thermal profile because Sn-flux Li–B–C borides require 1300 °C and 70 h [SS4]. Kinetics can be strong because molten flux improves transport, but foreign-flux incorporation and mixed boride formation are significant risks [SS4]. In-rich self-flux is a plausible adaptation, but it is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 avoids >1000 °C conditions but requires specialized pressure equipment and sealed capsules [TS3]. It is thermodynamically motivated because TS3 shows high-pressure stabilization of a metastable Li compound, but CS and SS provide insufficient high-pressure signal and are neutral 50 for this route [TS3, CS, SS]. Its kinetic uncertainty is high because lower-temperature pressure synthesis may not efficiently build the B–B network [TS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target does not obviously trigger the radius-mismatch or >40 atom/cell checks, based on target stoichiometry and P2₁ site count [Target data, Reasonable Inference]. The mixed-connectivity check is triggered because target coordination is dense and varied, while related Li/metal frameworks show mixed sharing motifs [CS4, CS5, SS2]. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high [CS4, CS5, SS2, Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₂InB₂ is most likely a metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.197 eV/atom rather than near-zero [Target data]. The analogue set supports synthesis moderately well: CS strongly supports Li-rich and sealed intermetallic/boride synthesis [CS1–CS5], SS supports Li–B framework formation under high-temperature flux/sealed conditions [SS1–SS4], and TS shows that metastable thermodynamic neighborhoods can still be experimentally reached [TS3–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is a Li-rich or Li–In liquid flux that can overcome B diffusion limits and promote crystal growth [CS1, SS1/TS1, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because Li-rich syntheses can be stoichiometry-sensitive and boride flux routes can generate competing phases or flux incorporation [CS1, SS4]. Overall, the best assessment is **moderately synthesizable but experimentally nontrivial** [CS1, SS1/TS1, TS3].
