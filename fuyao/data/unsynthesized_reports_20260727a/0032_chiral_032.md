CVT receives a relatively strong F2 score because the analogues use moderate temperatures rather than extreme thermal conditions [CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-3, SS-5]. The principal deductions arise from the required evacuated sealed system, volatile iodine/sulfur chemistry, and hazardous halogen handling [CS-3, CS-5; TS-1; Reasonable Inference]. ## F2: Route 2

Direct solid-state synthesis receives a lower F2 score because a practical procedure would likely require staged annealing and possibly regrinding to overcome incomplete diffusion [CS-2; TS-3]. ## F2: Route 3

The solvothermal route benefits from the moderate temperatures reported for related systems [CS-1; TS-2, TS-4, TS-5]. ## F3: Route 1

CVT has the strongest kinetic precedent because iodine transport repeatedly produces crystalline chalcogen-halide materials, including one-dimensional and mixed-anion structures [CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-3, SS-5; TS-1]. The main limitation is composition control: the target requires simultaneous control of Ga, S, and I chemical potentials and formation of a chiral \(P3_1\) arrangement [Target structure; Reasonable Inference].

## F3: Route 2

The direct route has simple precursor chemistry, but elemental mixing and diffusion are less controlled than in a transport process [CS-2; TS-3]. Gallium’s low melting behavior may provide a liquid-assisted reaction pathway, which is favorable for precursor contact, but this is a physicochemical extrapolation rather than a demonstrated target-specific effect [Target structure; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is scored lower because the target’s chiral mixed-connectivity framework is not directly represented by the direct-reaction analogues [CS-2; TS-3; Target structure].

## F3: Route 3

Solvothermal processing can improve precursor contact and reduce diffusion barriers through a liquid medium, as shown by the moderate-temperature solvothermal examples [CS-1; TS-2]. However, solvothermal systems often show solvent- and additive-dependent polymorphism or multiphase behavior [CS-1; TS-2, TS-5]. The absence of a direct Ga–S–I or chiral \(P3_1\) example creates substantial uncertainty in whether the target framework, rather than a hydrolyzed or sulfur-rich phase, will crystallize [CS/SS/TS insufficient evidence; Target structure; Reasonable Inference].
