### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.183 eV/atom falls in the 65–85 scoring bracket and is close to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as Li8Al3Si5 at 0.206 eV/atom and LiSi at 0.218 eV/atom (CS1, TS1, TS2). The score is not higher because 0.183 eV/atom indicates likely metastability rather than ground-state stability, while no CS/SS/TS source reports experimental Li3Al2B synthesis. SS gives supportive structural stability through ground-state Li3Al2 (SS1), but SS provides insufficient direct evidence for the ternary boride and is neutral for target experimental confirmation.

**F2 — Route 1**  
The elemental sealed route uses moderate temperatures comparable to 600–770 °C Li-containing analogues, so no high-temperature deduction is applied (CS2, CS4, SS1). Deductions come from Ar/inert control, Li volatility, multi-step annealing, and Li air sensitivity, all of which are supported by Li-containing analogue procedures (CS1, CS2, SS1). TS provides supportive sealed/controlled environment evidence but no direct Li–Al–B route, so target-specific TS transferability is partly neutral.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Elemental Li and Al are active enough for Li–Al and Li–Zn–Si analogues, but direct B incorporation is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS (CS2, SS1). Diffusion is feasible at 600–770 °C based on Li-containing analogues, but the target requires new ternary boride ordering [Reasonable Inference; CS2, SS1, SS5]. The structural score is moderate because SS1 supports Li–Al motifs but not B incorporation.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Levitation melting is supported by Li8Al3Si5 synthesis under Ar with careful evaporation control (CS1, TS1). The route receives the same Li/Ar/multi-step deductions as Route 1, and no >1000 °C deduction is applied because CS1/TS1 do not report a specific melting temperature. The main uncertainty is whether B can be fully incorporated during melt processing [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct B evidence from CS/TS].

**F3 — Route 2**  
Melt processing improves mixing and can access complex Li–Al–Si alloy phases, which supports a higher diffusion score than direct solid-state reaction (CS1, TS1). Structural reconstruction remains uncertain because the target P4_1 Li3Al2B structure has no direct melt-grown analogue in CS/SS/TS. SS provides insufficient evidence for levitation melting of the Li–Al structural analogue, so SS is neutral for this route-specific method signal.

**F2 — Route 3**  
The binary/alloy precursor route still requires inert handling, sealed containment, Li control, and multiple steps, so its severity remains 55 despite better phase-targeting logic (CS4, CS5, SS1). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions based on the 600–730 °C binary-assisted analogue syntheses (CS4, CS5). TS gives indirect support through moderate-temperature ternary synthesis at 650 °C/60 h (TS5), but no direct Li–Al–B binary route is reported.

**F3 — Route 3**  
This route has the best kinetics/phase-control balance because Li3Al2 supplies a structurally similar Li–Al framework and binary precursor strategies are documented for Li–Ni–Si and Li–Cu–Si analogues (SS1, CS4, CS5). The score is capped because B incorporation and chiral P4_1 ordering remain unproven by CS/SS/TS. The need to manage competing Li–Al phases is specifically supported by SS1, which reports Li3Al2 phase-purity challenges.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Mechanochemical activation under Ar is supported by the Li–Ca–Al hydride analogue using 30 h ball milling at 400 rpm (CS3, SS2). Severity deductions arise from inert handling, Li sensitivity, sealed annealing, and multiple steps, while no high-temperature or high-pressure deduction applies. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for mechanochemical synthesis, so TS is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Ball milling should improve precursor contact and lower diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], and CS3/SS2 supports mechanochemical conversion in a Li–Al-containing system. The structural score is lower because the cited mechanochemical analogue is a hydride/chloride reaction rather than a Li–Al–B boride. Therefore, Route 4 is kinetically attractive but more speculative than Route 3.

**F2 — Route 5**  
Pressure-assisted synthesis is operationally challenging, but the main scoring table only penalizes >5 GPa; the first trial at 1–3 GPa follows TS2 and avoids that deduction. If the TS4-like 8 GPa fallback is required, the route becomes much less favorable because TS4 uses 8 GPa and specialized multi-anvil hardware. **F4 — Amorphization Tendency**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li3Al2B is more likely a metastable phase than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.183 eV/atom, although dynamic stability and similar synthesized metastable analogues support possible isolation (TS1, TS2). The CS and SS analogues strongly support Li-containing intermetallic synthesis under Ar at 600–770 °C, especially via Li–Al and binary-assisted routes (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is using a Li–Al alloy/binary precursor to preserve the Li–Al framework and reduce reconstruction distance (SS1, CS4, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against competing Li–Al binaries and Li loss, both of which are highlighted by SS1 and CS1/TS1. Overall, the target is moderately synthesizable but likely requires careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry control rather than a simple open solid-state reaction.

|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | Li volatility and mass loss | High | Use sealed Ta/Nb or Ta-in-quartz containment and controlled heating; CS1/TS1 explicitly controlled heating to keep weight loss <1 mass%. |
| 2 | All | Air/moisture sensitivity of Li-containing feeds | High | Handle and load under Ar glovebox conditions, as done in CS1, CS2, CS3, SS1, and SS5. |
| 3 | Route 3 | Competing Li–Al binary phases | High | Control stoichiometry and cooling carefully because SS1 reports Li3Al2 can coexist with Li9Al4 and needs slow/stepwise thermal treatment. |
| 4 | Routes 1 & 3 | B incorporation and slow boride ordering | Medium–High | Use fine B or Al–B precursor and longer anneals [Reasonable Inference]; binary-precursor strategies are supported by CS4 and CS5. |
| 5 | Route 2 | B segregation or incomplete melting during levitation/arc processing | Medium–High | Follow melt processing with sealed annealing and mass-loss checks; CS1/TS1 supports careful heating/cooling and post-synthesis verification. |
| 6 | Route 4 | Milling contamination and non-equilibrium amorphous products | Medium | Use controlled milling under Ar and follow with crystallizing anneal; CS3/SS2 supports Ar milling but not direct boride crystallization. |
| 7 | Route 5 | High-pressure phase-purity sensitivity | High | Start with lower 1–3 GPa trials based on TS2 and avoid 8 GPa unless needed, because TS4 reports impurity and pressure-window challenges. |
| 8 | All | Novelty/no experimental precedent for Li3Al2B | Medium | Treat all routes as exploratory and screen products by powder XRD/phase mapping; no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Li3Al2B synthesis, so this evidence gap remains neutral-to-negative. |
