### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Li2Cu3B has E_hull = 0.196 eV/atom, so it scores in the upper part of the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom bracket but near the less favorable boundary [Target]. TS provides the strongest supporting signal because several thermodynamically similar compounds with E_hull ≈0.16–0.26 eV/atom have reported syntheses [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5]. CS and SS show many chemically/structurally adjacent compounds are stable or near-stable, but neither directly proves Li2Cu3B is a ground state; target-specific CS/SS thermodynamic signal is therefore limited [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Direct sealed-ampoule synthesis has moderate severity because the temperature range is only 600–900 °C and no high pressure is needed [CS1, CS2, CS5, TS2, TS5]. The main deductions are Li volatility/sealed-system need and air-sensitive Li handling [CS2, CS4, TS5]. The uncertainty is B incorporation, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence and neutral 50 signal.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are only moderate because direct elemental reaction must form the Cu–B network and the ternary phase in one sequence [Reasonable Inference]. Li intermetallic analogues form under similar temperatures, but no direct Li–Cu–B boride analogue is provided [CS1–CS5, TS5]. The score is therefore constrained by B diffusion and structural reconstruction uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 2**  
The binary-precursor route adds process steps, so it receives a multi-step deduction [CS3, CS4, SS5]. It still avoids >1000 °C and high pressure during the main Li reaction, which is favorable [CS3, CS4]. Li volatility and air sensitivity remain the key condition penalties [CS2, CS4, TS5].

**F3 — Route 2**  
This route scores best kinetically because binary-precursor chemistry is directly supported by Li + Ni2Si/Ni2Ge and Li/Cu silicide analogues [CS3, CS4]. Pre-forming Cu–B is expected to reduce the need for long-range B diffusion during final ternary formation [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not specify a real Cu–B precursor or phase window for Li2Cu3B.

**F2 — Route 3**  
The melt/arc/Bridgman route is more severe because structural analogues use high-temperature melt growth around 1450 K and multi-step anneal/quench sequences [SS1–SS4]. Vacuum annealing appears in TS1, giving a controlled-atmosphere deduction if that variant is used [TS1]. Li volatility makes this route substantially riskier than sealed lower-temperature routes [CS1, CS2, TS5].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Melt processing improves mixing and diffusion, which is favorable for Cu-rich frameworks [SS1–SS4, TS3]. However, long anneals, quenching, and observed secondary phases in related Cu-rich systems indicate phase selection is nontrivial [SS2–SS4]. The target’s chiral ordered phase may require narrow composition/timing control [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Mechanochemical activation uses moderate anneal temperatures similar to SS5, so thermal severity is not high [SS5]. The penalties come from multiple operations, Li-sensitive handling, and sealed annealing [CS2, CS4, SS5]. No CS/SS/TS source directly validates milling for Li–Cu–B, so this route is less evidence-backed than Route 2.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Milling should improve precursor contact and shorten diffusion lengths, supported by SS5 and CS2 grinding/milling practices [CS2, SS5]. The risk is that milling can introduce disorder or contamination before crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. Because no direct Li–Cu–B mechanochemical example is provided, the structural reconstruction component remains moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target likely has low-to-medium amorphization risk because the formula is compact and related intermetallic analogues crystallize in ordered cubic/metallic frameworks [CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, SS5]. One trigger is assigned for mixed connectivity by [Reasonable Inference] from analogues with face/edge/corner-sharing polyhedra [CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, SS5]. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for target ionic radii and exact unit-cell atom count, so those checks are treated as neutral 50 where source-specific evidence is required.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li2Cu3B is more likely a **metastable synthesizable candidate** than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.196 eV/atom, although the supplied target data says it passed dynamic-stability screening [Target]. CS supports the general feasibility of Li-containing intermetallic synthesis under sealed Ar at 600–900 °C, while TS shows that similarly metastable compounds can still be experimentally prepared [CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5]. SS supports accessibility of Cu-rich metallic frameworks, but several SS analogues also warn that secondary phases and long anneals can be needed [SS1–SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the binary-precursor strategy, because CS3/CS4 show Li can be introduced into preformed binary frameworks. The main limiting factor is the lack of direct Li–Cu–B boride precedent, combined with Li volatility and the target’s near-0.20 eV/atom metastability [Target, CS2, CS4].
