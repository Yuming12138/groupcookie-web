## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.090 eV atom⁻¹, which falls in the requested 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ interval and supports a moderate-to-good thermodynamic score. The TS analogues have similarly moderate hull energies, including 0.089 eV atom⁻¹ for Sn₂SbS₂I₃ and 0.107 eV atom⁻¹ for MoTe₄Br, while CS and SS include several phases at or near the calculated ground state. This indicates that the target may be kinetically accessible, but it is not predicted to be the unequivocal ground-state phase.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to CS-1/2 and TS-1/2, so it avoids the severe thermal penalty associated with very high-temperature synthesis. However, vacuum, a sealed ampoule, and volatile iodine/sulfur impose meaningful operational penalties, as reflected by CS-1/2, SS-2, and TS-1/2. The route is scored higher than the direct solid-state route because it can use vapor transport rather than relying entirely on bulk diffusion.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 requires vacuum sealing and volatile iodine containment, as in CS-3 and SS-2. Its score is lower because direct solid-state reactions commonly require long holds, controlled cooling, and sometimes repeated grinding/reheating; these features are explicitly reported or implied in CS-3 and SS-2. The route remains feasible because no high pressure or temperature above 1400 °C is required.

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the same sealed-tube and volatile-component penalties as Route 1, but it adds a precursor-preparation step for NbS₂, making it more operationally complex. The added step is justified by the binary-precursor strategy in CS-3 and TS-1. Its condition severity is therefore intermediate between a one-stage CVT experiment and a multicycle direct solid-state synthesis.

### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Nb is refractory, so precursor activity and solid-state diffusion are limiting factors; this is a physicochemical inference supported by the need for long high-temperature treatment in SS-2 and CS-2. Conversely, iodine-assisted transport provides a favorable low-temperature crystallization mechanism, demonstrated by CS-1/2 and TS-1/2. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because SS-2 and CS-3 provide one-dimensional Nb–chalcogen–halide ribbon analogues, but they do not establish the target’s sulfur-specific P3₁ chiral arrangement.

### F3 — Route 2

Preformed NbS₂ should reduce the chemical distance to the target relative to elemental Nb, but direct solid-state diffusion remains difficult for a refractory Nb-containing framework. CS-3 demonstrates that a binary niobium chalcogenide can participate in formation of a one-dimensional halide ribbon, while SS-2 shows that controlled thermal history is important for Nb–chalcogen–iodide phase purity. The absence of a direct Nb–S–I solid-state precedent leaves substantial uncertainty.

### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from both a preformed Nb–S substructure and vapor transport. This combination is supported by the binary-precursor CVT examples in CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, and TS-1. The score is still below the high range because no supplied example produces the same composition, chirality, or P3₁ space group, and phase competition is likely. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target appears to contain only one principal cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. However, the supplied data do not provide the target’s unit-cell atom count or a complete polyhedral-sharing analysis; therefore, **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for C2 and C3, and both are assigned neutral treatment. The resulting F4 score is intentionally conservative rather than implying demonstrably high amorphization resistance.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most reasonably classified as a potentially metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its calculated \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.090 eV atom⁻¹, although its reported dynamic-stability screening was favorable in the target validation data. SS-2 provides the strongest structural precedent by demonstrating a one-dimensional Nb–chalcogen–iodide ribbon from elemental precursors, while CS-2/3 and TS-1/2 support sealed-tube transport chemistry. The most important enabling factor is iodine-assisted vapor transport, which can bypass slow bulk diffusion and has produced related chalcogen–halide crystals at moderate temperatures in CS-1/2 and TS-1/2. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: sulfur/iodine volatility, competing Nb–S–I stoichiometries, and the unverified chiral ordering are not directly resolved by the analogues. Overall, the target is experimentally plausible but likely requires a systematic CVT optimization rather than a single guaranteed synthesis.
