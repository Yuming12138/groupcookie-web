### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cd₃BPt₂ is more likely a metastable phase than a true ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.148 eV/atom, although the reported dynamic stability improves plausibility. (Target data; CS1–CS5; TS1–TS5) The CS and SS analogues strongly support the feasibility of Pt–Cd intermetallic chemistry and transition-metal boride synthesis in sealed high-temperature systems. (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS4) The TS analogues support metastable accessibility but are chemically weaker, and TS1/SS5 contain synthesis descriptions that are not useful for Cd–Pt–B route design, so those route signals are treated neutral 50. (SS2–SS3; TS2; CS3) The key limiting factor is phase selectivity against stable Pt–Cd intermetallics, Pt–B binaries, and Cd loss during high-temperature processing. (CS4–CS5; SS1–SS3; TS2)

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy (from CS/SS/TS) |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Cd volatility and loss from target stoichiometry. | High | Use sealed Ta/Nb ampoule inside evacuated quartz/silica jacket and Cd excess/overpressure. (CS4; SS2–SS3; TS2) |
| 2 | All | Cd toxicity and environmental hazard. | High | Use sealed handling, secondary containment, and minimize open Cd exposure [Reasonable Inference]. (Target safety note; TS2) |
| 3 | All | Competing Pt–Cd and Pt–B phases. | High | Use annealing, stoichiometry screening, and phase checks because analogues show phase-equilibrium sensitivity. (CS4–CS5; SS1–SS3) |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow diffusion from dense elemental Pt/B powders. | Medium | Use long high-temperature holds, regrinding, and annealing as in boride/intermetallic analogues. (CS1–CS3; SS2–SS4) |
| 5 | Route 2 | Cd cannot be safely arc-melted with Pt/B because of volatility [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Arc-melt Pt–B first, then add Cd only in a sealed lower-temperature anneal. (CS5; TS2; SS2–SS3) |
| 6 | Route 3 | B incorporation into Cd-rich flux is uncertain. | Medium | Use Pt–B pre-reacted feedstock or fine B powder and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. (CS5; CS1–CS3; SS2–SS3) |
| 7 | Route 3 | Crystal growth may yield Cd-rich residual metal or secondary phases. | Medium | Slow cool and remove excess metal by decanting or chemical removal, analogous to excess Zn removal in boride synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. (CS3; TS2) |
| 8 | Route 4 | Thin-film stoichiometry control is narrow because Cd can re-evaporate. | High | Use Cd overflux, capping, low-temperature annealing, and composition calibration [Reasonable Inference]. (TS2; TS3) |
| 9 | Route 4 | Crystallization of the bulk chiral P4₃ phase is unproven in films. | High | Use post-deposition annealing and substrate/temperature screening [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS provide insufficient direct mitigation and are neutral 50. (TS3; insufficient evidence from CS/SS) |
