## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.060 eV atom⁻¹ lies in the supplied 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ interval, corresponding to a favorable but not unequivocally ground-state score. The thermodynamic analogue BaN₃Cl has E_hull = 0.087 eV atom⁻¹, and LiC₂N₃ and Cr₂(CN₂)₃ are similarly low at 0.080 and 0.064 eV atom⁻¹, respectively (TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The target’s low hull distance supports synthesis feasibility, but the supplied analogue data do not prove that the specific P3₁ polymorph is the experimentally preferred form.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because the directly analogous BaN₃Cl synthesis uses room-temperature aqueous metathesis and slow evaporation without high pressure or extreme temperature (TS-1). A deduction is retained for the multi-operation crystallization procedure and for NaN₃-related hazards, which are repeatedly relevant in the azide analogue set (TS-1; SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, SS-4). The main uncertainty is whether Mg²⁺ hydration and solubility behavior will permit formation of the target rather than a hydrate or another Mg azide/chloride phase. [Reasonable Inference]

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized for controlled N₂/vacuum operation, sealed-system processing, multiple preparation stages, and air-sensitive or hazardous precursors. CS-1 demonstrates that Mg-containing nitrogen-rich materials can require 800 °C and seven days under N₂ in sealed ampoules, supporting the severity deductions. No CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates the proposed MgN₃Cl solid-state conversion, so the temperature window and precursor combination remain extrapolations. [Reasonable Inference]

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids very high temperatures but still requires dry handling, controlled atmosphere, staged heating, and flux removal. TS-5 supports dynamic-vacuum molten-salt synthesis at 550 °C for nitrogen-containing frameworks, while azide analogues indicate additional safety concerns associated with N₃⁻ precursors (TS-5; SS-1, SS-3, SS-4). The principal uncertainty is whether N₃⁻ survives sufficiently in the chloride melt to form the target rather than decomposing to nitrogen-rich or chloride-rich competing phases. [Reasonable Inference]

### F3 — Route 1

Aqueous processing provides high precursor activity and rapid ion transport, as evidenced by the formation of BaN₃Cl from aqueous BaCl₂ and NaN₃ and by aqueous azide crystallization methods (TS-1; SS-1, SS-2, SS-4). The kinetic score is reduced because crystallization of the exact chiral P3₁ topology is not established in the supplied examples. This route is therefore kinetically favorable for nucleation and crystal growth but uncertain for polymorph and handedness selection. [Reasonable Inference]

### F3 — Route 2

The route uses preformed Mg₃N₂ and therefore requires substantial solid-state reconstruction to incorporate chloride and preserve N₃-like linkages. CS-1 confirms that Mg nitride solid-state reactions are possible but may require long holds at 800 °C, indicating slow ordering kinetics (CS-1). Magnesium’s relatively low melting point gives a favorable formal diffusion score under the provided rubric, but actual diffusion in a multiphase ceramic mixture may be poorer than this idealized criterion suggests. [Reasonable Inference]

### F3 — Route 3

The LiCl/KCl flux in TS-5 provides the strongest kinetic argument for a bulk synthesis alternative because a liquid medium can enhance ion transport and reduce diffusion limitations (TS-5). The route still requires significant structural reconstruction, since no supplied flux analogue contains the exact Mg–N₃–Cl connectivity or chiral P3₁ symmetry. The resulting score is intermediate-to-good, with the flux improving kinetics but not resolving phase-selection uncertainty. [Reasonable Inference]

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

There is no confirmed mixed-cation radius mismatch because the target contains only Mg as the crystallographic cation; however, the supplied CS/SS/TS data do not provide the target unit-cell atom count, so the C2 criterion has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is treated neutrally. Mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity is explicitly present in the target and is represented in related frameworks such as MgAl₂S₄ and BaN₃Cl (CS-5; TS-1). Accordingly, one confirmed trigger gives a low–medium amorphization risk rather than a high risk.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **low-energy metastable or weakly metastable phase**, rather than an unequivocally stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹ rather than zero; however, this energy is low and comparable to several synthesizable analogues (TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest support comes from the direct BaN₃Cl aqueous-metathesis precedent (TS-1), while Mg–N framework formation is supported by CS-1 and nitrogen-chain structural motifs are supported by SS-1 and related azides. The most important enabling factor is the high mobility of Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, and N₃⁻ in a concentrated solution, which should facilitate nucleation and ordering. The most critical limitation is polymorph and chirality selection: the supplied analogues do not directly demonstrate formation of a chiral P3₁ MgN₃Cl phase. Overall, aqueous metathesis followed by slow crystallization is the most credible first experiment, with flux synthesis as the best bulk-solid alternative.
