# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The supplied target energy above hull is 0.1000 eV/atom, which falls in the moderate-metastability interval of the requested rubric [Target record]. This is less favorable than the ground-state analogues in CS4, CS5, SS1, SS4, and TS1, which have reported hull values near 0–0.05 eV/atom [CS4, CS5, SS1, SS4, TS1]. The target’s dynamic-stability pass supports a local minimum but does not establish equilibrium synthesis; therefore, F1 is moderate rather than high [Target record].

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires vacuum sealing, volatile iodine/selenium handling, and several thermal-processing steps [CS1, CS5, SS1, SS4]. Its temperature is not intrinsically extreme relative to the 800–900 °C LnSI/LnSeI syntheses and 700–1173 K Ba chalcogenide syntheses [CS1, CS4, CS5, SS1, SS4]. The principal uncertainty is whether iodine remains incorporated into the target rather than escaping or forming BaI₂ [SS5; Reasonable Inference].

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the same nominal severity score because it still requires a sealed iodine-containing system, controlled atmosphere, and multistep thermal treatment [SS3, TS4]. The flux route does not require high pressure, which is favorable relative to the high-pressure Ba₃CrS₅ synthesis [TS1] and high-pressure BaI₂ polymorph preparation [SS5]. Its main penalty arises from chemical handling and phase-control complexity rather than temperature alone [SS3, SS5].

## F2 — Route 3

CVT uses temperatures comparable to established LnSeI/LnSI syntheses but requires carefully controlled halide transport and sealed ampoules [CS1, CS4, CS5]. CS1 specifically reports precursor dehydration/sublimation, narrow product zones, and competing phase formation in related transport chemistry [CS1]. Thus, the thermal burden is moderate, but the operational and transport-chemistry burden is high [CS1].

## F3 — Route 1

Direct solid-state kinetics are helped by the relatively low melting point of Ba compared with many transition-metal or rare-earth cations, but the target still requires formation of a complex mixed Ba–Se–I network [SS1, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. The closest structural analogues do not reproduce the target’s tetragonal P4₃ topology, so substantial structural reorganization is expected [SS1, SS4]. CS4 and CS5 demonstrate that ternary chalcogenide-halide phases can form by direct annealing, supporting a moderate rather than poor kinetics score [CS4, CS5].

## F3 — Route 2

The BaI₂ flux should increase precursor mobility and reduce the effective diffusion limitation, consistent with the successful BaI₂-assisted synthesis of tetragonal Ba₄Fe₂I₅S₄ [SS3]. Related chloride-flux and sealed-tube syntheses also show that halide fluxes can support crystal growth at approximately 900–1173 K [TS4]. However, the available flux precedent contains Fe/S and U-assisted chemistry rather than the Ba–Se–I ternary target, so the structural-reconstruction component remains only moderate [SS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 3

CVT can provide strong mass transport and single-crystal growth, as demonstrated for ErSeI [CS1]. Nevertheless, the ErSeI structure is orthorhombic and layered, whereas BaSeI is tetragonal, chiral, and contains two Ba sites plus Se–Se bonding [CS1; Target structure]. Therefore, transport kinetics may be favorable while nucleation selectivity for the exact target polymorph remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which is a structural-complexity trigger [SS1, SS4; Target structure]. The target contains only Ba as the cation, so a cation-radius-mismatch trigger is not supported, while the unit-cell atom count is not provided in CS/SS/TS and is therefore neutral due to insufficient evidence [CS/SS/TS]. The resulting score indicates low–medium amorphization risk, with crystallization more likely than glass formation but with possible polycrystalline or multiphase products [SS1, SS3, SS4].

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSeI is more likely to be a **metastable locally stable phase** than a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its supplied energy above hull is 0.1000 eV/atom, despite passing dynamic-stability screening [Target record]. CS supports the general feasibility of ternary chalcogenide-halide formation, while SS supports Ba–Se framework formation and a tetragonal Ba–halide/chalcogenide flux precedent [CS1, CS4, CS5, SS1, SS3, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is likely enhanced mass transport from BaI₂ flux or halide-mediated transport [SS3, CS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and iodine/selenium volatility, especially formation of BaI₂, BaSe, or other Ba-rich phases instead of the chiral P4₃ structure [SS1, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires optimization of halide activity, cooling rate, and stoichiometric compensation [CS1, SS3, SS4].
