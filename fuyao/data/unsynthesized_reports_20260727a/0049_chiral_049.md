### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
BaSeBr has **E_hull = 0.100 eV/atom**, so it is likely metastable rather than a clear ground-state phase [Target]. The score is still moderate because TS analogues with comparable or higher hull values are reported as synthesized, including Ba₃CrS₅ at 0.0969 eV/atom and Ba₃UMnSe₆ at 0.172 eV/atom [TS1, TS2]. CS/SS analogues mostly have lower hulls near 0–0.05 eV/atom, so they support the chemistry but do not fully remove the metastability concern [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids >1000°C and high pressure, while still requiring vacuum/Ar sealed-tube handling and volatile Se control [SS1, SS2, CS5]. Its kinetics are reasonable because Ba–Se and Ba–Br bond lengths/coordination are consistent with SS/CS analogues, but the chiral P3₂21 Se–Se-containing topology requires partial structural reconstruction [Target, SS1, SS2, CS1]. Insufficient evidence from TS for exact BaSeBr binary sealed-ampoule synthesis is treated neutral = 50 and not used as a negative.

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 benefits from reactive elemental/halogen precursors, as elemental Ba/Se and halogen routes are demonstrated in SS/CS analogues [SS3, CS3, CS5]. Its severity is higher than Route 1 because Ba metal and Br₂/Se vapor introduce handling and stoichiometry hazards [CS5, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether direct elemental reaction will favor BaSeBr over simpler BaSe/BaBr₂ or Ba–Se binaries [SS2, CS1; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has the best kinetic score because flux/halide-rich melts and slow cooling are repeatedly used to obtain ordered or crystalline chalcogenide/halide phases [CS1, SS1, SS5, TS2, TS4]. Its condition severity is reduced by flux processing complexity and possible flux separation, even though the temperature remains below 1000°C [TS2, TS4]. The uncertainty is that flux may stabilize competing halide-rich phases or incorporate alkali/chloride impurities unless a bromide/self-flux is used [TS2, TS4; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
CVT is supported only by the ErSeI chemically similar analogue, so it receives a lower F3 because Ba-containing vapor transport is not demonstrated [CS2]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for BaSeBr CVT is treated neutral = 50, not as a direct failure signal. The route is plausible for crystal growth but risky because Br transport can shift stoichiometry and Ba species may have low vapor transport efficiency [CS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 5**  
High-pressure synthesis is a fallback because the closest TS entry with similar hull, Ba₃CrS₅, required 5 GPa and 1200°C [TS1]. This route scores poorly on F2 because pressure equipment and quench processing are severe relative to sealed-ampoule SS/CS routes [TS1]. The uncertainty is high because CS/SS analogues suggest ambient sealed-tube synthesis may be sufficient, while TS1 is chemically less similar despite thermodynamic similarity [CS1, CS5, SS1, SS2, TS1].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation, Ba, so the cation-size mismatch trigger is absent [Target]. The target description includes corner- and edge-sharing motifs but not coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not activated [Target]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated neutral = 50, while the simple one-Ba/one-Se/one-Br site description supports low amorphization risk [Target; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSeBr is best interpreted as a **metastable but accessible** target because its E_hull is 0.100 eV/atom and it passed the supplied dynamic-stability screening [Target]. CS and SS analogues strongly support high-temperature sealed-ampoule Ba–chalcogenide/halide chemistry, especially with BaSe/BaX₂-type precursors and slow cooling [CS1, CS5, SS1, SS2, SS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of chemically matched BaSe/BaBr₂/Se precursor chemistry under sealed vacuum conditions [SS1, SS2, CS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target is theory-only and no exact BaSeBr/P3₂21 analogue is reported in CS/SS/TS [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Flux growth is nearly as attractive as the best route because it improves diffusion and ordering, but it adds impurity and stoichiometry risks [CS1, SS1, TS2, TS4].
