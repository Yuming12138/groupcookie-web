### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

[Target] TS is favorable because a very close Zn–Ir–B analogue at ~0.186 eV/atom is experimentally accessible, while CS/SS show related borides that are often on or near the hull. [CS-2; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1] Its kinetics are only moderate because elemental Ru/B require substantial diffusion, although Mg–Ru–B and Zn–Rh/Ir–B analogues demonstrate that long sealed reactions can overcome this barrier. [SS-2; CS-2; SS-1]

Route 2 has the same atmosphere and Zn-volatility penalties as Route 1, but the Zn-rich melt improves kinetic transport and crystal growth probability. [CS-1; SS-1; SS-4] The limiting uncertainty is composition control, because flux growth can bias Zn-rich competing phases and requires careful residual-Zn removal. [SS-1; CS-2; Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 4**

[CS-3; CS-4; TS-5; SS-1] SS provides insufficient evidence for a melt/anneal route to the Zn–Ru–B structural family, so SS is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor. [SS-1; SS-2]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ru3Zn3B2 is most likely a **metastable but accessible** phase rather than a clear ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.173 eV/atom but its phonon screen is stable. [Target] The CS and SS analogues strongly support high-temperature sealed synthesis of related Zn–Rh/Ir–B and Mg–Ru–B frameworks, while TS supports the idea that high-hull borides can still be experimentally realized. [CS-1; CS-2; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1] The most critical enabling factor is a sealed Zn-rich or Zn-buffered environment that maintains Zn chemical potential and improves mass transport. [CS-1; SS-1; SS-4] The most critical limiting factor is phase competition under narrow stoichiometry windows, which is repeatedly reported for Zn–Rh/Ir–B analogues. [CS-2; SS-3; SS-5; TS-1] Overall, the best practical attempt is Zn-rich self-flux growth followed by careful phase screening rather than an open melt route. [CS-1; SS-1; SS-4]
