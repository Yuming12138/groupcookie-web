## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported energy above hull is 0.030 eV atom⁻¹, placing it in the high-stability category specified by the rubric. This is consistent with thermodynamic analogues having E_hull values of 0.025–0.043 eV atom⁻¹ in TS-2, TS-3, and TS-5. However, CS and SS do not independently provide the target’s direct hull energy, so their direct evidence for F1 is neutral; TS is weighted more strongly because it contains the closest low-hull thermodynamic analogues.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because it avoids extreme temperature and pressure and requires only a sealed ampoule. The main deduction is for volatile sulfur, which motivates sealed operation [Reasonable Inference]; TS-3 provides direct precedent for evacuated ampoule synthesis of a Bi–S–halide compound. There is no strong CS/SS/TS evidence requiring a reactive gas or multistage reaction for this target-specific route.

### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate-high because sulfur is chemically active and the highest relevant cation melting point is low relative to refractory oxide synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Low-temperature formation of Sn–O–F and Te–O–F phases supports the feasibility of halide-containing solid-state reactions without very high temperatures (CS-4, CS-5). The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction: the target must simultaneously form a BiF₃ framework, S–S ribbons, and a chiral tetragonal ordering, which is not directly replicated in CS, SS, or TS [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized relative to Route 1 because autoclave synthesis introduces sealed pressure, sulfur speciation control, and a more complex isolation process [Reasonable Inference]. Hydrothermal Bi chalcogenide synthesis at 230 °C for 4 days with controlled cooling is directly demonstrated in TS-5. Aqueous fluoride crystallization from hot solutions is demonstrated in CS-1 and CS-3, but neither example contains the target Bi–S–F combination.

### F3 — Route 2

The liquid medium should substantially improve precursor contact and ion transport [Reasonable Inference], supporting a relatively high kinetic score. CS-1 and CS-3 show that fluorinated inorganic phases can crystallize from hot aqueous solutions during cooling, and TS-5 demonstrates hydrothermal crystallization of a Bi–chalcogen-containing phase. The uncertainty is that sulfur may form several solution species, and the target’s S–S ribbon may not be the favored product under aqueous redox conditions [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives the lowest F2 score because it requires vacuum or inert sealed operation, volatile sulfur, and likely a multistep temperature-gradient process [Reasonable Inference]. TS-4 directly supports the use of evacuated ampoules and temperature gradients for halide-containing CVT growth. SS-5 further shows that closed-ampoule sulfur/halogen chemistry can require careful handling and phase control.

### F3 — Route 3

The principal kinetic limitation is uncertain vapor transport of BiF₃. Although sulfur and metal halides can participate in vapor transport [Reasonable Inference], the supplied TS-4 example uses volatile chlorides rather than BiF₃ itself. Consequently, crystallization and composition-control scores are reduced because selective sulfur transport or transport-agent contamination could produce Bi–F, Bi–S, or mixed-halide competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

No cation-radius mismatch is present because the target contains only Bi as the cation, and the supplied data do not establish a unit-cell atom count above 40. The target description identifies a BiF₃ framework and an S ribbon, but does not explicitly prove the mixed sharing modes required by C3. Therefore, the formal rubric gives a low amorphization risk, although slow cooling remains advisable for chiral ordering [Reasonable Inference].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best regarded as a **near-ground-state candidate**, because its reported E_hull is only 0.030 eV atom⁻¹, although this value is target-provided rather than independently reproduced by CS/SS/TS. Thermodynamic analogues in TS-2, TS-3, and TS-5 strongly support the plausibility of low-energy Bi/halide/chalcogen chemistry, while CS-4 and CS-5 support low-temperature formation of mixed-anion fluoride frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is the combination of a BiF₃-like framework and a volatile sulfur component that can be reacted under sealed conditions (TS-3; CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is selective formation and ordering of the S–S ribbon without sulfur loss or competing Bi–S–F phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, a sealed-ampoule direct reaction is more credible than hydrothermal or CVT growth.
