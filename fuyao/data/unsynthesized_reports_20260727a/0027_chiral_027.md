# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹, which places it in the rubric’s near-hull 0.05–0.20 eV atom⁻¹ interval rather than the most favorable ≤0.05 eV atom⁻¹ interval (target record). CS analogues are generally at 0 eV atom⁻¹, while SS analogues span 0.004–0.089 eV atom⁻¹ and TS analogues span approximately 0.061–0.080 eV atom⁻¹. This supports a phase that is potentially accessible but may require kinetic control, slow ordering, or crystal-growth conditions rather than simple equilibrium annealing (CS; SS; TS).

## F2 — Route 1

Direct evacuated solid-state synthesis receives 65/100 because the temperature is moderate and no high pressure is required, but vacuum, iodine volatility, and Pb toxicity require substantial handling controls. These deductions are consistent with CS and SS, which use evacuated sealed ampoules for related Pb–chalcogenide and Bi–Se–I systems. The main uncertainty is whether iodine is retained sufficiently to form the target rather than Pb–S or Pb–I competing phases (CS; SS).

## F2 — Route 2

Iodine CVT receives 55/100 because it requires a sealed vacuum tube, a controlled temperature gradient, and at least a precursor-preparation plus transport-growth sequence. SS and TS directly support iodine-assisted CVT at 350–430 °C for a mixed S/I compound, and SS supports related BiSeI crystal growth by CVT. However, there is no direct CVT precedent for the Pb–S–I target composition, so the phase window is uncertain (SS; TS).

## F2 — Route 3

Melt/flux growth receives 55/100 because it combines vacuum sealing, iodine retention, multistage thermal treatment, and slow cooling. SS demonstrates that related heavy chalcogenide/halide crystals can be obtained by cooling a stoichiometric melt, while CS supports ampoule-based high-temperature reactions and annealing. The lack of a validated flux for Pb–S–I chemistry is a significant uncertainty (CS; SS).

## F3 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route has moderate precursor activity because it uses elemental starting materials, but Pb’s relatively low melting point favors diffusion and liquid-assisted reaction at the upper end of the proposed temperature range. CS reports successful Pb-containing chalcogenide synthesis between 300 and 800 °C, supporting a favorable diffusion score. Structural reconstruction remains only moderate because none of the CS materials reproduces the target’s simultaneous S–S bonding, Pb–I coordination, and chiral orthorhombic arrangement (CS).

## F3 — Route 2

CVT receives a moderate F3 score because iodine transport can enhance mass transfer at temperatures below those required for complete bulk diffusion. The Sn–Sb–S–I example in SS/TS uses iodine transport in a 430/350 °C gradient, and BiSeI is reported from a sealed CVT process near 600 °C (SS; TS). The uncertainty is not transport efficiency but selective nucleation of the target rather than a simpler binary iodide, sulfide, or nonchiral intermediate (SS; TS).

## F3 — Route 3

Melt or flux growth receives a moderate-to-good kinetic score because a liquid phase should improve cation and anion mobility. The SS Ag–Bi–Se–Cl example demonstrates crystal growth from a stoichiometric melt followed by slow cooling, and CS reports annealing-based phase development in sealed ampoules. Nevertheless, the target melting behavior, iodine retention, and compatible flux chemistry are not provided, so the score is provisional (CS; SS).

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target description does not provide a unit-cell atom count, explicit polyhedral-sharing classification, or a multi-cation radius mismatch suitable for the three specified tests. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for a non-neutral F4 assignment, and each unsupported factor is treated as neutral at 50. The target’s reported dynamic-stability pass is encouraging for local vibrational stability, but it does not resolve the rubric’s amorphization checks and is therefore not used to increase F4 (insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for these specific checks).

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best regarded as a **near-hull, potentially metastable or weakly metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.060 eV atom⁻¹ rather than zero, although the value remains within a commonly accessible near-hull range (target record; CS; SS; TS). The analogues provide meaningful support for sealed-ampoule Pb–chalcogenide synthesis and for iodine-assisted growth of structurally related chalcogenide/halide materials (CS; SS; TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of iodine-mediated mass transport or a low-melting Pb-containing reaction environment, which can promote ordering without requiring extreme temperature or pressure (CS; SS; TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: iodine loss, competing Pb–S/Pb–I phases, and the absence of a direct structural analogue may prevent formation of the chiral P2₁2₁2₁ phase (CS; SS; TS). Direct evacuated solid-state synthesis is therefore the recommended first experiment, followed by iodine CVT if phase-pure powder or seed crystals are not obtained.
