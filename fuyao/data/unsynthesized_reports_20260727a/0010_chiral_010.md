## SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The strongest evidence comes from SS/TS because both contain rhodium–sulfide solids and direct high-temperature synthesis of Rh–S frameworks, whereas CS is dominated by molecular chloride/nitride compounds and provides only indirect chloride-processing evidence [SS-1–SS-4; TS-1–TS-3; CS-1–CS-4]. The target’s predicted hull energy is 0 eV/atom and its source-paper validation reports dynamic stability, which supports feasibility but does not constitute experimental synthesis evidence [Target record].

### Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state synthesis

| Field | Details |

| **Reference basis** | Rh–S phases were prepared from pressed elemental mixtures in evacuated, sealed quartz tubes at 800–900 °C [SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2]. ## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an exact reported energy above hull of 0.0000 eV/atom and passed the cited dynamic-stability screening [Target record]. The TS set is consistent with this favorable thermodynamic environment: CuRh₂S₄ and Rh₃S₄ are at 0 eV/atom, while Rh₂S₃ is only approximately 0.0012 eV/atom above the ground state [TS-1–TS-3]. The uncertainty is that calculated stability does not establish the experimentally accessible reaction pathway or suppress competing phases [Target record; SS-4].

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives 65/100 because it uses a controlled vacuum environment and volatile sulfur/chloride chemistry, but avoids externally applied pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C [SS-1; SS-2; SS-4]. The main penalties arise from ampoule sealing, sulfur retention, and handling anhydrous RhCl₃ [SS-4; TS-3]. The exact severity remains uncertain because direct experimental conditions for RhS₂Cl are not reported [Target record].

### F3 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route is kinetically limited by refractory Rh and solid-state diffusion, which is consistent with the 800–900 °C and multi-day conditions used for Rh sulfides [SS-1; SS-2]. Partial structural inheritance from RhS₆ octahedra improves the score because Rh₂S₃, CuRh₂S₄, and Rh₃S₄ all contain Rh–S octahedral networks [SS-1; SS-2; SS-4; TS-1–TS-3]. The target’s Cl incorporation and chiral ordering are not demonstrated, so the structural-reconstruction score remains moderate **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives 45/100 because the closest compositionally relevant precedent operates at approximately 1047 °C under vacuum with a 30 K gradient and uses RhCl₃ [SS-4; TS-3]. The route also involves RhCl₃ preparation, careful ampoule sealing, and transport-zone control [SS-4; TS-3]. It does not require external high pressure, which prevents a larger deduction [TS-4; TS-5].

### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves precursor activity and mass transport relative to direct diffusion because RhCl₃ and sulfur participate in a vapor-mediated process [SS-4; TS-3]. The Rh₃S₄ precedent proves that a Rh–S–Cl charge can react and crystallize under these conditions, but it also reports unavoidable neighboring-phase formation, indicating difficult phase control [SS-4]. Consequently, the kinetics score is higher than Route 1 but not high enough to imply reliable target selectivity.

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is assigned 55/100 because the proposed lower-temperature flux process would avoid the high temperatures of Routes 1 and 2, but would still require controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur/chloride management, and multiple processing steps **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS supports mild chloride crystallization and improved crystal quality from excess chloride [CS-1; CS-3; CS-4]. However, CS provides no direct evidence for a condensed Rh–S–Cl flux reaction, so the score is uncertain [CS-1–CS-4].

### F3 — Route 3

A chloride flux should enhance precursor contact and ion transport relative to dense Rh powder **[Reasonable Inference]**. The chloride crystallization examples support the general ability of chloride-rich media to produce crystalline transition-metal compounds [CS-1; CS-3; CS-4]. However, the CS materials are molecular or zero-dimensional chloride complexes rather than extended chiral Rh–S networks, so structural reconstruction is the principal limitation [CS-1–CS-4].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target does not show an obvious cation-radius mismatch because only Rh is identified as the cation species **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its octahedral connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but no reported face sharing, so C3 is not triggered [Target structure description]. Unit-cell atom count is unavailable; therefore, **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for C2, and that check is treated as neutral at 50.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a thermodynamically stable ground-state phase rather than an intentionally metastable phase because its calculated energy above hull is exactly 0 eV/atom and it passed dynamic-stability screening [Target record]. SS and TS provide meaningful support through Rh₂S₃, CuRh₂S₄, and Rh₃S₄, which demonstrate stable Rh–S octahedral frameworks and successful synthesis from Rh/sulfur-containing charges [SS-1–SS-4; TS-1–TS-3]. The most important enabling factor is the close Rh–S structural and thermodynamic precedent, particularly the RhCl₃-assisted Rh–S–Cl CVT chemistry [SS-4; TS-3]. The most important limitation is phase selectivity: Rh₃S₄ synthesis already shows competing-phase formation, while the target additionally requires chlorine incorporation, S–S dimer ordering, and chiral P6₅22 crystallization [SS-4; Target structure description]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but experimentally unconfirmed, with Route 1 preferred for bulk screening and Route 2 preferred for crystal-growth attempts.
