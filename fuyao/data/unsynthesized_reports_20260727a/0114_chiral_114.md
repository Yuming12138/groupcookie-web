### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s supplied **0.159 eV/atom** hull energy places it in the metastable but plausible 65–85 rubric band, and the selected score of 75 reflects that it is not a ground-state prediction. The closest structural/thermodynamic parent Au₂Nb₃ has **E_hull = 0.1226 eV/atom** [SS1][TS1], while thermodynamic analogues include reported phases near **0.19–0.22 eV/atom** [TS3][TS4][TS5]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful hull-energy context, so no neutral 50 source adjustment is needed for F1.

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Condition severity is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C processing but still requires controlled inert/sealed conditions and long annealing [SS1][TS1]. Kinetics are the main penalty: the closest parent requires **700 h**, indicating slow ordering/diffusion [SS1][TS1]. The strong structural match to Au₂Nb₃ offsets this kinetic penalty because the target likely needs B incorporation into an already plausible Nb–Au framework [SS1][TS1][Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Arc melting is well supported for ternary borides and intermetallics under Ar [CS1][CS2][SS5], but the route is harsher because arc processing and boride heat treatments exceed 1000–1400 °C in analogues [CS2][SS3]. Kinetics improve through liquid-state mixing, but phase control remains uncertain because Au-containing intermetallics and boride analogues report foreign phases or metastability [CS4][SS5]. TS provides only indirect support for this route, but TS5 also supports arc-melt/heat-treatment behavior in a thermodynamic analogue [TS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Induction melting is supported by TS2 for Nb-rich intermetallics under purified He with long annealing [TS2]. The route is penalized because TS2 does not include B or Au, so CS and SS are insufficient direct evidence for induction melting of Nb–Au–B and are treated as neutral 50 signals for this route. Long homogenization remains a kinetic bottleneck because TS2 uses **800 °C for 1000 h** [TS2].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Thin-film severity is moderate because it avoids extreme bulk temperatures but requires high vacuum/Ar deposition [TS4]. TS4 supports access to metastable Nb-rich phases by sputtering, but CS and SS provide insufficient thin-film evidence and are treated as neutral 50 signals for Route 4. The main kinetic risk is composition control, because Nb–Au–B requires simultaneous control of refractory Nb, mobile Au, and B incorporation [TS4][Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the mixed-connectivity check is triggered because the target has corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedra and related Nb carbide/boride-like frameworks show complex shared polyhedra [SS2]. The unit-cell-size check is not triggered because the direct parent is only 20 atoms per reported cell, and the B-filled derivative is inferred to remain below 40 atoms [SS1][TS1][Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for F4 trigger evaluation and is treated as neutral 50 for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃Au₂B is most likely a **metastable but accessible** phase rather than a true ground-state compound because its supplied hull energy is **0.159 eV/atom**, while thermodynamic analogues show experimentally referenced materials in the ~0.12–0.22 eV/atom range [TS1][TS3][TS4][TS5]. The strongest synthesis support is the exact Nb–Au **P4₁32** parent framework Au₂Nb₃, which has matching Au–Au distances and similar Nb–Au connectivity [SS1][TS1]. The most important enabling factor is that B insertion/order may occur within an already known Nb–Au topology rather than requiring a wholly unrelated framework [SS1][TS1][Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is slow B/Nb/Au ordering kinetics, evidenced by the 700 h anneal for the closest parent and long anneals in related Nb systems [SS1][TS1][CS3]. Phase competition with the binary Au₂Nb₃ parent and other Nb–B/Nb–Au phases is the most likely practical failure mode [SS1][TS1][Reasonable Inference].
