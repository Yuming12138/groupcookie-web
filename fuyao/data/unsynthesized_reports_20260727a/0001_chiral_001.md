## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported DFT energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which supports a high F1 score of 95/100. Thermodynamic analogues also include several zero-hull compounds, including SbCrSe₃, CuBrSe₃, ZnSe₂, PbPSe₃, and CdPSe₃ [TS-1–TS-5]. The main uncertainty is that the target’s hull value is computational and does not establish experimental kinetic accessibility, polymorph selection, or chemical-potential compatibility with volatile chlorine [TS-1–TS-5; target validation data].

### F2 — Route 1

CVT requires controlled vacuum and a sealed transport tube, as shown for CuBrSe₃ [SS-3; TS-2]. The expected temperature is moderate, but volatile Se and AlCl₃ and selenium toxicity increase handling severity [SS-3; target safety note; Reasonable Inference]. The route is nominally single-step, so it avoids the repeated grinding/annealing penalty associated with some solid-state syntheses [SS-3; SS-2].

### F3 — Route 1

CVT receives a favorable crystallization score because CuBrSe₃ single crystals were obtained in a temperature gradient and iodine-assisted transport produced layered GaInS₃ crystals [SS-1; SS-3]. Its composition-control score is lower because the supplied sources do not describe Al–Se–Cl vapor equilibria, and the target requires chlorine retention together with Se–Se bond formation [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; target structure]. Thus, CVT is attractive for crystal growth but requires substantial transport-agent and temperature-gradient optimization [SS-1; SS-3; Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2

Evacuated solid-state synthesis is well precedented for layered selenides at 300–800 °C [SS-5; TS-4; TS-5]. However, analogues show long reaction times, repeated annealing, grinding, temperature gradients, and narrow phase-control windows [SS-2; SS-5; TS-1; TS-5]. Volatile Se and the proposed chloride source further increase the need for sealed processing [SS-3; target safety note; Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 2

Elemental Al and Se are simple precursors, but dense elemental powders can have lower effective reaction activity than solution or vapor precursors [SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. The relevant analogues often require days to months and repeated homogenization, indicating slow ordering and diffusion limitations [SS-2; SS-5; TS-1; TS-5]. Structural reconstruction is scored conservatively because none of the solid-state analogues reproduces the target’s chiral P2₁2₁2₁ structure and distorted AlSe₄Cl trigonal-bipyramidal environment [target structure; SS-2; SS-5].

### F2 — Route 3

The solvothermal reference operates at only 185–200 °C in sealed autoclaves for 5–10 days [CS-1]. This supports relatively mild thermal conditions, but AlCl₃ hydrolysis, selenium toxicity, and the need for a closed system impose substantial operational penalties [CS-1; target safety note; Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure equipment beyond a conventional autoclave is indicated by the analogue [CS-1].

### F3 — Route 3

The liquid-mediated environment should improve precursor mobility relative to dense solid-state mixtures, and layered In–Se products are obtained solvothermally [CS-1]. Nevertheless, the CS-1 example produces different dimensionalities depending on solvent and conditions, demonstrating that phase selectivity is not automatic [CS-1]. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for solvothermal synthesis of an Al–Se–Cl chiral sheet, so the structural-reconstruction score remains modest [insufficient evidence from SS/TS].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is not applicable [target structure]. The supplied data do not report the target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is neutral rather than positive [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The target description identifies corner-sharing Al polyhedra and Se–Se bonds but does not establish the simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity required for C3 [target structure].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a ground-state phase rather than a strongly metastable phase because its reported DFT energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although this does not guarantee experimental formation [target validation data]. The CS/SS/TS analogues provide good general support for layered metal–chalcogenide synthesis, especially through CVT of CuBrSe₃, iodine-assisted transport of GaInS₃, and evacuated solid-state synthesis of selenides [CS-1; SS-1; SS-3; SS-5; TS-1–TS-5]. The most important enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic driving force combined with demonstrated Se–Se/metal–Se–halide chemistry in related materials [target validation data; SS-3; TS-2]. The principal limiting factor is retention and precise control of chlorine during synthesis, compounded by the absence of a direct Al–Se–Cl experimental precedent [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]. Chirality and the specific P2₁2₁2₁ polymorph will also require direct structural verification because the analogues do not establish enantioselective or chiral phase formation [target structure; SS-1–SS-5].
