### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pd3BSb is best viewed as a near-hull metastable or marginal ground-state-accessible phase, because E_hull = 0.043 eV/atom is low but nonzero and the phase is theory-only in the provided validation [Target]. The CS/SS evidence strongly supports Pd–B framework formation because Pd2B and YPd7B2 share BPd6/Pd–B motifs and are experimentally synthesized [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1]. The TS evidence supports feasibility of Pd/Sb-containing intermetallic chemistry but also warns that antimonide phase control can require long anneals, flux growth, or repeated processing [TS3; TS4; TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the close Pd–B structural match to known Pd borides [CS1; SS1; SS2]. The most important limiting factor is ternary phase selection under Sb volatility and competing Pd–B/Pd–Sb equilibria [Reasonable Inference; CS5; TS3; TS4; TS5].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy (from CS/SS/TS) |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sb loss or off-stoichiometry during high-temperature processing [Reasonable Inference; CS5; TS3; TS4]. | High | Use sealed ampoule anneal, small Sb excess, slow heat-up through Sb volatilization regime, and mass-loss checks [CS5; TS3; TS4]. |
| 2 | All | Competing Pd–B and Pd–Sb phases may form because stable Pd2B and multiple Pd-pnictide/antimonide analogues exist [CS1; SS5; TS3; TS5]. | High | Use repeated remelting/annealing and PXRD-guided reannealing, following arc-melt and long-anneal analogue practice [CS1; SS2; TS1; TS4]. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Incomplete B dissolution or boron segregation during arc melting [Reasonable Inference; CS1; SS2]. | Medium | Use fine B or Pd2B master alloy, flip/remelt multiple times, and anneal near 850–900 °C [CS1; SS2; TS1]. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Slow solid-state ordering and diffusion through Pd2B/Pd/Sb powders [CS1; CS5; TS3; TS4]. | High | Use Pd2B master alloy, fine grinding, staged pre-reaction, long anneal, and optional regrind/reanneal [CS1; TS3; TS4]. |
| 5 | Route 3 | B incorporation into NaBr/KBr or Sb-assisted flux is unproven [CS1; TS5]. | High | Pre-alloy Pd–B before flux growth and treat flux growth as crystal-growth optimization rather than first-pass bulk synthesis [CS1; TS5]. |
| 6 | Route 4 | Sb evaporation and composition drift in levitated/high-temperature melt [Reasonable Inference; CS2; CS4; TS3]. | High | Minimize melt time, use slight Sb excess, track mass before/after melting, and post-anneal sealed [CS2; CS4; TS3]. |
| 7 | All | Silica contamination or reaction with B-containing mixtures may perturb phase formation [CS1; TS3]. | Medium | Use BN or alumina liners and avoid direct silica contact during high-temperature annealing [CS1; TS3]. |
| 8 | All | Novel chiral P2_13 ordering may have a narrow phase window because the target is not experimentally validated and similar antimonides can be phase-sensitive [Target; TS3; TS4; TS5]. | Medium | Run a small composition matrix around Pd3BSb, especially Sb ±1–3 mol% and B ±1–2 mol%, with repeated anneal/PXRD checks [Reasonable Inference; TS3; TS4; TS5]. |
