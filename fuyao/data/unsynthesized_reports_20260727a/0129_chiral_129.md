### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

#### Route 1: Sealed-Ampoule Direct Solid-State Reaction

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Closest Zn–Rh–B analogues were made from elemental Zn/Rh/B at 1200–1230 °C in BN crucibles sealed in Ta/quartz ampoules, and the exact 2:2:1 Zn–Ir–B analogue was made at 1100 °C in BN/Ta ampoules. [CS1; SS1; SS3] |
| Precursors | Zn metal, Rh powder, B powder, all high purity; slight Zn excess is recommended to compensate Zn loss. [CS1; SS3; Reasonable Inference] |
| Temperature program | Ramp ~50 °C/h → 1100–1230 °C → hold 50 h to 5 d → controlled cool 25–100 °C/h or quench as a screening variable. [CS1; SS3; TS1] |
| Atmosphere | Ar, sealed Ta ampoule with quartz secondary containment. [CS1; SS1; SS3] |
| Pressure | Ambient external pressure; sealed ampoule gives autogenous Zn vapor pressure. [CS1; SS3; Reasonable Inference] |
| Additional conditions | Use BN crucible, strict stoichiometry screening around Zn:Rh:B = 2:2:1, and compare quenched vs controlled-cooled products. [CS1; SS3; TS1] |
| Transferability to target | Strong chemical transferability because CS/SS contain synthesized Zn–Rh–B phases with similar Rh–Zn and Rh–B bonding; strong structural transferability because SS3 is Ir2Zn2B, the same 2:2:1 stoichiometry with a closely related noble-metal analogue. [CS1; SS1; SS3] |
| Estimated difficulty | Challenging, mainly from high temperature, sealed Zn handling, and reported phase-purity sensitivity in Zn–Rh/Ir–B analogues. [CS1; SS3; TS1] |

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Zn5Rh8B4 was reported under a flux-growth/metal-ampoule route at 1100 °C for 7 d, and Zn–Ir–B analogues required excess Zn removal with acid for single-crystal growth. [CS2; SS2; SS3] |

| Atmosphere | Ar-loaded sealed Ta or metal ampoule, preferably with BN crucible liner. [CS2; SS2; SS3] |

#### Route 3: Li-Assisted Flux Growth

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Li2Rh3B2 was synthesized from Rh/B with excess Li flux at 1000 °C, followed by dynamic-vacuum Li removal; this supports Li as a Rh–B flux but not specifically Zn2Rh2B. [SS4] |
| Precursors | Li metal flux, Zn metal, Rh powder, B powder. [SS4; Reasonable Inference] |
| Temperature program | Glovebox loading → heat at ~2 °C/min to ~1000 °C → hold ~61 h → slow cool ~1 °C/min → remove Li under dynamic vacuum or by controlled workup. [SS4] |
| Atmosphere | Ar glovebox loading, sealed Nb/Ta tube or equivalent, evacuated silica sheath; dynamic vacuum for Li removal. [SS4] |
| Pressure | Ambient external pressure during sealed-tube heating; dynamic vacuum during flux removal. [SS4] |

| Transferability to target | Limited-to-moderate because Li flux can dissolve Rh/B but may stabilize Li–Rh–B competing phases instead of Zn2Rh2B. [SS4; Reasonable Inference] |
| Estimated difficulty | Challenging, because Li is air-sensitive and adds contamination/phase-selection risk. [SS4; Reasonable Inference] |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is not predicted to be a ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.196 eV/atom, which is close to the 0.20 eV/atom upper boundary of the moderate synthesizability band. [Target] Dynamic stability from the no-imaginary-frequency screen supports a local minimum rather than a dynamically unstable structure. [Target] TS1 shows a related Zn–Ir–B boride with E_hull ≈ 0.186 eV/atom and reported high-temperature synthesis, so metastability at this scale is not automatically disqualifying. [TS1]

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
The direct solid-state route is penalized by 1100–1230 °C temperatures, sealed Ar/Ta ampoules, and multi-step ampoule handling. [CS1; SS3] Kinetics are only moderate because Rh/B are refractory and related reactions require long holds, but SS3 gives a close Zn2Ir2B analogue that improves structural confidence. [SS3; Reasonable Inference] Remaining uncertainty is high because CS1/TS1 report phase-purity problems in nearby Zn–Rh/Ir–B compositions. [CS1; TS1]

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
The Zn-rich flux route has the same sealed-ampoule and high-temperature penalties as Route 1, but molten Zn should improve mass transport and crystal growth. [CS2; SS3; Reasonable Inference] Its main kinetic advantage is that Zn is both a constituent and a flux, avoiding foreign-flux incorporation while improving reaction contact. [CS2; SS3; Reasonable Inference] The main uncertainty is stoichiometry control, because related phases show narrow windows and excess-Zn removal may be required. [CS1; SS3; TS1]

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Li flux is credible for Rh–B chemistry because SS4 synthesized Li2Rh3B2 at 1000 °C, but it is less directly transferable to Zn2Rh2B. [SS4] The route gains kinetic benefit from a reactive flux but loses score for glovebox handling, vacuum distillation, and possible Li–Rh–B impurity stabilization. [SS4] Insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports Li flux for Zn2Rh2B specifically, so those sources are neutral for this route-transferability factor. [CS1–CS5; TS1–TS5]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
No strong amorphization trigger is evident because the target is a compact intermetallic boride formula with no explicit >40-atom cell evidence and no explicit vertex+edge+face sharing in the target description. [Target] Structurally close crystalline Zn–Rh/Ir–B analogues support crystallization rather than glass formation. [CS1; SS3] The uncertainty is that the exact target cell multiplicity and polyhedral sharing topology are not fully specified, so C2/C3 are not scored above low risk. [Target; SS3; Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rh2Zn2B is most likely a metastable but dynamically stable phase, not a true ground state, because its E_hull is 0.196 eV/atom while phonons passed the no-imaginary-frequency screen. [Target] The synthesis outlook is meaningfully supported by known Zn–Rh–B compounds and an exact 2:2:1 Zn–Ir–B structural analogue made by sealed high-temperature reaction. [CS1; CS2; SS3] The most important enabling factor is that sealed Ta/BN ampoule chemistry already works for closely related Zn–Rh/Ir borides. [CS1; SS3] The most important limiting factor is phase competition in a narrow Zn–Rh–B composition window, especially under Zn-rich or high-temperature conditions. [CS1; TS1] Overall, the best first attempt is Zn-rich self-flux growth with careful stoichiometry and cooling-rate screening. [CS2; SS3; Reasonable Inference]
