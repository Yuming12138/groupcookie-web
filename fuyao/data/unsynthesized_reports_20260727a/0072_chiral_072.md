### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamics**  
The target is not predicted as a ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.150 eV/atom, but it lies in the synthesizable metastability window under the provided rubric (Target data). TS is the strongest thermodynamic analogue signal because TS1–TS4 cluster near 0.127–0.151 eV/atom and include synthesized phases, while SS mostly supports near-hull structural analogues (TS1–TS4; SS1–SS5). CS is chemically useful but thermodynamically mixed because CS1 has a high E_hull outlier, so CS is not weighted as strongly for F1 (CS1; CS2–CS5).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/sealed-ampoule handling, volatile sulfur chloride, multiple operations, and thallium toxicity (CS2; CS5; SS1; TS2; Target data; Reasonable Inference). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, which helps severity relative to high-temperature chalcogenide analogues (SS2; SS4; TS2). The remaining uncertainty is whether low-temperature sealed annealing can overcome phase competition without melt transport [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because reactive chloride/sulfur chloride precursors should be more active than refractory oxides, and Tl-only cation diffusion is not intrinsically severe [Reasonable Inference; CS5/SS1]. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor because no CS/SS/TS example directly forms the target chiral Tl–S–Cl network (insufficient exact-structure evidence from CS = neutral 50; insufficient exact-structure evidence from SS = neutral 50; insufficient exact-structure evidence from TS = neutral 50). The route receives a mid-high F3 because sealed Tl halide/chalcogenide reactions are repeatedly demonstrated (CS2; CS5; SS2; SS4; TS2).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has the same toxic/volatile/sealed-system penalties as Route 1, but it benefits from demonstrated Tl-halide melt-growth protocols (CS5; SS1; CS2). It avoids high pressure and can likely stay below the higher temperatures used for TlCoCl₃ or Tl chalcopyrites by using a TlCl-rich melt [Reasonable Inference from CS5/SS1; CS2; SS2; SS4]. The main severity uncertainty is sealed-ampoule pressure from sulfur chloride at growth temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2**  
Melt growth gives the best kinetic score because analogues show direct crystal growth from Tl-halide melts with controlled translation rates (CS5; SS1; CS2). Ion diffusion is favorable in a molten chloride-rich medium, and slow cooling improves ordering [Reasonable Inference; CS5/SS1]. The remaining kinetic penalty is structural novelty: no analogue proves that chiral TlSCl₂ with S–S units crystallizes directly from melt (Target data; insufficient exact-structure evidence from CS/SS/TS = neutral 50 for that subclaim).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 uses mild temperature and only autogenous pressure, supported by hydrothermal Tl₂PdCl₄ at 150 °C and solution chloride analogues (CS4; TS5; SS3; TS3). Deductions remain for sealed/dry handling, volatile sulfur chloride, multiple steps, and thallium toxicity (CS4; SS3; TS3; Target data; Reasonable Inference). The conflict is that CS4 supports aqueous chloride crystallization, while SS3/TS3 support dry chloride chemistry; dry solvothermal conditions are weighted more heavily for S₂Cl₂ compatibility [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Precursor activity and ion mobility are strong because solvothermal/solution routes allow low-temperature crystallization in related halide systems (CS4; TS5; SS3; TS3). The kinetic risk is not diffusion but assembly of the correct extended Tl–S–Cl structure instead of molecular chloride or hydrated phases (CS4; TS5; SS3; TS3; Target data). No source provides direct nucleation or growth kinetics for TlSCl₂; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target crystallization is neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is mild in temperature and pressure because slow evaporation and dry solution routes occur at room temperature to reflux (CS1; SS5; SS3; TS3). It still receives penalties for drybox/inert handling, volatile reagents/solvents, multistep crystallization, and thallium toxicity (SS3; TS3; Target data; Reasonable Inference). The main uncertainty is whether mild solution conditions can maintain the target redox state and avoid solvated side products [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4**  
Solution mobility is excellent, and halide analogues can crystallize by slow evaporation (CS1; SS5; TS5). Structural reconstruction is poor-to-moderate because the closest solution examples are simple halides or molecular clusters, not a polymeric chiral TlSCl₂ lattice (CS1; SS5; SS3; TS3; Target data). Therefore F3 is below Route 2 despite easier diffusion.

**F2 — Route 5**  
Route 5 is strongly penalized for >5 GPa pressure because TS4 uses 60–89 kbar high-pressure synthesis (TS4). It also requires sealed containment of volatile S/Cl reagents, multiple capsule/press steps, and toxic Tl handling (TS4; Target data; Reasonable Inference). CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure Tl–S–Cl synthesis, so their route-specific source scores are neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 5**  
High pressure is kinetically useful for stabilizing dense disulfide motifs, and TS4 directly supports S–S-containing sulfide formation under pressure (TS4). The route is still less attractive kinetically than melt growth because capsule reactions have limited precursor mobility and require reconstruction from compacted solids [Reasonable Inference; TS4]. Exact transfer to TlSCl₂ is uncertain because the pressure analogue is ZnS₂, not a Tl chloride sulfide (TS4; Target data).

**F4 — Amorphization**  
Only one F4 trigger is counted because the target has one cation type and likely fewer than 40 atoms per unit cell, but it does contain mixed corner/edge polyhedral connectivity (Target data; Reasonable Inference). Mixed connectivity can complicate ordering, yet crystallized analogues with edge- or corner-sharing halide/chalcogenide polyhedra reduce the amorphization concern (CS1; SS1; TS1; TS4). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct target-cell evidence for C1/C2, so those source-specific subchecks are neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlSCl₂ is best interpreted as a **metastable but plausible** phase rather than a ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.150 eV/atom while its dynamic stability screen passed (Target data). The analogue set supports synthesis moderately well: CS/SS strongly support Tl-halide/chalcogenide sealed-ampoule and melt routes, while TS supports the idea that ~0.15 eV/atom phases and S–S-containing sulfides can be experimentally accessible (CS2; CS5; SS1; SS2; SS4; TS1–TS4). The most important enabling factor is likely a chloride-rich sealed melt, which can provide high ion mobility and controlled crystallization as shown for TlCuCl₃ and TlCoCl₃ (CS5; SS1; CS2). The most important limiting factor is stoichiometric/redox control of volatile sulfur chloride species needed to form the target S–S/Tl–Cl framework [Reasonable Inference from Target data]. Overall, the best route is a carefully staged TlCl–S₂Cl₂ Bridgman or slow-cooling sealed-ampoule melt growth.
