### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**

The target’s E_hull of 0.023 eV/atom falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so the thermodynamic score is high; TS supports this because several thermodynamically similar materials are stable or near-hull, including Zn5Rh8B4, PdLi, and Ce6Pd13Cd4 [TS]. CS and SS include closest Rh–Zn–B analogues at E_hull 0.0 and 0.0055 eV/atom, which supports the plausibility of stable Rh–metal–B frameworks [CS][SS]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and Cd3BRh3 itself is not experimentally validated in CS/SS/TS, so near-hull status may still correspond to metastability [CS][SS][TS].

**F2 — Route 1 Direct Solid-State**

Route 1 inherits high-temperature sealed-ampoule severity from Rh7Zn5B3, which used 1200–1230 °C, Ar, BN crucibles, Ta ampoules, and quartz ampoules [CS][SS]. The route is penalized for Cd volatility/toxicity by [Reasonable Inference] from Cd-containing sealed intermetallic syntheses and the target safety note, while CS/SS directly support sealed high-temperature boride synthesis [CS][SS][TS]. Insufficient evidence from TS for an exact Cd–Rh–B direct solid-state protocol, so TS contributes neutral-to-supportive thermodynamic context rather than direct procedure evidence [TS].

**F3 — Route 1 Direct Solid-State**

Elemental Rh and B imply slow kinetics by [Reasonable Inference], and the closest Rh–B analogues require long holds of 5–7 days or 120 h, supporting a moderate-low kinetic score [CS][SS][TS]. Structural transfer is moderately favorable because Rh–Zn–B analogues contain Rh–B bonds and metal-rich coordination networks similar to the target, but space group and Cd substitution differ [CS][SS]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a demonstrated Cd3BRh3 ordering pathway, so reconstruction risk remains significant.

**F2 — Route 2 Flux-Assisted Growth**

Route 2 uses a 1100 °C, 7-day sealed metal ampoule / BN crucible / Ar flux analogue, which is severe but less thermally extreme than 1200–1230 °C direct solid-state conditions [CS][SS][TS]. It is still penalized for sealed volatile Cd, controlled atmosphere, multi-step flux handling, and Cd toxicity by [Reasonable Inference] grounded in Cd-containing analogue handling and target safety [CS][SS][TS]. CS/SS/TS provide useful signal for flux/sealed growth; none provide an exact Cd self-flux recipe, so that part is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 Flux-Assisted Growth**

Flux growth improves precursor transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, and the closest TS analogue Zn5Rh8B4 was made by sealed flux/ampoule synthesis at 1100 °C for 7 days [CS][SS][TS]. This supports a higher diffusion and precursor-activity score than Route 1, because liquid-mediated transport can overcome Rh/B solid diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference][CS][SS][TS]. The remaining uncertainty is Cd-rich melt chemistry and possible competing Cd–Rh or Cd–B phases, which are not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 3 Arc / Induction Melting + Annealing**

Cd intermetallic analogues support sealed melting plus annealing, including Pt2CdZn with pre-anneal/melt/homogenization and Ce6Pd13Cd4 with induction melting plus long annealing [CS][SS][TS]. This route receives moderate severity because it avoids high pressure but still requires high temperature, sealed containment, and multi-step annealing [CS][SS][TS]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for melt stability of Rh–Cd–B specifically, so Cd loss and Rh–B segregation remain inferred risks [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 Arc / Induction Melting + Annealing**

Melting can improve homogenization, and Cd intermetallic analogues show that Cd-bearing metallic phases can be produced by melt/anneal routes [CS][SS][TS]. However, Rh–B framework formation after melting likely requires ordering during annealing, and direct structural analogy is weaker than Route 2 because Cd intermetallic analogues lack boron [CS][SS][TS]. Therefore kinetics are moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 4 High-Pressure High-Temperature**

TS includes a high-pressure Pt–Hg analogue route at 20–60 kb and up to 1600 °C, which strongly penalizes condition severity [TS]. CS and SS closest boride analogues do not require high pressure, so high pressure is not justified as a first-line route [CS][SS]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for high-pressure Rh–Cd–B synthesis; this route is retained only as a fallback [CS][SS][TS].

**F3 — Route 4 High-Pressure High-Temperature**

High pressure may stabilize dense metallic phases, but the TS high-pressure analogue is chemically/structurally remote from Cd3BRh3, so precursor activity and structural reconstruction scores remain low [TS]. Short high-pressure dwell times may not fully equilibrate refractory Rh/B mixtures [Reasonable Inference][TS]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that HPHT improves this exact boride’s formation over sealed ampoule methods.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cd3BRh3 is most likely a near-hull metastable or low-energy synthesizable phase rather than a proven ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.023 eV/atom and the target is theory-only [TS]. CS and SS strongly support the existence of related Rh–metal–B frameworks through experimentally synthesized Rh–Zn–B analogues, while TS supports near-hull thermodynamic plausibility through Zn5Rh8B4 and other low-E_hull metallic analogues [CS][SS][TS]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed high-temperature metal/boride chemistry using Ta/BN containment, which is repeatedly demonstrated in the closest analogues [CS][SS][TS]. The most critical limiting factor is Cd management, because Cd is toxic and volatile at the proposed temperatures and may cause off-stoichiometry or competing Cd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference][CS][SS][TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry control and iterative phase-purity optimization [CS][SS][TS].
