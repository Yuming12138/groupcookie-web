### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.180 eV/atom, so it is more likely metastable than a ground-state compound under the scoring rubric [Target]. TS is strongly supportive because Fe2Ga2S5 analogues near 0.18 eV/atom were experimentally accessed by flux/CVT methods [TS1][TS2]. CS/SS are supportive for general synthesizeability of In/chalcohalide chemistry, but they mostly contain lower-hull analogues and therefore do not fully remove metastability risk [CS3][CS4][CS5][SS1][SS2].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which keeps severity moderate [SS2]. The main deductions are vacuum/dry sealed-ampoule operation, volatile InBr3/S species, and air-sensitive bromide handling [SS1][SS2][Reasonable Inference]. TS has insufficient route-specific severity evidence for this exact In–S–Br anneal, so TS is treated as neutral 50 for this route factor.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetics are favorable relative to refractory oxide synthesis because In–S–Br analogues form near 500 °C and InBr3 can assist transport [SS2][Reasonable Inference]. The penalty is structural/phase control because SS2 reports inhomogeneous products, by-products, and defect issues in related In5Ch5X materials [SS2]. This balance supports a moderate-good F3 rather than an excellent one [SS2].

**F2 — Route 2**  
CVT requires vacuum, sealed ampoules, volatile transport species, and potentially toxic iodine, so the condition-severity score is lower than Route 1 [CS3][CS5][SS4]. The temperature itself is not extreme for the proposed In–S–Br adaptation because SS4 uses 600–550 °C gradients [SS4]. Exact InSBr2 CVT evidence is absent, so transferability uncertainty remains [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2**  
CVT improves kinetics by moving material through vapor-phase transport and has produced In/Ga/S and In/Sb/S single crystals [CS3][CS5]. The structural-reconstruction score is limited because retaining the exact Br:S ratio in a volatile gradient is uncertain [SS1][Reasonable Inference]. TS2 supports CVT feasibility for metastable thermodynamic analogues but not exact chemistry [TS2].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth avoids high pressure and >1000 °C in the adapted program, but it still requires sealed evacuated tubes, volatile bromide/sulfur species, and multi-step flux workup [TS1][TS3][TS5][Reasonable Inference]. Direct CS/SS flux evidence for InSBr2 is insufficient, so those sources are neutral 50 for Route 3 severity. The main practical penalty is chemical handling rather than temperature [SS1][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux growth receives a high kinetic score because salt fluxes enabled difficult thermodynamic analogues and reduce solid diffusion constraints [TS1][TS3][TS5]. The structural score is limited because chalcohalide analogues show competing phases and failed family members, meaning the flux may stabilize alternatives [SS2][SS4]. Overall Route 3 is kinetically attractive but less directly supported than Route 1 [SS2][TS1].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Melt/quench screening has moderate severity because the temperature is comparable to CS2 at 550 °C and no high pressure is needed [CS2]. It still requires evacuated ampoules and volatile halide/chalcogen containment [CS2][Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct melt-route evidence for InSBr2, so SS/TS are neutral 50 for this route-specific severity.

**F3 — Route 4**  
The melt route has strong precursor mixing and diffusion advantages, but CS2 shows that chalcogen-halide phases can have narrow cooling-rate windows and competing products [CS2]. The target’s 0.18 eV/atom metastability increases the chance that quenching is needed to trap it [Target][Reasonable Inference]. This makes Route 4 useful for screening but weaker as a reliable preparative route [CS2].

**F2 — Route 5**  
Solvothermal conditions are mild in temperature, with CS1 using 185–200 °C autoclave synthesis [CS1]. The deductions come from NH3/amine chemistry, sealed autoclave operation, and bromide/amine handling hazards [CS1][SS1][Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient solvothermal evidence for the exact target and are neutral 50 for this route factor.

**F3 — Route 5**  
Solvothermal kinetics can be excellent because solution chemistry activates In/chalcogen reactions at low temperature [CS1]. The structural-reconstruction score is very low because CS1 produced ammonium/organic-associated indium selenide products, not an anhydrous In–S–Br phase [CS1]. Route 5 is therefore best treated as exploratory, not primary [CS1][Reasonable Inference].
