# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV/atom and passed the source paper’s dynamic-stability screening, giving strong target-specific evidence for a stable calculated structure. CS, SS, and TS references are also mostly at or very near their calculated ground states, including CS1, CS4, CS5, SS1/SS2, and TS1/TS2 (CS1; CS4; CS5; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated stability does not guarantee favorable reaction kinetics, phase selectivity, or experimental isolation.

## F2 — Route 1

CVT requires a controlled evacuated/sealed environment and volatile Br₂/Se species, producing deductions under the supplied rubric (CS1; CS5). The operating temperatures are moderate and similar to successful Se/halide transport syntheses, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS1; CS5; SS4). The main uncertainty is whether Br₂ transport will favor the desired ZrSe₃Br₂ composition rather than zirconium bromides or binary zirconium selenides. [Reasonable Inference]

## F2 — Route 2

The direct solid-state route inherits the vacuum, sealed-tube, volatile-precursor, and toxicity penalties from the rubric (CS4; SS2; TS2). It may also require repeated annealing or grinding because structurally related chalcogenide syntheses report extended treatments and difficult phase control (SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The uncertainty is whether ZrSe₂/ZrBr₄ will react more readily than the NbSe₂/NbCl₄ analogue or instead remain kinetically trapped.

## F2 — Route 3

The melt/quench route uses a sealed evacuated ampoule and volatile Se/Br chemistry, giving the same principal F2 deductions as CVT (TS5). It avoids very long solid-state annealing, but rapid cooling must be controlled because the Cu–Se–Br precedent shows that cooling rate can determine the product phase (TS5). Evidence for the exact Zr phase is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so this route is ranked below CVT despite its potentially favorable kinetics.

## F3 — Route 1

CVT receives the highest F3 because direct crystal growth from a temperature gradient is demonstrated in Nb–Se–Br and Cu–Se–Br systems (CS1; SS4; TS3). Br₂ and Se provide transport-capable vapor species, but zirconium itself has limited vapor availability, so precursor vapor availability is only moderate. Composition control is reduced because both Se and Br are volatile and the target contains a nontrivial Se:Br ratio; this is [Reasonable Inference] from the transport chemistry.

## F3 — Route 2

The direct solid-state route has the weakest kinetics because it relies on diffusion between a refractory Zr–Se precursor and a volatile Zr–Br precursor. Related reactions frequently use 5–15 days or longer, and some structurally related materials require repeated annealing or exhibit inhomogeneity (CS4; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The target’s mixed ribbon/sheet topology also implies substantial structural reconstruction, although the preassembled Zr–Se and Zr–Br components may reduce the reconstruction burden relative to elemental synthesis. [Reasonable Inference]

## F3 — Route 3

A halide-containing melt or partially molten phase should improve mass transport relative to a dry solid-state mixture; this is a physicochemical extrapolation from the successful CuBr/Se reaction and its sensitivity to cooling rate (TS5). The route receives only an intermediate structural-reconstruction score because it is unknown whether the target structure nucleates directly from the melt. The principal uncertainty is that the Cu chemistry may not transfer to Zr, whose stronger metal–chalcogen/halide interactions could shift the phase field. [Reasonable Inference]

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

No confirmed cation-size mismatch trigger exists because the target contains only Zr as the principal cation. Unit-cell size and explicit coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra are not supplied; therefore, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2 and C3, and those sources are scored neutral for those checks. Although SS2 and the target description indicate complex 2D connectivity and multiple coordination environments, this is not enough to formally trigger C3 under the stated criterion (SS2; target structure description).
