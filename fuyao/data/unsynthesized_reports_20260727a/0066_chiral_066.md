### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:**  
The target’s 0.140 eV/atom hull value is metastable but within the 65–85 scoring band, and TS analogues with comparable hull values are reported as synthesized phases. (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5) CS and SS analogues are mostly on-hull or near-hull, which supports chemical/structural plausibility but is less target-specific than TS. (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5) The remaining uncertainty is that TS similarity does not prove the exact SbO₂Br polymorph is a ground-state phase. (TS1–TS5)

**F2 — Route 1:**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and GPa pressure because the closest sealed-tube analogues operate at 140–400 °C. (CS1, CS3, CS5) The deductions come from controlled dry/Ar or sealed conditions, volatile Br₂, and hazardous halide handling. (CS3, CS5, TS5) Insufficient evidence from SS for bromide sealed-tube severity is treated as neutral = 50.

**F2 — Route 2:**  
Route 2 has the mildest thermal profile because acidic/hydrothermal analogues crystallize at 70–280 °C. (CS2, CS4, TS3) Deductions are assigned for sealed volatile/corrosive bromide chemistry, multi-step hydrolysis/digestion/washing, and toxic/air-sensitive halide precursors. (CS4, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]) Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal bromide conditions is treated as neutral = 50.

**F2 — Route 3:**  
Route 3 stays below 1000 °C based on the TS5 CVT analogue, but it requires sealed Br₂ transport, reactive atmosphere control, volatile precursors, and multi-step gradient processing. (TS5) The condition score is therefore lower than Routes 1–2 despite no high-pressure requirement. (TS5) Insufficient evidence from SS for CVT severity is treated as neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 1:**  
Solid-state bromination benefits from reactive Br₂, but Sb₂O₅ remains an oxide feedstock and the exact Sb–O–Br framework has no direct analogue. (CS5; [Reasonable Inference]) CS5 reports small crystals and amorphous material, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity reduce the score. (CS5)

**F3 — Route 2:**  
Solution-mediated hydrothermal chemistry gives high precursor activity and avoids long-range cation diffusion, as shown by low-temperature oxyhalide analogues. (CS2, CS4, TS3) The kinetic penalty is phase selection: CS2 reports the Sb oxychloride as a by-product, CS4 reports single-crystal growth failure, and TS3 reports PbCl₂ by-product formation. (CS2, CS4, TS3)

**F3 — Route 3:**  
CVT provides a plausible crystal-growth pathway because TS5 forms oxide-bromide crystals under Br₂ transport. (TS5) The score is limited by composition control because TS5 reports an inseparable second phase and CS5 reports amorphous material in a Br₂ oxide-bromide system. (TS5, CS5)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:**  
The formal trigger count is zero because there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch, no clear >40-atom unit-cell evidence, and no target evidence for simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity. [Reasonable Inference] The score is not raised above 90 because chemically related oxyhalide systems show glass formation or amorphous by-products. (CS3, CS5) Remaining uncertainty is that the target CIF-derived cell size and connectivity were not independently available from CS/SS/TS, so C2 and C3 include neutral source treatment where evidence is insufficient.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SbO₂Br is best viewed as a metastable but experimentally plausible phase because its 0.140 eV/atom hull value is close to synthesized TS analogues in the ~0.14–0.18 eV/atom range. (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5) CS analogues support oxyhalide synthesis under sealed-tube and acidic hydrolysis conditions, while SS analogues support the stability of Sb–O polyhedral networks but provide little direct bromide-route evidence. (CS2, CS4, CS5, SS1–SS5) The key enabling factor is low-to-moderate-temperature halide chemistry, which can preserve volatile bromine while allowing oxyhalide framework formation. (CS2, CS4, CS5, TS3) The key limiting factor is phase control, especially maintaining Sb(V), Br stoichiometry, and crystallinity without forming Sb oxides, lower oxybromides, amorphous material, or mixed phases. (CS2, CS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the recommended first attempt is Route 2, followed by Route 1 if solution chemistry over-hydrolyzes the product. (CS2, CS4, CS5)
