### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull of 0.1500 eV/atom places it in the 65–85 scoring band, so it is better viewed as metastable rather than a ground-state phase (Target). TS analogues show that phases at similar hull energies can be isolated, including SrCrF4 at 0.1418 eV/atom and ZrFeF6 at 0.1737 eV/atom (TS3, TS1). The main uncertainty is that TS2 reports a 0.2005 eV/atom YF3 phase as non-reproducible, so nonzero hull tolerance is not by itself enough to guarantee robust synthesis (TS2).

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires sealed ampoule handling, flux processing, and dry fluoride/sulfide practice, giving F2 = 70 (SS5, CS5, CS2, [Reasonable Inference]). Its kinetics are the best supported because SS5 demonstrates phase-pure rare-earth fluoride sulfides at 850 °C/7 d using NaCl flux, and ScF3 analogues support compatible Sc–F local geometry (SS5, SS1, SS2). The main kinetic uncertainty is that ScSF2 is F-rich and contains an S–S bond, so the LuSF analogue requires compositional and structural extrapolation (SS5, Target, [Reasonable Inference]).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Route 2 has manageable temperature severity but loses points for controlled sealed-tube handling, volatile sulfur/fluoride retention, and dry precursor requirements (CS5, CS2, TS3, [Reasonable Inference]). Its kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because no flux-assisted transport is present, and formation from ScF3/Sc2S3/S likely requires slower solid-state reconstruction (SS5, CS5, TS3, [Reasonable Inference]). The remaining uncertainty is phase purity, since direct sealed fluoride analogues exist but direct Sc–S–F sealed-tube evidence is insufficient from CS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** Route 3 has low thermal severity because ScF3 and fluoride hydrothermal analogues form at 150–230 °C, but autoclave operation, HF/NH4HF2, and sulfur-source hazards reduce F2 to 70 (SS2, CS3, TS5). Kinetically, soluble Sc/F precursors are favorable and diffusion is easy, but direct hydrothermal Sc–S–F crystallization has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and likely competes with ScF3 or oxyfluorides (SS2, CS3, [Reasonable Inference]). Therefore Route 3 is useful as a screening route but less reliable than sealed flux growth (SS5, SS2, CS3).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent (Target). The target description does not indicate >40 atoms per unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so no F4 triggers are assigned, though the exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided (Target, [Reasonable Inference]). CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target amorphization, so their F4 source signals are neutral 50, while SS provides only partial local-structure support through ScF3 and LuSF (SS1, SS2, SS5; insufficient evidence from CS/TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScSF2 is most likely a **metastable but dynamically plausible** phase, because its E_hull is 0.1500 eV/atom while the target passed the no-imaginary-frequency screening (Target). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS5, where rare-earth fluoride sulfides are obtained as pure single phases in sealed Ta ampoules with NaCl flux, and from SS1/SS2, where Sc–F distances closely match the target Sc–F environment (SS5, SS1, SS2, Target). TS1 and TS3 show that nonzero-hull fluoride phases near the target’s thermodynamic range can be experimentally accessed, but TS2 shows that reproducibility can degrade near ~0.20 eV/atom (TS1, TS3, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is sealed flux growth, which simultaneously retains volatile S/F components and improves diffusion/crystal growth (SS5, CS5, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity for a novel F-rich Sc–S–F phase with S–S bonding, since no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports ScSF2 or an isostructural analogue (Target, SS5, [Reasonable Inference]).
