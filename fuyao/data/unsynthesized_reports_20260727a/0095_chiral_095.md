### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom, which falls in the 90–100 score bin and is further supported by the supplied no-imaginary-frequency screening. (Target) CS/SS analogues include many hull-stable phases, while TS analogues include low-energy metastable Pt-rich intermetallics, so the target is thermodynamically plausible but not proven experimentally. (CS1, CS3–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5)

**Route 1 F2/F3.** Sealed solid-state synthesis is chemically well supported by noble-metal borides, but the route inherits >1000 °C temperatures, inert/sealed ampoules, long annealing, and Cd toxicity/volatility risk. (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, SS1–SS3, Target) Kinetics score low because elemental Pt and B are refractory and analogues often need long holds or repeated treatment. (CS1, CS2, CS5, SS2, SS3)

**Route 2 F2/F3.** Cd-rich flux/off-stoichiometric growth reduces effective diffusion severity and is supported indirectly by excess-metal or nonstoichiometric boride examples, but TS provides insufficient direct flux evidence and is neutral 50 for this route. (CS4, CS5, SS2, SS3) The limiting uncertainty is whether Cd-rich liquid can dissolve/react Pt and B sufficiently without forming competing Cd–Pt binaries. (SS1, SS5; [Reasonable Inference])

**Route 3 F2/F3.** Arc melting has strong precedent for Pt–Cu–B and Pt-rich intermetallics, so it scores well kinetically, but Cd makes the condition severity poor because direct high-temperature Cd exposure is risky. (CS3, SS4, TS3–TS5, Target) The recommended adaptation—Pt–B prealloy first, then Cd anneal—is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

**Route 4 F2/F3.** High pressure is supported by structurally/thermodynamically similar Pt₃Cd/Pt₃Hg-type motifs, but those references are not direct Cd–Pt–B boride syntheses. (SS5, TS1) It may help retain Cd and trap metastable order, but equipment severity and short diffusion times lower both F2 and F3; CS gives insufficient high-pressure evidence and is neutral 50. (SS5, TS1)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** Mixed corner/edge/face connectivity is present in the target and in several crystalline analogues, so it triggers complexity but does not imply unavoidable amorphization. (Target, CS1, CS5, SS1, SS4, SS5) Size mismatch is treated as a [Reasonable Inference] trigger, while unit-cell size is likely not >40 atoms; direct CS/SS/TS evidence for ionic radii and exact target cell count is insufficient and neutral 50 for those subchecks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt₃Cd₂B is best viewed as a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a confirmed ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom and it is theory-only. (Target) CS and SS analogues strongly support that Pt/Ir–B networks and Cd/Pt cuboctahedral motifs can crystallize under sealed high-temperature or annealed conditions. (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5) TS analogues support low-energy Pt-rich intermetallic metastability but are less chemically specific to borides. (TS1–TS5) The enabling factor is the strong precedent for elemental sealed-tube noble-metal boride synthesis and Pt–Cd structural compatibility. (CS1–CS5, SS1, SS5) The limiting factor is Cd management—toxicity, volatility, and stoichiometry control—combined with slow Pt/B ordering kinetics. (Target, SS1, CS1, CS2, CS5)
