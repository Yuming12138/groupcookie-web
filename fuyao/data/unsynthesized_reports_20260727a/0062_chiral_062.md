## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.130 eV/atom places NbSeBr2 in the metastable but commonly synthesizable 0.05–0.20 eV/atom band (Target). TS analogues with similar E_hull values, including Ta4S9Br8 at 0.1319 eV/atom and other synthesized phases up to ~0.18 eV/atom, support a score in the upper part of the metastable range (TS1–TS5). CS and SS analogues are mostly at or near the hull, showing that Nb–chalcogen–halide and edge-sharing halide frameworks are not inherently thermodynamically implausible (CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target composition is marked theory-only and absent from OQMD in the source-paper validation, so direct experimental ground-state confirmation is absent (Target).

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, because the relevant CVT analogues operate at 400–515 °C in sealed tubes (CS1, CS5, TS1). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Br2, and toxic/volatile Se/Br handling [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS5, TS1]. CS1 makes this route look operationally straightforward, while CS5 indicates longer transport times and phase-control demands; this conflict is reflected by a moderate rather than easy F2 score (CS1, CS5).

### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics benefit from volatile halogen transport because NbSeBr3 crystals form from elemental Nb + Se + Br2 at only 500–460 °C (CS1). The kinetics are still limited by refractory Nb and the need to select a different NbSeBr2 sheet topology rather than the known NbSeBr3 ribbon topology [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS1, CS5]. The main uncertainty is whether Br/Se chemical potentials can stabilize the chiral P3₁ sheet rather than known 1D or non-chiral Nb–Se–halide phases (CS1, CS5, SS1).

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures similar to 500–550 °C chloride analogues and avoids high pressure (SS3, SS4). It still requires sealed evacuated tubes and air/moisture-sensitive Nb-halide handling, as structurally similar NbBr4 chemistry requires anhydrous/N2 conditions (SS2). The severity score is not lower because the target-forming reaction can be a single sealed-ampoule anneal if NbSe2 and NbBr4 are pre-obtained [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS3].

### F3 — Route 2

Binary precursors should improve local chemical activity because Nb–Se and Nb–Br bonds are already present [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS3]. However, SS3 requires 2–3 weeks and SS4 reports side-product/inhomogeneity issues, so ordering and phase selection are slow and uncertain (SS3, SS4). The route is especially exposed to precursor reactivity mismatch because NbSe2/NbBr4 must reconstruct into chiral edge-sharing NbSeBr2 sheets [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS3, Target].

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is not high-temperature or high-pressure under the recommended bromide-flux adaptation, because the closest bromide/molten-salt analogues use ~450–500 °C sealed ampoules (CS2, CS4). Its severity is reduced by moderate temperature but increased by sealed Br2 chemistry, optional Cd toxicity, and flux-separation steps [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS4, TS5]. SS gives insufficient evidence for a flux route, so SS is neutral at 50 for this factor.

### F3 — Route 3

Flux/self-flux chemistry can improve kinetics by increasing ion mobility and crystal growth, as shown by bromide molten-salt growth and halide flux growth analogues (CS4, TS5). The kinetic score is higher than dry solid-state because the liquid/halide medium should reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS4, TS5]. The uncertainty is phase purity, because CS2 obtains polymorphs simultaneously and TS5 notes difficult crystallization/narrow composition ranges (CS2, TS5).

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a single cation species, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger C1 (Target). The target description implies a unit cell likely below 40 atoms and only edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered [Reasonable Inference from Target]. CS/SS/TS provide useful analog connectivity context but insufficient exact target CIF atom-count evidence, so those source-specific F4 signals are neutral where target-only data are needed (SS2, SS5).

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbSeBr2 is most likely a **metastable but accessible** phase rather than a ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.130 eV/atom, while TS shows synthesized analogues at comparable metastability (Target, TS1–TS5). The CS and SS evidence strongly supports the chemical space because NbSeBr3 is both the closest chemical and structural analogue, and NbSe2Cl2 supports layered Nb–Se–halide chemistry (CS1, SS1, CS5). The most critical enabling factor is sealed-tube Br2 transport, which can activate refractory Nb chemistry and control halogen chemical potential at moderate temperature (CS1, CS5, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selection, because related Nb–Se–halide systems show multiple polymorphs, side products, poor reproducibility, or narrow phase windows (CS5, SS3, SS4). Overall, the target is **moderately synthesizable**, with Route 1 preferred and careful Br2/Se stoichiometry control required (CS1, CS5, TS1).
