### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the highest scoring bin [target data]. The exact-composition tetragonal Sr₂MgOsO₆ reference is on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 [CS1, SS1], supporting chemical stability of the Sr–Mg–Os–O composition. However, the target P-1 polymorph is not the same as the reported tetragonal ground-state-like phase, so it should be treated as low-energy metastable rather than definitely ground state [CS1, SS1]. TS provides only indirect evidence because its nearest thermodynamic analogues are different Os/Re/Ir oxides; therefore, insufficient direct target evidence from TS, scored neutral-to-supportive rather than decisive [TS1–TS5].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is harsh because exact Sr₂MgOsO₆ synthesis required 6 GPa and 1500 °C in a Pt capsule [CS1, SS1]. Its kinetic advantage is structural certainty: the exact double-perovskite composition forms with high order under these conditions [CS1, SS1]. The major uncertainty is whether high pressure stabilizes the target triclinic P-1 polymorph or instead reproducibly gives the tetragonal I4/m polymorph [CS1, SS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate severity because Sr₂InOsO₆-type double perovskites were made at 950–960 °C in sealed quartz tubes under Ar, without external high pressure [SS5]. Kinetics are limited by refractory MgO and long diffusion distances, and the Sr₂InOsO₆ analogue required about 100 h plus regrinding [SS5]. The route is chemically plausible for Sr₂MgOsO₆, but direct evidence for Mg substitution under these sealed-tube oxide conditions is insufficient from SS5 alone.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from relatively low temperature, since Sr₂NiOsO₆ and Sr₂CuOsO₆ were formed near 800–850 °C [CS4, CS5]. Severity increases because the reactions require high-vacuum sealed ampoules, glovebox handling, Ag₂O oxygen control, and repeated heating cycles [CS4, CS5]. Kinetically, MgO is less reactive than NiO/CuO by reasonable inference, so phase formation may be slower than for the cited analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 receives the best kinetic score because citrate/nitrate processing produced homogeneous, reactive precursors for Sr₂MReO₆ phases before 1200 °C annealing [CS3]. This should reduce cation diffusion distances and improve Mg/Sr/Os homogeneity [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is that CS3 is a Re analogue rather than Os, while Os routes show volatility and oxygen-control problems [CS1, CS4, CS5].

#### F2/F3 — Route 5

Hydroxide flux growth has mild temperature severity, with Os flux reactions at 600 °C and Mg-Ir double-perovskite flux growth at 700 °C [SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3]. Flux transport can overcome solid-state diffusion limits, but the exact Sr₂MgOsO₆ stoichiometry is not demonstrated in flux [insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS]. The route is promising for crystals or metastable distortions but may have phase-selection problems because flux analogues include different structures and compositions [SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly size-mismatched cations Sr, Mg, and Os in different coordination environments, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is a crystalline ordered double perovskite with a small described P-1 cell, so there is insufficient evidence for a >40-atom cell trigger [target structure]. Mixed corner and face sharing is present, but edge sharing is not described, so the full mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered [target structure].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgOsO₆ in the target triclinic P-1 form is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than an unambiguously ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while a tetragonal exact-composition Sr₂MgOsO₆ analogue is reported at E_hull = 0.0 [target data; CS1, SS1]. The CS and SS evidence strongly supports formation of the Sr₂MgOsO₆ composition and double-perovskite framework, especially through high-pressure synthesis [CS1, SS1]. TS evidence supports the broader feasibility of Os oxides near similar metastability windows but is indirect for this exact composition [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is homogeneous cation mixing plus controlled oxygen activity to stabilize high-valent Os in the ordered double perovskite [CS1, CS3, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is Os volatility/toxicity and the risk that synthesis yields the known tetragonal polymorph instead of the target triclinic distortion [CS1, CS4, CS5].
