### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high thermodynamic score. CS supports the plausibility of stable Sr sulfides because SrZrS3 and Sr2SnS4 analogues have E_hull = 0.0 or near-zero values (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). SS/TS also include several E_hull = 0.0 tetrahedral chalcogenide analogues (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee an accessible kinetic pathway, especially for V redox control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Elemental solid-state

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because CS-1 and CS-5 use sealed ampoules, high temperatures near 900–1000 °C, glovebox handling, and grinding/reheating. Kinetics are limited by V metal diffusion and by the need to form the correct 1D Sr2VS4 ribbon rather than binary SrS/VSx products [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives strong evidence for elemental Sr–M–S synthesis, while SS/TS provide insufficient direct Sr–V elemental-route evidence and are neutral for precursor selection. This route is feasible but likely requires repeated annealing.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary sulfide ceramic route

CS-3 directly supports binary sulfide reactions using SrS and transition-metal sulfides in evacuated quartz bombs at 600–1000 °C for up to three weeks. The condition score is reduced by vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and air-sensitive sulfides, but it avoids H2S gas and extreme temperatures. Kinetically, sulfide precursors should be more reactive than elemental V and reduce structural reconstruction compared with oxide sulfidation [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is selecting the correct vanadium sulfide precursor and sulfur chemical potential to stabilize the target V oxidation state.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: H2S sulfidation

CS-2 supports preparing Sr–transition-metal sulfides from alkaline-earth carbonates and transition-metal oxides in flowing H2S at about 1373 K for a week. This is chemically plausible but conditionally harsher because H2S is toxic/reactive and the temperature exceeds 1000 °C. Kinetics benefit from gas-phase sulfidation but suffer from complete oxide-to-sulfide reconstruction and V redox complexity [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for H2S processing of related structures and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Reactive polysulfide flux

SS-3/TS-3 are the strongest structural-process analogues because KBaNbS4 is Pnma, contains tetrahedral NbS4 units, and is grown from K2S3/BaS/Nb/S flux at 500 °C. SS-5/TS-5 additionally support reactive flux formation of V/Nb chalcogenides at 350 °C. The flux route receives the best kinetic score because a polysulfide melt can dissolve/react refractory V species and support slow crystal growth at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is whether alkali flux cations will incorporate and outcompete pure Sr2VS4 formation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Solvothermal/methanolothermal

CS-4 supports low-temperature formation of a Pnma tetrahedral thiometallate, K2MoS4, from MoO3 + K2S in methanol at 443 K. This route has mild temperature and pressure conditions, but the transferability to dense Sr2VS4 is uncertain because CS-4 is an alkali thiomolybdate salt rather than an alkaline-earth vanadium sulfide framework. Kinetics may be favorable for forming thiovanadate species, but crystallization of the target structure may require post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient solvothermal evidence and are neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large size mismatch between Sr and V coordination environments, as reflected by Sr–S distances near 3.0 Å and V–S distances near 2.2–2.3 Å in the target description; this triggers C1. There is insufficient direct evidence that the target cell exceeds 40 atoms, and a Pnma Z = 4 cell for Sr2VS4 would likely contain ~28 atoms [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not explicitly show mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered. Thus amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2VS4 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple sulfide analogues in CS/SS/TS are also thermodynamically stable. The best evidence for synthesis comes from CS routes showing Sr-containing sulfides made in sealed tubes or from binary sulfides, and from SS/TS flux routes showing low-temperature growth of tetrahedral Pnma chalcogenides. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a sulfide-rich, oxygen-free environment with controlled sulfur chemical potential, supported by vacuum sealed ampoules and polysulfide fluxes in CS/SS/TS. The most critical limiting factor is vanadium redox/phase competition, because V can form multiple sulfides and oxidation states [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is synthesizable but will likely require careful screening of sulfur activity, flux composition, and annealing temperature.
