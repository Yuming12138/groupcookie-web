### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high-synthesizability boundary in the provided rubric and therefore supports a score of 90. The closest chemical/structural analogues Nd₂HgO₄ and Ho₂HgO₄ are calculated at or near the hull, with Nd₂HgO₄ at 0 eV/atom and Ho₂HgO₄ at ~0.003 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2). TS analogues around 0.05–0.10 eV/atom are experimentally reported, showing that this level of metastability is commonly accessible by bulk routes (TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the exact Gd phase is a true ground state or a slightly metastable member stabilized by finite-temperature/vapor-pressure conditions; TS provides only generic thermodynamic similarity, not Hg-specific evidence, so TS is supportive but indirect.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 has moderate severity because the temperature is low, 550 °C, but the reaction requires an evacuated sealed silica tube and secondary pressure containment (CS-1; SS-2). The key deductions are vacuum/sealed atmosphere, volatile HgO containment, multi-step handling, and Hg toxicity (CS-1; SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and SS give strong direct evidence for feasibility; TS gives insufficient route-specific signal for sealed Hg chemistry and is scored neutral 50 for this factor. The remaining uncertainty is pressure management, because HgO decomposition can generate hazardous internal pressure [Reasonable Inference consistent with sealed-tube precautions in CS-1/SS-2].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable for Route 1 because the closest R₂HgO₄ analogue forms at only 550 °C in ≥24 h, implying that HgO volatility or reactive Hg–O transport helps overcome the refractory nature of Gd₂O₃ (CS-1; SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). Structural reconstruction is relatively easy because the product is the same C2/m rare-earth mercury oxide family as the Nd and Ho analogues (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2). The main kinetic uncertainty is impurity formation: the reference notes phase sensitivity and possible rare-earth silicon oxide impurities (CS-1; SS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 is more equipment-intensive because Ho₂HgO₄ required 6200 bar dry oxygen at 620 °C for 8 days (CS-2; SS-1). Although the rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa and 6200 bar is below that threshold, practically the oxygen autoclave is specialized and hazardous (CS-2; SS-1). CS and SS strongly support the structural/chemical relevance, while TS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure Hg oxide synthesis and is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor. The major uncertainty is whether Gd₂HgO₄ benefits from such pressure given that the lower-pressure sealed route already includes Gd in the reported R series (CS-1; SS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The high-pressure route gives moderate kinetics: the temperature is only 620 °C, but the 8-day dwell for Ho₂HgO₄ suggests nucleation or diffusion is slow under those conditions (CS-2; SS-1). Structural transferability is good because Ho₂HgO₄ and Gd₂HgO₄ share the rare-earth mercury oxide motif with linear Hg–O units and 7-coordinate lanthanide sites (CS-2; SS-1). The uncertainty is size-dependent stability across the lanthanide series; CS-1 indicates Gd is accessible at lower pressure, so high pressure may be unnecessary or could alter phase selection (CS-1; SS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 has modest nominal temperature severity because the structurally related Gd₃FeO₆ flux route uses ~600 °C in sealed silver tubes (SS-4). However, it inherits HgO toxicity/volatility from the R₂HgO₄ chemistry (CS-1, CS-2) and adds caustic flux handling and post-synthesis washing (SS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Hg hydroxide-flux synthesis, so those are scored neutral 50 for direct route validation. The uncertainty is high because Hg speciation in molten KOH may not favor the desired linear Hg–O coordination [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux synthesis could improve kinetics by replacing slow solid-state diffusion with dissolution–reprecipitation, as seen for Gd₃FeO₆ crystals grown from KOH melt at 600 °C (SS-4). This is attractive for Hg oxides because low-temperature routes help reduce Hg loss (CS-1, CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). However, no direct R₂HgO₄ flux analogue is given, so the structural reconstruction score is conservative and CS/TS evidence is insufficient for this exact mechanism, neutral 50. The route is best viewed as a crystal-growth or troubleshooting option rather than the primary synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large 7-coordinate Gd³⁺ framework cation and small/linearly coordinated Hg²⁺, giving a significant cation size/coordination mismatch that triggers C1 (target description; CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). The close analogues are ordered crystalline C2/m phases rather than glass-formers, and their described unit cells are not extremely large, so C2 is not triggered (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2). The structure has extensive corner and edge sharing, but no clear face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not strictly triggered (target description; CS-1, CS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that complex corner/edge polyhedral networks may still broaden crystallization windows and promote defects, especially if Hg stoichiometry drifts [Reasonable Inference].

Gd₂HgO₄ is likely a near-ground-state or only mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and close R₂HgO₄ analogues lie on or very near the hull (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2). The chemical and structural analogue support is unusually strong: Nd₂HgO₄ is made by a route explicitly stated for R = La, Nd, Sm, Eu, Gd, and Ho₂HgO₄ confirms the same rare-earth mercury oxide structure under high-pressure oxygen (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature sealed synthesis, which preserves HgO/Hg–O chemistry while allowing the R₂HgO₄ framework to crystallize (CS-1; SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is HgO volatility/toxicity and pressure generation in sealed systems (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with sealed-tube solid-state synthesis being the most defensible first attempt.
