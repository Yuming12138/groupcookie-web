### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff, so it receives 90 rather than a lower metastability score. [Target] CS analogues Sr₂MgWO₆ and Ca₂MgWO₆ are at or near the hull, which supports Mg–W oxide accessibility. [CS] SS and TS analogues also include low-hull alkali oxometalates, but they do not prove the exact K₂MgWO₆ phase. [SS; TS]

**F2 / F3 — Route 1.** Route 1 is conditionally severe because CS analogues need high-temperature ceramic firing, repeated grinding, and pelletizing. [CS] Its kinetics are limited by refractory MgO and the need to form a low-symmetry K/peroxide-containing target from simple oxides. [CS; Target; Reasonable Inference] The main uncertainty is whether high-temperature air firing destroys the O–O unit or forms K₂WO₄ / MgWO₄ instead. [Target; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 / F3 — Route 2.** Route 2 has moderate severity because it avoids >1000 °C final firing but requires K₂O₂ handling and sealed containment. [SS; Reasonable Inference] Its kinetics are the best among bulk routes because MgWO₄ preorganizes Mg–W chemistry and K₂O₂ provides a reactive alkali/peroxide source. [CS; Target; Reasonable Inference] The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates this exact K₂O₂ + MgWO₄ transformation. [CS; SS; TS]

**F2 / F3 — Route 3.** Route 3 is more severe because SS K oxometalate analogues require sealed Au/Ni containers, inert atmosphere, and very long anneals. [SS] Kinetics benefit from flux-like alkali oxides, but Mg incorporation is not directly evidenced in SS. [SS] One SS entry has mismatched sulfide synthesis metadata, so its synthesis information is treated as insufficient evidence from SS for K–Mg–W–O route design and neutral where relevant. [SS]

**F2 / F3 — Route 4.** Route 4 has high operational difficulty because CS/TS high-pressure analogues require capsules and specialized pressure equipment. [CS; TS] It may help stabilize a metastable or oxygen-rich phase, but TS also shows that high-pressure tungstate products can appear as byproducts rather than controlled target phases. [TS] The route is therefore a backup rather than the first recommendation. [TS; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 / F3 — Route 5.** Route 5 has mild thermal conditions because TS hydrothermal tungstate/oxyfluoride chemistry occurs at low temperature. [TS] Its precursor activity is high in solution, but crystallizing an anhydrous K₂MgWO₆ phase rather than hydrated peroxotungstate or simple tungstate salts is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS. [TS; Reasonable Inference] This makes it a useful exploratory route but not the strongest primary recommendation. [TS; Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has a large cation size mismatch between K and Mg/W sites, triggering one amorphization-risk check. [Target; Reasonable Inference] The supplied target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell, and it does not explicitly show simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing in the target itself. [Target] SS and CS analogues show more complex connectivity in related materials, so C3 remains uncertain but not triggered. [CS; SS; Target]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂MgWO₆ is best interpreted as a **near-hull, potentially metastable but accessible** phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. [Target] CS strongly supports the Mg–W–O framework chemistry through successful Sr/Ca Mg–W oxide syntheses, but CS does not directly solve the K⁺ charge-balance and O–O bonding issue. [CS; Target] SS supports the use of sealed alkali oxide/tungstate chemistry for K–O–W frameworks, while TS supports backup hydrothermal or high-pressure strategies. [SS; TS] The most critical enabling factor is using a reactive oxygen-rich K precursor such as K₂O₂ with preformed MgWO₄ to match the target stoichiometry and preserve peroxide-like oxygen. [CS; Target; Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is controlling K volatility, peroxide decomposition, and competing K₂WO₄ / MgWO₄ phases during crystallization. [CS; SS; Target; Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | K stoichiometry loss from alkali volatility or soluble K-rich byproducts. [SS; Reasonable Inference] | High | Use sealed/lidded Au/Ni/Pt-compatible containment and slight K excess only after impurity screening. [SS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 2 | All | Retention of O–O / peroxide-like bonding in the target. [Target] | High | Avoid prolonged >1000 °C firing; use K₂O₂/KO₂ or low-temperature oxidizing routes. [Target; CS; SS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 3 | Route 1 | Competing K₂WO₄ and MgWO₄ formation during carbonate/oxide firing. [CS; Reasonable Inference] | Med–High | Use intermediate XRD, repeated grinding, and lower final temperatures than Sr/Ca analogues. [CS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 4 | Routes 1–3 | Slow Mg/W diffusion from refractory MgO or WO₃-derived phases. [CS; Reasonable Inference] | Medium | Pre-synthesize MgWO₄, use fine grinding, and use reactive peroxide / flux conditions. [CS; SS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 5 | Routes 2–4 | Air/moisture sensitivity of K₂O₂ or KO₂. [CS; SS; Reasonable Inference] | High | Load in dry glovebox and seal rapidly in compatible capsule/tube. [CS; SS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 6 | Route 3 | Very long annealing and incomplete reaction in sealed alkali fluxes. [SS] | Medium | Use small particle size, staged anneals, and slow cooling based on SS flux analogues. [SS] |
| 7 | Route 4 | High-pressure synthesis may yield byproducts or serendipitous phases. [TS] | High | Use small screening matrix of pressure / temperature / oxidizer loading and compare XRD after each run. [TS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 8 | Route 5 | Hydrothermal route may crystallize hydrates or soluble peroxotungstates instead of anhydrous K₂MgWO₆. [TS; Reasonable Inference] | Med–High | Control pH, H₂O₂ concentration, cooling rate, and use low-temperature post-drying only. [TS; Reasonable Inference] |
| 9 | All | Low-symmetry P1 ordering may require careful crystallization rather than rapid firing. [Target] | Medium | Use slow cooling, repeated anneals, or flux-assisted growth to improve cation/oxygen ordering. [SS; TS; Reasonable Inference] |
