### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable threshold for likely synthesizability. CS and SS strongly support stability because several close Zn–Ir–B phases have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and have reported syntheses, including Ir₇Zn₅B₃, Ir₂Zn₂B, Zn(IrB)₂, and Ir₄ZnB₃ (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). TS gives supportive but less specific evidence because Ir-containing borides with E_hull values from ~0.028 to ~0.172 eV/atom are synthesizable (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5), but insufficient evidence from TS exists for the exact Zn–Ir–B chemistry, so TS is not weighted as strongly.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires 1100 °C, Ar atmosphere, and sealed Ta ampoules in BN crucibles, all directly reported for Zn–Ir–B analogues (CS-3, CS-5, SS-2). The main severity penalties are high temperature above 1000 °C and volatile Zn requiring sealed containment, both indicated by the sealed-ampoule protocols in CS/SS. There is insufficient evidence from TS on Zn-containing sealed ampoule requirements, so TS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are limited by elemental Ir and B, which are refractory and low-diffusivity; this is consistent with 50 h holds needed in Zn–Ir–B analogues (CS-3, CS-5, SS-2). Structural similarity to known Zn–Ir–B phases improves the score, especially because Pmmm Ir₄ZnB₃ is reported in SS-2. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target Ir₁₁Zn₆B₆ ordering is accessible at only 1100 °C or requires the higher-temperature phase field represented by Ir₇Zn₅B₃.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses 1200–1230 °C and long annealing in sealed Ta/quartz ampoules under Ar, directly matching the most similar Ir₇Zn₅B₃ route (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3). The route is not high-pressure and does not require H₂/NH₃, but it requires sealed containment due to Zn volatility and high-temperature compatibility, giving moderate severity. CS and SS provide strong route evidence; TS is only indirectly supportive and therefore neutral for Zn-specific containment.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The higher temperature improves Ir/B diffusion relative to Route 1, and the strongest structural analogue Ir₇Zn₅B₃ was made under these conditions (CS-1, SS-1). However, the 5-day dwell and reports of difficult phase control in related Zn/Rh/Ir borides indicate slow ordering and a narrow compositional window (CS-1, CS-2, SS-3). The target’s similar Ir–Zn and Ir–B bond lengths to the analogues support framework formation, but exact Ir₁₁Zn₆B₆ stoichiometry may require optimization.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is more severe because arc melting involves very high local temperatures, repeated melting, and subsequent annealing, consistent with TS arc-melted Ir borides (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). Zn volatility is a major additional concern inferred from the fact that all direct Zn–Ir–B routes use sealed ampoules rather than open melting (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2). CS-4 supports arc-melt/anneal for a Cu–Pt–B boride, but Cu is less volatile than Zn at these conditions, so transferability is imperfect.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Arc melting improves bulk homogenization and reduces diffusion barriers, as TS sources describe repeated melting for homogeneity in Ir borides (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). Post-annealing can promote ordering, as shown for TS arc-melted and annealed borides (TS-3, TS-4). The uncertainty is substantial because Zn loss during arc melting can prevent the target stoichiometry, and no CS/SS Zn–Ir–B analogue is reported by arc melting.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an intermetallic boride with moderate structural complexity but strong crystalline analogue support in CS and SS, including high-similarity orthorhombic and Pmmm Zn–Ir–B phases (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2, SS-5). There is no clear radius-mismatch trigger between Ir and Zn, and the supplied target formula has only 23 atoms per formula unit, so the >40-atom criterion is not clearly triggered. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for amorphization in Zn–Ir–B, so TS is treated as neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ir₁₁Zn₆B₆ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the common practical boundary for accessible crystalline phases. The CS and SS analogues support synthesis strongly: several Zn–Ir–B compounds with very similar bond lengths, coordination motifs, and orthorhombic/Pmmm structures have been synthesized from elements in sealed Ta ampoules (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a well-populated Zn–Ir–B phase field accessible by high-temperature elemental reaction under Ar in BN/Ta containment. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity under Zn volatility, because the closest analogue set reports phase-width and impurity issues for related compositions (CS-1, CS-2, SS-3). Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require stoichiometry and cooling-rate optimization rather than a single guaranteed firing.
