### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and earns a high F1 score. This is consistent with several thermodynamic analogues at E_hull = 0, including FeSe (TS-1), CsFe₂Se₃ (TS-2), and other selenides (TS-3/4/5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exact polymorph selection.

**F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction.**  
Condition severity is moderate because the closest BaFe₂Se₃ solid-state analogue requires evacuated quartz ampoules, prereaction of Fe/Se, repeated grinding, and heating up to 750 °C (CS-2, SS-1), while SrZrSe₃ requires 800–950 °C sealed ampoules for days (CS-4). Kinetics are the main limitation: Fe metal is diffusion-limited, and the BaFe₂Se₃ analogue required iterative heating/regrinding until single phase (CS-2, SS-1). The route is nevertheless plausible because the target shares the FeSe₄ tetrahedral network motif with BaFe₂Se₃ analogues (CS-1/2, SS-1/4).

**F2/F3 — Route 2: melt growth / Bridgman.**  
Severity is moderate-to-high because Se volatility and sealed-ampoule handling are unavoidable, and full melt homogenization may require high temperatures [Reasonable Inference], although RbFe₂Se₃ Bridgman growth is reported around 850 °C (CS-3). Kinetics are favorable because liquid-mediated transport bypasses slow Fe diffusion, and BaFe₂Se₃ and RbFe₂Se₃ single crystals were obtained from stoichiometric melts or Bridgman growth (CS-1, CS-3, SS-4). The uncertainty is whether SrFe₂Se₃ melts congruently or whether competing Sr–Se/Fe–Se phases form during cooling.

**F2/F3 — Route 3: reactive flux.**  
Flux synthesis reduces temperature severity, as TS-3 demonstrates complex selenide flux growth at 450–500 °C, but it introduces multi-step precursor preparation, flux removal, and air/moisture-sensitive polyselenide handling (TS-3/TS-4). Kinetics should be better than direct solid-state reaction because the flux provides liquid/reactive transport, but structural selectivity is uncertain because no direct AFe₂Se₃ flux analogue is provided. Thus, it is a useful exploratory route but not the primary recommendation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large Sr/Fe size and coordination mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target bond lengths and coordination]. However, its structural motif is not unusually complex: the target and close analogues are dominated by corner- and edge-sharing FeSe₄ tetrahedra, not simultaneous face/edge/corner-sharing networks (CS-1/2, SS-1/4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for exact target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrFe₂Se₃ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis because BaFe₂Se₃ and RbFe₂Se₃ analogues with the same AFe₂Se₃ chemistry and FeSe₄ tetrahedral framework have been made by solid-state and melt/Bridgman methods (CS-1/2/3, SS-1/4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust FeSe₄ tetrahedral chain/ladder motif, which appears repeatedly across chemically and structurally similar selenides (CS-1/2/3, SS-1/4). The most critical limiting factor is control of volatile Se and reactive Sr/SrSe under sealed-tube conditions, especially during melt growth [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-2/3/4]. Overall, the material should be synthesizable with moderate experimental care, with Bridgman/melt growth the best route for phase formation and crystal quality.
