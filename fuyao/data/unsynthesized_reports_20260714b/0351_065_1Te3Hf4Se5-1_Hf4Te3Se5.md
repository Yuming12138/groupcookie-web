### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0040 eV/atom, placing it essentially on the convex hull and within the 90–100 score bracket [Target]. This is consistent with analogous HfTe₂ being exactly on the hull [SS1, TS1] and other chemically/thermodynamically similar phases being close to the hull, typically 0–0.032 eV/atom [CS2, SS4, TS2–TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee retention of the specific Te/Se ordering, especially if HfTe₂/HfSe₂-like binaries compete **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT

The CVT route avoids high pressure and very high temperature, consistent with HfTe₂ growth at 700 °C [SS1, TS1] and TaSe₂ transport across 900→700 °C [SS4, TS4]. Deductions arise from evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile chalcogens, halogen transport agents, and multi-step crystal-growth handling [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS on exact Hf–Te–Se mixed-anion CVT means the condition score remains moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT

Kinetics are helped by vapor transport, which is successful for the closest HfTe₂ omega analogue [SS1, TS1]. However, Hf is refractory and mixed Te/Se ordering must occur during growth, so precursor activity and diffusion are not scored as easy **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structural reconstruction score is relatively favorable because the target is omega-derived and shares edge-sharing Hf-chalcogen octahedra with HfTe₂ [Target, SS1, TS1].

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

Direct sealed-tube synthesis is supported by Hf-containing NaCuHf₂Se₅ at 1000 °C for 3 d [SS2] and HfGeSe-type solid-state reactions up to 1150 °C over long times [CS2]. The severity is moderate because vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogens, regrinding/pelletizing, and toxic Se/Te handling are expected [CS2, SS2, SS5]. The route does not require high pressure, which distinguishes it from CS3.

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because Hf metal is refractory and related Hf/Zr selenides often require multi-day or multi-week heating [CS2, SS2, SS3, SS5]. Unlike CVT, the route lacks a vapor-mediated Hf transport pathway, so diffusion through a growing chalcogenide layer may limit completion **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural formation is plausible but harder because Te/Se ordering and layer formation must happen in the bulk pellet without transport selection **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3: High Pressure

High-pressure synthesis is explicitly demonstrated for chemically similar chalcogenides at 35–50 kbar and 850–1050 °C [CS3]. This route receives large severity deductions because it requires specialized multi-anvil/DAC-type equipment and a severe pressure/temperature window [CS3]. The evidence is weaker than for CVT because the closest HfTe₂ structural analogue is made at ambient pressure [SS1, TS1].

#### F3 — Route 3: High Pressure

High pressure can accelerate densification and stabilize otherwise inaccessible phases **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS3 shows missing chalcogenide phases can be synthesized under 35–50 kbar [CS3]. However, the target’s closest structural analogue does not require pressure [SS1, TS1], so high pressure may be unnecessary and may even favor denser competing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetic scoring is therefore only moderate-low.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hf, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The structure is described as edge-sharing Hf–Te/Se octahedral layers rather than a complex mixture of vertex/edge/face-sharing networks [Target]. SS5 reports amorphous Zr–Se competition in a different Sr/Ba/Zr/Se system [SS5], but there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/TS that Hf₄Te₃Se₅ itself has a high amorphization tendency.

Hf₄Te₃Se₅ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.004 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from HfTe₂, which is structurally and thermodynamically close, has the same 2D omega-type edge-sharing Hf-chalcogen framework, and is grown by iodine CVT [SS1, TS1]. Additional support comes from broader CVT and sealed-tube syntheses of layered Ta/Nb chalcogenides and Hf-containing selenides [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The most important enabling factor is the structural continuity between HfTe₂ and the target mixed Te/Se omega-derived layer [Target, SS1, TS1]. The main limiting factor is precise Te/Se stoichiometry and anion ordering during synthesis, especially because both Se and Te are volatile and may form competing Hf–chalcogen binaries **[Reasonable Inference]**.
