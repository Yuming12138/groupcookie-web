### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score [Target]. CS analogues occupy similar low-energy metastability windows, including hydrophilite PtO₂ at 0.0404 eV/atom and another hydrophilite PtO₂ at 0.0 eV/atom [CS1, CS5]. SS and TS analogues also include many stable or low-hull Hg-containing fluorides/halides, supporting that Hg–F/halide frameworks can be isolable [SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS2]. The uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not specify whether the P4₂/mnm hydrophilite polymorph is the ambient-pressure ground-state polymorph or a quenchable metastable phase [Target; insufficient evidence from SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately mild in temperature because Hg fluoride analogues were synthesized at 450–500 °C for 10 h [SS1, SS4]. It is penalized for sealed-tube operation and toxic/moisture-sensitive Hg fluoride handling because SS analogues explicitly require rigorously dry grinding and sealed gold tubes [SS1, SS4]. It avoids >5 GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, unlike CS high-pressure PtO₂ routes [CS1, CS3]. Remaining uncertainty is whether direct annealing is sufficient to reach the target hydrophilite polymorph, since SS does not report binary hydrophilite HgF₂.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

HgF₂ as a direct single-composition precursor should minimize long-range cation diffusion, which is favorable by [Reasonable Inference] from SS use of HgF₂ in related solid-state reactions [SS1, SS4]. The 450–500 °C formation of related Hg fluoride frameworks suggests acceptable kinetics under sealed dry conditions [SS1, SS4]. Structural reconstruction may be only a polymorph conversion, but direct evidence for the exact hydrophilite HgF₂ transition is insufficient from SS/TS. CS hydrophilite PtO₂ shows the topology is accessible, but it may require pressure for some chemistries [CS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is severe because exact-structure CS analogue synthesis uses high pressure, 40–60 kbar at 500 °C, and other related HP routes use 6–17 GPa [CS1, CS3]. It also requires capsule loading, quenching, and controlled decompression, as described in CS high-pressure procedures [CS1, CS2, CS3]. HgF₂ toxicity and sealed containment add further burden by analogy to Hg fluoride SS syntheses [SS1, SS4]. The main uncertainty is the minimum pressure needed for HgF₂, since no SS/TS high-pressure HgF₂ analogue is given.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High pressure can directly favor dense octahedral hydrophilite connectivity, and CS1 provides strong structural transferability for the P4₂/mnm hydrophilite network. The short dwell times in CS analogues, 0.5–1 h, indicate kinetics can be adequate once the correct P–T window is reached [CS1, CS3]. However, CS chemistry is Pt–O, not Hg–F, so kinetics involving Hg volatility and fluoride mobility are inferred rather than demonstrated [CS1, SS1]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for HP HgF₂ kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Hydrothermal Route 3 operates at relatively low temperatures, 200–270 °C, based on Hg oxyfluoride and thermodynamic hydrothermal analogues [SS3, TS1, TS3]. It is still penalized for sealed autoclave operation, multi-step processing, and hazardous Hg/HF precursors [SS3, TS1]. It does not require external GPa pressure or very high temperature [SS3, TS1, TS3]. The uncertainty is whether aqueous HF conditions are compatible with anhydrous HgF₂ rather than oxyfluoride/hydroxide formation [SS3, TS1].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution transport in HF should give high precursor activity and overcome solid-state diffusion limitations, as Hg nitrate plus AlF₃ in 32 wt% HF produced Hg oxyfluoride crystals [SS3, TS1]. TS3 shows hydrothermal halide/oxide crystallization at 200 °C over several days, supporting favorable solution-mediated kinetics [TS3]. The structural reconstruction score is limited because hydrothermal Hg fluoride evidence points to oxyfluoride phases and impurities rather than the binary hydrophilite HgF₂ phase [SS3, TS1]. CS provides insufficient evidence for hydrothermal formation of hydrophilite structures.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-radius mismatch penalty [Target]. The hydrophilite MO₂/MF₂-type network is a simple octahedral framework rather than a large multi-component unit cell, as supported by CS hydrophilite PtO₂ analogues [CS1, CS5]. The target has mixed edge- and corner-sharing octahedra, but not face-sharing, so the specified C3 check is not triggered [Target]. Uncertainty is low for this factor because the structural description is explicit.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgF₂ in the specified hydrophilite P4₂/mnm structure is likely a **low-energy metastable or borderline stable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis evidence is mixed: CS gives strong structural support for hydrophilite octahedral frameworks but mainly under high pressure and for Pt oxides [CS1, CS5], while SS gives strong chemical support for Hg fluoride handling and sealed-tube synthesis but not the exact binary hydrophilite phase [SS1, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary composition and direct availability of HgF₂, which should reduce cation ordering and diffusion barriers [SS1, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is retention of the desired polymorph without Hg/F loss or hydrolysis, because structurally similar Hg fluorides require rigorously dry sealed conditions [SS1, SS4]. If ambient-pressure annealing fails, high-pressure quenching is the most defensible escalation because the exact hydrophilite topology is pressure-accessible in CS analogues [CS1, CS3].
