### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Ca₂YOsO₆ has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom band. [Target] CS and SS analogues include hull-stable Ca₂ScOsO₆ and Sr₂YOsO₆, while TS analogues show experimentally accessible phases near similar metastability windows. [CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4, TS5] The remaining uncertainty is that the target is borderline rather than clearly below the hull. [Target]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic Os chemistry, PbO₂ oxidation, and multiple firing steps. [CS1, SS2] The absence of >1000 °C temperature or high pressure keeps severity moderate rather than extreme. [CS1] TS gives insufficient evidence for PbO₂-controlled oxidation, so TS is neutral 50 for this route. [insufficient evidence from TS]

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetic bottleneck is slow diffusion through dense CaO/Y₂O₃/OsO₂ oxide precursors. [CS1, SS1] Structural transfer is favorable because Ca₂ScOsO₆ has nearly matching octahedral tilts, but the target still requires reconstruction and ordering from binary oxides. [SS2, Target] The uncertainty is whether Y³⁺ substitution changes the preferred Ca–Y–Os symmetry or causes competing phases. [SS1, SS2]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids vacuum and high pressure, but still requires sealed-tube handling, toxic Os species, peroxide redox control, and repeated regrinding. [SS1, SS4, CS1] Its condition severity is lower than Route 1 because the oxidant is incorporated into the initial redox-balanced batch rather than a separate PbO₂ post-oxidation step. [SS1, SS4] TS2/TS3 are chemically less relevant and neutral 50 for this route. [insufficient evidence from TS2/TS3]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are moderate because peroxide/redox precursors improve oxygen activity, while Y₂O₃ and Os species still limit diffusion. [SS1, SS4, TS1] Structural reconstruction is strongly supported by Sr₂YOsO₆ and Ca₂ScOsO₆ analogues, which bracket the target’s chemistry and tilt magnitude. [SS1, CS1, SS2, Target] The main uncertainty is whether Ca₂YOsO₆ prefers the target trigonal R3 variant rather than a monoclinic double-perovskite polymorph. [Target, SS1, SS2]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is severely penalized by 1500 °C, 6 GPa pressure, controlled Ar/Pt-capsule handling, toxic Os, KClO₄ oxidizer, and rapid quench requirements. [CS5] The high-pressure route is credible but operationally demanding. [CS5] SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure Ca₂YOsO₆, so they are neutral 50 for this route. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₂YOsO₆ is best viewed as a near-hull or weakly metastable oxide rather than an unambiguous ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. [Target] The CS/SS analogues strongly support synthesis because Sr₂YOsO₆ provides exact Y–Os chemistry and Ca₂ScOsO₆ provides closely matched Ca–Os tilted octahedral chemistry. [SS1, CS1, SS2] The most critical enabling factor is controlled Os⁵⁺ oxygen activity in a sealed tube, as shown by peroxide/PbO₂/redox strategies across Os double perovskites. [CS1, SS1, SS4] The most critical limiting factor is phase purity and B-site ordering under slow solid-state diffusion, especially because Ca₂YOsO₆ may compete with monoclinic perovskite-like variants or binary oxides. [CS1, SS1, SS2] High pressure and flux growth are plausible backups, but they are less directly supported for Ca₂YOsO₆ than the peroxide/redox sealed-tube route. [CS5, SS3, SS5]
