### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.023 eV/atom places it in the high-stability/metastable-accessible regime, so F1 = 95 by the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low hulls include Pd₂OCl₂ at 0.036 eV/atom, PdCl₂ at 0.032 eV/atom, ReNCl₄ at 0.036 eV/atom, WOCl₃ at 0.060 eV/atom, and MoCl₃ at 0.034 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). SS analogues also include stable layered halide/oxohalide phases such as VOBr₂ at 0 eV/atom and IrCl₃ at 0 eV/atom (SS-2, SS-3). CS examples are chemically dominated by Hg/organic halides with high hulls and are not useful for predicting Ru/Ir oxychloride stability; insufficient evidence from CS, neutral source score 50.

#### F2 and F3, Route 1

Route 1 inherits demanding sealed/vacuum and volatile chloride transport requirements from IrCl₃ CVT and MoCl₃ CVT analogues (SS-2, TS-5), giving a low-moderate F2. Its kinetic score is moderate because vapor transport can bypass some solid diffusion, as in SS-2 and TS-5, but the target requires simultaneous Ru/Ir/O/Cl assembly rather than a binary chloride. Structurally, the target closely resembles VOCl₂/VOBr₂-type mixed edge/corner octahedral sheets (SS-1, SS-3), which improves reconstruction probability. Uncertainty remains high because no direct Ru–Ir oxychloride CVT analogue is provided; insufficient evidence from CS for this route, neutral source score 50.

#### F2 and F3, Route 2

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because TS-1 shows noble-metal oxychloride synthesis at 450 °C but requires molten salts, long durations, and flux handling, and TS-4 shows sealed ampoule halide processing under Ar. Kinetics are favorable because molten fluxes improve mass transport and enable crystallization below the cation melting/diffusion limit, as demonstrated for Pd₂OCl₂ in nitrate/TlCl flux (TS-1). The route is chemically transferable because the target is also a noble-metal oxychloride with short M–O and M–Cl bonds, though Ru/Ir ordering and avoiding binary chlorides remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides little route-specific support beyond general halide crystallization; insufficient evidence from CS for molten-flux Ru/Ir oxychloride synthesis, neutral source score 50.

#### F2 and F3, Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest nominal conditions, supported by CS slow-evaporation syntheses at room temperature to weeks (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5) and SS oxohalide precipitation/concentration near 80 °C (SS-1, SS-3). Its precursor activity and ion mobility are high in solution, but structural reconstruction is weak because the CS analogues are mostly Hg-organic or simple halide frameworks rather than Ru/Ir extended oxychloride sheets. The key uncertainty is whether Ru and Ir chloride complexes will hydrolyze or crystallize as molecular chloro/aquo species instead of the target layered solid [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides only indirect support because thermodynamic analogues are mostly solid-state/flux chloride systems, not solution crystallizations; insufficient evidence from TS for solution synthesis, neutral source score 50.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically similar octahedral Ru and Ir cations, so large ionic-size mismatch is unlikely [Reasonable Inference]. The described Cmmm phase is a simple 2D sheet made of corner- and edge-sharing octahedra with no face sharing, and the formula contains only 8 atoms per formula unit [Target description]. These features imply low topological frustration and low amorphization risk relative to large, mixed-polyhedra materials. Remaining uncertainty is the degree of Ru/Ir site ordering; no CS/SS/TS source directly reports a Ru/Ir mixed-cation analogue, so cation-ordering disorder remains possible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RuIr(OCl₂)₂ is likely a **near-ground-state metastable phase** rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.023 eV/atom [Target data]. The strongest support comes from SS analogues showing very similar MCl₄O₂ octahedral sheet motifs in VOCl₂/VOBr₂ (SS-1, SS-3) and TS analogues showing low-hull noble/transition-metal chlorides and oxychlorides accessible by flux or sealed-ampoule routes (TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known layered oxyhalides with linear O bridges and L-shaped halide bridges (SS-1, SS-3). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ru/Ir ordering and O/Cl chemical potentials, because overchlorination may give RuCl₃/IrCl₃-like phases while underchlorination may give RuO₂/IrO₂ [Reasonable Inference from SS-2, TS-1, TS-5]. Overall, a flux-assisted sealed-ampoule route is the most balanced strategy.
