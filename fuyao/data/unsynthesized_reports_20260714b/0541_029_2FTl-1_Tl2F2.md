### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, directly placing it in the highest stability range. SS/TS analogues include multiple stable fluorides and Tl-containing compounds with E_hull = 0, including SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS3, and SS5/TS4. CS is less consistent because chemically similar oxide entries range from stable CS3 to highly metastable CS2/CS4/CS5, so CS provides insufficient reliable signal for TlF thermodynamics and is treated near neutral relative to the target’s direct E_hull.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses mild temperatures and ambient pressure, consistent with SS3 hot-solution crystallization and TS5 aqueous precipitation. The main deductions come from multiple operations and toxic thallium/fluoride reagents; CS provides insufficient evidence for this low-temperature fluoride route. The remaining uncertainty is whether TlF crystallizes readily from water or remains too soluble, which is a [Reasonable Inference] concern rather than a CS/SS/TS-proven limitation.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

TS5 supports fast thallium precipitation chemistry at 100 °C, and SS3 supports fluoride-containing crystals forming by simple cooling from hot solution. Because solution growth bypasses solid-state diffusion, the kinetic score is high. The uncertainty is polymorph control: SS3 reports small crystals and does not prove the PbO-like TlF structure will be the only crystallizing polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

SS2/SS4 use Teflon-lined autoclaves at 150 °C, and TS4 uses hydrothermal Tl chemistry at 503 K for 96 h, so temperatures are moderate and pressures are autogenous rather than extreme. Deductions arise from sealed volatile fluoride/solvent conditions, multiple processing steps, and toxic Tl/F chemistry. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for solvothermal fluoride TlF synthesis and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

SS2/SS4 demonstrate effective fluoride transport and crystallization in MeOH solvothermal media, while TS4 demonstrates hydrothermal formation of Tl-containing single crystals. These analogues support good precursor mobility and slow-cooling crystallization. The uncertainty is that the closest fluoride solvothermal examples involve Sn and alkali cations, not binary TlF.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperatures but requires sealed Ar/vacuum or corrosion-resistant containment, supported by SS1/TS1 sealed Au-tube fluoride annealing practices. Deductions are significant for sealed/controlled atmosphere, volatile fluoride risk, multi-step handling, and Tl toxicity. CS1/CS2 show moderate-temperature deposition of binary oxides, but this is only weakly transferable to TlF.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

If pre-formed TlF is used, the system is compositionally correct, so only recrystallization or polymorph ordering is needed; this is favorable by [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 shows fluoride materials can be annealed in sealed noble-metal tubes, supporting the containment concept. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly confirms the annealing temperature needed for the specific Pmmn TlF phase.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

SS1/TS1 pressure fluorination uses 350–400 atm F₂ and elevated temperatures, with pre-fluorination specifically to avoid explosions. This route is therefore penalized for reactive atmosphere, volatile/sealed fluorination, multiple steps, and toxic/reactive reagents. It does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty because SS1/TS1 pressure is hundreds of atm, not multi-GPa.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

F₂ is highly reactive and SS1/TS1 demonstrates it can form fluorides, so chemical conversion is plausible. However, SS1/TS1 also shows long fluorination times and specialized pressure control, implying kinetic and safety barriers. For TlF, direct fluorination may overdrive the reaction or introduce defects/volatility rather than improving phase purity, so F3 is lower than solution and annealing routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary TlF phase with a small described coordination network, so amorphization risk is low. The structure contains edge- and corner-sharing FTl₄ tetrahedra, but the rubric’s highest-risk mixed-connectivity trigger requires vertex + edge + face sharing together, which is not reported. CS/SS/TS analogues include many crystalline fluorides and Tl compounds, but no direct amorphization evidence; thus the main basis is target structural simplicity plus the given crystallographic description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlF is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The SS/TS data support fluoride and Tl-containing crystal growth particularly well through low-temperature solution, solvothermal, and hydrothermal routes, while CS oxide data provide only weak or neutral transferability to a binary fluoride. The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary stoichiometry combined with high thermodynamic stability and low-temperature ionic mobility in solution. The most critical limiting factor is safe and clean handling of thallium and fluoride chemistry, especially preventing contamination, solubility losses, and possible volatile/corrosive HF exposure. Among the plausible methods, ambient aqueous precipitation/cooling crystallization is the most attractive first attempt, with solvothermal growth as the best backup for larger crystals.
