### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability cutoff [target]. NdPdSb₂ is on the hull [CS2, SS2], and LaPdSb₂/CePdSb₂ are only ~0.014/~0.009 eV/atom above hull [CS1, CS4], indicating that the rare-earth PdSb₂ P4/nmm family is thermodynamically plausible. TS analogues are less chemically exact and include near-hull metastable systems [TS1–TS5], so TS supports accessibility but not guaranteed ground-state behavior.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Arc melting plus annealing at 800 °C is directly reported for NdPdSb₂ [CS2, SS2], and similar P4/nmm antimonides use Ar arc melting, annealing, and quenching [CS1, CS4, SS1]. The main deductions come from vacuum/inert atmosphere, sealed handling of volatile Sb, multi-step processing, and Pr/Sb handling risks [CS2, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or sustained >1000 °C treatment is required [CS2].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Arc melting overcomes slow elemental diffusion by producing a homogenized alloy, and direct NdPdSb₂/LaPdSb₂ analogues confirm the same structure type [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3]. The long 100 h anneal and noted minor secondary phase for NdPdSb₂ reduce the kinetic/phase-purity score [CS2]. Remaining uncertainty is whether PrPdSb₂ has a narrower stability window than Nd/La analogues.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Solid-state Pr–Pd–Sb synthesis can require 900–1100 °C annealing and multiple heating steps [CS3]. Sealed ampoules under Ar or vacuum are needed for antimonides [CS3, TS2], and Sb volatility creates an additional containment penalty [TS5]. This route is harsher than the direct arc-melt route because it may exceed 1000 °C [CS3].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Elemental solid-state reactions are slower than flux or arc-melt homogenization because dense metals must interdiffuse [Reasonable Inference], although molten Sb may assist transport above its melting point [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 shows that Pr–Pd–Sb phases can form by heating elements, but it also indicates a different competing stoichiometry, Pr₃Pd₆Sb₅ [CS3]. Thus, kinetics and phase selection are only moderate.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux growth near 850 °C under vacuum is supported for layered REAuBi₂ [SS5], and no high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is needed [SS5]. Deductions arise from sealed vacuum tubes, large volatile/toxic Sb excess, and the multistep heat–slow-cool–decant process [SS5, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. The route is experimentally manageable but requires careful ampoule safety.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A liquid pnictogen flux provides high mass transport and should reduce diffusion limitations relative to solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], consistent with successful Bi-flux growth of related layered compounds [SS5]. The main uncertainty is that the provided flux example is Bi/Au-based rather than Sb/Pd-based [SS5], so direct transfer is inferred rather than proven. Composition control during decanting is likely the largest kinetic/phase-selection issue.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
High-pressure rare-earth antimonide synthesis uses 15–70 kbar and up to 1800 °C [TS1], making this route much more severe than CS/SS ambient-pressure routes. Specialized tetrahedral anvil equipment and high-temperature quenching are required [TS1]. Because direct PdSb₂ analogues synthesize without high pressure [CS1, CS2], this route is a fallback rather than optimal.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
High T and pressure can accelerate reaction and stabilize dense/metastable phases [TS1], but the short dwell and cell-limited sample volume make homogeneity uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. TS1 is not structurally close to the target P4/nmm Pd antimonide, so structural reconstruction confidence is low [TS1]. CS/SS ambient analogues provide much better kinetic transferability [CS1, CS2, SS2].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target belongs to a small-cell ordered intermetallic family, which lowers amorphization risk [CS1, CS2, SS2]. However, the large rare-earth/transition-metal size contrast and mixed edge/corner/face-sharing motifs in close analogues raise disorder/stacking-defect risk [CS1, CS4, SS1, Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for amorphization tendency and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PrPdSb₂ is likely a **near-hull metastable or borderline stable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [target]. The strongest support comes from chemically and structurally close LaPdSb₂ and NdPdSb₂, which share the P4/nmm RTSb₂ structure and are synthesized by elemental arc melting plus annealing [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a robust rare-earth PdSb₂ structure family across neighboring rare-earth elements [CS1, CS2, CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control involving volatile Sb and related Pr–Pd–Sb/Pd–Sb phases [CS2, CS3, TS5]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible in a well-equipped intermetallic laboratory, with flux growth and arc-melt annealing as the most promising routes.
