### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to lie on the convex hull [target data]. This is reinforced by multiple chemically and thermodynamically similar Nb–Se–Cl compounds also having E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including NbSe₂Cl₂, Nb₃Se₅Cl₇, and NbSeCl₃ (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computational ground-state stability does not guarantee an accessible kinetic pathway or phase purity under sealed-tube conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 400–515 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS1/SS5/TS5). However, it requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile halogen/chalcogen transport, multiple practical CVT handling steps, and corrosive/moisture-sensitive halide chemistry (CS1/SS5/TS5; Reasonable Inference). The main uncertainty is whether Zr chloride volatility and transport equilibria will match Nb chloride analogues closely enough [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

CVT improves kinetics by using volatile halide species, which is supported by successful growth of large NbX₂Y₂ crystals by chemical transport (CS1/SS5/TS5). The target is structurally very close to NbSe₂Cl₂, so the reconstruction burden is relatively low compared with forming a completely unrelated structure (CS1/SS5/TS5; target data). Long growth times and reported phase/polymorph sensitivity in NbX₂Y₂ reduce the kinetics/phase-selectivity confidence (CS1/SS5/TS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions and high pressure, matching NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ synthesis at ~530 °C and NbCl₅/Nb/Se synthesis near 477 °C (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile chloride/selenium species, and air-sensitive chloride handling (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference). The evidence is strong for Nb analogues but indirect for Zr, so halide activity may require optimization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Using ZrSe₂ and ZrCl₄ should provide more chemically activated Zr–Se and Zr–Cl building blocks than elemental Zr alone, by analogy to NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ forming Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ (CS2/SS2/TS2). Kinetics are still limited by solid-state diffusion and structural rearrangement into the Se–Se dimerized layered target [Reasonable Inference]. Phase control is a significant uncertainty because Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ reproducibility was poor and NbSeCl₃ was obtained as a low-yield side-product in inhomogeneous mixtures (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses higher but still sub-1000 °C ampoule temperatures, consistent with Zr/Hf selenide syntheses at 800 °C (SS4/TS4). It still requires vacuum sealing and volatile Se/chloride containment, so the same sealed-tube and precursor hazard penalties apply (SS4/TS4, CS4; Reasonable Inference). Its uncertainty is greater because high temperature may destabilize the desired chloride-rich layered compound [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Elemental Zr has slow solid-state kinetics due to its refractory nature [Reasonable Inference], even though Zr/Hf selenides can form in sealed ampoules at 800 °C (SS4/TS4). Complete assembly of the layered ZrSe₂Cl₂ framework from elements is more demanding than starting from ZrSe₂/ZrCl₄ [Reasonable Inference]. By-product formation in high-temperature halide chalcogenide synthesis, as seen for CrSBr, further lowers confidence (CS4).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered [target data]. The described C2/m 2D layered structure does not indicate a very large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [target data]. The amorphization risk is therefore low, although local disorder in Se/Cl or stacking faults could still occur in a layered material [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(SeCl)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target data], and several Nb–Se–Cl analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, CS2, CS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). The analogue support is strong chemically and structurally: NbSe₂Cl₂ is a very close 2D MSe₂Cl₂ layered compound made by CVT (CS1, SS5, TS5). The most important enabling factor is volatile halide/chalcogen sealed-tube chemistry, which can transport and equilibrate components at moderate temperature (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS5). The key limiting factor is phase control, because related Nb–Se–Cl systems show polymorphs, poor reproducibility, low-yield side-products, and sensitivity to stoichiometry/temperature gradients (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful ampoule preparation, stoichiometry tuning, and possibly several gradient/temperature trials.
