### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is on the boundary of the high-synthesizability bin in the requested rubric. CS analogues include stable or near-stable Na/Mg/tellurate-related oxides, including NaMgLaTeO6 at E_hull = 0 [CS1] and Na2Cu2TeO6 at ~0.005 eV/atom [CS4]. SS analogues include stable ilmenite-derived Na2GeTeO6 and near-stable R3 Li2ZrTeO6 [SS2, SS4]. TS analogues around 0.05–0.06 eV/atom are experimentally accessible [TS2–TS5], but TS1 warns that some Na oxide polymorphs near higher metastability need high pressure.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses air, ambient pressure, and ≤850 °C, which is mild relative to many ceramic oxides and is directly supported by NaMgLaTeO6 made at 773/973 K in air [CS1, SS1]. The deductions arise from multistep decomposition/grinding/annealing and Te precursor toxicity [Reasonable Inference; Te sources used in CS1 and SS2–SS5]. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Nitrate/hydroxide/Te(OH)6 precursors are highly reactive, as shown by NaMgLaTeO6 synthesis at only 973 K for 6 h [CS1, SS1]. The target’s Sc³⁺ site adds diffusion/ordering difficulty because Sc–Te–O crystal formation used long flux heating from Sc2O3 [SS5], but using Sc nitrate should reduce this [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally, the R3 ilmenite-derived network is directly supported by Li2ZrTeO6 [SS2], giving a favorable but not perfect kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 2

The oxide/carbonate route is standard and uses air, ambient pressure, and standard crucibles, matching Li2ZrTeO6, Sr2MgTeO6, and Na2GeTeO6 syntheses [SS2–SS4]. It remains multistep, with calcination, repeated grinding, and extended annealing [SS2–SS4]. It is not penalized for high pressure or controlled atmosphere because those are absent in the SS ceramic examples [SS2–SS4].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are weaker because Sc2O3 is refractory and oxide/carbonate mixtures are less reactive than nitrate/hydroxide mixtures [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Related tellurates needed multi-day or multi-temperature treatments, including 4 days for Li2ZrTeO6 and stepwise 600–1000 °C heating for Na2GeTeO6 [SS2, SS4]. The structural match is good, but repeated cycles and possible cation ordering lower the score [SS2, SS4].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux synthesis can remain below 850 °C using nitrate or chloride fluxes, supported by Sc–Te–O flux growth at 850/650 °C [SS5] and flux growth of Na2BaMgP2O8 [CS3]. The recommended nitrate flux avoids evacuated sealed ampoules, while the chloride flux variant would add atmosphere/sealing penalties [SS5]. The route is still multistep and uses toxic Te compounds [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux improves transport and can overcome Sc2O3 reactivity limitations; this is supported by Sc–Te–O crystals grown in CsCl flux [SS5]. Na2BaMgP2O8 flux growth also supports molten-flux crystallization of complex Na/Mg oxides [CS3]. The uncertainty is that no source demonstrates the exact Na–Mg–Sc–Te quaternary phase from flux, so phase selectivity and impurity incorporation remain concerns.

#### F2 — Route 4

The high-pressure route is penalized strongly because TS1 used 10.5 GPa, 1150 °C, Au capsules, quenching, and decompression [TS1]. Such conditions are far more severe than CS/SS ambient-pressure tellurate routes [CS1, SS2, SS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS suggests high pressure is needed for the target, so this route is exploratory rather than preferred.

#### F3 — Route 4

Using pre-reacted powder may help kinetics, as TS1 first formed ilmenite NaSbO3 before HPHT conversion [TS1]. However, the target is already ilmenite-derived and has ambient-pressure structural analogues [SS2, SS4], so HPHT may promote unwanted dense polymorphs rather than the target [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetics score is therefore only moderate despite high temperature.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size/charge diversity and mixed corner/edge/face-sharing octahedral connectivity, both of which raise disorder and amorphization risk [Target; SS4 shows similar mixed connectivity in an ilmenite-derived tellurate]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral/not triggered. The score is medium because two structural complexity checks are triggered, but crystalline analogues are known [SS2, SS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaMgScTeO6 is likely a marginally metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and comparable TS materials near 0.05–0.06 eV/atom have been synthesized [TS2–TS5]. The strongest support comes from SS: Li2ZrTeO6 is an ilmenite-derived R3 tellurate made by air solid-state synthesis, and Na2GeTeO6 is another ilmenite-derived Na tellurate [SS2, SS4]. CS support is also strong because NaMgLaTeO6 uses the same Na–Mg–Te chemical framework and is made from reactive nitrate/hydroxide/Te(OH)6 precursors in air [CS1]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining Te(VI) under oxidizing conditions while using reactive precursors [CS1, SS2, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is slow Sc incorporation/cation ordering, especially if using refractory Sc2O3 [SS5; Reasonable Inference].
