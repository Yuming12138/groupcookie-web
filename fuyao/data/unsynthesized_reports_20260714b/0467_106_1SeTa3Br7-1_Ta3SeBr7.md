### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the provided stability scale. [Target] This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues such as Nb3Br8 and several Ta/Nb chloride cluster phases that also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. [TS2/TS3/TS4] The main uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee easy experimental phase isolation, as Ta3SeI7 and Ta3TeI7 analogues show side products despite low E_hull values. [SS3/SS5]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses only moderate temperature, ~550 °C, which is favorable. [CS1/SS1/TS1] However, it requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile Br2, glovebox/vacuum-line handling, and multiple processing steps, all of which reduce the condition-severity score. [CS1/SS1/TS1] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether Se substitution lowers or raises the required temperature relative to Ta3SBr7, so the score remains conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1

The closest analogue forms under direct elemental reaction, which supports the kinetic feasibility of Route 1. [CS1/SS1/TS1] The limiting kinetic factor is refractory Ta, especially if foil is used; nevertheless, halogen vapor likely assists transport, since Ta3SBr7 forms at 550 °C, far below the melting point of Ta. [CS1/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference] Long reaction and crystal-growth times in the analogue indicate slow ordering and/or phase equilibration. [CS1/SS1/TS1]

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains below 1000 °C, as Nb3Br8 CVT is reported over 500–760 °C and Ta3SBr7 crystal growth uses ~505–495 °C. [SS2/TS2; CS1/SS1/TS1] The severity arises from high-vacuum ampoule sealing, volatile halogens, and multistep transport/purification. [SS2/TS2] The uncertainty is that iodine transport may introduce iodide substitution because Ta3SeI7 is itself a stable related compound. [SS3]

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT can improve mass transport and crystal growth, as shown for Nb3Br8 and Ta3SBr7. [SS2/TS2; CS1/SS1/TS1] However, no source directly demonstrates CVT growth of Ta3SeBr7, and adding I2 could shift chemistry toward Ta3SeI7-like phases. [SS3; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ta3SeBr7 CVT] The kinetics are therefore rated moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids direct Br2 dosing by using TaBr5, but it still requires dry atmosphere, sealed-tube conditions, and an air-sensitive halide precursor. [CS2; TS3/TS4] The temperature range is moderate, supported by Ta chloride cluster reactions at 600–800 °C and the Ta3SBr7 synthesis at 550 °C. [TS3/TS4; CS1/SS1/TS1] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact TaBr5 vapor pressure and optimal Ta3SeBr7 temperature window, so the score is not raised further.

#### F3 — Route 3

TaBr5 should increase precursor reactivity compared with Ta metal alone, consistent with NbBr5 + Nb and TaCl5 + Ta routes to reduced halide frameworks. [CS2; TS3/TS4] The route may still suffer from competing TaBr5-rich or reduced Ta bromide phases, because Ta3SBr7 synthesis explicitly reports TaBr5 as a side product. [CS1/SS1/TS1] The lack of a direct Ta3SeBr7 TaBr5 route is the major uncertainty. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ta, so cation-size disorder is not a concern. [Target] Its structure is an ordered 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet rather than a mixed-connectivity network, which lowers amorphization risk. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta3SeBr7 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. [Target] The strongest support comes from Ta3SBr7, which is essentially the S analogue with the same Ta3QBr7 layered motif and is synthesized by direct sealed-tube reaction.
