### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which sits at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom cutoff and is therefore scored 90 (target data). TS provides strong support because several thermodynamically similar compounds at ~0.05–0.07 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, including RbPrNb₂O₇ and Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ (TS1, TS2). CS and SS also support synthesizability of related K/Rb–Ta/Nb oxides, including K₂SrTa₂O₇ and KTaO₃ (CS5, SS1, SS2). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is borderline, so the target may be weakly metastable rather than a robust ground state.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct ceramic synthesis

Condition severity is moderate because analogous K–Ta oxides are made in air using standard ceramic conditions, but temperatures above 1000 °C and repeated grinding/firing are likely (CS5, SS1, SS2). K loss is a known issue in K-containing tantalates, requiring excess K₂CO₃ or powder covering (CS5, SS1). Kinetics are the main weakness because Ta₂O₅ is refractory and analogues require high-temperature sintering or long anneals (SS1, SS3, SS4). The target’s TaO₅/VO₅ trigonal-bipyramidal framework differs from common TaO₆ perovskite analogues, so full reconstruction may be required [target structure; SS1/SS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Nitrate/carbonate molten-salt-assisted synthesis

Condition severity is relatively favorable because CS2/CS3 demonstrate alkali nitrate/carbonate routes in air without high pressure or special atmosphere. The route still involves multiple operations and toxic V₂O₅ [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, molten nitrate/alkali salt should improve precursor activity compared with dry oxide diffusion, and CS2/CS3 use excess alkali nitrate and milling/washing for related alkali niobate/tantalate oxides. The uncertainty is that the CS2/CS3 structures are layered perovskites rather than the target Cm trigonal-bipyramidal network, so phase selectivity remains uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Alkali vanadate/V₂O₅ flux route

Flux processing has moderate condition severity because SS5 and TS3 show oxide flux/melt growth at ambient pressure with slow cooling, and no high pressure is required. A K–V–O flux is inferred rather than directly demonstrated, so this route is more speculative than the ceramic route [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, flux growth is attractive because it can dissolve refractory Ta₂O₅ and crystallize the target from solution rather than requiring solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference; flux precedent from SS5/TS3]. The limiting uncertainty is that V₂O₅/KVO₃ flux may preferentially stabilize simple potassium vanadates or leave Ta₂O₅ undissolved [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/charge contrast between K⁺ and high-valent Ta⁵⁺/V⁵⁺, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The target connectivity is described as corner-sharing TaO₅ and VO₅ trigonal bipyramids, so the mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity check is not triggered [target structure]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization evidence for K₂TaVO₆; therefore, those sources are insufficient for this specific factor and are treated as neutral where not structurally informative. The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂TaVO₆ is likely a borderline stable or weakly metastable crystalline oxide because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the favorable cutoff but not clearly on the convex hull (target data). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of related alkali tantalates/niobates by standard ceramic methods and, in some cases, flux or molten-salt approaches (CS5, SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the compatibility of high-valent d⁰ Ta⁵⁺/V⁵⁺ oxides with oxidizing air synthesis and the availability of alkali-rich flux/ceramic routes (CS5, SS1, SS5, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: K loss, V₂O₅ volatility, refractory Ta₂O₅, and competing K–V or K–Ta oxides can all interfere [Reasonable Inference; K-loss issue supported by CS5/SS1]. Overall, the best strategy is likely flux-assisted crystallization followed by careful XRD-guided optimization.
