### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the strongest thermodynamic stability category. Many close analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including TlGaCl4 and TlAlCl4 in CS-1/CS-2, Tl2SnCl6 in CS-4/SS-3/TS-3, and InBr3 in SS-4/TS-4. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to hydrolysis/reduction.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, because TlAlCl4/TlGaCl4 form at 150 °C and related TlGaBr4/TlGaI4 at 200 °C in sealed ampoules (CS-1, CS-2), while AlCl3-type InBr3 is handled around 190–100 °C (SS-4, TS-4). The severity comes from vacuum/dry sealed handling and volatile/toxic halides, supported by glove-box/vacuum ampoule requirements in CS-1/CS-2 and strictly anhydrous requirements in SS-4/TS-4. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or high temperature is needed.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Halide precursors should react faster than refractory oxides, and CS-1/CS-2 show direct reactions at 150–200 °C, supporting favorable kinetics. SS-4/TS-4 show the structural motif of edge-sharing octahedral indium halide sheets, which reduces reconstruction difficulty relative to a completely unrelated structure. The uncertainty is cation ordering between Tl and In in a 2D sheet, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct demonstration.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it likely requires a pre-synthesis step plus sealed temperature-gradient growth. SS-4/TS-4 support anhydrous temperature-gradient sublimation for InBr3, while CS-1/CS-2 support sealed ampoules for Tl group-13 halides. The main penalties are volatile chloride transport, air sensitivity, and possible multistep processing.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
SS-4/TS-4 directly support crystallization of an AlCl3-type indium halide by sublimation, so crystal growth kinetics are plausible. SS-1/TS-1 also show volatile chloride crystallization can be controlled at low temperature. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Tl and In chloride vapor pressures match well enough for congruent transport, so composition control is the limiting kinetic factor.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is mild in temperature because CS-3 uses 80 °C for HgGaCl4 solution crystallization, and SS-2/TS-2 use room temperature or reflux for chloro-complex formation. The main severity is strict dry handling, as CS-3 specifies deoxygenated/dried N2 with <1 ppm O2/H2O and SS-2/TS-2 use drybox operations. No high pressure or extreme temperature is supported by CS/SS/TS.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solution mixing provides high precursor activity and avoids bulk diffusion barriers, supported by rapid low-temperature chloride solution reactions in CS-3 and SS-2/TS-2. The weak point is structural reconstruction: CS-3 and SS-2/TS-2 mainly yield zero-dimensional molecular/chloro-complex phases, not layered AlCl3-derived sheets. Therefore Route 3 scores high kinetically but carries structural-transfer uncertainty.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, and no mixed vertex/edge/face network is described; this lowers amorphization risk. The Tl–Cl and In–Cl bond lengths are close, suggesting compatible octahedral site sizes [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the exact target unit-cell atom count, but no provided target description indicates a large complex cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlInCl6 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar chlorides are also hull-stable (CS-1 to CS-5; SS-1 to SS-4; TS-1 to TS-5). The analogue support is good for low-temperature halide chemistry: CS-1/CS-2 support sealed-ampoule Tl/group-13 chloride synthesis, and SS-4/TS-4 support AlCl3-type layered indium halide crystallization. The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of anhydrous chloride precursors at only ~100–220 °C, as shown by CS-1/CS-2, CS-3, and SS-4/TS-4. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Tl in the required chloride/oxidation environment and preventing loss or disproportionation of volatile/toxic Tl/In chlorides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will require strict anhydrous, chloride-rich, carefully sealed conditions.
