### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0420 eV/atom**, placing it in the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom window and justifying a high score of 92. TS provides the strongest contextual support because several thermodynamically similar halide/chalcogenide materials with E_hull ≈0.021–0.067 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by hydrothermal or sealed-tube methods (TS-1 to TS-5). SS also supports synthesizability of slightly metastable Ag iodide analogues, especially Ag₃SI at E_hull 0.0668 eV/atom (SS-4). CS materials are very close to the hull but are CdI₂ polytypes rather than Ag–I compounds, so CS gives insufficient Ag-specific thermodynamic evidence and is treated as neutral for this factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Acidic iodide hydrothermal / solvothermal crystallization in HI

The condition severity is moderate because TS-3 uses only 155–175 °C hydrothermal treatment, but HI/I₂ handling and sealed autogenous pressure add hazards. Kinetics should be favorable because TS-3 demonstrates AgI reacts/crystallizes in aqueous HI, and TS-1 shows concentrated HX hydrothermal media can yield halide crystals with controlled cooling. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural selectivity: TS-3 reports twinning and low yield of untwinned crystals, so nucleation of the exact P1 layered Ag–I phase may be difficult. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for AgI solubility, but it supports the general idea that layered iodides can grow from solution under mild conditions (CS-1 to CS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-temperature sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction / iodine vapor-assisted anneal

F2 is lower because SS and TS analogues commonly require evacuated sealed tubes (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, TS-4, TS-5), and the proposed binary AgI + I₂ route adds volatile iodine overpressure [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are acceptable for soft iodides because SS-1 forms an Ag iodide analogue at 120 °C and SS-3 at 473 K, but long reaction times indicate slow ordering. SS-1 requires 4 months and SS-4 reports prolonged annealing for phase stabilization, so the structural-reconstruction component is penalized. TS supports sealed-tube halide synthesis but does not directly prove binary Ag–I vapor annealing, so that part remains uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Melt / Bridgman-type crystallization

SS-2 directly supports AgI-containing melt growth near 700 °C, and liquid transport gives high kinetic mobility. However, iodine-rich target formation from a melt is more severe because I₂ is volatile and high temperature may shift stoichiometry or favor AgI-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 reports phase mixtures from which Ag₃SI crystals are separated, and SS-4 shows phase-specific annealing/quenching issues; these observations lower the structural-selectivity score. CS provides little useful evidence for melt growth, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Room-temperature slow evaporation / diffusion crystallization

CS strongly supports mild solution growth of layered iodides: CdI₂ crystals are obtained by aqueous evaporation or solution growth at room temperature/ambient conditions (CS-1 to CS-5). However, the chemical transfer is weak because CdI₂ is soluble whereas AgI is poorly soluble; therefore, HI/I₂ complexation is required [Reasonable Inference], with TS-3 providing the AgI + HI precedent. F2 is excellent due to low temperature and ambient pressure, but F3 is lower than Route 1 because slow evaporation may not maintain the iodine activity and supersaturation needed for the target. Phase/polytype control concerns in CS-2 to CS-4 and twinning in TS-3 are the principal uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ag, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; no CS/SS/TS source suggests a hidden mixed-cation disorder issue for the target. The described P1 formula unit contains six Ag and eight iodine sites/clusters, below the >40 atom threshold. Although the target has multiple Ag coordination environments, the provided structure description does not establish simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that low symmetry and iodine clustering may still produce disorder, but this is not the same as the specified amorphization triggers.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **slightly metastable but accessible** phase because its E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom, below the 0.05 eV/atom practical metastability threshold, while SS and TS contain experimentally synthesized analogues at comparable or even higher E_hull values. The strongest support comes from TS-3, where AgI reacts in aqueous HI to form a complex Ag iodide, and from SS-1/SS-3/SS-4, where AgI-containing halides form in sealed low-to-moderate-temperature conditions. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a high-iodide/high-iodine-activity medium, especially HI/I₂, to dissolve AgI and stabilize the iodine-rich target composition. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing AgI, polyiodide salts, twinned crystals, or alternate Ag–I arrangements may form, as suggested by twinning in TS-3, phase mixtures in SS-2, and polytype-control issues in CS-2 to CS-4. Overall, hydrothermal HI/I₂ crystallization is the preferred route because it balances low temperature, high precursor mobility, and direct Ag iodide precedent.
