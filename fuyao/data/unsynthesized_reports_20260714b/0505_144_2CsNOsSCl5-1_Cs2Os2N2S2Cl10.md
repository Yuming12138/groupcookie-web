### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly indicates a ground-state composition in the computed phase diagram [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull = 0 are experimentally accessible by evaporation, hydrothermal, direct chlorination, and sealed-tube methods [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5]. Chemically similar stable alkali chalcogenides are also synthesized by flux/solid-state methods [CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to hydrolysis during synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses mild temperature and ambient pressure, consistent with Cs₂RhOCl₅ slow evaporation [SS1, TS4] and Mo–N–S–Cl solution chemistry [SS4]. It avoids high temperature, GPa pressure, and sealed volatile-reactant pressure penalties. The main severity penalty is reagent handling: Os chlorides and (NSCl)₃ are toxic/moisture-sensitive [TS2, SS4]. CS gives limited direct signal for Os–N–S–Cl solution chemistry; insufficient evidence from CS for the key N–S ligand installation, scored neutral qualitatively.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because the reaction is solution-mediated, as in SS1 and SS4, rather than requiring long-range diffusion through dense solids [SS1, SS4]. Structural assembly is the main uncertainty: Cs₂RhOCl₅ closely supports Cs–M–Cl₅ terminal-ligand crystallization [SS1, TS4], but the target’s linear Os–N–S unit is only indirectly supported by Mo–N–S–Cl chemistry [SS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS for the exact N–S–Cl coordination environment; CS3 supports chloride sheet crystallization by evaporation but not Os–N–S chemistry [CS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile halogen/thiazyl reagents are involved, as in WCl₆ transport and Re chalcogenide chloride syntheses [SS2, SS5, TS5]. OsCl₄ synthesis itself may require 525 °C and 6–8 atm chlorine to prevent dissociation [TS2]. No GPa pressure or >1000 °C condition is needed, so the difficulty remains challenging rather than prohibitive. CS analogues support sealed-tube/flux handling generally [CS1, CS4, CS5], but insufficient evidence from CS for sealed Os–N–S–Cl formation specifically.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reactive chlorides and possible vapor transport reduce diffusion barriers, supported by SS2, SS5, and TS5 where halide-rich compounds form at 150–400 °C [SS2, SS5, TS5]. However, solid CsCl and OsCl₄ require assembly into a layered CsOsNSCl₅ sheet, and thiazyl ligand incorporation may compete with decomposition or side products [Reasonable Inference from SS4 and TS2]. Structural reconstruction is therefore moderate. SS and TS provide useful signal; CS is less direct because the CS flux examples are sulfides rather than chloronitrosulfide Os phases [CS1, CS2, CS5].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits sealed-ampoule and volatile/corrosive solvent penalties from SOCl₂ solvothermal chemistry [SS3]. The temperature is mild, around 120–160 °C, matching SS3 and TS3 [SS3, TS3]. Toxic and air-sensitive reagents are unavoidable because SOCl₂, Os chlorides, and thiazyl chloride sources are reactive [SS3, SS4, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS for SOCl₂-mediated synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

SOCl₂ provides a reactive chlorinating/dehydrating medium, and SS3 reports nearly quantitative conversion in a sealed ampoule at 150 °C [SS3]. The solution/solvothermal environment should minimize ion diffusion limitations, consistent with TS3 hydrothermal halide crystallization at 150 °C [TS3]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is selective formation of the Os–N–S terminal ligand without hydrolysis or overchlorination; SS4 supports M–N–S–Cl chemistry but only for Mo [SS4]. Thus F3 is good but not excellent.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes, with large high-coordinate Cs and small octahedral Os, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The formula unit is small, and there is insufficient evidence that the unit cell atom count exceeds 40 [Target]. The described structure does not explicitly contain mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, so that check is not triggered [Target]. Uncertainty remains because low-temperature solution routes may form molecular or poorly ordered salts if crystallization is too rapid.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsOsNSCl₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is fairly strong structurally: Cs₂RhOCl₅ demonstrates crystallization of Cs-containing pentachloro transition-metal units with a terminal ligand [SS1, TS4], while MoCl₅/(NSCl)₃ chemistry demonstrates that N–S–Cl ligands can bind early transition-metal chlorides [SS4]. Thermodynamic support is also strong because multiple hull-stable halide analogues are experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5]. The most important enabling factor is low-temperature chloride-rich crystallization, which should preserve volatile/reactive N–S–Cl fragments [SS1, SS3, SS4]. The main limiting factor is selective installation of the Os–N–S unit without hydrolysis, overchlorination, or decomposition of thiazyl chloride reagents [SS4, TS2].
