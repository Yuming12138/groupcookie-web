### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high end of the 90–100 scoring bin. CS supports synthesis of chemically related oxides even when metastable: CS-2 has E_hull ≈0.167 eV/atom, CS-4 ≈0.094 eV/atom, and CS-5 ≈0.124 eV/atom. TS supports similar metastability because TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, and TS-5 are experimentally reported around 0.060–0.090 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity does not guarantee the specific Ti–Cr–Ag ordering in triclinic P1.

#### F2: Route 1 — High-pressure oxide solid-state

Route 1 is severe because CS-1, CS-2, and CS-5 require 9–15 GPa and 900–1000 °C-class conditions for chemically similar multication oxides. The target’s Ag content adds volatility/reduction risk, which is not directly resolved by CS and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence that high pressure is necessary for this exact structure, but CS strongly supports high pressure as a way to stabilize unusual transition-metal oxide arrangements.

#### F3: Route 1 — High-pressure oxide solid-state

Kinetics are only moderate-to-poor because TiO₂ and Cr₂O₃ are refractory oxides, and target formation likely requires substantial cation rearrangement. CS analogues often use short high-pressure holds, suggesting high thermodynamic driving force under pressure but also specialized kinetic activation. The structural mismatch between CS perovskite-type phases and the P1 target lowers the structural reconstruction score.

#### F2: Route 2 — Ag⁺ ion exchange

Route 2 has mild exchange conditions because TS-3 reports AgLaTiO₄ from molten AgNO₃ at 250 °C for 12 h, and TS-5 reports low-temperature ion exchange at 325 °C. The main severity deductions are the multistep requirement and the oxidizing/toxic nature of AgNO₃. CS gives insufficient evidence for this factor because CS routes are high-pressure solid-state rather than ion exchange.

#### F3: Route 2 — Ag⁺ ion exchange

Kinetics are favorable if a suitable parent exists, because Ag⁺ exchange in molten nitrate is demonstrated by TS-3 and titanate ion exchange is demonstrated by TS-1/SS-3. Structural reconstruction could be low if the exchange is topotactic, but no source proves an alkali Ti–Cr parent with the required P1 topology. Therefore the F3 score is good but capped by the parent-phase uncertainty.

#### F2: Route 3 — Sealed-tube flux / low-temperature solid-state

Route 3 avoids high pressure and very high temperature, supported by SS-4/SS-5 flux reactions at 800 °C and SS-2 evacuated-tube oxide reactions at 700–900 °C. Severity remains moderate because sealed tubes, volatile Ag precursors, controlled oxygen fugacity, and flux removal are required. TS-2 shows Ag–Ti oxides can survive air firing at 1000 °C, but that evidence does not include Cr and does not remove the Ag volatility concern.

#### F3: Route 3 — Sealed-tube flux / low-temperature solid-state

Flux improves precursor activity and diffusion, as SS-4/SS-5 explicitly state chloride salts homogenize reaction mixtures. However, direct assembly from TiO₂, Cr₂O₃, and Ag salts requires complete structural reconstruction into a low-symmetry P1 phase. SS-2 reports multiple compounds from similar evacuated-tube conditions, implying phase selectivity may be difficult.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has strong cation-size and coordination diversity: Ti is 4–5 coordinate, Cr is 5 coordinate, and Ag is linear, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination mismatch criterion. The described P1 cell has only 10 atoms in the formula description, so the large-cell criterion is not triggered. The target description gives corner-sharing TiO₄–CrO₅ connectivity but no edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the connectivity criterion is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂CrAgO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a strongly stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS supports synthesis of chemically complex Cr-containing oxides, but mostly under high pressure, while TS supports Ag–Ti oxides and metastable Ag incorporation by ion exchange. The most important enabling factor is the moderate thermodynamic accessibility combined with precedent for low-temperature Ag⁺ topochemical incorporation from TS-3. The most important limiting factor is the lack of a demonstrated Ti–Cr alkali parent framework for ion exchange; without that parent, high-pressure or flux synthesis may be needed. Overall, synthesis is plausible but phase-purity control will likely dominate experimental difficulty.
