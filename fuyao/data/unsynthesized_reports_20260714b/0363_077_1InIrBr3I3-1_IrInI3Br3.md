### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0280 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability scoring bin and closely matches experimentally synthesized analogues such as CS3 at ~0.029 eV/atom and TS2 at ~0.028 eV/atom. TS4 at ~0.031 eV/atom also indicates that phases in this energy range can be accessible. The uncertainty is that CS2 shows low E_hull alone does not ensure phase purity, so thermodynamics are favorable but not decisive.

**F2 — Route 1 CVT severity.**  
CVT requires sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile halogens, as seen in CS1, CS5, SS2, and TS2, so deductions are applied for vacuum/controlled atmosphere and volatile sealed-tube operation. Temperatures are modest, generally 150–430 °C in the analogues, so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty is applied. The main uncertainty is the lack of direct Ir–In mixed-halide CVT evidence; SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ir transport chemistry.

**F3 — Route 1 CVT kinetics.**  
CS1, CS5, SS2, and TS2 show that halide crystals can grow by vapor transport at low-to-intermediate temperature, supporting good crystallization kinetics. However, CS5 reports low yields/equilibrium mixtures and TS2 reports by-products, indicating composition and phase control are not trivial. Mixed I/Br ordering in the target is likely a narrow-window problem [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 direct solid-state severity.**  
CS2–CS4 show direct sealed-tube solid-state synthesis at 400–600 °C without external high pressure, giving a moderate F2 score. Vacuum sealing and volatile/air-sensitive halides require deductions, consistent with CS2, CS3, and CS4. This route avoids the extra multi-step CVT/flux handling if one firing suffices, but that is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2 direct solid-state kinetics.**  
Halide precursors are chemically active compared with oxides, as supported by CS2–CS4 and SS2. The limiting factor is Ir: direct diffusion/reconstruction involving a refractory transition metal is likely slow [Reasonable Inference], and CS2 shows phase purity can be difficult even for low-E_hull analogues. Thus kinetics are less favorable than for a flux route.

**F2 — Route 3 flux severity.**  
The flux route uses low-to-moderate temperatures supported by SS1, SS2, and TS3, but it still requires dry handling, sealed ampoules, and volatile halide control. These conditions are standard for halide chemistry but impose safety and procedural penalties. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalties apply.

**F3 — Route 3 flux kinetics.**  
Molten indium halides and halogen-rich media should provide high ion mobility and chemical activity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with low-temperature crystallization of halides in SS1, SS2, and TS3. Slow cooling, as emphasized in SS2 and CS3, should allow ordering and crystal growth. The remaining uncertainty is whether Ir dissolves/reacts adequately in the InX₃/I–Br flux.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one likely amorphization trigger: a >15% size mismatch between octahedral Ir³⁺ and In³⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. The target description indicates edge-sharing octahedra only, so mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity is not triggered. CS/SS/TS do not directly measure amorphization for this target, so the score is based primarily on the structural description and ionic-radius reasoning.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

IrIn(IBr)₃ is likely a near-stable metastable or accessible low-energy phase rather than a highly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom and several CS/TS analogues in the 0.02–0.03 eV/atom range are experimentally synthesized. The CS/SS/TS data strongly support sealed-ampoule halide chemistry, especially low-temperature vapor/flux crystallization, but they do not directly demonstrate the exact Ir–In mixed I/Br layered framework. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of halide species in sealed ampoules or molten halide fluxes, supported by CS1, SS2, TS3, and TS2. The most critical limiting factor is mixed-halide and mixed-cation phase control: CS2 and CS5 show that related multicomponent halides/chalcohalides can suffer from impurities, low yield, or unreacted halides. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance coming from low-temperature halide flux crystallization under carefully buffered I/Br chemical potential.
