### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which falls in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom range. Chemically and thermodynamically similar MnInBr₃ has E_hull = 0.0041 eV/atom and was synthesized in quantitative yield (CS-2, TS-1), supporting that near-hull Mn bromides can be experimentally accessible. Several structural/thermodynamic analogues are also on or near the hull, including SS-1/SS-2/SS-3/SS-4 and TS-2/TS-4/TS-5, supporting halide phase accessibility. Remaining uncertainty is that no source directly reports MnAgBr₃, so the precise competing phases are not identified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures near 450 °C, directly supported by MnInBr₃ and CrInBr₃ molten salt syntheses (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1). The severity penalty comes from evacuated ampoules, volatile halide handling, multi-step loading/heating/slow cooling, and air/moisture sensitivity, all reported for related bromides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). There is insufficient evidence from SS for this exact molten bromide route, so SS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity. Overall F2 is moderate-low because the temperature is benign but ampoule/halide handling is nontrivial.

#### F3 — Route 1

Molten halide chemistry should provide high precursor activity and fast ion transport, as MnInBr₃/CrInBr₃ form at 450 °C over 5 days in evacuated ampoules (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1). The structural penalty reflects that the closest Mn bromide analogue is 1D MnInBr₃, while the target is 2D MnAgBr₃, requiring different cation ordering and sheet topology (CS-2/TS-1; target description). SS gives insufficient direct evidence for this route’s kinetics because its closest examples emphasize Hg/Ag chalcogenide halides rather than Mn–Ag bromides. The resulting F3 is favorable but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 2

Direct solid-state synthesis uses standard evacuated ampoules and moderate 350–450 °C annealing, consistent with binary-halide reactions in CS-5, SS-4, and TS-2. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile halide containment, likely multi-step grind/anneal/regrind practice, and air-sensitive halide handling (CS-5, SS-5/TS-3). CS/SS/TS support this as a general halide method, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms direct MnBr₂ + AgBr formation of MnAgBr₃. The F2 score is therefore the same as Route 1 but without the kinetic advantage of a flux.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary bromides are more reactive than refractory oxides, and many ternary halides are made from binary halides in sealed ampoules (CS-5, SS-4, TS-2). However, the target requires ordered MnBr₆ and AgAgBr₃ connectivity, and solid-state ordering may be slow, as analogues require long anneals or repeated processing (CS-5, SS-5/TS-3). There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that Ag⁺ and Mn²⁺ diffuse/order rapidly into the trigonal 2D phase. This justifies a moderate F3 rather than a high value.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from moderate temperatures and avoids explicitly reactive gases or high pressure, while being supported by melt/anneal synthesis of TlMnI₃ and melt handling of CuAlBr₄-type halides (SS-5/TS-3, CS-4). It still requires sealed ampoules, dry handling, and multiple steps, as shown by TlMnI₃ processing and halide melt routes (SS-5/TS-3, CS-4). CS gives support for melt processing but not for Mn–Ag–Br specifically; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for congruent MnAgBr₃ melting. F2 is the best among the routes because the preferred Ar-sealed implementation can avoid a formal vacuum penalty.

#### F3 — Route 3

A melt or partially molten path gives high ion mobility and good homogenization, supported by TlMnI₃ preparation from melted binary iodides and by CuAlBr₄ melt growth/quenching (SS-5/TS-3, CS-4). The main kinetic risk is selecting the desired trigonal 2D phase during crystallization rather than binary AgBr/MnBr₂ or alternative polymorphs; CS-4 shows cooling-rate-sensitive metastability and SS-5/TS-3 shows prolonged annealing may be needed. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the MnAgBr₃ liquidus/solidus behavior, so the crystallization score is not higher. Overall Route 3 has the best balance of diffusion and phase-ordering control.

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT/vapor-assisted growth is supported by related halides grown in evacuated ampoules with temperature gradients (SS-1, TS-5). It receives penalties for vacuum, volatile halide transport, multi-step pre-reaction plus transport, and air/moisture-sensitive halides (SS-1, TS-5; CS-1). CS gives insufficient evidence for Mn–Ag–Br CVT because the closest Mn bromide analogues use molten salt rather than transport (CS-1, CS-2). Thus the severity is comparable to other sealed-ampoule routes, with added vapor-transport hazards.

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor transport can grow high-quality crystals in structurally similar halides (SS-1, TS-5), but SS-1 also reports low yields and phase-purity challenges. No CS/SS/TS source confirms volatile Mn–Ag–Br species suitable for congruent transport, so precursor vapor availability and composition control remain uncertain. Br chemical potential could enable transport [Reasonable Inference], but could also drive AgBr/MnBr₂ segregation. Therefore F3 is the lowest of the proposed routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains unlike cation environments—MnBr₆ octahedra and AgAgBr₃ trigonal pyramids—which implies a significant cation size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The structure is not a large complex cell by the provided description, and no face-sharing connectivity is reported, so only one amorphization check is triggered. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data, so they are insufficient for F4 beyond showing many related halides crystallize under ampoule conditions (CS-1/CS-2, SS-5, TS-1/TS-3). The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnAgBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom, well inside the range where many inorganic phases are experimentally accessible. The strongest support comes from MnInBr₃, which is both chemically and thermodynamically similar and synthesized at 450 °C in evacuated ampoules with quantitative yield (CS-2, TS-1). Structurally similar halides support sealed-ampoule, solid-state, melt, and CVT routes, but they do not directly establish the trigonal 2D MnAgBr₃ topology (SS-1 to SS-5). The most important enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical softness of bromide/halide melts or fluxes, which should help overcome diffusion limitations (CS-1, CS-2, SS-5/TS-3). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity against MnBr₂, AgBr, and possible alternative Mn–Ag–Br polymorphs, especially because no direct MnAgBr₃ experimental analogue is provided.
