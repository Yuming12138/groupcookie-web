### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.025 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability scoring bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. [Target] Thermodynamic analogues include phases near the hull, such as CrSe₂ at 0.015 eV/atom and NaCr₅Se₈ at 0.014 eV/atom, that have reported syntheses, supporting the idea that small positive hull energies can still be accessible. [TS2/TS4] The remaining uncertainty is whether the target is a true equilibrium polymorph or a metastable layered Mn₂Se₃ polymorph requiring kinetic trapping. [Target/TS4]

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Route 1 uses standard evacuated silica/quartz tubes and moderate temperatures, which are well supported by MnSe₂ and related Mn/Cr/Fe selenide syntheses. [CS1/SS2/SS5/TS1] The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, volatile Se, toxic Se handling, and multiple firing steps. [CS1/TS1] Kinetically, dense Mn metal and structural mismatch between pyrite/tetrahedral Mn–Se phases and layered Mn₂Se₃ lower the score. [Target/CS1/CS4] The main uncertainty is phase competition among MnSe, MnSe₂, and Mn₂Se₃. [CS1/CS4/Target]

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, molten-salt flux growth

Route 2 is supported by CrSe₂, which is a 2D octahedral selenide sheet analogue synthesized in LiCl/KCl flux at 780 °C under vacuum with slow cooling. [CS5/TS4] The condition severity remains moderate because the method requires sealed vacuum ampoules, volatile Se chemistry, multistep precursor/flux processing, and flux removal. [CS5/TS4] The kinetics are better than direct solid-state synthesis because a molten salt flux enhances mass transport and crystal growth. [CS5/TS4] The main uncertainty is stoichiometric control for Mn₂Se₃ because the analogue is MSe₂ rather than M₂Se₃. [Target/CS5/TS4]

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, high-pressure synthesis

Route 3 is justified by high-pressure synthesis of chemically similar Cr₅Se₈ at 65–89 kbar and 1200 °C. [CS2] Its condition severity is significantly worse because it requires >5 GPa pressure, >1000 °C temperature, high-pressure capsules, and quenching. [CS2] Kinetics benefit from high temperature but not from flux-mediated transport, and structural transferability to the layered trigonal Mn₂Se₃ target is weak. [CS2/Target] Insufficient evidence from SS and TS specifically supports high-pressure Mn₂Se₃, so those signals are treated as neutral, score 50. [SS/TS]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Mn, so cation-radius disorder is not expected to drive amorphization. [Target] The reported structure is compositionally simple and does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell; CS/SS/TS do not provide evidence for a large-cell amorphization risk, so they are neutral for that sub-check. [Target/CS/SS/TS] The target has corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing coexistence, so the specified mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met. [Target] Remaining uncertainty is mostly crystallographic: if the experimentally realized cell is a large superstructure, F4 would decrease. [Target]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂Se₃ with E_hull = 0.025 eV/atom is likely a near-hull metastable or possibly temperature-stabilized phase rather than an obviously unstable compound. [Target] The analogue set supports synthesis well: chemically identical Mn–Se phases form in sealed evacuated tubes, and structurally related 2D CrSe₂ forms by flux growth under accessible conditions. [CS1/SS1/TS1/CS5/TS4] The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed ampoule or flux chemistry to maintain selenium chemical potential and improve mass transport. [CS1/CS5/TS4] The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with MnSe and MnSe₂, both of which are known Mn–Se phases with accessible syntheses. [CS1/CS4] Overall, flux-assisted synthesis is the best first attempt because it combines moderate conditions with the strongest structural analogy to the target layered octahedral sheet. [Target/CS5/TS4]
