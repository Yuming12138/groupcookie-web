### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull of 0.034 eV/atom places it in the high-synthesizability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. TS analogues are especially supportive because AgTl₂I₃ has E_hull = 0.0272 eV/atom and several thermodynamic analogues lie near 0.02–0.03 eV/atom (TS1, TS3–TS5). CS also supports the plausibility of layered iodide phases by showing CdI₂ polytypes essentially on the hull (CS3–CS5), while SS is mixed because Ag₃SI analogues include metastable phases up to 0.116 eV/atom (SS2, SS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is not the AgI ground-state polymorph, so kinetic trapping or phase selection is likely required [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-tube elemental synthesis.**  
F2 is reduced mainly by the need for an evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile/toxic iodine handling, consistent with sealed-tube practice in CS1 and Ag–I analogues in SS1/SS2/SS4. F3 is moderate because chemical reaction between Ag and I₂ should be facile [Reasonable Inference], but metal–solid diffusion and polymorph selection are less favorable than solution transport. SS2/SS3 and CS3–CS5 show that related Ag–I and layered iodide systems can require quenching, long annealing, or polytype selection, lowering the structural-reconstruction score.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, hydrothermal HI recrystallization.**  
F2 is favorable because TS1 uses only 155–175 °C hydrothermal conditions rather than extreme temperature or high pressure; the main severity penalty is corrosive/toxic HI. F3 is strong because solution-mediated transport should bypass bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and TS1 directly demonstrates AgI participation in aqueous HI to form Ag–iodide crystals. The main uncertainty is crystal/perfect-phase control because TS1 reports twinned crystals and low yield of untwinned crystals.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, vapor-phase transport.**  
F2 is moderate because vapor transport requires sealed evacuated ampoules and controlled temperature gradients, consistent with sealed iodide syntheses in SS1–SS4 and TS4–TS5. F3 is reasonably high because CS5 shows vapor-phase growth of layered CdI₂ polytypes, suggesting vapor recrystallization can work for heavy iodides. However, no source directly demonstrates vapor-grown P31m AgI, and iodine volatility creates composition and polymorph-control uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4, solution precipitation/crystallization.**  
F2 is mild because room-temperature aqueous crystallization is demonstrated for CdI₂ in CS3/CS4, and no high temperature or pressure is required. F3 kinetics are excellent for making AgI precipitate [Reasonable Inference], but structural selection is weak because rapid precipitation commonly gives kinetically uncontrolled powders [Reasonable Inference]. CS3–CS5 show that even simple layered iodides can form multiple polytypes or stacking variants, so phase-pure P31m AgI is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, so the ionic-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described structure is a small binary AgI sheet with 3-coordinate Ag/I environments, not a large mixed-polyhedral network [Target], so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization tendencies for the target; they instead show crystalline iodides are generally obtainable, so their direct F4 evidence is neutral to mildly favorable (CS3–CS5; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable AgI polymorph rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.034 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest analogue support comes from TS1, where AgI reacts/recrystallizes in aqueous HI under mild hydrothermal conditions, and from SS1/SS2/SS4, where AgI-containing crystalline phases are made in sealed low-to-moderate-temperature conditions. CS3–CS5 support the broader concept that layered iodides can crystallize under mild solution or vapor conditions, but they also warn that polytypism and stacking disorder may be significant. The key enabling factor is the high mobility/soft bonding of Ag–I chemistry, which allows low-temperature crystallization and annealing [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1, TS1]. The key limiting factor is selective stabilization of the specific trigonal P31m 2D sheet rather than common AgI polymorphs or stacking variants [Reasonable Inference supported by SS2/SS3 and CS3–CS5].
