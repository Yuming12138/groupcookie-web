### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E above hull is 0.0280 eV/atom, which lies in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic range of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally synthesized, including ReNCl₄ at 0.036 eV/atom (TS-1), Mo(SeCl₆)₂ at 0.034 eV/atom (TS-2), and WCl₆(Te)₂ at 0.024 eV/atom (TS-3). This supports the interpretation that RuSCl₂ may be a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly unstable hypothetical compound. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity analogues are mostly chloride-rich molecular/cluster systems rather than the same 2D Ru–S–Cl topology.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high external pressure, consistent with CS chalcogenide-halide sealed-tube reactions at 400–530 °C (CS-3–CS-5). The main deductions come from vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile sulfur/chlorine components, and toxic/corrosive chloride or halogen reagents, all of which are supported by SS Ru halide syntheses and CS/TS sealed-tube examples (SS-1, SS-2, CS-5, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a required ≥3-step repeated firing process, so no multi-step deduction was applied. The route is conditionally moderate but chemically hazardous.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct solid-state conversion may be slowed by refractory Ru chemistry [Reasonable Inference], despite using RuCl₃ rather than Ru metal. CS-3 and CS-5 show related chalcogenide halides often need weeks or careful transport gradients, implying slow ordering and narrow windows. SS-1/SS-2 show Ru–halide octahedral frameworks are accessible, but sulfur incorporation into the octahedral network requires partial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the kinetic score is modest.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has the same sealed reactive halogen atmosphere hazards as RuBr₃/RuCl₃ CVT in SS-1 and SS-2 and the same evacuated-gradient tube requirements as layered NbSe₂Cl₂ in CS-5. It also commonly requires a pre-reacted source powder, as SS-1 describes powder synthesis followed by crystal growth, so the process is treated as multi-step. No >1000 °C or >5 GPa penalty is justified because analogous CVT ranges are below those limits (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2). Thus condition severity is challenging but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves mass transport by forming volatile halide species, supported by successful transport growth in CS-5 and SS-1/SS-2. This largely bypasses slow bulk diffusion and is the strongest kinetic advantage of Route 2 [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction remains nontrivial because the target is a 2D mixed-anion RuSCl₂ sheet, whereas SS-1/SS-2 are binary Ru halide chains and CS-5 is Nb-based. The overall kinetic score is therefore relatively favorable but not high enough to ignore stoichiometry sensitivity.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from lower temperatures, supported by TS halide reactions at 140–190 °C (TS-1–TS-5) and solvothermal chloride chemistry at 473–573 K (SS-5). However, it still requires evacuated sealed ampoules, toxic volatile chloride solvents or fluxes, and air-sensitive handling, all supported by TS-4 and SS-5. Washing/drying or flux removal adds procedural complexity, as seen in SS-4 and inferred for chloride flux [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the severity score is similar to Route 2 despite lower temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Reactive chloride flux/solvothermal chemistry should provide high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], consistent with low-temperature crystallization in TS-4 and TS-5. However, most TS analogues are molecular or cluster chlorides rather than extended layered solids, so evidence for forming the target’s 2D RuSCl₂ network is weak. SS-5 warns that chloride-solvothermal systems can have multiple polymorphs and high phase sensitivity. This gives good transport kinetics but uncertain structural assembly.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no mixed-cation radius mismatch because Ru is the only cation in the formula, so C1 is not triggered. The target description does not provide a unit-cell atom count above 40, and CS/SS/TS give insufficient evidence to infer one, so C2 is treated as non-triggered. Although the target has edge- and corner-sharing RuS₃Cl₃ octahedra, the criterion requires vertex, edge, and face sharing together; face sharing is not reported for the target, while face-sharing in RuBr₃ analogues belongs to SS-1/SS-2 and cannot be transferred directly. The amorphization risk is therefore low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RuSCl₂ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a clearly unstable hypothetical phase, because its E above hull is only 0.028 eV/atom and TS analogues with comparable E_hull are experimentally accessible (TS-1–TS-3). The CS data strongly supports the broader feasibility of layered transition-metal chalcogenide halides under sealed-tube/CVT conditions, especially NbSe₂Cl₂ and Nb(SBr)₂ (CS-4, CS-5), while SS data specifically supports Ru halide octahedral chemistry and halogen vapor transport (SS-1, SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is controlled halogen/chalcogen chemical potential in a sealed ampoule, because volatile transport can overcome slow Ru solid-state diffusion and support crystal growth. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing Ru chlorides, Ru sulfides, and off-stoichiometric sulfur chlorides are plausible [Reasonable Inference], and related chalcogenide halides show narrow phase windows or poor reproducibility (CS-3, CS-5). Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require careful CVT optimization rather than simple open-atmosphere synthesis.
