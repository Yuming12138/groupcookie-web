### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target lies at **0.0500 eV/atom above hull** (Target), exactly at the favorable cutoff in the provided rubric, so a high score of 90 is assigned. Thermodynamic analogues include Na2MoO4-like phases with E_hull values of 0.000–0.0406 eV/atom (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1), supporting that Na–Mo–O compounds near this chemistry can be stable or only weakly metastable. However, the target contains Li and an O–O unit not directly represented in the closest Na2MoO4 analogues, so the upper end of 95–100 is not used. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Na2LiMoO6 phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Oxidative solid-state sintering

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because solid-state analogues are experimentally simple at 500–530 °C or 973 K (CS-1, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1), but the target likely needs oxygen-rich or peroxide/superoxide chemistry due to its O–O bond (Target; **[Reasonable Inference]**). Kinetics are only moderate because solid-state routes require grinding, long annealing, controlled cooling, and still show residual impurities in related Na–Mo–O work (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1). The MoO4 tetrahedra are transferable from Na2MoO4 analogues (SS-1, SS-3), but Li incorporation and O–O retention are uncertain. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for high-temperature stability of the target O–O unit.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Alkali molybdate melt / self-flux

Melt growth is supported by Na/Li vanadate crystals grown at 900 °C with slow cooling and quenching (CS-5), and by melt/quench tungstate precedent (TS-3). The route has good kinetics because a molten alkali molybdate flux should enhance transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the condition severity is reduced by possible alkali volatility and the need for oxidizing conditions inferred from the target O–O bond (Target). Phase control is a concern because CS-5 reports phase separation for unfavorable Na/Li ratios. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for melt growth of the exact Na–Li–Mo oxide.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Aqueous crystallization

This route has the mildest conditions because RbLiCrO4 single crystals were grown from aqueous Rb2CrO4/Li2CrO4 solution without special pressure or atmosphere (CS-3, CS-4). Kinetics are favorable because solution transport avoids solid-state diffusion, and the target’s LiO4/MoO4 tetrahedral framework is chemically analogous to mixed alkali tetraoxo salts (CS-3, CS-4, Target). The main uncertainty is whether Na2LiMoO6 with an O–O unit can crystallize directly rather than forming ordinary Na/Li molybdates; this is a **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than a demonstrated CS/SS/TS result. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports aqueous crystallization specifically for Na–Li–Mo–O.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Hydrothermal oxidative synthesis

Hydrothermal molybdates form at 150–230 °C under autogenous pressure for 20–48 h (TS-4), making this route less severe than high-temperature solid-state methods. Kinetics should be good because soluble species and hydrothermal transport aid crystallization (TS-4). However, TS-4 also reports impurities and the need to optimize temperature/reagent ratios, so phase control is only moderate. The use of O2/H2O2 to preserve the target O–O unit is a **[Reasonable Inference]** from the Target, not directly shown by TS-4.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains substantially different cation environments: NaO5 trigonal bipyramids, LiO4 tetrahedra, and MoO4 tetrahedra (Target), so ionic size/coordination mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. The structure does not appear to exceed 40 atoms per formula description, and no face-sharing connectivity is reported; only corner and edge sharing are present (Target). Thus the amorphization tendency is low-to-medium rather than high. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no direct amorphization data, so the score is based primarily on the Target structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiMoO6 is likely a **near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom (Target). The strongest analogue support comes from Na–Mo–O phases containing MoO4 tetrahedra and similar Mo–O distances, which are synthesizable by moderate-temperature solid-state routes (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1). Mixed alkali–Li tetraoxo frameworks are also supported by aqueous RbLiCrO4 crystallization and Na/Li vanadate melt growth (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the apparent low-temperature accessibility of related alkali tetraoxo frameworks via solution or flux routes (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is control of the unusual O–O unit and oxidation state/oxygen stoichiometry, because none of the CS/SS/TS examples directly demonstrates this feature for Na–Li–Mo–O.
