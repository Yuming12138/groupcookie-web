### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold and justifies a high F1 score of 90. Chemically similar U double perovskites are often calculated on or very near the hull, including Ba₂UNiO₆, Ba₂UZnO₆, Ba₂UMgUO₆, and Ba₂UCdO₆ at E_hull = 0 (CS-2–CS-5). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.055–0.079 eV/atom are experimentally accessible, including SrTcO₃ and CaNbO₃ (TS-1, TS-4), so a 0.05 eV/atom phase is plausibly synthesizable but may be metastable. SS gives useful structural support from perovskites at E_hull = 0–0.024 eV/atom (SS-2–SS-4), although chemical transferability is weaker than CS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard high-temperature ceramic conditions comparable to Ba₂UCuO₆ at 1100 °C/48 h in air and Ba₂UNiO₆ at 1000 °C/120 h in air (CS-1, CS-2). The deductions arise from T >1000 °C, multiple grinding/pelletizing steps, and toxic U/Os precursors. CS supports feasibility with standard furnaces, but TS-4 indicates that Sr-based 4d/5d perovskites may require tighter atmosphere control than simple air firing. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by Os volatility and achieving U/Os rock-salt ordering.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate-to-slow because CS solid-state analogues require long holds and repeated regrinding to complete reaction (CS-1, CS-2). Oxide/carbonate precursors are less reactive than solution-derived nitrate/gel precursors, though uranyl nitrate can improve U dispersion as in Ba₂UCuO₆ (CS-1). The perovskite framework is structurally compatible with CS double perovskites, but B-site ordering between U and Os likely requires extended diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS for Os-specific ordering kinetics; SS is scored neutral for that subpoint.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits moderate severity from CS-3: carbonate flux growth at 950–1150 °C in air with slow cooling and water/sonication flux removal. It avoids high pressure and vacuum, unlike some SS/TS routes (SS-2–SS-4, TS-1, TS-2), but still requires multiple operations and toxic U/Os handling. A deduction is included for probable Os volatility/containment needs **[Reasonable Inference]**, because CS-3 does not address Os. The route is less severe than sealed-tube synthesis but more operationally complex than a single firing.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux-assisted synthesis receives the best kinetics score because molten carbonates provide a liquid transport medium, and CS-3 directly shows U perovskite-derived crystals can be grown this way. Slow cooling in CS-3 supports crystal growth and cation ordering, which should help the low-symmetry ordered Sr₂UOsO₆ target. The main uncertainty is whether Os remains in the melt without oxidation to volatile OsO₄ or reduction/segregation **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS flux examples support flux methods generally (SS-5, TS-3), but they are not chemically close to Sr–U–Os, so their signal is secondary.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized heavily because sealed-tube or vacuum processing is a controlled-atmosphere method, and SS/TS analogues such as EuNbO₃ and LaTiO₃ require evacuated silica tubes and high-temperature treatment (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2). Additional severity comes from volatile Os containment and toxic/radiological handling. This route is justified if redox control is essential, but it is more technically demanding than the air/flux methods. CS provides insufficient evidence for sealed-tube U double perovskite synthesis and is therefore neutral for that specific condition.

#### F3 — Route 3

Reaction kinetics remain limited by solid-state diffusion, similar to SS reduced perovskites requiring multi-step preparation and high-temperature annealing (SS-2, SS-3). Sealed conditions may preserve Os but do not inherently improve diffusion compared with a flux. Structural reconstruction is plausible because perovskite products are obtained in SS/TS sealed or vacuum routes (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2), but cation ordering may be slow **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS for sealed controlled-redox kinetics; CS is treated as neutral for that subfactor.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline perovskite-derived corner-sharing octahedral network, matching the robust framework type seen in CS double perovskites (CS-1–CS-5). The main amorphization trigger is the large A-site/B-site cation size contrast between Sr and U/Os **[Reasonable Inference]**, but this contrast is normal for perovskites and does not by itself prevent crystallization. No evidence indicates a >40-atom cell, and the target connectivity is not mixed edge/face/corner sharing. Uncertainty remains because the reported triclinic P-1 symmetry may reflect ordering/distortion that requires slow crystallization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂UOsO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data strongly supports U-containing double perovskite synthesis by both ceramic and carbonate-flux routes, especially Ba₂MUO₆ analogues made in air or molten carbonates (CS-1–CS-3). The TS data supports that perovskite oxides with comparable thermodynamic metastability can be synthesized, including SrTcO₃ near 0.055 eV/atom (TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the structural compatibility of Sr₂UOsO₆ with corner-sharing perovskite/double-perovskite frameworks already demonstrated for U oxides (CS-1–CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is Os redox/volatility control, especially possible formation of volatile OsO₄ under oxidizing high-temperature conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**.
