### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull of 0.0070 eV/atom falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so it scores in the 90–100 band by the given rubric. Closest structural analogues HoSI, ErSeI, GdSI, DySI, and DySBr are all E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1–SS5), while thermodynamic analogues cluster around 0.0067–0.013 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), closely matching the target. CS also supports low-energy halochalcogenide chemistry with several examples from 0 to ~0.022 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee the ordered S/Se arrangement is kinetically accessible.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Direct synthesis uses standard sealed-ampoule temperatures near 850–900 °C, consistent with HoSI/GdSI/DySI syntheses (SS1, SS3, SS4), so no >1000 °C penalty is applied. The main severity comes from vacuum sealing and volatile iodine/chalcogens, both directly represented in SS1/SS4 and TS1. Toxic/air-sensitive handling is also relevant because I₂ and reactive metals are used in SS4 and TS2-like environments. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for elemental Sc–S–Se–I direct synthesis and is treated as neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The strongest kinetic benefit is structural similarity: the target’s 2D edge/corner-sharing Sc chalcogenide iodide sheets closely resemble LnSI/LnSeI sheets (SS1–SS4). The limitation is Sc metal’s high melting point and refractory behavior [Reasonable Inference], though TS1/TS4 show Sc chalcogenides can form from elements in evacuated ampoules. Long annealing times in SS1/SS4 and TS1 imply solid-state diffusion is not fast, just manageable.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Flux growth lowers the likely reaction temperature to 750–850 °C, supported by CS2 and by halide flux examples in CS5 and TS3. It still requires sealed vacuum/Ar conditions and dry handling, as in CS1/CS2/TS2, and introduces extra steps such as flux preparation, slow cooling, and flux removal. The presence of ScI₃/I₂/Se adds toxicity/moisture-sensitivity risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives strong structural transferability but does not directly prove the exact iodide-flux recipe, so that part remains uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten halide media can enhance ion mobility and crystal growth; CS2 explicitly says NaCl or excess MCl₃ flux secures complete and fast reaction, and TS3 shows KBr improves crystal size. This should help S/Se ordering and reduce the penalty from refractory Sc chalcogenides [Reasonable Inference]. The downside is phase competition: CS1 required U assistance for single crystals, and CS2 notes impurity formation, so the phase window may need optimization.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and volatile transport agent, both supported by SS2 and TS5. It is multi-step because it ideally begins with pre-reacted feed powder, paralleling TS5 where synthesized ZnSc₂S₄ powder is transported. The conditions are not high-pressure and can be kept at or below ~1000 °C, but I₂/HCl transport chemistry introduces chemical hazard and composition-shift risk. CS1 supports temperature-gradient halide chemistry but not exact Sc₂SeSI₂ CVT, so its evidence is partial.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport bypasses long-range bulk diffusion and can grow crystals from preformed material, as shown for ZnSc₂S₄ with I₂/HCl transport (TS5). SS2 supports sealed-ampoule/CVT chemistry for lanthanide selenide halides, making the route plausible for a layered iodide chalcogenide. However, the volatile Sc–S/Se–I species chemistry is not directly provided, and TS5 reports contamination/growth limitations, so the score is below an ideal CVT route. CS evidence is only general for gradient/halide growth and is therefore moderate rather than decisive.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Sc, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger; this is a direct target-structure assessment, while CS/TS mixed-cation analogues are not decisive and are neutral for this check. The reported structure is a relatively simple layered Pmm2 phase, and closest SS analogues are simple layered Pmmn phases (SS1–SS4), so there is no evidence for a >40 atom cell. Edge and corner sharing are present, but no face sharing is reported in the target or SS analogues, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂SeSI₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.007 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues occupy the same near-hull range (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from the structural analogues: LnSI and LnSeI compounds form 2D sheets with edge/corner-sharing metal–chalcogen–iodide octahedra and L-shaped iodide coordination, closely matching the target topology (SS1–SS4). The most important enabling factor is sealed-tube halogen/chalcogen chemistry, which provides both iodine chemical potential and possible vapor/flux-assisted mass transport (SS1, SS4, CS2, TS5). The main limiting factor is achieving ordered mixed S/Se occupancy around small Sc³⁺ without segregating into ScSI, ScSeI, Sc₂S₃, Sc₂Se₃, or ScI₃-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with flux-assisted sealed-ampoule synthesis slightly preferred over purely direct solid-state reaction.
