### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.045 eV/atom, which lies inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. CS-1 and SS-1 report a same-composition KAgSe/K₂Ag₂Se₂ phase at E_hull = 0.0, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. TS-1 to TS-4 include experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.03–0.067 eV/atom, supporting that slightly positive-hull compounds can be made. The main uncertainty is whether the target’s “iron pnictide-derived” P4/nmm variant is the same polymorph as the matlockite P4/nmm analogue in CS-1/SS-1 or a closely related metastable distortion.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct elements

CS-1/SS-1 demonstrate direct synthesis at 800 °C under Ar for 24 h with slow cooling, so the route avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature. The main severity penalties are K/Se volatility and air sensitivity, supported by the sealed steel crucible and dry Ar/paraffin oil handling in CS-1/SS-1. TS provides insufficient route-specific evidence for this exact direct elemental KAgSe synthesis, so TS is neutral for route severity. Overall condition severity is moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct elements

K and Se are highly reactive at 800 °C, and CS-1/SS-1 show that these elements react successfully with Ag to give KAgSe. Ag is a dense metal, but the successful 24 h synthesis in CS-1/SS-1 indicates diffusion is not prohibitive. The structural reconstruction score is high because the analogue has the same formula and P4/nmm structure in CS-1/SS-1. Remaining uncertainty is the slight difference between target coordination description and the matlockite description in CS-1/SS-1.

#### F2 — Route 2: Carbonate/selenium/silver fusion

CS-3/SS-2 and SS-3/SS-4 show K/Rb/Cs–Ag–Se phases can be made at 1000 °C in dry oxygen-free Ar using alkali carbonate, Ag, and Se. The process is more severe than Route 1 because it commonly includes excess flux, slow cooling, washing, and vacuum drying in SS-3/SS-4/SS-5. Se volatility and air-sensitive products are recurring concerns in these flux-derived chalcogenides, as noted in SS-3/SS-4/SS-5. TS gives insufficient evidence for this route and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2: Carbonate/selenium/silver fusion

Flux/fusion chemistry should improve kinetics because CS-3/SS-2 and CS-5/SS-5 obtain related phases in 4–6 h. The main kinetic concern is not diffusion but phase selectivity: CS-3/SS-2 produce K₂Ag₄Se₃ under similar K–Ag–Se chemistry rather than KAgSe. Thus the structural reconstruction/phase-selection component is only moderate. Stoichiometry, flux excess, and cooling profile are the major uncertainties.

#### F2 — Route 3: Binary selenide route

CS-2 shows alkali selenide solid-state reactions at 673–873 K for 2–7 days under Ar-sealed conditions, and TS-4 confirms K₂Se can be used in selenide synthesis. The lower temperature is favorable, but sealed/inert handling and K₂Se air sensitivity impose penalties. The exact K₂Se + Ag₂Se route is a [Reasonable Inference], so the evidence is weaker than for Route 1. TS is supportive only for K₂Se use, not for KAgSe formation.

#### F3 — Route 3: Binary selenide route

Binary selenides are chemically closer to the final product than elemental mixtures, which should improve precursor activity [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-2]. However, CS-2 reactions require days, indicating slower diffusion or ordering at lower temperature. Structural reconstruction is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates KAgSe formation from K₂Se and Ag₂Se. The route is plausible but not preferred.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film route

No CS/SS/TS data report thin-film KAgSe or related K–Ag–Se P4/nmm film growth, so all source categories provide insufficient direct evidence and are neutral. The route is a [Reasonable Inference] based on possible stabilization of layered/metastable phases, but K volatility makes condition severity and composition control poor. Vacuum processing, post-annealing, and volatile K/Se control lower both F2 and F3. This route is exploratory rather than a primary synthesis recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size contrast between K and Ag, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion by [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is simple by comparison with CS-1/SS-1 K₂Ag₂Se₂, so the >40 atom criterion is not triggered. The target description does not report complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing networks, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for such connectivity in the target. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KAgSe is likely synthesizable, with the target sitting very close to the convex hull at 0.045 eV/atom and a same-composition P4/nmm KAgSe analogue reported as a ground-state phase in CS-1/SS-1. The CS and SS data support synthesis very strongly because the best analogue is essentially the target composition and structure, whereas TS mainly supports the general accessibility of low-positive-hull chalcogenides but gives little route-specific information. The most important enabling factor is the experimentally demonstrated direct elemental synthesis of KAgSe at 800 °C under Ar with slow cooling in CS-1/SS-1. The most important limiting factor is stoichiometry control for volatile/reactive K and Se, especially because competing K–Ag–Se phases such as K₂Ag₄Se₃ are accessible under related conditions in CS-3/SS-2. Overall, the direct solid-state elemental route is the most reliable route.
