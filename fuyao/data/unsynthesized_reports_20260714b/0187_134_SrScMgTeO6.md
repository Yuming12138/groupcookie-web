### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-confidence metastability cutoff used in the provided scoring table [Target]. Chemically similar Sr₂MgTeO₆ and NaMgLaTeO₆ are on the hull [CS5, CS1], and structurally similar oxides include stable or near-stable phases [SS1–SS5]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.056–0.070 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS3, TS5], so the target’s energy is not prohibitive. Remaining uncertainty is that the target’s unusual triclinic P1 ordering and apparent Te–O distortion may create competing lower-symmetry or off-stoichiometric phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, conventional ceramic.**  
Condition severity is moderate because direct Sr₂MgTeO₆ synthesis uses 900–1100 °C in air with repeated grinding [CS5], and Sr₂InNbO₆ needs 1230 °C and re-pelleting [SS5]. Kinetics are only moderate-to-poor because Sc₂O₃ and MgO are relatively refractory [Reasonable Inference], and ceramic analogues require long firing/regrinding [CS5, SS5, TS1]. CS strongly supports the route, SS supports perovskite-derived oxide processing, while TS gives general solid-state support but not exact chemistry. Main uncertainty is whether Sc enters the Mg/Te oxide lattice without forming Sc₂O₃-rich or Sr–Sc–O impurity phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, reactive nitrate/sol–gel.**  
NaMgLaTeO₆ forms from nitrate/hydroxide/telluric acid precursors at 773 and 973 K in air [CS1], supporting milder conditions and higher precursor activity. The sol–gel extension is a [Reasonable Inference] to improve cation mixing for Sr/Mg/Sc/Te, and SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sol–gel synthesis of this target, so those signals are neutral/50 for the route. Kinetic score is higher than ceramic because nitrate/hydroxide precursors decompose intimately, but final crystallization and Sc ordering still likely need calcination [CS1, SS5]. Uncertainty is organic burnout and possible Te loss during final firing.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux-assisted crystallization.**  
Flux growth is supported by Na₂BaMgP₂O₈ single-crystal growth in sodium molybdate flux at 1273 K with slow cooling [CS3] and Mg₄Nb₂O₉ growth from Cs-containing flux at 1400 °C in air [SS3]. Flux should improve reaction kinetics by dissolving refractory oxides and enabling slow crystallization [CS3, SS3]. TS gives only weak support because the flux-grown thermodynamic analogue is chemically different [TS4], so TS is not heavily weighted. Main uncertainty is flux cation incorporation or formation of simpler Sr–Mg–Te, Sr–Sc–O, or Sc–Te–O phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, high-pressure.**  
Structurally similar BiScO₃ requires 6 GPa and 1413 K [SS1], showing pressure can stabilize distorted ScO₆ frameworks. However, CS chemically similar Sr₂MgTeO₆ forms at ambient pressure [CS5], so high pressure is probably unnecessary unless the P1 phase is metastable. F2 is low due to the 6 GPa requirement [SS1], and F3 is moderate because pressure may help structural stabilization but not long-range four-cation ordering. CS and TS provide insufficient direct high-pressure evidence for this chemistry, so both are neutral/50 for this route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size disparity, as reflected by long Sr–O bonds versus shorter Mg/Sc/Te–O bonds [Target], and similar large A/B-site size separation occurs in Sr₂MgTeO₆ [CS5]. However, the target description does not show a >40 atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing; it shows corner and edge sharing only [Target]. CS/SS/TS contain examples with complex connectivity including face sharing [CS5, SS3], but insufficient evidence that this target has face sharing, so those checks remain untriggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrMgScTeO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The chemical analogue support is strong: Sr₂MgTeO₆ is directly synthesized by ceramic reaction [CS5], and NaMgLaTeO₆ demonstrates Mg–Te ordered oxide formation under relatively mild air firing [CS1]. Structural support is moderate because ScO₆-containing distorted oxides and Sr perovskite-derived oxides are known [SS1, SS5], but no structurally identical Sr–Mg–Sc–Te P1 phase is provided. The most critical enabling factor is high cation mobility and homogeneity, best supplied by flux or nitrate/sol–gel routes [CS1, CS3, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and slow ordering among Sr, Mg, Sc, and Te, especially because Sc₂O₃ is refractory and the target has low symmetry [SS5; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Te loss or Te oxidation-state drift during high-temperature treatment | High | Use oxidizing atmosphere, covered crucible, slight Te excess, or flux/closed capsule; Te oxide precursors are used in CS5 and hydrated telluric acid in CS4, while air firing is validated in CS1/CS5. |
| 2 | All | Competing Sr₂MgTeO₆, Sr–Sc–O, Mg–Te–O, or Sc₂O₃ impurity phases | High | Use repeated grinding/re-pelleting as in Sr₂MgTeO₆ and Sr₂InNbO₆ syntheses [CS5, SS5]; use flux to enhance dissolution and recrystallization [CS3, SS3]. |
| 3 | Routes 1 and 4 | Slow Sc incorporation from refractory Sc₂O₃ | Medium–High | Use fine Sc₂O₃, nitrate-derived Sc precursor, or pre-reacted gel; high-temperature ceramic Sc-containing analogues require extended firing [SS5], while reactive precursor strategy is supported by CS1. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Long-range cation ordering may be kinetically slow in ceramic route | Medium | Multi-cycle firing and intermediate regrinding, as used for Sr₂MgTeO₆ and Sr₂InNbO₆ [CS5, SS5]. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Organic/nitrate decomposition may produce inhomogeneous foams or local Te loss | Medium | Use slow burnout at 300–450 °C and staged calcination; staged low-temperature heating is supported by CS1. |
| 6 | Route 3 | Flux cation incorporation or residual Mo/Cs/Na contamination | Medium | Screen flux/charge ratios, wash thoroughly with warm water as in CS3, and compare Na/K/Cs-free fluxes [CS3, SS3]. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Crystal growth may favor simpler phases rather than target P1 phase | Medium | Use slow cooling, pre-reacted target-composition feed, and small Te excess; slow cooling is used in CS3. |
| 8 | Route 4 | Specialized high-pressure apparatus and small batch size | High | Reserve high pressure as contingency only; SS1 shows 6 GPa synthesis is feasible but specialized, while CS5 indicates ambient-pressure chemistry may suffice. |
| 9 | All | Stoichiometry control across four cations | Medium | Use high-purity stoichiometric powders, ball milling or solution mixing, and repeated phase checks by powder XRD; analogous studies emphasize stoichiometric mixtures and regrinding [CS1, CS5, SS5, TS1]. |
