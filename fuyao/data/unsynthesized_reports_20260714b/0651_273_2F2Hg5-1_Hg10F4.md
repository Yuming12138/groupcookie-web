### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold, so it receives 90/100 by the required rubric. TS strongly supports this score because thermodynamically similar Hg-containing compounds with E_hull near 0.03–0.067 eV/atom are reported as synthesized (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). SS also supports feasibility because several structurally similar Hg compounds are on or near the hull and synthesized (SS-1 to SS-5). CS is less directly useful chemically because it contains Tl/Bi oxides rather than Hg fluorides; CS-5 shows a similar E_hull but requires high pressure, so CS suggests possible metastability rather than easy synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The condition penalty is substantial because SS and TS analogues repeatedly require evacuated sealed tubes or ampoules for Hg compounds (SS-1, SS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The target likely requires volatile/toxic Hg-bearing reagents by [Reasonable Inference], consistent with Hg precursor hazards noted in SS/TS reasoning. Temperature severity is not high because analogous Hg compounds form at 280–420 °C rather than >1000 °C (SS-1, SS-3, TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is the lack of direct Hg–F sealed-tube examples in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Kinetics are moderate because Hg-containing analogues can form at relatively low temperatures, indicating favorable transport compared with refractory oxides (SS-1, SS-3, TS-4). However, the target’s Hg clusters and 1D Hg₈F₃ ribbon require major reconstruction from HgF₂/Hg, and long 120 h holds in several analogues imply slow ordering (SS-1, SS-3, TS-4). CS gives insufficient useful kinetic signal for Hg–F because its examples are Tl/Bi oxides, although CS-1/CS-2 show that heavy p-block solids may require long annealing.

#### F2 — Route 2: hydrothermal/solvothermal

Route 2 has milder nominal temperature and pressure conditions because hydrothermal Hg₂OI forms at 450 K and hydrothermal CuHgSBr forms at 670 K (SS-2, TS-1). It is still penalized for toxic/corrosive reagents and sealed-system operation, especially if HF/NH₄HF₂ is used by [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 supports autoclave crystal growth at 220 °C but is Bi₂O₃, not Hg fluoride. The key uncertainty is whether hydrothermal media can maintain the reduced Hg-rich, oxygen-free target chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2: hydrothermal/solvothermal

Solution transport should give high precursor mobility, supported by single-crystal growth of Hg₂OI under hydrothermal conditions (SS-2) and CuHgSBr hydrothermal growth (TS-1). The structural-reconstruction score is low because the target contains Hg–Hg bonding and a binary fluoride framework, while the hydrothermal analogues include O/I or S/Br chemistry (SS-2, TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports hydrothermal stabilization of Hg-rich fluoride clusters, so this route is speculative despite favorable kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3: vapor transport / self-transport

Route 3 is penalized for vacuum, sealed-ampoule operation, volatile Hg species, and toxic precursors, all consistent with Hg-containing sealed-ampoule syntheses (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5, TS-5). It avoids high temperature and high pressure, since structurally related Hg phases crystallize at 320–420 °C or by gas-phase growth (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). The uncertainty is ampoule compatibility with fluorides, which is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3: vapor transport / self-transport

This route has the best kinetic match because SS-5 explicitly reports growth of Hg-containing crystals from the gas phase in evacuated quartz ampoules. Low-dimensional Hg compounds also form via sealed-tube slow-cooling routes in SS-1 and SS-3, supporting the idea that vapor/condensed transport can assemble Hg frameworks. The main uncertainty is that none of SS/TS directly covers Hg–F vapor transport, so fluoride volatility/reactivity and silica compatibility remain unresolved.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Hg, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered based on the target composition. The described structure is complex but appears to have a small Hg/F formula-unit content rather than a >40-atom unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates otherwise. Mixed polyhedral vertex/edge/face sharing is not clearly described for the target, which is instead a 1D Hg cluster/HgF/Hg₈F₃ ribbon structure. Therefore amorphization tendency is scored low-risk, though disorder between Hg-rich subunits remains possible by [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a borderline metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and not clearly zero. TS analogues strongly support synthesizability of Hg-containing phases near this energy range, while SS analogues support low-temperature sealed-tube or vapor-growth access to low-dimensional Hg compounds (SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is Hg mobility/volatility in sealed systems, which can promote crystal growth at modest temperatures as seen in evacuated-tube and gas-phase Hg analogues (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and redox control for a Hg-rich fluoride with Hg–Hg bonding, for which direct CS/SS/TS evidence is insufficient. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require sealed, dry, low-temperature crystal-growth screening rather than ordinary open solid-state heating.
