### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0200 eV/atom, so it falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom “highly synthesizable” bin. CS provides supportive precedent that chemically similar In compounds can be stable or near-stable, including InSe at E_hull = 0 and In4Se3 around 0.019 eV/atom (CS-1/CS-5). SS and TS are more directly relevant because they include In–Br and bromide analogues at or near the hull, such as In8Br16 at E_hull = 0 (SS-5) and KSn2Br5 at 0.0197 eV/atom (TS-2). The main uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target P4/nmm InBr may be metastable relative to another InBr polymorph even if chemically close to the hull.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures only, with SS-5 reporting 340 °C reaction and 90 °C sublimation, so no high-temperature penalty applies. Deductions come from vacuum/sealed-ampoule operation, volatile bromide handling, multiple steps, and air/moisture-sensitive halide chemistry; these are supported by SS-5 and by air/humidity sensitivity in related In–Br bromides (SS-3/SS-4/TS-4/TS-5). CS gives only general support for evacuated ampoule synthesis in In–chalcogenides and does not directly address InBr volatility; therefore, insufficient evidence from CS for detailed Route-1 severity beyond ampoule precedent, treated as neutral where not applicable.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because SS-5 directly reports InBr formation from InBr3 + In and further purification by low-temperature vacuum sublimation. Low reaction temperatures imply that the relevant mass transport occurs through molten/volatile bromide species rather than slow diffusion through refractory solids [Reasonable Inference based on SS-5]. Structural reconstruction is not expected to be severe because the route forms the target composition directly, but exact P4/nmm selection is not guaranteed. TS provides thermodynamic similarity but insufficient evidence from TS for the kinetic pathway, so TS kinetic signal is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 also avoids extreme temperature and pressure, because analogous InBr3 flux syntheses use 450–475 °C and ambient-pressure sealed ampoules (SS-2/SS-3/SS-4; TS-4/TS-5). The penalties are identical to Route 1: vacuum/inert handling, volatile InBr3, multiple operations, and air-sensitive bromide precursors. SS/TS strongly support these severity assignments; CS is less useful because the chemically similar In–Se reactions use higher temperatures but do not capture halide volatility, so insufficient evidence from CS for Route-2 halide-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

The molten-salt/self-flux route benefits from high precursor activity because InBr3 is explicitly used as a melt/flux in structurally and thermodynamically similar In–Br compounds (SS-2–SS-4; TS-4/TS-5). Diffusion should be rapid relative to ceramic solid-state routes because the reaction occurs in a halide melt and all relevant cations are low-melting or dissolved [Reasonable Inference from SS-2–SS-4]. The structural reconstruction score is lower than Route 1 because the analogues are ternary chain/ribbon compounds, not the binary P4/nmm target. The 4–5 day reaction times and slow cooling in SS-2–SS-4 indicate some ordering and crystallization burden.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has low thermal severity, but it requires UHV, controlled effusion-cell evaporation, low substrate temperatures, and careful phase-transition management, as shown for InCl (CS-4/SS-1/TS-3). The formal F2 rubric penalizes vacuum/controlled atmosphere, volatile source handling, multiple steps, and air-sensitive halides, but does not explicitly penalize specialized UHV equipment; thus the numerical F2 may understate practical difficulty. SS-5 supports In–Br volatility/sublimation, but not UHV film growth of InBr itself. Therefore, structural/chemical analogues support the concept but not the target phase specifically.

#### F3 — Route 3

Precursor vapor availability is plausible because SS-5 reports low-temperature vacuum sublimation of In–Br products and CS-4/SS-1/TS-3 demonstrate effusion-cell deposition of InCl. Crystallization is the limiting kinetic issue because the InCl analogue forms metastable phases through low-temperature deposition and heating/cooling transitions (CS-4/SS-1/TS-3), implying that P4/nmm InBr may require a narrow kinetic window. Composition control is moderate because molecular InBr evaporation could be stoichiometric [Reasonable Inference], but bromide volatility and disproportionation can shift the film composition. TS provides no direct thin-film kinetic data beyond the InCl analogue, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F2 — Route 4

The direct element route uses moderate temperatures but introduces significant handling severity due to Br2 volatility, toxicity, corrosivity, and sealed-ampoule pressure [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports elemental sealed-ampoule synthesis in In–Se systems (CS-1/CS-5), while SS/TS support sealed dry ampoules for bromide products (SS-3/SS-4/TS-4/TS-5). However, no CS/SS/TS source directly reports elemental In + Br2 producing the target P4/nmm InBr, so the route is more speculative. The score is limited by safety and redox-control difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 4

Kinetically, Br2 should react readily with In [Reasonable Inference], and low-melting In plus volatile bromide species should reduce diffusion limitations. The major kinetic/chemical issue is not reaction completion but phase selectivity: SS-5 reports multiple In–Br products, so over-bromination or mixed-valence bromides are plausible. CS elemental reactions provide general precedent but with Se rather than Br, so insufficient evidence from CS for InBr phase selectivity. SS gives the strongest cautionary signal through the observed mixture of InBr/In2Br3/InBr2-type products in SS-5.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-size-mismatch check is not triggered. The target is described as a 0D structure containing two InBr clusters with single In–Br bonding, so the unit cell is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms and mixed polyhedral connectivity is absent [Reasonable Inference from target description]. Remaining uncertainty is mainly polymorph selection rather than amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target InBr phase is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.020 eV/atom, within the commonly synthesizable metastability window. The strongest support comes from SS-5, which directly reports InBr plates from InBr3 + In shot followed by vacuum sublimation, while SS-2–SS-4 and TS-4/TS-5 support broader InBr3 molten-flux chemistry. CS provides useful but less direct evidence from In–Se and InCl analogues, especially that indium compounds can be made in sealed ampoules or by vapor deposition. The most critical enabling factor is the high volatility/reactivity of In–Br species, which allows low-temperature sublimation and purification. The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control among multiple indium bromides, especially InBr, InBr2/In2Br3, and InBr3-rich products.
