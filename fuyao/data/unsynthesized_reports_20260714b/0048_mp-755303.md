### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which sits at the high end of the 90–100 scoring bin [Target]. Similar or slightly less stable oxides are experimentally accessible, including FeNbO4 at E_hull ≈0.0629 eV/atom and Fe2TiO5 at E_above_ground_state ≈0.0464 eV/atom [SS1, TS1, TS2]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the specific C2/c FeTaO4 polymorph is the experimentally selected phase rather than a competing Fe–Ta oxide [Target, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Solid-state sintering is moderately severe because it requires 1350–1400 °C and a multi-step pellet/heat/cool procedure, but it avoids high pressure, vacuum, H2, NH3, and sealed volatile precursors [SS1, TS1, CS2]. The FeNbO4 and AlTaO4 analogues both support air/oxidizing ambient-pressure synthesis [SS1, TS1, CS2]. The main uncertainty is whether FeTaO4 requires temperatures above the FeNbO4/AlTaO4 range due to Ta2O5 sluggishness [CS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta2O5 and solid-state cation diffusion, while Fe oxalate can improve Fe dispersion and reactivity [SS1, TS1, CS2]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because FeNbO4 is a close Fe–group-V oxide analogue, but the target C2/c structure differs from the FeNbO4 P2/c hydrophilite analogue [SS1, TS1, Target]. This gives moderate kinetics rather than easy kinetics [SS1, TS1].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth is penalized by high temperature, slow cooling, and flux separation steps [CS1, CS5, TS2]. It remains less severe than high pressure because analogous flux methods operate at ambient pressure [CS1, CS5, TS2]. Toxicity is route-dependent: PbO–V2O5 flux is supported by TS2 but should be avoided if a lead-free flux works [TS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux media improve mass transport and can grow single crystals, as shown for InTaO4, NiWO4, and Fe2TiO5 [CS1, CS5, TS2]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity because Fe2TiO5 flux growth was sensitive to cooling rate and impurity stabilization [TS2]. Therefore flux kinetics are somewhat better than ceramic sintering but phase control remains moderate [CS1, TS2].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Laser/melt growth is severe mainly because NiTa2O6 required >1800 °C and CO2 laser heating [CS4, SS2]. The rubric does not directly penalize specialized equipment, but practical difficulty is high because a laser furnace/floating-zone setup is non-standard [CS4, SS2]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct melt growth of thermodynamic analogues is treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt conditions improve diffusion but require full recrystallization from a high-temperature liquid [CS4, SS2]. This creates risk of off-stoichiometry or competing tantalate formation, especially because NiTa2O6 is Ta-rich relative to FeTaO4 [CS4, SS2, Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are moderate, but phase selection is uncertain [CS4, SS2].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized by pressure and multi-step quench handling [SS3, SS5, TS5]. Similar oxide polymorphs required 25–35 kbar or 8 GPa plus quenching [SS3, SS4, SS5, TS5]. This route is a backup rather than first choice because ambient FeNbO4/AlTaO4 analogues already support lower-severity synthesis [SS1, TS1, CS2].

**F3 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure reactions can stabilize dense or metastable Ta/Nb oxide structures, but short reaction times and dense oxide precursors limit diffusion [SS3, SS5, TS5]. Phase control is uncertain because high-pressure Ta2O5 formed polymorph/intergrowth complexity and AgNb3O8-II required quenching to preserve the phase [SS3, SS5, TS5]. Therefore kinetics and phase purity are scored low-to-moderate [SS3, SS5, TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target does not show the full vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence criterion, and close FeNbO4 analogues use corner/edge-connected octahedral frameworks rather than face-sharing networks [Target, SS1, TS1]. The target also appears to be a relatively simple FeTaO4 oxide rather than a very large-cell multicomponent phase [Target, Reasonable Inference]. Exact target unit-cell atom count is not directly supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that source component is neutral 50 and remains a minor uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeTaO4 is best viewed as a borderline metastable, experimentally plausible oxide because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from FeNbO4, which is both structurally and thermodynamically similar and is synthesized by air sintering at 1400 °C despite E_hull ≈0.0629 eV/atom [SS1, TS1]. Chemically similar Ta-based oxides also form by Ta2O5 + oxide reactions at high temperature, especially AlTaO4 at 1350 °C [CS2]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of robust oxide/oxalate precursors and oxidizing ambient-pressure synthesis conditions [SS1, TS1, CS2]. The most important limiting factor is sluggish Ta-containing solid-state diffusion and possible competition with other Fe–Ta–O phases, which makes slow cooling, regrinding, and phase screening critical [SS1, TS1, CS4, Reasonable Inference].
