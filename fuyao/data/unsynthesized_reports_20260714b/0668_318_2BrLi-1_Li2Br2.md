### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, placing it well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bracket. TS gives the strongest supporting signal because TS-2 and TS-3 have very similar E_hull values near 0.020 eV/atom, and these are experimentally synthesized bromide materials. CS and SS also support low-energy halide polymorph accessibility: CS-2 has LiI at 0.0289 eV/atom, and SS includes several bromides at or near the hull. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee that the specific P4/nmm polymorph is kinetically accessible at room temperature.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for high vacuum, volatile halide evaporation, multi-step handling, and moisture-sensitive LiBr, all supported by CS-1/SS-1 and CS-2. The temperature itself is not severe because CS-1/SS-1 evaporate at 430 °C, far below 1000 °C. TS provides insufficient evidence for vapor-deposition severity and is neutral for this route. The main severity is not heat but cryogenic/vacuum infrastructure and phase-preserving transfer.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

LiBr vapor availability is strongly supported because CS-1/SS-1 directly evaporated LiBr under high vacuum. Crystallization is given an intermediate score because CS-1/SS-1 and CS-2 show metastable Li-halide phases form by cryogenic condensation, but they also indicate sensitivity to warming and transformation. Composition control is favorable because the target is binary LiBr, unlike multication bromides in SS/TS, but LiBr volatility and hygroscopicity still impose control requirements. TS gives insufficient direct vapor-deposition evidence and is neutral for this kinetic route.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed-ampoule methods, supported by SS-3 and TS-2. It is penalized for controlled Ar/vacuum atmosphere, sealed volatile halide handling, and moisture-sensitive anhydrous LiBr, which are supported by SS-3, TS-2, and CS-1/SS-1. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for melt crystallization of LiBr polymorphs and is neutral for this route. The processing is experimentally less severe than cryogenic deposition, but it is not phase-selective by itself.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The melt route has good precursor activity and diffusion because SS-3 and TS-2 show bromide melts crystallize on slow cooling. The structural-reconstruction score is low because no CS/SS/TS source reports P4/nmm LiBr from a melt, and [Reasonable Inference] bulk LiBr melt cooling is likely to produce the stable LiBr polymorph. TS-2 is chemically relevant because it uses LiBr in an ampoule melt, but it forms Li–Mg bromides rather than binary P4/nmm LiBr. Thus, kinetics of crystallization are favorable, but kinetics of selecting the desired polymorph are unfavorable.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized for controlled dry/vacuum or Ar atmosphere, sealed-system handling, multi-step annealing/quenching, and hygroscopic LiBr handling. TS-3 specifically requires dry, OH-free LiBr, and CS-1/SS-1 emphasize anhydrous LiBr and controlled atmosphere. SS-2/SS-5/TS-1/TS-4 support sealed-ampoule bromide annealing at moderate temperatures. The route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but it remains operationally demanding because phase preservation is delicate.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solid-state conversion has moderate precursor activity because dry LiBr is chemically simple but not a highly reactive molecular precursor. Ion diffusion is likely acceptable for Li halides [Reasonable Inference], and SS/TS bromide analogues anneal at moderate temperatures, but the target polymorph requires a reconstructive change. The low structural-reconstruction score is supported indirectly by CS-1/SS-1 and TS-3, where metastable Li-halide-related phases show restricted stability and transformation behavior. The largest uncertainty is whether a topotactic pathway to P4/nmm LiBr exists at all.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally simple: it is binary LiBr with one cation species, so the cation-radius-difference check is not triggered. The target description indicates a simple P4/nmm sheet structure with LiBr₄ tetrahedra and no large multicomponent cell; therefore the >40 atom-cell risk is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for P4/nmm LiBr and are neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target LiBr P4/nmm phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the conventional ground-state form, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.0210 eV/atom and CS-1/SS-1 show Li-halide metastable phases can require low-temperature deposition. The CS and SS data support synthesis most strongly through cryogenic vapor deposition, especially because CS-1/SS-1 directly involve LiBr and tetrahedrally coordinated Li-halide phases. TS supports the thermodynamic plausibility because several thermodynamically similar bromides have E_hull values near 0.02 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature kinetic trapping during or immediately after LiBr deposition, as supported by CS-1/SS-1 and CS-2. The most critical limiting factor is phase retention: warming, annealing, or melt crystallization may drive LiBr toward a more stable polymorph rather than the target P4/nmm sheet structure.
