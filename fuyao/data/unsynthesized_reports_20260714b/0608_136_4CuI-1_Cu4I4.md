### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0310 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. Similar low-positive-hull compounds are reported as synthesized, including CuHgSBr at E_hull ≈ 0.0309 eV/atom (SS-5/TS-2), CuHgSI at E_hull ≈ 0.0210 eV/atom (SS-1/TS-1), and BiI at E_hull ≈ 0.0270 eV/atom (CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that CuI polymorph competition may matter more than formation energy alone, as polymorphism is problematic for BiI (CS-5) and Ag₃SI (SS-4/TS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube direct combination

Condition severity is moderate because CS-2 and CS-5 show elemental iodides can be made in sealed ampoules at only 250–285 °C, but vacuum/Ar sealing and volatile I₂ are required. Kinetically, Cu + I₂ should react readily [Reasonable Inference], but dense Cu and solid-state polymorph selection make reconstruction less favorable; CS-3 shows Cu metal reactions can require long anneals for bulk chalcogenides. The main uncertainty is whether direct formation yields the target orthorhombic Pbcm CuI rather than a more common CuI polymorph [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-5 and SS-4/TS-5].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal HI recrystallization

This route scores best because SS-3 directly demonstrates CuI in concentrated HI at 160 °C forming iodocuprate crystals, and TS-3 shows similar iodide hydrothermal crystallization at 155–175 °C. Solution-mediated transport gives high precursor activity and avoids long-range solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference], which is favorable for metastable low-temperature crystallization. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS-3 reports twinning and low yield for some iodide crystals, so the Pbcm CuI polymorph may need careful cooling, concentration, and redox optimization.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Low-temperature thin-film/solution iodination

The low severity comes from CS-1, where chemically similar CdS films are deposited at 80 °C in air using simple bath chemistry. However, CS-1 also reports mixed phases and impurities, which lowers the crystallization and composition-control scores. The extrapolation to CuI is plausible for metastable thin films [Reasonable Inference], but direct SS/TS support for CuI bath deposition is insufficient, so the route is less certain than hydrothermal HI recrystallization.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so no cation-radius mismatch trigger applies; this follows from the target formula, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient additional evidence. The structure appears small from one Cu and one I crystallographic site [Reasonable Inference], so the >40 atom cell trigger is not applied. Mixed corner/edge-sharing ICu₄ connectivity is explicitly present in the target and similar mixed sharing appears in CuHgSBr (SS-5/TS-2), so one amorphization trigger gives a low–medium risk score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CuI phase is likely **metastable rather than a ground-state phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0310 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of low-positive-hull iodide materials: BiI at similar hull energy is synthesized in sealed ampoules (CS-5), and CuHgSBr/CuHgSI with similar hull energies are synthesized by hydrothermal or sealed-tube methods (SS-5/TS-2, SS-1/TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature iodide transport chemistry, especially CuI + concentrated HI hydrothermal crystallization shown in SS-3. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selection, because iodide systems such as BiI and Ag₃SI show multiple modifications, metastability, and annealing/quenching sensitivity (CS-5, SS-4/TS-5). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best prospects in low-temperature HI-mediated recrystallization rather than high-temperature bulk equilibration.
