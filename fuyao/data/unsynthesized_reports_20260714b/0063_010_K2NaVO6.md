### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s **0.0500 eV/atom** energy above hull places it at the edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin [Target]. CS analogues are largely stable or nearly stable, SS analogues include several stable alkali vanadates, and TS analogues show related metastable oxides can be isolated around similar hull energies [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5]. The uncertainty is that the target’s O–O unit may reflect unusual redox chemistry not captured by hull energy alone [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, matching TS4’s low-temperature diffusion/electrolysis chemistry and SS3’s solution crystallization of potassium vanadate [TS4; SS3]. The main severity deductions are multi-step processing and oxidizer/V-handling hazards [TS4; SS3; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact low-temperature K–Na–V–O6 route is treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetics are favorable because dissolved vanadate, alkali ions, and oxidants remove most solid-state diffusion barriers [SS3; TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The structural penalty is moderate because the target is an anhydrous mixed-alkali phase with an O–O unit, while TS4 and SS3 only demonstrate related solution crystallization behavior [Target; TS4; SS3]. This is why Route 1 scores high but not near-perfect.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Hydrothermal synthesis is supported by CS1/CS2 but requires sealed ampules/autoclaves and concentrated K2CO3 mineralizer [CS1; CS2]. The provided structure does not prove a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing coexistence, so C2/C3 are not triggered and CS/SS/TS give insufficient amorphization-specific evidence, scored neutral 50 [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2NaVO6 is best viewed as a **near-hull, likely metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase**, because its energy above hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. CS and SS analogues strongly support the broader feasibility of alkali vanadate frameworks, while TS4 and the target O–O bond support a low-temperature oxidative crystallization strategy [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS4; Target]. The most critical enabling factor is using solution or hydrothermal transport to crystallize VO4/alkali species without requiring long solid-state diffusion [SS3; CS1; CS2; TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling the unusual oxygen stoichiometry and short O–O unit, which may decompose or transform under high-temperature solid-state or melt conditions [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears **moderately likely**, but phase verification should focus on O–O vibrational signatures, K:Na:V stoichiometry, and exclusion of simpler KVO3/K3VO4/NaVO3 impurities [SS2; SS3; SS5; Reasonable Inference].
