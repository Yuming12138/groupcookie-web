### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0340 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range associated with high synthetic accessibility. TS analogues with similar E_hull values around 0.034–0.048 eV/atom are experimentally realized, including TS1, TS3, TS4, and TS5. SS1 and SS3 are even stronger because structurally similar hydrosulfides are on the hull and experimentally synthesized. CS gives mixed evidence: CS3/CS4 are stable amides, but CS2/CS5 have high calculated hull energies, so CS is not decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and avoids >5 GPa pressure, which is favorable and consistent with SS1/SS3 low-temperature autoclave hydrosulfide synthesis. However, SS1/SS3 require H₂S/D₂S handling, glovebox loading, gas dosing/condensation, and autoclave containment, so deductions are applied for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed precursor, multi-step handling, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. CS provides insufficient direct severity guidance for H₂S hydrosulfides and is scored neutral for this route. TS provides insufficient route-specific evidence and is also neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because SS1/SS3 show that metal hydrosulfides form at 50–150 °C rather than requiring high-temperature solid-state diffusion. The target S–H bond lengths, 1.35–1.38 Å, closely match the 1.34 Å S–H distances in SS1/SS3, supporting structural motif transfer. The main uncertainty is that Ca²⁺ will require a different charge-balanced framework than NaHS or KHS, so the exact CaH₄S₃ stoichiometry may need a narrow H₂S chemical potential.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more demanding because SS5 and CS1/CS2/CS4 show that H-rich Ca/metal amides may require kbar autoclave synthesis and long durations. The pressure is severe in practical terms but below the specified >5 GPa deduction threshold, so no formal high-pressure deduction is applied. Deductions are still applied for controlled reactive atmosphere, volatile sealed molecular precursor, multi-step operation, and toxic/air-sensitive handling. TS gives insufficient evidence for supercritical H₂S synthesis, so it is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Fluid-mediated transport should improve kinetics, as SS5 forms RbCa(NH₂)₃ from metals in supercritical NH₃ at 573 K, and CS1/CS4 show related high-pressure NH₃ chemistry. However, NH₂⁻ and HS⁻/H₂S have different acid–base and coordination behavior, so structural reconstruction is less certain than in Route 1. The route may be useful if Route 1 fails to crystallize the 2D Pmc2₁ phase.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest nominal conditions, consistent with TS1/TS3/TS4/TS5 solution or precipitation syntheses at room temperature to 65 °C. Deductions remain necessary because H₂S is volatile and toxic, and inert or H₂S-saturated atmospheres are likely required. SS and CS provide insufficient evidence for aqueous or low-temperature solution crystallization of Ca–S–H hydrosulfide sheets, so both are neutral for this route. The severity score is therefore better than Routes 1–2, but not risk-free.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution kinetics are intrinsically favorable because ion mobility is high, and TS1/TS3/TS4/TS5 demonstrate mild crystallization of H-containing salts. The weak point is structural selectivity: TS1 reports contamination by Ag₂MoS₄/Ag₂S-like impurities, and TS5 reports pH-dependent impurity formation, so solution chemistry can easily produce competing phases. For the target, water or solvent coordination may stabilize hydrates or simple Ca(HS)₂ rather than the desired CaH₄S₃ sheet.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: the target contains mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing CaS₇ polyhedra, which increases the possibility of disordered or poorly crystalline products. SS1/SS3 show that hydrosulfides can have multiple crystalline modifications and narrow thermal windows, which supports a non-negligible phase-ordering risk. The target has only one cation, so ionic-radius mismatch is not applicable. Unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not prove a >40-atom cell, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaH₄S₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0340 eV/atom, which is within the range where many experimental compounds are accessible; this is supported by TS analogues with similar hull energies. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS1/SS3 because they are direct M–S–H hydrosulfides with nearly identical S–H bond lengths and successful low-temperature autoclave synthesis from metals and H₂S. CS analogues support the broader concept that H-rich ionic frameworks can be synthesized, but they are NH₃/amide systems and therefore chemically less direct. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high H₂S chemical potential in a sealed, dry, oxygen-free environment. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among CaS, Ca(HS)₂-like hydrosulfides, H₂S-rich adducts, and possible poorly ordered layered products.
