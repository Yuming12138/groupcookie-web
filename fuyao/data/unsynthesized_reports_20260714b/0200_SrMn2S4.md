### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the provided rubric. Several chemically/thermodynamically related sulfides are also on the hull, including RbCr5S8, KCr5S8, CsCr5S8, TlCr3S5, and CsCuS4 (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4; TS-1, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS directly confirms SrMn2S4 thermodynamics, so SS is neutral aside from showing near-hull MnS and CrS2 analogues (SS-1, SS-2).

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed sulfide route inherits moderate severity from evacuated quartz/sealed-tube methods used for SrTiS3 and transition-metal sulfides (CS-5, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1). It avoids flowing H2S but requires vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur control, moisture-sensitive sulfides, and likely repeated grinding/annealing. Insufficient evidence from TS on Sr-specific sealed-ampoule behavior means some risk is inferred from CS-5 rather than directly demonstrated for Sr–Mn–S.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary sulfides should be more chemically matched to SrMn2S4 than oxides/carbonates, as SrS is directly used in SrTiS3 synthesis (CS-5). However, long annealing up to three weeks for ternary sulfides (CS-5) and repeated recycling until single phase in sulfide spinels (SS-5) indicate slow solid-state ordering. Structural reconstruction from SrS/Mn2S3 into the target mixed SrS12/MnS4 network is substantial, so kinetics are only moderate.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The carbonate/metal H2S route is directly supported by ACr5S8 syntheses using Cr powder and alkali carbonates under dry H2S at 1270–1370 K for 12 h (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4; TS-4). It has relatively simple steps, but flowing H2S is toxic/corrosive and requires controlled gas handling. The target-specific transfer from alkali chromates to alkaline-earth manganese sulfide is not directly proven, so this route remains challenging.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Gas-phase sulfidation can be kinetically effective because chemically similar thiochromates form in 12 h under H2S (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). Still, Mn metal and SrCO3 require full conversion, and carbonate decomposition/sulfidation may produce intermediate SrS or MnS phases. The target structure is not the same as the CrS6 thiochromate frameworks in CS, so structural reconstruction remains a kinetic uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The flux route uses sealed or gas-tight equipment and sulfur-rich fluxes, so it has penalties for controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive polysulfides. Its temperature can likely remain below 1000 °C, supported by K–Cr–S flux experiments at 900–1000 °C (CS-3), avoiding the highest-temperature penalty. Insufficient evidence from SS for flux synthesis of the exact target means severity is inferred mainly from CS-3 and TS-5.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten carbonate/polysulfide fluxes were deliberately used for complex K–Cr–S sulfides (CS-3), and sulfur-rich media produced chalcogenide crystals in TS-5. Liquid flux should reduce cation diffusion limitations and improve crystal growth compared with dry pellets. The limiting kinetic issue is phase selectivity: CS-3 reports narrow stability windows and transformation risks among KCrS2/KCr3S5/KCr5S8-type phases.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has very different cation environments, with large SrS12 cuboctahedra and much smaller MnS4 tetrahedra, triggering a cation-size/polyhedral mismatch concern from the target structure and [Reasonable Inference]. It also has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity explicitly in the target description, which increases structural complexity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives the exact unit-cell atom count for the target, so C2 is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(MnS2)2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest direct synthesis support comes from sulfide solid-state and flux precedents: Sr-containing ternary sulfides form from SrS under evacuated, moisture-free high-temperature conditions (CS-5), and complex transition-metal sulfides form in H2S or polysulfide/flux environments (CS-1 to CS-4; TS-1, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high sulfur chemical potential to stabilize the formal Mn-rich sulfide framework while avoiding MnS or SrS impurities. The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because related sulfide systems show narrow stability windows, low crystal yields, repeated annealing needs, or competing phases (CS-3, CS-5, SS-5, TS-1). Overall, synthesis is plausible and moderately favorable, with a flux route preferred for kinetics and crystallinity.
