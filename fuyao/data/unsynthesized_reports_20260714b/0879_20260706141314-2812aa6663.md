### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it on the computed convex hull and warrants a high thermodynamic score. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide an experimental Mg–Zr–Cl phase diagram, so the computed ground state could still compete with hydration, volatility, or kinetic trapping during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, consistent with sealed-tube chloride syntheses at 550–900 °C in CS/SS/TS. Its severity comes from controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoule operation, volatile ZrCl4, and moisture-sensitive chloride precursors, all supported by sealed Ta/silica/glass ampoule and glovebox/protective handling in CS/SS/TS. The main uncertainty is whether MgZrCl6 forms at moderate temperature or requires higher-temperature annealing like some Zr cluster chloride analogues in CS.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary chlorides should be more reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and phase-pure chloride analogues can be made from dried binary chlorides in sealed ampoules (SS/TS). However, solid-state ordering into alternating MgCl6/ZrCl6 edge-sharing sheets may be slower than melt crystallization because no CS/SS/TS example directly matches the target topology. Long sintering times in Zr cluster chloride analogues indicate that Zr–Cl phase formation can be kinetically demanding (CS).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses relatively low temperatures, consistent with Mg(AlCl4)2 crystallization at 190–250 °C and VAl2Cl8 at 473 K (CS/SS/TS). The penalties arise from sealed dry handling, volatile chloride species, and multistep fractional crystallization/slow cooling, all directly reflected in Mg(AlCl4)2 and CVT protocols (CS/SS/TS). There is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for ZrCl4–MgCl2 melt behavior, so the exact pressure and crystallization window remain uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 scores highest kinetically because molten chloride media provide rapid ion mobility [Reasonable Inference], and Mg(AlCl4)2 demonstrates that Mg chloride frameworks can crystallize from Lewis-acid chloride melts (CS/SS/TS). HfCl4 and other chloride CVT examples show that volatile chloride species can redistribute and crystallize efficiently at moderate temperatures (SS/TS). The residual kinetic risk is ordering Mg and Zr into the specific 2D AlCl3-derived sheet, which is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with HfCl4, Dy chloride, and VAl chloride transport at moderate temperatures (CS/SS/TS). It still requires sealed ampoules, volatile ZrCl4 management, dry handling, and a controlled thermal gradient, which are all nontrivial operations documented in CS/SS/TS. The uncertainty is that MgCl2 is not shown to co-transport in the reference data, so the practical setup may need optimization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can provide high crystallinity and good transport kinetics because HfCl4 and Dy chloride analogues crystallize or purify by vapor transport/sublimation (SS/TS). ZrCl4 volatility should help move Zr chloride species [Reasonable Inference], while MgCl2 likely remains less volatile and may react at an interface rather than co-transport [Reasonable Inference]. This makes kinetics favorable for crystal growth but less certain for stoichiometric Mg:Zr incorporation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a relatively simple crystalline chloride framework with only edge-sharing octahedra in the provided structural description. Mixed connectivity is not present in the target description, and the Mg/Zr octahedral size match is favorable by ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide many crystalline chloride analogues but insufficient direct evidence on MgZrCl6 glass formation, so the amorphization risk is judged low but not experimentally proven.
