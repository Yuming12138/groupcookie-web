### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull of 0.0190 eV/atom places it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic range, and several thermodynamically similar phases with E_hull ≈0.018–0.029 eV/atom were synthesized (TS1–TS4). Chemically and structurally similar references include both ground-state phases and slightly metastable phases, supporting the idea that this target is accessible rather than prohibitively unstable (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed P1 S/Se ordering corresponds to the experimentally preferred ordering or whether configurational disorder will form instead. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state.**  
The route has moderate condition severity because evacuated ampoules, volatile S/Se, and toxic Sb/Se are required, consistent with SS1, SS2, SS4, CS4, and CS5. Kinetics are acceptable because Bi/Sb chalcogenides form at 650–725 °C in SS1/SS2, but phase purity is a concern because SS1/TS5 obtained mixtures and SS4 needed regrinding/reheating. The key uncertainty is whether Bi/Sb and S/Se ordering can equilibrate without melting or transport.

**F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT.**  
CVT severity is moderate because it requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile transport agents, and multi-step growth, as in CS1 and TS2. Kinetically it is attractive because vapor transport bypasses slow solid diffusion and has produced Bi chalcogenide-related crystals at moderate temperatures (CS1, TS2, TS4). The main uncertainty is halide incorporation or formation of Bi/Sb chalcogenide-halide side phases, a risk implied by halide-containing CVT analogues in CS1 and TS2.

**F2/F3 — Route 3: Bridgman/melt growth.**  
Severity is similar to CVT because sealed ampoules and volatile S/Se are needed, and SS5 used vacuum-sealed Bridgman growth at 800 °C. Kinetics are favorable in the melt, and SS5 shows structurally similar heavy-metal sulfide crystals can be grown as ingots. The uncertainty is congruent melting: if Bi₃Sb₃Se₂S₇ decomposes peritectically, Bridgman growth may yield competing Bi₂S₃/Sb₂S₃/Bi₂Se₃/Sb₂Se₃ phases. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 4: high-pressure.**  
This route is conditionally severe because SS3/TS1/TS3 require 5.5 GPa and up to 1200 °C using cubic-anvil equipment. Kinetics are good under melt/high-pressure conditions, and similar BiS₂/BiSe₂ layered phases were stabilized this way (SS3, TS1, TS3). The limitation is that pressure windows are narrow: SS3/TS1/TS3 report different outcomes at different pressures, so transferability to the Bi–Sb–S–Se quaternary target is uncertain.

**F2/F3 — Route 5: ionothermal.**  
Ionothermal synthesis has mild temperature severity because CS2 reports 165 °C growth, but it requires air-sensitive Lewis-acidic media and glovebox handling. Kinetics may be good in a dissolving ionic liquid, but only CS2 gives useful evidence; insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports halide-free Bi–Sb–S–Se ionothermal crystallization. Thus the route is exploratory rather than the recommended primary synthesis path.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The cation-size mismatch between Bi³⁺ and Sb³⁺ triggers one amorphization/ordering-risk criterion, but both are lone-pair p-block cations commonly found in related chalcogenide frameworks (SS1, SS2, TS5). The target has corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing in the provided structure, reducing severe amorphization risk. The remaining uncertainty is site disorder between Bi/Sb and S/Se, which may broaden diffraction peaks or produce average higher-symmetry/disordered products. [Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Bi₃Sb₃Se₂S₇ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.0190 eV/atom and thermodynamically similar synthesized phases occur in the same energy range (TS1–TS4). The CS/SS/TS evidence supports synthesis of related layered/ribbon Bi/Sb/S/Se chalcogenides by sealed ampoule, CVT, Bridgman, and high-pressure methods, with the strongest practical support coming from CVT and sealed-ampoule chalcogenide growth (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, heavy p-block chalcogenide chemistry, which permits crystallization at moderate temperatures and allows vapor/melt transport (CS1, SS5, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering control, since several close analogues form mixtures or require narrow growth windows (SS1, SS2, TS4). Overall, the target is synthesizable but likely requires careful chalcogen chemical-potential and temperature-gradient control.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile S/Se loss and internal ampoule pressure | High | Use evacuated sealed ampoules and slight chalcogen excess; sealed-tube practice is supported by SS1, SS2, CS4, CS5, and TS2. |
| 2 | All | Competing Bi₂S₃, Sb₂S₃, Bi₂Se₃, Sb₂Se₃ or mixed sulfoselenide phases | High | Use prereacted binaries, long anneals, regrinding, and slow cooling; mixed-phase issues are reported in SS1/TS5 and TS4, while regrinding improves purity in SS4. |
| 3 | All | Slow Bi/Sb and S/Se site ordering in triclinic P1 | Medium–High | Add post-growth anneal at 400–550 °C for several days; long sintering and controlled cooling are supported by SS2, SS4, CS4. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Halide incorporation during CVT | Medium–High | Use minimal I₂/Br₂/S₂Cl₂ loading and screen transport agents; halide transport is effective in CS1/TS2 but target is halide-free, so low transport-agent activity is needed. [Reasonable Inference] |
| 5 | Route 2 | Crystal growth difficulty / nucleation of binary chalcogenides | Medium | Pre-react target powder before CVT and use a shallow gradient; TS2 uses prereaction before transport, and TS4 shows mixed Bi₂S₃ crystals when phase control is imperfect. |
| 6 | Route 1 | Incomplete reaction due to solid-state diffusion | Medium | Use fine grinding, pelletizing, repeated annealing; SS4 reports regrinding/reheating to improve phase purity. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Incongruent melting or segregation during Bridgman growth | Medium | First establish DTA/DSC behavior, then use pre-reacted charge and post-annealing; Bridgman growth is supported by SS5, but decomposition risk is [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 8 | Route 4 | High-pressure polymorph sensitivity | High | Screen 3–7.5 GPa and 650–1200 °C; SS3/TS1/TS3 show pressure-dependent reaction/polymorph outcomes. |
| 9 | Route 5 | Halide-rich ionothermal medium may form halide phases instead of target | High | Treat as exploratory; lower halide activity or use alternative chalcogenide fluxes; CS2 supports ionothermal Bi/Se halide growth but insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports halide-free target synthesis. |
| 10 | All | Toxicity of Sb/Se and corrosive/volatile transport agents | Medium | Load in glovebox or fume hood, use secondary containment, and avoid excess transport agent; glovebox/controlled handling appears in CS2 and CS3. |
