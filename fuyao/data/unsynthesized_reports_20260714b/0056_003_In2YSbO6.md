### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable/metastable boundary of the provided scoring rubric [Target]. CS analogues are mostly stable or near-stable, while TS analogues around 0.058–0.070 eV/atom are experimentally realized, supporting a high but not perfect F1 score [CS-1; CS-2; CS-3; CS-4; CS-5; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether the target is a true isolable polymorph or competes with lower-energy Y–In–Sb oxide assemblages not represented in the references [Target; TS-1; TS-5; Reasonable Inference].

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Solid-State.** Severity is moderate because the route avoids high pressure and extreme temperature but needs N₂/Ar or sealed/vacuum-style control, repeated firings, and toxic/volatile Sb₂O₃ handling [SS-2; SS-5; TS-5]. Kinetics are limited by refractory Y₂O₃/In₂O₃ and full reconstruction into a P1 quaternary oxide, even though solid-state analogues show this chemistry can work with grinding and long holds [CS-3; CS-5; TS-3; Target].

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Flux.** Severity remains moderate because flux processing adds slow cooling, containment, and contamination control, but it avoids multi-GPa pressure and can be kept below ~1000 °C [CS-2; SS-2; TS-2]. Kinetics improve because flux-mediated transport reduces Y/In diffusion constraints and better supports crystal growth, which is why Route 2 receives the highest F3 among ambient-pressure routes [CS-2; SS-2; TS-2; Reasonable Inference].

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal.** Condition severity is mild because CS/SS hydrothermal analogues operate near 200–240 °C under autogenous pressure, but strong base and Sb handling still add process risk [CS-4; SS-1]. Kinetics/phase selectivity are poor because there is no TS hydrothermal analogue and no direct Y–In–Sb hydrothermal precedent, so TS is neutral 50 for that route signal and structural reconstruction is highly uncertain [TS; CS-4; SS-1; Reasonable Inference].

**Route 4 F2/F3 — High Pressure.** Severity is high because SS/TS high-pressure analogues require ~5–6 GPa-class apparatus, capsules, high temperature, and quenching [SS-3; SS-4; TS-1]. Kinetics and structural stabilization are better than ordinary solid-state for unusual In oxide motifs, but phase purity remains risky because HP analogues report impurity or single-phase difficulty [SS-3; SS-4; TS-1].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** One trigger is assigned because the target combines substantially different cation sizes and coordination environments, including Y 6-coordinate, In 5-/3-coordinate, and Sb see-saw-like bonding [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The atom count trigger is not assigned and the mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing trigger is not assigned because the target description does not report >40 atoms or coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing [Target]. Remaining uncertainty comes from the unusual O–O contact, which resembles SS Bi₂O₃ O–O bonding but is not enough by itself to trigger the rubric’s C3 criterion [Target; SS-1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YIn₂SbO₆ is best treated as a **borderline metastable but likely accessible oxide**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and TS analogues with similar metastability are experimentally synthesized [Target; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5]. CS data strongly support oxide synthesis using conventional solid-state or flux conditions, while SS data support the presence of Sb(III)-like low-coordinate motifs but also warn that some In/Bi structural analogues require high pressure or show phase-purity challenges [CS-1; CS-3; CS-5; SS-2; SS-4]. The most critical enabling factor is the chemically simple Y³⁺/In³⁺/Sb³⁺ oxide charge balance with accessible oxide precursors [Target; CS-3; SS-2; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Sb₂O₃ volatility/redox state and crystallization of the unusual P1 framework with a 3-coordinate In site and O–O contact [Target; SS-5; SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, flux-assisted oxide growth is the best first attempt because it improves diffusion and crystal growth without committing immediately to high-pressure synthesis [CS-2; SS-2; TS-2].
