### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for high synthesizability. Similar stable or near-stable oxides occur in the references: Na3OsO5 and Na5OsO6 have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1, SS-4), while Li2Cu2TeO6 has E_hull ≈0.0556 eV/atom and is accessible by ion exchange (TS-3). The uncertainty is that the target may be metastable at the boundary, and small errors in calculated energy could shift it into the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom regime.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, supported by Na3OsO5 at 400 °C (SS-1) and Na5OsO6 at 750 °C (SS-4), so no high-temperature deduction is applied. The route is penalized for reactive/air-sensitive peroxide chemistry and possible sealed-container requirements, both supported by SS-1 and SS-4. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Na–Li–Ir peroxide route, so TS contributes only neutral indirect support.

#### F3 — Route 1

The peroxide route benefits from active Na2O2 chemistry, directly shown in Na3OsO5 and Na5OsO6 syntheses (SS-1, SS-4). However, Ir or IrO2 is refractory by physicochemical reasoning, and Li incorporation adds another diffusion/order requirement not present in SS-1 or SS-4. CS-2/CS-3 show Li-containing 5d oxides can form by ceramic synthesis, but those are perovskite Sr2LiReO6 analogues, not the same P1 trigonal-bipyramidal framework.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 operates at low ion-exchange temperatures: 300–310 °C in molten LiNO3 for LiEuTiO4 (CS-4) and 300 °C for Li2Cu2TeO6 (TS-3). It is penalized because it requires at least three operations: parent synthesis, ion exchange, and washing/drying, as described in CS-4 and TS-3. The main uncertainty is the lack of a directly reported Na–Ir parent in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

LiNO3 molten-salt exchange has excellent kinetics for Li incorporation, as shown by Na→Li exchange in CS-4 and TS-3. TS-3 is especially relevant because Li2Cu2TeO6 was inaccessible by direct high-temperature reaction but accessible topochemically. The uncertainty is whether a Na-rich Ir oxide parent has a sufficiently similar framework to preserve the target Na2LiIrO6 topology.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate flux temperatures; TS-4 demonstrates oxide/hydroxide flux growth at 430 °C for 20 h in air, and SS-1 shows high-valent Na–Os oxide synthesis at 400 °C under O2. The route avoids high pressure and very high temperature, so its condition severity score is high. The penalty comes from corrosive/reactive alkali fluxes and oxidizers, consistent with TS-4 and SS-1/SS-4.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth improves kinetics by providing a liquid medium; TS-4 obtained single crystals at 430 °C, and SS-1 shows alkali peroxide chemistry can stabilize high-valent 5d oxides at low temperature. However, flux crystallization is not topotactic and may produce competing phases, as TS-4 reports minor by-products and SS-1 reports persistent Na5OsO6 impurity. Insufficient evidence from CS for Na–Li–Ir flux growth specifically, so that aspect is inferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very different cations, Na, Li, and Ir, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion by [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives corner-sharing LiO5–IrO5 connectivity but does not show edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, so C2 is treated as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiIrO6 is likely a borderline ground-state / low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high-synthesizability cutoff. The strongest support comes from structurally similar Na3OsO5 and Na5OsO6, which show that alkali-rich high-valent 5d oxides can be made under oxidizing low-to-moderate temperature conditions (SS-1, SS-4). The best enabling factor is low-temperature alkali mobility, especially molten LiNO3 ion exchange, which successfully stabilizes Li oxides that are difficult by direct synthesis (CS-4, TS-3). The main limiting factor is oxidation-state and phase control around Ir, because high-valent Os analogues show impurity formation and moisture/air sensitivity (SS-1, SS-4). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires careful topochemical or flux-assisted control rather than a simple one-step ceramic route.
