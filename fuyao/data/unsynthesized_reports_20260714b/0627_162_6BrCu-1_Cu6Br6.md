### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0180 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window **[Target data]**. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, including CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom, CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom, CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom, and AuBr at 0.0178 eV/atom, all have reported synthesis routes (TS-1 to TS-5). This supports a high F1 score, but the target is likely not the unique deep ground state because it is still 18 meV/atom above the hull **[Target data; TS-1 to TS-5]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures compared with CuSe at 1170 K and CuS at 620–670 K (CS-1, CS-3), so no high-temperature deduction is applied. The main severity comes from evacuated sealed-tube operation, volatile Br₂, and Br₂ toxicity/corrosivity **[Reasonable Inference]**, with sealed volatile/bromide handling supported by TS-5. **Insufficient evidence from SS** directly supports elemental Br₂ handling for CuBr, so SS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The highly reactive bromine source should accelerate conversion, but Cu metal is a dense precursor and may limit reaction kinetics **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with long sealed-tube treatments for elemental CuSe in CS-1. Diffusion is mitigated by volatile/liquid bromine transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, analogous to sulfur/selenium sealed-tube transport in CS-1/CS-3. The largest uncertainty is polymorph selection: no CS/SS/TS source directly shows the triclinic 2D CuBr phase from elements.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 is mild because it uses preformed CuBr and temperatures similar to or lower than CuHgSX evacuated-tube syntheses at 553–573 K (SS-2, SS-4; TS-2, TS-3). Vacuum sealing and bromide volatility still impose deductions **[Reasonable Inference]**, supported by repeated use of evacuated tubes for halide/chalcogenide analogues (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). **Insufficient evidence from CS** specifically supports CuBr polymorph annealing, so CS is neutral for this condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Using CuBr as the precursor eliminates long-range compositional diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CuX precursors participate effectively in related evacuated-tube reactions (SS-2, SS-4; TS-2, TS-3). However, transforming a conventional CuBr polymorph into a triclinic 2D P1 sheet may require substantial reconstructive rearrangement **[Reasonable Inference]**. Long times in SS-2/SS-4 and TS-3 indicate that halide/chalcogenide ordering can be slow even at modest temperatures.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure; the best analogue uses 670 K hydrothermal conditions for 24 h in sealed ampoules (SS-1; TS-1). Deductions arise from sealed HBr pressure, corrosive/toxic HBr, and multiple handling/crystallization steps **[Reasonable Inference]**, with HBr explicitly used in SS-1/TS-1. **Insufficient evidence from CS** supports hydrothermal CuBr chemistry, so CS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal recrystallization should provide the best kinetics because dissolution/reprecipitation bypasses bulk Cu diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**. This inference is strongly supported by CuHgSBr growth from CuBr/HBr at 670 K in only 24 h with high-quality crystals and no reported side products (SS-1; TS-1). The remaining uncertainty is that HgS in the analogue may template or stabilize the ternary structure, whereas binary CuBr must select the desired P1 sheet without that structural scaffold **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Physical vapor transport uses moderate temperatures and no GPa pressure **[Reasonable Inference]**, supported generally by sublimation growth in CS-5 and vacuum/dry N₂ bromide processing in TS-5. It requires sealed or dry/vacuum conditions and careful volatile-bromide management **[Reasonable Inference]**, so the severity is moderate. **Insufficient evidence from SS** directly supports CuBr vapor transport.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Vapor transport should offer good mass transport for volatile CuBr **[Reasonable Inference]**, and sublimation growth is established for ZnS platelets (CS-5). However, CS-5 also reports difficult control of multiple ZnS polytypes, and TS-5 reports challenging phase control for AuBr, so crystallization into the exact low-symmetry target may be uncertain. Composition control is easier for a binary than a multication phase **[Reasonable Inference]**, but Br loss or overpressure remains limiting, consistent with bromide volatility concerns in TS-5.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Cu as the cation, so cation-size mismatch does not apply **[Target data]**. The described P1 cell has six Cu and six Br inequivalent sites, implying a relatively small 12-atom primitive description rather than a >40-atom complex cell **[Target data; Reasonable Inference]**. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct evidence of amorphization for this exact structure, and the absence of multication disorder supports a low amorphization risk **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0180 eV/atom **[Target data]**. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values have been synthesized, especially CuHgSBr/CuHgSCl/CuHgSI and AuBr (TS-1 to TS-5), so the energy penalty is not prohibitive. The strongest enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halide routes, especially CuBr/HBr hydrothermal chemistry shown for CuHgSBr (SS-1; TS-1). The main limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: none of CS/SS/TS directly reports the triclinic 2D P1 CuBr polymorph, and low-dimensional halide/chalcogenide phases can require careful cooling or long annealing **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with polytype/phase-control issues in CS-5 and TS-5. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with hydrothermal recrystallization the most promising first attempt.
