### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0440 eV/atom, so it falls in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. CS supports this because very close chalcogenide halides are stable or nearly stable: NbSe₂Cl₂ is at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1), Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ is at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-2), and Nb(SBr)₂ is near the hull at 0.0008 eV/atom (CS-3). TS also supports synthesizability at similar metastability because phases at 0.033–0.047 eV/atom are experimentally made (TS-1 to TS-5). SS is structurally supportive but less chemically direct; insufficient evidence from SS for the specific Y–S–Cl thermodynamic competition, so SS is treated as supportive but not decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to 500 °C for Nb(SBr)₂ (CS-3), 530 °C for Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ (CS-2), and 600–650 °C for LuPS₄ (SS-1), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The route does require vacuum/sealed-tube conditions and volatile sulfur/halide containment, supported by CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, and SS-4. Moisture-sensitive Y chloride handling is supported by CS-4, so an air-sensitive precursor penalty is applied. TS provides insufficient route-specific evidence for direct Y–S–Cl solid-state synthesis, so TS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate because sulfur and chloride species can be reactive/volatile, but Y metal is refractory and likely slow to homogenize [Reasonable Inference]. Solid-state analogues often require days to weeks, including Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ at 2–3 weeks (CS-2), LuPS₄ at 24 total days (SS-1), and Hf phosphosulfides over weeks (SS-5). The exact topology is supported by CS-3, but direct formation from Y/YCl₃/S likely requires substantial reconstruction. TS gives general support that solid-state sulfides can be synthesized at similar thermodynamic stability, but insufficient evidence from TS for this exact structural reconstruction.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids very high temperature because CS-1 reports CVT for NbX₂Y₂ at 400–515 °C and SS-2 reports ScPS₄ vapor transport at 690→670 °C. The penalties come from vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile transport agents, multi-step pre-reaction/transport/separation, and halogen/YCl₃ handling, all consistent with CS-1, SS-2, and CS-4. No high-pressure penalty applies because the references use sealed quartz tubes rather than GPa-scale pressure (CS-1, SS-2). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for CVT, so TS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best kinetics because vapor transport reduces dependence on bulk diffusion; CS-1 explicitly grows chemically close layered chalcogenide halides by chemical transport. Structural reconstruction is favored because CS-1 and CS-3 contain the same essential 2D M-chalcogen-halide motif with chalcogen–chalcogen dimers and bridging halides. The main uncertainty is whether Y forms a sufficiently volatile and correctly reducing chloride/sulfide transport species [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 supports the general viability of CVT for layered chalcogenides, while TS is thermodynamic rather than kinetic evidence and is therefore neutral for vapor-transport kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/chloride flux, multi-step growth/separation, and moisture-sensitive flux handling. TS-4 directly supports an 800 °C rare-earth sulfide flux route using GdCl₃ in evacuated silica ampoules, and SS-3 supports molten chloride synthesis under sealed/inert conditions. No >1000 °C or high-pressure penalties apply. CS provides insufficient direct flux evidence for Y(SCl)₂, so CS is neutral for this route’s severity beyond general halide sensitivity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth should improve kinetics by providing a molten ionic medium, supported by GdCl₃ flux synthesis at 800 °C for 7 days (TS-4) and molten chloride synthesis in SS-3. The precursor activity and diffusion scores are therefore higher than the direct solid-state route. Structural confidence is lower than CVT because no exact M(SCl)₂ rare-earth chloride-flux analogue is given; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from rare-earth sulfide chloride flux chemistry. CS gives insufficient direct flux-growth signal, so it is neutral for Route 3 kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Y, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger does not apply. The provided target description does not state a unit cell atom count >40, and CS/SS/TS do not provide target-specific cell-size evidence, so C2 is not triggered. The target is a relatively ordered 2D crystalline sheet with specific Y–S, Y–Cl, and S–S distances; however, mixed edge/vertex/face connectivity is not explicitly described, so C3 is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is modest because layered chalcogenide halides can have stacking/order polymorphism, as seen for Nb(SBr)₂ polymorphs (CS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y(SCl)₂ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase rather than an unambiguous ground state**, because its E_hull is 0.0440 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom. The CS evidence is strong structurally and chemically, especially NbSe₂Cl₂ by CVT (CS-1) and C2/m Nb(SBr)₂ with the same layered motif (CS-3), but the change from Nb to Y introduces a nontrivial redox/valence uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. SS supports the general feasibility of layered sulfide synthesis in sealed ampoules and CVT, but it is less chemically direct. TS supports the metastability window because several phases near 0.03–0.05 eV/atom are synthesized (TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is sealed-tube halogen/chalcogen transport that can maintain S/Cl chemical potentials, while the main limiting factor is competing YCl₃/Y₂S₃/Y–S–Cl phase formation and possible mismatch between Y³⁺ chemistry and the M(SX)₂ disulfide-halide topology.
