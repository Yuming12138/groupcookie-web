### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold in the required rubric; this justifies F1 = 90. TS analogues near this hull range, especially TS2 at 0.0549 eV/atom, TS3 at 0.0581 eV/atom, and TS5 at 0.0580 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized, supporting that this energy range is accessible. CS and SS include experimentally reported materials with much larger calculated hull energies, so they suggest that molecular salts may be kinetically isolable, but they are not as directly predictive of target stability; insufficient exact hull-transfer evidence from CS/SS is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because SS1 uses anhydrous HF, AsF₅, dry-box handling, all-Teflon vacuum-line apparatus, and month-long temperature-gradient crystallization. These features trigger deductions for controlled/vacuum handling, volatile sealed reagents, multiple steps, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors. The low temperature is favorable, but the chemical hazard and equipment burden dominate the score.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is strong because SS1 is solution-mediated and directly forms discrete AsF₆⁻ octahedra with As–F distances matching the target. Diffusion barriers are low in HF solution compared with solid-state synthesis. The main uncertainty is that SS1 stabilizes ammonium-type species, not the target C/O molecular cation.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less thermally severe but still hazardous because TS4 uses volatile liquid SO₂, reactive SO₂ClF, and a sealed two-bulb vessel. The method is room-temperature and avoids high pressure in the >5 GPa sense, but volatile/toxic reagents and multi-step solvent transfer justify substantial deductions. CS3 supports that related fluorinated molecular chemistry often needs inert/vacuum technique, reinforcing the controlled-handling penalty.

#### F3 — Route 2

TS4 shows efficient room-temperature formation of large AsF₆⁻ salt crystals, so precursor activity and molecular mobility are high. The route is well matched to a zero-dimensional AsF₆ molecular salt, and SS1 further confirms the robustness of isolated AsF₆⁻ octahedra. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether the target organic/oxocarbon cation forms cleanly before competing decomposition or polymerization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is mild because CS2, CS4, SS3, and TS5 all demonstrate room-temperature or low-temperature crystallization by evaporation/diffusion without high pressure or high temperature. Deductions remain for arsenic salt toxicity and a likely metathesis/filtration/crystallization sequence, analogous to CS4 silver-salt anion exchange. If the target cation requires strictly dry inert conditions, Route 3 would become more severe, but that is not directly proven by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution mobility and crystallization kinetics are favorable: SS3 reports nearly millimeter crystals overnight, while CS2 and CS4 show room-temperature evaporation crystallization of fluoride-containing molecular salts. However, these analogues do not show formation of the target’s unusual C/O fragments, so the structural-reconstruction component is penalized. The route is best if a stable preformed cation precursor is available; otherwise, it may fail by hydrolysis or side reactions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is zero-dimensional, with isolated AsF₆ and molecular C/O clusters, which lowers amorphization risk relative to extended mixed-polyhedral networks; this is consistent with SS1 and TS4 zero-dimensional AsF₆ salts. The atom count in the described cell exceeds 40, triggering one amorphization/complexity penalty. Ionic-radius mismatch and mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing criteria are not meaningfully applicable to this molecular salt, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to trigger them.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a borderline thermodynamically stable or very low-energy metastable phase because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which sits at the favorable cutoff in the required rubric. The strongest analogue support comes from SS1 and TS4, both of which demonstrate isolated AsF₆⁻ cluster salts, while CS and SS3/CS2/CS4 support mild crystallization of discrete fluoride-containing salts. The most important enabling factor is the robustness and transferability of octahedral AsF₆⁻ units, whose bond lengths in SS1 and TS4 are very close to the target. The most important limiting factor is the uncertain chemistry and stability of the target C/O molecular cation, which is not directly represented in CS/SS/TS. Therefore, synthesis is plausible, especially by low-temperature solution crystallization, but it depends strongly on identifying or generating the correct organic/oxocarbon cation precursor.
