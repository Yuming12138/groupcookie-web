### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the upper edge of the most favorable scoring bin in the provided rubric. Chemically similar CS-1, CS-2, and CS-5 include phases at or near the hull, supporting the idea that Sr/Li-containing complex oxides can be stable or nearly stable. TS analogues span ~0.058–0.070 eV/atom and are still experimentally synthesized, including TS-1 to TS-4, so 0.050 eV/atom is not prohibitive. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed phase is a true ground-state peroxide/aluminate or a metastable low-temperature oxidation product.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Conventional ceramic solid-state reaction

CS-1 and CS-2 strongly support high-temperature solid-state access to Sr-containing aluminates/complex oxides, with Sr₆Y₂Al₄O₁₅ made at 1500 °C and Sr₂ScGaO₅ made at 1200 °C. CS-5 supports Li-containing oxide synthesis by staged heating, but it also shows long treatment times and oxygen flow. SS gives a warning rather than support: the target’s O–O bond resembles peroxides, and SS peroxide analogues are commonly prepared by lower-temperature peroxide/precipitation/sealed routes, not by high-T ceramic sintering. Thus Route 1 is feasible for cation mixing but kinetically and chemically risky for peroxide retention.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Oxidative peroxide solid-state route

SS-1, SS-2, and SS-5 directly support peroxide-like O–O distances of 1.49–1.52 Å, matching the target’s 1.52 Å O–O contact. SS-4 demonstrates a closed-bomb peroxide oxide synthesis at 800 °C using Na₂O₂, indicating that sealed oxidative solid-state chemistry can preserve peroxide units. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for peroxide aluminates, but CS-5 supports the need for oxygen-rich conditions in Li-containing oxides. The main uncertainty is balancing peroxide stability with enough thermal energy for Al incorporation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Oxidizing flux growth

CS-3 and TS-1 support flux or melt growth with slow cooling for complex oxides, and SS-3 shows peroxide complex crystallization from oxidizing alkaline peroxide media. Flux should improve kinetics by lowering diffusion limitations, especially for refractory Al₂O₃ [Reasonable Inference]. However, the flux must not reduce or hydrolyze peroxide units, and washing may damage the product, consistent with moisture sensitivity issues noted for peroxide analogues SS-1, SS-2, and SS-5. Therefore the route scores high kinetically but carries phase-control uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-pressure oxidizing synthesis

TS-2 and TS-3 show that thermodynamically similar oxides near this energy range can require high pressure, gold capsules, and rapid quenching. This supports high pressure as a contingency if the P1 phase is metastable. However, CS and SS provide insufficient evidence that LiSr₂AlO₆ specifically needs pressure, and high-pressure work adds major equipment severity. The route is useful but not preferred unless ambient-pressure peroxide/flux methods fail.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations of very different size and coordination preference: Li in distorted four-coordinate geometry, Sr in 6–9 coordination, and Al in trigonal bipyramidal coordination, so C1 is triggered. The target description does not establish a >40 atom cell, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target cell-size-driven amorphization. The target also does not clearly show simultaneous corner/edge/face-sharing coexistence, unlike some CS/SS/TS analogues that explicitly mention face/edge/corner mixtures. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiSr₂AlO₆ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable threshold. The strongest support comes from SS peroxide analogues, whose O–O distances match the target’s 1.52 Å contact, while CS supports the broader feasibility of Sr–Al and Li-containing oxide synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high oxygen chemical potential/peroxide character during crystallization, as supported by SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, and SS-5. The most critical limiting factor is the competition between peroxide stability at lower temperature and the slow incorporation of Al into an ordered crystalline framework. Flux or sealed peroxide synthesis is therefore more credible than a purely high-temperature ceramic route.
