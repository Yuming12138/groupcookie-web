### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring range. CS supports synthesizability of chemically similar oxides at E_hull = 0–0.0597 eV/atom, including BiCa₂VO₆ at E_hull = 0 and La₂Ti₃(AgO₅)₂ at E_hull ≈0.0597 (CS-1, CS-4). TS provides strong support that phases in the 0.055–0.076 eV/atom range can be experimentally obtained (TS-2, TS-3, TS-4), while SS similarly contains synthesized metastable layered oxides near 0.04–0.06 eV/atom (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that target stability may depend on oxygen chemical potential and Ti valence, which is not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is not extremely hot if modeled after CS-1 at 950 °C for 30 h, and standard ceramic equipment is supported by CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, and CS-5. However, target redox chemistry likely requires inert/vacuum or low-pO₂ conditions, supported indirectly by TS-3 and TS-5. Bi₂O₃ volatility and Ti redox control add severity by [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives insufficient useful signal for this direct ceramic route because SS examples are mostly intercalation/topochemical syntheses rather than direct oxide ceramic synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS-1 shows a chemically similar Ca–Bi oxide can form in a simple 950 °C/30 h ceramic firing, which is favorable. CS-2 and CS-5 show that complex multication oxides can require long firings or multiple grinding/heating steps, which lowers the kinetics score. The target’s P1 symmetry and mixed TiO₄/TiO₆ framework imply a reconstructive reaction from oxides [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives limited direct kinetic guidance for Ca–Ti–Bi oxide formation, so it is only partially useful here.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from no high pressure and moderate maximum temperature relative to TS-3, which reaches 1450 °C. It is penalized for sealed/vacuum operation, possible Bi volatility, and multistep flux processing, all consistent with sealed-tube complexity in TS-3. CS-1 supports lower-temperature Bi/Ca oxide chemistry, but does not directly demonstrate flux growth. SS gives insufficient direct evidence for flux growth of this target, since its closest structural analogues are mainly intercalation products.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux is expected to improve ion transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], addressing the slow diffusion issues implied by CS-2 and CS-5. Controlled cooling is experimentally used in CS-1 and TS-2/TS-3, supporting the idea that crystallization control matters. The main uncertainty is whether CaTi₂BiO₆ has a stable liquidus/flux window before Bi loss or decomposition. TS supports sealed high-temperature synthesis of metastable/reduced oxides but not this exact flux chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C processing, which reduces condition severity relative to CS-2/CS-3/CS-5 and TS-3. It is penalized for multistep gel/burnout/crystallization and for controlled final atmosphere, motivated by TS-5 Ar-flow redox-sensitive synthesis. Direct sol-gel evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS; this is a reasoned extrapolation rather than a demonstrated analogue route. The use of nitrate/alkoxide chemistry adds handling complexity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Sol-gel/Pechini chemistry gives high precursor homogeneity and short diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], which is advantageous for low-symmetry multication oxides. This route is intended to mitigate long firing times observed in CS-2 and repeated firing in CS-5. The main kinetic risk is that an amorphous precursor may crystallize first into simpler competing phases rather than the P1 target. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct signal for whether CaTi₂BiO₆ nucleates from an amorphous gel.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different coordination preferences: Ca in 6-coordinate geometry, Bi in 4-coordinate geometry, and Ti in both tetrahedral and octahedral environments, as stated in the target structure. This triggers the ionic-size/coordination mismatch check and suggests moderate glass/amorphous or multiphase risk during low-temperature routes. The target description shows corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues often contain mixed polyhedral networks, but insufficient evidence shows the target has >40 atoms per cell or face-sharing coexistence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaTi₂BiO₆ is likely a mildly metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. CS analogues strongly support that Ca/Bi/Ti-related oxides can be made by ceramic methods, especially BiCa₂VO₆ at 950 °C (CS-1) and Ti-containing perovskite-derived oxides by solid-state sintering (CS-3, CS-5). TS and SS analogues support that phases with similar metastability can be synthesized, although many structurally similar examples require intercalation or controlled atmospheres (SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is homogeneous cation mixing combined with controlled oxygen potential to stabilize mixed Ti valence. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Bi volatility and Ti redox state while avoiding simpler Ca–Ti–O, Bi–Ti–O, and Bi₂O₃-rich impurity phases.
