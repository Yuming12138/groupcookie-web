### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target Ag2S is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, which strongly supports synthesizability as a thermodynamic ground-state phase. TS analogues contain multiple E_hull = 0 compounds synthesized by standard or high-pressure methods, reinforcing that the thermodynamic similarity set is compatible with experimentally accessible phases (TS2–TS5). CS and SS include several low-energy Ag sulfide/chalcogenide analogues, though some are metastable by 0.04–0.12 eV/atom, so they provide supportive but less decisive evidence (CS2/CS3; SS3/SS4/SS5). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target tetragonal P4/nmm Ag2S may compete with other known Ag2S polymorphs even if the composition is stable [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses vacuum/sealed ampoules, which are standard for Ag sulfide/chalcogenide analogues (SS3/SS4/TS2). The main penalty is sulfur volatility and the need for staged heating to prevent pressure buildup and stoichiometry loss [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for elemental Ag2S synthesis and is scored neutral-to-supportive only through related chalcogenide handling, while SS and TS provide the main support. The uncertainty is whether low-temperature annealing can select the tetragonal sheet polymorph rather than a conventional Ag2S phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are acceptable but not ideal because elemental Ag is a dense metal precursor, while sulfur is reactive [Reasonable Inference]. SS3 shows Ag3CuS2 can be made at moderate temperature from binary sulfides but requires 2 weeks to 1 month and sometimes mechanical alloying, indicating slow ordering/diffusion can occur in Ag sulfides. SS4 shows direct elemental synthesis is possible, but at much higher temperature for a ternary analogue. The target binary nature improves the outlook, but complete reconstruction from elements keeps the structural-reconstruction score moderate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because the sulfide formation step can occur at ~80 °C by analogy with CdS chemical bath deposition (CS5). The deductions arise from multi-step processing, possible sulfur retention during annealing, and use of sulfide/thiourea/Ag salt precursors that require careful handling [Reasonable Inference; Na2S in SS1/TS1 and thiourea in CS5]. SS and TS support the use of sulfide mineralizer chemistry, but they also show that poor crystallization conditions can yield amorphous products (SS1/TS1). The key uncertainty is whether the anneal crystallizes the exact P4/nmm polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable because solution precipitation creates highly mixed Ag–S at short length scale, unlike dense elemental solid-state routes [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 demonstrates rapid low-temperature sulfide formation, and SS1/TS1 show Na2S/S/Ag chemistry can crystallize Ag–S frameworks when adequate crystallization conditions are used. The structural-reconstruction score is not maximal because a precipitated Ag2S may be amorphous or nanocrystalline and require ordering anneals; analogous CdS films can contain mixed phases/impurities (CS5), and subcritical Ag sulfide reactions can be amorphous (SS1/TS1). Overall, this route balances mild conditions and good precursor activity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is condition-severe because SS1/TS1 require a high-pressure autoclave, Ar counterpressure, sealed quartz tube, and supercritical ethylenediamine at 300 °C for six days. Although the pressure is far below the >5 GPa deduction threshold, the apparatus and solvent hazard are significant [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for supercritical Ag2S and is neutral for this route; SS and TS are strong because the same KAg3S2 entry appears as both closest structural and thermodynamic analogue. The limiting factor is specialized equipment and solvent safety rather than thermodynamics.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are strong because SS1/TS1 demonstrate crystalline Ag–S framework formation at 300 °C through supercritical-fluid transport. The close local Ag–S bonding in KAg3S2, with two-coordinate Ag and Ag–S ≈2.47 Å, is close to target Ag–S = 2.43 Å, supporting structural transferability (SS1/TS1 and target). However, the same source reports subcritical reactions failed and produced amorphous products, so the crystallization window is sensitive (SS1/TS1). Omitting K/alkali species for binary Ag2S may alter nucleation and phase selection [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 requires sealed ampoules and high-temperature melt or near-melt treatment, supported by Ag3SI melt growth around 700 °C in quartz ampoules (CS2/SS2). It is not GPa-scale or >1000 °C, but sulfur vapor pressure and ampoule safety are significant concerns [Reasonable Inference]. CS3/SS5 further show Ag2S-containing sealed ampoule reactions at 973 K and phase-dependent annealing. The condition severity is therefore moderate to challenging.

#### F3 — Route 4

Melt-mediated growth offers excellent mass transport, as Ag3SI crystals were grown by melting and slow translation through a temperature gradient (CS2/SS2). Using preformed Ag2S feedstock avoids elemental diffusion and improves precursor activity (CS2/SS2; CS3/SS5). The weaker point is structural reconstruction and polymorph selection: high-temperature growth may favor a different Ag2S polymorph, and Ag3SI analogues show phase mixtures or phase-treatment sensitivity (CS2/SS2; CS3/SS5). Thus the kinetics are good, but phase selectivity is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally simple: a binary Ag2S sheet with one Ag and one S site, linear Ag coordination, and four-coordinate S according to the target description. This avoids the three listed amorphization triggers: there is no multi-cation radius mismatch, no evidence for a large >40-atom cell, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network. In contrast, several analogues with more complicated connectivity or multiple cations report phase control issues (CS1, CS4, SS3/SS4), but those complications are not present in the target. The remaining uncertainty is that solution routes can initially give amorphous Ag–S if crystallization conditions are insufficient, as seen for the supercritical analogue under subcritical conditions (SS1/TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **ground-state Ag2S phase** thermodynamically because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of Ag–S and Ag-containing chalcogenides by sealed-tube, melt, and solvothermal routes, with the strongest structural evidence coming from KAg3S2, which has similar two-coordinate Ag–S bonding (SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary Ag–S chemistry and high thermodynamic stability, which should allow formation under mild to moderate conditions. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: Ag2S can plausibly form readily, but obtaining the tetragonal P4/nmm 2D sheet rather than another Ag2S polymorph may require careful annealing, cooling, or nanocrystalline/film stabilization [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is highly synthesizable, with Route 2 preferred for low temperature and high precursor mixing.
