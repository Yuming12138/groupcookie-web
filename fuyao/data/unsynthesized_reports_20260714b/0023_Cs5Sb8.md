### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Cs5Sb8 has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly in the rubric’s ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score bin [Target]. CS and SS analogues are mostly ground-state or near-ground-state alkali antimonides, strengthening confidence that the Cs–Sb chemical space is synthetically accessible [CS1, CS2, SS1–SS4]. TS analogues show that materials with ~0.05–0.075 eV/atom hull energies can be synthesized, though sometimes requiring special conditions [TS1, TS3]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Cs5Sb8 is an equilibrium low-temperature phase or a kinetically accessible metastable phase [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Condition Severity**  
Route 1 is moderate because it uses standard sealed ampoule/Ta-crucible solid-state methods at ≤650 °C, but Cs volatility and air sensitivity require sealed inert handling [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4]. Route 2 has similar severity but adds flux/melt handling and possible flux separation, supported by slow-cool RbSb/RbSb2 analogues [SS2, CS2, SS4]. Route 4 is practically challenging due to multi-anvil equipment, although the rubric does not penalize 4 GPa because the high-pressure cutoff is >5 GPa [TS1].

**F3 — Reaction Kinetics**  
Route 1 has favorable kinetics because Cs/Sb and Rb/Sb analogues form directly from elements, but long anneals suggest ordering is not instantaneous [CS1, SS1, SS2]. Route 2 is kinetically best because liquid alkali or flux-assisted transport should accelerate diffusion and crystal growth, while slow cooling supports phase selection [SS2, CS2, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Route 4 has good high-temperature diffusion but poor structural-transfer confidence because TS1 is not a MgB4-like antimonide and reports impurity issues [TS1].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs5Sb8 is best viewed as a borderline metastable but plausible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than exactly on-hull [Target]. The strongest support comes from CS/SS analogues: CsSb and RbSb are MgB4-like or closely related alkali antimonides synthesized directly from elements, and RbSb2 shows that Sb-rich alkali antimonides can be isolated with slow cooling [CS1, SS1, SS2, CS2, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is high chemical/structural transferability within alkali antimonides, especially matching Cs/Rb–Sb and Sb–Sb bond-length ranges [Target, CS1, SS2, CS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, because Cs5Sb8 sits between CsSb and more Sb-rich antimonide compositions [Target, CS1, CS2]. Overall, the material should be experimentally accessible with careful sealed Ta slow-cool synthesis, but phase-purity optimization is likely required [SS2, CS2, SS4; Reasonable Inference].
