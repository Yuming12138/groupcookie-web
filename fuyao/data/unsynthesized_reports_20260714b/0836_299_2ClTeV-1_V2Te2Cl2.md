### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase. This is strongly supported by chemically and thermodynamically related stable halide/chalcogenide phases such as RhTeCl [CS2], Nb₃TeI₇ [TS5], and VTe₂/V₃Te₆ [SS2, TS1]. Slightly metastable V₃Te₄ also synthesizes successfully at only ~0.0145 eV/atom [SS1], reinforcing that this energy scale is accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a wide finite-temperature phase field.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Direct sealed-tube synthesis uses standard ampoule equipment, like V₃Te₄ and VTe₂ syntheses [SS1–SS3], and avoids high pressure. The penalties arise from the need for vacuum/sealed conditions and volatile/toxic Te/Cl-bearing species, consistent with halide syntheses in sealed tubes [CS2, TS5]. Temperatures can likely remain below 1000 °C because Nb₃TeI₇ forms at 600–800 °C [TS5] and V–Te phases form around 1000–1173 K [SS1–SS3]. Uncertainty is the correct chlorine activity needed for VTeCl.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are only moderate because V metal is refractory and solid-state diffusion can be slow; long anneals are used for V₃Te₄ [SS1] and VTe₂-type phases [SS2–SS3]. The V–Te octahedral motif is favorable because structurally similar V–Te phases already contain VTe₆ octahedra [SS1–SS3]. However, ordering Cl into a VTeCl sheet requires new bonding relative to binary V–Te phases, so reconstruction is not trivial. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a directly demonstrated V–Te–Cl direct reaction, so this route remains less certain.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

CVT has more operational severity than direct annealing because RhTeCl synthesis uses chlorine chemistry, an ampoule, and a two-zone temperature gradient [CS2]. It does not require high pressure or >1000 °C conditions, so the thermal/mechanical severity is moderate [CS2]. The main deductions are chlorine toxicity/corrosivity and volatile transport species [CS2]. Insufficient evidence from SS for chlorine CVT of VTeCl specifically; SS mainly supports V–Te structural chemistry, not Cl transport.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because CVT transports components through volatile chloride species, and RhTeCl forms platelet crystals under a 900 → 700 °C gradient [CS2]. RhTeCl is a close MTeCl layered analogue, making the crystallization pathway more directly relevant than binary V–Te solid-state reactions [CS2]. The limiting kinetic/compositional issue is that RhTeCl synthesis produced a Te₄(AlCl₄)₂ byproduct [CS2], implying that Te/Cl activities can drift. V-specific redox behavior is an additional [Reasonable Inference] uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

The halide-flux route uses evacuated silica tubes and temperature gradients similar to alkali V selenide ion-exchange syntheses [SS5, TS3, TS4]. It avoids high pressure and can likely operate below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference from halide flux/mineralizer chemistry and CS2]. However, it is multi-step and involves corrosive chloride fluxes/mineralizers, so severity remains significant. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence exists for VTeCl formation in a chloride flux, so it is a secondary route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Molten halides can improve transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, as ion-exchange reactions in evacuated tubes proceed over one week [SS5, TS3, TS4]. The method should help overcome refractory V diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. Structural ordering remains uncertain because flux chemistry could favor alkali-intercalated V chalcogenides, as in K/Rb V selenide analogues [SS5, TS3, TS4], rather than neutral VTeCl. Thus F3 is good but not as directly supported as Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation size mismatch is not a concern. The structure explicitly contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing VTe₄Cl₂ octahedra, which triggers one amorphization/defect-connectivity risk; similar mixed connectivity appears in V₃Te₄ [SS1]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large target unit cell, and the formula/space group suggest it is probably not very large. Overall amorphization tendency is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VTeCl is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong but not perfect: V–Te layered/octethedral frameworks are well supported by SS data [SS1–SS3], and MTeCl layered chemistry is directly supported by RhTeCl CVT [CS2], but no provided source demonstrates V–Te–Cl specifically. The most critical enabling factor is controlled halogen chemical potential in a sealed ampoule or CVT gradient, as shown for RhTeCl and Nb–Te–I halides [CS2, TS5]. The main limiting factor is competing phase formation among V–Te binaries, vanadium chlorides, and Te/chloride-rich byproducts, as suggested by Te impurities in VTe₂ syntheses [SS2–SS3] and byproducts in RhTeCl CVT [CS2]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with CVT or chloride-assisted sealed-tube growth preferred over a purely diffusion-limited direct reaction.
