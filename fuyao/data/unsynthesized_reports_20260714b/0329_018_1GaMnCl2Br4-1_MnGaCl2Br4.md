### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0120 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bracket. TS provides strong supporting evidence because the closest thermodynamic analogues TS1–TS4 have E_hull ≈0.011–0.024 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized. SS is also supportive because several structurally similar halides are low-hull and synthesized, especially SS2/SS3/SS5, though SS4 is higher hull. CS is mixed: CS2 and CS3 are low hull, but CS1 and CS5 are much less stable, so CS is not decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, volatile halides, multistep loading/growth, and air-sensitive/toxic precursors, all consistent with SS1–SS3 and TS1–TS2/TS5. The temperature is mild, so no high-temperature or high-pressure deductions apply. Remaining uncertainty is whether the halogen pressure needed to maintain Mn(III) can be safely and reproducibly tuned; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Mn/Ga mixed-halide vapor transport.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics should be favorable because SS/TS analogues crystallize halides at 150–360 °C through vapor/transport mechanisms rather than refractory diffusion. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic term because many analogues are 0D clusters, while the target is a 2D edge-sharing sheet; TS5 and SS4 provide partial 2D halide support. CS gives insufficient evidence for a 2D Mn/Ga halide sheet, so Route 1 remains moderate rather than easy.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids sealed pressure operation and high temperatures, which improves F2 relative to ampoule routes. It is still penalized for dry inert handling, multistep crystallization, and air-sensitive/toxic halide/oxidant precursors, consistent with CS3 and SS5/TS3 moisture-exclusion solution chemistry. CS1 reports room-temperature CH₂Cl₂ chemistry without a stated special atmosphere, but because the target uses Ga halides and likely Mn(III), dry handling is still a reasonable requirement.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution-phase kinetics are strong because CS1 and CS3 demonstrate room-temperature halide/chalcogenide crystallization and diffusion growth. Ion transport is essentially not bulk-diffusion-limited, so the main uncertainty is structural assembly. The risk is that solution chemistry may favor solvated Mn/Ga halide complexes or molecular salts instead of the extended AlCl₃-derived 2D network; insufficient direct SS/TS evidence for solution synthesis of this exact layered motif.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures but requires an evacuated sealed ampoule, volatile Ga halides/halogens, multistep melt-quench-anneal processing, and air-sensitive precursors, as in TS4 and related ampoule halide routes. It therefore scores similarly to Route 1 in condition severity. CS4 also indicates sealed-tube halide/chalcogenide reactions can be long and reproducibility-limited, reinforcing the challenging rating.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary halide melts should provide better kinetics than oxide solid-state routes, and TS4 shows single-phase halide materials can be obtained by melt/quench/anneal. However, cation ordering and retention of Mn(III) during melting are significant kinetic/chemical risks. CS4’s poor reproducibility in a sealed low-dimensional halide reaction lowers confidence in this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple formula unit and a single edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk based on the given criteria. The cation radius criterion is not triggered under the most plausible Mn(III)/Ga(III) assignment; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from charge balance, because CS/SS/TS do not directly establish the target oxidation states. Remaining uncertainty is mixed Br/Cl occupational ordering, which could create disorder, but it is not one of the specified F4 triggers.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnGa(Br₂Cl)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0120 eV/atom, and several TS analogues with similar hull energies have reported syntheses. The strongest support comes from SS/TS halide ampoule and CVT chemistry, where volatile, moisture-sensitive low-dimensional bromide/chloride phases are commonly crystallized at mild temperatures. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of anhydrous metal halides, especially GaCl₃/GaBr₃, which can support solution, vapor, or molten-halide pathways. The most critical limiting factor is controlling Mn oxidation state and Br:Cl stoichiometry without forming MnX₂, GaX₃-rich molecular phases, or halide-deficient byproducts. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but requires careful dry, halogen-rich conditions and slow crystallization.
