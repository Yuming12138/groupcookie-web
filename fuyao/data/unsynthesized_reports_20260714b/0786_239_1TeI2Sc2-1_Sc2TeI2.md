### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_above_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which indicates predicted ground-state stability by the provided target data. Multiple close analogues are also calculated or reported at E_hull = 0, including La₂TeI₂ (TS-3), La₂PI₂ (CS-2), La₂PBr₂ (CS-4), and several rare-earth iodides (TS-1 to TS-4), supporting thermodynamic plausibility. Insufficient evidence from SS alone for the exact ternary Sc₂TeI₂ hull stability, although SS-5 shows Sc–Te bonding is chemically accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits the sealed Ta ampule, Ar glovebox, evacuated silica jacket, and 900 °C heating from La₂TeI₂ (TS-3). CS-2 and CS-4 independently show that layered M₂ZX₂ halide analogues require high-temperature sealed Ta/silica setups under Ar, not open-air ceramic processing. The main penalties are not extreme temperature or pressure, but air-sensitive iodides, volatile halogen/chalcogen components, and multi-step ampoule handling. Insufficient evidence from SS for this exact route, except that SS-5 confirms Sc–Te phases require controlled high-temperature handling.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is that Route 1 uses a stoichiometric halide/metal/chalcogen mixture exactly analogous to La₂TeI₂ synthesis (TS-3), and the target’s P-3m1 sheet topology is essentially matched by La₂PI₂ (CS-2). Kinetic difficulty remains because Sc metal is dense and relatively high-melting [Reasonable Inference], and CS-2/CS-4 report side phases, polytypism, and modest yields in related layered halides. Overall, halide-mediated transport in a sealed Ta ampule should partially offset diffusion limitations, but purity may require extended annealing.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized more heavily because SS-5 uses high-temperature arc melting and long annealing for ScTe, followed by an additional iodination anneal [Reasonable Inference]. The controlled-atmosphere and sealed-container requirements are supported by SS-5 for ScTe and TS-3 for La₂TeI₂. No high pressure is required, but the multi-step nature, volatile iodine/tellurium, and air-sensitive ScI₃ make the route challenging. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for pre-reacted Sc–Te precursor use.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

SS-5 shows that forming crystalline ScTe can require 1573 K and 200 h, implying slow solid-state diffusion and ordering in Sc–Te binaries. Once ScTe/Sc₂Te₃ is formed, conversion to Sc₂TeI₂ requires iodine insertion and structural rearrangement, so the structural reconstruction penalty is significant [Reasonable Inference]. This route may reduce Te loss but risks leaving persistent ScTe impurities. TS-3 supports the final ternary motif but does not show that binary ScTe is an efficient precursor.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C operation by using flux/CVT-like transport conditions inspired by ErSeI/ErSeBr at 800–850 °C (CS-5) and Sm iodide oxide growth near 903 K (SS-4). However, it still requires sealed ampoules, vacuum or inert gas, volatile halide transport, and air-sensitive precursors, so its F2 is similar to Route 1. There is insufficient direct TS evidence for CVT growth of La₂TeI₂-type phases; TS-3 only supports sealed solid-state reaction.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux/CVT should improve mass transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, supported by CS-5 where rare-earth chalcogenide halides produce crystals by transport. SS-4 also shows iodide-rich sealed-ampoule growth with controlled cooling, supporting the general concept of halide-assisted crystallization. The uncertainty is compositional: Sc, Te, and I vapor activities may not align in the narrow window needed for Sc₂TeI₂ [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are promising but less proven than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered P-3m1 structure with one cation species and no reported large unit cell, so it does not trigger cation-size disorder or large-cell amorphization penalties. The structure includes corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, but not the vertex + edge + face-sharing coexistence specified by C3. Similar layered compounds such as La₂PI₂ and La₂TeI₂ are crystalline under sealed solid-state conditions (CS-2, TS-3), supporting a low amorphization tendency. Remaining uncertainty is mostly stacking disorder/polytypism, which CS-2/CS-4 report for related layered systems, but that is not the same as amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂TeI₂ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close analogues such as La₂TeI₂, La₂PI₂, and La₂PBr₂ are also stable or experimentally accessible (TS-3, CS-2, CS-4). The best-supported route is the sealed-Ta solid-state reaction because TS-3 gives an almost formula-identical La₂TeI₂ recipe, while CS-2 gives an exact P-3m1 structural analogue. The critical enabling factor is halide-assisted solid-state transport in sealed Ta ampules, which permits volatile iodine retention and formation of layered M₂ZX₂ sheets (TS-3, CS-2/CS-4). The critical limiting factor is phase competition with Sc–Te binaries and possible Sc iodides, supported by the robust ScTe chemistry in SS-5 and impurity/polytype issues in layered halide analogues (CS-2/CS-4, TS-3). Overall, synthesis is feasible but will require rigorous inert handling and stoichiometry control rather than exotic pressure or thin-film methods.
