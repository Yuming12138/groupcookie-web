### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.068 eV/atom places SrNiS₂ in the moderately metastable range, so the score is below that of a ground-state phase but still favorable. TS analogues show that sulfides with comparable or higher E_hull can be synthesized, including Ba₆Fe₈S₁₅ at 0.1068 eV/atom and BaCoS₂ at 0.1446 eV/atom (TS1; TS3). The strongest positive signal is the closest structural analogue BaNiS₂, which has low E_hull and an established sealed-tube synthesis (SS1/TS2). Remaining uncertainty is whether replacing Ba with smaller Sr destabilizes the P4/nmm framework enough to favor competing Sr–Ni–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but it still requires vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur control, as in BaNiS₂ synthesis (SS1/TS2). Kinetically, Ni metal diffusion is the main limitation, but the direct BaNiS₂ analogue forms at 820 °C over several days, indicating the route is feasible if long annealing is allowed (SS1/TS2). The structural reconstruction score is high because the target and BaNiS₂ share the same P4/nmm topology and similar Ni–S coordination (SS1/TS2; Target). Uncertainty remains in Sr-specific phase competition, for which CS gives insufficient direct evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it adds quench control and likely precursor optimization, following BaCoS₂ synthesis where quenching was needed for phase retention (SS2/TS3). The kinetic score remains moderate-good because using NiS can improve local sulfide chemistry, by analogy to CoS use in BaCoS₂ (SS2/TS3). The limiting uncertainty is whether SrNiS₂ actually requires quenching; the target’s E_hull suggests possible metastability, but the exact decomposition pathway is not supplied by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Flux growth has favorable kinetics because CS3 demonstrates that K₂S₃ flux can dissolve and transport Ni–S species at 750 °C, below typical diffusion-limited solid-state temperatures. However, F2 is reduced by air-sensitive flux precursors, volatile sulfur, sealed-tube handling, and flux-removal steps (CS3). Transferability is only moderate because CS3 concerns K₂Ni₃S₄, not the P4/nmm SrNiS₂ structure, and the same source reports difficulty preparing single-phase powder. The main uncertainty is alkali incorporation or formation of K–Ni–S phases instead of SrNiS₂ [Reasonable Inference from CS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis receives a low F2 score because TS5 used 5 GPa/1200 °C conditions and specialized multi-anvil equipment, while CS1 also involved high-pressure sulfide synthesis. Kinetics may improve at 1000–1200 °C, but high pressure could stabilize different coordination motifs rather than the target layered P4/nmm structure [Reasonable Inference; TS5]. This route is best viewed as a contingency for phase stabilization if ambient-pressure synthesis fails. SS gives insufficient evidence that pressure is necessary for this exact structure because isostructural BaNiS₂ forms at ambient pressure (SS1/TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger because Sr and Ni have very different cation sizes [Reasonable Inference], but the structure is small and ordered, and the target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing without face-sharing coexistence (Target). The closest analogue BaNiS₂ forms crystalline plate-like material, supporting a low-to-medium amorphization risk (SS1/TS2). CS provides insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior in SrNiS₂ and is treated as neutral for this factor. The remaining uncertainty is whether Sr/Ni ordering kinetics are slow enough to produce nanocrystalline or mixed phases under insufficient annealing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrNiS₂ is most likely a moderately metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.068 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS evidence is generally favorable, especially because BaNiS₂ is an isostructural P4/nmm analogue made by a simple sealed-tube solid-state route (SS1/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining the correct sulfur chemical potential in a sealed ampoule so that Ni–S layers form without sulfur loss, as supported by sulfur-containing sealed-tube routes for BaNiS₂ and BaCoS₂ (SS1/TS2; SS2/TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition/metastability, since related sulfides sometimes require quenching, repeated heating, or high pressure for phase control (TS1; TS3; TS5). Overall, synthesis should be feasible in a competent solid-state sulfide laboratory, with sealed-tube SrS/Ni/S synthesis as the first route to attempt.
