### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the highly accessible boundary of the scoring rubric [Target]. TS-1 shows a thermodynamically similar oxide, AgBiO3, with E_hull ≈ 0.0499 eV/atom prepared by mild ion exchange, supporting experimental accessibility near this hull energy [TS-1]. However, TS-2 through TS-4 show that thermodynamic similarity can correspond to hydrothermal or high-pressure synthesis depending structure and chemistry, so the E_hull score is high but not decisive [TS-2; TS-3; TS-4]. Remaining uncertainty is mainly the high formal Ni(V) oxidation state required by KNiTeO6 [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solid-State Sintering

The solid-state route is strongly supported structurally by SrGeTeO6, which has the same P312 space group and a TeO6 octahedral framework [CS-3; Target]. Its condition severity is reduced by multi-step heating and the likely need for flowing O2, because CS-3 required initial 600 °C oxidation and successive higher-temperature treatments, and the target likely requires Ni(V) [CS-3; Target; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate because ceramic reactions among K/Ni/Te oxides require cation diffusion, but the existence of a directly related P312 tellurate improves confidence [CS-3]. The main uncertainty is whether the target decomposes to K–Te–O plus Ni oxide before Ni(V) is incorporated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal Oxidative Crystallization

The hydrothermal route has mild thermal severity because K–Cu–Te oxides were crystallized from KOH, CuO, and Te(OH)6 at 230 °C in Teflon-lined autoclaves [CS-4; CS-5; SS-4; SS-5]. Kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated transport avoids slow solid-state diffusion, as also indicated by hydrothermal analogues around 220–230 °C [CS-4; CS-5; TS-5]. The structural uncertainty is larger than for solid-state synthesis because no CS/SS/TS example directly shows the P312 KNiTeO6 framework forming hydrothermally [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The route is nevertheless preferred because high K activity and oxidizing solution chemistry can be tuned more easily than in a ceramic reaction [CS-4; CS-5; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Oxidizing Flux

The flux route is inferred rather than directly demonstrated, combining KOH/K-rich tellurate chemistry from hydrothermal K2CuTeO7 [CS-4; CS-5] with the solid-state P312 tellurate precedent from SrGeTeO6 [CS-3]. Condition severity is moderate because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures but likely requires oxidizing atmosphere and hazardous molten salts [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics should be good due to molten-salt transport, but phase selectivity is uncertain because no direct flux analogue is present in CS/SS/TS [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for flux synthesis]. This route is a useful backup if hydrothermal products are hydrated, poorly crystalline, or compositionally off-target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch, evident from K–O = 2.76 Å versus Ni–O = 1.88 Å and Te–O = 1.96 Å [Target]. The unit cell is not indicated to exceed 40 atoms, and the connectivity described is primarily edge-sharing NiO6–TeO6 without reported coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing [Target]. Therefore only one amorphization trigger is applied, giving a low–medium risk [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is whether rapid precipitation in hydrothermal synthesis could produce amorphous or poorly crystalline K–Ni–Te oxides, as precipitation analogues sometimes required careful conditions to obtain good crystals [SS-1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KNiTeO6 is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The best support comes from CS-3, where a very close P312 tellurate framework, SrGeTeO6, is a ground-state phase made by solid-state sintering [CS-3], and from CS-4/CS-5, where K–Te–O crystals form hydrothermally from KOH and Te(OH)6 [CS-4; CS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Te(VI)O6 octahedral chemistry, which appears repeatedly in the structural and chemical analogues [CS-3; CS-4; CS-5; SS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is the formal Ni(V) oxidation state required by charge balance, which is not directly validated by any CS/SS/TS example [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful oxidizing chemistry and phase-screening rather than a simple stoichiometric ceramic firing [CS-3; CS-4; CS-5; Reasonable Inference].
