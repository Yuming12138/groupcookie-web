### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability threshold, giving F1 = 90. CS analogues are stable or low-hull, including GdZnTl at 0.0 eV/atom and La/NdZnTl at ~0.024–0.028 eV/atom, supporting a stable RE–Tl–metal phase field (CS1–CS3). TS analogues DyCuPb, HoCuPb, and ErCuPb are all synthesized at ~0.055–0.061 eV/atom, indicating that phases slightly above the hull are experimentally accessible in this chemistry (TS1/TS4/TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that no source directly reports DyCuTl itself, so possible Dy–Cu, Dy–Tl, or Cu–Tl competing binaries are not resolved by the provided data.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because closest analogues require induction melting in Ta vessels under Ar and 1 week annealing at 500 °C (CS1–CS3; TS1/TS4/TS5). The main deductions arise from likely >1000 °C melting for Cu-containing alloys [Reasonable Inference], sealed containment for Tl based on Ta-vessel use in Tl analogues (CS1–CS3), and toxic/air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS suggests no additional severity beyond what CS/TS already show. This route avoids high pressure, reactive gas, and complex multistep processing.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable after melting because CS and TS analogues use induction melting to form well-melted ingots, then anneal for homogeneity (CS1–CS3; TS1/TS4/TS5). Precursor activity is only moderate-low because dense elemental metals are used, not reactive salts or sol-gel precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is not severe because target is close to omega-derived RE–Tl–metal and RE–Cu–p-block analogues (CS1–CS3; TS1), but the 1 week anneal indicates ordering is not instantaneous. Insufficient evidence from SS for Tl-specific kinetics, but SS supports RE–Cu intermetallic formation in Ta/induction routes (SS1–SS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has greater severity because the solid-state RECuBi analogue used 1000–1100 °C, grinding, and a second heat treatment under Ar (CS4). For DyCuTl, Tl volatility makes open high-temperature sintering risky, so sealed Ta containment is a required adaptation [Reasonable Inference; CS1–CS3]. The multistep heat–grind–temper procedure incurs a processing penalty based on CS4. Insufficient evidence from TS that such high temperatures are necessary for DyCuTl, since TS1/TS4/TS5 succeed with melt plus 500 °C anneal.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable than melt growth because elemental metal powders/turnings must interdiffuse, as in CS4. High temperature partially offsets diffusion limitations, and molten Tl could provide local liquid transport by [Reasonable Inference]. The structural target is related to omega-derived RE–Cu–p-block phases in CS4, but intermediate grinding and tempering imply nontrivial phase formation. Remaining uncertainty is whether Tl loss or Tl-rich liquid formation would improve or degrade phase purity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses arc/induction melting plus extended annealing and quenching, so it is more operationally complex than Route 1 (TS3; SS4–SS5). SS4–SS5 used induction melting at 1373 K and annealing to select polymorphs, supporting the need for temperature control in RE–Cu–Cd analogues. TS3 used very long annealing and quenching, which increases practical burden. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS that DyCuTl requires quenching; this is a fallback adaptation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Initial melting helps homogenize Dy/Cu/Tl, consistent with TS3 and SS4–SS5. However, TS3’s 1500 h anneal and SS4–SS5’s polymorph sensitivity show that some RE–Cu–p-block systems can have slow ordering or competing structures. The target’s simpler P-3m1 cell likely reduces this burden relative to complex Cd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains in whether low-temperature annealing improves DyCuTl ordering or allows decomposition.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is severe because TS2 used 7.7 GPa and heating above the melting temperature followed by rapid quench. This triggers a high-pressure penalty and makes equipment requirements much higher than Routes 1–3. Tl containment under high pressure adds an additional practical risk by [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates high pressure is required for DyCuTl, since closer RE–Tl and RE–Cu–Pb analogues form at ambient pressure (CS1–CS3; TS1/TS4/TS5).

#### F3 — Route 4

High-pressure melting gives good diffusion and homogenization, as in TS2. However, structural reconstruction is uncertain because TS2 concerns a binary Laves YbCu₂ phase, not a ternary DyCuTl P-3m1 phase. The route may help if DyCuTl is metastable, but target E_hull is only 0.0500 eV/atom, so pressure stabilization is probably unnecessary [Reasonable Inference; TS1/TS4/TS5]. The lack of direct Tl high-pressure analogues is a significant uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target likely has one size-mismatch trigger because Dy is much larger than Cu, and the target bond distances show a large contrast between Dy–Cu and Cu–Cu separations [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The unit cell is likely small because the target P-3m1 description contains one crystallographic site each for Dy, Cu, and Tl; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40 atom cell. The target reports edge-sharing TlDy₃Tl₃ octahedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

DyCuTl is likely a mildly metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and synthesized thermodynamic analogues DyCuPb, HoCuPb, and ErCuPb lie near 0.055–0.061 eV/atom (TS1/TS4/TS5). The strongest support comes from the overlap between CS Tl-containing REZnTl phases made by Ta-vessel induction melting/500 °C anneal (CS1–CS3) and TS RECuPb phases made by essentially the same route (TS1/TS4/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is liquid-phase homogenization in sealed Ta under Ar, which addresses slow metal diffusion and preserves Tl stoichiometry (CS1–CS3; TS1). The most critical limiting factor is Tl volatility/toxicity and possible loss during melting or high-temperature sintering [Reasonable Inference], which could shift the composition into competing Dy–Cu or Dy–Tl phases. Overall, the target is quite feasible for an intermetallic if handled with sealed inert-metal containment and careful stoichiometry control.
