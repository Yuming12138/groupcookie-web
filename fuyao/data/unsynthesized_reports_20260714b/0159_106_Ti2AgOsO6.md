### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring bin specified in the prompt. CS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable, with CS-1/CS-2/CS-4 at 0 eV/atom and CS-3/CS-5 near 0.007–0.008 eV/atom. SS analogues also include several 0 eV/atom Os/Re oxides, e.g. SS-2, SS-3, and SS-4, while TS analogues around 0.057–0.064 eV/atom show that related metastable oxides can be synthesized but may require careful conditions. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific Ti–Os–Ag redox combination has competing binary/ternary phases not represented by the analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from strong precedent for solid-state oxide synthesis in CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5, TS-1, and TS-3, but Os-containing analogues in SS-3/SS-4 and Re analogues in TS-3 indicate sealed or controlled environments are often required. The condition severity is therefore penalized for vacuum/sealing, Os volatility, repeated firing, and toxic Os handling. Kinetically, dense TiO₂/OsO₂-type precursors are expected to react slowly [Reasonable Inference], consistent with repeated regrinding/refiring in CS-5 and TS-3. The largest uncertainty is whether the target P1 phase can nucleate before decomposition into Ag, TiO₂, OsO₂, or ternary oxide impurities.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by CS-3 for flux growth of complex oxides and by SS-4 for KCl/KClO₄-assisted Os oxide crystal formation. Its lower synthesis temperature improves severity relative to dry solid-state routes, but vacuum sealing, oxidizer control, and Os volatility remain serious penalties. Flux improves kinetics by enhancing cation mobility, but SS-4 explicitly reports crystals isolated among other compounds, so phase selectivity is uncertain. This route is experimentally attractive for crystal discovery but may be less ideal for bulk single-phase powder.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is justified by TS-2 and TS-4, where thermodynamically similar Re/Os oxides require high pressure to form. Its score is lowered sharply by the >5 GPa pressure requirement and by capsule handling of volatile/toxic Os species. Kinetics may be acceptable over short dwell times as in TS-2/TS-4, but precursor homogenization is still critical. The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the target Ti₂OsAgO₆ topology or instead drives denser perovskite/pyrochlore-like competing phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so it is explicitly a [Reasonable Inference]. It scores best because molecular precursor mixing can address slow diffusion and repeated regrinding problems indicated by CS-5 and TS-3, while final sealed annealing borrows Os-retention logic from SS-3/SS-4. Its main weakness is that amorphous-to-crystalline conversion into a triclinic P1 ordered phase may be difficult, and Os soluble precursors introduce safety and redox hazards. The route is best viewed as a low-temperature discovery route followed by optimization.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different preferred coordination/bonding environments, with short Ti–O/Os–O bonds and much longer Ag–O contacts in the target description; this triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check by [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide enough evidence for a >40 atom unit cell, and neither CS/SS/TS gives direct evidence for the target’s full cell size. The target has mixed coordination geometries, but explicit coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing is not stated, so C3 is not triggered. Thus, amorphization/crystallization risk is moderate but not prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂OsAgO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS and SS analogues support the general feasibility of synthesizing complex Ag oxides and Os/Re oxides, while TS analogues show that related metastable oxides can be made under sealed, controlled, or high-pressure conditions. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high cation homogeneity while controlling oxygen fugacity, which favors a sol–gel precursor plus sealed anneal or a chloride flux route. The most critical limiting factor is Os volatility/redox chemistry, especially the risk of forming volatile OsO₄ under overly oxidizing conditions. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful optimization rather than a simple one-shot ceramic firing.
