### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring range and suggests it is at least metastably accessible [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar or slightly higher hull energies, including Ba₂PdO₂F₂ at 0.0518 eV/atom and Ba₃WO₆ at 0.0675 eV/atom, are reported as synthesizable/known [SS3, TS1, TS5]. Chemically similar Ba₂InO₃Br and Ba₂InO₃Cl are on the hull, supporting Ba oxyhalide stability [CS1, CS2], but insufficient evidence from CS directly proves Ba–Pd–O–Br stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Conventional ceramic / solid-state sintering

The severity score is reduced because Ba oxyhalide sintering commonly requires 1025–1075 °C with long holds/regrinding [CS1, CS2], and BaBr₂ volatility is a concern in high-temperature Ba–Br synthesis [CS4]. The kinetic score is modest because Pd metal and carbonate/oxide powders react slowly, and Ba₂PdO₃ required careful optimization and showed metastability/decomposition issues [SS1]. Thermodynamic analogues show repeated grinding/heating can produce Ba oxides [TS4, TS5], but insufficient evidence from TS specifically addresses bromide retention in Ba–Pd–Br–O.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: BaBr₂ self-flux / bromide flux growth

The flux route has moderate condition severity: BaBr₂/BaCl₂ flux syntheses succeed near 890–1050 °C without high pressure [CS3, CS4, SS4], but the multi-step thermal profile and bromide volatility lower the score [CS4]. Kinetically, the molten halide flux should improve diffusion, supported by successful Ba–Ru–Br–O and Ba–Ca–Pd–O crystal growth in halide fluxes [CS4, SS4]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity, because Ba–Ru–halide systems show multiple possible products depending on conditions [CS3, CS4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Topochemical bromination / low-temperature anion insertion

This route earns the best severity score because the decisive anion-insertion step can plausibly be done far below 1000 °C, analogous to Ba₂PdO₂F₂ fluorination at 200–350 °C [SS3, TS1]. Kinetically, a preformed Ba₂PdO₃-like precursor preserves the square-planar Pd–O motif found in both the precursor and target [SS1, Target], lowering structural reconstruction relative to full ceramic synthesis. The main uncertainty is that bromination is extrapolated from fluorination; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates low-temperature Br insertion into Ba–Pd–O frameworks.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size contrast between high-coordinate Ba and square-planar Pd, consistent with Ba–Pd–O analogues containing large Ba polyhedra and PdO₄ units [SS1, SS3]. It also contains mixed corner- and face-sharing polyhedra, and similar mixed connectivity appears in Ba₂PdO₃ and Ba₂PdO₂F₂ [Target, SS1, SS3]. The exact unit cell atom count is not established by CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₄Pd₃O₆Br is best viewed as a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from structurally similar Ba₂PdO₃ and Ba₂PdO₂F₂, which share square-planar Pd–O geometry and OBa₄Pd₂-type connectivity [SS1, SS3], and from chemically similar Ba oxyhalides that can be synthesized by high-temperature routes [CS1, CS2]. The most critical enabling factor is preservation or formation of square-planar PdO₄ units, which are repeatedly observed in Ba/Sr–Pd–O and oxyfluoride analogues [SS1, SS2, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is Br retention and stoichiometry control, because BaBr₂ volatility/hygroscopicity and flux loss are explicitly reported in Ba–Br syntheses [CS1, CS4]. Overall, the material appears moderately synthesizable, with the best chance coming from a preformed Ba–Pd–O precursor followed by controlled low-temperature bromination.
