### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0320 eV/atom, placing it within the high synthesizability range for metastable/near-ground-state oxides. Similar thermodynamic references also lie in the same low-energy window: TS#1 has E_hull = 0.0423 eV/atom, TS#2 has 0.0254 eV/atom, and TS#4 has 0.0328 eV/atom. CS#1 is on the hull and SS#2/SS#3 are near the hull, supporting that layered tellurite molybdate/tungstate-like oxides can be experimentally accessible. The remaining uncertainty is whether WZnTeO6 is a true ground-state polymorph or a low-energy metastable variant.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state

The condition severity is moderate because SS#2 used evacuated silica tubes and repeated grinding/pelletizing, and the sealed tube was specifically needed to prevent volatilization. The route is strongly structurally justified because SS#2 is essentially the Mo analogue of the target, but kinetics are limited by oxide diffusion and the need to substitute less labile WO₃ for MoO₃ [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether WZnTeO6 forms directly at the same 823 K/550 °C range or requires slightly higher temperatures that could increase TeO₂ volatility.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal

The hydrothermal route has the mildest conditions because CS#2/CS#3 and SS#5 show tellurium molybdate/tungstate oxides forming at only 220–230 °C in Teflon-lined autoclaves without special gas atmospheres. Kinetically, solution transport should overcome the slow solid-state diffusion of ZnO/WO₃ [Reasonable Inference], and CS#2/CS#3 explicitly include WO₃ in related hydrothermal chemistry. However, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that the exact P2₁2₁2 WZnTeO6 sheet topology crystallizes hydrothermally, so this source-specific gap is treated as the main uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux / solution growth

Flux growth is supported by SS#3, where MgTeMoO6 was grown from TeO₂–MoO₃ self-flux, and by TS#2, where MnTeMoO6 was grown by top-seeded solution growth. Kinetically, a molten flux gives high cation mobility and avoids solid-state diffusion limits, but the route requires precise cooling and has greater operational complexity. TS#2 also reports long growth times and polymorph sensitivity for MnTeMoO6, so phase selection for WZnTeO6 may be challenging.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Open ceramic

Open ceramic synthesis is supported by CS#1/SS#1, where CdTeMoO6 was prepared at 600 °C for 20 h with slow cooling. It is less severe than sealed-tube synthesis because it avoids vacuum ampoules, but SS#2 indicates that the ZnTeMoO6 analogue required sealing to prevent reagent loss, so TeO₂/W oxide volatility is a concern. Kinetics are only moderate because oxide ceramic reactions at ~600 °C may be slow and incomplete for W-based compositions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines W(VI) tetrahedra, ZnO₆ octahedra, and Te(IV) see-saw units, implying significant cation size/coordination diversity [Reasonable Inference from target description], so one amorphization risk check is triggered. However, the structure is relatively simple in formula size and uses corner-sharing connectivity rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, reducing glass/amorphous trapping risk. Structurally similar layered phases are experimentally crystalline by ceramic, solid-state, hydrothermal, and flux routes (CS#1; SS#2; SS#3; SS#5), which further supports a moderate-low amorphization tendency.

---

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

WZnTeO6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a highly metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.0320 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues occupy similar low-energy ranges (TS#1; TS#2; TS#4). The strongest support comes from SS#2, which is essentially the Mo analogue ZnTeMoO6 with the same P2₁2₁2 layered topology, and from CS#2/CS#3 showing WO₃ participates in related hydrothermal tellurium oxides. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match to known ZnTeMoO6, making Mo→W substitution chemically plausible. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control involving TeO₂ volatility and the lower reactivity of WO₃ relative to MoO₃ [Reasonable Inference supported by SS#2 volatilization control]. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with hydrothermal screening recommended first and sealed-tube solid-state synthesis as the most structure-directed backup.
