### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, so it falls at the high end of the 90–100 score range. CS and SS show exact-composition Sr₂LaSbO₆ as a stable monoclinic P2₁/c phase with E_hull = 0, which strongly supports chemical synthesizability but also implies the trigonal R-3 target may be a metastable polymorph. TS gives close analogues around 0.050–0.052 eV/atom that are still associated with reported ceramic syntheses, supporting accessibility of slightly metastable double perovskites. The main uncertainty is not whether Sr₂LaSbO₆ can form, but whether the trigonal polymorph can be isolated instead of the monoclinic ground-state polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is strongly supported by CS/SS because it is the exact Sr₂LaSbO₆ chemistry, but it reaches 1770 K / 1497 °C and uses many repeated firing/grinding steps. TS supports similar air-fired carbonate/oxide processing for Ca₂LaSbO₆. The route uses only standard furnace/alumina crucible equipment according to CS/SS/TS, but antimony oxides introduce toxicity and possible high-temperature loss risk [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the route is chemically reliable but thermally severe.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS/SS exact Sr₂LaSbO₆ requires repeated grinding and multiple 24 h firings, indicating slow solid-state diffusion and cation ordering. The ordered LaO₆/SbO₆ corner-sharing network is structurally compatible with the target, so the reconstruction is not fundamentally prohibitive. TS Ca₂LaSbO₆ uses the same stepwise approach, further supporting kinetic manageability. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection between trigonal R-3 and monoclinic P2₁/c.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses lower maximum temperatures than Route 1, based on CS/SS Sr₂YSbO₆ at up to 1275 °C and TS Sr₂YSbO₆ around 1090–1100 °C. It remains a multi-step ceramic process with long dwell times and regrinding, supported by CS/SS/TS. No high pressure or controlled atmosphere is needed because CS/SS/TS use air. The main severity comes from antimony oxide handling and long thermal exposure.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

TS Sr₂YSbO₆ requires 135 h sintering, indicating that ordered Sr₂B′SbO₆ phases can have slow B-site ordering kinetics. CS/SS Sr₂YSbO₆ also requires repeated intermittent regrinding, which supports moderate diffusion limitations. Transfer to La is plausible because CS/SS exact Sr₂LaSbO₆ exists, but the larger La³⁺ radius may shift the tilt/polymorph stability [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are acceptable but not fast.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS, so the route-specific conditions are [Reasonable Inference]. It likely avoids the >1400 °C penalty because molecular mixing should allow crystallization near 1000–1200 °C, but it remains multi-step due to gelation, burnout, calcination, and final annealing. Air/O₂ processing is consistent with all CS/SS/TS evidence for oxide antimonates. The uncertainty is highest for Sb precursor chemistry and whether sol-gel processing preserves the desired stoichiometry.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Sol-gel processing should improve precursor activity and shorten diffusion lengths compared with CS/SS carbonate/oxide routes [Reasonable Inference]. This may help access a metastable trigonal polymorph by reducing the need for very high-temperature equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source confirms this route for Sr₂LaSbO₆, and crystallization from an amorphous gel requires full development of B-site ordered La/Sb octahedral networks. Thus, the kinetic score is high but adjusted downward for uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different sizes and charges, Sr²⁺/La³⁺/Sb⁵⁺, and the CS/SS bond lengths show large differences between Sr–O, La–O, and Sb–O distances. This triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check and gives some amorphization/ordering risk. However, the structure is a regular oxide double perovskite with corner-sharing LaO₆/SbO₆ octahedra, and CS/SS/TS repeatedly show crystalline analogues, so amorphization risk is not high. Unit-cell size >40 atoms is not established from CS/SS/TS and is scored as not triggered/insufficient evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LaSbO₆ as a composition is very likely synthesizable because CS and SS include exact Sr₂LaSbO₆ prepared by conventional solid-state reaction. The target trigonal R-3 polymorph is probably metastable rather than the ground state, because CS/SS list monoclinic P2₁/c Sr₂LaSbO₆ at E_hull = 0 while the target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom. The strongest enabling factor is the extensive analogue support: CS/SS/TS all show Sr/Ca/Ba double perovskite antimonates forming in air by standard ceramic routes. The main limiting factor is polymorph control, since long high-temperature annealing may equilibrate the sample to monoclinic Sr₂LaSbO₆ rather than retaining trigonal R-3. A kinetically assisted route such as sol-gel plus moderate-temperature annealing may offer the best balance, but it is less directly evidenced than the conventional ceramic route.
