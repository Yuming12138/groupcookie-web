### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0340 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin and therefore receives **94/100**. This is comparable to several synthesizable analogues with small positive hull energies: SmAsSe at 0.0239 eV/atom (CS3), PrAsSe at 0.0321 eV/atom (CS4), SrThBr₆ at 0.0451 eV/atom (TS1), and Ba₃InSe₄Cl at 0.0347 eV/atom (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee the specific Pmma polymorph is kinetically accessible; however, CS/SS/TS show multiple layered rare-earth chalcogenide/halide phases can be isolated in sealed tubes (SS2, SS4, SS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile/toxic Br₂, consistent with DySBr synthesis using Br₂ under vacuum in sealed quartz (SS5). The temperature is not extreme because analogous GdSI and DySBr form at 900 °C and 750 °C, respectively (SS4, SS5), and CS rare-earth chalcogenides form at 500–900 °C (CS2–CS4). Insufficient evidence from CS for elemental bromide handling specifically, so the Br₂ risk is weighted mainly from SS5 and general physicochemical reasoning.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by the use of elemental Y, a refractory dense metal, but halogen-assisted sealed ampoule chemistry improves reactivity, as shown by DySBr and GdSI syntheses (SS5, SS4). Complete structural assembly from elements is required, but the target sheet topology is very similar to SS2/SS4/SS5 layered LnQX phases. Remaining uncertainty is whether Te-rich competing phases such as Y₂Te₃ or YTe can consume Y/Te before bromide incorporation; SS3 shows rare-earth telluride formation is facile, which is helpful but also indicates possible competition.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has a lower condition score because it adds precursor synthesis/purification and anhydrous halide handling, similar to ErSeI work involving Er₂Se₃ and rare-earth trihalides with dehydration/sublimation/high vacuum (SS2). The temperature remains moderate, around 800–900 °C, based on SS2, SS4, SS5, and CS2–CS4. Insufficient evidence from TS for Y₂Te₃/YBr₃ specifically, so TS is used only to support sealed bromide handling and annealing, not the exact precursor choice.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetically, pre-formed Y₂Te₃ and YBr₃ should reduce the reconstruction burden compared with elements because Y–Te and Y–Br bonds already exist, [Reasonable Inference] based on rare-earth chalcogenide/halide precursor use in SS2 and SS5. However, it remains a bulk solid-state reaction and likely requires grinding/pelletizing/reheating, as reported for CS2–CS4. The main uncertainty is whether YBr₃ behaves as a reactive solid, transient melt, or vapor transport agent under these conditions; SS2 and TS1–TS3 suggest halide mobility but do not prove it for YBr₃/Y₂Te₃.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 requires controlled vacuum sealing, volatile transport species, and multiple steps, as in ErSeI and Er₂Te₃ CVT syntheses (SS2, SS3). It avoids very high temperature if run at 850–950 °C, supported by SS2 at 800–850 °C and SS3 at 950→800 °C. CS1/CS5 mention transport up to 1050 °C, so if optimization requires >1000 °C the severity would increase; the recommended initial window avoids that penalty.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT has the strongest kinetic rationale because it bypasses slow bulk diffusion through vapor-phase transport, and SS2/SS3 directly report single-crystal growth of structurally/chemically relevant rare-earth chalcogenide systems. The target’s layered YTeBr topology is closest to ErSeI/DySBr/GdSI analogues (SS2, SS4, SS5), improving the chance that transported species crystallize into the desired sheet structure. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS2 reports narrow product zones and competing products for related halide chalcogenides, so transport-agent loading and temperature gradient are critical.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization check is triggered: target octahedra exhibit mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing connectivity, explicitly stated for YTe₄Br₂ octahedra in the target and partially mirrored by mixed edge/corner connectivity in SS2/SS4/SS5. No mixed-cation radius mismatch applies because Y is the only cation, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom unit cell. The remaining uncertainty is that face-sharing connectivity can increase local strain relative to SS2/SS4/SS5 analogues, so the score is low–medium rather than very high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YTeBr is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0340 eV/atom, within the high-synthesizability thermodynamic window. The structural analogues support synthesis strongly: ErSeI, GdSI, and DySBr all show 2D rare-earth chalcogenide halide sheets with LnQ₄X₂ octahedra and L-shaped halide coordination (SS2, SS4, SS5). The most important enabling factor is halide-assisted sealed-ampoule or CVT transport, which improves mass transport and has precedent in SS2, SS3, and SS5. The most important limiting factor is stoichiometry control for volatile Br and Te under sealed-tube conditions, supported by Br₂ handling in SS5 and sealed bromide chemistry in TS1–TS3. Overall, the phase appears reasonably synthesizable, with CVT best suited for single crystals and direct/binary routes plausible for powders.
