### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the thermodynamic convex hull and receives a high score of 98 according to the provided rubric. Many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar reference phases also have E_hull = 0.0, including BaAgAs, KMgAs, KSnAs, Rb₂SnAs₂, K₂Zn₅As₄, Rb₂Zn₅Sb₄, and CsZn₄As₃ (CS1–CS4, SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3, TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that computational hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or single-phase synthesis, especially in Rb–Zn–As systems where side phases are reported (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 580–620 °C, consistent with Rb samples in K/Rb–Zn–As analogues at 600 °C for 10 d and RbZn₄As₃-type reactions at 550 °C (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4). Deductions come from the need for controlled Ar/vacuum sealed conditions and Rb/As air sensitivity/volatility, which are implied by glovebox and evacuated sealed tube protocols in analogues (SS1/TS1, CS5). There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS for high pressure or >1000 °C being required for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because Rb is low-melting/reactive and related K/Rb–Zn–As compounds form at only 550–650 °C, indicating effective diffusion/transport under sealed conditions (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4; low-melting Rb is Reasonable Inference). However, long 10 d anneals and reported minor impurities/side phases suggest ordering and phase selection are not instantaneous (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4). The same P4/nmm layered tetrahedral chemistry in KMgAs supports structural accessibility but does not eliminate composition-specific uncertainty (CS2).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because the strongest P4/nmm analogue, KMgAs, uses a 1150 °C step followed by 800 °C annealing in Ta crucibles/iron ampoules under Ar (CS2). Nb-tube Rb/Zn/Sb analogues require arc-welded Nb tubes, silica jacketing, and controlled cooling, adding equipment and atmosphere complexity (SS3/TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for external high pressure, so that deduction is not applied.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-temperature treatment should improve homogenization and diffusion, as shown by successful synthesis of KMgAs and Rb₂Zn₅Sb₄/Rb₂Cd₅As₄-type phases under sealed ampoule annealing (CS2, SS3/TS3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because KMgAs is a direct P4/nmm analogue with tetrahedral MgAs₄ layers (CS2). The main uncertainty is whether the 1:1:1 RbZnAs phase remains stable at the higher homogenization temperature or instead converts to Zn-rich/Rb-rich competing phases, which are reported in related Rb–Zn–As chemistry (SS4/TS4).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 remains below 1000 °C in the recommended form and is supported by molten alkali pnictide syntheses at 950–1200 K under Ar (CS4). Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed containment, and toxic/air-sensitive Rb/As precursors, consistent with the handling requirements of alkali pnictide syntheses (CS4, SS3/TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure or >1400 °C conditions.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A Rb-rich melt should improve mass transport compared with dry powders, analogous to molten elemental mixtures producing Rb₂SnAs₂ and related alkali pnictides (CS4). This gives high diffusion and precursor-activity scores. The structural score is lower because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports flux growth of P4/nmm RbZnAs, and Rb–Zn–As systems can crystallize competing Zn-rich phases such as RbZn₄As₃ (SS4/TS4).

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 uses sealed binary precursors and moderate final annealing, analogous to KSnAs prepared from KSn + As at 590 °C and K₂NaInAs₂/K₂LiInAs₂ prepared from KAs/InAs-type precursor sets in sealed ampoules (CS3, SS2/TS2, SS5/TS5). The route still requires controlled atmosphere and sealed ampoules because the reagents are alkali arsenides/intermetallics and contain volatile/toxic As [Reasonable Inference; sealed precursor routes in CS3, SS2/TS2]. No evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a need for high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C for the preferred version.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Binary precursors should improve local chemical homogeneity and reduce the direct Rb–As reaction barrier, following the KSnAs two-step route and KAs/InAs precursor precedent (CS3, SS2/TS2, SS5/TS5). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbZnAs is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many similar alkali/alkaline-earth pnictides are also hull-stable in the reference set (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong chemically and structurally: KMgAs is an exact P4/nmm layered tetrahedral pnictide analogue (CS2), while K/Rb–Zn–As/Sb compounds are experimentally accessible by sealed solid-state or Nb-tube annealing (SS1/TS1, SS3/TS3, SS4/TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of alkali/arsenide components under sealed moderate-temperature annealing, which allows synthesis well below refractory solid-state regimes (SS1/TS1, CS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in Rb–Zn–As chemistry, since related syntheses report minor impurities, Zn-rich phases, and optimization needs (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4). Overall, the material should be synthesizable in a well-equipped solid-state pnictide lab, with the binary-precursor sealed-ampoule route offering the best balance of kinetics and condition severity.
