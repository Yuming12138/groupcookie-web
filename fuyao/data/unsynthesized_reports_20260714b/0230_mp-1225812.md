### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the boundary of the high-score regime in the prompt, so the thermodynamic score is high but not equivalent to a robust ground-state compound. TS analogues with very similar hull energies were synthesized, but many were explicitly high-pressure or metastable phases, which lowers confidence for straightforward ambient synthesis (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS analogues are stable rare-earth germanides with E_hull = 0, which supports chemical plausibility but not the target’s exact Cmcm framework (CS-1–CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ga incorporation stabilizes the Cmcm Eu–Ge framework enough for ambient-pressure recovery [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed-ampoule solid-state

The severity is moderate because CS sealed-tube syntheses use 1073–1273 K, vacuum or purified Ar, and no high pressure (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The main deductions are controlled atmosphere/sealing and air-sensitive Eu handling, which are consistent with glovebox use in CS/SS/TS reactive germanide syntheses (CS-1; SS-1, SS-2; TS-4, TS-5). SS/TS evidence conflicts with CS because structurally and thermodynamically similar Ge-rich frameworks often required 8–15 GPa, so ambient synthesis may be less reliable (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1–TS-5). The final F2 score of 65 reflects manageable but nontrivial sealed intermetallic synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed-ampoule solid-state

Kinetics are limited by using elemental metals/powders rather than highly reactive molecular precursors, as in CS elemental routes (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). Diffusion is not severe because the highest relevant elemental melting point is Ge at about 938 °C [Reasonable Inference], and CS syntheses operate near this range (CS-1, CS-2). Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-hard because the target Cmcm network differs from CS tetragonal I4mm analogues, while the closest Cmcm SS analogue is chemically different and high-pressure-derived (SS-1). The F3 score of 62 captures acceptable but not fast solid-state formation.

#### F2 — Route 2 flux growth

Flux growth has moderate condition severity because it avoids high pressure but requires sealed/inert handling, slow cooling, and flux removal, as seen for CS Bi-flux rare-earth germanides (CS-3, CS-5). Bi-flux growth can involve inclusions/removal complications, specifically noted for Ce-based analogues (CS-5). Ga self-flux is a reasonable adaptation for the target because Ga is a constituent and low-melting [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates EuGa2Ge4 flux growth. The F2 score of 55 reflects added process complexity despite less harsh thermodynamic conditions than HPHT.

#### F3 — Route 2 flux growth

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because a liquid medium improves transport and can bypass slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS directly supports flux crystallization of chemically similar rare-earth germanides by Bi flux (CS-3, CS-5), and CS-2 supports slow-cooling crystal growth from an excess Ge-containing charge. The key uncertainty is whether the target Cmcm EuGa2Ge4 phase nucleates instead of Eu–Ge binaries, Ga–Ge binaries, or flux-stabilized off-stoichiometric products [Reasonable Inference]. The F3 score of 77 reflects strong kinetic benefit with composition-control uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 HPHT

The HPHT route is conditionally severe because SS/TS analogues repeatedly require 8–15 GPa, graphite heaters, h-BN crucibles, MgO octahedra, and Walker/Kawai modules (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). Strict inert handling is also required for reactive metals in analogous syntheses (SS-1, SS-2; TS-4, TS-5). CS gives no strong high-pressure requirement for chemically similar rare-earth germanides, so the HPHT route is primarily justified by SS/TS rather than CS. The F2 score of 40 reflects the major equipment and pressure penalty.

#### F3 — Route 3 HPHT

Kinetically, HPHT synthesis can be effective because several SS/TS germanides form within short anneals at high pressure, including BaGe6, BaGe5, and EuGe3 (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). Structural reconstruction is favored relative to Route 1 because the closest structural/thermodynamic analogues are HPHT Ge frameworks (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). Precursor activity remains only moderate because the route uses elemental or pre-reacted solid intermetallic feedstocks rather than solution precursors (SS-2, SS-4; TS-2, TS-3). The F3 score of 68 reflects good phase-driving conditions but limited precursor activity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization trigger is clearly assigned: large size mismatch between Eu and Ga/Ge network atoms [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide a >40 atom unit cell or explicit mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral coexistence, so evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS for triggering C2 or C3. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

EuGa2Ge4 is most likely a near-threshold metastable or weakly stable phase rather than an unambiguously robust ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and TS analogues near this hull energy are often metastable/high-pressure phases (TS-1–TS-5). The CS data strongly support elemental sealed-tube or flux synthesis of rare-earth germanides, but those analogues are structurally different tetragonal phases rather than the target Cmcm phase (CS-1–CS-5). The SS/TS data strongly support that similar Ge-framework motifs are experimentally reachable, but they also warn that high pressure may be needed for closely related frameworks (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1–TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is use of a liquid flux, especially Ga self-flux, to improve diffusion and crystallization while maintaining target stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-3/CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is competing phase formation/metastability near 0.05 eV/atom, especially if the Cmcm framework is only stabilized under pressure, as seen in SS/TS germanide analogues (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3).
