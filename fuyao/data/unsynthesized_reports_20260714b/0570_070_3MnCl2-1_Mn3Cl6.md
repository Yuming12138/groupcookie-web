### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has Eₕᵤₗₗ = 0.0390 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability scoring bin [Target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally synthesized, including K₃Pb₂Cl₈ at 0.0364 eV/atom [TS2] and SrFMnSb at 0.0468 eV/atom [TS5]. Chemically similar metastable compounds at higher hull energies, such as β-TcCl₃ at 0.0709 eV/atom [CS5] and MnS₂ at 0.1106 eV/atom [CS2], further support accessibility. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph competition: the target is not a ground-state phase but a low-energy metastable one [Target data].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperature comparable to the 450 °C sealed-tube TcCl₃ synthesis [CS5], so temperature severity is not high. The major penalties are Cl₂ toxicity/volatility and sealed-tube handling, both directly indicated by CS5. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically supports direct Mn + Cl₂ chlorination conditions; therefore CS5 is weighted most heavily. The route is feasible but safety- and containment-limited.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct chlorination benefits from the high reactivity of Cl₂, as shown by Tc metal + Cl₂ reaction [CS5]. However, Mn metal is a dense elemental precursor, so cation diffusion and surface passivation can slow conversion by [Reasonable Inference]. The target tetrahedral MnCl₄ sheet differs structurally from the octahedral TcCl₆ layered chloride [CS5], suggesting significant reconstruction. The main uncertainty is whether the target C2 polymorph nucleates before a more stable MnCl₂ structure forms [Target data; CS5].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with mild chloride crystallizations by slow evaporation or slow cooling from water [SS2, TS2]. The penalties arise from controlled dry HCl/vacuum dehydration, multistep processing, and corrosive chloride media, which are [Reasonable Inference] but consistent with the need for controlled atmospheres in sealed halide/solid-state analogues [CS5, TS3, TS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly proves MnCl₂ dehydration to the target polymorph, so this route has condition uncertainty despite mild temperatures. It is the least thermally severe route but not operationally trivial.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Solution-derived MnCl₂ provides high precursor activity because Mn and Cl are already mixed at ionic scale; solution chloride crystallization is supported by SS2 and TS2. Ion diffusion is favorable compared with metal/oxide solid-state routes because dehydration occurs from a preformed chloride salt by [Reasonable Inference]. The weaker kinetic element is structural reconstruction: hydrate-to-anhydrous conversion may favor known dense polymorphs rather than the tetrahedral 2D C2 target [Target data]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS identifies an exact hydrate precursor with the same MnCl₄ sheet topology, so structural selectivity remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures but requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile halogen transport agents, as supported by I₂-assisted crystallization [CS1] and Cl₂ sealed-tube chloride synthesis [CS5]. The multistep feedstock preparation and ampoule growth add operational difficulty by [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for vapor transport of simple MnCl₂, so CS1/CS5 provide the strongest support. Safety and ampoule pressure control dominate severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Halogen-assisted transport improves mass transfer and crystallization, supported by CS1 where iodine favors crystallization and by SS5 where flux/slow-cooling yields single crystals. Starting from anhydrous MnCl₂ also avoids forming the binary composition from separate elements by [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target’s tetrahedral 2D structure differs from the octahedral layered chloride analogue in CS5, and metastable chloride polymorphs can transform during annealing [CS5]. Thus kinetics are favorable for crystallinity but uncertain for polymorph selection.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Target data]. The described structure contains mixed edge- and corner-sharing MnCl₄ tetrahedra, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Target data]. Similar mixed-connectivity frameworks are nonetheless synthesized in Mn/transition-metal chalcogenide analogues [SS3, SS4, TS5], suggesting this risk is manageable. Remaining uncertainty is whether the low-dimensional tetrahedral chloride sheet is kinetically preserved during dehydration or annealing [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its energy above hull is 0.0390 eV/atom [Target data]. The thermodynamic analogues support synthesis of compounds in this metastability range, including TS2 and TS5 with similar hull energies and reported experimental routes [TS2, TS5]. The strongest enabling factor is the simple binary Mn–Cl chemistry and the availability of mild chloride crystallization or sealed halogen routes supported by SS2, TS2, and CS5. The strongest limiting factor is polymorph control: CS5 shows that layered chloride polymorphs can be low-yield and transform with annealing, and MnCl₂ may prefer a different coordination environment by [Reasonable Inference from Target data and CS5]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, but obtaining the specific monoclinic C2 tetrahedral sheet phase will likely require careful low-temperature kinetic control.
