### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary for synthesizability. CS gives multiple chemically similar RE(IrB)₂ materials at E_hull = 0.0, and SS gives structurally similar La/Ce/Pr/Nd/Sm RE(IrB)₄ materials at E_hull = 0.0. TS is also supportive because several experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues lie near the same metastability range, about 0.024–0.068 eV/atom. The main uncertainty is polymorph selection: CS says LaIr₂B₂ is a high-temperature phase that decomposes on annealing, so the target is likely metastable or high-temperature-stabilized.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, arc melting.**  
CS gives the strongest support for this route because chemically similar RE(IrB)₂ phases are made by twice arc-melting pressed elemental mixtures under Ar. SS and TS also support arc melting as a common route for Ir–B borides. The severity is moderate because it requires high temperature, inert atmosphere, repeated melting, and air-sensitive La, but no high pressure. Kinetics are limited by refractory Ir/B and by the need to select the tetragonal polymorph rather than the common Fddd analogue.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, sealed melt growth.**  
SS directly shows La–Ir–B tetragonal borides can be crystallized from high-temperature sealed melts at 1600 °C with controlled cooling. This improves diffusion and crystallization relative to solid-state annealing, but the condition severity is higher because T > 1400 °C and Ta/alumina containment are required. The uncertainty is compositional transfer: SS is La(IrB)₄, not La(IrB)₂, so the melt chemistry is relevant but not identical.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, solid-state sintering.**  
SS supports BN/Ta-contained sintering at 1400 °C for related RE–Ir–B borides, including crush/recompact/final heat-treatment cycles. However, CS warns that LaIr₂B₂ decomposes on annealing, making long solid-state sintering risky for the target. Kinetically, this route is less favorable because Ir and B are refractory and diffusion is slow below full melting.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, high-pressure synthesis.**  
TS provides precedent for pressure-stabilizing tetragonal rare-earth borides at 5–9 GPa and 1200 °C, followed by quench. This route may help trap a metastable tetragonal polymorph, but neither CS nor SS indicates pressure is necessary for La–Ir–B, so the evidence is indirect. Severity is high due to >5 GPa pressure and specialized apparatus, while kinetics remain limited by short dwell times and refractory components.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has chemically and size-mismatched La and Ir sites, which triggers one amorphization/complexity check by [Reasonable Inference] from the large La–Ir size/electronegativity contrast and high-coordinate environments described in the target and CS/SS analogues. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing all coexist. Thus amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La(IrB)₂ is likely a marginally metastable or high-temperature polymorph rather than a robust ambient-temperature ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom and CS reports LaIr₂B₂-type phases can decompose on annealing. The synthesis is nevertheless plausible: CS gives very strong chemical support for RE(IrB)₂ formation by arc melting, SS supports La–Ir–B tetragonal boride crystallization from high-temperature melts, and TS shows comparable Ir/Rh borides near this metastability range can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is rapid high-temperature homogenization by arc melting or melt growth, which bypasses slow Ir/B solid-state diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection and retention of the tetragonal I4₁/amd polymorph during cooling, especially against decomposition or transformation to competing La–Ir–B/IrBₓ phases. Overall, the best practical strategy is arc melting under very clean Ar with rapid cooling and minimal annealing.
