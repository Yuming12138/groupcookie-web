### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a very high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues are also mostly stable or nearly stable, including KNO₃ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS1), Zn nitrate hydrate at ~0.0048 eV/atom (TS2), Mg nitrate hydrate at ~0.0052 eV/atom (TS3), and Cs₂PbCl₂(NO₃)₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS4). Structurally similar nitrate hydrates are likewise near/on hull (SS1–SS3), while CS is mixed because chemically similar sulfate/phosphate hydrates include both stable and high-above-hull entries, so CS alone is less decisive. Remaining uncertainty is mainly finite-temperature hydration stability and possible competing RbNO₃/Ca(NO₃)₂ hydrate phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses mild aqueous crystallization at ambient pressure, matching KNO₃ slow cooling/evaporation from saturated water solutions (SS3, SS4, TS1) and Zn nitrate hydrate evaporation (SS1, TS2). No high temperature, high pressure, reactive atmosphere, or sealed volatile precursor is required if nitrate salts and water are used. CS5 also supports mild 80 °C cooling crystallization of hydrated oxo-salts. The main uncertainty is whether dilute HNO₃ is needed; if used, corrosive acid handling would modestly increase severity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because all ions are mobile in aqueous solution, and nitrate salts crystallize readily from saturated solutions in SS3/SS4/TS1. The target structural motifs—trigonal planar nitrate groups, alkali/alkaline-earth oxygen coordination, and water ligands—match nitrate hydrate analogues in SS1/SS2/TS2/TS3. CS5 confirms that mixed hydrated oxo-salts can crystallize from slow cooling/evaporation. Remaining uncertainty is cation ordering between Rb⁺ and Ca²⁺ and obtaining the exact dihydrate rather than another hydration state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is also low-temperature and ambient-pressure, based on hydrated nitrate melt crystallization at 410 K in SS2/TS3. It requires no high-pressure apparatus, reactive gas, or high temperature. The route may require covered or lightly sealed vessels to regulate water activity, but this is not necessarily a high-severity sealed volatile-precursor condition [Reasonable Inference]. The main handling issue is hygroscopicity of nitrate hydrates, noted in SS2/TS3.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrated nitrate melts provide high ionic mobility, as SS2/TS3 report crystallization after several hours at 410 K. Precursor activity is high because Ca nitrate hydrate can act as both reagent and liquid medium [Reasonable Inference], while RbNO₃ is a soluble/mobile nitrate salt supported by KNO₃ analogues (SS3, SS4, TS1). Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-favorable because nitrate hydrate motifs are retained, but formation of a mixed Rb/Ca double salt is less directly proven than formation of simple divalent nitrate hydrates. The key uncertainty is water loss and crystallization of incorrect hydrate stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Hydrothermal synthesis is supported by CS1–CS4 and TS4, with temperatures from 140 to 240 °C and sealed autoclaves. These conditions are moderate rather than severe because they do not require >1000 °C, high pressure >5 GPa, or reactive atmospheres. A deduction is applied for the multi-operation nature of autoclave synthesis and recovery, consistent with the hydrothermal procedures in CS1–CS4 and TS4. The main uncertainty is whether hydrothermal temperatures are too high for retaining the target dihydrate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Aqueous hydrothermal media provide excellent mass transport, as shown by successful crystallization of hydrated oxo-salts and nitrate-containing phases in CS1–CS4 and TS4. Soluble nitrate precursors should react rapidly, and slow cooling can assist crystal growth, as in TS4 and CS4. Structural formation is plausible because nitrate groups and hydrated metal–oxygen coordination are common in the analogues, but the target may dehydrate or transform under prolonged hydrothermal heating [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are strong but phase selectivity is less certain than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

The sealed solid-state/hydrate-assisted route is based on TS5, where alkaline-earth hydroxy-nitrates were made in evacuated quartz ampoules with glove-box handling. It is not high-temperature by ceramic standards, but it involves vacuum/sealed ampoules, controlled water content, multiple handling steps, and moisture-sensitive nitrate/hydrate precursors [Reasonable Inference]. These factors justify a substantially lower F2. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS supports sealed solid-state routes for this exact double nitrate hydrate, so CS and SS are neutral for this specific route-severity factor.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Nitrates and hydrates are more reactive than refractory oxides, and TS5 shows nitrate/hydroxide mixtures can form crystalline nitrate-containing alkaline-earth phases. However, TS5 required days of annealing and showed impurity formation for the Ba analogue, indicating phase-control and diffusion/transport limitations. For RbCa(NO₃)₃·2H₂O, solid-state ordering of Rb/Ca plus preservation of water is less direct than solution crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are acceptable but clearly inferior to solution or melt routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Rb⁺ and Ca²⁺, reflected by long Rb–O contacts of 2.92–3.24 Å and shorter Ca–O contacts of 2.35–2.83 Å in the target structure; this triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target formula contains 20 atoms, and the provided structure lists 20 inequivalent sites, but no full unit-cell Z is given; therefore, evidence for >40 atoms is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and is scored neutral for that check. The target description does not explicitly report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, so evidence for that amorphization criterion is also insufficient from CS/SS/TS. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, with crystallization favored by solution-based routes shown in SS/TS nitrate analogues.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbCaN₃H₄O₁₁ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state hydrated double nitrate because the reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple nitrate hydrate or nitrate analogues in SS/TS are on or near the hull. The synthesis evidence is strongest from SS and TS, which directly include nitrate hydrates and alkali nitrate crystallization from aqueous solution; CS adds broader support for hydrated oxo-salt crystallization but is less chemically specific. The most important enabling factor is the high solubility and mobility of nitrate salts in water, allowing low-temperature crystallization without solid-state diffusion barriers, as demonstrated by SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, and TS2. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible RbNO₃, Ca(NO₃)₂ hydrates, and alternative double-salt hydration states [Reasonable Inference]. Hydrothermal and sealed routes are plausible backups but are less attractive because the target contains coordinated water and may dehydrate at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference].
