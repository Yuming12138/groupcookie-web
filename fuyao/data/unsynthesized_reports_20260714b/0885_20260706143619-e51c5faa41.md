### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound. This is reinforced by the presence of many zero-hull related sulfides in the evidence set, including CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5, and TS-1–TS-5. The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide synthesis window or fast formation kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state.**  
This route is moderately severe because it uses sealed vacuum/Ar ampoules, sulfur-bearing volatile reagents, air-sensitive sulfides, and repeated grinding/refiring; similar requirements occur in CS-3, SS-3/TS-2, and TS-5. It avoids high pressure and can likely stay below 1000 °C, based on CS-3’s 550–850 °C range and TS-5’s 600–800 °C main reaction range. The main uncertainty is whether Mn-containing competing phases require higher temperature or longer annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state.**  
Binary sulfides give moderate precursor activity, supported by TS-5’s use of elemental A + sulfur + powdered monosulfides and CS-3’s sulfide/element high-T routes. Diffusion and structural reconstruction are only moderate because related solid-state syntheses require intermittent grinding, homogenization, reheating, or refiring (CS-3, SS-3/TS-2, TS-5). The target’s cubic ordered Sr–Mn–Ge framework may require careful equilibration [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 flux.**  
Flux growth still requires vacuum sealing, sulfur/GeS2 volatility management, air-sensitive sulfide handling, and post-growth flux removal; comparable requirements are reported in CS-1, CS-2, SS-4/TS-3, and SS-5/TS-4. It avoids >1000 °C conditions, with many analogues crystallizing at 500–850 °C (CS-1, CS-2, SS-4/TS-3, SS-5/TS-4). The severity is therefore moderate rather than low.

**F3 — Route 2 flux.**  
Flux provides the best kinetics because related complex chalcogenides form from molten halide or polysulfide media at relatively modest temperatures, e.g. Li2EuGeS4 at 600–650 °C (SS-1/TS-1), K–Sn–As–S at 500 °C (CS-2), and Cs/Gd/Sm thio-frameworks at 750–850 °C (SS-4/TS-3, SS-5/TS-4). The main kinetic risk is phase selectivity: byproducts were reported in Li2EuGeS4 flux synthesis (SS-1/TS-1), and CsSmGeS4 had flux/byproduct and 50% yield issues (SS-5/TS-4). Overall, flux growth is the most favorable route for obtaining crystals.

**F2 — Route 3 direct melt.**  
Direct elemental synthesis is more severe because it uses reactive Sr metal, volatile sulfur, sealed ampoules, and likely >1000 °C annealing, as in CS-5 and SS-3/TS-2. It does not require high pressure, and related syntheses use standard tube furnaces and quartz/silica ampoules (CS-5, SS-2, SS-3/TS-2). The largest operational uncertainty is pressure buildup and reaction exothermicity during sulfurization [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 direct melt.**  
Elemental routes can work for related sulfides, including Y3CuGeS7 (CS-5), Eu2GeS4 (SS-2), and Sr2SnS4 (SS-3/TS-2). However, CS-5 required slow cooling plus 240 h annealing, and SS-3/TS-2 required homogenization/reheating, indicating slower kinetics than flux routes. The target may form but likely needs extended annealing and phase-purity optimization [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines large Sr cations with smaller Mn/Ge framework cations, so cation size mismatch is significant [Reasonable Inference from target coordination; related size-contrast sulfides are seen in SS-3/TS-2 and TS-5]. The formula and cubic I-43d symmetry imply a likely large unit cell, although direct Z information is not provided; insufficient direct unit-cell atom-count evidence from CS/SS/TS. The target does not report extensive edge/face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is not strongly triggered by the target description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr6Mn3(GeS4)4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports the broader feasibility of complex sulfide and thiogermanate synthesis by sealed-tube solid-state and flux methods, especially through halide or polysulfide fluxes (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1/TS-1, SS-4/TS-3, SS-5/TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust formation of GeS4 tetrahedra in many related sulfides, including CS-3, SS-1/TS-1, SS-2, and SS-5/TS-4. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Sr–Mn–S, Sr–Ge–S, Mn–Ge–S, and binary sulfide phases [Reasonable Inference], consistent with byproduct and polymorph sensitivity seen in SS-1/TS-1, SS-5/TS-4, and TS-5. Overall, flux growth is the most promising first attempt, while solid-state annealing is a plausible backup for powder synthesis.
