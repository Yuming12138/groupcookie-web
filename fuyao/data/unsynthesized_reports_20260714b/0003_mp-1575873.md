### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places Ca3Cr2O7 at the favorable near-hull boundary, so F1 is scored 90 [Target]. Synthesized analogues include CaCrO3 at 0.065 eV/atom and BaCrO3 at 0.084 eV/atom, showing that comparable metastability is experimentally accessible under high pressure [CS1/SS1/TS1/SS2/TS3]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the n=2 layered Ca3Cr2O7 polymorph competes with CaCrO3, Ca2Cr2O5, or Ca–Cr impurity phases, which are reported concerns in CaCrO3/Ca–Cr systems [CS1/CS3].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for >5 GPa pressure and dry/toxic precursor handling, because CaCrO3 and related Ba/Ca chromates require 65–70 kbar or 9 GPa equipment [CS1/CS3/SS2/TS1/TS3]. Its temperature severity is moderate because the analogues form at 900–1100 °C rather than >1400 °C [CS3/SS2/TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates volatile-precursor sealing is not required, so that item is neutral 50 and not deducted.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to ordinary ceramics because CaO/CrO2 directly match the target stoichiometry and have direct CaCrO3 precedent [CS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is strongly supported because CaCrO3 and the target both use corner-sharing CrO6 octahedra with very similar Cr–O distances, while Ca3Mn2O7 supplies the same A3B2O7 Cmc2_1 topology [Target/CS1/CS4]. The main uncertainty is layer ordering of Ca3Cr2O7 versus formation of simpler CaCrO3-type perovskite [CS1/Target].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids >5 GPa pressure but requires high temperature, controlled O2/N2 selection, and multistep grinding/sintering similar to Ca3Mn2O7 and Sr2CrO4 preparations [CS4/SS3/TS4]. The extra >1400 °C deduction is justified by ceramic analogues that use 1673 K or 1500 °C treatments [CS4/SS4/TS2]. The major weakness is that CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate ambient-pressure Ca3Cr2O7, so the ambient Ca–Cr–O transferability is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Bulk ceramic kinetics are slower because oxide/carbonate precursors require repeated grinding, long firing, and high temperatures [CS4/SS3/TS4]. The structural match to Ca3Mn2O7 is strong [CS4], but Cr4+ Ca–Cr perovskites are better supported under high pressure [CS1/TS1]. Therefore F3 is moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has mild temperature and ambient-pressure advantages based on Ca2MnO4 flux growth at 900 °C [CS2]. It is penalized for multistep feed/flux/wash operations and for inferred redox-atmosphere control needed for Cr4+ [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS for Cr-based flux growth is treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux should improve precursor activity and cation transport because Ca2MnO4 crystals grow from CaCl2–CaO flux at 900 °C [CS2]. However, structural reconstruction is uncertain because CS2 is Mn-based, n=1 layered, and does not address Cr4+ redox stability [CS2]. The flux route is therefore plausible for crystal growth only after the target phase or a close Ca–Cr–O feed is available [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the cation-radius mismatch trigger is counted, based on the large Ca2+ versus Cr4+ size contrast [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives corner-sharing CrO6 rather than mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral connectivity, and the same-structure Ca3Mn2O7 analogue has a modest reported formula setting rather than a >40-atom cell [Target/CS4]. Exact target unit-cell atom count is insufficiently evidenced from SS/TS, so those sources are neutral 50 for C2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca3Cr2O7 is best viewed as a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than an unquestionably robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports high-pressure stabilization of Cr4+ perovskite-like Ca/Sr/Ba chromates, especially CaCrO3 and BaCrO3 [CS1/SS1/TS1/SS2/TS3]. The same Cmc2_1 A3B2O7 framework is validated by Ca3Mn2O7, but that analogue does not solve Cr4+ redox stability for Ca–Cr–O [CS4]. The key enabling factor is high pressure with CrO2/CaO chemistry, which directly supports corner-sharing CrO6 Ca–Cr perovskite formation [CS1/TS1]. The key limiting factor is phase and redox control, because CaCrO3 synthesis reports impurity phases and narrow condition windows [CS1].
