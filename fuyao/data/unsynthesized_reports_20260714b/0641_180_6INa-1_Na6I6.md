### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0320 eV/atom, which falls in the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. TS-1 reports a chemically similar LiI polymorph at E_hull = 0.0289 eV/atom that was experimentally obtained by low-temperature vapor deposition, supporting the idea that this metastability magnitude can be experimentally trapped. CS-1/SS-5 also report a LiI polymorph accessible by low-temperature deposition, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms whether the exact NaI P4/mmm polymorph is a true equilibrium phase. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target is dynamically stable at room temperature or only cryogenically recoverable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a low-to-moderate severity score because CS-1/SS-5 require high vacuum, a −196 °C substrate, precursor drying, and inert/vacuum XRD handling. CS-2/TS-1 is even more demanding, using 20 K condensation and vacuum better than 5×10⁻⁸ mmHg. These sources directly support severe atmosphere and temperature-control deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether NaI specifically needs 20 K rather than liquid-N₂ temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 1

The vapor availability score is strong because CS-1/SS-5 and CS-2/TS-1 demonstrate direct evaporation of alkali iodides/halides. The crystallization pathway is moderately uncertain: these analogues show metastable iodide phases can form by cryogenic deposition, but none directly reports tetragonal P4/mmm NaI. Composition control is simpler for binary NaI than for multicomponent iodides [Reasonable Inference], but volatile iodine-containing salts and moisture sensitivity require careful control as indicated by CS-1/SS-5 and CS-2/TS-1.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids extreme high temperatures and high pressure, consistent with SS-1/TS-2 and TS-4, where iodide/bromide crystals are grown around 350–400 °C or similar moderate temperatures. However, sealed ampoules, dry-box or Ar handling, vacuum sealing, and slow cooling are required in SS-1/TS-2, SS-2/TS-5, and TS-4. The volatile halide component and moisture sensitivity justify deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that simple NaI melt growth stabilizes the target polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 2

The precursor activity and diffusion scores are high because halide salts are mobile, melt-processable, and used successfully in SS-1/TS-2, SS-2/TS-5, and TS-4. The structural reconstruction score is much lower because the target is a polymorph of NaI, and bulk melt cooling is likely to favor the stable NaI polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2/TS-5 show that even related iodide solid-state reactions sometimes require repeated annealing and grinding to reach single phase, implying phase-control difficulty. The route is kinetically feasible but not strongly phase-selective.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures like those in SS-2/TS-5 and SS-3, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. It still requires evacuated ampoules or Ar, sealed handling of volatile iodides, and likely multiple processing steps, all supported by SS-2/TS-5 and SS-3. The severity is therefore similar to Route 2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any simpler air-stable annealing route for NaI.

#### F3 — Route 3

NaI and related binary halides are reactive salts, so precursor activity and ion diffusion are favorable, as supported by SS-2/TS-5 and SS-3. The weak point is structural reconstruction: converting ordinary NaI into a layered tetragonal polymorph without a vapor-deposition or epitaxial pathway is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2/TS-1 specifically indicate metastable alkali iodide polymorphs can transform toward stable phases, which lowers confidence in simple anneal/quench retention. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS shows successful bulk quenching of P4/mmm NaI.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered; this is based on the target formula, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient additional signal. The target cell appears small from the listed inequivalent Na/I sites, so the >40 atom-count check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity is triggered by the target description and is common among structurally similar iodides in SS-1, SS-2, and TS-5, suggesting some topological complexity but not an extreme amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is whether the 2D sheet character promotes glassy/poorly crystalline films during low-temperature deposition.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable NaI polymorph rather than an easily accessed ambient ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.032 eV/atom and CS-2/TS-1 show a comparably metastable LiI polymorph obtained only by cryogenic deposition. The CS/SS/TS analogues support general alkali iodide synthesis well, especially by vapor deposition, sealed ampoule growth, and halide annealing, but none directly confirms P4/mmm NaI. The most critical enabling factor is the low energy above hull, supported by the target value and the TS-1 metastable LiI analogue. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection against stable bulk NaI, for which cryogenic deposition evidence from CS-1/SS-5 and CS-2/TS-1 is much stronger than conventional bulk synthesis evidence. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, but the best chance of isolating the reported 2D tetragonal polymorph is kinetic trapping, not equilibrium melt growth.
