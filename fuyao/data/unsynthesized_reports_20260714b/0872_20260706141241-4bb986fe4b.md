### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase rather than a high-energy metastable phase. This is consistent with chemically and structurally related bromides being at or near the hull, including RbPb2Br5 at ~0.008 eV/atom (CS-1; SS-1; TS-2), CsPbBr3 at ~0.0046 eV/atom (CS-4; SS-4), and Cs3Bi2Br9 at 0 eV/atom (SS-3; TS-1). The remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic existence but kinetic and phase-selective isolation from competing CsPbBr3/PbBr2-rich products.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct aqueous cooling crystallization

Route 1 has very mild severity because the directly relevant CsBr/PbBr2 route uses water, 80 °C dissolution, cooling, and air/ambient pressure conditions (CS-4; SS-4). The only major penalty is the use of toxic PbBr2, which is a standard lead-salt hazard [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are excellent because CsPb2Br5 crystallizes directly from ionic solution in the supplied evidence, and the isostructural RbPb2Br5 analogue also forms by solution cooling (CS-1; SS-1; TS-2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Aqueous slow evaporation

Route 2 remains mild in temperature and atmosphere because CsPbBr3 was grown at 35 °C in air after CsPb2Br5 appeared during cooling (CS-4; SS-4), and Cs3Bi2Br9 grows by aqueous slow evaporation as a thermodynamic analogue (SS-3; TS-1). The lower F2 reflects longer and more multi-step processing, plus lead toxicity. Kinetically, solution-mediated transport is favorable, but phase selectivity is less certain because prolonged evaporation in the cited Cs–Pb–Br system ultimately produced CsPbBr3 rather than retaining CsPb2Br5 as the final selected product (CS-4; SS-4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Route 3 is more severe because analogous bromide solid-state syntheses require argon glovebox handling, dry silica ampoules, vacuum sealing, heating to 523 K, and slow cooling (CS-2; CS-3; SS-2; SS-5). The route is plausible for CsBr/PbBr2 [Reasonable Inference], but not directly demonstrated for CsPb2Br5 in the supplied data. Kinetics are still reasonably good for a solid-state route because halide salts are more mobile/reactive than refractory oxides and similar bromides form at modest temperature, but impurity formation is reported for the Bi bromide analogues (CS-2; CS-3; SS-2; SS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Gel diffusion

Route 4 has mild conditions because PbI2 thermodynamic analogues grow in silica gel at room temperature to 323 K under ordinary room conditions (TS-3; TS-4; TS-5). The kinetic score is good for ion transport because diffusion occurs through aqueous gel, but the structural/phase-selection component is weaker because no supplied source demonstrates ternary CsPb2Br5 gel growth; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS directly supports gel growth for CsPb2Br5, so CS and SS are neutral, 50, for the gel-specific route evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination contrast between Cs and Pb sites, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target and the Rb analogue have ordered I4/mcm structures with relatively simple corner-sharing descriptions rather than a highly mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing network (target description; CS-1; SS-1). Insufficient evidence from TS specifically addresses amorphization of CsPb2Br5, so TS is neutral, 50, for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsPb2Br5 is very likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related halide phases are also on or very near the hull (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1; TS-2). The synthesis evidence is unusually strong because CsPb2Br5 itself was observed as white scales during aqueous CsBr/PbBr2 cooling (CS-4; SS-4), and the isostructural RbPb2Br5 analogue forms by cooling crystallization from solution (CS-1; SS-1; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated crystallization, which bypasses slow solid-state diffusion and allows the layered/halide framework to assemble at low temperature. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against CsPbBr3 and residual PbBr2/salt-rich impurities, as the same aqueous system can produce CsPbBr3 after subsequent evaporation (CS-4; SS-4). Overall, the best practical approach is to harvest CsPb2Br5 from Pb-rich aqueous cooling before conditions shift toward CsPbBr3 growth.
