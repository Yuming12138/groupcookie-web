### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom category. CS strongly supports this because V5S8 has E_hull = 0.0376 eV/atom and is experimentally grown by CVT [CS1], while VS2 is reported as a stable or near-stable V–S layered phase [CS2, CS3]. TS is also strongly supportive because the closest thermodynamic analogues NiV2S4, V2CrS4, and V5S8 have E_hull values of ~0.035–0.040 eV/atom and reported syntheses [TS1–TS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not prove the hexagonal P-6m2 polymorph is the experimentally preferred V2S3 polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, I2-CVT.**  
The severity score is moderate because V5S8 CVT uses 900→800 °C, an evacuated sealed tube, and iodine transport agent [CS1, TS3]. The main penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube handling, volatile S/I2, and iodine toxicity [CS1, TS3 plus physicochemical knowledge]. Kinetics are better than direct solid-state reaction because the transport agent enables vapor-phase redistribution, as demonstrated by single-crystal V5S8 growth [CS1, TS3]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the same V–S chemical space contains VS2, V5S8, VS, and related phases [CS1–CS3, SS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, direct solid-state.**  
The severity is moderate because analogues use 800–1000 °C evacuated silica tubes and repeated processing rather than high pressure [SS1, TS2]. Kinetics are the weakest of the routes because V metal is refractory and analogues require slow heating, long tempering, or multiple firings/grinding [SS1, TS2]. This route is simple and scalable, but it may favor the most stable bulk V–S phases rather than the 2D P-6m2 target [Reasonable Inference]. Some SS/TS entries include chemically inconsistent precursor descriptions such as cobalt/tellurium for V–S entries [SS4, TS5], so those are down-weighted as unreliable for route design.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, oxide sulfidation.**  
Severity is higher because H2S sulfurization is used in related syntheses and is toxic/corrosive [CS2, SS5, TS1]. Kinetics are better than direct V + S because oxide powders can form reactive sulfide intermediates, and NiV2S4 synthesis uses H2S followed by sulfur adjustment and sealed annealing [SS5, TS1]. However, oxygen removal and final stoichiometry control add uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 shows H2S routes can be quite harsh, up to 1200 °C for a VS2-related compound, although the proposed target route follows the milder SS5/TS1 temperatures.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, low-T topochemical conversion.**  
The severity is favorable because CS3 reports conversion of VS4/rGO to VS2/rGO at only 350 °C in vacuum for 10 h. Kinetics are scored high because preformed V–S bonding and nanoscale morphology can reduce long-range diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS3]. The main uncertainty is that CS3 demonstrates VS2, not V2S3, and SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for V2S3 formation by this low-temperature path. Therefore, Route 4 is best but receives a negative expert adjustment for speculative phase control.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Only the mixed-connectivity check is triggered because the target explicitly contains distorted face-, corner-, and edge-sharing V–S polyhedra [target description]. The single-cation chemistry avoids cation-radius mismatch, and the formula is small, so the other checks are not triggered [target description]. CS/SS/TS analogues with mixed V–S connectivity are nevertheless crystalline and experimentally synthesized [CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1–TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that low-temperature topochemical routes can produce nanocrystalline or poorly ordered products if sulfur loss is too rapid [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0370 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized V5S8 at 0.0376 eV/atom [CS1, TS3]. The CS and TS analogues provide strong support that V–S phases in this energy range are accessible by sealed-tube, CVT, or sulfidation methods [CS1–CS3, TS1–TS3]. The SS analogues support the plausibility of mixed edge/corner/face-sharing V–S frameworks, although some structurally similar entries have less directly applicable or chemically inconsistent precursor data [SS1–SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature or transport-assisted pathways that bypass slow bulk diffusion of refractory V [CS1, CS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among many nearby vanadium sulfides with different sulfur chemical potentials and V:S ratios [Reasonable Inference from CS1–CS3 and SS1].
