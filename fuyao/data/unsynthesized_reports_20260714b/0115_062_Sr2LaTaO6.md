### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high-synthesizability boundary according to the supplied scoring rubric. Similar thermodynamic-neighbor oxides with E_hull values near 0.056–0.067 eV/atom have reported syntheses [TS1, TS3, TS4], and closer Sr₂HoTaO₆/Sr₂YTaO₆ analogues are experimentally accessible [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact Sr₂LaTaO₆ C2/m polymorph may be slightly metastable relative to another ordering/tilt variant [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull and multiple Sr₂YTaO₆ polymorphs in CS2/CS3].

**F2 — Route 1.** The ceramic route is moderately severe because close analogues require >1000 °C, typically 1280–1400 °C [CS1/SS1, CS3/SS2]. It avoids high pressure and controlled atmospheres because air or ordinary furnace conditions are reported [CS1/SS1, CS5]. Multi-step grinding/calcination/sintering lowers the score [CS1/SS1, CS3/SS2].

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta₂O₅ and rare-earth oxide reaction, consistent with long/high-temperature treatments in Sr₂HoTaO₆ and Sr₂YTaO₆ [CS1/SS1, CS3/SS2]. The structural target is the same broad double-perovskite family as the analogues [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3], which helps, but reconstruction from binary oxides is still substantial. Insufficient evidence from TS for faster kinetics in the exact Sr–La–Ta system.

**F2 — Route 2.** Severity is similar to Route 1 because the closest Y analogue uses 1400 °C for 3 days [CS3/SS2]. The method remains ambient-pressure and does not require reactive atmosphere [CS3/SS2]. The extra LaTaO₄ pre-synthesis adds process complexity [Reasonable Inference from CS3/SS2].

**F3 — Route 2.** Using LaTaO₄ should improve local La–Ta mixing relative to La₂O₃ + Ta₂O₅ [Reasonable Inference], and this is strongly suggested by the YTaO₄ + SrCO₃ precedent for Sr₂YTaO₆ [CS3/SS2]. Diffusion remains slow because the reported analogue still requires 3 days and intermittent regrinding [CS3/SS2]. The route is therefore kinetically better than binary-oxide ceramic synthesis but not fast.

**F2 — Route 3.** The Pechini route uses more chemically involved precursors and multiple processing steps, as seen for Sr₂LuNbO₆ [CS4]. It may require high final crystallization temperatures, with CS4 using 1773 K for 60 h [CS4]. It does not require high pressure or inert/reducing atmosphere [CS4].

**F3 — Route 3.** Molecular mixing strongly improves precursor activity, as expected for polymeric complex routes and supported by CS4. However, high-temperature crystallization remains necessary in CS4, so diffusion and ordering are not fully eliminated [CS4]. The main uncertainty is whether a Ta-based analogue crystallizes as cleanly as the Nb-based Sr₂LuNbO₆ case [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for Pechini Sr₂LaTaO₆].

**F2 — Route 4.** Flux growth is low-temperature relative to ceramic methods because SS4 reports 600 °C growth of La₂NaTaO₆. The penalties arise from sealed hydroxide melt handling, caustic flux, and multi-step washing/recovery [SS4]. It does not require high pressure in the >5 GPa sense [SS4].

**F3 — Route 4.** Hydroxide flux improves kinetics by dissolving/recrystallizing refractory La/Ta oxides, as shown by La₂NaTaO₆ growth at 600 °C [SS4]. The main kinetic/phase uncertainty is whether Sr can be incorporated instead of Na/K from the flux [Reasonable Inference], and TS4 warns that related Na/Ca/Ta systems can yield mixtures requiring crystal selection. Therefore, Route 4 is promising for crystals but less robust for phase-pure bulk Sr₂LaTaO₆.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has large cation size/charge contrast among Sr²⁺, La³⁺, and Ta⁵⁺, triggering the ionic-radius check [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The target framework is mainly corner-sharing LaO₆/TaO₆ octahedra, matching the ordered double-perovskite analogues [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3], so mixed edge/face connectivity is not a concern. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell contains >40 atoms, so that check is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LaTaO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is favorable because very close Sr₂HoTaO₆ and Sr₂YTaO₆ double perovskites are reported from standard high-temperature solid-state routes [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3]. The most important enabling factor is the robust A₂BB′O₆ perovskite-derived framework with strong TaO₆ octahedra, which appears repeatedly in the closest analogues [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3]. The main limiting factor is kinetic: Ta-containing oxide precursors are refractory, and analogues often need high temperature, long dwell times, or repeated grinding [CS1/SS1, CS3/SS2]. Flux or Pechini chemistry may reduce kinetic barriers [CS4, SS4], but these routes introduce extra phase-control uncertainty.
