### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull of 0.0310 eV/atom falls in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic window, so F1 is scored 94. This is supported by TS analogues such as ZrBr₂ at E_hull = 0.0319 eV/atom [TS2], ScCl at 0.0214 eV/atom [TS5], and Sc₇Cl₁₂ at 0.0145 eV/atom [TS1], all of which have reported synthetic routes. SS also supports the feasibility of near-hull reduced layered Zr halides such as ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ [SS1, SS5]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for reduced Hf/Zr mixed halides and is treated as neutral for F1.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which is favorable, but requires sealed Ta, volatile tetrahalides, vacuum/inert handling, and air-sensitive halides, as in related ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ CVT routes [SS1, SS5]. The long gradient transport step is a practical severity factor because SS1 reports 4–10 weeks and SS5 reports 4 weeks. CS1 also shows sealed ampoule bromine chemistry can be done, but with corrosive volatile bromine, reinforcing the handling penalty [CS1].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic score is moderate because vapor transport can bypass some diffusion barriers, as demonstrated for ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ [SS1, SS5]. However, SS1 reports multiple phases/polytypes and slow equilibration in reduced Zr chloride systems, indicating that nucleation and ordering are not trivial. The target’s Hf/Zr and Br/Cl ordering adds an additional kinetic uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses temperatures comparable to CS2 and TS3/TS5, so it avoids ultra-high temperature and high pressure [CS2, TS3, TS5]. The penalties arise from sealed-Ta processing, volatile halide precursors, drybox handling, and the multistep nature of grinding/pelletizing/sealing/annealing [CS2, TS3]. SS3 and TS5 show that reduced metal halides often need weeks to months, making the route operationally severe even without high pressure.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct solid-state reduction has poorer kinetics than CVT because Hf and Zr metals are refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the long reaction times reported for reduced Sc/Zr halides [SS3, TS5]. CS2 shows that ZrCl₄ + Zr powder can form reduced Zr chloride compounds at 800 °C, which supports feasibility [CS2]. The main uncertainty is whether mixed Hf/Zr/Br/Cl ordering can be achieved before competing binary or ternary halides form [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The H₂ route is not high-pressure, but it requires reactive hydrogen, sealed Ta, air-sensitive reduced halides, and potentially volatile bromides/chlorides [SS4, TS2]. SS4 reports 750 °C for 20 days with H₂ at 1 atm, which is operationally demanding but less thermally severe than >1000 °C synthesis. The “hydrothermal” label in TS2 conflicts with the evidence snippet describing sealed Ta/H₂ chemistry, so the sealed-Ta/H₂ interpretation is weighted more heavily [TS2].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Hydrogen can enable reduced Zr chloride formation through disproportionation, as shown for K₂Zr₇Cl₁₈-type phases [SS4]. However, those products contain alkali cations and cluster/complex frameworks rather than the target neutral 2D HfZr₃BrCl₇ sheet [SS4], so structural selectivity is a major concern. TS2 supports similar thermodynamic territory, but not the exact Hf/Zr mixed-anion layered structure.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has similar Hf and Zr cations, so the cation radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described target is an ordered edge-sharing octahedral 2D sheet rather than a highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, so the connectivity check is also not triggered [Target structure]. SS1 and SS5 demonstrate that related 2D reduced Zr halide sheets crystallize, lowering amorphization concern, though triclinic anion/cation ordering remains uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfZr₃BrCl₇ is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0310 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized reduced halides in TS [TS1, TS2, TS5]. The strongest support comes from SS analogues: molybdenite-like ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ are 2D reduced Zr halides made by sealed-tube vapor transport [SS1, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is use of volatile metal tetrahalides in sealed Ta under a temperature gradient, which can overcome slow Hf/Zr solid diffusion [SS1, SS5]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: reduced group-IV halides show multiple stoichiometries, polytypes, intergrowths, and slow equilibration [SS1, SS3, TS5]. Mixed Hf/Zr and Br/Cl ordering adds extra configurational complexity beyond the binary ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ analogues [Reasonable Inference].
