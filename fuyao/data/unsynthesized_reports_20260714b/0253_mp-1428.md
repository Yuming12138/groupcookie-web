### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target sits at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high end of the 90–100 score bin by the provided rubric [Target]. Thermodynamically similar compounds around 0.055–0.059 eV/atom have reported syntheses by conventional solid-state methods [TS-1; TS-2], while NiGe2 at 0.085 eV/atom requires high pressure and is metastable [CS-2; TS-5]. This supports feasibility but not guaranteed equilibrium stability; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves monoclinic Ni5Ge3 is the ambient ground-state polymorph.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc-melting/annealing is supported by CoGe synthesis under Ar with subsequent annealing and quenching [CS-5]. The severity score is reduced because arc melting is very high temperature, the route is multi-step, and Ge retention benefits from sealed annealing [CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports arc-melting for Ni5Ge3, so TS is treated as neutral for this route-specific equipment choice.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are helped by liquid-phase alloying during arc melting, as CS-5 uses arc-melted Ge intermetallic alloys [CS-5]. However, long annealing in CS-5 indicates ordering/homogenization is not instantaneous [CS-5]. Structural reconstruction from a melt is substantial, so the kinetics are moderate rather than easy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed-ampoule route is supported by SS-2, where Ni–Ga–Ge alloys are heated in evacuated quartz ampoules up to 1350 °C and quenched [SS-2]. Deductions come from >1000 °C temperature, vacuum/sealed-tube handling, possible volatile Ge control, and multi-step heat treatment [SS-2; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives general support for elemental germanide synthesis but insufficient direct evidence for this exact staged ampoule program, so CS is neutral for detailed severity.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
High temperatures in SS-2 improve diffusion and can partially melt/react Ge-containing alloys [SS-2; Reasonable Inference]. The need for multiple heat treatments and quenching in SS-2 indicates significant ordering and phase-control constraints [SS-2]. Kinetics are therefore slightly below Route 1 despite similar chemistry.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The best route has low condition severity because TS-1 and TS-2 show 800–1000 °C Ar solid-state syntheses without high pressure or vacuum [TS-1; TS-2]. The main deduction is multi-step processing with intermediate grinding, explicitly reported in TS-1/TS-2. Insufficient evidence from CS alone confirms this exact low-temperature route for binary Ni5Ge3, so CS is neutral for the severity estimate.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Elemental Ni and Ge have modest precursor activity, but thermodynamically similar Ni–Ge-containing materials form at 800–1000 °C with grinding [TS-1; TS-2]. The target’s Ni–Ni and Ni–Ge distances are close to those in TS-1 Mg(NiGe)6, supporting manageable structural rearrangement [Target; TS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether binary Ni5Ge3 orders cleanly or instead partitions into nearby Ni–Ge phases [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for chemically similar NiGe2 at 5.5 GPa and 700 °C [CS-2; TS-5]. The large penalty is the >5 GPa pressure requirement and pressure-cell processing [CS-2; TS-5]. SS gives insufficient evidence for requiring pressure in structurally similar compounds, so SS is neutral for this route’s necessity.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
NiGe2 forms rapidly at 700 °C under 5.5 GPa [CS-2; TS-5], suggesting high pressure can overcome some stability barriers. However, TS-4 shows high-pressure NiSi can have narrow phase windows and minor-phase issues [TS-4]. The kinetics are therefore only moderate-to-low because diffusion length is limited in pressure capsules and structural reconstruction may be metastable [TS-4; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 5.**  
CVT is supported by Fe2Ge crystallization using iodine in evacuated quartz ampoules at 500–850 °C [CS-1]. Deductions arise from vacuum/sealed-tube handling, volatile iodine, multi-step pre-reaction/transport, and iodine toxicity/corrosivity [CS-1; Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for CVT transport of Ni5Ge3, so they are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 5.**  
Vapor transport can enhance crystal growth kinetics relative to bulk diffusion, as CS-1 directly crystallizes a germanide by iodine CVT [CS-1]. The main kinetic uncertainty is composition control because Ni and Ge iodides may transport at different rates [CS-1; Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore attractive for crystal growth but less reliable for first-pass bulk phase discovery.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a dense intermetallic with high Ni/Ge coordination and no reported face-sharing plus edge/corner/vertex mixture; only edge/corner-sharing cuboctahedra are stated [Target]. Size mismatch is low by metallic-radius reasoning, and the formula unit is small [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly addresses amorphization, so those sources are neutral except for showing related crystalline intermetallics are readily obtained [CS-5; SS-2; TS-1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni5Ge3 is likely a slightly metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The analogues support synthesis well: Ni–Ge and Ni–Si/Ge-containing compounds are reported by solid-state sintering, arc-melting, CVT, and high-pressure routes [CS-2; CS-5; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2; TS-5]. The most important enabling factor is that similar Ni–Ge coordination networks form at accessible temperatures of ~800–1000 °C under Ar [Target; TS-1; TS-2]. The main limiting factor is phase competition in the Ni–Ge binary system because the target is not clearly on the hull and some chemically similar Ni–Ge phases require high pressure or show metastability [Target; CS-2; TS-5]. Overall, conventional Ar solid-state sintering with grinding is the best first route, while arc-melting and high-pressure synthesis are useful backups [CS-5; TS-1; TS-2; TS-5].
