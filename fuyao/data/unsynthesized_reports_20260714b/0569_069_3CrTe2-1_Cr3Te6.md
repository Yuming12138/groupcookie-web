### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0290 eV/atom, which is within the commonly synthesizable near-hull regime and maps to a 90–100 score by the provided rubric. CS evidence is favorable because Cr₂Te₃ is on hull and VTe₂, an MTe₂ layered analogue, is only 0.0185 eV/atom above hull (CS1, CS3, CS4). SS evidence is also favorable because several Cr–Te octahedral phases are stable on hull, including CuCr₂Te₄, AgCr₂Te₄, Cr₂Te₃, KCr₅Te₈, and CrAuTe₄ (SS1–SS5). TS evidence strongly supports low metastability because the nearest thermodynamic analogues lie around 0.014–0.029 eV/atom above hull (TS1–TS5), but the remaining uncertainty is whether binary CrTe₂ is kinetically trapped against Cr₂Te₃ + Te or other Cr–Te phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the best analogue VTe₂ is made in evacuated quartz ampoules at 1000 K for 1 week with slow cooling (CS3, TS2), which avoids extreme temperature or pressure but requires vacuum and sealed Te handling. Kinetics are less favorable because elemental Cr is refractory and dense [Reasonable Inference], so long reaction times are expected; VTe₂ required a full week and still showed Te impurity/crystal-growth difficulty (CS3, TS2). The strongest support is structural: VTe₂ has the same edge-sharing MTe₆ layered motif as the target (CS3, TS2). The main uncertainty is the phase boundary between CrTe₂ and Cr₂Te₃, since Cr₂Te₃ is a stable chemically close phase (CS1/SS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Bridgman/self-flux

Condition severity is slightly worse than direct solid-state because Bridgman growth may require higher homogenization temperatures and controlled gradients [Reasonable Inference], though the method is directly demonstrated for Cr₂Te₃ single crystals (CS1/SS3). Kinetically, melt/self-flux transport should be faster than solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], which justifies a higher F3 score. The limitation is phase selection: Bridgman evidence is for Cr₂Te₃ rather than CrTe₂ (CS1/SS3), so Te-rich conditions and careful cooling are needed [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact CrTe₂ melting behavior, so that part is treated as uncertain rather than definitive.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT

CVT has moderate condition severity because TS1 demonstrates evacuated silica-tube transport at 700/750 °C for 8 days with I₂, and CS5 demonstrates bromine transport for layered Mo/W tellurides. Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport avoids slow bulk diffusion and can grow crystals at temperatures similar to or below solid-state synthesis temperatures [Reasonable Inference based on TS1/CS5]. The route is transferable because layered tellurides are explicitly grown by vapor transport (TS1, CS5), while the target’s MTe₂ structural motif is supported by VTe₂ (CS3/TS2). The principal uncertainty is that no CrTe₂-specific CVT example is provided, so chromium halide transport chemistry may require optimization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Salt flux

Flux growth is supported by CrSe₂, the closest structural chalcogen analogue, which was crystallized phase-pure in LiCl/KCl flux at 780 °C for 165 h under vacuum with slow cooling (TS3). The condition score is reduced because the route is multi-step and requires sealed vacuum tubes, binary precursors, flux handling, and slow cooling (TS3). Kinetically it is attractive because liquid flux improves mass transport and can crystallize refractory Cr chalcogenides at moderate temperatures (TS3). The uncertainty is whether the Se-based flux chemistry transfers cleanly to Te; Te volatility and Cr–Te competing phases may be more severe [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation radius mismatch does not apply; mixed-cation disorder seen in some SS analogues is not intrinsic to CrTe₂ (SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5). The structure is a simple layered CrTe₂ network rather than a large complex cell, and the provided target description indicates edge-sharing CrTe₆ octahedra only. Mixed edge/corner/face connectivity appears in Cr₂Te₃ (CS1/SS3), but the target does not show that connectivity. Therefore, amorphization risk is low, though thin-film or rapid-quench synthesis could still create amorphous Cr–Te if crystallization temperature is too low [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CrTe₂ at E_hull = 0.029 eV/atom is best interpreted as a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a deeply metastable compound. The strongest support comes from VTe₂, which has the same layered edge-sharing MTe₆ topology and is synthesized by direct evacuated-ampoule reaction (CS3/TS2), and from CrSe₂ flux growth as the direct chalcogen analogue (TS3). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high Te chemical potential while using sealed-tube, flux, or vapor-transport conditions that compensate Te volatility (CS3/TS2, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is competition with stable Cr-rich tellurides such as Cr₂Te₃, which is both chemically and structurally close and readily grown by Bridgman (CS1/SS3). Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with CVT or flux growth preferred for crystals and direct solid-state reaction preferred for initial powder screening.
