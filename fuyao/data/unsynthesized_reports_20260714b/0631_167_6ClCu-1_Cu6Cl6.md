### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0160 eV/atom, which is within the normally accessible metastability window ≤0.05 eV/atom, so the score is high. TS analogues with similar hull energies, such as CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS3]. SS also supports accessibility of slightly metastable halide/chalcogenide phases, including CuHgSCl and CuHgSBr [SS1, SS2]. CS is mixed: CuS is stable and directly synthesized [CS1], while ZnS polytypes include metastable phases with phase-control issues [CS2–CS4].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The sealed solid-state route uses moderate temperatures comparable to CuHgSCl at 573 K [SS1, TS1], CuHgSI at 553 K [TS3], and RbCuCl3 at 450/300 °C [TS5], so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main penalties are evacuated sealed-tube operation [SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3, TS5], volatile/corrosive chloride control inferred from AuCl and CuCl2 systems [SS4, TS5], and CuCl/CuCl2 sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives only indirect support via sealed-tube CuS synthesis [CS1] and insufficient direct evidence for CuCl.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binary halide precursors should be moderately reactive, and analogous halide/chalcogenide reactions proceed at low-to-moderate temperatures in sealed ampoules [SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3, TS5]. However, many analogues require long anneals of around 2 weeks [SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3, TS5], suggesting slow ordering or phase equilibration. The target is not just CuCl formation but a specific 2D polymorph, so structural reconstruction/polymorph selection lowers the kinetic score [Reasonable Inference; supported by CS2–CS4 polytype issues].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The hydrothermal route avoids >1000 °C and GPa pressure; the close analogue uses 670 K for 24 h in sealed ampoules/autoclaves [SS2, TS2]. Deductions come from sealed volatile/corrosive HX medium, multiple handling/cooling/washing steps, and the hazards of concentrated acid/Cu(I) chloride [SS2, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient hydrothermal evidence and is treated as neutral.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal dissolution/reprecipitation gives high precursor mobility, and the CuHgSX analogue forms high-quality crystals quantitatively in 24 h with controlled cooling [SS2, TS2]. This supports strong kinetic accessibility for Cu–Cl species under chloride-rich conditions. The uncertainty is polymorph selection: none of CS/SS/TS reports the exact Pmn2_1 CuCl sheet, and CS ZnS polytypes show that layered/stacking variants can be difficult to isolate [CS2–CS4].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT uses relatively low temperatures, based on AuCl transport at 247–280 °C [SS4]. The route is penalized for Cl2/reactive vapor atmosphere, sealed volatile halide transport, multistep gradient operation, and toxic/corrosive chlorine handling [SS4]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for CVT and is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport is kinetically favorable for crystal growth because AuCl single crystals are grown by transport over about one week [SS4], and ZnS vapor methods access multiple polytypes [CS2–CS4]. However, phase control is uncertain: AuCl is reported as metastable and requiring low-temperature handling [SS4], while ZnS polytypes have narrow/reproducibility-sensitive phase windows [CS2–CS4]. Composition control in Cu–Cl vapor depends on chlorine fugacity, making the route less robust [Reasonable Inference from SS4].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Cu, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger [target structure]. The described cell is small, with three inequivalent Cu and three inequivalent Cl sites, and the bonding is a relatively simple 3-coordinate sheet rather than a mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing polyhedral network [target structure]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization evidence for CuCl, so the score relies primarily on the target structural description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for amorphization tendency is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CuCl phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than a clearly established ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0160 eV/atom rather than zero [target data]. The thermodynamic analogue set supports accessibility of phases in this metastability range, since several TS compounds near 0.02–0.03 eV/atom are synthesized [TS1–TS5]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chloride-rich sealed/hydrothermal chemistry using CuCl and concentrated HX, which gives high-quality crystals in CuHgSX analogues [SS2, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature, high-chloride-activity crystallization that may avoid relaxation to the common CuCl polymorph [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2]. The key limiting factor is polymorph control, because ZnS polytypes and AuCl show that structurally related metastable halide/chalcogenide phases can be sensitive to growth and cooling conditions [CS2–CS4, SS4].
