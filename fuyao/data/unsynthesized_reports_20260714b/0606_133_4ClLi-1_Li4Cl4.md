### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.011 eV atom⁻¹ [Target], which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV atom⁻¹ high-synthesizability range. TS analogues with similar low hull energies are reported as synthesized, including RbLiCl₂ at 0.0047 eV atom⁻¹ [TS1], K₂ZnCl₄ at 0.0146 eV atom⁻¹ [TS2], and Na₃GdBr₆ at 0.0156 eV atom⁻¹ [TS3]. SS also supports this because RbLiCl₂ is both structurally similar and low-hull [SS2]. CS provides only partial support: ScCl is a low-hull 2D chloride but difficult to synthesize [CS2], while other CS entries are chemically less relevant to LiCl; insufficient direct CS evidence for binary LiCl polymorphism.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits dry sealed-ampoule complexity from Cs–Li and Rb–Li chloride syntheses [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1]. The temperature is not severe compared with high-temperature solid-state synthesis, but vacuum/dry handling, sealed ampoules, precursor drying, and moisture sensitivity all reduce the score [SS1, SS4]. CS gives limited direct guidance for LiCl, although LiP shows that sealed inert solid-state treatment of Li compounds is feasible [CS1]; insufficient CS evidence for LiCl polymorph control. Remaining uncertainty is whether melt–annealing pure LiCl can overcome the strong tendency toward the common bulk LiCl structure **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, LiCl should have favorable ion mobility under near-melting or molten-salt conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**, and Li-containing chloride analogues crystallize at moderate temperature [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1]. The limiting kinetic factor is not diffusion but structural reconstruction into the layered P-3m1 sheet, which no analogue directly demonstrates; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for that exact transformation. The similar Li–Cl bond distances in SS analogues support local coordination plausibility [SS1, SS2, SS4]. Long anneals and cooling sensitivity in Cs–Li chlorides imply that phase selection may be slow or history-dependent [SS1, SS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from moderate temperatures and established dry chloride flux/ampoule methods [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1]. The main severity deductions are sealed-tube operation, multi-step drying/mixing/heating/cooling, and hygroscopic chloride handling [SS1, SS4]. Unlike CS2 ScCl, no two-month high-temperature treatment or tantalum tube is required for the SS/TS Li-chloride analogues [CS2 vs SS1/SS2/SS4/TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is flux removal without dissolving or transforming the target **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth gives the best kinetic outlook because chloride melts promote rapid ion transport and crystallization, as shown by RbLiCl₂ and Cs–Li chloride syntheses [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1]. The local LiCl₄ coordination motifs in SS analogues are close to the target’s LiCl₄ trigonal pyramidal environment [SS1, SS2, SS4], although the exact topology is different. Structural reconstruction is therefore scored moderate rather than high because the known analogues are ternary salts and the desired product is a binary metastable polymorph; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for pure layered LiCl. The route is still preferred because it combines high precursor activity with experimentally validated chloride-crystal-growth conditions [SS2, TS1, SS5].

#### F2 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition is speculative because no CS/SS/TS source reports vapor deposition of LiCl or related Li-chloride sheets; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives neutral source support. The severity is dominated by vacuum operation, possible volatile LiCl flux, and need for anhydrous handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its temperature could be low compared with bulk routes **[Reasonable Inference]**, but equipment and stoichiometry control make it challenging. The score is therefore not low for thermal severity but reduced for operational complexity.

#### F3 — Route 3

The thin-film route is kinetically attractive for a 2D target because epitaxy can bypass bulk nucleation constraints [Target; Reasonable Inference]. However, there is no confirming CS/SS/TS analogue, so crystallization pathway and composition-control scores remain moderate to low. LiCl volatility/hygroscopicity may complicate stoichiometric film formation **[Reasonable Inference]**. This route is most useful as a metastable-phase stabilization strategy if bulk flux experiments fail **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The unit cell appears simple and binary, with no evidence of >40 atoms [Target]. The main amorphization risk is mixed corner- and edge-sharing LiCl₄/ClLi₄ polyhedral connectivity [Target], but structurally similar crystalline Li–chlorides also contain connected LiCl₄ units [SS1, SS2, SS4]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the 2D sheet topology can nucleate before transforming to a denser LiCl form **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state metastable phase rather than a deep metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.011 eV atom⁻¹ [Target]. The SS and TS analogues strongly support synthesis of Li-containing chlorides from dry binary chlorides in sealed ampoules or flux/melt conditions [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1], while CS gives weaker but relevant evidence that 2D chloride compounds can exist [CS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of chloride salts in molten/flux media, which is supported by RbLiCl₂ and Cs–Li chloride syntheses [SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selection: none of the CS/SS/TS sources directly shows pure binary P-3m1 LiCl, so competing ordinary LiCl or ternary flux phases may dominate **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target appears plausible but will likely require careful dry processing, cooling-rate screening, and possibly epitaxial or flux stabilization.
