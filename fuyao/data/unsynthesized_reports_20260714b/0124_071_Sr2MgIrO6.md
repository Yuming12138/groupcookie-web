### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is given as 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom boundary, so the assigned F1 score is 90. CS supports this because Sr₂MgReO₆ is nearly stable at 0.0042 eV/atom and Sr₂MgOsO₆ is on the hull (CS2, CS3). TS gives a slightly less favorable but still experimentally relevant signal because several thermodynamic neighbors are 0.057–0.090 eV/atom and have reported syntheses (TS2–TS5). SS gives insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence for Sr₂MgIrO₆, so it is treated as neutral for this factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 flux.**  
Condition severity is moderate because TS1 and SS2–SS4 use only 650–700 °C and no high pressure, but sealed Ag tubes, caustic hydroxide flux, and possible oxidizers add handling complexity. Kinetics are favorable because flux chemistry provides liquid-mediated transport, directly shown for Ln₂MgIrO₆ in TS1 and Ba–Ir oxides in SS2–SS4. The main uncertainty is whether the oxidizing flux can reliably stabilize Sr₂MgIrO₆ with Ir⁶⁺ rather than forming lower-valent Sr–Ir phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 sealed solid-state.**  
Condition severity is high because CS1, CS2, and CS4 use sealed tubes, controlled Ar/vacuum conditions, long heating, and in some cases high-pressure oxygen post-anneals. Kinetics are limited by refractory MgO and IrO₂/Ir diffusion, consistent with long reaction times and repeated treatments in CS1/CS4 and related ceramic analogues. The strength of this route is structural transferability from Sr₂MgReO₆ and Sr₂MgOsO₆ (CS2, CS3), but the oxidation-state requirement for Ir⁶⁺ remains uncertain.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 high pressure.**  
Condition severity is the worst because CS3 uses 6 GPa, 1500 °C, Pt capsules, glovebox handling, and rapid quenching. Kinetically, high temperature and pressure can overcome slow diffusion, and CS3 demonstrates ordered Sr₂MgOsO₆ formation in only 1 h. This route is likely the most robust if the target is pressure-stabilized or if Ir⁶⁺ requires high oxygen activity, but specialized equipment severely limits practicality.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 ceramic air.**  
Condition severity is moderate because SS5, TS4, and TS5 show air-fired Ir oxides can be synthesized with standard furnaces and crucibles. Kinetics are poor to moderate because repeated grinding and long firings are often required in SS5 and TS4/TS5, and dense MgO/IrO₂ are slow-reacting. The biggest uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Sr₂MgIrO₆ or Ir⁶⁺ Sr–Mg–Ir double perovskite formation in ordinary air.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large A/B cation size contrast, as indicated by Sr–O distances of 2.56–3.11 Å versus Mg–O ≈2.07 Å and Ir–O ≈1.94–1.95 Å in the target description, so C1 is triggered. The target framework is a corner-sharing double perovskite rather than a highly mixed edge/face/vertex network, so C3 is not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a >40 atom unit cell in the target, so C2 is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgIrO₆ is likely a borderline-stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable cutoff. The CS analogues strongly support the Sr₂MgB′O₆ double-perovskite framework through Sr₂MgReO₆ and Sr₂MgOsO₆, while TS and SS support Mg–Ir and Ir oxide crystallization by flux and ceramic methods. The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential, because Sr₂MgIrO₆ charge balance implies high-valent Ir⁶⁺ **[Reasonable Inference]**, and SS2–SS3 plus CS3 show oxidizing additives can stabilize high-valent 5d oxides. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition or reduction to lower-valent Sr–Ir/Mg–Ir oxides, since CS2 reports impurity sensitivity and TS1 flux analogues may correspond to lower Ir valence. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with oxidizing flux growth as the best first attempt and high-pressure oxidizing synthesis as the most forceful backup.
