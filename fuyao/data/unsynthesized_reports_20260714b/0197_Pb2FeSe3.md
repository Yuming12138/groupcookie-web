### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range; this is direct target evidence. TS analogues strongly support that related layered or complex selenides can be ground-state phases, including K₂Bi₈Se₁₃, MnBi₂Se₄, Bi₂Se₃, KNb(AgSe₂)₂, and YbCuPbSe₃, all with E_hull = 0 in TS1–TS5. CS and SS also contain near-ground-state Pb/Bi/Se and Fe/selenide examples, including CS1/SS3 and SS2. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS specifies the Fe–Pb–Se ternary phase diagram around FePb₂Se₃.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity — Route 1

Route 1 uses sealed evacuated or inert ampoules, which is supported by CS1/SS3, SS2, and TS5. It avoids >1000 °C if run at 700–800 °C, but it requires a sealed system because Se and Pb are volatile/toxic at high temperature [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed conditions in CS1/SS3 and TS5]. Multi-step staged heating is modeled on CS1/SS3 and is needed to avoid rapid Se pressure buildup [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS determines the exact decomposition temperature of FePb₂Se₃, so the thermal window remains uncertain.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics — Route 1

Elemental precursors are well supported by CS1/SS3, SS2, and TS5, but Fe metal has relatively slow diffusion and high melting point [Reasonable Inference]. Pb and Se can provide liquid/vapor-mediated transport at the proposed temperatures [Reasonable Inference], but Fe incorporation may still be sluggish. SS2 reports FeSe impurity in a Fe-containing selenide, indicating competing binary formation and imperfect kinetics. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows direct formation of FePb₂Se₃ by dry sintering.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity — Route 2

Route 2 is not extremely hot because CS3/TS2 obtains MnBi₂Se₄ crystals by cooling 800→400 °C, and SS4/TS1 uses even lower-temperature polychalcogenide flux for layered selenides. It still requires sealed evacuated tubes and toxic/volatile Pb/Se handling, consistent with sealed chalcogenide syntheses in CS3/TS2 and CS1/SS3. The route is multi-step because it includes loading, sealing, heating, slow cooling, and flux removal, as in CS3/TS2. If alkali-polychalcogenide flux is used instead of LiCl–RbCl, air sensitivity increases as indicated by SS4/TS1.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics — Route 2

Flux growth receives the highest practical kinetic score because CS3/TS2 explicitly reports good crystals from a transition-metal selenide system using LiCl–RbCl flux. The molten flux should reduce diffusion limitations relative to pellet sintering [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling promotes ordered layered crystallization as in CS3/TS2 and SS4/TS1. However, CS3/TS2 also reports stoichiometry/defect problems and difficulty preparing stoichiometric MnBi₂Se₄, suggesting FePb₂Se₃ may have a narrow growth window. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly identifies FePb₂Se₃ solubility in LiCl–RbCl.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity — Route 3

CVT uses sealed evacuated ampoules and a temperature gradient, directly supported by SS1 and CS4/SS5. It avoids >1000 °C but adds iodine, which is corrosive/toxic, and requires volatile transport chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The route is multi-step because a prereacted charge is likely needed before transport, as in SS1 and CS4/SS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS demonstrates stable iodine transport without forming Fe/Pb iodide byproducts.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics — Route 3

Vapor transport can bypass slow solid-state cation diffusion, and SS1 demonstrates I₂-assisted transport of a structurally similar Pb-containing selenide material at 800–850 °C. CS4/SS5 supports temperature-gradient crystal growth for Bi–Se–Br compounds. The limitation is chemical specificity: Fe–Pb–Se transport equilibria are not given by CS/SS/TS, so transport may fractionate Pb, Fe, and Se [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is good, but practical uncertainty is high.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity — Route 4

Route 4 is most severe because melt/Bridgman-style synthesis likely requires temperatures above 1000 °C, similar to SS2 and TS5. It requires sealed evacuated ampoules because Se/Pb are volatile at these temperatures, as implied by sealed-tube practice in SS2 and TS5. Multi-step slow cooling and annealing are supported by SS2 and TS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows FePb₂Se₃ remains stable at the proposed melt temperature.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics — Route 4

Melting improves mixing and is supported for CuFe₂InSe₄ in SS2 and Bi₂Se₃ in TS3. However, the target’s low-symmetry layered structure may not crystallize directly from a melt without long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS2 reports a significant FeSe impurity, indicating Fe-containing selenides can suffer from competing binary formation even after long anneal. Thus kinetics are moderate but phase selectivity is risky.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target combines small tetrahedral Fe centers with much larger Pb–Se coordination environments, so cation size mismatch likely triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is ordered and crystalline in the target description, and related layered chalcogenides are crystalline in CS1/SS3, CS3/TS2, SS4/TS1, and TS5, which supports a moderate rather than high amorphization risk. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms or contains simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing networks. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether rapid quenching or non-equilibrium deposition would disorder the Pb/Fe sublattices.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FePb₂Se₃ is likely a **ground-state or very near-ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are also hull-stable. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through sealed-tube and flux routes: CS1/SS3 and TS5 support Pb-containing selenide solid-state chemistry, while CS3/TS2 supports flux growth of transition-metal selenides. The most critical enabling factor is sealed-ampoule chalcogenide chemistry, which maintains Se activity and allows Pb/Se vapor or flux-assisted transport. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against FeSe, PbSe, and possibly metallic Pb/Fe residues, as suggested by impurity issues in SS2 and phase-control concerns in CS3/TS2. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful temperature programming and stoichiometry control.
