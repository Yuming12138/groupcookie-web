### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high thermodynamic score [Target]. Chemically similar phases are also close to the hull, including RbFe₂Se₃ at 0.0249 eV/atom and SrZrSe₃ at 0.0271 eV/atom, while Sr₂GeSe₄ is on the hull [CS-1, CS-4, CS-5]. Thermodynamic analogues in TS are mostly on the hull, further supporting stability, although they are chemically less direct than CS [TS-1–TS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee a broad kinetic stability window or easy phase selectivity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 solid-state severity

Route 1 uses temperatures similar to BaFe₂Se₃ and SrZrSe₃ sealed-tube syntheses, typically 750–950 °C, which are moderate for chalcogenide solid-state chemistry [CS-2, CS-4]. The severity deductions come from evacuated ampoules, volatile Se, air/moisture-sensitive Sr/SrSe, and multistep grinding/reheating [CS-2, CS-4]. SS-2 confirms that related Ba–Fe–Se phases can require vacuum sealing and high-temperature holds up to 1050 °C [SS-2]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct solid-state Sr–Fe–Se severity; TS is treated as neutral for this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 1 solid-state kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are only moderate because Fe is a dense, high-melting precursor and Sr/Fe/Se must reorganize into an ordered mixed-polyhedral Pnma structure [CS-2; Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 reports intermediate regrinding and multiple overnight heatings until BaFe₂Se₃ became single phase, indicating diffusion and phase-selection limitations [CS-2]. CS-4 also reports phase-control issues in SrZrSe₃ due to competing amorphous Zr–Se material, suggesting Sr selenide reactions can be sensitive [CS-4]. Insufficient evidence from TS for the specific Sr–Fe–Se solid-state kinetic pathway.

#### F2 — Route 2 melt/Bridgman severity

Route 2 avoids many solid-state diffusion penalties but still requires sealed high-temperature ampoules because Se is volatile and Sr/SrSe is air-sensitive [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 used vacuum-sealed ampoules and controlled cooling for RbFe₂Se₃, while CS-3 reports Bridgman growth of BaFe₂Se₃ from high-purity elements [CS-1, CS-3]. No high pressure or >1400 °C processing is indicated by CS/SS/TS, so those deductions are not applied [CS-1, CS-3]. The main uncertainty is whether Sr₂FeSe₃ melts congruently; there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on this exact phase relation.

#### F3 — Route 2 melt/Bridgman kinetics

Melt growth improves transport substantially because cations and Se can mix through liquid or partially molten phases rather than relying solely on solid diffusion [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. Direct evidence for Fe–Se chalcogenide melt growth is strong: RbFe₂Se₃ and BaFe₂Se₃ crystals were obtained by slow cooling/Bridgman methods [CS-1, CS-3]. Structural reconstruction is still not trivial because the target Sr₂FeSe₃ stoichiometry differs from RbFe₂Se₃/BaFe₂Se₃ analogues [Target; CS-1, CS-3]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Bridgman growth of Sr–Fe–Se specifically and are therefore neutral for this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 3 flux severity

Flux synthesis uses lower effective reaction temperatures in some cases, such as 450–500 °C for KTa/KAg selenide flux analogues and 800–870 °C for K₂NbCuSe₄ [SS-1, TS-5]. However, it requires reactive alkali polyselenide fluxes, evacuated sealed tubes, glovebox handling, and post-synthesis flux removal [SS-1, SS-3, TS-4, TS-5]. The route is therefore conditionally moderate-to-challenging despite favorable transport [SS-1, TS-4]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Sr–Fe–Se flux synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity estimate.

#### F3 — Route 3 flux kinetics

Reactive fluxes provide high precursor activity and liquid-phase transport, as explicitly described for alkali-metal selenide fluxes acting as both reagent and reaction medium [TS-4]. SS-1 and TS-5 show that crystalline selenides can grow under flux conditions with slow programmed cooling [SS-1, TS-5]. The kinetic uncertainty is chemical selectivity: alkali fluxes may stabilize K/Cs-containing products or dissolve/attack Sr–Fe–Se components rather than crystallize Sr₂FeSe₃ [SS-1, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for direct transfer of flux methods to Sr₂FeSe₃.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains both large Sr coordination polyhedra and smaller FeSe₄ tetrahedra, giving a large cation-size and coordination mismatch [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Its reported connectivity includes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, satisfying the mixed-connectivity amorphization-risk criterion [Target]. The unit cell is likely not above 40 atoms by analogy to Pnma BaFe₂Se₃ with 24 atoms per conventional cell [CS-2], so only two of three checks trigger. CS-4’s report of competitive amorphous Zr–Se formation in SrZrSe₃ synthesis reinforces caution for Sr selenides, though it is not direct evidence for Sr₂FeSe₃ [CS-4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂FeSe₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Fe–Se alkaline-earth/alkali analogues, especially BaFe₂Se₃ and RbFe₂Se₃, which can be made by sealed-tube solid-state or melt/Bridgman methods [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated stability and crystallization of FeSe₄ tetrahedral selenide frameworks under sealed high-temperature conditions [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under volatile Se and air-sensitive Sr/SrSe conditions [CS-2, CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth is plausible for single crystals but is less directly supported chemically and may introduce alkali-containing competing phases [SS-1, TS-4, TS-5; Reasonable Inference].
