### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the ≤0.05 eV/atom favorable threshold in the supplied scoring rubric. Close chemical and structural analogues In2Ni21B6, Sc2Ni21B6, and Ni20Al3B6 are reported with E_hull = 0.0, showing that this Fm-3m Ni-rich boride family contains stable compounds (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS3). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull near or above the target, such as Mn3Ni20P6 at 0.0568 eV/atom and Rh2B at 0.0745 eV/atom, have reported syntheses, supporting accessibility of slightly metastable phases (TS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS prove nearby compositions, not the exact Ni-richer In(Ni11B3)2 phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is based on the direct In2Ni21B6 analogue prepared at 950 °C for 300 h under vacuum with controlled cooling (CS1, SS1). The temperature is moderate for borides, but vacuum sealing and long duration impose a significant penalty (CS1, SS1). In volatility at 950 °C is a [Reasonable Inference] from the need to seal an In-containing reaction and from basic In behavior, not explicitly quantified in CS/SS/TS. TS gives insufficient route-specific evidence for this exact route and is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The long 300 h anneal required for In2Ni21B6 indicates slow ordering/diffusion in the In–Ni–B framework (CS1, SS1). Elemental Ni and B powders are lower-activity precursors than soluble salts or flux media, and B is refractory, so the kinetic score is only moderate ([Reasonable Inference], supported by CS1/SS1 duration). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the analogue is nearly identical in chemistry and symmetry, but the target stoichiometry differs and must reorder Ni/In site occupancy (CS1, SS1). Insufficient evidence from TS on In–Ni–B diffusion kinetics; TS neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Arc-melting plus annealing is supported by Sc2Ni21B6 at 950 °C for 14 days in evacuated silica/Ta containment and by Zr2Co21B6-type samples prepared by arc melting in Ar with optional vacuum annealing (CS2/SS2; CS4). Severity comes mainly from controlled atmosphere, sealed annealing, and multi-step processing, not from high sustained annealing temperature. For the In-containing target, In loss during arc melting is a significant inferred risk requiring rapid melting and mass checks ([Reasonable Inference], supported indirectly by sealed In–Ni–B route CS1/SS1). TS does not provide a specific In arc-melt signal and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Arc-melting improves homogenization compared with simple powder sintering, and this is demonstrated for Sc2Ni21B6-like compositions (CS2, SS2). However, the reported 14-day anneal shows that crystallographic ordering and phase equilibration are still kinetically demanding (CS2, SS2). The route should transfer reasonably because several Ni-rich borides tolerate melt/anneal processing, but In volatility makes the target more delicate than Sc–Ni–B analogues ([Reasonable Inference], CS2/SS2 versus CS1/SS1).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route is inferred from Ni20Al3B6, where elements were heated to 1200–1300 °C under Ar, slowly cooled, and quenched, with excess Al acting as flux (CS3, SS3). For the target, using In self-flux is a [Reasonable Inference], so severity includes T >1000 °C, multi-step cooling/flux removal, and volatile In containment. The route avoids high pressure and avoids vacuum if run under sealed Ar, which keeps F2 somewhat better than Routes 1–2. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because a liquid metal flux can increase transport and allow slow crystallization rather than relying on solid-state diffusion; this is supported by the Al-flux observation for Ni20Al3B6 single crystals (CS3, SS3). The close In2Ni21B6 analogue confirms that In–Ni–B Fm-3m frameworks are chemically plausible (CS1, SS1). The uncertainty is that In self-flux could favor different In-rich phases or dissolve B poorly, so the route is plausible but not guaranteed ([Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient TS flux evidence remains neutral.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Cold-pressed powder sintering is supported by Co21B6Sb2-type materials at 1000–1100 °C for 10–20 h in quartz tubes and by SrNi12B6 at 1000 °C for 5 days in Ar/Ta/iron containment (CS5; SS5). The temperature can exceed 1000 °C, and the target’s In content likely requires sealed containment to prevent loss ([Reasonable Inference], supported by CS1/SS1). The route is operationally simple and avoids arc-melting or flux removal. TS gives insufficient evidence for this exact cold-pressed In–Ni–B route and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

This route has weaker kinetics because cold-pressed elemental powders have limited contact area and Ni/B diffusion is slow ([Reasonable Inference], supported by long annealing in CS1/SS1 and SS5). Use of pre-synthesized Ni2B is a possible improvement because Ni2B is used as a precursor in SrNi12B6 synthesis (SS5), but this is only indirectly transferable. The route may produce polycrystalline material but is less likely to produce large single crystals than flux growth (CS3/SS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether short 10–20 h sintering from CS5 is sufficient for the In–Ni–B target.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The rubric triggers high amorphization/structural-complexity risk because the target has a large In/Ni size mismatch, a likely large Fm-3m unit cell, and multiple coordination motifs (target structure; [Reasonable Inference]). Related Fm-3m analogues explicitly contain mixed edge/corner/face-sharing networks, which supports the mixed-connectivity trigger (CS1/SS1; CS3/SS3). However, CS/SS analogues also show that such complex metallic boride structures can crystallize with long annealing or flux growth, so the F4 penalty should be interpreted as ordering difficulty rather than impossibility (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS3). TS has insufficient direct evidence on amorphization tendency and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a marginally metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold, while several close analogues are on the hull (target; CS1–CS3; SS1–SS3). The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis because the closest analogue, In2Ni21B6, is an experimentally synthesized cubic Fm-3m In–Ni–B phase prepared from the elements (CS1, SS1). TS evidence is supportive but less specific: thermodynamically similar phases with comparable or higher E_hull have been synthesized, but they do not directly resolve In–Ni–B phase competition (TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of closely related Ni-rich boride frameworks that tolerate long annealing or flux growth (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS3). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the exact Ni-rich/In-poor stoichiometry while preventing In loss or formation of competing In–Ni–B phases ([Reasonable Inference], supported by sealed In analogue in CS1/SS1).
