### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has **E above hull = 0.008 eV/atom**, which is very close to the convex hull and falls in the 90–100 scoring band by the provided rubric (**Target**). Thermodynamically similar low-hull phases are experimentally synthesized, including BaAl₄Se₇ at 0.0186 eV/atom (**TS1**) and AlBi₂Se₂BrCl₄ at 0.0074 eV/atom (**TS5**). The closest chemical analogue GaTeCl is calculated on the hull and experimentally made (**CS5**). Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific AlSeCl polymorph competes with Al₂Se₃, AlCl₃-derived salts, or other Al–Se–Cl phases not listed in the references.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate condition-severity score because the closest GaTeCl analogue uses only **400 °C** and 7 days, which is mild for solid-state chemistry (**CS5**). The main penalties are evacuated ampoule use, volatile chloride retention, and air/moisture-sensitive chloride/chalcogenide handling, all implied by inert/evacuated preparation of GaTeCl and related Al–Se compounds (**CS5, SS1, SS2**). Insufficient evidence from **TS** exists for the exact AlSeCl ampoule route, so TS contributes mainly thermodynamic support and is neutral for detailed processing severity. The uncertainty is ampoule pressure from AlCl₃ and possible reaction with silica at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because AlCl₃ is reactive/volatile and the binary Al₂Se₃ precursor already contains Al–Se bonding [Reasonable Inference], while the direct analogue GaTeCl forms at 400 °C (**CS5**). Al–Se tetrahedral frameworks are common in sealed-tube solid-state chemistry (**SS1, SS2, SS3; TS1**), supporting feasible crystallization. Structural reconstruction should be limited because GaTeCl has the same layered MChCl tetrahedral motif (**CS5**). The main uncertainty is whether smaller Al/Se relative to Ga/Te changes the stability field or favors denser Al₂Se₃-derived phases.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has mild nominal temperatures because AlCl₃/NaCl and ionothermal analogues crystallize chalcogenide halides at **130–165 °C** (**SS5, TS5**) and Al chloride melt growth occurs at 190 °C (**TS3**). It is penalized for volatile AlCl₃, inert handling, and multistep flux removal/sublimation, which are present in the AlCl₃/NaCl melt workflow (**SS5**). Insufficient evidence from **CS** exists for AlSeCl flux growth specifically, so CS is neutral for this route’s severity. The uncertainty is whether Al₂Se₃ dissolves and recrystallizes into neutral AlSeCl rather than AlCl₄⁻ salt frameworks [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux/liquid transport should give excellent mass transport because low-temperature AlCl₃-based melts produce crystals in related systems (**SS5, TS3, TS5**). Precursor activity is high in Lewis-acidic chloride media (**SS5, TS5**). However, the known flux products are AlCl₄-containing Bi–Se halides rather than exact AlSeCl sheets (**SS5, TS5**), so structural selectivity is less proven. Insufficient evidence from **CS** exists for low-temperature flux synthesis of the exact MChCl analogue, so the structural-reconstruction component is penalized.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because direct sealed-tube Al–Se analogues often require 800–1000 °C or repeated treatments (**SS1, SS2, SS3**), although the proposed AlCl₃-assisted version is kept to 500–700 °C [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for vacuum/sealed tubes, volatile Se/AlCl₃, staged heating, and air-sensitive/toxic reagents, consistent with sealed chalcogenide syntheses (**SS1–SS3**). CS gives insufficient direct evidence for an elemental route to MChCl, so CS is neutral for this route. The largest uncertainty is pressure and stoichiometry control in the presence of both Se and AlCl₃ vapors.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic score is lower because elemental Al requires breaking its oxide shell and forming all Al–Se/Al–Cl connectivity during reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Elemental routes for Al–Se compounds are possible (**SS2, SS3**), but they generally use high temperatures, long holds, or sealed-tube protocols (**SS1–SS3**). Structural reconstruction is complete rather than topotactic, unlike Route 1. The uncertainty is whether molten/volatile AlCl₃ sufficiently accelerates transport to compensate for elemental precursor sluggishness.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Al, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered (**Target**). The target description reports a simple corner-sharing AlSe₃Cl tetrahedral sheet without edge or face sharing (**Target**), so mixed-connectivity frustration is low. Insufficient evidence from **CS/SS/TS** indicates a large target unit cell >40 atoms; based on the reported simple formula and sites, no penalty is applied. Remaining uncertainty is whether stacking disorder in the 2D sheets could broaden diffraction or mimic amorphous character [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlSeCl is likely a **near-ground-state or very mildly metastable** phase because its energy above hull is only **0.008 eV/atom** (**Target**). The strongest support comes from **GaTeCl**, which has the same layered MChCl stoichiometry and corner-sharing MCh₃Cl tetrahedral motif and is synthesized directly from GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ at 400 °C (**CS5**). Structural and thermodynamic analogues show that Al–Se tetrahedral frameworks are accessible in sealed-tube chemistry (**SS1, SS2, SS3; TS1**). The most critical enabling factor is the use of **preformed Al₂Se₃ plus volatile/reactive AlCl₃**, which should reduce the reconstruction burden relative to elemental synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by **CS5, SS1, SS2**]. The main limiting factor is **chloride volatility and moisture sensitivity**, requiring careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry control (**CS5, SS5, TS3**).
