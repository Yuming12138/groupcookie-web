### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. This is reinforced by stable or nearly stable analogues: ZrCl₂ has E_hull = 0 [CS5, SS3, TS2], monoclinic ZrI₂ is essentially on the hull [SS1, TS1], and Nb₃Br₈ is also on the hull [CS3]. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee that the specific monoclinic P2₁/m polymorph forms easily rather than another ZrBr₂ polytype, as ZrCl₂ reports multiple polytypes/intergrowths [CS5, SS3, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is conditionally severe because the closest ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ analogues require sealed Ta tubes, vacuum/inert handling, drybox operations, high-temperature gradients, and weeks-long heat treatment [SS1, TS1; CS5, SS3, TS2]. The temperature itself is moderate for refractory Zr chemistry, but volatile halide transport and air-sensitive Zr halides impose substantial experimental complexity [CS5, SS3, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact ZrBr₄ CVT parameters means bromide-specific vapor pressure tuning is an uncertainty and is treated by analogy.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by vapor transport, since ZrI₂ forms from ZrI₄ + Zr under a gradient [SS1, TS1] and ZrCl₂ forms via chemical transport/equilibration with ZrCl₄ [CS5, SS3, TS2]. Structural reconstruction should be favorable because the target is very close to monoclinic P2₁/m ZrI₂ [SS1, TS1]. The main kinetic uncertainty is slow ordering/polytype selection, since ZrCl₂ requires 4–10 weeks and suffers from intergrowths/twinning [CS5, SS3, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids a deliberate gradient but still requires sealed Ta tubes, controlled inert/vacuum conditions, volatile ZrBr₄, and air-sensitive handling, all supported by ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ analogues [CS5, SS3, TS2; SS1, TS1]. The temperature is below 1000 °C, so thermal severity is not extreme. The long duration and sealed halide chemistry make the route moderately challenging rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2

Isothermal comproportionation is chemically well matched because ZrCl₂ forms by equilibrations in sealed Ta tubing [CS5, SS3, TS2], and metal + higher halide reduction is supported by ZrI₂ and TiBr₃ analogues [SS1, TS1; CS4]. Kinetics are likely slower than in a gradient CVT experiment because transport is less directed **[Reasonable Inference]**. The layered ZrX₂ framework should be accessible, but polytype and stoichiometry equilibration may require weeks [CS5, SS3, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 3

The direct vapor route has a comparatively high F2 because TiBr₂ synthesis from Ti + Br₂ uses 900 °C Ar flow rather than sealed high-vacuum Ta tubes [CS2, SS5]. However, Br₂ is volatile, toxic, and corrosive, and the process requires controlled delivery, dry gas handling, and rapid cooling [CS2, SS5]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct elemental vapor synthesis of ZrBr₂ makes the route less certain despite favorable condition scoring.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetics of bromination can be fast because Br₂ vapor reacts with transition metals, as shown for TiBr₂ [CS2, SS5] and Nb bromides [CS3]. However, composition control is likely difficult for Zr because higher bromides such as ZrBr₄ are plausible under high Br₂ chemical potential **[Reasonable Inference]**. The TiBr₂ analogue required a cooler zone and rapid cooling to avoid back-reaction [CS2, SS5], indicating a narrow kinetic window.

#### F2 — Route 4

The H₂-assisted route uses 750 °C for 20 days in sealed Ta with H₂, which is experimentally demanding due to flammability, controlled gas pressure, and sealed-container requirements [CS1, SS2]. The evidence is weaker because the snippet discusses chloride hydride decomposition and cluster phases rather than a clean direct bromide synthesis [CS1, SS2]. #### F3 — Route 4

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrBr₂ in the stated monoclinic molybdenite-like P2₁/m structure is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and close ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ analogues are stable or nearly stable [CS5, SS3, TS2; SS1, TS1]. The strongest synthesis support comes from the structural and thermodynamic match to monoclinic ZrI₂ made by ZrI₄ + Zr transport in sealed Ta tubes [SS1, TS1] and from ZrCl₂ sealed-tube equilibrations [CS5, SS3, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of volatile zirconium tetrahalide chemistry, which can transport halide and enable equilibration at 600–850 °C [CS5, SS3, TS2; SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among ZrBr₂ polytypes, Zr-rich cluster phases, and higher bromides, analogous to the polytype/intergrowth difficulty reported for ZrCl₂ [CS5, SS3, TS2]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but likely requires classical air-free high-temperature halide methods rather than simple open-tube solid-state reaction.
