### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase. TS provides strong support because multiple thermodynamically similar sulfide/selenide phases also have E_hull = 0 [TS:1–TS:5]. CS is supportive but mixed: K/Rb V₅S₈ are slightly above hull [CS:1, CS:2], whereas K/Rb Cr₅S₈ are stable [CS:3, CS:4]. SS is moderately supportive because structurally similar phases range from near-stable to modestly metastable [SS:1–SS:5].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct sealed-tube route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, consistent with NaVS₂ at 600 °C and NiV₄S₈-type sulfide anneals below 900 °C [SS:2, SS:4, CS:5]. The main severity penalties come from evacuated ampoules, sulfur volatility, multistep annealing, and air/moisture-sensitive Sr sulfide handling [CS:5; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for exact SrV₄S₇ sealed-tube conditions, so the score remains moderate.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory V diffusion and the need to build a complex low-symmetry VS₆ network [Reasonable Inference]. However, SS and CS show V sulfides can form in sealed tubes or sulfur anneals over days to weeks [SS:2, SS:4, CS:5]. The absence of a structurally identical precursor lowers the structural-reconstruction score; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a topotactic SrV₄S₇ pathway.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
H₂S sulfidation is operationally more hazardous because H₂S is toxic and corrosive, but the temperature window is still below 1000 °C [CS:5]. The route is multistep, requiring gas sulfidation, grinding, sulfur addition, and final evacuated-tube anneal [CS:5]. It avoids high-pressure equipment unlike high-pressure Cr₅S₈ synthesis [TS:3].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
CS:5 provides the strongest kinetic support because oxide-to-sulfide conversion is reported complete in about 48 h and simultaneously reduces vanadium. Gas-phase sulfidation should improve precursor activity relative to direct metal diffusion [CS:5; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates SrV₄S₇ from oxide/carbonate precursors.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Ion exchange operates at relatively modest temperatures in evacuated silica tubes [CS:1, CS:2, TS:2, TS:4]. Severity arises from precursor pre-synthesis, molten halides, sealed tubes, and likely Tl toxicity [CS:1, CS:2; Reasonable Inference]. The route is less severe thermally but more problematic chemically.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten-halide ion exchange has strong kinetic support for monovalent K/Rb/Cs V₅S₈ and V₅Se₈ analogues [CS:1, CS:2, TS:2, TS:4]. The key uncertainty is that Sr²⁺ exchange changes charge balance and the target stoichiometry differs from AV₅S₈; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for divalent Sr ion exchange. Thus, kinetics are good if a suitable parent exists, but transferability is uncertain.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
CVT uses standard evacuated ampoules and temperatures below 1000 °C for V sulfide thiospinels [SS:1]. Penalties arise from volatile/corrosive transport agents and the need for pre-synthesized feedstock [SS:1; Reasonable Inference]. CS/TS give insufficient evidence for SrV₄S₇ under halide transport, so decomposition risk remains.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Gas-phase transport improves mass transport, and SS:1 shows V sulfide crystals can be transported in about 60 h. If SrV₄S₇ powder is already available, crystallization kinetics may be favorable [SS:1; Reasonable Inference]. As a primary synthesis route, however, CVT is uncertain because Sr halide formation could alter stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Sr/V size mismatch, visible in the long Sr–S bonds versus shorter V–S bonds, and it contains mixed corner/edge/face-sharing VS₆ connectivity. These two triggers give a medium amorphization or kinetic trapping risk. CS/SS/TS analogues show such mixed octahedral networks can crystallize [CS:1–CS:5, SS:1–SS:5, TS:1–TS:5], but none prove this exact triclinic SrV₄S₇ structure.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrV₄S₇ is likely a ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamically similar chalcogenides include several stable phases [TS:1–TS:5]. The analogue support is chemically good for V–S octahedral sulfides [CS:1, CS:2, CS:5] and thermodynamically good for related sulfide frameworks [TS:1–TS:5], but structurally incomplete because no source reports the exact SrV₄S₇ P-1 framework. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a sufficiently high sulfur chemical potential while reducing vanadium into the sulfide framework, for which H₂S sulfidation plus sulfur annealing is directly supported in V sulfide chemistry [CS:5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Sr–V–S binaries/ternaries and possible slow ordering into the low-symmetry mixed-connectivity structure [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require iterative annealing and careful sulfur/oxygen control.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy (from CS/SS/TS) |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sulfur volatility and sulfur chemical-potential control | High | Use evacuated silica tubes and slight sulfur excess; sulfur addition and sealed annealing are used for V sulfide synthesis [CS:5]. |
| 2 | All | Competing phase formation in Sr–V–S system | High | Use repeated grinding/long annealing as in NiV₄S₈-related synthesis [CS:5]; monitor by powder XRD after each anneal [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 3 | All | Slow ordering into low-symmetry triclinic P-1 phase | Medium–High | Extend anneal to 1–2 weeks, consistent with long sulfide anneals in CS:5; use slow cooling unless quenching is needed to retain a phase, as quenching was used for NaVS₂ [SS:2, SS:4]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Low reactivity of V metal or dense binary sulfides | Medium | Use pre-sulfidized V₂S₃/VS₂ or finely ground powders; staged heating and regrinding are supported by sulfide protocols [CS:5, SS:2, SS:4]. |
| 5 | Route 2 | H₂S toxicity and gas-handling difficulty | High | Use dry H₂S with proper flow, graphite boat, exhaust scrubbing, and low leak-rate tube furnace setup; H₂S stream is explicitly used in CS:5 and CS:3/CS:4/TS:5. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Incomplete oxide-to-sulfide conversion or residual oxygen | Medium | Use prolonged H₂S exposure; CS:5 reports sulfidation complete after about 48 h, so 48–72 h plus mass-change/XRD verification is recommended. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Divalent Sr ion exchange may not follow monovalent A⁺ exchange chemistry | High | Treat as exploratory only; monovalent K/Rb/Cs exchange is supported [CS:1, CS:2, TS:2, TS:4], but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports Sr²⁺ exchange. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Tl precursor toxicity and prior synthesis burden | High | Minimize scale, use sealed-tube handling and avoid if direct/sulfidation routes work; Tl-based precursors are part of the ion-exchange basis [CS:1, CS:2, TS:2, TS:4]. |
| 9 | Route 4 | CVT transport agent may form Sr halides and change stoichiometry | Medium–High | Use low transport-agent loading and pre-reacted feedstock; SS:1 uses controlled transport-agent concentration of 5 mg/mL ampoule volume for V sulfide CVT. |
| 10 | Route 4 | Crystal growth may be low yield or non-stoichiometric | Medium | Optimize temperature gradient and duration; CVT gradients of 800–900 °C to 720–800 °C are demonstrated for V sulfides [SS:1]. |
