### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

**Citation key:** `[CS#]`, `[SS#]`, and `[TS#]` refer to the numbered entries in the Chemical, Structural, and Thermodynamic Similarity datasets; `[Target]` refers to the provided target structure; `[Reasonable Inference]` marks extrapolated synthesis logic.

**Key target-specific note:** The 1.33 Å O(1)–O(1) contact suggests an O–O dimer, likely superoxide/peroxide-like, so oxygen chemical potential is a major control variable for K2MgVO6 [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS analogues provide strong K–V–O framework evidence but **insufficient evidence from CS/SS** for direct O–O-dimer stabilization, so that sub-signal is treated as neutral 50 [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5]. TS gives only indirect O–O/peroxo-relevant evidence through peroxodiphosphate solution/diffusion chemistry, not through K–Mg–V oxide synthesis [TS3].

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the favorable near-hull cutoff, so it is likely accessible as a ground-state-adjacent or low-metastability phase [Target]. CS analogues are all hull-stable, SS analogues are mostly hull-stable or near-hull, and TS analogues show that materials in the ~0.06–0.10 eV/atom range can still be experimentally accessed [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5]. The main uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee stabilization of the target O–O unit under the same synthesis conditions [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 Solid-State**  
The solid-state route scores well on severity because it can be done below 1000 °C at ambient pressure using air/O2 and common precursors [CS5; SS2; SS3]. Its kinetics are weaker because SS1 required 46 days for K2LiVO4, and MgO diffusion may be slower than alkali/vanadate transport [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether O–O ordering survives a high-temperature ceramic route [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Hydrothermal**  
Hydrothermal synthesis has strong K-rich vanadate precedent through K3ScV2O8 and K3Y(VO4)2 in 4–10 M K2CO3 at 560 °C [CS1; CS4]. Kinetics are favorable because dissolution/reprecipitation bypasses long-range solid diffusion [CS1; CS4]. The score is limited because Mg-specific hydrothermal incorporation and O–O dimer stabilization are not directly demonstrated by CS/SS analogues [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Flux Growth**  
Flux growth receives the best combined score because K–V flux chemistry improves transport while preserving strong chemical overlap with K–vanadate analogues [CS2; SS3; SS5]. Slow cooling is directly supported by flux-grown vanadate and sealed oxide crystal examples [SS5; TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is oxygen chemical potential control, since the O–O dimer is inferred from the target but not directly shown in the closest CS/SS analogues [Target; CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 4 High Pressure**  
High-pressure synthesis is plausible as a fallback because TS analogues near the target’s thermodynamic regime were made at 1.5–4 GPa and ~900 °C [TS2; TS5]. Its condition score is lower due to sealed capsules, oxidizer handling, and specialized pressure apparatus [TS2; TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetic/phase-control score is limited because TS2 was serendipitous and TS5 reported impurity issues [TS2; TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The largest amorphization risk is the large cation size/coordination contrast among K, Mg, and V sites [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target does not appear to contain the edge/face/vertex sharing coexistence that would strongly raise amorphization tendency, because the key framework is MgO6–VO4 corner sharing [Target]. Unit-cell atom count cannot be confirmed from the supplied data, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50 [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2MgVO6 is best interpreted as a near-ground-state or low-metastability phase rather than an unambiguous ground-state compound because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. CS and SS analogues strongly support K–V–O and MO6–VO4 framework formation, especially through solid-state, hydrothermal, and flux routes [CS1; CS2; CS5; SS2; SS5]. TS analogues support the idea that near-hull oxide phases can be made, but several TS examples are chemically distant or require high-pressure/serendipitous conditions [TS2; TS5]. The most important enabling factor is high cation mobility from a K–vanadate flux or K-rich mineralizer [CS1; CS4; SS5]. The most important limiting factor is stabilizing the target’s short O–O unit while preventing K loss and competing K–V oxide formation [Target; SS3; Reasonable Inference].

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | O–O dimer stabilization may require high oxygen chemical potential, but CS/SS give insufficient direct evidence for this chemistry [Target; CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5]. | High | Use O2-rich conditions, optional peroxide/superoxide additives, and lower-temperature crystallization/flux holds [Target; TS3; Reasonable Inference]. |

| 3 | Route 1 | Slow solid-state diffusion and MgO refractory behavior can limit phase completion [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Use fine MgO/MgCO3, repeated grinding, prereaction, and longer anneals [CS5; SS2; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Hydrothermal pressure/corrosion and mineralizer strength complicate scale-up [CS1; CS4]. | Medium–High | Use compatible Ag/Pt liners and the proven 4–10 M K2CO3 window as a starting point [CS1; CS4]. |
