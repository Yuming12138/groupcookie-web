### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target], which is at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin in the rubric. CS analogues include both ground-state tungstates such as Ca₂MgWO₆ and Ca₂CoWO₆ with E_hull = 0 (CS-1, CS-5) and metastable Na-containing oxides near 0.07–0.09 eV/atom that are still synthesized (CS-3, CS-4). TS analogues include phases around 0.04–0.09 eV/atom that are experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5), supporting synthesizeability despite slight metastability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Ambient solid-state synthesis is not severe because CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, SS-1, and TS-2 use ordinary furnaces, oxide/carbonate precursors, and air or unspecified atmospheres. The proposed route avoids >1000 °C to limit alkali loss, unlike CS-1 at 1573 K and CS-5 at 1100 °C. The main deduction is multi-step processing, consistent with grinding/calcination/annealing steps in CS-1, CS-3, and SS-1.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are the weakness of the ceramic route because WO₃/carbonates require solid-state diffusion and reconstruction, and CS-1 specifically notes forming MgWO₄ precursor to avoid WO₃ sublimation. SS-1 and SS-2 show long annealing times and residual impurities for structurally related alkali oxides. The score is partially rescued by SS-1/SS-3/TS-2 showing alkali molybdates can crystallize near 530 °C.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Flux/peroxide synthesis uses moderate temperature and ambient pressure, consistent with TS-5 flux growth at 430 °C and SS-1/TS-2 annealing at 530 °C. The severity comes from air-sensitive/reactive peroxide or hydroxide precursors [Reasonable Inference] and multi-step heating/cooling/washing, analogous to flux washing in CS-2. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for peroxide fluxes in Na–Li–W–O specifically, so safety and phase screening are important.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Reaction kinetics should be favorable because flux media increase ion mobility, as shown by CS-2 and TS-5 flux-grown oxide crystals and by low-temperature alkali molybdate formation in SS-1/SS-3/TS-2. The short target O–O bond suggests an oxidizing alkali peroxide/hydroxide environment may be chemically matched [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the desired P1 phase nucleates instead of simpler Na/Li tungstates.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The citrate route is operationally moderate: CS-5 demonstrates soft-chemistry tungstate synthesis using evaporation, drying, decomposition, and final annealing in air. It avoids high pressure and strongly air-sensitive reagents if nitrates/carbonates are used. The main penalty is the number of steps, as in CS-5.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Citrate processing gives high precursor homogeneity, supported by CS-5 for Ca₂TWO₆ tungstates. However, the target is a low-symmetry Na–Li–W–O phase, and CS-5 still required multistage decomposition and high-temperature crystallization, with minor impurities. Thus kinetics are better than ceramic mixing but phase selectivity remains uncertain.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is more severe because TS-1 used 1.5 GPa Ar, sealed gold tubing, and specialized high-pressure equipment. Although the rubric only penalizes >5 GPa, the controlled atmosphere, sealed containment, and multistep setup justify a lower F2. TS-1 also involved toxic Hg/Cd salts, but the proposed target route can avoid them, so no toxicity penalty was applied.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
TS-1 shows Na–W–O phases can crystallize under 900 °C and 1.5 GPa, supporting feasibility. However, the phase in TS-1 was found unexpectedly during another growth experiment and coexisted with other phases, indicating poor phase-control kinetics. High pressure may help stabilize dense/metastable motifs, but it does not guarantee selective Na₂LiWO₆ formation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains Na, Li, and W in very different coordination environments, including NaO₆, LiO₄, and WO₅ motifs [Target], so cation-size/field-strength mismatch triggers C1. The unit cell size is not proven to exceed 40 atoms from the target description, and explicit mixed vertex/edge/face sharing is not given for the target, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS/TS analogues show complex polyhedral connectivity can be crystallized, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact connectivity of Na₂LiWO₆.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂LiWO₆ is likely a **borderline metastable phase** rather than a deeply stable ground state because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis of alkali tungstate/molybdate-related oxides by solid-state, flux, soft-chemistry, and high-pressure routes, but none directly demonstrate Na₂LiWO₆, so transferability is moderate rather than definitive. The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated low-temperature crystallization of alkali molybdate/tungstate-like frameworks in SS-1, SS-3, TS-2, and TS-5. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Na/Li/W/O chemistry may prefer simpler tungstates such as Na₂WO₄/Li₂WO₄ or oxygen-deficient/ordinary W⁶⁺ oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The short O–O bond in the target makes oxygen chemical potential a critical variable [Target; Reasonable Inference].
