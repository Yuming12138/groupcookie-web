### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.022 eV/atom, so it falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally synthesized: InGaS3 has E_hull ≈0.017 eV/atom and is grown by CVT (TS-1), ZnIn2S4 has E_hull ≈0.014 eV/atom and is made by high-pressure synthesis (TS-2), and Li2In2GeS6 has E_hull ≈0.014 eV/atom and is made by solid-state reaction (TS-4). The residual uncertainty is that 0.022 eV/atom indicates likely metastability rather than guaranteed ground-state stability.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses sealed ampoules and vacuum, which are supported by CS-1 and SS-2, and volatile sulfur requires sealed containment as in CS-1/SS-1/SS-2. It avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, unlike SS-3/TS-2 and CS-4, so condition severity is moderate. The uncertainty is the exact temperature window for the monoclinic C2 In2S3 polymorph, which is not directly specified by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, In/S direct reaction should be favorable because In is not refractory and sulfur is reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, while analogous In sulfides are obtained by long annealing from elements or binaries (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5). The main kinetic penalty is structural ordering into a specific 2D monoclinic C2 polymorph, as long anneals and repeated homogenization are reported in SS-1/SS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact nucleation pathway of this specific In2S3 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile iodine, directly supported by SS-4/TS-1 and CS-3. It avoids external pressure, but it adds toxicity/corrosion and ampoule-gradient complexity. Phase competition is a concern because CS-3 reports difficulty obtaining pure Rh3S4 without Rh17S15, and SS-4/TS-1 produced two InGaS3 modifications.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by vapor-phase mass transport, as demonstrated for InGaS3 single crystals using iodine (SS-4, TS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the target monoclinic C2 In2S3 polymorph nucleates preferentially rather than another In2S3 polymorph **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact In2S3–I2 transport chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure treatment is substantially more severe because related compounds require 20 kbar for ZnIn2S4 (SS-3/TS-2), 40–50 kbar for InSe (TS-5), and up to 60–65 kbar or more for pyrite-like sulfides (CS-4). Specialized belt/anvil equipment and capsules are required, as in SS-3/TS-2 and CS-4. The route is therefore challenging despite potentially short reaction times.

#### F3 — Route 3

High-pressure quench routes can transform chalcogenide polymorphs rapidly, as ZnIn2S4-II formed after 45 min at 20 kbar and 1100 °C (SS-3/TS-2). However, TS-5 reports disorder and metastability in high-pressure InSe, indicating that reconstruction and quench retention can be difficult. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists that the target monoclinic C2 In2S3 is specifically pressure-stabilized.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film sulfurization uses lower temperatures but requires controlled vapor/UHV or sulfur-rich atmospheres. CS-5 supports advanced vapor deposition for an indium halide, but it does not directly support sulfide film growth, so the evidence is weak. Toxicity is route-dependent: H2S would be hazardous **[Reasonable Inference]**, while elemental sulfur vapor is less severe.

#### F3 — Route 4

Thin-film methods may kinetically stabilize 2D/metastable phases **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS-5 demonstrates that deposition and temperature cycling can access layered indium compounds. However, the target requires precise In:S control and crystallization from a film, and no CS/SS/TS source confirms In2S3 vapor-deposition crystallization. This gives moderate-to-low confidence despite plausible feasibility.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-radius mismatch penalty. Mixed edge- and corner-sharing InS5 square pyramids are present in the target structure, and similar mixed connectivity appears in structurally similar In sulfides (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5), which can slow crystallization and increase amorphous or poorly ordered products. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact unit-cell atom count, but the provided target description does not indicate a very large cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable In2S3 polymorph rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.022 eV/atom. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: structurally similar In–S compounds are made by sealed-ampoule solid-state reactions and long anneals (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5), while thermodynamically similar compounds near 0.01–0.02 eV/atom are experimentally accessible by CVT, solid-state reaction, or high-pressure routes (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary chemistry and relatively low thermodynamic penalty. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because In2S3 has multiple possible structures **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS/SS/TS analogues show that phase competition or long ordering anneals can occur (CS-3, SS-2, SS-5). Overall, sealed-ampoule synthesis followed by annealing is the most practical first attempt, with CVT as the best route for single crystals.
