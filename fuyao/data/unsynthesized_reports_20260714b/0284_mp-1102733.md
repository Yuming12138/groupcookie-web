### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary for likely synthesizability. The exact HoAsS entry in CS/SS is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS1, SS1], which conflicts with the target’s 0.0500 eV/atom; the conservative target value was used. TS includes sulfide analogues near 0.05–0.09 eV/atom that are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS4], supporting that small positive hull energies can still be accessible. Remaining uncertainty is whether the 0.05 eV/atom phase is the same thermodynamic state as the reported HoAsS polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high pressure and likely avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, consistent with ordinary solid-state sintering rather than DAC/multi-anvil methods [CS2, SS5]. The main deductions arise from vacuum/sealed ampoule need, As volatility, and As toxicity; these are inferred from fundamental chemistry and supported by TS sealed-tube handling of sulfides [TS1, TS5]. CS/SS provide insufficient evidence for exact temperature/time, so the severity score is conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1

The HoS + As precursor pair is chemically preorganized relative to elemental Ho + S + As, and LnAsS analogues are reported from LS + As [CS2, CS5, SS4, SS5]. Diffusion remains moderate because HoS is a refractory solid and reaction likely requires long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target is the same Matlockite/Pnma LnAsS structure family [CS2, CS5, SS4, SS5].

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT is directly supported for exact HoAsS and Er/Tb analogues [CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1–SS3]. However, CVT imposes extra severity through sealed evacuated ampoules, temperature gradients, volatile As/S, and multi-step polycrystalline-feed preparation [CS1, SS1]. The absence of specified transport agent, temperature, and time in CS/SS adds uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves mass transport and crystal growth, as exact HoAsS single crystals are reportedly grown this way [CS1, SS1]. The elemental starting mixture has slower initial kinetics than HoS + As, but vapor transport can overcome some diffusion limitations [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference]. The structural pathway is excellent because the target phase itself is the CVT product [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 3

Direct sealed-ampoule elemental synthesis is supported indirectly because CS/SS state that polycrystalline LnAsS feed is synthesized from stoichiometric elements [CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3]. It is penalized for possible >1000 °C treatment based on TS sulfide sealed-tube precedent [TS1], vacuum sealing, volatile As/S, and toxic precursors. There is no high-pressure requirement, unlike some TS polysulfide analogues [TS2–TS4].

#### F3 — Route 3

Reaction from elemental Ho, As, and S requires complete compound formation and is more kinetically demanding than using HoS [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference]. Long sealed-tube reactions can work for related sulfides [TS1], but competing binary phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-good because the exact family is known from elemental mixtures [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux growth can lower temperature, with TS showing sulfide crystals grown at 850 °C in evacuated ampoules [TS5]. However, for HoAsS this route is not directly reported in CS/SS, and using preformed feed plus flux adds steps [Reasonable Inference]. Volatility/toxicity of As-containing material and sealed-tube requirements remain.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux improves kinetics by enabling liquid-phase transport, and TS5 demonstrates this for sulfide single crystals. Transferability to HoAsS is uncertain because no CS/SS evidence reports flux growth for LnAsS; therefore, the structural-reconstruction component is held moderate. The route is plausible for crystal growth but less evidence-backed than Route 1 or Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

HoAsS has only one cation type, so large cation radius mismatch does not apply. The structure described in CS/SS contains edge- and corner-sharing SHo₅ trigonal bipyramids but no face-sharing coexistence [CS1, SS1]. The small Ho₄As₄S₄ cell also argues against high glass-forming complexity [CS1, SS1]. Remaining uncertainty is minor and mainly concerns possible disorder under rapid quenching, which is not suggested by CS/SS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HoAsS is likely synthesizable and may be either a near-ground-state phase or a very low-energy metastable phase, depending on whether the target E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom or the CS/SS E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom is taken as authoritative [CS1, SS1]. The CS and SS evidence are especially strong because the exact HoAsS structure and synthesis by chemical transport are included, and adjacent LnAsS members are made by solid-state routes [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust LnAsS Matlockite/Pnma structure across many lanthanides [CS2–CS5, SS2–SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is control of volatile and toxic As/S species in sealed high-temperature reactions [Reasonable Inference; TS1, TS5]. Route 1 is recommended for powder synthesis, while Route 2 is preferred if single crystals are required.
