### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

KScS₂ is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase rather than a high-energy metastable compound. CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, and TS-3 include multiple alkali sulfides with E_hull = 0, supporting that this chemical family can contain stable crystalline phases. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for experimental KScS₂ specifically, but the thermodynamic signal is very favorable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for high temperature and H₂S use: KYS₂ uses 1323 K ≈ 1050 °C in H₂S (CS-3), while RbLnS₂ uses flowing H₂S at 1000 °C (SS-3, TS-3). No high pressure or sealed volatile precursor is required in this route according to CS-3/SS-3/TS-3. The main safety/severity concern is toxic H₂S, which is explicitly part of the successful analogue syntheses.

#### F3 — Route 1

The carbonate/oxide sulfidation route has moderate kinetics because carbonate and oxide precursors react successfully in H₂S for KYS₂ and RbLnS₂ analogues (CS-3, SS-3, TS-3). However, [Reasonable Inference] Sc₂O₃ is refractory and may sulfidize more slowly than some lanthanide oxides, lowering the precursor-activity score. The large alkali carbonate excess used in CS-3 and SS-3/TS-3 likely assists mass transport and crystal growth.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C temperatures because K₂TiS₃ and K₃GaS₃ are made near 752–797 °C in sealed ampoules (CS-1, CS-5). It is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive K₂S, and possible K₂S pre-synthesis, all indicated or implied by CS-1. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS gives no stronger support for Sc-specific direct reaction behavior, so the route is scored conservatively.

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct reaction from K₂S/Sc/S likely has slower kinetics because Sc metal is refractory and may have a passivating oxide layer [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 shows that K₂TiS₃ requires a 6 d anneal at 1070 K, supporting slow bulk diffusion and ordering for direct potassium transition-metal sulfides. The complete reconstruction from elemental or binary sulfide reactants into layered KScS₂ lowers the structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth benefits from lower temperatures, as reactive flux analogues are prepared at 350 °C for 6 d (SS-2, TS-2) or 773–793 K for 96 h (SS-5, TS-5). It still requires glovebox handling, sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/polysulfide control, and post-growth washing steps, all supported by SS-2/TS-2 and SS-5/TS-5. No high-pressure equipment is needed, consistent with those flux precedents.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux/liquid-mediated growth improves kinetics relative to bulk diffusion, and alkali polychalcogenide fluxes are proven for related alkali chalcogenides (SS-2, TS-2). SS-5/TS-5 also show sealed sulfide flux routes with controlled cooling, supporting crystal-growth feasibility. The uncertainty is that no direct Sc-containing flux precedent is provided, so the transfer to KScS₂ is partly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

KScS₂ has a large K⁺/Sc³⁺ size contrast, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion by [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is a relatively simple layered edge-sharing ScS₆ network, so mixed polyhedral connectivity is not strongly indicated by the target description. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no sign of large unit-cell complexity for KScS₂, and many related sulfides crystallize readily under solid-state or flux conditions (CS-3, SS-3, TS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KScS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from KYS₂, which has the same K–M³⁺–S₂ stoichiometry and is synthesized from alkali carbonate plus Ln₂O₃ in H₂S (CS-3), with additional support from RbLnS₂ made similarly in flowing H₂S (SS-3, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated ability of H₂S/alkali-carbonate flux conditions to convert stable trivalent-cation oxides into crystalline layered sulfides (CS-3, SS-3, TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is likely kinetic: Sc₂O₃ is refractory and may require optimized H₂S exposure, K activity, and hold time [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed sulfide and polysulfide flux routes are plausible backups, but they introduce greater air-sensitivity and stoichiometry-control challenges (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2).
