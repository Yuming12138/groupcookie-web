### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.042 eV/atom lies below 0.05 eV/atom, so it is in the high-probability metastable/near-stable range by the requested rubric. CS strongly supports this because multiple RTeCl analogues are at or very near the hull and were synthesized experimentally [CS1–CS5]. TS also supports accessibility because thermodynamically similar halide phases with E_hull ~0.035–0.061 eV/atom were synthesized by sealed solid-state routes [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact YTeCl polymorph is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, and the small Y³⁺ size may shift the preferred structure [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because the CS analogue route uses 1050 °C, 5 d, sealed Ta containers, glovebox handling, He-arc welding, and evacuated silica jackets [CS1–CS5]. These conditions trigger deductions for T >1000 °C, controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile components, multistep preparation, and air-sensitive/toxic precursors [CS1–CS5]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for YTeCl high-temperature Ta-tube requirements and is scored neutral for this route-specific condition choice. The uncertainty is whether YTeCl can form at lower temperature than La–Nd RTeCl because the target structure differs from the CS structures [CS1–CS5], target structure.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The CS route uses dense rare-earth metal, RCl₃, and Te, so precursor activity is moderate to low [CS1–CS5]. The successful 1050 °C synthesis of RTeCl suggests diffusion can be overcome, likely aided by halide/chalcogenide transport or local melt formation [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is not trivial because CS analogues are 9-coordinate La–Nd phases, while target YTeCl is a 2D octahedral Pmma phase [CS1–CS5], target structure. Phase control is a known weakness because dimorphism and difficulty beyond Nd are reported [CS1–CS5].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is less severe thermally than Route 1 because SS analogues GdSI/HoSI were made at 900 °C in sealed fused-silica ampoules [SS4–SS5]. It still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed-tube operation, volatile chalcogen/halide containment, and air-sensitive precursors [SS4–SS5], [CS1–CS5]. CS indicates that Te–Cl rare-earth chemistry may require more robust Ta containment than the S/I SS analogues [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives general support for sealed-tube halide synthesis but not for this exact Y–Te–Cl route, so TS is only weakly informative [TS1–TS5].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

SS analogues show that equimolar lanthanoid metal + sulfur + iodine can give phase-pure MSI phases at 900 °C over 10 d [SS5]. This supports the feasibility of forming 2D MChX sheets, but the target replaces S/I with Te/Cl, which changes volatility and bonding and may reduce the composition window [SS4–SS5], [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference]. The precursor activity remains limited by dense Y metal and solid YCl₃, and the long anneal suggests slow diffusion [SS5]. The exact Pmma YTeCl structure is not reported in SS, so structural-transfer confidence is moderate rather than high [SS2, SS4, SS5].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C processing by using CVT/flux temperatures analogous to ErSeI synthesis around 800–850 °C [SS2]. However, CVT requires high vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile halide transport agents, pre-synthesized feed, and careful inert handling [SS2]. Air-sensitive rare-earth halides and toxic/corrosive volatile chlorides remain significant hazards [SS2], [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for CVT of YTeCl and is scored neutral for this route-specific method.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT/flux improves kinetics because transport occurs through vapor or molten salt species rather than purely through solid-state diffusion [SS2]. SS2 directly demonstrates single-crystal growth for a structurally related 2D rare-earth chalcogenide halide, supporting higher precursor mobility and crystallization probability [SS2]. Starting from pre-synthesized YTeCl feed further reduces structural reconstruction, although this is an inferred optimization rather than directly reported for YTeCl [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is composition control because Te and Cl activities may drift in a thermal gradient [CS1–CS5], [SS2], [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [target structure]. There is no evidence from the target description or CS/SS/TS that the Pmma cell exceeds 40 atoms, so the large-cell amorphization trigger is not applied. The target does have mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing YTe₄Cl₂ octahedra, which triggers one amorphization/frustration criterion [target structure]. The resulting risk is low–medium, with uncertainty arising from the face-sharing component and possible polymorphism [target structure], [CS1–CS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YTeCl is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom, below the common synthesizability threshold but not exactly zero [target structure]. The CS analogues strongly support the Y–Te–Cl chemistry because RTeCl phases for La–Nd are experimentally accessible from RCl₃ + R + Te in sealed Ta tubes [CS1–CS5]. The SS analogues strongly support the 2D MChX structural motif, especially ErSeI/GdSI/HoSI-type sheets with MCh₄X₂ octahedra and L-shaped halide coordination [SS2, SS4, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed anhydrous halide/chalcogenide chemistry, which stabilizes volatile Cl/Te components and permits transport or local fluxing [CS1–CS5], [SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: CS reports dimorphism and difficulty extending RTeCl beyond Nd, so smaller Y may require a narrow temperature/chemical-potential window [CS1–CS5], [Reasonable Inference].
