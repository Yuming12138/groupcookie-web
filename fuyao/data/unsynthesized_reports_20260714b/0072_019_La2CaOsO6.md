### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high end of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” bin and therefore receives 90/100 by the required rubric. CS strongly supports stability because NaLa₂OsO₆, NaNd₂OsO₆, LiNd₂OsO₆, and NaLa₂IrO₆ include multiple E_hull = 0.0 analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). SS and TS also support accessible metastability because structurally/thermodynamically similar LiLa₂ReO₆ has E_hull ≈ 0.062 eV/atom and is synthesized (SS1, TS1), while La₅Os₃MnO₁₆ has E_hull ≈ 0.044 eV/atom and is synthesized (TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that CaLa₂OsO₆ is not reported directly in the provided data.

#### F2 — Route 1, flux

Flux conditions are moderate because closely related Os double perovskites grow at only 600–625 °C in hydroxide fluxes (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS3, SS4), and Ir analogues grow at 700 °C (CS5). The deductions arise from sealed silver tubes, controlled water content, multistep handling, and Os toxicity/oxidation risk, all supported by flux analogue descriptions (CS1, CS4). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydroxide flux synthesis, so TS contributes neutral support. The main uncertainty is whether a KOH/Ca-containing flux can incorporate Ca²⁺ rather than forming competing Ca–Os or La–Os oxides.

#### F3 — Route 1, flux

Kinetics are favorable because molten hydroxide flux enables reaction at 600–700 °C, far below temperatures normally required for refractory oxide diffusion, as shown by Na/Li Os perovskite analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS3). Structural reconstruction is also favorable because the target’s CaO₆/OsO₆ corner-sharing framework closely resembles NaLa₂OsO₆ and NaPr₂OsO₆ frameworks with similar Os–O distances and tilt angles (CS1, SS3, SS4). The score is not higher because CaLa₂OsO₆ requires nominal Os⁴⁺ rather than the Na/Li analogues’ nominal Os⁵⁺, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from charge balance and is not validated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2, solid-state

Solid-state synthesis is more severe because analogous La–Re/Os oxides require 1000–1050 °C, vacuum or Ar, and long heating times (CS3, SS1, TS1, TS3, TS4). Sealed-tube operation and oxygen-potential control are supported by La₅Os₃MnO₁₆ and La₅Re₃MO₁₆ syntheses (TS3, TS4, SS2). Toxic OsO₂ handling further lowers the condition score, as TS3 explicitly notes OsO₂ and sealed-tube precautions. The uncertainty is whether lower-temperature prereaction or reactive precursors could reduce the severity.

#### F3 — Route 2, solid-state

Kinetics are limited by refractory oxide diffusion and cation ordering, consistent with LiLa₂ReO₆ requiring staged heating and Re/ReO₃ chemistry (CS3, SS1, TS1). La–Re/Os analogues require 24–48 h high-temperature holds in sealed tubes (SS2, TS3, TS4), indicating slow equilibration. The route is chemically attractive for Os⁴⁺ because OsO₂ can be used directly, but this is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than a demonstrated CaLa₂OsO₆ route. Remaining uncertainty concerns whether competing La–Os or Ca–Os phases form before the ordered perovskite-derived phase.

#### F2 — Route 3, high-temperature sealed-tube/mineralizer variant

This route is harsh because TS2 uses 1450 °C sealed tantalum tubes under vacuum for a Ca-containing refractory oxide/oxyfluoride, while TS3/TS4 use high-temperature sealed tubes for Os/Re oxides. The high-temperature and vacuum/sealed-system requirements trigger multiple severity deductions. CS provides insufficient evidence for this exact high-temperature Ca–La–Os route and is neutral. The route may be useful only if lower-temperature flux or solid-state methods fail.

#### F3 — Route 3, high-temperature sealed-tube/mineralizer variant

Higher temperature can improve diffusion compared with Route 2, as suggested by high-temperature crystal growth in TS2 and sealed-tube solid-state reactions in TS4. However, Os volatility/oxidation risk and possible chloride/mineralizer contamination are [Reasonable Inference] limitations not resolved by the provided data. Structural reconstruction remains substantial because no direct CaLa₂OsO₆ precursor or topotactic pathway is given in CS/SS/TS. Therefore, kinetics are only moderate despite high T.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization-risk trigger: large cation size/coordination mismatch among CaO₆, LaO₉, and OsO₆ environments, supported by the target description and analogous size-contrasted perovskite frameworks in CS1/SS3. The framework is not reported as mixed edge/face/vertex sharing; the target description states corner-sharing CaO₆ and OsO₆ octahedra, consistent with CS1/SS3. Unit-cell atom count above 40 is not established from the provided target or CS/SS/TS data, so that check is treated as not triggered/uncertain. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, score 80.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaLa₂OsO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a strongly unstable compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and several close Os/Re perovskite-derived analogues are experimentally synthesized at E_hull values from 0 to about 0.06 eV/atom (CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1). The CS and SS data strongly support formation of La–Os perovskite-derived frameworks by hydroxide flux, especially NaLa₂OsO₆ with nearly matching octahedral tilts and Os–O distances (CS1, SS3). The most critical enabling factor is use of a liquid flux or sealed controlled-atmosphere route to overcome slow diffusion in refractory La–Os/Ca–Os oxides, as demonstrated by flux-grown Na/Li Os analogues (CS1, CS4). The most critical limiting factor is oxidation-state and composition control: Ca²⁺ substitution requires nominal Os⁴⁺, whereas the best flux analogues stabilize Na/Li phases with nominal Os⁵⁺ (CS1, CS4; charge-balance inference is [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require empirical optimization of Ca activity, flux chemistry, and oxygen potential.
