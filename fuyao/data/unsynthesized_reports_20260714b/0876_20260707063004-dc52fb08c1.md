### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state or tie-line-stable phase under the computational reference conditions [Target]. Many similar borates in the data also sit at or near the hull, including K₃B₆O₁₀Cl, K₃B₆O₁₀Br, Ba₂Cd(BO₂)₆, Ba₂B₅O₉(OH), and Sr₂B₅O₉(OH) (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, CS3/TS5, SS5/TS4). The remaining uncertainty is kinetic and compositional rather than thermodynamic, because zero hull energy does not guarantee easy crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: solid-state

Solid-state analogues use accessible furnaces, air, and ambient pressure, which supports a moderate condition-severity score (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS3). The deductions arise from multi-step calcination/regrinding and the likely need to control volatile K/Pb/B components [SS1/TS1, CS1; Reasonable Inference from SS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an exact K–Lu–Pb borate solid-state recipe, so the precursor excess and sealing strategy are extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 1: solid-state

The kinetics are the weakest for the ceramic route because analogues require long holds and repeated grindings, indicating diffusion/reconstruction limitations (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS3). The target contains refractory Lu₂O₃ and a large, multi-cation borate framework, which likely increases diffusion and ordering difficulty [Reasonable Inference]. The route is plausible but may produce competing borates, PbO-rich phases, or LuBO₃-like impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: flux

The flux route avoids extreme pressure and can operate below 1000 °C, as shown by borate flux growth at 750→600 °C (SS4). It still requires controlled cooling, Pt hardware, flux removal, and handling of PbO, so deductions for multi-step processing, volatile/toxic components, and stoichiometry control are appropriate [SS4, CS4; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact K₇Lu₂B₁₅PbO₃₀ flux composition, so the PbO–K₂O–B₂O₃ self-flux is inferred.

#### F3 — Route 2: flux

Flux growth improves kinetics by dissolving precursors and enabling solution-mediated transport, which is supported by successful borate and framework single-crystal growth in SS4 and CS4. The score is not higher because SS4 reports impurities and difficulty with solid-state access, indicating a narrow phase window. Lu₂O₃ dissolution into borate/PbO flux is plausible but not directly proven by CS/SS/TS, so this remains a key uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: hydrothermal

Hydrothermal analogues form borates at lower temperatures than ceramic routes, including 170–280 °C autoclave syntheses and 565–575 °C high-pressure syntheses (CS2, SS2/TS2, CS3/CS5/SS5). The condition score is limited by sealed vessels, pressure, mineralizers, and toxic Pb-containing feeds [SS2/TS2, CS5/SS5; Reasonable Inference]. The pressure is substantial but below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty threshold (SS2/TS2, CS5/SS5).

#### F3 — Route 3: hydrothermal

Hydrothermal media can accelerate borate assembly and crystal growth, as shown for K borogermanate and K bromo-borate analogues (CS2, SS2/TS2). However, close high-pressure borate analogues are often hydroxylated/hydrated, such as Ba₂B₅O₉(OH) and Sr₂B₅O₉(OH), which raises concern that the anhydrous target may not be favored hydrothermally (CS3, CS5, SS5/TS4, TS5). The route is therefore useful for exploratory crystal growth but is less directly transferable than flux growth.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target’s formula contains 55 atoms per formula unit and a very broad cation size/coordination range, both of which increase glass-forming or amorphization risk [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Borate systems commonly support both crystalline and glassy networks, and CS5 reports concern about glassy products in related hydrothermal/borate processing [CS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes explicit vertex+edge+face sharing coexistence in the target, so only two F4 triggers are counted.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₇Lu₂B₁₅PbO₃₀ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support borate crystallization by solid-state, flux, and hydrothermal methods, with especially close structural/thermodynamic support from K₃B₆O₁₀Cl/K₃B₆O₁₀Br and Ba₂Cd(BO₂)₆ (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3). The most critical enabling factor is solution or melt-mediated borate assembly, because flux/hydrothermal routes overcome slow cation diffusion seen in solid-state analogues (SS4, CS4, CS2, SS2/TS2). The most critical limiting factor is compositional control of K, Pb, and B under high-temperature conditions, especially PbO toxicity/volatility and alkali/boron loss [Reasonable Inference from SS4 and SS1]. The best practical route is therefore a PbO–K₂O–B₂O₃ self-flux with slow cooling and small precursor screens.
