### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. The closest chemical/structural/thermodynamic analogue Nb₃TeCl₇ is also on the hull (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1), and many Nb chloride cluster analogues are on or very near the hull (SS-2 / SS-5; TS-2 / TS-3 / TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or survival against sulfur/chlorine volatilization during heating [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires sealed-ampoule high-temperature synthesis around 817 °C, directly supported by Nb₃TeCl₇ synthesis at 1090 K for 1 week (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires controlled atmosphere, volatile chloride handling, and air-sensitive NbCl₅ (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). The severity is therefore moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strong kinetic advantage is that the nearest analogue has the same Nb₃ChCl₇ layered motif and forms quantitatively in one step (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). The kinetic limitation is the use of Nb metal, which is refractory and may require chloride-mediated transport to react fully [Reasonable Inference]. The 1-week reaction time in the analogue indicates that ordering and diffusion are not instantaneous (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 operates at lower temperature, guided by NbSeCl₃ formation at 750 K in an evacuated Pyrex tube (CS-3). It still incurs penalties for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile NbCl₅, sulfur volatility [Reasonable Inference], and air-sensitive precursor handling (CS-3). The low temperature reduces thermal severity but does not remove atmosphere and volatility constraints.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The lower-temperature route is kinetically less favorable because the CS-3 analogue formed as a side product in <2% yield and produced an inhomogeneous mixture with unidentified phases (CS-3). It also forms a 1D NbSeCl₃ ribbon rather than the target 2D Nb₃SCl₇ sheet, so structural reconstruction transfer is weak (CS-3). The main uncertainty is whether sulfur stabilizes the desired Nb₃SCl₇ sheet more strongly than selenium under similar conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route is based on molten KCl/LiCl synthesis of K₄Nb₆Cl₁₈ at 600–800 °C under vacuum using specialized quartz/Nb crucible apparatus (TS-5). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C, but it is multi-step and requires vacuum, dry salts, and sensitive Nb chlorides (TS-5). Hence the severity is somewhat worse than the direct sealed-ampoule route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Molten chloride flux should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, as TS-5 demonstrates crystallization of Nb chloride phases from molten salt. Structurally related Nb chloride compounds also form in high yield at 720–800 °C in sealed tubes (SS-3 / SS-4; TS-4). However, alkali chloride flux may stabilize alkali niobium chlorides instead of neutral Nb₃SCl₇, as seen for K/Rb/Cs-containing analogues (SS-4 / SS-5; TS-3 / TS-5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple cation chemistry with only Nb as the framework metal, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference; no contrary evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The target formula unit is small, and the closest Nb₃TeCl₇ analogue forms ordered layered crystals rather than reported amorphous material (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). Direct amorphization data are absent, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct amorphization behavior**, but structural simplicity supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SCl₇ is likely a **ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest analogue Nb₃TeCl₇ is also on the hull (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis because Nb₃TeCl₇ has essentially the same layered Nb₃ChCl₇ topology and is obtained quantitatively from NbCl₅, Nb, and chalcogen in a sealed quartz ampoule (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a directly transferable chloride-mediated redox route using NbCl₅ + Nb to generate reduced Nb chloride sheets (CS-1 / SS-1 / TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is volatility/stoichiometry control for NbCl₅ and especially sulfur under sealed-tube heating [Reasonable Inference], compounded by possible competing Nb–S–Cl phases suggested by the poor phase control in the NbSeCl₃ case (CS-3). Overall, the material is quite synthesizable by expert sealed-ampoule solid-state methods, but optimization of sulfur chemical potential will likely be essential.
