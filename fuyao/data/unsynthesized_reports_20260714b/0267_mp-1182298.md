### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability boundary specified by the rubric, so a score of 90 is assigned. CS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable, including Ba/Sr/Mg silicates and Na–Ba–Sc silicate with E_hull near 0–0.015 eV/atom (CS-1/CS-2/CS-3). SS borate analogues are also stable or near-stable, especially Ba₂Mg(BO₃)₂ at E_hull = 0 and Sr₂Sc₂B₄O₁₁ at 0.006 eV/atom (SS-1/SS-4). TS analogues show that materials around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom can still be synthesized, but they also show possible phase competition near this metastability level (TS-1/TS-2/TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires >1000 °C annealing, consistent with Ba₂Mg(BO₃)₂ at 1273 K (SS-4), Sr₂Sc₂B₄O₁₁ at 950–1050 °C plus melting/cooling variants (SS-1), and Ba/Sr/Ca silicate analogues at 1100 °C (CS-1/CS-2). Air and ambient pressure are supported by SS-1, SS-4, and TS-2, so no atmosphere or pressure penalty is applied. Multi-step grinding/regrinding is required because similar ceramic reactions explicitly use regrinding or repeated heat treatments (CS-1/CS-2/SS-4/TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is whether ScBaNa(BO₃)₂ forms directly or through competing Ba/Na/Sc borates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Carbonates and H₃BO₃/B₂O₃ are reactive precursor choices supported by SS-4 and CS-4/SS-3, but Sc₂O₃ is refractory and used in high-temperature flux/ceramic Sc systems (CS-3, SS-1). Solid-state diffusion is therefore expected to be only moderate, especially for Sc–O network formation [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally, the target must reconstruct from carbonate/oxide precursors into ordered ScO₆ and BO₃ layers; repeated firing in analogues supports a kinetic penalty (SS-1, SS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS on Sc-bearing borate kinetics specifically; TS-2 only supports general solid-state feasibility for a Na-containing oxide.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses 1050–1150 °C flux conditions, matching NaBaScSi₂O₇ at 1150 °C (CS-3) and Na–La borate growth at 1120–1160 °C (CS-4/SS-3). Ambient air synthesis is directly supported by CS-3 and CS-4/SS-3. The route is multi-step because it requires melting, controlled cooling, and flux removal, as seen in CS-3 and CS-4. Volatility is a concern for Na/B components [Reasonable Inference], but the reference flux routes do not require sealed vessels, so no sealed-system penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth is kinetically favorable because it provides a liquid transport medium; Na₂CO₃/H₃BO₃ fluxes are demonstrated for Na borates (CS-4/SS-3), and MoO₃-based flux crystallizes NaBaScSi₂O₇ from Sc₂O₃ at 1150 °C (CS-3). Ion diffusion is therefore much easier than in a dry solid-state route, especially for refractory Sc₂O₃ [Reasonable Inference]. Structural assembly remains moderately challenging because flux reactions can yield multiple phases and require washing/isolation, as explicitly noted for NaBaScSi₂O₇ flux growth and related flux methods (CS-3, SS-5). TS gives mixed signals: TS-4 shows easy single-phase borate formation, whereas TS-1 and TS-5 report side products/amorphous or unidentified phases, so TS is treated as neutral-to-cautionary.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Melt growth likely requires >1000 °C and possibly substantially higher temperature, as close borate melt analogues use 1465–1480 °C (SS-1, SS-2). The baseline score assumes a Na/B-rich self-flux may lower the effective liquidus below 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], but this is uncertain. A sealed or covered setup may be needed because Na and boron species can volatilize at melt temperatures [Reasonable Inference]; sealed Pt ampoules are used for volatile flux systems in CS-5, though not directly for borates. Equipment and thermal control are more demanding than Route 2, especially if Czochralski-type growth is attempted (SS-2).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Once a melt is formed, diffusion is rapid and direct crystallization can be efficient, as shown by congruent-melting Ca₄GdB₃O₁₀ (SS-2) and slow-cooled borate crystals (SS-1, SS-5). The main kinetic uncertainty is not diffusion but whether ScBaNa(BO₃)₂ has a stable primary crystallization field or decomposes/incongruently melts [Reasonable Inference]. Pre-reaction of the batch is recommended because SS-1 uses binary borate intermediates before crystal-growth steps. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for melt growth of Na–Ba–Sc borates, so this route is scored below the flux route despite good liquid-state kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very differently sized cations, especially large Ba²⁺/Na⁺ and small B³⁺/Sc³⁺, so cation-size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s formula unit contains 11 atoms, and the R-3 cell is not clearly above 40 atoms from the provided target description; CS/SS/TS do not provide direct unit-cell atom-count evidence for the target, so C2 is not triggered. The target description lists coordination environments but not explicit coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing; therefore C3 has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is not triggered. The resulting F4 score of 80 reflects low–medium amorphization risk rather than high glass-forming tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScBaNa(BO₃)₂ is likely a marginally metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability threshold, and thermodynamic analogues near this energy are reported as synthesizable (TS-1/TS-2/TS-5). The strongest support comes from structurally similar borates, especially Ba₂Mg(BO₃)₂ with trigonal Ba/BO₃/MO₆ motifs (SS-4), and chemically similar NaBaScSi₂O₇, which confirms Na–Ba–Sc oxide frameworks can crystallize from high-temperature flux (CS-3). The most critical enabling factor is use of a molten borate/alkali flux, because it improves Sc incorporation and allows slow crystallization of BO₃-containing frameworks (CS-3, CS-4, SS-3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control of Na and B at high temperature, since alkali/borate components can volatilize or shift flux composition [Reasonable Inference; alkali excess is used in TS-3]. Overall, the target should be synthesizable, with Route 2 offering the best balance of thermodynamic access, kinetic mobility, and phase-control flexibility.
