### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a near-maximum thermodynamic score. This is reinforced by several related rare-earth selenides having E_hull = 0.0, including CS2/CS3 and TS1–TS4. The main uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclude competing polymorphs, but the thermodynamic signal is strongly favorable.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is not temperature-extreme because the closest LnCuSe₂ flux synthesis uses 1173 K / 900 °C, below the >1000 °C penalty threshold (CS1). However, it requires Ar/vacuum sealed-tube handling, volatile Se, multiple operations, and air-sensitive La/Se handling, all supported by CS1. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS that this exact halide-flux route for LaFeSe₂ is lower-severity than CS1, so SS/TS are treated as neutral-to-supportive rather than overriding CS1.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The halide flux substantially improves transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, as inferred from successful LnCuSe₂ crystal growth in KBr/KI flux (CS1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target has the same P2₁/c LnMSe₂ topology as CeCuSe₂/GdCuSe₂ analogues (CS1/CS3). The uncertainty is Fe: CS/CS3 closest analogues use Cu, while Fe analogues in SS2/TS1 have different structures, so Fe incorporation may require tuning.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct solid-state synthesis is simpler in step count, as CS2 reports single-step fusion in evacuated silica tubes. It is penalized for likely >1000 °C operation based on CS2/SS4, vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and air-sensitive/toxic precursor handling. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that LaFeSe₂ can form cleanly by one low-temperature direct reaction, so the route remains conditionally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction from La, Fe, and Se has lower precursor activity because La and Fe metals are dense elemental sources, and Fe diffusion is slow **[Reasonable Inference]**. Related direct routes can form transition-metal selenides, but SS4/TS3 reports only a few crystals and mostly amorphous byproducts for ThMnSe₃, indicating possible kinetic/phase-control problems. CS2 supports direct fusion for LnCuSe₂-like chemistry, but the reported chemistry is not Fe-specific and therefore only partially transferable.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Reactive alkali flux routes operate at moderate temperatures of 973–1173 K in SS3/TS2 and SS5/TS4, avoiding the >1000 °C penalty if kept at ≤900 °C. They still require sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile Se, multistep flux preparation/washing, and air-sensitive alkali/rare-earth components, all indicated by SS3/TS2 and SS5/TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS that alkali selenide flux is necessary for the exact P2₁/c phase, so severity is not reduced relative to Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Reactive alkali selenide fluxes are kinetically favorable because they produced rare-earth selenide single crystals across multiple lanthanides (SS3/TS2) and high-yield quaternary rare-earth selenides (SS5/TS4). The limitation is structural selectivity: SS3/TS2 and SS5/TS4 demonstrate formation of alkali-containing products, so the flux may stabilize Rb/Cs–La–Fe–Se side phases rather than LaFeSe₂. This route is therefore good for crystal growth screening but less directly targeted than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large La/Fe cation size contrast, which triggers C1 by fundamental ionic-radius reasoning **[Reasonable Inference]** and is consistent with the long Ln–Se versus shorter transition-metal–Se distances in CS1/CS3. C2 is not triggered because the closest P2₁/c analogues have simple 16-atom conventional formulas such as Ce₄Cu₄Se₈/Gd₄Cu₄Se₈ (CS1/CS3). C3 is not triggered because the target and close analogues report corner/edge sharing but not simultaneous face-sharing connectivity; remaining uncertainty is that SS4/TS3 report amorphous byproducts for some related transition-metal selenides.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LaFeSe₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related rare-earth selenides are also on or near the hull (CS2/CS3, TS1–TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1, where a very close monoclinic P2₁/c LnMSe₂ analogue was grown by KBr/KI flux under sealed-tube conditions. The most critical enabling factor is flux-assisted mass transport and slow cooling, which should help overcome sluggish Fe/La solid-state diffusion and promote the ordered P2₁/c framework (CS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among La–Se binaries, Fe–Se binaries, and possible flux-stabilized side phases, a risk consistent with low yields or difficult phase control in CS1 and SS1/SS2/TS1. Overall, the target is synthetically plausible, with best prospects from halide-flux screening rather than direct dry sintering.
