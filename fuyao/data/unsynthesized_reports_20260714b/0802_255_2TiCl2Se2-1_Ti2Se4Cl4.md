### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0480 eV/atom, placing it just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so a high F1 score is justified. Several chemically/structurally similar Nb-Se-Cl and Nb-S-Br materials are stable or nearly stable, including NbSe₂Cl₂ with E_hull = 0.0 and Nb(SBr)₂ with E_hull ≈ 0.0008 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, SS-5). Thermodynamic analogues with higher hull energies around 0.07–0.10 eV/atom are experimentally accessible, indicating that 0.048 eV/atom is not prohibitive (TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that Ti-specific competing phases such as TiSe₂, TiCl₃, TiCl₄, or TiSeClₓ are not directly represented in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile halogen/Se species, and toxic or air-sensitive halogen chemistry. These penalties are supported by NbSe₂Cl₂ CVT in evacuated sealed tubes with a gradient (CS-1, SS-2) and chlorine/halogen vapor transport precedents (TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The temperature itself is not severe because close analogues form at 400–515 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS-1, SS-2). The main uncertainty is whether TiCl₄ volatility makes pressure and stoichiometry control more difficult than in Nb analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderately favorable because vapor transport bypasses slow bulk diffusion, and CVT successfully produces closely related layered MSe₂Cl₂ phases (CS-1, SS-2). The strongest structural support comes from NbSe₂Cl₂, which shares the 2D sheet, 8-coordinate metal, Se–Se dimer, and bridging halide motif with the target (CS-1, SS-2). However, the 3–4 week growth time in the Nb analogue indicates slow ordering/crystal growth kinetics (CS-1, SS-2). Uncertainty remains due to the absence of a direct Ti-Se-Cl synthesis example.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar sealed-tube and volatile-chloride penalties, supported by NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ sealed-tube synthesis of Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ and NbCl₅/Nb/Se sealed-tube synthesis of NbSeCl₃ (CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3). The route does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, since analogues use about 477–530 °C (CS-2, CS-5). It is penalized for multistep character if TiSe₂ is pre-synthesized and for moisture-sensitive chloride precursors. The uncertain point is whether a single-step mixture can directly form Ti(SeCl)₂ without first preparing TiSe₂.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable than CVT because Ti/Se/chloride diffusion and reconstruction are required over long durations. Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ requires 2–3 weeks at 530 °C and poor reproducibility is reported, while NbSeCl₃ appears as a minor side product in inhomogeneous mixtures (CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3). Use of TiSe₂ as a precursor should reduce reconstruction relative to elemental Ti, but exact stoichiometry is nontrivial. The main uncertainty is whether TiSe₂ chlorination gives the desired Se–Se dimerized TiSe₂Cl₂ sheet instead of TiSe₂, TiCl₃, or TiCl₄-derived products.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from low to moderate temperatures, supported by Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ synthesis at 180 °C in Se/SeCl₄ melt and flux selenide synthesis at 450–500 °C (TS-4, TS-1). It is still penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile SeCl₄/Se-halide reagents, multistep handling/purification, and toxic/corrosive precursors (TS-4). No high external pressure is required, but TS-4 uses closed silica ampoules inside a steel autoclave for containment. The uncertainty is whether Ti dissolves and reacts sufficiently in the low-temperature selenium chloride melt.

#### F3 — Route 3

The SeCl₄/Se melt route may provide high precursor activity and good mass transport, as shown by Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ formation in a Se-halide melt (TS-4). However, structural transferability is weaker because the TS-4 analogue is a 1D Pd Se-polychloride compound rather than a 2D Ti/Nb-like MSe₂Cl₂ sheet. Flux routes can improve diffusion, but they may also stabilize Se-rich molecular or chain compounds rather than the desired layered phase (TS-4). Thus kinetics are chemically promising but structurally uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, Ti, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern. The described formula and layered C2/m structure do not indicate a very large unit cell or highly mixed polyhedral network; close analogues are crystalline layered or ribbon phases rather than glasses (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact unit-cell atom count or full connectivity topology, so the amorphization risk is judged low but not zero. Volatile-component loss may cause secondary phases, but that is a phase-purity issue rather than intrinsic amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti(SeCl)₂ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase, since its E_hull is 0.048 eV/atom, just below the high-synthesizability threshold. The CS and SS analogues strongly support feasibility because NbSe₂Cl₂ and Nb(SBr)₂ show very similar layered motifs, 8-coordinate transition-metal centers, chalcogen–chalcogen dimers, and bridging halides (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted sealed-tube transport, which can form layered metal chalcogenide halides at only ~400–530 °C (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase control under volatile Se/Cl chemical potentials, since Nb-Se-Cl analogues show long reaction times, polymorph/stoichiometry sensitivity, poor reproducibility, or side-product formation (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sealed-ampoule design and systematic tuning of Ti:Se:Cl activity.
