### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0240 eV/atom, so it lies well within the commonly synthesizable metastability window ≤0.05 eV/atom. CS provides useful support because chemically similar BiI phases have E_hull ≈0.027–0.028 eV/atom in CS1/CS2 and TlBr has E_hull ≈0.0047 eV/atom in CS3. SS and TS also support the score: structurally/thermodynamically similar Tl iodides are often on or near the hull, including SS1/SS3/SS4 and TS1–TS5. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull supports thermodynamic accessibility but does not identify whether the P4/mmm TlI polymorph is the room-temperature ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because sealed-tube synthesis uses controlled Ar/vacuum environments and volatile iodine, both supported by CS1/CS2 and CS5. Toxic Tl handling is inferred from Tl glovebox handling in CS5, and multi-step annealing/powdering is supported by TS2. The temperatures are mild relative to refractory solid-state synthesis, so no high-temperature or high-pressure deductions apply. Uncertainty comes from whether elemental Tl + I₂ can be kept stoichiometric without iodine transport in the ampoule.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from reactive iodine chemistry and moderate temperatures, as CS1/CS2 form BiI at 558 K. Ion diffusion is likely favorable because Tl/I systems are soft, low-melting halides [Reasonable Inference], and TS2 iodides react at 250–350 °C. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction/polymorph selection: CS1/CS2 explicitly report multiple BiI modifications and difficult phase purity. Thus the route is chemically plausible but phase-selective rather than automatically reliable.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because SS2/SS4 and TS1/TS3 demonstrate TlI-based hydrothermal syntheses at only 160–175 °C without high pressure beyond autogenous autoclave pressure. No inert or vacuum atmosphere is required in those sources. The main deduction is toxicity/corrosivity from TlI and HI, supported by their use in SS2/SS4/TS1/TS3 and standard chemical hazard knowledge. Remaining uncertainty is whether binary TlI will recrystallize into the target rather than a more stable common TlI polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal HI should provide high precursor activity because TlI participates in iodide-rich acidic solution reactions in SS2/SS4/TS1/TS3. Solution-mediated transport largely bypasses solid-state diffusion limitations. Structural reconstruction receives a moderate score because the method can form complex Tl–iodide crystals, but TS1 reports twinning and low yield of untwinned crystals for AgTl₂I₃. The key uncertainty is whether HI stabilizes the desired P4/mmm layered binary phase or simply dissolves/reprecipitates another TlI form.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 requires sealed ampoules, vacuum/inert atmosphere, and temperature-gradient equipment, all supported by SS1/SS3/CS5. It also involves toxic thallium handling, for which CS5 explicitly describes glovebox/Ar handling of Tl. No extreme high temperature or high pressure is required, so the severity is moderate rather than severe. The main uncertainty is iodine partial pressure control during melt growth.

#### F3 — Route 3

Melt growth has favorable kinetics because the reacting species are in a molten or highly mobile state; SS1/SS3/TS5 demonstrate Bridgman growth of related Tl iodide/chalcogenide crystals. Ion diffusion should be excellent compared with solid-state powder reaction. The structural reconstruction score is limited by the need for slow growth/annealing and by phase sensitivity: SS1 reports month-long annealing, and CS1/CS2 show monoiodide polymorph problems. This route is better for single crystals than rapid phase screening.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 avoids high temperature and high pressure but requires vacuum and low-temperature deposition equipment, as shown in CS3/SS5. It is penalized for multi-step deposition/temperature-ramp control and thallium-containing vapor hazards [Reasonable Inference]. CS3/SS5 provide useful evidence for low-temperature halide evaporation; TS provides insufficient direct thin-film evidence, so TS is neutral, score 50, for this route’s deposition severity. The route is experimentally specialized but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 4

CS3/SS5 support vapor availability because TlI is included among evaporated halide salts. However, crystallization is uncertain because those same sources emphasize metastable halide polymorphs that are temperature-sensitive and disappear near room temperature. Composition control is moderate for binary TlI but can be affected by volatile iodide species [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for vapor-deposition kinetics, so TS is neutral, score 50, for this kinetic factor.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has no multication radius mismatch because Tl is the only cation; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a cation-radius amorphization issue and are neutral for C1. The target description does not indicate a unit cell larger than 40 atoms; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a large-cell amorphization penalty and are neutral for C2. C3 is triggered because the target contains edge- and corner-sharing octahedra plus square-pyramidal coordination, and SS1/SS3 show structurally similar Tl–I frameworks with varied coordination. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium, mainly relevant for vapor deposition or rapid quenching.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an obviously stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.0240 eV/atom. CS, SS, and TS collectively support synthesis of near-hull Tl/iodide materials: CS1/CS2 show monoiodide formation, SS2/SS4 and TS1/TS3 show TlI-based hydrothermal crystallization, and SS1/SS3/TS5 show Bridgman growth of related Tl iodides. The most critical enabling factor is the soft, mobile iodide chemistry that allows low-temperature solution, sealed-ampoule, or melt routes rather than refractory solid-state synthesis, supported by CS1/CS2 and SS2/SS4. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: CS1/CS2 report multiple BiI modifications, CS3/SS5 show metastable halide phases can be temperature-sensitive, and TS1 reports crystal twinning/low yield. Overall, synthesis is feasible, but confirming the P4/mmm structure will require careful screening of cooling rate, iodine activity, and possibly low-temperature stabilization.
