### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.048 eV/atom, just inside the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom window, so it is likely accessible as a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase [Target]. Many structurally and chemically related chalcophosphates are calculated at or near the hull, including CuPS₃ [CS-2], CrAg(PS₃)₂ [CS-3], AgVP₂S₆ [SS-1], and NaSbP₂S₆ [SS-2]. Thermodynamic analogues span ~0.029–0.099 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized, supporting that modest hull energies do not preclude synthesis [TS-1, TS-4, TS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly validates hydrogenated P₃HS₆, so H-specific phase stability has insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to sealed-tube thiophosphate syntheses: 623 K for CuPS₃ [CS-2], 600 °C for AgVP₂S₆ [SS-1], and 773 K for NaSbP₂S₆ [SS-2]. The major severity penalties are from sealed vacuum/inert ampoule operation and volatile/toxic H/S-containing reagents; sealed tubes are directly supported [CS-2, SS-1, SS-2], while H₂S use is a **[Reasonable Inference]**. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is required, so the score remains moderate. Uncertainty comes from how much H₂S pressure is required to stabilize the S–H site.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

P₂S₅ is a chemically active P–S precursor used in several thiophosphate syntheses [CS-2, SS-2, SS-4], and the absence of refractory metal cations should reduce diffusion barriers compared with Ag/V/Cr/Fe systems [SS-1, CS-3, CS-4]. Structural reconstruction is still nontrivial because the target is a specific 2D P₃HS₆ sheet with one S–H site [Target], whereas many analogues are metal thiophosphate frameworks [CS-2, SS-1, SS-2]. Long reaction times in AgVP₂S₆ and NaSbP₂S₆ indicate ordering/crystallization can require days to weeks [SS-1, SS-2]. Thus kinetics are favorable but not trivial.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is low-temperature and avoids high-temperature solid diffusion, consistent with P₄S₃ solution crystallization under reflux/cooling [SS-3] and solution diffusion chalcogenide synthesis at ambient temperature [TS-1]. However, it requires multiple operations, dry/inert handling, and likely H₂S or hydrosulfide reagents by **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS-1 also uses Schlenk/nitrogen methods, supporting the controlled-atmosphere penalty. The score is lower than Route 1 because operational complexity and precursor sensitivity are higher.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution transport gives high effective mobility, and SS-3 shows that P–S molecular phases can crystallize from solution. TS-1 supports diffusion-based crystallization of a thermodynamic chalcogenide analogue, although it is As/Cu/Cl rather than P–S–H. The limiting kinetic/selectivity issue is that SS-3 reports metastable P₄S₃ crystallization with coexisting phases and very low yield, implying a risk of crystallizing molecular P–S impurities instead of the desired 2D sheet. Therefore kinetics are high for mass transport but only moderate for structural selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate CVT temperatures around 380–450 °C, guided by TiP₂S₆ CVT at 420 °C [CS-5]. Vacuum ampoules, temperature gradients, and possible halogen transport agents are supported by CS-5 and TS-3. The penalties arise from controlled atmosphere, volatile species, multi-step pre-reaction plus transport, and toxic/corrosive transport chemistry [TS-3; Reasonable Inference]. No high external pressure or ultrahigh temperature is involved.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can improve crystallinity and bypass bulk diffusion, as shown for TiP₂S₆ [CS-5] and MnIn₂Se₄ [TS-3]. Vapor transport is kinetically favorable, but the target’s H-containing composition has no direct CVT precedent in CS/SS/TS. TS-3 notes that transport conditions required optimization to avoid multiphase condensation, which is a relevant warning for P–S–H volatility. The route is plausible for crystals but less robust for stoichiometric powder synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target lacks multiple size-mismatched metal cations and has a relatively simple P–S–H composition [Target]. The provided structure description does not indicate >40 atoms per unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks [Target]. Structurally similar materials can crystallize as ordered 2D sheets in monoclinic cells, e.g., AgVP₂S₆ [SS-1] and NaSbP₂S₆ [SS-2], which supports low amorphization risk. The uncertainty is the exact unit-cell Z and whether hydrogen disorder could occur, neither of which is resolved by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

P₃HS₆ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.048 eV/atom, just within the high-stability threshold [Target]. CS and SS analogues strongly support that P–S and thiophosphate frameworks can be synthesized in sealed ampoules at moderate temperatures, especially using P₂S₅, elemental P, and sulfur [CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, SS-2]. TS analogues support that layered chalcogenides with similar thermodynamic accessibility can be made, but they provide weaker chemical transferability because they do not contain P–S–H chemistry [TS-1 to TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed-ampoule control of sulfur chemical potential, including slight sulfur excess as used in NaSbP₂S₆ [SS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is hydrogen incorporation and retention as an S–H bond, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS and which likely requires controlled H₂S or hydrosulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference].
