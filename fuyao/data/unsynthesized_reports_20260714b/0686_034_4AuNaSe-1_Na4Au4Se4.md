### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is only 0.0110 eV/atom above the hull, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. This is reinforced by chemically similar alkali gold chalcogenides that are on the hull, including LiAuS, NaAuTe, KAuS, and K4Au6S5 (CS-1–CS-4). Thermodynamically similar examples also sit near the hull, such as KAg3S2 at 0.0093 eV/atom and RbAuCdS2 at 0.0127 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that a low E_hull does not guarantee the specific orthorhombic Aem2 polymorph will be the experimentally selected phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 600 °C, directly supported by NaAuTe synthesis from elements at 600 °C for 4 days (CS-2). The main penalties are the need for sealed evacuated/inert ampoules and air-sensitive Na/selenide chemistry, consistent with inert ampoule handling and product sensitivity in related systems (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2). There is no evidence for >1000 °C or high-pressure requirements from CS/SS/TS, so those deductions are not applied. The main uncertainty is the Se vapor pressure and possible Na attack on the ampoule, which are [Reasonable Inference] concerns.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental synthesis has a kinetic bottleneck because Au metal is dense and relatively slow to react, while Na and Se are reactive [Reasonable Inference supported by Au powder preparation complexity in CS-1/CS-3]. Related alkali gold chalcogenides do form at 500–750 °C, so diffusion is not prohibitive when melts/chalcogen activity are present (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). However, long reaction times, slow cooling, and annealing in analogues show that ordering is not instantaneous (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2). Therefore Route 1 is feasible but less kinetically favorable than flux growth.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains in the moderate temperature regime of 500–550 °C, matching LiAuS/NaAuS flux growth and A2Sx flux growth (CS-1, SS-3, TS-2). It is penalized for vacuum/inert sealed-tube handling, volatile Se-rich flux, air-sensitive Na2Se, and multiple operations including precursor handling, heating, slow cooling, and flux removal (CS-1, SS-3, TS-2). No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates multi-GPa pressure or >1000 °C conditions. The uncertainty is solvent/flux removal for a selenide product, because methanol washing is reported for sulfide fluxes but not directly for NaAuSe (CS-1, SS-3).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux growth improves precursor activity and ion transport, as shown by alkali–Au–chalcogenide crystals grown in reactive A2Sx fluxes at 550 °C (SS-3, TS-2) and LiAuS/NaAuS-type reactions at 500–550 °C (CS-1). The flux route directly mitigates dense Au diffusion limitations by providing a chalcogenide melt/flux environment [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/SS-3]. Structural reconstruction remains moderately demanding because the target is a specific 2D ternary phase and analogues require slow cooling (CS-1, SS-3, TS-2). Overall, kinetics are favorable among bulk routes.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The supercritical route has a low temperature of 300 °C, supported by KAg3S2 synthesis in supercritical ethylenediamine (SS-1, TS-1). It is still penalized for sealed evacuated quartz/autoclave operation, Ar counterpressure, toxic/air-sensitive chalcogenide reagents, and multistep workup (SS-1, TS-1; CS-3/SS-2 for alkali chalcogenide sensitivity). The pressure is not high by the scoring definition because 2400 psi is far below 5 GPa (SS-1, TS-1). Its main uncertainty is whether Au–Se chemistry will crystallize as NaAuSe rather than soluble complexes or binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Supercritical ethylenediamine gives excellent transport kinetics, and the structurally/thermodynamically similar KAg3S2 required supercritical conditions to avoid amorphous products (SS-1, TS-1). This supports a high ion-transport score, but transferability is weaker because the analogue is Ag–S rather than Au–Se (SS-1, TS-1). Au may be less reactive/soluble than Ag under the same conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetic transport is strong, but target phase selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant Na/Au cation size and bonding mismatch, giving one amorphization-risk trigger [Reasonable Inference from standard cation radii and different coordination environments in the target]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell has more than 40 atoms, and the target description does not specify mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity. Similar crystalline analogues with alkali–Au–chalcogen frameworks are repeatedly synthesized by slow cooling rather than quenching (CS-1–CS-4, SS-2/SS-3), suggesting crystallization is achievable. The remaining uncertainty is whether the 2D Aem2 framework requires a narrow ordering window [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaAuSe is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, and several chemically or thermodynamically similar alkali/coinage-metal chalcogenides are on or near the convex hull (CS-1–CS-4, TS-1/TS-2). The synthesis evidence is strong chemically: NaAuTe is directly accessible by elemental ampoule fusion at 600 °C (CS-2), and alkali–Au–chalcogenide crystals are routinely obtained by flux/slow-cooling methods (CS-1, CS-3, SS-3, TS-2). The most important enabling factor is a sealed, chalcogen-rich or polyselenide flux environment that can mobilize Au and control Na/Se activity (CS-1, SS-3, TS-2). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Na–Au–Se binaries/ternaries and possible volatility or loss of Na/Se under sealed-tube conditions [Reasonable Inference supported by sensitivity and phase-control issues in CS-2, CS-3, TS-3]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with flux growth preferred over direct elemental sintering.
