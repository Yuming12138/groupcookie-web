### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore merits a high F1 score of 90 [target data]. Close chemical/structural analogues KCrF6, RbCrF6, CaCrF6, SrCrF6, MgCrF6, and CrF5 are reported with E_hull = 0.0, supporting accessibility of related chromium fluorides [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.03–0.07 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, showing that this metastability range can be accessible [TS1–TS5]. Uncertainty remains because TS analogues are chemically less direct, so TS gives only moderate support for NaCrF6 specifically [TS1–TS5].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high multi-GPa pressure because reported ACrF6 photochemistry occurs at ambient temperature [CS2, SS1, SS2]. The severity score is reduced because the route requires elemental F2, anhydrous HF, sealed fluoropolymer apparatus, UV irradiation, and moisture exclusion [CS2, SS1, SS2]. CS and SS provide strong direct evidence; TS provides insufficient useful signal for this anhydrous photochemical fluorination route and is treated as neutral 50. The remaining uncertainty is mainly operational safety rather than thermodynamic feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the reported route uses anhydrous HF solvent plus photochemical oxidation, bypassing slow bulk diffusion [CS2, SS1, SS2]. The direct ACrF6 family precedent strongly supports forming the target network from NaF/CrF3/F2 [CS2, SS1, SS2]. However, SS notes polymorphism for NaCrF6 and phase-purity issues in alkali chromium fluorides, so the structural reconstruction score is not maximal [SS1, SS5]. TS gives insufficient route-specific kinetic evidence for this route and is neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

The autoclave fluorination route uses only moderate temperature, about 300 °C, but requires high-pressure corrosive F2 in Monell autoclaves [CS1, CS3, CS5]. Although the pressure is far below the >5 GPa penalty threshold, F2 toxicity, sealed volatile reagents, and multi-step gas handling impose major severity deductions [CS1, CS3, CS5]. CS gives strong evidence for feasibility in related MCrF6 frameworks, while SS gives indirect support via CrF5/F2 autoclave handling [SS3]. TS provides insufficient useful signal for this fluorine-autoclave route and is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reaction kinetics are aided by the extreme reactivity of F2 and the low 300 °C synthesis temperature used for related MCrF6 materials [CS1, CS3, CS5]. The target’s corner-sharing NaF6/CrF6 network is structurally close to MgCrF6 and CaCrF6, which also contain corner-sharing MF6/CrF6 octahedra and bent ~150° M–F–Cr geometry [CS1, CS5]. The key kinetic/phase uncertainty is oxidation-state control, because NaCrF6 requires Cr(V), whereas divalent MCrF6 analogues imply different charge balance [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS5]. SS and TS are less direct for this route, with TS neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has low target-step temperature but inherits major severity from the need to prepare and handle CrF5, which is made using F2 in a Monel autoclave and purified by volatile handling/sublimation [SS3]. The route also likely requires anhydrous HF or dry fluorine-compatible apparatus, similar to ACrF6 photochemical chemistry [CS2, SS1]. CS and SS support the required precursor chemistry, but no CS/SS/TS source explicitly reports NaF + CrF5 to NaCrF6, so direct precedent is insufficient and treated as neutral 50 for that specific transformation. The severity is therefore comparable to Route 1, but with added precursor-preparation burden.

#### F3 — Route 3

CrF5 is a chemically activated Cr(V) source and contains CrF6 octahedral units, which should reduce oxidation-state and structural-reconstruction barriers [SS3, Reasonable Inference]. NaF is the same alkali fluoride precursor class used in reported ACrF6 synthesis [CS2, SS1, SS2]. If HF or volatile CrF5 transport is used, ion diffusion should be much easier than in a dry refractory solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS2/SS1/SS3]. The uncertainty is direct experimental transferability, because the provided data do not explicitly demonstrate this metathesis route for NaCrF6 [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; neutral 50].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization-risk trigger: a large size/charge contrast between Na+ and high-valent Cr, consistent with the very different target Na–F and Cr–F bond lengths of 2.33 Å and 1.81 Å [target data; Reasonable Inference]. The target framework is simple corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, and similar MgCrF6/CaCrF6 corner-sharing frameworks are known [CS1, CS5]. No provided source indicates a large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence for the target, so C2 and C3 are not triggered [CS1, CS5, target data]. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection rather than amorphization itself [SS1, SS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaCrF6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and related chromium fluorides are often on the hull [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3]. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/CS photochemical ACrF6 chemistry, which explicitly reports preparation of alkali chromium fluorides A = Li–Cs from AF/CrF3/F2 in anhydrous HF [CS2, SS1, SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is access to highly oxidizing, anhydrous fluoride chemistry that stabilizes Cr(V) without hydrolysis [CS2, SS1, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and polymorph control, especially because alkali chromium fluorides are moisture-sensitive and NaCrF6 polymorphism/impurities are noted [SS1, SS5]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but requires a specialized fluorine/HF laboratory rather than conventional solid-state equipment [CS2, SS1, SS2].
