### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, placing it in the highest scoring bin for likely synthesizability as a near-hull phase [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values are reported as synthesized, including 0.037 eV/atom Tl₄SnSe₃ (TS1), 0.047 eV/atom In₄PbBi₃ (TS2), 0.039 eV/atom InTe (TS3), and 0.035 eV/atom InGaTe₂ (TS4). CS evidence is mixed because chemically similar TlTe is much higher at 0.150 eV/atom yet reported synthesized (CS3), while PbTe and GaTe are on hull (CS1/CS5), suggesting both stable and metastable heavy p-block binaries are accessible. Remaining uncertainty is whether the monoclinic Hazelwoodite-like TlAs polymorph is the experimentally selected polymorph rather than another lower-energy Tl–As phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule direct solid-state / melt-anneal

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because evacuated ampoules and toxic volatile elements are required; evacuated Tl–Te ampoules and Ar glovebox handling are directly supported by CS3/SS4, while As-containing sealed dry-Ar ampoule syntheses are supported by SS3/TS1/TS2. Kinetically, direct elemental synthesis is plausible because Tl-containing and p-block binary/ternary analogues are made from elements at 550–700 °C (CS3/SS4/SS5/CS5/TS4). The main uncertainty is whether annealing can select the target 2D monoclinic phase instead of competing Tl–As phases; related systems report phase-purity challenges and long anneals (CS2/TS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Bridgman–Stockbarger melt growth

The severity score is similar to Route 1 because Bridgman still requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, toxic Tl/As handling, and multistep thermal processing [CS3/SS4; SS3/TS1/TS2]. Kinetics are slightly better because melt growth bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion, and Tl–Te single crystals were grown from melts by Bridgman–Stockbarger (CS3/SS4). Phase control remains uncertain because CS3/SS4 explicitly discusses homogeneity ranges and compositions that may or may not be single phase, so slow cooling must be optimized.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: I₂-assisted CVT

CVT has moderate thermal demands but requires sealed evacuated ampoules and a transport agent, as shown for Bi₈Te₉ with I₂ at 770–820 K (CS2). The vapor transport mechanism may help crystal growth of layered phases [CS2; Reasonable Inference], but Tl and As may form competing iodides, so composition control is less favorable [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for TlAs CVT, so the route is plausible but not preferred.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: thin-film vapor deposition

Vacuum deposition is strongly supported for PbTe films and related heavy p-block phases (CS1/SS1), giving a plausible route for metastable TlAs thin films. However, SS1 reports defect/disordered phases and narrow composition ranges, and Tl/As volatility makes stoichiometric control difficult [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for thin-film growth of the thermodynamic analogues, so the kinetic score is low.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: mechanochemistry plus anneal

Mechanochemical synthesis is supported by InTe ball milling from elemental powders under Ar (TS3), showing that binary p-block compounds with similar thermodynamic stability can form at room temperature. The route has attractive low-temperature kinetics, but TS3 also reports multiple phases and minor oxide, indicating phase selection is not automatic. For TlAs, toxic Tl/As dust and the need for post-anneal ordering are significant uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is compositionally simple, with only Tl and As and a described 2D sheet network; the target description does not indicate a very large cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network. Structurally similar entries often involve complex coordination environments and sometimes defect/disordered phases (SS1/SS2), but this specific target description is simpler than those analogues. Therefore amorphization risk is scored low, though thin-film deposition could still produce amorphous or poorly crystalline material if substrate temperature is too low [CS1/SS1; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlAs at 0.044 eV/atom above hull is best interpreted as a near-hull metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground state [target data]. The strongest support comes from thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values that were synthesized by sealed-ampoule, melt, or mechanochemical routes (TS1–TS4), and from Tl-containing analogues made in evacuated ampoules or by Bridgman growth (CS3/SS4/SS5). The most important enabling factor is the low-to-moderate synthesis temperature window enabled by low-melting/volatile p-block elements and sealed-ampoule melt chemistry [CS3/SS4/TS1/TS2; Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is controlling Tl:As stoichiometry under sealed conditions because both elements are hazardous and As is volatile [SS3/TS1/TS2; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but should be approached as a controlled sealed-ampoule crystal-growth problem rather than a routine open solid-state reaction.
