### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0210 eV/atom is well within the high-synthesizability range ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. TS analogues near this hull value, including CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized, indicating that this metastability magnitude is experimentally accessible (TS2; TS5). The closest CuBr₂ polymorphs are at 0 and 0.0084 eV/atom, suggesting the CuBr₂ composition itself is robust (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the P2₁/c target is above the lowest CuBr₂ ground-state reference and may require kinetic or crystallization-condition selection [Target; CS1/CS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, aqueous slow evaporation

The condition severity is minimal because exact CuBr₂ crystals were made by slow evaporation of a water solution at room temperature, with no pressure, furnace, or controlled atmosphere reported (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). Kinetics are highly favorable because Cu²⁺ and Br⁻ are already dissolved and crystallize from solution rather than requiring solid diffusion (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). The main uncertainty is not chemical formation but phase/crystal quality, because larger CuBr₂ crystals were reported to be twinned and sometimes unsuitable for X-ray work (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). The route therefore scores very high overall but not perfect after expert adjustment.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, vapor transport / sublimation

The sublimation route is supported indirectly by FeBr₂, which forms single crystals by sublimation at 773 K under Ar (CS4). This suggests layered metal bromides can be vapor-grown, but there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for CuBr₂ sublimation specifically, so transferability is only moderate. Kinetics can be favorable in a vapor phase, but composition control is a concern because bromine volatility may alter CuBr₂ stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore useful for exploratory polymorph screening but less reliable than aqueous evaporation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, sealed hydrothermal / solvothermal

Hydrothermal Cu-containing bromide crystallization is supported by CuHgSBr synthesis in concentrated HBr at 670 K in sealed ampoules/autoclaves with controlled cooling (SS3; TS2). Fluid-mediated transport gives favorable kinetics, and the analogue reportedly forms high-quality crystals with no side products (SS3; TS2). The route is more severe than ambient evaporation because it uses concentrated HBr, sealed vessels, and controlled cooling (SS3; TS2). The uncertainty is redox and phase control for binary CuBr₂, since hydrothermal synthesis of the exact target is not directly reported [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, sealed-tube solid-state / halogen-pressure annealing

Sealed-tube halide/chalcogenide reactions are supported by CuHgSX preparation in evacuated Pyrex tubes at 553–573 K for 2 weeks and by CuSe₂ synthesis in sealed ampoules at 200 °C for 6 days (SS4; TS3; TS5; CS5). This route can maintain volatile components, but it is more conditionally demanding because vacuum/sealing and bromine chemical-potential control are likely needed [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are slower than solution routes, as related sealed solid-state analogues require long reaction times (SS4; TS3; TS5). The route is plausible but less attractive than direct solution crystallization for CuBr₂.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary CuBr₂ phase with one cation species and edge-sharing CuBr₆ octahedra [Target]. The closest CuBr₂ analogue is also a simple layered edge-sharing CuBr₆ system, which argues against glassy or amorphous products under crystallization conditions (CS1; SS1; TS1). No mixed cation-radius mismatch or complex mixed-connectivity framework is present in the target description [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is exact unit-cell size, which is not provided, but the AB₂ binary chemistry makes a >40-atom cell unlikely [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target P2₁/c CuBr₂ is most likely a **low-energy metastable polymorph**, not the absolute ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0210 eV/atom while a chemically identical CuBr₂ analogue is reported at 0 eV/atom (Target; CS2; SS2). The CS/SS/TS data strongly support synthesis of CuBr₂ as a composition, especially by room-temperature slow evaporation from aqueous cupric bromide solution (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the very simple solution chemistry: CuBr₂ can crystallize directly from water under mild conditions (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and crystal-quality control, because the reported CuBr₂ crystals can be twinned and the target symmetry differs from the directly reported C2/m references (CS1/CS2; SS1/SS2; TS1; Target). Overall, synthesis is highly feasible, but isolating the exact P2₁/c polymorph may require careful evaporation rate, seeding, hydration, or temperature screening [Reasonable Inference].
