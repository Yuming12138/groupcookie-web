### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.022 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS is strongly supportive because the closest thermodynamic analogues NbSe₂ polymorphs have E_hull ≈0.024–0.025 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized (TS1/TS2). CS and SS also include multiple stable or near-stable layered selenides, such as Ta₂PdSe₆ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS1), TlV₅Se₈ at 0.0078 eV/atom (CS2/SS1/TS3), and molybdenite-like NbSe₂ analogues (SS4/SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee the specific ordered Pmm2 V/W arrangement rather than competing VSe₂/WSe₂-like phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state.**  
Severity is high-moderate because direct routes require sealed evacuated tubes and often high temperatures: CS1 uses 1050 K for 10 days, CS2/SS1/TS3 uses 1350 K for several days, SS4/TS1 uses 600–1100 °C, and TS4 uses 1000 °C. Kinetics are the main penalty because W is refractory and V/W interdiffusion may be slow **[Reasonable Inference]**, while CS1 and TS4 show the need for regrinding/repeated firings to improve phase purity. The route is nevertheless plausible because elemental sealed-tube synthesis is repeatedly successful across CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, CVT.**  
CVT avoids the worst bulk diffusion limitations because SS5/TS2 shows transport growth of Nb/Ta/Mo/W selenides and SS2 shows Br₂-assisted growth of complex layered selenides. Its severity is moderate due to vacuum ampoules, volatile halogens, and multi-step feed preparation, all supported by SS5/TS2 and SS2. The uncertainty is composition control: V and W may form volatile halides at different rates, so the transported crystal composition may deviate from V₃WSe₈ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, iodine-assisted mineralizer.**  
This route scores best because CS4 demonstrates iodine-assisted growth at only 600 °C for 15 days in evacuated quartz tubes, and SS5/TS2 shows iodine transport is compatible with W-containing chalcogenides. The lower temperature reduces ampoule risk and may favor ordering, but the lower thermal budget may also slow W incorporation **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether trace iodine provides enough transport to homogenize V and W without producing separate binary selenides.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target’s V–Se and W–Se bond lengths are close, 2.50–2.54 Å, suggesting low local strain in the mixed-metal sheet; this is directly from the target structure. Molybdenite-like layered analogues with simple 6-coordinate metal–selenium networks are experimentally accessible in SS4/TS1 and SS5/TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Pmm2 cell size and glass-forming tendency, but the crystalline analogues argue against strong amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃WSe₈ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally accessible NbSe₂ polymorphs in TS1 and TS2. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis well: V-rich selenide frameworks are demonstrated in TlV₅Se₈ (CS2/SS1/TS3) and NiV₂Se₄ (TS4), while W/Nb/Ta molybdenite-like chalcogenides are accessible by sealed-tube and CVT methods (SS4/TS1, SS5/TS2). The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for evacuated-ampoule selenide chemistry and halogen-assisted crystal growth in related systems (CS4, SS5/TS2). The most important limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control of V/W ordering in a low-symmetry Pmm2 layered phase, which is not directly proven in the references **[Reasonable Inference]**. Competing binary or ternary selenides are likely unless Se activity, iodine loading, and thermal history are tightly controlled, consistent with phase-control difficulties in CS1, TS1, and TS4.
