### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0460 eV/atom lies below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold, so it receives a high stability score. CS analogues with E_hull ≈0.054–0.066 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, showing that slightly metastable multicomponent chalcogenides can be accessible (CS-1 to CS-5). TS analogues are especially relevant because their E_hull values span about 0.034–0.069 eV/atom and they are known phases (TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed S/Se and low-symmetry P1, which may introduce configurational/order-disorder competition not captured by E_hull alone [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile S/Se, multistep annealing, and toxicity, all consistent with sealed-tube chalcogenide syntheses in CS-3, CS-4, SS-2, TS-1, and TS-2. It avoids extreme pressure, and high pressure is not broadly supported except for one structurally similar pyrite-related case (SS-3), so that deduction is not applied. The uncertainty is whether temperatures below 900–950 °C are sufficient to equilibrate Pd/Ni ordering in the target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state kinetics are limited by dense Pd/Ni metal diffusion and by the need to form a low-symmetry mixed-anion layered phase [Reasonable Inference]. TS-1 and TS-2 require multiple firings and intermediate grinding, supporting a moderate-to-slow kinetic expectation. CS-3/CS-4 also require high-temperature holds and long annealing for multicomponent chalcogenides. The main uncertainty is whether preformed binary chalcogenides would greatly improve kinetics; TS-5 suggests binary precursors can help, but it is not directly Pd/Ni/S/Se.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar sealed-system penalties because flux/transport analogues use vacuum tubes and volatile chalcogen chemistry (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5). It avoids >1000 °C processing, which is an advantage over some direct solid-state routes (CS-3, TS-1). Optional K₂Se or I₂ increases air-sensitivity/toxicity, supported by K₂Se handling in SS-1 and iodine addition in CS-4. The uncertainty is whether a halide or chalcogenide flux will incorporate unwanted halide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux lowers diffusion barriers relative to direct reaction, supported by low-temperature flux/transport formation of chalcogenides in SS-1, SS-4, and SS-5. However, SS-1 reports mixed phases and unsuccessful pure AuCuSe₄ isolation, so the structural-reconstruction score is not high. The target’s Pd/Ni ordering and S/Se distribution may require a narrow growth window [Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are better than direct solid-state but phase selectivity remains a major uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has low thermal severity because CS-1/CS-2 demonstrate quaternary sulfide solvothermal synthesis at 200 °C. It is still penalized for controlled N₂/vacuum handling, sealed autoclave use, volatile/toxic chalcogen sources, and multistep workup/annealing. SS and TS provide insufficient useful evidence for solvothermal synthesis of structurally/thermodynamically similar Pd/Ni octahedral sheets, so they are treated as neutral for route feasibility. The main condition uncertainty is whether post-annealing is necessary and whether it preserves the solvothermal product stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Solvothermal metal salts and chalcogen donors provide high precursor activity, consistent with CS-1/CS-2. Solution-mediated nucleation reduces diffusion length, so cation mixing should be easier than direct Pd/Ni metal diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The weak point is structural crystallization: CS-1/CS-2 form tetrahedral stannite-type sulfide, while the target requires octahedral edge-sharing Pd/Ni mixed Se/S sheets. Because SS and TS give insufficient solvothermal evidence, the structural component is penalized despite favorable precursor kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target triggers one amorphization-risk check because Pd and Ni octahedral ionic radii differ substantially [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell or coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, so those checks are not triggered. Edge-sharing octahedra are common in SS-2 and TS-type selenides, suggesting crystallinity is plausible. The uncertainty is that P1 symmetry and mixed S/Se ordering could increase configurational disorder beyond the simple F4 criteria [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PdNi₃Se₅S₃ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.0460 eV/atom and TS analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally realized (TS-1 to TS-5). The analogue support is strongest thermodynamically and moderately strong chemically for chalcogenide synthesis, but structurally mixed: Pd/Ni octahedral selenide chemistry is supported by SS-2 and TS-2, while the exact mixed S/Se P1 sheet is not directly demonstrated. The most critical enabling factor is sealed or solution-mediated chalcogen chemistry that maintains S/Se stoichiometry while allowing Pd/Ni mixing, supported by CS-1/CS-2 and sealed-tube methods in CS-3/CS-4/TS-1/TS-2. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against binary Pd/Ni sulfides/selenides and against S/Se segregation [Reasonable Inference]. A solvothermal-to-annealed route scores best because it reduces diffusion length, but flux-assisted growth may be the more realistic route for crystalline single-phase bulk samples if solvothermal products are poorly ordered.
