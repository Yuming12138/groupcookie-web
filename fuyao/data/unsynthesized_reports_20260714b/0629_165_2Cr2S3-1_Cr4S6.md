### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.030 eV atom⁻¹, placing it in the most favorable scoring bin and indicating near-ground-state stability. CS1/SS1 report a Cr2S3 polymorph with E_hull = 0.0, so the binary composition is thermodynamically well established, but the target P-6m2 structure is likely metastable relative to the R-3 phase. TS1 and TS2 include reported syntheses of chromium sulfide-related phases with E_hull values around 0.036–0.052 eV atom⁻¹, supporting the idea that phases in this energy range can be made. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific, not composition-specific.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard evacuated silica-tube synthesis and sintering, directly supported by CS1/SS1 for Cr2S3. Severity deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur containment, multistep grinding/pressing/sintering, and optional I2 toxicity. TS1 similarly indicates that repeated heating/grinding can be needed for single-phase chromium sulfides, reinforcing moderate condition severity. The main uncertainty is whether the same thermal schedule will favor the target P-6m2 polymorph or relax to R-3 Cr2S3.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are limited by Cr metal’s refractory nature and solid-state diffusion, consistent with the week-long sintering in CS1/SS1 and repeated cycling in TS1. Sulfur vapor and possible I2 mineralization can improve reactivity, as suggested by CS1/SS1 and CS3. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the composition is identical to a known Cr2S3 phase, but the target polymorph differs. Thus, kinetics are acceptable for Cr2S3 formation but less favorable for polymorph-selective formation.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, matching the moderate CVT conditions reported for V5S8 in CS4. Deductions come from sealed evacuated tubes, volatile S/I2, and toxic/corrosive iodine. CS1/SS1 and CS3 support iodine’s usefulness in chromium/chalcogenide crystallization, while insufficient evidence from TS addresses iodine CVT for binary Cr2S3. Overall condition severity is moderate and experimentally accessible.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by using gas-phase transport, reducing reliance on long-range diffusion through refractory chromium solids; this is supported by crystal growth of V5S8 at 900→800 °C in CS4. I2-assisted crystallization in CS1/SS1 and CS3 further supports enhanced material transport. The structural uncertainty remains significant because no source reports the exact P-6m2 Cr2S3 target. This route scores best because it balances kinetic activation with relatively mild conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is conditionally severe because CS2/SS2/CS5 use 65–89 kbar and 1200 °C. The required multi-anvil equipment, quenching, and possible CS2 extraction make the route challenging. TS1 also reports high-pressure treatments up to 80 kbar for related chromium sulfides, confirming that such conditions are relevant but demanding. The high-pressure route is plausible for metastable polymorph access but penalized heavily for equipment and safety burden.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure and 1200 °C can accelerate reaction kinetics, as CS2/SS2 report successful Cr5S8 synthesis in 2 h. However, starting from elemental Cr and S still involves refractory-metal reaction kinetics, and the structural relationship to Cr2S3 is indirect. Quenching may help preserve a metastable P-6m2 polymorph [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull and CS2/SS2 quench], but no source confirms this target. Kinetics are moderate, but transferability is less certain than Routes 1–2.

#### F2 — Route 4

The H2S route uses a reactive and toxic gas stream, as in SS3/SS4, and temperatures near or slightly above 1000 °C. It avoids high pressure and sealed sulfur ampoules during the main sulfurization step. However, H2S handling requires specialized gas safety infrastructure, scrubbers, and corrosion-resistant flow control [Reasonable Inference from SS3/SS4 use of dry H2S]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence that H2S is optimal for binary Cr2S3 polymorph control.

#### F3 — Route 4

H2S provides a reactive sulfur source and has been used to build chromium sulfide frameworks in SS3/SS4. Nevertheless, the reported products are alkali thiochromates rather than binary Cr2S3, so the structural pathway is indirect. Diffusion through chromium metal remains a kinetic limitation, though high temperature improves reaction rate. The route is plausible but less targeted than CVT or sealed-tube synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size disorder is not an amorphization driver. The structure contains mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing CrS6 octahedra, which triggers one amorphization/structural-frustration criterion; the same kind of mixed connectivity is seen in crystalline Cr2S3 and Cr5S8 analogues (CS1/SS1, SS2). The likely small formula-unit size reduces glass-forming tendency, although the exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given. Overall amorphization risk is low to medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target P-6m2 Cr2S3 is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the ground-state Cr2S3 phase, because the target has E_hull = 0.030 eV atom⁻¹ while the R-3 Cr2S3 analogue in CS1/SS1 has E_hull = 0.0. The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis of Cr2S3 chemistry and CrS6-octahedral sulfide frameworks, especially through evacuated sealed-tube synthesis and iodine-assisted processing. TS data support that related chromium sulfides near this energy range can be synthesized, but TS provides insufficient direct evidence for the exact binary P-6m2 polymorph. The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed-tube or CVT conditions to manage volatile sulfur and improve crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: prolonged high-temperature annealing may favor the known R-3 Cr2S3 ground-state phase rather than the desired omega-like layered P-6m2 structure.
