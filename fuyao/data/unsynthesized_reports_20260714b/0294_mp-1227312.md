### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom is at the top of the most favorable rubric bin, so it is likely near-ground-state or weakly metastable. TS gives the strongest support: MgNi6Si6 at 0.059 eV/atom and MgNi6Ge6 at 0.055 eV/atom are close in hull energy and have reported solid-state synthesis routes [TS1, TS3], while TiNi2Sn and ZrNi2Sn are similarly low-energy Heusler-type intermetallics [TS4–TS5]. SS gives mixed support because Ni2Si is stable and easily synthesized [SS2], but a 0.0966 eV/atom NiSi polymorph needs 17.5 GPa [SS3]. CS supports intermetallic phase stability but does not include Be, so the chemical transfer is incomplete [CS1–CS5].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 850–1000 °C, Ar atmosphere, ambient pressure, and repeated grinding/annealing, which is consistent with Mg–Ni–P, Mg–Ni–As, and Mg–Ni–Si/Ge syntheses [CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1, TS3]. The main severity penalties are multi-step processing and Be toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify Be volatility, so no separate volatile-precursor deduction was applied; Mg sealed-container behavior is only an indirect warning [CS2]. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition in the unreported Be–Ni–Si ternary.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental powders/chips have modest activity compared with solution precursors, and Ni/Si/Be are relatively refractory, so kinetics are not excellent [Reasonable Inference]. However, many Ni intermetallic analogues form by solid-state reaction at 700–1000 °C with intermediate grinding [CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1, TS3], suggesting diffusion is manageable. Structural formation from separate elements requires significant ordering into tetragonal P4/mmm, so repeated annealing is likely needed [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Arc melting has a high effective temperature and requires purified Ar plus vacuum annealing, as shown in Ni–Si–Au and Heusler analogues [SS4, TS4–TS5]. The route is penalized for >1000 °C, >1400 °C melt conditions, vacuum annealing, multiple steps, and Be toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. The CS/SS/TS evidence supports arc melting for intermetallics but not specifically for Be-bearing compounds, so safety and Be loss remain uncertain. This route is feasible but practically more demanding than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melting improves homogenization and reduces the diffusion burden, which is why arc melting is effective for Ni–Si–Au and Heusler-type intermetallics [SS4, TS4–TS5]. Post-annealing remains necessary because ordered intermetallic phases often require heat treatment after melting [SS4, TS4–TS5]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Be evaporates or segregates during melting before the ordered BeNi2Si phase forms [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The high-pressure route is directly inspired by high-pressure cubic NiSi at 17.5 GPa and ~950–1037 °C [SS3, TS2]. It is penalized for pressure above 5 GPa, likely >1000 °C operation, multiple handling steps, and Be toxicity [SS3, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure BeNi2Si synthesis and is neutral for this route. This route is a fallback for metastable phase recovery rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A prealloyed precursor can improve reaction homogeneity, consistent with the high-pressure NiSi route starting from NiSi [SS3, TS2]. High pressure and temperature can access metastable polymorphs, but diffusion volumes are small and long dwell times may be needed [SS3, TS2]. Phase control is uncertain because high-pressure NiSi itself can involve minor or competing phases [SS3, TS2]. Thus kinetics are acceptable but not superior to melt-assisted processing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple tetragonal P4/mmm ordered intermetallic description with Be, Ni, and Si in high coordination environments, not a large, open, mixed-polyhedral framework [Target structure]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for BeNi2Si amorphization tendency, so they are neutral for F4. Based on the target’s small-cell ordered metallic structure, amorphization risk is low [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether rapid quenching from melt could trap disorder, which would be route-specific [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeNi2Si is most likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable intermetallic because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable stability cutoff. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: direct solid-state reactions of Ni-rich intermetallics at 700–1000 °C are common [CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1, TS3], and low-energy thermodynamic neighbors around 0.035–0.059 eV/atom are experimentally accessible [TS1, TS3–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for Ni–Si frameworks and Ni–Si bond lengths similar to the target’s 2.37 Å Ni–Si bonds [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is practical Be handling: toxicity, possible oxide passivation, and possible composition drift are not directly addressed by the CS/SS/TS datasets [Reasonable Inference]. Phase competition with binary Ni–Si or Be–Si phases is also a concern because the target is only marginally above hull [Reasonable Inference].
