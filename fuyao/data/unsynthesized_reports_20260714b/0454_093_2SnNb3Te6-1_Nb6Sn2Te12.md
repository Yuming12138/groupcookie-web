### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0030 eV/atom is essentially on the convex hull and receives a high score under the provided rubric. TS analogues are mostly stable ground-state or near-ground-state tellurides, including Nb₂GeTe₄, Nb₂SiTe₄, Nb₃Te₄, and Nb₃Sb₂Te₅ with E_hull = 0 (TS1–TS4). CS and SS also show many stable layered or Nb–Te analogues (CS1/CS3/CS5, SS1–SS4), although SS5 is a high-E_hull outlier. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility of the exact Pmc2₁ Nb₃SnTe₆ phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: direct solid-state

The F2 score is moderately high because the route uses standard evacuated quartz/silica tubes and furnace conditions, as in CS1, CS4, CS5, SS4, and TS4, without high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile Te/Sn-containing sealed-tube chemistry, consistent with the telluride analogues in CS/SS/TS. The F3 score is lower because elemental Nb is refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference], even though Te-rich reaction pathways enable analogous tellurides at 600–1000 °C (CS4/CS5/SS4). Structural similarity to Nb₂GeTe₄/Nb₂SiTe₄ supports transferability (CS1/SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2), but exact Sn substitution and phase purity remain uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT

The F2 score is lower due to halogen transport agents, vacuum sealing, volatile species, and the multi-step nature of CVT. CVT is strongly supported for layered tellurides by NbPdTe₅ using I₂ (CS2), Ta/Rh/W telluride-related TeBr₄ transport (CS3), and FeTaTe₃ with I₂ (TS5). The F3 score is better than simple solid-state because vapor transport improves mass transport and crystal growth, as stated for NbPdTe₅ where I₂ appeared necessary for crystal growth (CS2). However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for clean Sn transport chemistry, so composition control may be difficult.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: molten-salt/Te-rich flux

The F2 score is intermediate because this route still needs an evacuated ampoule and volatile Te but avoids mandatory halogen CVT agents. SS3/TS3 directly support molten-salt ion exchange or ternary insertion for many niobium chalcogenides, including Sn-containing compositions, at 1000 °C for ~10 d. Kinetically, Te flux or molten salt should improve precursor activity and cation mobility [Reasonable Inference], but the target structure differs from Nb₆X₈/insertion-type frameworks in SS3/TS3. The uncertainty is whether Sn inserts into a useful Nb–Te precursor or instead stabilizes competing SnTe/Nb–Te phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The amorphization risk is assessed as low because CS/SS/TS analogues are successfully crystallized as layered or complex niobium/tantalum tellurides using conventional high-temperature routes (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The target formula is not obviously a very large-cell multication oxide-like network, and its bonding is metallic/covalent telluride-like [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS/SS/TS tellurides]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so the score relies partly on the successful crystallization of related tellurides. The main uncertainty is the exact conventional cell size and whether multiple coordination motifs create local disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SnTe₆ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0030 eV/atom, and TS analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 Nb–Te/tetrel–Te materials (TS1–TS4). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well at the level of chemistry, local coordination, and processing conditions: layered Nb/Ta tellurides are routinely made in evacuated tubes or by CVT (CS1–CS5), and structurally similar Nb₂GeTe₄/Nb₂SiTe₄ motifs strongly resemble the target’s Nb–Te/tetrel–Te network (SS1/SS2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a Te-rich, sealed environment that promotes Nb tellurization and layered crystal growth, as supported by excess Te and sealed-tube precedents (CS4/CS5/SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among Nb–Te, Sn–Te, and Nb–Sn–Te phases, because related systems report persistent impurities or narrow stoichiometry windows (CS4/CS5/SS4/TS4). Overall, the target appears experimentally accessible but will likely require careful stoichiometry and thermal-profile optimization.
