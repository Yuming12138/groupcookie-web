### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target data. CS analogues such as Ta₂PdSe₆ and Ta₂NiSe₅ are also on the hull (CS1, CS4), SS analogues TaSe₂/Ta₂NiSe₅ are at or near the hull (SS1–SS5), and TS analogues are deliberately thermodynamically similar with several E_hull = 0 entries (TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Si-containing Ta selenides.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses standard evacuated sealed ampoules and 650–850 °C synthesis conditions, which are common in CS2, CS4, SS1, and TS1. The penalties arise from vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile Se, toxicity of Se, and likely regrinding, consistent with CS1 and TS5. The route avoids high pressure and avoids >1000 °C operation.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Elemental Ta and Si are kinetically sluggish solid precursors [Reasonable Inference], and ternary Ta selenides often require days to weeks in CS1/CS2/CS4. The layered Ta–Se motif is favorable because structurally similar TaSe₂ and Ta₂NiSe₅ form readily (SS1–SS5), but Si ordering is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. Therefore, kinetics are the main weakness of direct synthesis.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 retains the same sealed-tube and Se-volatility penalties as Route 1, supported by SS1/TS1 and CS4/SS5/TS3. It adds a deliberate precursor step, so it is multi-step by design. It remains moderate rather than severe because it uses ambient pressure and ≤850 °C temperatures.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Pre-forming TaSe₂ is expected to improve Ta–Se contact and reduce the need for direct Ta–Se diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and TaSe₂ formation is directly supported by SS1/TS1. However, Si must still enter and order within a new Ta–Si–Se framework, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for that step. This gives better kinetics than Route 1 but not as good as vapor transport.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT uses evacuated ampoules, volatile halogens, volatile Se, and a two-zone gradient furnace, all supported by SS2/SS4/TS2, CS3, CS4/SS5/TS3, and TS5. It avoids high pressure and usually can remain below or near 900 °C in the recommended variant. The hazardous I₂/Br₂ transport agent is the main severity penalty.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
CVT improves mass transport through volatile halide/chalcogen species, as demonstrated for TaSe₂ and related Ta–M–Se crystals (SS2/SS4/TS2, CS3, TS5). This makes Route 3 kinetically preferable for crystallinity and phase selection. The uncertainty is that Si transport and retention in Ta₃SiSe₆ are not evidenced by CS/SS/TS, so transport-agent loading and gradient may require optimization.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains very different cations, Ta and Si, so cation-size/coordination mismatch is a real amorphization or disorder risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula is not large, and the provided structure description does not prove a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence; CS/SS/TS are insufficient for those two checks. Therefore the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃SiSe₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS data strongly support the general accessibility of layered Ta selenides and Ta–transition-metal selenides by sealed-tube solid-state synthesis and CVT, but they provide insufficient direct evidence for Si-containing Ta–Si–Se chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ta–Se layered chemistry, supported by TaSe₂ and Ta₂NiSe₅ analogues in SS1–SS5 and TS1–TS3. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control of Si incorporation into a refractory Ta–Se framework, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly contains Si. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require sealed-tube optimization and possibly CVT for high-quality crystals.
