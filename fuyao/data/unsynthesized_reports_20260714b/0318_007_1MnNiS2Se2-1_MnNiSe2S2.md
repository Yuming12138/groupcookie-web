### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E\_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, which falls within the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom range. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, such as FeRh₂Se₄ at 0.045 eV/atom, CrRh₂Se₄ at 0.043 eV/atom, and NiRh₂Se₄ at 0.042 eV/atom, have reported direct solid-state syntheses [TS2/TS4/TS5]. Structurally similar MnSe₂ is less stable at 0.069 eV/atom but still synthesized in evacuated tubes [SS1/TS1], supporting the idea that a 0.041 eV/atom phase is accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed-anion and mixed-cation, so configurational ordering may alter the practical stability window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated ampoules and moderate-to-high temperatures, matching MnSe₂ and Rh selenide syntheses [SS1/TS1/TS2/TS5]. The severity score is reduced because vacuum sealing, volatile S/Se, and repeated grinding/annealing are expected; these features are explicitly present in MnSe₂ and Rh selenide preparations [SS1/TS1/TS2]. No high pressure or >1400 °C processing is required in the analogues [CS3/SS1/TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether the target requires temperatures high enough to risk decomposition or S/Se loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct reaction from Mn/Ni metals has moderate-to-slow kinetics because dense metals require diffusion through growing chalcogenide layers [Reasonable Inference], consistent with multiple firings in MnSe₂ and repeated firings/grinding in Rh selenides [SS1/TS1/TS2]. Chalcogen vapor and possible transient liquid phases may improve reaction rates, as analogues form below or near the metal melting points [SS1/TS1/TS4]. Structural reconstruction is nontrivial because no exact MnNi(SeS)₂ analogue is provided; however, octahedral Mn–Se and edge-sharing transition-metal selenide motifs are present in SS/TS analogues [SS1/TS2/TS5]. The score is therefore moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has the same sealed-tube and volatile-component penalties as Route 1, plus handling of I₂ or Br₂ transport agents. The temperature range is moderate because CVT analogues operate at 650–850 °C [CS3/SS4]. Equipment needs are specialized but standard for solid-state labs: evacuated silica tubes and two-zone furnaces [CS3/SS4]. The main hazard is halogen transport chemistry and sealed-tube pressure from S/Se [CS3/SS4; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by transporting material through the vapor phase, reducing reliance on solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by successful crystal growth of Cu₂MnGeS₄ using iodine and Nb₂Pd₃Se₈ using bromine [CS3/SS4]. The structural reconstruction score is higher than direct synthesis because CVT can select crystalline low-dimensional products, but the exact target topology and mixed S/Se ordering are not demonstrated in the sources. Remaining uncertainty is transport-agent compatibility with Mn/Ni sulfide/selenide chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth can use lower temperatures, as shown by AuCuSe₄ at 310 °C and CuSe₂Br at 550 °C [SS2/SS5]. However, the route still requires vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogens, air-sensitive alkali chalcogenide fluxes, and post-growth flux removal [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. SS2 also requires glovebox handling and controlled cooling, increasing practical difficulty. No high pressure is indicated in the flux analogues [SS2/SS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux conditions should improve ion mobility and precursor activity compared with direct metal diffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with low-temperature crystal growth in SS2. However, phase selectivity is a significant concern: SS2 reports persistent impurity phases, and SS5 reports strong cooling-rate dependence and metastability. Therefore, kinetics are favorable, but structural targeting is less reliable than CVT. Insufficient evidence from CS for a directly analogous Mn/Ni/S/Se flux route; CS is neutral 50 for this route-specific transferability.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger: Mn²⁺ and Ni²⁺ octahedral ionic radii differ by slightly more than 15% [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives edge-sharing octahedra but not mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given, and CS/SS/TS do not provide useful evidence for this target’s cell size; therefore C2 is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50 for that check. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnNi(SeS)₂ is likely a mildly metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a strongly unstable compound, because its E\_hull is only 0.041 eV/atom and similar-hull chalcogenides were synthesized [TS2/TS4/TS5]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well at the level of chemistry and processing: evacuated-tube solid-state reaction, CVT, and flux growth all appear in related chalcogenides [CS3/SS1/SS2/SS4/TS1/TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed-tube chalcogen chemistry, which maintains S/Se chemical potential and is common across successful analogues [CS3/SS1/TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is ordering and phase competition in a mixed Mn/Ni and mixed S/Se layered octahedral framework, because no exact analogue is reported and several sources note phase-purity or cooling-window challenges [SS2/SS5/TS2]. CVT is the best route because it combines high crystallinity with improved mass transport, as shown in multicomponent chalcogenide analogues [CS3/SS4].
