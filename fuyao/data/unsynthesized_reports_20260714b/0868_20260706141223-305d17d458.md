### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores at the top of the rubric. TS analogues Ba₂CdS₃, Ba₃UAgS₆, Ba₃SmInS₆, Ba₃Ga₂S₆, and BaMnS₂ are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing that Ba sulfides with complex coordination environments can be thermodynamic ground states (TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee rapid ordering into the monoclinic P2₁/c structure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids external high pressure and can operate near 900–950 °C, consistent with Ba₃UAgS₆/Ba₃FeUS₆ synthesis at 1223 K (SS3, TS2). The main severity penalties are evacuated sealed-tube handling, sulfur volatility, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors, all consistent with the sealed-tube procedures in SS3/TS2. CS evidence gives insufficient direct support for Ba–Fe–Ag in this route because CS analogues are alkali/rare-earth copper sulfides rather than Ba–Fe–Ag sulfides (CS1–CS4).

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state reaction kinetics are moderate because BaS/Ag₂S/FeS are chemically compatible sulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference], and Ba–Ag/Fe sulfides are synthesized in sealed tubes (SS3, TS2). However, the target requires ordered Ba, Fe, Ag, and six inequivalent S sites, while the closest SS/TS Ba–Ag analogue is trigonal rather than monoclinic (SS3, TS2). Long annealing and possible regrinding are likely needed [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long Ba sulfide syntheses in SS1 and SS3.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth has moderate condition severity because CS analogues crystallize at relatively low temperature, 973 K, but still require Ar glovebox loading, 10⁻⁴ Torr sealed tubes, and controlled slow cooling (CS1, CS3, CS4). Sb₂S₃ or halide flux introduces toxicity/contamination risks [Reasonable Inference], while Sb₂S₃ is explicitly used in Ba₃UAgS₆/Ba₃FeUS₆ precursor sets (SS3, TS2). TS evidence supports Ba–Ag sulfide sealed-tube chemistry, while CS evidence supports flux growth generally but not the exact Ba–Fe–Ag chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because reactive fluxes dissolve and transport cations, reducing the need for long-range solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues show multiple quaternary sulfides can be grown reproducibly by reactive flux at 973 K with slow cooling (CS1, CS3, CS4). The uncertainty is that the target’s P2₁/c Ba₄FeAgS₆ structure is not the same as the Cmcm CS structures or R-3c Ba₃UAgS₆, so nucleation selectivity is not guaranteed (CS1–CS4, SS3, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 3

Solid–gas sulfidation avoids sealed ampoules and high pressure but uses hazardous reactive gases such as CS₂/N₂ or H₂S, directly reported for Ba₂CoS₃ and BaMnS₂ (SS5, TS5). It also typically requires high temperature near 1000 °C and regrinding, as seen for Ba₂CoS₃ (SS5). There is insufficient evidence from CS for gas-phase sulfidation, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Gas sulfidation can form Ba transition-metal sulfides, as shown by Ba₂CoS₃ and BaMnS₂ (SS5, TS5). However, SS5 reports multiphasic Ba₂CoS₃ with BaS impurity and possible amorphous Co-containing phases, which lowers confidence for a more complex Ba–Fe–Ag ordered phase. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Ag-bearing quaternary formation by solid–gas sulfidation, so Ag ordering is a key uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is the most severe route because structurally similar Ba₂ZnS₃ and Ba₂S₃ use ~50–60 kbar pressure (SS2, SS4). It also requires specialized multianvil/tetrahedral press hardware and encapsulation, directly reported in SS2 and SS4. Because the target is on the hull, high pressure is not clearly necessary; this route is therefore penalized as a backup rather than preferred [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can stabilize Ba sulfide frameworks that are difficult to obtain at ambient pressure, as seen for Ba₂ZnS₃ and Ba₂S₃ (SS2, SS4). Kinetics can be helped by densification under pressure [Reasonable Inference], but phase control is difficult because SS2 notes metastable polymorphs and high-pressure-specific products. CS evidence gives insufficient useful signal for high-pressure Ba–Fe–Ag synthesis, so it is neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size/coordination mismatch: Ba is 7-coordinate with long Ba–S bonds, while Fe and Ag are tetrahedral with much shorter M–S bonds; this pattern is also seen in Ba₂CdS₃ and Ba₂CoS₃ analogues (SS1, SS5). The target likely has a large ordered cell and explicitly contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, which triggers all three amorphization checks. This does not mean the phase is unlikely, but it means slow cooling or flux-mediated crystallization is important [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS flux growth and TS slow-cooling examples (CS1, CS3, CS4, TS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₄FeAgS₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic Ba sulfide analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: Ba–Ag sulfide sealed-tube chemistry is directly supported by Ba₃UAgS₆/Ba₃FeUS₆ routes (SS3, TS2), while flux growth of complex quaternary sulfides is well supported by CS analogues (CS1, CS3, CS4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur activity in a sealed tube or sulfide flux, because sulfur-containing sealed systems are common across the successful analogues (CS1–CS4, SS3, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is cation/anion ordering in a complex monoclinic P2₁/c framework with mixed BaS₇, FeS₄, AgS₄, and S-centered polyhedra [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, flux-assisted sealed-tube growth is the best balance of thermodynamic favorability, manageable conditions, and improved kinetics.
