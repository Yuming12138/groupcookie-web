### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.015 eV/atom, so it falls in the high-probability region for synthesizability. TS gives strong supporting signal because TS1, TS2, and TS3 are also near-hull phases synthesized by mild precipitation, crystallization, or hydrothermal methods. SS also supports near-hull fluoride phases, especially SS1/SS2 at 0 eV/atom and SS4 near 0 eV/atom. CS is less decisive because CS4 is highly metastable at 0.449 eV/atom, while CS2/CS5 are stable and CS1/CS3 are near-hull; therefore CS is supportive but not uniformly predictive.

#### Route 1 — F2 and F3

F2 is moderate because SS5 and TS4 show that sealed low-temperature fluoride reactions are feasible, but TS4 uses inert/vacuum handling and TS4/SS3 indicate that fluoride systems can require sealed corrosion-resistant containers. The expected temperature is far below 1000 °C, so there is no high-temperature penalty. F3 is moderate because the reaction uses binary InF₃ and In metal rather than highly reactive soluble precursors; however, the low melting point of In and the low-temperature success of SS5/TS4 improve kinetic prospects. The main uncertainty is whether the ordered 1D InF ribbon forms before disproportionation or phase separation, which is not directly addressed by CS/SS/TS.

#### Route 2 — F2 and F3

F2 is relatively favorable because SS1/SS2 demonstrate fluoride solvothermal synthesis at 150 °C for 24 h with slow cooling, avoiding extreme temperature or pressure. The penalties come from sealed autoclave operation, multi-step filtration/washing, and possible use of toxic fluoride mineralizers by **[Reasonable Inference]**. F3 is strong because solution transport avoids solid diffusion barriers, and SS1/SS2 plus SS4/TS2 show that fluoride frameworks and sheets can crystallize from solvothermal or cooling-crystallization routes. The largest uncertainty is redox control for In(I), because SS1/SS2 involve Sn(II) fluoride chemistry rather than In(I) formation.

#### Route 3 — F2 and F3

F2 is lower than Route 2 because CS2 shows electrochemical fluoride synthesis requires optimized current, voltage, concentration, washing, and vacuum drying, and because In(I) stabilization may require inert handling by **[Reasonable Inference]**. F3 is reasonably high because electrochemical formation can directly tune oxidation state, and CS2 demonstrates phase formation at 0 °C in a fluoride electrolyte. However, CS2 also shows narrow phase-control requirements and impurity sensitivity, so crystallizing the exact Pmm2 1D InF phase remains uncertain. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for electrochemical InF-like synthesis, so their route-specific signal is neutral.

#### Route 4 — F2 and F3

F2 is moderate-to-low because CS3 uses controlled-atmosphere milling, and combining milling with sealed annealing introduces multiple operations. F3 benefits from mechanochemical activation, as CS3 demonstrates indium-containing phase transformation by high-energy milling, and SS5/TS4 show low-temperature sealed annealing can crystallize fluoride materials. The structural-ordering score is limited because a 1D ribbon phase may amorphize or require careful annealing; this is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for mechanochemical synthesis of binary In fluorides specifically, so the transferability is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so cation radius mismatch is not a concern by target chemistry; CS/SS/TS do not provide additional target-specific radius-disorder evidence. The described target has two inequivalent In and two inequivalent F sites and no indication of a >40-atom cell, so the unit-cell complexity risk is low by **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target description mentions corner-sharing FIn₄ tetrahedra but not coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. Remaining uncertainty is that low-dimensional materials can be sensitive to defects, but this is not explicitly quantified by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InF is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.015 eV/atom, and TS/SS analogues include many successfully synthesized near-hull fluorides. The strongest support comes from SS and TS rather than CS: SS1/SS2/SS5 and TS1/TS2/TS4 show that low-dimensional or complex fluorides can form under mild solvothermal, crystallization, or sealed-tube conditions. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature fluoride mobility in solution or sealed systems, supported by SS1/SS2 and SS5. The most critical limiting factor is redox/phase control of In(I), because none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates formation of binary InF and competing In metal/InF₃ disproportionation is a **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with the best chance from anhydrous solvothermal crystallization or sealed-tube comproportionation.
