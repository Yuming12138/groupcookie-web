### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0410 eV/atom, which falls in the highest scoring range ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS analogues with similar metastability, including Li₂CrCl₄ at 0.0394 eV/atom (TS-3), SrThBr₆ at 0.0451 eV/atom (TS-1), and CaThBr₆ / BaThBr₆ near 0.05–0.06 eV/atom (TS-2, TS-4), are reported as experimentally synthesized. This supports high thermodynamic feasibility, although the phase is not guaranteed to be the ground-state BeCl₂ polymorph. Insufficient direct BeCl₂ phase-selection evidence is provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1: Sublimation / Vapor Transport

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures and no high pressure, consistent with halide sublimation and CVT examples: FeCl₃ at 593→373 K (CS-2), FeBr₂ at 773 K under Ar (CS-3), and Si₄Cl₈ cold-finger sublimation (TS-5). The deductions come from vacuum/dry atmosphere, sealed handling of volatile chloride, and air-sensitive/toxic precursor handling; moisture sensitivity is supported by CS-2, SS-2, SS-3, and TS-5, while Be-specific toxicity is **[Reasonable Inference]**. The remaining uncertainty is whether sublimation will crystallize the desired 2D tetragonal P-4m2 polymorph rather than a more stable BeCl₂ form.

#### F3 — Route 1: Sublimation / Vapor Transport

The vapor availability score is high because several halide analogues crystallize by sublimation or temperature-gradient transport (CS-2, CS-3, SS-3, SS-5, TS-5). The crystallization score is slightly reduced because no CS/SS/TS entry demonstrates BeCl₂ specifically or the target P-4m2 topology. Composition control is easier for a binary precursor than for multication chlorides, but chloride volatility and hydrolysis risk remain, as suggested by the sealed/dry handling in CS-2, SS-2, SS-3, and TS-5.

#### F2 — Route 2: Melt Growth / Bridgman

Route 2 is supported by FeCl₂ Bridgman growth (CS-1) and sealed-ampoule slow cooling of chloride melts (SS-1, SS-2). It avoids high pressure and extreme temperature, but still requires dry sealed conditions and handles volatile/toxic BeCl₂. The procedure is not very step-intensive relative to multistep solid-state routes in TS-1–4, but the sealed ampoule and vapor pressure make it more severe than ordinary air firing.

#### F3 — Route 2: Melt Growth / Bridgman

The kinetics are favorable because the reaction/crystallization occurs from a melt, bypassing slow solid-state diffusion; analogous chloride melt crystallizations occur at moderate temperatures in SS-1 and SS-2. The precursor activity is high for molten BeCl₂ by **[Reasonable Inference]**, and the target contains only one cation, reducing long-range cation ordering demands compared with TS-1–4. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction: melt growth may select the thermodynamic BeCl₂ polymorph rather than the 2D P-4m2 sheet phase.

#### F2 — Route 3: Direct Chlorination + CVT

Route 3 is more severe because it uses reactive Cl₂, by analogy to FeCl₃ growth from Fe + dry Cl₂ (CS-2), and halogen vapor transport as in TiBr₂ synthesis (CS-4). It also involves multiple operations—chlorination, vapor transport, and crystallization—and greater safety demands. No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required by the analogy, but the Cl₂/Be combination makes this route challenging. Insufficient direct Be chlorination crystallization evidence is provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3: Direct Chlorination + CVT

The kinetics are plausible because halogenation can generate an active volatile chloride in situ, as shown for FeCl₃ (CS-2) and TiBr₂ (CS-4). However, Be metal may be passivated and is not itself volatile, so the reaction depends on forming BeCl₂ at the metal surface; this is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Composition control is less robust than starting from pure BeCl₂, because chlorine dose, vapor pressure, and possible oxide/hydroxychloride impurities must be controlled.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered by the target composition. The target is a simple binary BeCl₂ sheet of corner-sharing BeCl₄ tetrahedra, so mixed edge/face/corner polyhedral coexistence is absent by the target description. The small formula and simple topology imply low amorphization tendency, although CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphous BeCl₂ formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target BeCl₂ P-4m2 phase is likely metastable rather than a strict ground state because its E above hull is 0.0410 eV/atom. The thermodynamic analogues strongly support that phases in this energy range can be synthesized, as TS-1 and TS-3 have comparable hull energies and reported preparations. The CS/SS analogues support practical chloride crystal-growth methods—especially sealed ampoule melt growth, Bridgman growth, sublimation, and vapor transport. The most important enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility and volatility of metal chlorides, which allows crystallization without refractory solid-state diffusion, as shown in CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, and SS-2. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: a volatile, moisture-sensitive, toxic Be chloride may crystallize as another BeCl₂ polymorph or hydrolyze if handling is imperfect.
