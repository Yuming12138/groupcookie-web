### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound. This agrees with several stable Pt–chalcogenide analogues: PtGeS and PtGeSe are on the hull in CS #2/#5, Pt2TlS3 is on the hull in CS #4, and PtSe2/Pt2TlSe3 are on the hull in SS #1/#3 and TS #1/#3. Remaining uncertainty is experimental: being on the convex hull does not guarantee easy kinetic access or suppress all competing PtS2/PtSe2-type phases.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because evacuated sealed tubes are required, as in CS #2/#5, and volatile S/Se require containment. The temperature is moderate for Pt chemistry, since CS #2/#5 used 600 and 900 °C, but the duration is long and repeated grinding is sometimes needed. The main uncertainty is whether Pt4Se5S3 orders cleanly or decomposes into PtSe2/PtS2-like domains.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Pt has low precursor activity due to refractory metallic bonding; this is consistent with long 4–6 week reactions in CS #2/#5. Chalcogen vapor/liquid should help transport S and Se, but Pt diffusion remains slow [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally, the target is close to PtSe2 in SS #1/TS #1, but anion ordering makes kinetics more difficult than binary PtSe2.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 still requires a sealed ampoule and controlled vacuum/Ar, as used for Pt–chalcogenide melting reactions in CS #4 and SS #3/TS #3. It avoids >1000 °C conditions and high pressure, so its severity is moderate rather than high. Volatile chalcogen handling remains the main safety and practical penalty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt/flux-assisted growth improves kinetics compared with purely solid-state reaction because chalcogen-rich liquid/vapor can transport material; CS #4 and SS #3/TS #3 support 400–950 °C melting/sintering routes. Slow cooling supports ordering and crystallinity, as specifically reported in CS #4 and SS #3/TS #3. The uncertainty is that analogues include Tl/Na/Hg-containing phases, while the target is a pure Pt–S–Se layered compound.

#### F2 — Route 3

The sonochemical route is mild in temperature because PtSe2 forms at 35 °C followed by 500 °C heat treatment in SS #1/TS #1. It is still multi-step and uses corrosive/toxic reagents such as H2PtCl6 and Se, which lowers the condition score. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for using this method to make ordered Pt4Se5S3 specifically.

#### F3 — Route 3

The precursor activity is high because soluble Pt salts and activated chalcogen chemistry are much more reactive than metallic Pt; this is directly supported by SS #1/TS #1. The kinetic risk is structural/compositional: PtSe2 formation is supported, but controlled S incorporation and long-range S/Se ordering are not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. Thus this route may produce nanocrystalline PtSe2−xSx or multiphase products before it produces ordered Pt4Se5S3.

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT uses moderate temperatures similar to CS #1, but it requires vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile halogen transport agents. That makes the route more severe than simple annealing, and the need for pre-synthesized feedstock makes it effectively multi-step. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports direct CVT for Pt–S–Se phases, so this route is best considered a crystal-growth follow-up.

#### F3 — Route 4

Gas-phase transport can overcome slow solid-state diffusion, as implied by the successful CVT growth of BiSeI crystals in CS #1. However, there is no direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that Pt4Se5S3 forms stable volatile transport species without side reactions. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether iodine transports Pt, S, and Se congruently.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Pt, so cation radius mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference]. Its framework is dominated by edge-sharing octahedra, matching the low-dimensional edge-sharing motifs in PtSe2 from SS #1/TS #1 and Pt2TlS3 from CS #4. The low trigger count suggests low amorphization tendency, though nanochemical Route 3 could still yield poorly crystalline material if annealing is insufficient.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt4Se5S3 is likely a **ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically close PtSe2 in SS #1/TS #1 and layered Pt–chalcogenide phases synthesized by sealed-tube melting/sintering in CS #4 and SS #3/TS #3. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Pt–chalcogen edge-sharing octahedral sheet motif, which is repeatedly observed in stable analogues. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control of S/Se ordering, because CS/SS/TS strongly support Pt–Se and Pt–S frameworks but do not directly demonstrate ordered Pt4Se5S3. Overall, the material should be experimentally accessible, with sealed-ampoule melt/slow-cooling or long annealing the most credible bulk route.
