### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. CS analogues include stable or near-stable double perovskites such as La₂NiRuO₆ and La₂MgRhO₆ at E_hull = 0 (CS1/CS3) and Nd₂MgIrO₆ at 0.0067 eV/atom (CS2). SS analogues such as La₂LiSbO₆ are also near hull (SS5), and TS4 shows an experimentally synthesized double perovskite-like phase at E_hull ≈0.0504 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that no source directly reports MgLa₂SnO₆, so competing La–Sn–O or Mg–Sn–O phases remain possible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for high temperature and multistep processing because CS1 requires 1400 °C/120 h, SS1–SS4 use repeated high-temperature calcination/sintering, and TS4 uses repeated heating up to 1770 K. It is not penalized for pressure or reactive atmosphere because those analogues are ambient-pressure air syntheses. The main uncertainty is whether SnO₂ reacts fully with MgO/La₂O₃ at the selected temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Oxide starting materials are less reactive, consistent with long high-temperature firings in CS1, SS1–SS4, and TS4. The target structure is similar to ordered perovskite analogues in CS1/CS3/SS5, which helps structural reconstruction, but B-site ordering between Mg and Sn may require extended annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Hence the kinetics are acceptable but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids the >1400 °C penalty because CS3 and SS5 show nitrate/ceramic syntheses around 1073–1100 °C. It still receives penalties for T >1000 °C and multistep decomposition/calcination, as directly shown in CS3 and TS3. No high pressure or H₂/NH₃/vacuum is required by the evidence.

#### F3 — Route 2

Nitrates and co-precipitated gels provide high precursor activity, supported by CS3 for La/Mg nitrate chemistry and TS3 for solution-derived mixed oxides. The shorter diffusion length should improve Mg/Sn/La homogeneity relative to oxide grinding [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains around choosing a chloride-free, homogeneous Sn precursor [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 scores best for condition severity because CS2 demonstrates Mg double-perovskite flux growth at 700 °C without GPa pressure. The method is still multistep because sealed-tube loading, slow cooling, flux dissolution, sonication, and filtration are used in CS2. The main practical hazards are KOH causticity and sealed-tube compatibility, but these are not part of the specified deduction list except as operational complexity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth improves transport dramatically, and CS2 specifically uses KOH flux to crystallize Ln₂MgIrO₆ after solid-state routes failed to give pure phases. Because the target shares Mg-containing ordered perovskite topology with CS2 and has conventional Sn⁴⁺ oxide chemistry, the kinetic outlook is strong [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether SnO₂ solubility and La/Mg/Sn ratios in KOH favor MgLa₂SnO₆ rather than binary stannates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is strongly penalized for high pressure because CS4 uses 8 GPa. It is not penalized for extreme temperature because CS4 operates at 900 °C, but capsule preparation and quenching add multistep complexity. Since MgLa₂SnO₆ contains normal oxidation states, high pressure is not chemically demanded by the source data [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can promote dense perovskite frameworks, as CS4 demonstrates for La-based perovskite-related oxides. However, oxide precursors remain relatively unreactive, and the target does not have an unusual oxidation state such as Fe⁵⁺ in CS4. Thus high pressure is a viable fallback but not the most kinetically efficient route.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has large A-site/B-site cation size contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target network is corner-sharing perovskite-derived, matching many crystalline CS/SS analogues, and no mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is described for MgLa₂SnO₆. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgLa₂SnO₆ is likely a mildly metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS4 shows a synthesized analogue at essentially the same metastability level. The CS evidence is strong for Mg-containing double perovskite synthesis by flux and ceramic routes, especially CS2 and CS3, while SS evidence strongly supports La-based perovskite framework formation and an Sb⁵⁺ double-perovskite analogue. The most critical enabling factor is the robust perovskite-derived corner-sharing octahedral topology, which is repeatedly synthesized in CS1–CS3 and SS1–SS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and B-site ordering between Mg²⁺ and Sn⁴⁺, for which direct Mg–Sn evidence is absent and must be inferred. Overall, flux-assisted synthesis is the best first attempt, with nitrate/citrate ceramic synthesis as a practical powder route.
