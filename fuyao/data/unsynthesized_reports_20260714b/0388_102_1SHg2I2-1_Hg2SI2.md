### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.035 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable metastability window, giving a score of 94. This is supported by CS/SS analogues: Hg3S2I2 is calculated on the hull (CS1, SS3), Hg3Se2I2 is on or near the hull (CS2, CS3, SS2, SS4), and Hg3S2ICl is near the hull at 0.0139 eV/atom (SS1, TS1). TS also shows experimentally synthesized Hg–S–halide-related phases with E_hull values comparable to or slightly below the target, e.g. CuHgSBr at 0.03085 eV/atom (TS2). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull = 0.035 eV/atom means the material is likely metastable with respect to some competing Hg–S–I assemblage, not a guaranteed ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state

The direct route is mild in temperature because Hg3S2I2 and Hg3Se2I2 analogues form at 260 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS1, CS3, SS2, SS3). Deductions are dominated by the need for vacuum/sealed ampoules and toxic volatile Hg/I chemistry, both supported by the repeated sealed-ampoule use in CS/SS and the mercury handling notes in CS3/SS2. There is insufficient evidence from TS for the exact Hg2SI2 direct route, but TS4/TS5 support related low-temperature evacuated-tube Hg–S–halide chemistry and are therefore partially supportive rather than neutral. The final F2 of 65 reflects moderate procedural hazard, not harsh thermodynamic conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state

Kinetics should be relatively favorable because Hg–S–I analogues form in only 2–3 days at 260 °C (CS1, CS3, SS2, SS3), implying that long-range refractory diffusion is not the dominant barrier. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: known Hg3S2I2 analogues have more extended Hg–S–I connectivity and higher Hg coordination (CS1, SS3), while the target is a discrete zero-dimensional cluster. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for this exact molecular Hg2SI2 topology, so the structural reconstruction subscore is not placed in the highest range. The F3 score of 78 reflects good chemical mobility but nontrivial phase selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 2 CVT/gas-phase crystallization

CVT/gas-phase growth uses evacuated quartz ampoules, directly supported by Hg3S2ICl synthesis (SS1, TS1) and Hg–S–Br/Cl sublimation/CVT at 500–540 °C (SS5). The process does not require high external pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, but it does require sealed vacuum operation with toxic volatile mercury halide species. CS provides indirect support through Hg–S–I low-temperature ampoule chemistry (CS1, CS3), but insufficient CS evidence exists for exact CVT growth of Hg2SI2, so CS is supportive only by chemical analogy. F2 remains 65 because volatility and toxicity offset otherwise mild temperature conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2 CVT/gas-phase crystallization

Gas-phase transport can bypass solid-state diffusion limitations, which is why this route receives the highest F3 score. SS1/TS1 explicitly report evacuated-ampoule gas-phase growth of a very close Hg–S–I–Cl structural analogue, and SS5 reports controlled sublimation/CVT for Hg–S–Br/Cl. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: vapor transport may preferentially crystallize Hg3S2I2, HgI2, or other Hg–S–I phases rather than Hg2SI2 [Reasonable Inference]. The final F3 of 85 reflects excellent transport kinetics but only moderate confidence in target-specific nucleation.

#### F2 — Route 3 HgI2 self-flux

The flux route inherits sealed-tube and mercury toxicity penalties from CS4 and CS1/SS3. It also incurs a multi-step deduction because flux growth involves heating, slow cooling, and flux removal, similar in complexity to the Hg/Tl chalcogenide flux workflow in CS4. There is insufficient direct SS/TS evidence for HgI2 self-flux growth of Hg2SI2, so the severity estimate depends partly on [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is therefore lower at 55.

#### F3 — Route 3 HgI2 self-flux

Flux/melt assistance should improve precursor activity and reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the successful flux growth of low-dimensional Hg/Tl chalcogenides in CS4. The route may be especially useful for growing molecular crystals because slow cooling can promote ordered crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates HgI2 self-flux growth of binary Hg–S–I, so phase purity and residual HgI2 contamination are uncertain. The F3 score is high at 85 for transport but not a guarantee of selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 4 hydrothermal/solvothermal

TS2 shows hydrothermal synthesis of CuHgSCl/Br from HgS and halide media at 670 K in sealed glass ampoules inside autoclaves. This supports moderate temperature and efficient crystallization, but it also introduces sealed-vessel handling, corrosive acid, and toxic mercury chemistry. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal synthesis of Hg2SI2, so the route is more speculative. The F2 score of 70 reflects acceptable temperature but added autoclave/acid safety concerns.

#### F3 — Route 4 hydrothermal/solvothermal

Hydrothermal media can accelerate reactions involving HgS and halides, as shown by TS2 where crystals form in 24 h. The limitation is that TS2 contains Cu, and Cu may be essential to stabilizing the CuHgSX framework; removing Cu changes charge balance and nucleation chemistry. CS/SS evidence for hydrothermal binary Hg–S–I phases is insufficient, so the structural pathway is assigned only a moderate score. The F3 score of 73 reflects good mobility but high chemical uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hg, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence of multi-cation disorder for this target. The described structure contains two Hg2SI2 clusters, far below the >40 atom threshold, and the local bonding is molecular rather than a mixed polyhedral network. This gives a low amorphization risk and a score of 95. The remaining uncertainty is not amorphization but competing crystalline phase formation, which is a separate phase-selectivity issue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg2SI2 is likely a **low-energy metastable phase** rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.035 eV/atom, but that value is within the commonly accessible experimental metastability range. The CS and SS data strongly support Hg–S–I chemistry under low-temperature evacuated ampoule conditions, especially because Hg3S2I2 is directly synthesized at 260 °C from HgS/HgI2-type mixtures (CS1, SS3). The TS data also supports the broader idea that near-hull Hg–S–halide compounds with E_hull values up to ~0.03 eV/atom can be synthesized (TS2, TS4, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/volatility of mercury iodide species in sealed ampoules, which enables low-temperature reaction and vapor transport [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1, SS1, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among HgS, HgI2, Hg3S2I2, and other Hg–S–I frameworks, since the target’s molecular zero-dimensional structure is not directly demonstrated in the references.
