### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the highest scoring range and suggests likely accessibility, possibly as a low-energy metastable phase. CS strongly supports stability because Sr₂LiReO₆ analogues are experimentally prepared and have E_hull values of 0.0–0.0108 eV/atom (CS2, CS3). TS supports metastable accessibility because several thermodynamically similar oxides with E_hull ≈0.055–0.095 eV/atom were synthesized by flux, soft chemistry, hydrothermal, or high-pressure routes (TS2–TS5). Remaining uncertainty comes from the target’s unusual P1 Na/Li/Re/O framework and O–O contact, which are not directly reproduced in CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 ceramic route.**  
F2 is favorable because CS2/CS3 synthesize Sr₂LiReO₆ at only 600–800 °C with ordinary ceramic equipment, and CS4 demonstrates Na oxide solid-state synthesis under air with excess Na₂CO₃. F3 is weak because Re metal is refractory and slow to oxidize, so despite the success of CS2/CS3, diffusion and ordering into a P1 Na₂LiReO₆ structure are likely slow. SS provides insufficient direct support for this exact ceramic Na–Li–Re phase, so SS is treated as neutral for the ceramic route beyond general Na–5d oxide behavior.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 peroxide/O₂ route.**  
F2 is moderately reduced because Na₂O₂/Li₂O₂ are air-sensitive oxidizers, but SS2 demonstrates a related Na–5d oxide can be made at 400 °C under O₂ flow. F3 is better than the carbonate/Re route for oxidation chemistry because peroxide precursors are highly reactive, but low-temperature diffusion and phase selectivity remain limiting. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for peroxide synthesis of Li–Re oxides, so CS is neutral for this route except for the general Re–Li oxide precedent in CS2/CS3.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 high-pressure route.**  
F2 is lower because SS1, TS1, and TS3 all require specialized Au capsules and high-pressure apparatus, even though the recommended 1.5–4 GPa is below the scoring penalty threshold of >5 GPa. F3 is moderate because pressure and high temperature enhance reaction and can stabilize metastable dense phases, but SS1 and TS1 show mixed products, hygroscopicity, or serendipitous crystallization. This route is best viewed as a rescue strategy if ambient flux/ceramic methods fail.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 flux route.**  
F2 is high because CS5 and TS2 show low-temperature molten salt/flux routes at 300–430 °C without high pressure or vacuum. F3 is the best among routes because nitrate/alkali flux can overcome Re diffusion limitations by liquid-phase transport, and NH₄ReO₄ can provide a more oxidized Re source than Re metal [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that neither CS nor SS nor TS directly reports Na₂LiReO₆ flux growth, so optimization of Na/Li/Re ratio and oxygen activity is expected.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation-size and coordination mismatch between Na, Li, and Re, which triggers C1 and lowers the score [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The unit cell atom count is not shown to exceed 40, and the target description does not explicitly report simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS and TS analogues show that complex alkali transition-metal oxides can crystallize, but SS1/SS2 also indicate hygroscopicity and impurity formation can occur.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂LiReO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The chemical analogues strongly support Li–Re oxide formation by ceramic synthesis, especially Sr₂LiReO₆ (CS2, CS3), while structurally similar Na–5d oxides support oxygen-rich alkali routes but warn of impurity and hygroscopicity issues (SS1, SS2). The most important enabling factor is maintaining high oxygen chemical potential while providing mobile Na/Li species, which is best addressed by nitrate/peroxide flux chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS5, TS2, and SS2]. The most important limiting factor is selective crystallization of the low-symmetry P1 Na₂LiReO₆ phase instead of competing Na–Re–O, Li–Re–O, or simple perrhenate/peroxide phases. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful optimization rather than a single direct firing.
