### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase in the supplied target data. This is reinforced by ReSe₂ being on the hull (SS2; TS1) and the closest Re–Se–Cl structural analogue being nearly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0016 eV/atom (SS1; TS2). CS also shows many chemically related chalcogenides at or near the hull, although CS lacks a direct Re–Se–F example and therefore provides only general stability support (CS1–CS5; insufficient direct CS evidence for Re–F).

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-ampoule solid-state.**  
The severity is high-moderate because the closest Re–Se–Cl analogue requires 900 °C, sealed vacuum ampoules, long reaction time, and slow cooling (SS1; TS2). Kinetics are limited by refractory Re metal [Reasonable Inference], but the target framework is closely related to the Re–Se–Cl sheet, which improves structural transferability (SS1; TS2). The main uncertainty is whether ReFₓ can deliver F without forming volatile Re fluorides or attacking the ampoule [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, CVT/vapor-assisted growth.**  
This route is severe because ReSe₂ CVT uses high temperatures around 1080–1000 °C, evacuated tubes, and halogen transport agents (SS2; TS1). Kinetics are better than direct solid-state reaction because vapor transport is proven for ReSe₂ and related Ta–Se chalcogenides (SS2; TS1; TS3; TS4). The uncertainty is fluorine incorporation: the sources support halogen-assisted transport, but none proves Re–Se–F crystal growth directly (insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS).

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux synthesis.**  
The flux route keeps the maximum temperature comparable to or slightly below the 900 °C Re–Se–Cl synthesis while improving mass transport, which is consistent with flux-grown complex selenides (CS2; CS3; SS1). Kinetics benefit from molten salt transport, but Re remains refractory and F activity must be controlled [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides useful flux evidence for complex selenides, SS/TS provide the closest Re–Se–halide structural basis, but none directly validates fluoride flux chemistry for Re₃Se₄F.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Re, so cation size disorder is absent [Target]. The described structure is ordered and low-dimensional with edge/corner-sharing ReSe₅ units but no face-sharing network complexity [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is the actual crystallographic cell size, but the formula and description do not indicate a large >40 atom unit cell [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re₃Se₄F is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from the very close Re–Se–Cl layered analogue Tl₂Re₆Se₈Cl₄ and from ReSe₂, both of which share low-dimensional Re–Se bonding motifs and are experimentally accessible by sealed-tube or CVT methods (SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or molten-salt fluxing, which can overcome slow Re diffusion and promote crystallization (SS1; SS2; CS2; CS3; TS1). The most critical limiting factor is controlled fluorine chemical potential because none of the supplied CS/SS/TS references directly demonstrates Re–Se–F synthesis, and volatile/corrosive ReFₓ species may cause stoichiometry loss or ampoule attack [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but experimentally nontrivial.
