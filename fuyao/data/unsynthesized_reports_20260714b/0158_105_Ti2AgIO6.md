### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-stability/metastability threshold in the provided rubric. TS analogues with E hull values of about 0.062–0.098 eV/atom were synthesized by melt, solid-state, hydrothermal, or ion-exchange methods, including KIn(WO₄)₂ in TS3, Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ in TS4, and LaO₃I-related material in TS2. This supports feasibility for a slightly metastable phase, but the target is unlikely to be a robust deep ground-state phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal conditions are moderate because CS2/CS3 used 190 °C for 3 days and CS4 used 230 °C for 4 days, both with standard Teflon-lined autoclaves. The main severity penalties are sealed operation with iodine oxide species, multi-step processing, and oxidizing/corrosive reagents. TS2 adds uncertainty because La–O–I hydrothermal synthesis was extremely sensitive to pH, concentration, and temperature.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Kinetics are favorable because CS2/CS3 demonstrate formation of a complex oxyiodate framework at only 190 °C from soluble/reactive precursors. Hydrothermal transport reduces cation diffusion limitations compared with solid-state synthesis, and CS4 supports related hydrothermal oxide crystallization at 230 °C. The main uncertainty is TiO₂ dissolution/reactivity; therefore hydrous TiO₂ or anatase is preferred by reasonable inference.

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed solid-state/flux

This route has higher severity because it likely requires evacuated sealed tubes, as used in CS1, SS3, SS4, and SS5, and iodine oxide volatility makes sealed containment important. Temperatures can probably remain below 1000 °C based on SS1, SS3, and CS1, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The key penalties are vacuum/sealing, volatile iodine oxide, multi-step ampoule handling, and oxidizing/toxic precursor hazards.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed solid-state/flux

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because TiO₂ is refractory and the target requires simultaneous Ti–O, Ag–O, and I–O network formation. Flux methods in CS5, SS3, and SS5 improve transport, but SS/CS analogues also report phase-control issues: CS1 required rigorous reaction conditions and washing, while SS2 reported unintended product formation and modest yield control. Thus the route is feasible but more likely to produce competing titanates, silver oxides, iodates, or volatile iodine loss.

#### F2 — Route 3 ion-exchange/topotactic

Ion-exchange severity is moderate because TS1 and TS5 use low temperatures and aqueous processing rather than high-temperature/high-pressure equipment. However, the route is inherently multi-step: making parent titanate, proton exchange, and Ag/I insertion. If the final insertion is hydrothermal, sealed-vessel handling and iodine oxidants add modest hazard.

#### F3 — Route 3 ion-exchange/topotactic

TS1 shows titanate ion exchange can proceed at 333 K over 5 days, and TS5 shows aqueous cation exchange into niobate frameworks, so cation mobility can be high. The kinetic advantage is retention of Ti–O framework fragments, reducing full reconstruction. The uncertainty is structural: no CS/SS/TS source proves that a layered titanate can topotactically transform into the target P1 Ti₂AgO₆I framework.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different size and coordination preferences—tetrahedral Ti, linear Ag, and see-saw-like iodine—so C1 is triggered by reasonable inference from the target structure. C2 is not triggered because the provided formula and P1 description do not establish a >40 atom unit cell, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this target-specific cell-size factor. C3 is not triggered because the target description emphasizes corner-sharing TiO₄ tetrahedra and does not report edge/face-sharing coexistence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂AgO₆I is best viewed as a borderline metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E above hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS2/CS3, where a chemically similar oxyiodate was hydrothermally synthesized cleanly at 190 °C, and from TS2, which confirms hydrothermal oxide–iodine chemistry but warns of narrow synthesis windows. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution-mediated transport, which can assemble Ti/Ag/I–O units without long-range solid-state diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is iodine/oxygen chemical-potential control, because iodine oxide species are volatile/reactive and hydrothermal iodide/iodate systems can be pH- and concentration-sensitive. Overall, the target appears reasonably synthesizable, with hydrothermal crystallization the preferred first attempt.
