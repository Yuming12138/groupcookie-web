### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s **0.0500 eV/atom** hull energy places it at the boundary between likely accessible metastability and stronger instability [Target]. TS analogues with comparable hull energies are reported as synthesized, including LiLa2ReO6, Ca2Ta2O6F, Nd3ReO7, and YTiO3 [TS1][TS3][TS4][TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that none of the TS examples is chemically identical to Zr–Cr–O, so the Cr²⁺ redox requirement is inferred rather than directly proven [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 has mild thermal severity because CS1 uses combustion at **800 °C** for a Cr oxide [CS1]. The score is reduced because the target likely needs a controlled low-pO₂ final anneal to stabilize Cr²⁺, which is inferred from charge balance and supported indirectly by sealed/reducing analogues [CS2][TS4][Reasonable Inference]. **Insufficient evidence from SS for combustion severity; SS scored neutral = 50.**

**F3 — Route 1.** Nitrate/fuel chemistry gives high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths, directly supported by CS1 [CS1]. Kinetics are not scored near-perfect because the target is not the same structure as YCrO3 and likely needs redox reconstruction from Cr³⁺-bearing combustion ash toward Cr²⁺ ZrCrO3 [CS1][Target][Reasonable Inference]. **Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for combustion kinetics; SS = 50 and TS = 50 neutral.**

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 inherits high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere penalties because solid-state analogues use evacuated, Ar, or vacuum firing at elevated temperatures [CS2][SS1][SS5][TS4]. It avoids high pressure, but multiple firing/regrinding steps and Cr handling reduce the score [CS2][SS5][Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS all provide useful signals for this route, so no source is neutral for Route 2 severity [CS2][SS1][SS5][TS4].

**F3 — Route 2.** Dense ZrO2 and Cr2O3 are expected to react slowly, consistent with SS5 requiring several firings with regrinding for titanate phase purity [SS5][Reasonable Inference]. CS2 also uses **48 h** sealed sintering, supporting a slow bulk-diffusion picture [CS2]. The uncertainty is whether a Cr/Cr2O3 redox buffer can produce phase-pure Cr²⁺ ZrCrO3 without trapping binary oxides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.** High-pressure synthesis receives the largest severity penalty because CS4/CS5 use **5–5.5 GPa** and specialized multi-anvil assemblies [CS4][CS5]. The route is still plausible because CS4/CS5 show that chemically related oxide phases can require pressure to stabilize [CS4][CS5]. **Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for high-pressure severity; SS = 50 and TS = 50 neutral.**

**F3 — Route 3.** High pressure can shorten the path to dense metastable phases, as shown by CS4/CS5 forming Y–V–O phases at **1200 °C for 2 h** [CS4][CS5]. Kinetics remain only moderate because the target composition and redox state are not directly demonstrated under pressure [Target][Reasonable Inference]. **Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for high-pressure kinetics; SS = 50 and TS = 50 neutral.**

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target appears to have no formal F4 triggers: the likely Zr⁴⁺/Cr²⁺ radius mismatch is below 15%, Pnma ABO3 analogues are 20-atom cells, and the target reports corner/edge sharing but not face sharing [CS1][CS3][SS5][TS5][Target][Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is the unusual distorted ZrO6 “pentagonal pyramid” description, which may increase local strain even without triggering the formal amorphization checks [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct ionic-radius data, so those sources are neutral for C1 as stated above.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrCrO3 is best viewed as a **borderline metastable but accessible** target because its hull energy is exactly **0.0500 eV/atom** [Target]. The CS data strongly supports reactive Cr-oxide synthesis by combustion, but the easiest CS analogue is Cr³⁺ YCrO3 in air, which conflicts with the likely Cr²⁺ requirement of ZrCrO3 [CS1][Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS data support the need for sealed, vacuum, Ar, or reducing conditions when low-valent or phase-sensitive oxides are targeted [SS5][TS4][TS5]. The most important enabling factor is **nanoscale precursor mixing followed by controlled oxygen fugacity** [CS1][CS2][TS4][Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is stabilizing Cr²⁺ without leaving ZrO2/Cr2O3 impurities or requiring high-pressure equipment [CS2][SS5][CS4][CS5][Reasonable Inference].
