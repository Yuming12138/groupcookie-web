### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a high thermodynamic score. The closest Ta₃SeI₇ and Ta₃TeI₇ analogues are also near-hull, with E_hull values of 0.0103 and 0.0062 eV/atom, respectively (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). Thermodynamic similarity data reinforces that the known Ta₃QI₇ family is near the same stability regime (TS1–2). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee clean experimental phase formation, as Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ show side products despite near-hull stability (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses only moderate temperature, around 450 °C, which is directly supported by Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ synthesis (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). The severity deductions arise from vacuum/sealed ampoule use and volatile iodine, both explicitly present in the analogue syntheses (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). Iodine toxicity/corrosivity and sulfur volatility are physicochemical risks added as [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids high pressure and complex multistep processing, so the severity is moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic limitation is Ta metal activation: Ta foil was used in the analogues (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2), but refractory Ta should react slowly [Reasonable Inference]. The fact that Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ form at 450 °C suggests iodine-mediated transport or surface iodination can overcome some diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference based on CS1–2/SS1–2/TS1–2 conditions]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target is essentially the S analogue of the same Ta₃QI₇ family (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). The main uncertainty is whether sulfur substitution changes the stability window or generates sulfur iodide side chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is based on direct elemental synthesis of Nb₃TeI₇ in evacuated tubes at 600–800 °C (CS5; SS4–5; TS4–5). This remains below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies, but vacuum and sealed volatile iodine still impose penalties. The higher temperature may be risky for Ta₃SI₇ because Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ analogues are reported to have a narrow stability range up to ca. 550 °C (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). Thus, the route is conditionally feasible but less directly supported than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Higher temperature may improve Ta diffusion and reaction completion [Reasonable Inference], and Nb₃TeI₇ analogues demonstrate formation of the same M₃QI₇ layered motif from elements (CS5; SS4–5; TS4–5). However, Nb₃TeI₇ exhibits multiple polytypes depending on temperature, indicating phase selection is nontrivial (CS5; SS4–5; TS4–5). The closest Ta analogues also have side-product issues (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). Therefore, the kinetic score is slightly better than Route 1 for reaction completion but still limited by phase competition and volatile-component control.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses vacuum ampoules and halogen transport, supported by Nb₃I₈ CVT in quartz ampoules under temperature gradients (SS3; TS3). It is more operationally complex than direct heating because it requires either pre-reaction or a transport/purification step [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is not extreme, but iodine transport chemistry increases sealed-tube and toxicity risks. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Ta₃SI₇ CVT; CS is neutral, 50, for CVT-specific transferability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport can improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and this is consistent with Nb₃I₈ purification by chemical transport (SS3; TS3). However, Nb₃I₈ lacks sulfur, so it does not fully establish ternary Ta–S–I transport chemistry. Excess iodine could favor TaI₅/Ta₆I₁₄ side phases, which are specifically observed in Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ syntheses (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). The route may be valuable for single crystals or purification but is less certain for first synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, Ta, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula unit Ta₃SI₇ contains 11 atoms, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom unit cell for the P3m1 target; structurally similar P3m1 Nb₃TeI₇ is also a compact layered motif (CS5; SS4; TS5). The local coordination is ordered and crystalline in close analogues, including Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ and Nb₃TeI₇ (CS1–2, CS5; SS1–2, SS4–5; TS1–2, TS4–5). Remaining uncertainty is the lack of explicit connectivity analysis for edge/face/vertex-sharing coexistence, so that sub-check is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃SI₇ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stabilized phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis is well supported by close Ta₃SeI₇ and Ta₃TeI₇ analogues, which share the Ta₃QI₇ chemistry and layered Ta–I coordination environment and were prepared by sealed-ampoule reactions (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). The most critical enabling factor is iodine-assisted sealed-tube chemistry, which likely activates refractory Ta and stabilizes layered Ta iodide frameworks [Reasonable Inference based on CS1–2/SS1–2/TS1–2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: Ta₃SeI₇/Ta₃TeI₇ analogues reportedly form with TaI₅, Ta₆I₁₄, and Ta metal side products and have a narrow temperature stability range (CS1–2; SS1–2; TS1–2). Overall, the material is synthesizable but likely requires careful ampoule loading, temperature control, and quenching rather than simple high-temperature ceramic processing.
