### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.047 eV/atom falls just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bracket, so a score of 92/100 is assigned. This is consistent with MoCl3, the closest structural and thermodynamic analogue, which is also near hull at 0.034 eV/atom [SS1, TS1], and with OsCl4 being calculated on the hull [CS5, SS5]. The main uncertainty is that the target is a mixed-cation ordered Mo/Os chloride, and ordering enthalpy/entropy may shift the accessible phase field [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions, volatile chloride transport, multiple handling/heating steps, and air-sensitive/toxic chlorides, all of which are reflected in MoCl3 CVT and related chloride syntheses [SS1, TS1, CS4]. It avoids >1000 °C and avoids GPa pressure, consistent with the moderate-temperature chloride analogues [SS1, TS1, CS4, CS5]. The remaining uncertainty is the safe and reproducible control of Os chloride vapor pressure in the same ampoule as Mo chloride vapor species [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by volatile chloride transport, as MoCl3 and TaCl4 are grown by CVT/sealed-tube methods [SS1, TS1, CS4]. However, Os starts as a refractory metal in this route, and OsCl4 synthesis requires high chlorine pressure and several days, implying sluggish or condition-sensitive Os chlorination [CS5, SS5]. The structure is favorable because MoCl3 already supplies the AlCl3-type layered edge-sharing motif [SS1, TS1], while OsCl4 demonstrates stable OsCl6 edge-sharing units [CS5, SS5].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses direct chlorination, which is experimentally supported for OsCl4 and TcCl3-like chlorides [CS5, SS5, SS2, TS2]. The route is not ultra-high-temperature and not GPa-pressure, but it is penalized for dry Cl2, sealed-tube volatile pressure, and toxic/corrosive chlorine handling [CS5, SS5, TS3]. The main uncertainty is whether pressure needed to stabilize Os chlorides also overchlorinates Mo into volatile higher chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct reaction from Mo and Os powders has the weakest kinetics because both metals are dense/refractory and must chlorinate and intermix [Reasonable Inference]. Multi-day direct chlorinations are reported for OsCl4 and TcCl3 [CS5, SS5, SS2, TS2], supporting the need for prolonged reaction. The AlCl3-type layered trichloride structure is plausible from MoCl3/TcCl3 analogues [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2], but complete framework formation from elements lowers the kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is less severe than Route 1 because it can use preformed chlorides and a simpler sealed-ampoule anneal, analogous to IrCl3 recrystallization and Re/W chloride ampoule syntheses [SS3, CS1, TS4, TS5]. It is still penalized for vacuum ampoule work, volatile chlorides, and air-sensitive precursors [SS3, CS1, TS4]. The source data do not provide a direct Mo–Os mixed chloride synthesis, so difficulty remains moderate-to-challenging [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; neutral for direct target precedent].

#### F3 — Route 3

This route has the best kinetics because it starts from chloride frameworks, especially MoCl3 with the same AlCl3-derived 2D edge-sharing topology [SS1, TS1]. IrCl3 demonstrates that preformed trichloride powders can be recrystallized in sealed evacuated ampoules into edge-sharing chloride polymorphs [SS3]. The uncertainty is Mo/Os cation ordering: similar Mo–Cl and Os–Cl distances support compatibility [Target], but ordering may be slow in a layered solid [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has simple edge-sharing octahedral connectivity and no reported coexistence of edge, corner, and face sharing [Target], so the structural glass-forming tendency is low. The Mo–Cl and Os–Cl bond lengths are close enough that a large cation-radius mismatch is unlikely [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that subfactor is treated as neutral/no-trigger rather than as evidence for complexity [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MoOsCl6 is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.047 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from structural and thermodynamic analogues: MoCl3 has the same AlCl3-derived layered edge-sharing octahedral topology [SS1, TS1], and OsCl4 confirms robust Os–Cl octahedral edge-sharing chemistry [CS5, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is halide volatility/chemical transport, which allows crystalline transition-metal chlorides to form at 200–525 °C rather than requiring cation diffusion near refractory-metal melting points [SS1, TS1, CS4, CS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and chlorine-potential control, because related Mo/Tc/Os chloride systems show multiple oxidation states, polymorphs, dissociation, low yields, or narrow phase windows [SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2, CS5, SS5]. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible but likely requires careful sealed-ampoule chloride chemistry rather than a simple open solid-state route.
