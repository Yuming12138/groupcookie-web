### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0320 eV/atom is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom window, giving a high thermodynamic score. TS provides useful support because several thermodynamically similar materials near 0.02–0.05 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable, including TlAsO4 (TS-1), TlFeO3 (TS-2), CsCuF4 (TS-3), FeF3 (TS-4), and BaFeCuF7 (TS-5). SS also supports synthesizability of near-hull thallium-containing analogues, especially TlAsO4 at 0.0289 eV/atom and TlFeO3 at 0.0473 eV/atom (SS-1, SS-3). CS is mixed: several chemically similar fluorides are on-hull and synthesized (CS-2 to CS-5), but BiF3 is listed at 0.2677 eV/atom while still experimentally prepared (CS-1), so CS gives only moderate thermodynamic discrimination.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: high-pressure fluorination

Route 1 has severe but established conditions: TS-3 reports CsCuF4 from high-pressure F₂ at 400 °C and ~350–360 bar, while CS-4 reports 350–400 atm F₂ at 390–450 °C. These sources justify the F2 penalties for reactive atmosphere, pressure-vessel operation, and toxic precursors, but not the GPa-pressure penalty because the route is not >5 GPa. Kinetically, F₂ fluorination should be effective for reaching Tl(III), and the single-cation target avoids slow multication ordering; however, structural reconstruction from TlF to layered TlF3 is uncertain and phase control in high-valent fluorides is difficult in TS-3, CS-4, and SS-5.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: sealed solid fluorination

Route 2 borrows the sealed-ampoule concept from Tl2SnF6 synthesis using TlF in Pt ampoules under Ar at 300 °C (CS-3) and from sealed fluoride syntheses in copper/noble-metal tubes (SS-4, CS-2, CS-5). The F2 score is lower because the route requires multiple handling steps, volatile/reactive fluorinating agents, and sealed-tube safety controls, with XeF2/AgF2 as [Reasonable Inference] reagents rather than directly reported CS/SS/TS precursors. Kinetics are favorable because solid fluorinating agents provide high local fluorine activity, but the absence of a direct topotactic analogue for Pmmn TlF3 keeps the structural-reconstruction score moderate-low. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that this lower-temperature route reaches the same TlF3 polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: precipitation / solvothermal route

CS-1 strongly supports mild precipitation for BiF3, and SS-1/TS-1 support room-temperature solution growth for a thallium sheet-like arsenate. Therefore, F2 is high because temperature, pressure, and equipment severity are low. However, F3 is limited by phase formation uncertainty: CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that binary TlF3 can be precipitated from solution as the target orthorhombic Pmmn phase. The main physicochemical concern is Tl(III) hydrolysis and possible fluoro-complex formation in solution, which is a [Reasonable Inference] not contradicted by the supplied analogues.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so cation-size mismatch is not a risk by the defined criterion. The target is a simple binary fluoride sheet structure, and the prompt does not indicate a >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network. CS/SS/TS contain examples of more complex mixed-cation or mixed-connectivity fluorides that are still crystalline, such as CS-4, CS-5, and TS-5, but direct evidence for TlF3 amorphization is insufficient; therefore, the low amorphization risk follows mainly from the target’s simple composition and small formula.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlF3 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its target E_hull is only 0.0320 eV/atom, and thermodynamically similar near-hull phases are experimentally accessible in TS-1 to TS-5. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through high-fluorine-potential routes: high-pressure F₂ fluorination is repeatedly used for high-valent fluorides in TS-3, CS-4, and SS-5. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high fluorine chemical potential to oxidize Tl(I) to Tl(III) and suppress lower fluoride phases, supported by TS-3 and CS-4. The most critical limiting factor is phase and safety control under reactive fluorination conditions, especially because Tl compounds are highly toxic and fluorine chemistry is hazardous, as implied by CS-3, TS-3, CS-4, and SS-5. Overall, the material is synthesizable in a specialized fluoride-chemistry laboratory but is not a routine ceramic target.
