### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category by the required rubric [Target]. Several close SS/TS analogues, including IrBr₃, RuBr₃, OsCl₄, and MoCl₅, also have E_hull ≈0 eV/atom, showing that edge-sharing transition-metal halides can be thermodynamic ground states [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. However, thermodynamic stability does not guarantee ease of synthesis because SS/TS analogues report narrow phase windows, polymorphism, and halogen-pressure sensitivity [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for sealed halogen vapor and toxic/volatile Br₂/Cl₂, which are directly supported by IrBr₃/RuBr₃/OsCl₄ syntheses using Br₂/Cl₂ in sealed systems [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. It is not penalized for >1000 °C because even the harshest IrBr₃ analogue used 900→450 °C [SS1, TS1], and the proposed target route is inferred at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target needs higher halogen pressure to stabilize Ni³⁺, analogous to OsCl₄ needing 6–8 atm Cl₂ to avoid dissociation [SS4, TS4].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Ir is refractory and dense, which should slow direct reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Halogen CVT improves transport, as shown by IrBr₃ and RuBr₃ crystal growth under Br₂/halogen gradients [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The structural match to AlCl₃-type IrBr₃ is strong [SS1, TS1], but the target requires cation and mixed-halide ordering not demonstrated by the binary analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from low temperature because AlCl₃/NaCl flux syntheses operated at 130 °C [CS3, CS4]. It is still penalized for glovebox/vacuum handling, volatile AlCl₃, possible halogen oxidants, and multi-step ampoule/flux processing [CS3, CS4]. CS precipitation examples at room temperature [CS1] provide insufficient evidence for making the extended IrNi halide sheet and are scored as neutral signal rather than lowering the severity estimate.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The flux route has the best kinetic score because molten AlCl₃/NaCl can dissolve and redistribute halide species, and CS3/CS4 demonstrate crystalline products after low-temperature flux treatment. This liquid-mediated mechanism should mitigate Ir/Ni diffusion limitations relative to dense-metal halogenation [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural: CS3/CS4 products are chemically similar halide compounds but not AlCl₃-type bimetallic Ir/Ni sheets, so the assembly pathway is inferred rather than proven.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, following CS5 sealed fused-silica-tube reactions at 350–400 °C. It is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile halides/halogens, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive/toxic halides [CS5, SS5, TS5]. SS5/TS5 show that sealed halide chemistry can require strict glovebox and controlled cooling procedures, supporting a moderate-to-challenging severity score.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Preformed halides should react faster than elemental Ir/Ni because they already contain metal–halogen coordination [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 supports sealed-tube formation of crystalline transition-metal halide compounds, and SS/TS analogues show that halide vapor/mineralizer transport can assist crystal growth [CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. Kinetics remain limited by solid-state ordering of Ir/Ni and Br/Cl, and MoCl₅ analogues show polymorph sensitivity in halide systems [SS5, TS5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple edge-sharing octahedral layer, not mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk [Target]. The Ir–Cl and Ni–Cl bond lengths are similar, suggesting octahedral size compatibility and low strain [Target]. Remaining uncertainty comes from formal oxidation/spin-state radii for Ni³⁺/Ir³⁺, but the structural bond lengths argue against severe size mismatch [Target; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

IrNi(BrCl₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS: IrBr₃ is an AlCl₃-structured 2D edge-sharing octahedral halide synthesized by sealed halogen transport [SS1, TS1]. CS data support low-temperature halide flux chemistry through AlCl₃/NaCl melts [CS3, CS4], but chemically similar precipitation examples are mostly molecular/zero-dimensional and provide insufficient evidence for the extended target sheet [CS1, CS2]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high halogen chemical potential in a sealed system to form octahedral Ir/Ni halide sheets [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ni oxidation state, Ir/Ni ordering, and Br/Cl stoichiometry, which is not directly demonstrated in the reference set [Reasonable Inference].
