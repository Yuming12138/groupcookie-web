### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0320 eV/atom, which falls in the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom band specified by the prompt. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized, including MnIn₂Te₄-II at E_hull ≈ 0.0354 eV/atom under high pressure (TS-1), MnIn₂Se₄-related material at ≈0.0316 eV/atom by CVT (TS-2), and Bi₂TeI at ≈0.0388 eV/atom by gas-phase synthesis (TS-3). CS and SS also include several stable or near-stable analogues, including InTeBr at E_hull = 0 (CS-1/SS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that a small positive hull may indicate metastability or a narrow phase window, especially for iodides (TS-3, TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 Bridgman / Melt Growth

The 520 °C Bridgman synthesis of InTeBr directly supports moderate-temperature melt growth for an In–Te–halide analogue (CS-1, SS-4). The deductions arise from the need for sealed, dry, controlled handling because iodides/iodine are volatile and moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference supported by iodine ampoule practice in TS-5 and iodide transport in TS-3]. No high external pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required based on the InTeBr analogue (CS-1/SS-4). The main uncertainty is whether InTeI melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically, since this is not reported in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 Bridgman / Melt Growth

Kinetics should be favorable because the closest analogue, InTeBr, crystallizes directly from the melt at 520 °C (CS-1, SS-4). The target and analogue share the same InTe₃X tetrahedral sheet motif and trigonal non-coplanar Te coordination (Target; CS-1/SS-4). Iodide substitution may alter melting behavior and volatility, so the score is not maximal [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for Bridgman kinetics in In–Te–I.

#### F2 — Route 2 Direct Solid-State / Fusion

Sealed evacuated ampoule synthesis is well supported for related chalcogenides and iodides, including AgIn₅Te₈, Cd₄GeSe₆, and BiI (SS-5, TS-4, TS-5). The key penalties are vacuum/sealed atmosphere, volatile iodine, and hazardous iodine/tellurium handling (TS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Although some structural analogues require >1000 °C (SS-5, TS-4), InTeI is expected to be synthesized at lower temperature due to iodine volatility and In/halide melting behavior [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is competing In–Te and In–I binary formation.

#### F3 — Route 2 Direct Solid-State / Fusion

Elemental fusion benefits from low-melting In and mobile iodine species [Reasonable Inference], and BiI forms at relatively low temperature in closed ampoules (TS-5). However, assembling an ordered 2D ternary phase from elements is less kinetically direct than using InI₃/In₂Te₃, and iodide systems can show polymorphism or mixed products (TS-3, TS-5). SS-5 shows direct fusion can work for In–Te systems but at high temperature and long duration. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for elemental InTeI fusion.

#### F2 — Route 3 CVT / Gas-Phase Growth

CVT uses evacuated sealed tubes and volatile transport agents, as shown for MnIn₂Se₄ with AlCl₃ and Bi₂TeI with iodide/telluride transport species (TS-2, TS-3). The penalties reflect vacuum, volatile iodides, multi-step prereaction plus transport, and hygroscopic/corrosive transport agents (TS-2, TS-3; SS-3). Temperatures can likely remain below 1000 °C based on TS-2 and TS-3. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for InTeI CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3 CVT / Gas-Phase Growth

Vapor transport is plausible because iodide species are mobile and Bi₂TeI was grown from a gas-phase iodide/telluride system (TS-3). MnIn₂Se₄ CVT also shows that difficult In-containing chalcogenides can be crystallized by transport when prereaction does not yield single phase (TS-2, SS-3). The main kinetic limitation is composition control: TS-3 reports two types of small crystals and phase complexity. Therefore CVT is better as a crystal-growth/optimization route than as the first discovery route.

#### F2 — Route 4 Hydrothermal HI

Hydrothermal AuTeI synthesis in hydroiodic acid directly supports low-temperature formation of Te–I plate crystals in an iodide-rich medium (CS-3). The method avoids high temperature, vacuum, and high external pressure, but it requires sealed autoclaves and corrosive volatile HI/I₂ chemistry (CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal In–Te–I, so the score is penalized indirectly through expert adjustment rather than F2. The uncertainty is vessel compatibility and In hydrolysis/redox behavior.

#### F3 — Route 4 Hydrothermal HI

Solution-mediated transport can overcome solid-state diffusion, and AuTeI forms plate-like crystals hydrothermally in HI (CS-3). The target is an iodotelluride sheet, so a similar iodide-rich growth environment is chemically plausible [Reasonable Inference]. However, Au and In have different redox/coordination chemistry, and no SS/TS source confirms hydrothermal synthesis of an In telluroiodide. Thus the kinetic score is favorable but not as secure as the InTeBr Bridgman route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered (Target). The structure is a simple layered P3m1 InTeI phase rather than a >40-atom complex cell, and the target description reports corner-sharing InTe₃I tetrahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity (Target). CS/SS analogues such as InTeBr also form ordered 2D sheets rather than amorphous products (CS-1/SS-4). The remaining uncertainty is stacking disorder in layered iodides, but that is not the same as bulk amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InTeI is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.032 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues with similar positive hulls have been synthesized (Target; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The strongest synthesis support comes from InTeBr, which has nearly identical InTe₃X tetrahedral sheet chemistry and is grown by Bridgman from InBr₃/In₂Te₃ (CS-1, SS-4). Hydrothermal HI synthesis is attractive because AuTeI forms as plate crystals in hydroiodic acid (CS-3), but its transferability to In chemistry is less certain. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of closely matched layered tellurohalide analogues with single-bonded halides and trigonal non-coplanar Te coordination (CS-1, CS-3, SS-4). The most critical limiting factor is iodine chemical-potential control, because TS iodides show volatility, phase multiplicity, polymorphism, and small product amounts in gas-phase/ampoule syntheses (TS-3, TS-5).
