### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0130 eV/atom, placing it in the high-probability synthesizable range of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamically similar synthesized fluorides include CaTiF5 at 0.0119 eV/atom, NbFeF6 at 0.0204 eV/atom, and VNbF6 at 0.0086 eV/atom (TS1, TS2, TS3). CS and SS also include low-hull synthesized fluorides such as VNbF6 and CaTiF5 (CS3, SS1, SS2), supporting the idea that small positive hull energies are not prohibitive. The remaining uncertainty is that TS analogues are fluorides without Te, so thermodynamic similarity may not fully capture mixed telluride-fluoride stability; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Nb–Te–F phase equilibria.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 500–540 °C, directly supported by NbFeF6, VNbF6, and NbCrF6 syntheses (SS3/TS2, CS3/SS1/TS3, CS1/TS5). It requires sealed tubes and dry glovebox handling because Nb fluorides are moisture-sensitive and sealed systems are repeatedly used in the analogues (CS1, CS3, SS3, TS2/TS3/TS5). There is no high pressure and no >1000 °C treatment in the proposed route, but volatile/corrosive fluoride handling and possible Te volatility impose deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Te volatility management in this specific chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Reactive Nb fluorides are favorable precursors because NbF4/NbF5 are used successfully in several Nb fluoride analogues (CS1, CS3, SS3, TS2/TS3/TS5). However, the route includes Nb metal, whose refractory nature slows diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the target’s 2D Nb–Te–F sheet is structurally different from common 3D NbMF6 frameworks in CS/SS/TS. Multi-day anneals in Nb fluoride analogues indicate kinetics are manageable but not fast (CS3, SS3, TS2/TS3). The biggest uncertainty is whether Te can be incorporated before competing Nb fluorides or Nb tellurides form; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Flux analogues require high but not extreme temperatures: 1053 K for CsTiF4 flux growth (SS4), 800 °C for NaTiF4 flux growth (SS5), and 800 °C for CaTiF5 melt growth (SS2/TS1). These methods require inert atmosphere, controlled slow cooling, and specialized crucibles/reactors but not high pressure (SS2, SS4, SS5, TS1). The route is penalized for multi-step processing and sealed/anhydrous handling of fluoride-containing melts. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Te compatibility with chloride/fluoride fluxes.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth improves mass transport and can promote crystallization of complex fluorides, as shown for CsTiF4 and NaTiF4 (SS4, SS5). This helps offset the diffusion difficulty of Nb-containing solids [Reasonable Inference], but the target has no direct structural analogue in the flux data. Slow cooling is beneficial for crystal growth, supported by SS4 and SS5, yet it may also allow competing binary or ternary phases to equilibrate. The uncertainty is high because CS/SS/TS do not show Te-bearing fluoride flux growth.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The NH4HF2 route operates at lower temperatures, 380–450 °C, in sealed Monel ampoules, as shown for Hf/Zr ammonium fluorides (CS4). Despite the lower temperature, it is penalized heavily because NH4HF2 generates corrosive HF/NH3 species and requires sealed ampoules (CS4). It also likely requires a preformed Nb–Te precursor, making it multi-step [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports NH4HF2 chemistry for the target; only CS4 gives a partial signal.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

NH4HF2 provides high fluorinating activity, as demonstrated in CS4 for metal-to-fluoride conversion. If a layered Nb–Te precursor can be prepared, low-temperature fluorination could be topochemical and kinetically favorable [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS4 forms Hf/Zr fluoride-ammonia sheets rather than Nb–Te–F sheets, so structural transferability is weak. The key uncertainty is over-fluorination or Te loss under HF/NH3 conditions; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target formula is small, Nb3TeF7, and the described structure is an ordered 2D sheet rather than a very large multi-cation framework. The target does not obviously trigger the ionic-radius mismatch check because Nb is the only clear metal cation and Te appears as a bonded telluride-like anion [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on Te oxidation state. The target description does not indicate simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. Remaining uncertainty comes from the 2D mixed-anion nature, which may favor stacking disorder even if not true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb3TeF7 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0130 eV/atom, similar to several synthesized low-hull fluoride analogues in TS data (TS1–TS3). CS/SS/TS strongly support the feasibility of making Nb-containing fluorides under dry, sealed, moderate-temperature conditions, especially using NbF4/NbF5 in copper or metal tubes (CS1, CS3, SS3, TS2/TS3/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive Nb fluoride precursors that can form Nb–F frameworks at ~500 °C rather than requiring extreme temperatures. The most critical limiting factor is the absence of direct evidence for Te incorporation in any CS/SS/TS analogue, making control of Nb–Te versus Nb–F competing phases uncertain. Overall, the target is plausible but requires careful fluorine-potential and Te-activity control.
