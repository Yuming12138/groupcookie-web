### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable/metastable boundary. TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, and TS-5 include synthesized hydrides with comparable or higher E_hull values around 0.054–0.087 eV/atom, supporting feasible metastable synthesis under pressure. CS and SS include many stable or near-stable hydrides, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact Na–Al–Bi–H phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is favored because SS-5 directly demonstrates Na–Al complex hydride formation by ball milling plus 180 °C/30 bar D₂ annealing, and SS-1 demonstrates a structurally similar Na-containing hydride via ball milling plus 315 °C/50 bar H₂ annealing. The main severity penalties are H₂/inert atmosphere and air-sensitive hydride precursors, both clearly supported by SS-1/SS-5 and CS-4. Kinetically, mechanochemistry improves precursor intimacy, but the target’s triclinic P1 structure and Bi incorporation are not directly supported by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Direct sintering is supported by CS-4 for Na₂PtH₄ and by TS-2 for NaBaPdH₃, both using NaH/metal or hydride/metal mixtures under H₂. The route is more severe than Route 1 because it likely requires longer pellet sintering and higher pressure/temperature, as seen in CS-1/CS-2 and TS-2. Kinetic uncertainty is larger because Al/Bi metal reaction with hydride under H₂ is less directly evidenced than preformed Na–Al complex hydrides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Flux synthesis is plausible because CS-5 shows LiH/LiD flux can form complex hydrides at 500–530 °C and 100–150 bar H₂/D₂. The flux could improve cation and hydride transport, but CS-5 also reports impurity phases, so phase selectivity is a significant concern. Direct transfer to Na₂AlBiH₆ is uncertain because CS-5 involves Ba/Mg/Os rather than Na/Al/Bi chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

High-pressure anvil synthesis is supported only thermodynamically/phenomenologically by TS-1 and TS-4, where high-pressure cells produce hydrides near similar metastability. It receives a low condition score because >5 GPa pressure and H₂/internal hydride handling are severe. It is a fallback route for metastable trapping rather than the preferred synthesis, because insufficient evidence from TS supports Na–Al–Bi hydride chemistry specifically.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target likely has one amorphization trigger from large cation size/chemistry mismatch among Na, Al, and Bi [Reasonable Inference]. The described formula unit is small, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not activated. Mixed polyhedral sharing is common in analogues such as SS-1 and CS-1, but the target description does not clearly establish simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so that check is treated as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂AlBiH₆ is likely a **borderline metastable but accessible** hydride rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS-5 and SS-1, which show that Na-containing complex hydrides can be made by mechanochemistry plus moderate-pressure H₂/D₂ annealing, and from TS-2/TS-3 showing hydrides at similar hull energies can be synthesized under H₂ pressure. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high hydrogen chemical potential while using reactive hydride precursors, especially NaAlH₄/NaH. The most critical limiting factor is Bi incorporation into a hydride framework, since no CS/SS/TS analogue directly validates Na–Al–Bi–H bonding. Phase competition with NaAlH₄-derived phases, metallic Bi, NaH, and possible Na–Bi intermetallics is likely the main experimental obstacle.
