### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

CaAgCl3 is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. CS analogues include several stable chloride phases, including Na2PdCl4, Na3InCl6, Na2ZnBr4, and KCuCl3 with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-2–CS-5). SS and TS analogues also contain many E_hull = 0.0 phases, especially BaZnCl4 and SrZnCl4 (SS-2/SS-3; TS-1/TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that 0 K convex-hull stability does not guarantee a wide finite-temperature synthesis window or absence of polymorphs.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 500–575 °C, supported by BaZnCl4/SrZnCl4 sealed chloride reactions at 550 °C (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). The main deductions are for vacuum/sealed ampoule handling and volatile/hygroscopic chloride control, consistent with evacuated ampoules in SS-2/SS-3 and CS-2. No high pressure or >1000 °C heating is needed. Uncertainty remains about whether CaAgCl3 forms cleanly before AgCl volatilization or segregation becomes significant.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary chlorides are relatively reactive compared with refractory oxides, and direct binary-chloride reactions give phase-pure BaZnCl4/SrZnCl4 (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). The Ca–Ag chloride target is structurally different from zircon-like Ba/Sr zinc chlorides, so structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable. Partial melting or high ionic mobility in chlorides should improve kinetics [Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is whether the Ag–Ag bonded trigonal-pyramidal motif requires slow ordering not captured by the analogues.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires more procedural control than Route 1 because it involves melting, homogenization, and slow cooling or Bridgman growth. This is supported by Na2PdCl4 melt growth in evacuated quartz ampoules with 1 °C/h cooling (CS-2), Na3InCl6 Bridgman growth (CS-3), and BaZnCl4 Bridgman synthesis (SS-1; TS-3). Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, so thermal severity is moderate. The uncertainty is ampoule compatibility with chloride melts near 800–850 °C.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt growth has excellent mass transport, as shown by CS-2 and CS-3 chloride melt syntheses. Controlled slow cooling can enable large crystals and ordering, as in CS-2 and SS-1/TS-3. However, SS-1/TS-3 show that BaZnCl4 polymorph selection depends on maximum temperature and cooling rate, indicating possible phase-selection sensitivity. CaAgCl3 may similarly require a narrow cooling window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is mild in temperature and pressure, analogous to KCuCl3 slow evaporation from concentrated HCl (CS-5) and SrBr2 evaporation from alcohol under dry air (SS-4). Its only severity penalty is chemical handling, because concentrated HCl is corrosive/toxic and silver chloride chemistry is light-sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. No sealed high-temperature ampoule is required. The uncertainty is that mild conditions may not yield the anhydrous extended solid.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization bypasses solid-state diffusion, which is why KCuCl3 can crystallize by slow evaporation (CS-5). However, the Ca–Ag chloride target may instead form hydrated calcium chloride salts, AgCl precipitate, or molecular chloroargentates [Reasonable Inference]. SS-4 warns that evaporation routes can produce unstable or hydrated small crystals in halide systems. Thus, kinetics are fast, but structural selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small, ordered ternary chloride framework, and the described polyhedral network does not include simultaneous corner, edge, and face sharing. CS-3 shows that more complex chloride frameworks with face-sharing can exist, but that mixed connectivity is not stated for CaAgCl3. The Ca/Ag cation size mismatch is not extreme [Reasonable Inference], so glass/amorphous trapping is not expected to dominate. Remaining uncertainty is that layered halides can show stacking disorder even when they do not amorphize.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaAgCl3 is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from SS/TS binary-chloride sealed-ampoule syntheses, where BaZnCl4 and SrZnCl4 are obtained phase-pure at ~550 °C from simple chlorides (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). CS data also support chloride melt growth and solution growth as possible alternatives, especially Na2PdCl4 melt growth (CS-2) and KCuCl3 evaporation (CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of anhydrous binary chlorides in sealed ampoules, which preserves chloride stoichiometry and improves mobility. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving AgCl, including light sensitivity, possible volatility/segregation, and competition with simple CaCl2/AgCl or reduced Ag metal.
