### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.031 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic category [Target]. This is consistent with nearby Cr–Se analogues: Cr₅Se₈ is calculated on the hull [CS-1, SS-1], Cr₃Se₄ is nearly stable at 0.0051 eV/atom [CS-2, SS-4, TS-5], and CrSe₂ is only 0.0154 eV/atom above the ground state [CS-3, TS-3]. The uncertainty is polymorphism: a low E_hull supports synthesis of some Cr₂Se₃ phase, but not necessarily the trigonal P-6m2 omega-like polymorph [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated-tube synthesis, supported by direct element reactions in evacuated quartz/silica ampoules [TS-2, TS-4]. The severity is moderate because vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and extended annealing are needed [TS-2, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact temperatures for Cr₂Se₃ itself; CS-2 only states direct reaction of high-purity elements without temperature/time details.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The main kinetic limitation is Cr metal reactivity; direct solid-state reaction from elements is reported [CS-2, TS-5], but related evacuated-ampoule syntheses require several weeks [TS-2]. The target’s P-6m2 layered polymorph differs from the monoclinic C2/m phases commonly present among analogues [CS-2, SS-4, TS-5], so structural reconstruction is nontrivial. The score is therefore modest despite simple precursors.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The flux route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, with CrSe₂ grown at 780 °C [CS-3, TS-3]. However, it requires vacuum sealing, volatile Se-containing reagents, long slow cooling, and post-growth flux removal [CS-3, TS-3]. These factors make the route experimentally moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux improves transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, and LiCl/KCl flux growth gave phase-pure CrSe₂ crystals [CS-3, TS-3]. The structural match is strong because CrSe₂ is omega-like, layered, and built from CrSe₆ octahedra [CS-3, TS-3], while the target is also omega-like and 2D [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is composition control because Cr₂Se₃ lies between multiple stable or near-stable Cr–Se stoichiometries [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

High-pressure synthesis is the most severe route because analogues require 65–89 kbar and 1200 °C [CS-1, CS-5, SS-1]. The need for a tetrahedral/multi-anvil press, BN crucible, graphite heater, quench, and possible CS₂ extraction increases complexity [CS-1, CS-5, SS-1]. Insufficient evidence from TS for high-pressure synthesis of the exact target composition, so the route is plausible but not directly validated.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High temperature and pressure can accelerate reaction and stabilize dense/metastable phases [CS-1, CS-5, SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Cr₅Se₈ shares CrSe₆ octahedral connectivity and similar tilt angles with the target [CS-1, SS-1], which supports some structural transferability. However, the stoichiometry and symmetry differ, so pressure may instead favor Cr₅Se₈-like products [CS-1, SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Thin-film deposition avoids sealed ampoules and high mechanical pressure [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires high vacuum/UHV and selenium handling, so the condition score is not maximal [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS directly supports this route.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Vapor deposition could bypass bulk diffusion and kinetically stabilize a 2D metastable polymorph [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is crystallization: no provided analogue reports direct P-6m2 Cr₂Se₃ film growth, and competing Cr–Se stoichiometries are well represented [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3]. Composition control under Se overpressure is likely the limiting kinetic/process issue [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size-disorder amorphization is not expected [Target]. No evidence from the target description or analogues indicates a very large unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell. The mixed face/edge/corner-sharing octahedral network triggers one amorphization/structural-frustration flag [Target], giving a low–medium risk score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cr₂Se₃ with E_hull = 0.031 eV/atom is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unambiguously established ground-state phase [Target]. The analogue set strongly supports the general accessibility of Cr–Se octahedral chalcogenides, including Cr₅Se₈, Cr₃Se₄, and omega-like CrSe₂ [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-4, TS-3, TS-5]. The most important enabling factor is the close structural relationship to flux-grown omega-like CrSe₂, which suggests that a salt-flux route can support layered CrSe₆ frameworks [CS-3, TS-3]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition among multiple Cr–Se stoichiometries and polymorphs, especially CrSe₂, Cr₃Se₄, and Cr₅Se₈ [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful control of Se chemical potential and crystallization pathway.
