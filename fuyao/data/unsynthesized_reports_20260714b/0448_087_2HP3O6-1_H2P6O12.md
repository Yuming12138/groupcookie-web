### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin by the required rubric [Target]. Several chemically or thermodynamically related oxyanion materials also have E_hull = 0, including Al(OH)SO4 [CS1], MnH2(SO4)2 [CS2], SnPO3F [SS3/TS1], CsMnHP3O10 [TS3], and other TS analogues [TS2–TS5]. This supports the target being a thermodynamic ground-state candidate rather than a high-energy metastable phase [Target, TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for metal-free P–O–H phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled Ar/vacuum handling, volatile phosphorus oxide chemistry, likely multistep processing, and moisture/air sensitivity, all consistent with the P4O8 synthesis requiring Ar, sublimation, extraction, and vacuum treatment [SS2]. Its kinetics are moderate because P4O10/red P are chemically reactive, but SS2 reports low yield and demanding purification, indicating slow or competitive phase formation [SS2]. Structural reconstruction from molecular/solid phosphorus oxides into HPO4/PO ribbons is not directly demonstrated in SS2, so this route remains challenging [SS2, Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for this P-only sealed-ampoule route, so CS is neutral for this factor [insufficient evidence from CS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has mild severity because TS3 demonstrates hydrothermal phosphate crystallization at 250 °C for 48 h with slow cooling, and TS4 shows similar hydrothermal oxyanion synthesis at ~500 K over days [TS3, TS4]. Its kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal media provide solution transport and high precursor activity, avoiding the solid-state diffusion barriers observed in long annealing syntheses such as SS5 [TS3, SS5]. The main uncertainty is that TS3 contains Cs/Mn cations, whereas the target is metal-free; therefore, the transfer to pure P–O–H condensation chemistry is partly inferred [TS3, Reasonable Inference]. CS1 shows hydrothermal oxyanion phases can need harsh conditions, but that sulfate analogue required ~1 GPa and is less compositionally relevant than TS3 [CS1, TS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has very mild severity because CS3 used 80 °C aqueous reaction with H3PO2, and SS4 used room-temperature aqueous crystallization over days [CS3, SS4]. Its precursor activity and diffusion are high because all phosphorus sources are molecular/solution-phase reagents [CS3, SS4]. However, CS3 explicitly reports variations in cell parameters, water content, and crystallinity, so phase control and hydrate avoidance lower the kinetics/phase-selection score [CS3]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The analogues support synthesis best through solution or hydrothermal phosphate chemistry: TS3 demonstrates hydrogen phosphate framework crystallization from phosphoric acid at 250 °C, and CS3/SS4 show low-temperature P–O–H or hypophosphite/phosphorus-oxo crystallization routes [TS3, CS3, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the high solution mobility and tunable water activity/acid condensation chemistry in hydrothermal phosphoric acid media [TS3, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among competing hydrated phosphates, condensed polyphosphates, and reduced phosphorus oxides, especially because no reference exactly matches the metal-free HPO4(PO)2 composition [CS3, SS2, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears highly synthesizable, but likely requires careful control of P oxidation state and water activity rather than extreme temperature or pressure [TS3, CS3, Reasonable Inference].
