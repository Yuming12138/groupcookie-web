### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0230 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability scoring bracket of the rubric. The exact-composition GaTeCl analogue in CS1/SS1 is on the hull at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing that Ga–Te–Cl chemistry is intrinsically stable. TS analogues with similar low positive hull energies are experimentally accessible, e.g., TS1 at 0.0217 eV/atom, TS2 at 0.0316 eV/atom, TS4 at 0.0354 eV/atom, and TS5 at 0.0132 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: CS1/SS1 validates Pnnm GaTeCl, whereas the target is Pca2₁.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is only moderately severe because the closest analogue CS1/SS1 uses 400 °C, 7 days, and evacuated glass ampoules, with no high pressure or extreme temperature. Deductions are mainly for vacuum/sealed handling, volatile GaCl₃, and hygroscopic/air-sensitive precursor handling, all supported by CS1/SS1. There is insufficient evidence from TS for additional severity specific to GaTeCl, so TS is neutral for this route. The main uncertainty is whether a modified cooling/annealing schedule is required to isolate the Pca2₁ polymorph rather than Pnnm.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because CS1/SS1 demonstrates direct formation of GaTeCl from GaCl₃ and Ga₂Te₃ at only 400 °C. The halide precursor likely enhances mass transport [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the successful low-temperature anneal in CS1/SS1. Structural reconstruction is modest because the product local motif in CS1/SS1—GaTe₃Cl tetrahedra in 2D sheets—is essentially the same as the target, but the exact polymorph differs. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact kinetic pathway is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has higher severity than Route 1 because Bridgman/melt growth adds gradient control and a multi-step growth protocol, although CS3/SS4 shows related InTeBr can be grown from melt at only 520 °C. The sealed ampoule and volatile halide issues remain because GaCl₃ is used in CS1/SS1 and the target contains chlorine. TS1/TS5 support melt/fusion in chalcogenides but often at much higher temperatures; those high temperatures are not adopted for GaTeCl due to chloride volatility [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether GaTeCl melts congruently enough for controlled Bridgman growth.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt/Bridgman kinetics are favorable because liquid/near-liquid transport should reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and CS3/SS4 validates melt growth for the related layered InTeBr compound. However, the target’s chloride volatility and possible incongruent behavior may narrow the usable temperature window, and CS3/SS4 specifically notes InTeBr has incongruent melting behavior. Structural formation is plausible because the InTeBr analogue has similar tetrahedral group-13 tellurohalide sheets, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS demonstrates Pca2₁ GaTeCl growth from melt.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT is conditionally plausible but more severe due to temperature gradients, sealed-vacuum operation, and chloride transport chemistry. CS2 uses 900 → 700 °C CVT for RhTeCl under chlorine-related transport, and TS2 uses AlCl₃ CVT under high vacuum for MnIn₂Se₄. For GaTeCl, temperatures should likely be lowered because CS1/SS1 already forms GaTeCl at 400 °C and GaCl₃ is volatile [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether a stable transport window exists without decomposing or overchlorinating GaTeCl.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are strong because vapor transport minimizes solid-state diffusion, supported generally by CVT success in CS2 and TS2. Precursor vapor availability is good for chloride-mediated transport because GaCl₃/AlCl₃ are volatile halides [Reasonable Inference, with GaCl₃ use in CS1/SS1 and AlCl₃ use in TS2]. The structural uncertainty is significant: CS2’s RhTeCl has octahedral RhTe₃Cl₃ coordination rather than the target GaTe₃Cl tetrahedral sheets, while SS1 supports the target-like sheet but not CVT. Thus F3 is high but not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct elemental/binary fusion has moderate-to-high severity because TS1/TS3/TS5 and CS4 commonly use evacuated ampoules and high-temperature schedules for chalcogenides. For GaTeCl, direct elemental chlorination is less attractive because chlorine activity and GaCl₃ volatility must be controlled [Reasonable Inference], and CS/SS provide no direct elemental synthesis of GaTeCl. The score is therefore limited by hazardous/volatile precursors, controlled atmosphere, and probable staging. TS supports the general route type but not the halide-specific details.

#### F3 — Route 4

Reaction kinetics are the weakest among the routes because elements must reconstruct into a layered GaTeCl framework, unlike Route 1 where CS1/SS1 uses preorganized Ga₂Te₃ plus reactive GaCl₃. TS3 shows that direct solid-state chalcogenide formation may require very long annealing, and TS1 reports a second phase impurity, indicating possible phase-control limitations. Elemental Ga and Te are not refractory [Reasonable Inference], so diffusion is not extremely poor, but chlorine/halide control dominates the difficulty. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct elemental GaTeCl formation is treated as a major uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ga, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered. The structure description indicates corner-sharing GaTe₃Cl tetrahedra and 2D sheets, not mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity, consistent with the simpler connectivity in CS1/SS1. The unit-cell atom count for the target Pca2₁ setting is not explicitly given, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom amorphization risk; neutral interpretation favors low risk because the formula and local motif are simple. Remaining uncertainty is that polymorph disorder or stacking faults in layered materials could occur even without true amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target GaTeCl Pca2₁ phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the ground-state polymorph, because its E_hull is 0.023 eV/atom while the Pnnm GaTeCl analogue in CS1/SS1 is E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. The CS and SS evidence are very strong for synthesizing GaTeCl chemistry and the GaTe₃Cl layered tetrahedral motif, since the exact composition is made at 400 °C from GaCl₃ and Ga₂Te₃ in evacuated ampoules. TS evidence supports that low-positive-hull chalcogenides can be experimentally accessible, but it gives insufficient evidence for the exact Ga–Te–Cl polymorph. The most critical enabling factor is the highly reactive GaCl₃/Ga₂Te₃ precursor pair demonstrated in CS1/SS1. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the known synthesis yields Pnnm GaTeCl, while the desired target is Pca2₁.
