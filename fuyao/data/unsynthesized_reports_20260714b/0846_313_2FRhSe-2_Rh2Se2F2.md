### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0030 eV/atom, which is essentially on the convex hull and falls in the 90–100 scoring range by the provided rubric. TS contains a close thermodynamic analogue, Pd(Se₃Br)₂, with E_hull ≈0.0035 eV/atom that was experimentally synthesized hydrothermally, supporting the idea that a near-hull mixed chalcogen-halogen phase can be accessible. SS also includes multiple E_hull = 0 layered Pt selenide analogues, but SS does not directly address Rh–F chemistry. CS provides mixed-anion synthesis precedent but its listed E_hull values are generally higher, so CS is less decisive for thermodynamic stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits the sealed-tube, high-temperature, slow-cooling approach from SS/TS Pt/Tl/Hg selenides and from CS CoOBr sealed-tube synthesis. The condition severity is moderate because the proposed temperatures are below 1000 °C, but sealed vacuum/Ar containment and volatile/toxic Se/F species lower F2. Kinetics are the main concern: Rh metal is refractory, and SS/TS analogues often require long anneals, regrinding, or slow cooling, so F3 is only moderate. The structural match to layered edge-sharing selenide octahedra from SS/TS improves plausibility, but CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for bulk RhSeF ordering.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 scores best because CS hydrothermal oxyhalides form at 210–250 °C and TS Pd–Se–Br forms hydrothermally at ~180 °C for 5 days, indicating mild conditions for mixed-anion crystallization. F2 remains high because no extreme temperature, vacuum, or high pressure is needed, although toxic/corrosive fluoride and SeO₂ handling impose penalties. F3 is strong because solution chemistry avoids slow solid-state diffusion and uses more reactive dissolved Rh/Se/F species. The uncertainty is redox and speciation: fluoride can complex Rh³⁺, while Se must be reduced enough for Rh–Se bonding without precipitating elemental Rh or Se.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported indirectly by CS CVD/CVT methods and by SS/TS layered PtSe₂ heat-treatment behavior, but the CS vapor-route analogues explicitly report phase-control and orientation reproducibility difficulties. F2 is lower due to vacuum/controlled atmosphere, multiple processing steps, volatile Se/F sources, and toxic fluorinating reagents. F3 is moderate because Rh, Se, and F sources can be supplied in thin-film tools, but crystallization and stoichiometry are likely narrow. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that a RhSeF thin film will crystallize without losing Se or over-fluorinating Rh.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is motivated by SS/TS PtSe₂ sonochemical synthesis using H₂PtCl₆, Se, and Na₂SO₃, followed by 500 °C annealing. Its F2 is favorable because the main chemistry occurs at low temperature and ambient pressure, with only a modest annealing step. F3 benefits from nanoscale precursor reactivity and short diffusion lengths, but the structural reconstruction score is limited because F incorporation into an ordered RhSeF lattice is not demonstrated by the analogues. CS provides general hydrothermal/solution mixed-anion support, but insufficient direct evidence for sonochemical Rh fluoroselenide formation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered; this conclusion comes from the target composition and is not contradicted by CS/SS/TS. The P3m1 RhSeF formula unit is small, and SS/TS include crystalline layered edge-sharing octahedral analogues, suggesting low amorphization risk. The connectivity is described as edge-sharing RhSe₃F₃ octahedra, not a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network. Remaining uncertainty is mostly synthetic: low-temperature solution routes could yield amorphous Rh–Se–F precursors unless annealing/crystallization is well controlled.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RhSeF is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0030 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from TS and SS: layered edge-sharing noble-metal selenides are experimentally accessible, and TS includes a hydrothermally synthesized near-hull Pd–Se–Br phase. CS supports mixed-anion halide frameworks and hydrothermal/sealed-tube conditions, but CS analogues are mostly oxyhalides rather than fluoroselenides, so chemical transfer is incomplete. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic driving force against decomposition combined with a simple 2D edge-sharing octahedral structure. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Se redox/volatility and F activity without forming Rh metal, Rh₂Se₃, RhF₃, or soluble Rh–fluoride complexes.
