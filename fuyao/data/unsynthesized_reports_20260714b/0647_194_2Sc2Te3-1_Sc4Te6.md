### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0280 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket, so it is plausibly synthesizable as a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase [Target]. Exact Sc₂Te₃ in the orthorhombic Fddd form is extremely close to the hull at E_hull ≈ 0.0012 eV/atom (CS1/SS1), showing strong chemical stability of the Sc₂Te₃ composition. Thermodynamic analogues in TS mostly have low E_hull values around 0.016–0.025 eV/atom or stable ground-state entries, supporting that telluride phases in this energy range are experimentally accessible (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: low hull energy does not guarantee isolation of the trigonal P-6m2 polymorph over the known orthorhombic form (CS1/SS1).

#### F2 — Condition Severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses 800–900 °C sealed evacuated ampoules, long annealing, and controlled cooling (CS1/SS1; SS2/SS4), but avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures. Route 2 is more severe because CS2 requires arc melting, Ta containers, 1573 K annealing, Ar/vacuum handling, and 200 h duration. Route 3 has moderate severity: CVT occurs at 950 → 800 °C for 3 d, but requires sealed ampoules, Ar glovebox/high vacuum, and halide transport-agent control (CS5/SS5). Route 4 is similar in severity to Route 1/3 but uses flux/mineralizer and long 900 °C holds in sealed ampoules (SS3); Route 5 is most severe due to up to 100 kbar pressure and 1200 °C temperature (CS4/TS4).

#### F3 — Reaction Kinetics

Route 1 has limited kinetics because direct reaction of dense Sc metal with Te requires long annealing, and the known Sc₂Te₃ product is a different orthorhombic polymorph (CS1/SS1). Route 2 improves homogenization by arc melting and high-temperature annealing, but the CS2 process still needs 200 h and targets ScTe rather than Sc₂Te₃. Route 3 is kinetically attractive because CVT produced millimeter-scale Er₂Te₃ crystals in only 3 d, suggesting vapor transport can bypass slow solid diffusion (CS5/SS5), although structural transfer to Sc₂Te₃ is uncertain. Route 4 is also kinetically favorable because RbBr flux and iodine mineralizer precedents improve transport in Sc tellurides (SS3; SS2), but SS3 explicitly indicates unexpected phase formation and failed direct synthesis attempts, limiting the structural-reconstruction score.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The target is described as a relatively simple P-6m2 Sc₂Te₃ sheet rather than a large complex cell, so the >40 atom-cell criterion is not triggered [Target]. The mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing ScTe₆ octahedral network does trigger the polyhedral-connectivity criterion [Target], and similar mixed connectivity appears in structurally related Sc tellurides (SS3) and ScTe (CS2). The resulting amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Sc₂Te₃ trigonal P-6m2 phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the established ground-state form, because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom [Target] while an orthorhombic Sc₂Te₃ polymorph is known very near the hull and experimentally obtained (CS1/SS1). The chemical evidence strongly supports formation of Sc₂Te₃ composition (CS1/SS1), and structural analogues support Sc–Te octahedral frameworks with comparable mixed sharing and large tilt angles (SS3; SS2/SS4). The most critical enabling factor is controlled sealed-ampoule vapor/flux transport, which can maintain Te chemical potential and improve crystal growth kinetics (CS5/SS5; SS3). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity against orthorhombic Sc₂Te₃ and other Sc–Te phases, as side-product formation is already reported for Sc₂Te₃ (CS1/SS1). TS data supports general low-energy telluride accessibility but gives insufficient evidence for the specific trigonal Sc₂Te₃ polymorph.
