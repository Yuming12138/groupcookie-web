### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0150 eV/atom, which falls in the highest scoring ≤0.05 eV/atom range [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable metastability are experimentally accessible, including AgSe₃I-like material at 0.0161 eV/atom (TS1), WCl₆Se₆I at 0.0158 eV/atom (TS2), and CoTe₂ at 0.0192 eV/atom (TS4). CS gives strong support because multiple chemically similar Te–metal–halide materials are reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1–CS4), while SS also includes stable or near-stable related halide chalcogenides (SS1–SS4). Remaining uncertainty is that a 15 meV/atom phase may still require careful chemical-potential control to suppress adjacent Co–Te and Co–Br phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is moderate because it uses only sealed ampoule annealing at 430–600 °C, comparable to Pd–Te–Br at 700 K (CS1/SS1), GaTeCl at 400 °C (CS4), and CoTe₂ at 600 °C (TS4). The main penalties are vacuum/reduced-pressure sealing, volatile bromide chemistry, and toxic/moisture-sensitive halide handling, which are consistent with sealed halide ampoule routes in CS1/SS1 and SS4. CS and TS both provide useful signals for this factor; SS supports the same sealed-tube Pd–Te–Br route through the shared CS1/SS1 entry. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is supported by CS/SS/TS.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Kinetics are moderately favorable because Pd, Te, and PdBr₂ form Te₃(PdBr)₄ at only 700 K within several days (CS1/SS1), indicating halide-assisted solid-state reactivity. CoTe₂ requires much longer annealing at 600 °C for one month (TS4), so Co–Te ordering may be slower than Pd–Te–Br formation. Structural reconstruction is not severe because CoTe₂ already has omega-derived Co–Te octahedral sheets (TS4), and RhTeCl has the same MTe₃X₃ octahedral sheet connectivity (CS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether CoBr₂ incorporation into the CoTe₂-like framework is kinetically facile [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
CVT is more severe because it uses sealed ampoules, thermal gradients, and reactive volatile halogen transport agents, as shown for RhTeCl under chlorine transport (CS3), Re–Te–Br with Br₂ in evacuated ampoules (SS4), and NbPdTe₅ with I₂ under vacuum (TS5). The temperature remains below 1000 °C in the proposed adaptation, so no high-temperature penalty is applied; RhTeCl’s 900 °C hot zone is still below the threshold (CS3). Toxicity/corrosion and over-transport risks are significant for Br₂/I₂ chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signals for CVT or halogen-assisted growth.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
CVT kinetics are favorable because vapor transport can bypass slow solid diffusion; RhTeCl crystals form in 72 h in a two-zone furnace (CS3), WCl₆Se₆I transports in one day (TS2), and NbPdTe₅ crystals grow by I₂-assisted CVT over 8 days (TS5). The structural match to RhTeCl is strong because RhTeCl is also an omega-derived MTe₃X₃ layered octahedral compound (CS3). The uncertainty is bromine chemical potential: too little Br may give Co–Te binaries, while too much may stabilize CoBr₂ or tellurium bromides [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 supports Br-containing ampoule chemistry but is structurally less directly transferable because its product is a cluster compound.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature, with analogues at 180–220 °C for 5 days to two weeks (SS2, SS5, TS1). The severity comes from sealed acidic autoclave operation, volatile/corrosive HBr, and multi-step handling rather than high temperature or high pressure; SS2 and TS1 both use quartz ampoules inside steel autoclaves. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Co/Te/Br sheet synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route-severity factor at 50. SS and TS provide useful evidence for hydrothermal halide chalcogenide synthesis.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Solution-mediated reaction should improve mass transport because Pd–Se–Br and Ag–Se/I products form at low temperature in hydrohalic acid media (SS2, TS1). However, the structurally similar hydrothermal products are mostly polychalcogenide ribbons or molecular/low-dimensional halide phases, not CoTeBr-type omega-derived sheets (SS2, SS5, TS1). Thus precursor activity and diffusion are favorable, but structural reconstruction/nucleation is poor. CS provides insufficient evidence for hydrothermal kinetics in this chemical family, so CS is neutral at 50 for this route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one cation species, a small CoTeBr formula, and a single reported edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers is activated [Target]. CS and SS analogues demonstrate crystallization of layered or low-dimensional metal–chalcogen–halide phases rather than amorphous products (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5). TS gives a useful low-risk structural signal through crystalline CoTe₂ omega-type sheets (TS4), though TS1 shows multiphase hydrothermal products rather than amorphization. Remaining uncertainty is local disorder from Br/Te site competition, but the target description has distinct Te and Br sites [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoTeBr is likely a slightly metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its energy above hull is only 0.015 eV/atom [Target], comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues near 0.016–0.021 eV/atom (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). The CS and SS analogues strongly support synthesis because Pd–Te–Br and Rh–Te–Cl compounds show that transition-metal tellurium halide networks with related coordination environments can crystallize under sealed-tube or CVT conditions (CS1/SS1, CS3). The most critical enabling factor is the structural relationship to CoTe₂ omega-derived sheets, which provides a plausible Co–Te parent lattice into which Br can be incorporated [Target, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is bromine chemical-potential control: Br loss or excess Br can favor Co–Te binaries, CoBr₂, or TeBrₓ-type side products [Reasonable Inference based on stoichiometry sensitivity in CS1/SS1 and halogen transport chemistry in SS4/TS5]. Overall, a dry sealed-ampoule solid-state route is the best first attempt, with CVT as the best route for larger single crystals.
