### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold in the required rubric. CS and SS analogues are generally more stable, including Ag₂HgSnSe₄ at 0 eV/atom and Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ near 0.005–0.009 eV/atom (CS-2/CS-4/CS-5; SS-1/SS-5). TS analogues are especially relevant because they cluster near 0.05 eV/atom and are still synthesized as stannite selenides, e.g., Cu₂CoSnSe₄ and Cu₂FeSiSe₄ (TS-1/TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports Ag₂ZnSnSe₄ synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum handling, volatile Se/Zn in sealed ampoules, multi-step melt/anneal processing, and Se toxicity; these issues are supported by evacuated quartz ampoule use and long anneals in CS-1, CS-5/SS-1, and TS-1. The temperature is not penalized above 1000 °C if kept near 850–950 °C, consistent with Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ solution-fusion at 1123 K and Ag₂In₂GeSe₆ at 1250 K (CS-4/CS-1). The uncertainty is whether the target needs a higher melting temperature or narrower annealing window than analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental precursors give modest kinetic activity, as the successful CS/SS/TS bulk routes often require high temperature and long anneals (CS-1, CS-5/SS-1, TS-1). Diffusion should be acceptable because the cations have relatively low melting points and ampoule syntheses likely involve liquid or semi-liquid transport at high temperature; solution-fusion success for Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ supports this (CS-4/SS-5). Structural reconstruction is manageable because the target is the same tetrahedral stannite family, but slow cation ordering is suggested by 250–500 h anneals in Ag selenide analogues (CS-1, CS-5/SS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest condition severity because Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ forms solvothermally at 250 °C for 24 h without a vacuum or high-pressure apparatus beyond a standard autoclave (CS-2/SS-3). Deductions are mainly for operational multi-step handling and hazardous reagents such as Se, ethylenediamine, and tin chloride, all consistent with the solvothermal reagent set in CS-2/SS-3. The main uncertainty is whether Ag-containing target chemistry will remain as tolerant as Cu₂ZnSnSe₄.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solvothermal kinetics are favorable because soluble salts and coordinating/reducing solvents provide high precursor activity, as shown by the one-pot Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ synthesis (CS-2/SS-3). Diffusion barriers are low in a liquid medium, and the stannite-like structure forms far below bulk melting temperatures in the analogue (CS-2/SS-3). The principal kinetic uncertainty is nucleation selectivity: Ag⁺ may form Ag₂Se or AgCl side phases depending on chloride and selenium activation chemistry. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized similarly to Route 1 for vacuum ampoule use, volatile components, multistep crystal growth, and Se toxicity; these are directly associated with the Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ solution-fusion method in CS-4/SS-5. The temperature is moderate for crystal growth, about 850 °C in the analogue, so no >1000 °C deduction is applied (CS-4/SS-5). The difficulty is higher than powder synthesis because temperature gradients and growth rates must be precisely controlled, as noted in CS-4/SS-5.

#### F3 — Route 3

Elemental precursor activity is moderate-low, but a melt/solution-fusion environment provides good ion transport, supported by successful Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ crystal growth at 1123 K (CS-4/SS-5). Structural formation is supported by exact stannite similarity to Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ (CS-4/SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is the unknown Ag₂ZnSnSe₄ liquidus/solidification behavior; CS/SS/TS do not provide its phase diagram.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 requires evacuated ampoules and volatile iodine/selenium handling, as in iodine-assisted TS syntheses (TS-2/TS-4). It avoids extreme temperature and pressure because TS-2/TS-4 use 600–950 °C and standard evacuated quartz ampoules. The added chemical hazard and possible iodide contamination make the severity comparable to bulk sealed-tube methods.

#### F3 — Route 4

Iodine improves reaction kinetics by transport, which TS-2/TS-4 explicitly cite as the reason for adding I₂. Diffusion should be strong because iodine transport can mobilize metal species, but the elemental starting mixture still needs cation ordering into the stannite lattice. Remaining uncertainty is significant for Ag chemistry because AgI formation is plausible and not addressed directly in CS/SS/TS. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a corner-sharing tetrahedral stannite network, and analogous stannite Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ and Ag₂HgSnSe₄ crystallize successfully rather than becoming glassy/amorphous (CS-4/CS-5; SS-1/SS-5). One amorphization risk is cation size/charge mismatch among Ag⁺, Zn²⁺, and Sn⁴⁺, inferred from standard tetrahedral ionic radii and supported qualitatively by different target bond lengths. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large unit cell or mixed edge/face connectivity; the provided target and analogues describe corner-sharing tetrahedra only.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag₂ZnSnSe₄ is likely a near-threshold metastable or weakly stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom; TS analogues near 0.05 eV/atom are nevertheless experimentally accessible stannite selenides (TS-1/TS-2). CS and SS evidence strongly support the structural chemistry: Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ and Ag₂HgSnSe₄ show that tetrahedral I₂–II–IV–Se₄ stannite/stannite-like frameworks are synthetically realistic (CS-4/CS-5; SS-1/SS-5). The most important enabling factor is the existence of low-temperature solvothermal Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ synthesis at 250 °C, which suggests a kinetically gentle route to the same framework (CS-2/SS-3). The most important limiting factor is phase purity and cation ordering, especially avoiding Ag₂Se, ZnSe, SnSe₂, and off-stoichiometric products; long anneals in CS-1 and CS-5/SS-1 show that Ag-containing selenides may require substantial equilibration. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, with solvothermal synthesis as the most attractive first attempt and sealed-ampoule annealing as a robust fallback.
