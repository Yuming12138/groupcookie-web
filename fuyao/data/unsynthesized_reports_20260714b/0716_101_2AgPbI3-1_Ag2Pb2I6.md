### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0120 eV/atom, which is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. Similar iodides close to the target also lie near the hull: TlPbI3 has E_hull = 0.01137 eV/atom (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1), TlCdI3 has E_hull = 0.01669 eV/atom (CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2), and AgTl2I3 has E_hull = 0.02721 eV/atom (CS-2). The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull energy does not identify whether the specific trigonal P-31m polymorph is the experimentally selected one; it only indicates a small thermodynamic driving force against decomposition.

#### F2 — Route 1, Sealed-ampoule melt growth

The melt route is moderately severe because TlPbI3 requires evacuated ampoules, controlled heating/cooling, Bridgman growth, and long annealing (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). The temperature itself is not extreme, since the closest Pb analogue uses 870 K ≈ 597 °C (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1), but sealed-tube iodide handling and Pb toxicity add practical deductions [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct AgPbI3 melt behavior, so phase selection remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1, Sealed-ampoule melt growth

Kinetically, molten iodides should react efficiently, and direct iodide melting successfully produces TlPbI3 (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). Structural transferability is good because the target and TlPbI3 both contain 2D Pb–iodide motifs and PbI6 octahedra (target; CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). The main uncertainty is Ag ordering: Ag in the target forms distorted AgAgI3 trigonal pyramids, unlike Tl’s low-coordinate environment in TlPbI3 (target; CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2, Hydrothermal HI

This route has the mildest severity because AgTl2I3 and CuTl2I3 were made in HI at only 160–175 °C in pressure vessels/autoclaves (CS-2 / CS-5). There is no reported need for vacuum or inert gas for these hydrothermal analogues (CS-2 / CS-5). Deductions remain for corrosive/volatile HI in a sealed autoclave and toxic Pb chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2, Hydrothermal HI

The kinetics are favorable because HI solution can mobilize AgI and related iodides, directly shown by AgTl2I3 synthesis from AgI + TlI in 25% HI (CS-2) and CuTl2I3 synthesis in 30% HI (CS-5). Solution growth avoids the solid-state diffusion bottleneck and can support slow crystallization into low-dimensional iodometallate structures (CS-2 / CS-5). The uncertainty is phase control: CS-2 reports twinning and low yield of untwinned AgTl2I3 crystals, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Pb–Ag–I hydrothermal crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 3, Low-temperature sealed solid-state annealing

Low-temperature halide reactions at 150–200 °C in sealed ampoules are supported for TlAlI4-type compounds (SS-4), and inert/dry handling is supported for TlMI3 iodides (CS-4 / TS-3). However, vacuum sealing, possible repeated grinding/annealing, and toxic Pb precursor handling reduce the F2 score [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that AgPbI3 specifically forms quantitatively by low-temperature annealing, so this route is less secure.

#### F3 — Route 3, Low-temperature sealed solid-state annealing

Halide lattices are softer than oxides, so diffusion is expected to be easier than in refractory oxide syntheses [Reasonable Inference], and SS-4 demonstrates low-temperature halide reactivity. Still, the target requires ordered Ag/Pb arrangement and 2D sheet formation, which may not proceed completely below the melt. Repeated annealing required for TlFeI3/TlMnI3-type materials (CS-4 / TS-3) suggests possible kinetic sluggishness and phase-purity challenges.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: a significant Ag/Pb coordination and radius mismatch inferred from Ag’s low-coordinate trigonal-pyramidal environment and Pb’s octahedral environment in the target structure. The cell/motif is small and the target description includes corner and edge sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS analogues such as TlPbI3, PbI2, and CdI2 crystallize as layered iodides (SS-1 / SS-3 / SS-5), reducing concern for glass formation, but direct AgPbI3 crystallization evidence is absent.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgPbI3 is likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0120 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized near-hull iodides such as TlPbI3 and TlCdI3 (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well at the level of chemistry and structure: PbI6 layered iodides are accessible by melt growth, and Ag-containing iodometallates are accessible from concentrated HI solution (CS-2 / CS-3 / CS-5; SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, polarizable iodide lattice, which allows low-temperature solution or melt crystallization rather than refractory solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-2 / CS-5 and CS-3]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity between AgI, PbI2, and possible alternative Ag–Pb–I polymorphs, because no reference directly reports AgPbI3 and AgTl2I3 already shows twinning/low untwinned crystal yield (CS-2). Overall, the compound appears quite synthesizable, with hydrothermal HI crystallization recommended first and sealed-ampoule melt growth as a robust backup.
