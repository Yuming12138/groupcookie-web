### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, which places it in the 90–100 score band by the provided rubric. TS analogues also show strong thermodynamic support: Na₂Ga₂GeS₆, Li₂EuGeS₄, Na₂Zn(GeS₃)₂, Na₂CdGe₂S₆, and ZnGa₂S₄ are all listed as hull-stable (TS1–TS5). SS data are also supportive because the closest Fdd2 analogue Na₂Ga₂GeS₆ is hull-stable and Li₂Ga₂GeS₆ is only 0.035 eV/atom above hull (SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee easy phase selection in a multication sulfide reaction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate-to-high temperatures but not extreme temperatures, matching SS1 at up to 950 °C and CS5 at 900 °C. The main severity penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multi-step grinding/sealing/cooling, and air-sensitive Eu handling, all consistent with sealed sulfide synthesis in SS1, CS1, CS5, and SS3/TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact EuZnGeS₄ conditions, so CS contributes general lanthanide sulfide handling evidence rather than target-specific conditions and is treated neutral-to-supportive for the specific temperature choice. Insufficient evidence from TS on exact precursor handling beyond analogous sealed/flux routes, so TS is neutral for detailed procedural severity except where TS2/TS3 report vacuum/sealed synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfide precursors are reasonably reactive because SS1, SS4, SS5, and TS3 successfully use sulfide/elemental precursors for Ge-containing chalcogenides. Diffusion should be acceptable at 850–950 °C because closely related sulfides form in this range (SS1, SS2, SS5), although repeated heating and controlled cooling in analogues indicate nontrivial kinetics. Structurally, Fdd2 analogues strongly support the framework type (SS1, SS2; TS1), but Eu/Zn substitution into that framework requires partial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition among EuS, ZnS, GeS₂-derived phases, and ternary Eu–Ge–S or Zn–Ge–S sulfides [Reasonable Inference; by-product concerns supported generally by SS4, SS5, TS2, TS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C temperatures because SS3/TS2 use 600–650 °C, which reduces thermal severity. It still requires vacuum sealing, sulfur-rich volatile flux, glovebox handling, slow cooling, and post-synthesis washing/separation, all reported for Li₂EuGeS₄ flux growth (SS3/TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS for polysulfide flux growth of Eu–Zn–Ge sulfides specifically, so CS is neutral for this route. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for alkali-free EuZnGeS₄ in this flux, so the route receives a phase-control penalty despite favorable transport.

#### F3 — Route 2

The flux route has the best kinetic score because molten Li₂S₉ enables Eu and Ge dissolution at 600–650 °C in Li₂EuGeS₄ synthesis (SS3/TS2). This supports high precursor activity and enhanced ion transport compared with dry powders. The structural penalty is moderate because the closest Eu-containing flux analogue is tetragonal Li₂EuGeS₄, not orthorhombic Fdd2 EuZnGeS₄ (SS3/TS2), while Fdd2 analogues exist but are alkali/Ga systems (SS1, SS2; TS1). The key uncertainty is whether Zn enters the desired framework or whether alkali-containing Eu–Ge–S phases dominate [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is conditionally plausible because CS2–CS5 show that sealed halogen-assisted lanthanide thio-compound synthesis is feasible at 900–1000 °C over about 7–10 days. It receives the same main severity penalties as other sealed-tube routes, plus halogen volatility/toxicity concerns supported by CS2–CS5. Insufficient evidence from SS for halide-assisted EuZnGeS₄ specifically, so SS is neutral for this route beyond general sealed chalcogenide handling. Insufficient evidence from TS for halide transport, so TS is neutral for route-specific halide feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 3

Halides should improve mass transport and crystal growth because CS2–CS4 used temperature gradients in sealed tubes to grow lanthanide thiohalide single crystals. However, the target is halide-free, and CS2–CS5 show that halides can become part of the final structure, which creates a strong competing-phase risk. The structural reconstruction score is therefore lower than for Routes 1–2 because the closest halide-assisted chemical analogues are not Fdd2 Eu–Zn–Ge sulfides. The uncertainty is whether trace iodine acts only as a mineralizer rather than stabilizing Eu/Zn/Ge iodide-sulfide by-products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination contrast: Eu is 8-coordinate with Eu–S bonds near 3.05–3.13 Å, while ZnS₄ and GeS₄ tetrahedra have much shorter Zn–S and Ge–S bonds in the target description. The target also has many inequivalent sites and mixed corner/edge sharing, which increases structural complexity and amorphization or glass-forming risk [Reasonable Inference]. Similar sulfide systems can require slow cooling, sealed ampoules, and long holds to crystallize well (SS1, SS2, CS5; TS3). The remaining uncertainty is the exact unit-cell multiplicity, but Fdd2 symmetry with 15 inequivalent sites strongly suggests a large cell [Reasonable Inference from target structure].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

EuZnGeS₄ is likely a **ground-state phase** because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: Fdd2 sulfide frameworks are directly represented by SS1/SS2, Zn–Ge–S tetrahedral frameworks are represented by SS5/TS3, and Eu–Ge–S chemistry is represented by SS3/TS2. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube or flux chalcogenide routes that maintain sulfur activity and allow slow crystallization, as shown in SS1–SS5 and TS1–TS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in a multication sulfide containing Eu, Zn, Ge, and volatile sulfur, because related systems report secondary phases, disorder, or difficult crystal growth (SS3, SS4, SS5; TS2, TS4). Overall, the target appears synthesizable but likely requires careful sulfur chemical potential and slow crystallization control rather than a simple open-atmosphere firing.
