### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the edge of the high-synthesizability range in the supplied scoring rubric [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues near this energy, such as Tl2CN2 with E_hull = 0.0536 eV/atom, are experimentally accessible by solution precipitation [TS1]. The closest chemical/structural analogue has a higher computed hull energy of 0.218 eV/atom yet is experimentally synthesized, showing that Tl hydroxocarbonates can be kinetically trapped or stabilized by solution chemistry [CS1, SS1]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is a different 0D polymorph from the 1D analogue, so polymorph competition is possible [CS1, SS1].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses only 80 °C evaporation in open air and standard wet-chemistry equipment, so condition severity is low [CS1, SS1]. Deductions are applied for multiple handling steps and toxic thallium precursors [CS1, SS1]. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Tl4CO5 evaporation route; TS is neutral for this route-specific severity comparison, score 50.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because the closest analogue forms from aqueous solution, eliminating long-range solid-state diffusion [CS1, SS1]. The main kinetic uncertainty is polymorph selection, because the target is described as 0D whereas the closest analogue is described as 1D ribbons [target structure, CS1, SS1]. The direct chemical match makes this the strongest practical route despite the reported reproducibility limitations [CS1, SS1].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Slow evaporation conditions are mild and ambient, as shown by Tl3CO3F and TlNO2 solution crystallizations [SS2, TS2]. The score is reduced for toxic thallium carbonate and likely recrystallization/washing/drying steps [SS2, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct slow-evaporation thallous carbonate synthesis of pure Tl4CO5; CS neutral score 50 for this route variant.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution transport should give fast local equilibration, and thallium carbonate species are demonstrably crystallizable from solution [SS2, TS2]. However, SS2 notes accidental formation and rapid decomposition of Tl3CO3F, indicating that carbonate-containing thallium phases may have narrow stability windows [SS2]. This makes the route plausible but less controlled than the direct Ba(OH)2/Tl2SO4 evaporation route [CS1, SS1].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Hydrothermal analogues use moderate temperature near 230 °C, standard Teflon-lined autoclaves, and no special redox atmosphere [CS2, SS3, SS4, TS5]. The only formal deduction is for toxic thallium carbonate [CS2, SS3, SS4]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that hydrothermal conditions produce Tl4CO5 specifically, so this route remains an extrapolation.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis improves dissolution–reprecipitation kinetics and has produced crystalline Tl oxoanion phases from Tl2CO3 [CS2, SS3, SS4]. The structural reconstruction score is lower because the analogues are arsenates or other oxoanion compounds, not carbonates/hydroxocarbonates [CS2, SS3, SS4]. The route is attractive for crystal growth but may shift equilibria toward denser Tl carbonate or oxide phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Solid-state Tl oxide analogues form at moderate 500–650 °C, so high-temperature penalties do not apply [CS3, CS5]. Deductions are taken for toxic Tl precursors, likely volatile Tl/carbonate loss control, and multi-step grinding/annealing or excess-removal procedures [CS5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a solid-state route to the target carbonate; neutral source scores 50 for this route-specific feasibility.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Kinetics are inferior to solution routes because solid-state formation of a low-dimensional hydroxocarbonate-like cluster requires reconstruction and retention of carbonate/hydroxide species **[Reasonable Inference]**. Tl carbonate/oxide precursors are more reactive than refractory oxides, and Tl oxide analogues form at moderate temperatures [CS3, CS5]. The main uncertainty is whether the target decomposes before sufficient diffusion and ordering occur **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains very different cation sizes and bonding motifs, Tl–O plus trigonal-planar carbonate C–O, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check **[Reasonable Inference]** from the target structure. The atom-count check is not triggered because four Tl4CO5 clusters correspond to 40 non-H atoms, not >40 [target structure]. The mixed-connectivity check is not triggered because the target is described as zero-dimensional clusters rather than an extended vertex/edge/face-sharing network [target structure]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl4CO5 at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a deeply metastable compound [target data]. The strongest support comes from the nearly identical Tl4CO5 / Tl4(OH)2CO3 analogue synthesized by open-air aqueous evaporation, although that analogue is described as 1D rather than the target’s 0D cluster structure [CS1, SS1]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible by solution methods, supporting practical synthesizability [TS1, TS2]. The key enabling factor is highly mobile aqueous Tl(I)/OH⁻/carbonate chemistry, which avoids refractory diffusion barriers [CS1, SS1]. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity among TlOH, Tl2CO3, hydrated/hydroxylated thallium carbonates, and possible polymorphs, as shown by incomplete reproducibility and minor-product behavior in the closest analogue [CS1, SS1].
