### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0290 eV/atom, within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range assigned 90–100, so a score of 94 is appropriate [Target]. Chemically similar RTeCl phases are on or very near the hull, including E_hull = 0 and 0.0026 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5), which supports feasibility of rare-earth/Sc-type telluride halides. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.022–0.045 eV/atom have reported syntheses (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5), supporting that a 29 meV/atom metastable phase can be isolable. Remaining uncertainty is whether ScTeI is a true equilibrium phase or kinetically trapped layered polymorph, since the target is above hull [Target].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because the closest CS route uses 1050 °C for 5 days in sealed Ta tubes with drybox handling and controlled heating/cooling (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The need for Ta/Nb sealing is also consistent with Sc iodide syntheses at 750–950 °C for weeks (SS-2). Iodide and tellurium volatility require a sealed system [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-1, SS-4, SS-5], and ScI₃/Sc/Te handling requires air/moisture control (CS-1/2/5; SS-2). Thus F2 = 45 reflects harsh but standard solid-state halide-chalcogenide conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are moderate-to-slow because Sc metal is a dense refractory precursor [Reasonable Inference], while the analogues require long high-temperature reactions: 5 days for RTeCl (CS-1/2/5) and 2–4 weeks for Sc/Y iodide compounds (SS-2). Halide chemistry can improve transport relative to oxide solid-state reactions, so the ion-diffusion score is not extremely low (SS-2; CS-1/2/5). Structural reconstruction is significant because binary ScI₃/Sc/Te precursors must form a ternary 2D ScTe₃I₃ octahedral sheet [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control issues reported for RTeCl dimorphs and impurity-prone analogues lower the kinetics score (CS-1/2/5).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C, because direct iodide formation can begin at low temperatures as in YI₃ staged heating at 373–503 K and HfI₄ at 250 °C (SS-1, SS-4), followed by a proposed 750–900 °C ternary anneal [Reasonable Inference from SS-2/SS-5]. However, it still requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile I₂, and moisture exclusion (SS-1, SS-4). The staged iodination plus high-temperature anneal makes it multi-step [Reasonable Inference based on SS-1/SS-4]. F2 = 55 reflects lower temperature but higher pressure/volatility hazards from I₂.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct elements have mixed kinetic advantages: I₂ is highly reactive in sealed tubes, as shown for YI₃ and HfI₄ (SS-1, SS-4), but Sc metal and Te still require solid-state interdiffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The target ternary phase may have to compete with binary ScI₃ and scandium tellurides, and no CS/SS/TS source confirms direct Sc + Te + I₂ formation of ScTeI. Because analogues show phase-control problems and unexpected products (CS-1/2/5; SS-5), the structural-reconstruction score is low. F3 = 45 captures the risk that direct reaction locks into binaries before the ternary layered phase forms.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses 800–900 °C, consistent with ErSeI-related synthesis around 800 °C (SS-3) and RbSc₅Te₈ flux growth at 900 °C for 14 days (SS-5), so no >1000 °C penalty is applied. It still requires evacuated sealed tubes, inert handling, volatile iodide/Te components, and flux or transport post-processing (SS-3, SS-5). Toxic/air-sensitive precursor deductions are justified by anhydrous ScI₃, Te, optional I₂, and hygroscopic halide fluxes [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-1/3/5]. F2 = 55 reflects moderate temperature but demanding sealed-tube chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux/transport conditions improve precursor activity and mass transport, as ErSeI-type chalcogenide iodides form via sealed-ampoule transport methods (SS-3) and Sc–Te octahedral compounds can crystallize from RbBr flux (SS-5). Ion diffusion is easier than in dry solid-state synthesis because the halide flux/transport medium can dissolve or mobilize species [Reasonable Inference from SS-3/SS-5]. However, SS-3 reports narrow product zones/difficulty for some halide variants, and SS-5 reports unexpected RbSc₅Te₈ formation instead of the intended phase, so composition/phase control remains a kinetic limitation. F3 = 70 makes Route 3 the best kinetic option.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small, simple ScTeI formula in trigonal P3m1 and consists of ordered 2D edge-sharing ScTe₃I₃ octahedra [Target]. There is only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity; only edge-sharing octahedra are specified [Target]. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct amorphization evidence for ScTeI, so the high F4 score is mainly based on structural simplicity and the existence of crystalline 2D iodide/chalcogenide iodide analogues (SS-1, SS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScTeI is likely a **slightly metastable but experimentally accessible** phase because its E_hull is only 0.029 eV/atom [Target], and thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies have been synthesized (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The CS data strongly support the general feasibility of rare-earth/Sc-type telluride halides via sealed high-temperature reactions, although the closest CS examples are chlorides rather than iodides and use larger lanthanides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The SS data are especially important because they show both 2D iodide octahedral sheets and Sc–Te octahedral compounds can be made under sealed-tube or flux conditions (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is controlling iodine activity in a sealed halide-rich environment, which promotes formation and transport of Sc–I species (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among ScI₃, scandium tellurides, Te-rich phases, and possible flux-derived alkali scandium tellurides, consistent with phase-control problems in RTeCl and RbSc₅Te₈ analogues (CS-1/2/5; SS-5).
