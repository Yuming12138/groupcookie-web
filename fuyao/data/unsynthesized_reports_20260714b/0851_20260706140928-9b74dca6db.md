### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E above hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is scored in the top thermodynamic bracket at 95/100. Thermodynamic analogues are also stable or nearly stable: LaH₃, PrAlH₆, NdAlH₆, and LaAlH₆ are on hull in TS-1 and TS-3 to TS-5, and La₂H₅ is only 0.00026 eV/atom above hull in TS-2. CS is also supportive because Eu₂H₃Br, TbH₂Br, and Eu₂H₃Cl are stable or near-stable hydride/anion analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic and chemical rather than thermodynamic, because SS/TS do not directly show Nd₂NH₃ synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed hydride handling, likely multistep precursor preparation, and air-sensitive Nd nitride/hydride chemistry; sealed Ta ampoules and inert handling are directly supported by CS-2 and CS-4. The 850–950 °C thermal window is moderate and follows Eu₂H₃Cl at 900 °C for 13 h and Tb hydride-bromide at 850 °C for 24 h (CS-2, CS-4). Kinetically, the close P-3m1 Eu₂H₃Cl/Br framework supports transferability (CS-1, CS-2), but NdN/NdHₓ interdiffusion and ordering of N/H sites require substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact solid-state route and are effectively neutral for Route 1 kinetics.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is severe because it uses reactive H₂/NH₃ or N₂/H₂ atmospheres and likely pressure equipment; CS-5 directly supports high-pressure H₂/D₂ autoclave hydride synthesis up to 10 MPa but not nitriding. Kinetics are uncertain because gas-phase H is mobile, but nitrogen incorporation and avoidance of binary NdN/NdHₓ are difficult [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 reports multiple hydride phases and phase-purity challenges in Eu–Pd–H, which lowers the route’s phase-control score. SS and TS give insufficient direct evidence for gas nitriding/hydrogenation of Nd₂NH₃ and are neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has the best condition score because SS-4/TS-4 show room-temperature mechanochemical synthesis of NdAlH₆-type rare-earth hydrides using NdCl₃ and NaAlH₄ under Ar/H₂, avoiding very high temperatures. It is still penalized for air-sensitive reagents, controlled atmosphere, and multistep milling/annealing/byproduct management, all consistent with SS-4/TS-4. Kinetically, mechanochemistry should improve precursor mixing and reduce diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], but SS-4/TS-4 warn that rare-earth hydride metathesis can produce low yields, NaCl/Al byproducts, and partial decomposition. CS provides supporting evidence for NaH/NaCl-containing rare-earth hydride-halide synthesis in sealed systems (CS-2), but the nitride-donor chemistry is extrapolated.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no mixed-cation size mismatch because Nd is the only cation [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is likely a small P-3m1 cell rather than a >40-atom complex cell [Reasonable Inference], but exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided by CS/SS/TS. The mixed corner/edge/face-sharing NNd₆, HNd₄, and HNd₆ network triggers one amorphization-risk criterion; similar mixed connectivity is present in CS-1/CS-2 and SS-1/SS-2. The resulting F4 = 78 reflects low–medium amorphization risk, with uncertainty mainly in mechanochemical Route 3, where salt-rich milling can yield poorly crystalline products [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nd₂NH₃ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also on or very near the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The CS evidence is strong for rare-earth hydride/anion frameworks, especially P-3m1 Eu₂H₃Cl/Br with closely related rare-earth coordination and H-site topology (CS-1, CS-2), but no source directly proves nitride-hydride Nd₂NH₃ synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature, highly activated rare-earth hydride chemistry via mechanochemistry, supported by NdAlH₆ synthesis from NdCl₃/NaAlH₄ under Ar/H₂ (SS-4, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of N³⁻ and H⁻ stoichiometry without forming binary NdN, NdHₓ, or salt-contaminated low-crystallinity products [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears feasible but experimentally nontrivial, with the best chance coming from mechanochemical metathesis plus careful low-temperature crystallization.
