### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is explicitly 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. Many analogues in CS and TS are also on the hull, including CS1–CS5 and TS1–TS5, which supports the general stability of related oxide/chalcogen/large-cation frameworks. The main uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee a kinetically accessible mixed-anion ordering, especially when Se²⁻ must coexist with O²⁻ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed solid-state

The solid-state route avoids >1000 °C, unlike some SS/TS germanate ceramic analogues that reach 1100–1330 °C or 1200 °C (SS1, SS2, SS5; TS4–TS5). It still requires sealed vacuum/Ar handling because the target contains reduced selenium, while the cited oxide ceramic routes in air provide insufficient evidence for Se²⁻ retention, neutral 50 (SS1, SS2, TS1). The severity score is lowered by air-sensitive/toxic SrSe/Se handling and the need for sealed-tube processing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed solid-state

GeO₂ is a common precursor in germanate syntheses (SS1–SS3; TS2–TS3), but as a refractory oxide it limits kinetics relative to soluble hydrothermal precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Solid-state formation requires reconstructing SrSe and GeO₂ into a monoclinic mixed-anion tetrahedral framework, so structural reconstruction is significant [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether SrSe + GeO₂ reacts cleanly before Se loss or formation of competing SrO/SrGeO₃/GeSe₂ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal / solvothermal

Hydrothermal analogues use moderate temperatures: ~500 K in CS1–CS3, 443 K in TS2, and 165 °C in SS3. The lower temperature reduces thermal severity, but selenide solutions require air-free/reducing conditions and may release toxic H₂Se upon acidification [Reasonable Inference]. HF is present in SS3 and TS2, but because HF is optional here, the score assumes a safer alkaline route first [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 hydrothermal / solvothermal

CS1–CS3 and TS2 demonstrate that solution-mediated synthesis can crystallize chemically or thermodynamically related oxides over 5–7 days, and SS3 shows GeO₂ can be mobilized solvothermally. This gives favorable kinetic scores for precursor activity and diffusion. The uncertainty is high because no cited hydrothermal example directly contains Sr²⁺, Ge⁴⁺, O²⁻, and Se²⁻ in the same phase; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS, neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3 sealed halide-flux growth

CS5 demonstrates flux growth with CsBr, Ar/vacuum handling, 700 °C, 7 days, and slow cooling, which maps well onto the proposed sealed halide-flux route. TS3–TS5 also support high-temperature solution/flux growth and controlled cooling. The severity remains moderate because the target requires sealed reduced-Se chemistry, which is not directly proven by CS5 because CS5 contains Se in an oxoselenate environment rather than Se²⁻; insufficient direct evidence from CS5 for Se²⁻, neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 sealed halide-flux growth

Flux growth receives strong kinetic credit because molten flux reduces diffusion limitations and supports slow assembly of ordered crystals, as shown in CS5 and TS3–TS5. The structural reconstruction penalty is moderate because the target’s ordered GeO₄/GeSeO₃ network must crystallize from a melt/flux rather than transform topotactically [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is avoiding halide incorporation or oxidation of Se²⁻ during flux growth [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 CVT / vapor-assisted recrystallization

CS4 directly supports use of evacuated sealed quartz tubes and 900 K-class CVT for an oxide-halide/chalcogen-containing crystal. The route avoids high pressure and can remain below 1000 °C, so condition severity is not extreme. However, volatile halogen/Se species and sealed vacuum handling impose moderate penalties, and CS4 provides insufficient direct evidence for transporting SrGe₂SeO₄ without decomposition, neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 4 CVT / vapor-assisted recrystallization

Vapor transport kinetics are uncertain because Sr, Ge, O, and Se species will likely have very different transport volatilities [Reasonable Inference]. CS4 shows CVT can produce single crystals, but that analogue contains different cations and anions, so composition transferability is limited. The low F3 reflects likely narrow composition control and the risk of forming binary halides/selenides/oxides instead of the quaternary target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Sr and Ge, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives corner-sharing Ge-centered tetrahedra and does not indicate extensive mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity, so C3 is not triggered from the target structural description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior of SrGe₂SeO₄ itself, neutral 50, so the F4 score remains an informed estimate.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrGe₂SeO₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the supplied target hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is moderately supportive: SS/TS strongly support formation of Ge–O tetrahedral germanate frameworks by ceramic or flux methods, CS supports hydrothermal/flux/sealed-tube methodologies, but none directly proves Sr–Ge–O–Se with reduced Se²⁻. The most critical enabling factor is the robust GeO₄ tetrahedral chemistry, which appears repeatedly in SS and TS germanate analogues. The most critical limiting factor is control of selenium chemical potential and oxidation state, since CS5 involves Se–O oxoselenate chemistry rather than selenide, and oxide ceramic analogues are mostly air-fired. Overall, the target is feasible but likely requires sealed, air-free, chalcogen-buffered synthesis rather than a simple open-air ceramic route.
