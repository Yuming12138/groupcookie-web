### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s energy above hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, which falls at the favorable boundary specified in the prompt. TS analogues with similar or higher energies above hull are experimentally synthesized, including TS4 at 0.0518 eV/atom and TS1/TS2/TS5 at ~0.067–0.093 eV/atom. This supports a high but not perfect F1 score because the target may be slightly metastable rather than a robust ground state. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for direct target thermodynamic stability beyond stable analogues, so CS/SS are treated as supportive but not decisive.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Direct arc-melting is experimentally supported by CS1/SS1 and TS1/TS2/TS5, and it avoids vacuum sealing and long multistep processing. The main severity penalties are high effective temperature and air-sensitive Ho [Reasonable Inference; rare-earth glovebox handling in CS5/SS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high pressure, so no high-pressure penalty is applied.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetics benefit from full or partial melting, as direct melting gives products in CS1/SS1 and TS1/TS2/TS5. However, Rh is refractory and the target cubic cluster framework differs from the closest Ho₂Rh₃Sn₅ orthorhombic analogues (SS2–SS5), so structural selection may be nontrivial. This gives moderate kinetic confidence.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The 1173 K sealed-ampoule method is directly supported for Ho₂Rh₃Sn₅ and related TR₂Rh₃Sn₅ phases (CS5/SS2; SS3–SS5). It avoids >1000 °C treatment but requires glovebox handling, vacuum degassing, sealing, and multistep processing. These controlled-atmosphere and sealed-system requirements drive the moderate F2 penalty.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The presence of liquid Sn at 900 °C should enhance reaction compared with a fully solid-state route [Reasonable Inference], and TR₂Rh₃Sn₅ analogues are formed after 3 days (CS5/SS2). Rh diffusion and the need to reconstruct into a different cubic cluster phase remain limiting. Thus, kinetics are slightly better than direct arc-melting for ordering, but not dramatically better.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Arc-melt plus anneal combines the severity of arc-melting with sealed evacuated tube annealing, both seen in CS3 and CS4. It is more operationally demanding than Route 1 because it includes multiple processing steps, vacuum sealing, and long heat treatments. No high-pressure penalty is applied because CS3/CS4 use evacuated tubes rather than high-pressure apparatus.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
This route is kinetically attractive for homogenization because CS3 and CS4 show that long annealing improves phase formation and purity in complex stannides. The downside is that long annealing may also allow decomposition into competing equilibrium Ho–Rh–Sn phases if the target is only marginally stable [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are therefore moderate.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Sn self-flux is supported by TS4 for PdSn₄, but direct ternary Ho–Rh–Sn evidence is insufficient from TS; therefore, the route is plausible but less proven. It requires sealing, slow cooling, and flux removal, and Ho handling remains air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference; CS5/SS2]. The severity is moderate-to-high due to these operational controls.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
A liquid Sn flux should improve transport and enable crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with TS4 showing Sn-rich stannide crystals under self-flux conditions. However, Rh dissolution in Sn and selective nucleation of the exact Ho₅Rh₆Sn-rich cubic phase are uncertain. The kinetic score is the highest because liquid flux helps diffusion, but phase selectivity remains the key uncertainty.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a complex cubic cluster/framework description with multiple coordination environments and likely a large unit cell, which triggers structural complexity concerns [Reasonable Inference from target description]. CS1/SS1 and CS5/SS2 show that complex rare-earth/Rh/Sn stannides do crystallize, so the risk is not prohibitive. The high F4 risk mainly reflects possible disorder, glassy trapping, or multiphase products during rapid solidification rather than impossibility.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely **borderline ground-state / low-energy metastable**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom from the prompt and TS analogues with comparable E_hull values are experimentally accessible (TS1–TS5). The CS and SS evidence is strong for the **Ho–Rh–Sn chemical system**, especially Ho₂Rh₃Sn₅ synthesized from elemental Ho/Rh/Sn (CS5/SS2), but the exact cubic F-43m cluster structure is not directly demonstrated in the references. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of high-temperature metallic routes—especially arc-melting and Sn-assisted annealing—that overcome Rh diffusion limitations (CS1/SS1, CS3, CS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in a Sn-rich, complex intermetallic system, particularly because Sn can be lost at high temperature [Reasonable Inference; excess/controlled processing in CS2 and sealed processing in CS5/SS2]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires iterative optimization and careful phase analysis.
