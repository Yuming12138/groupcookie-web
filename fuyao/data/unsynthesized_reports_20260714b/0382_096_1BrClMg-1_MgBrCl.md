### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0070 eV/atom is very close to the convex hull and falls in the highest stability scoring band. TS provides strong support because thermodynamically similar halides have low E_hull values, including Li₂MgBr₄ at 0.0146 eV/atom (TS1), BaZnCl₄ at 0.0019 eV/atom (TS2), SrBr₂ at 0.0012 eV/atom (TS3), and Sr₅(Br₄Cl)₂ at 0.0060 eV/atom (TS5). SS overlaps with several of these stable halides (SS1, SS4, SS5), reinforcing that low-energy halide phases are synthetically accessible. CS oxyhalides do not directly inform MgBrCl thermodynamics, so **insufficient evidence from CS; CS neutral score = 50**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule melt growth

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it requires inert/vacuum ampoule handling, as used for Li₂MgBr₄ in evacuated quartz under Ar (SS1/TS1) and for BaZnCl₄ in evacuated silica ampoules (SS5/TS2), but it does not require >1000 °C or high pressure. Kinetically, the melt route is favorable because SS1/TS1 directly shows Mg-containing bromide halides can be formed from molten anhydrous salts, and TS5 shows mixed Br/Cl halides can be produced by high-temperature treatment and controlled cooling. The remaining uncertainty is whether MgBrCl forms congruently or instead phase-separates into MgCl₂/MgBr₂, which is a [Reasonable Inference] based on the phase-region complexity in TS5. CS provides only indirect support through layered oxyhalide CVT and is therefore secondary for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: solid-state annealing

Route 2 is harsher kinetically than Route 1 because TS4 reports several-week tempering below the melting point for halogenozincates, indicating slow diffusion/order development in some anhydrous halide systems. TS5 supports annealing and slow cooling for mixed Sr–Br–Cl phases, but also shows that phase control depends on composition and thermal history. The main advantage is that the maximum temperature can be lower than full melt processing [Reasonable Inference], but the drawback is slower Br/Cl ordering and possibly incomplete homogenization. CS2 supports sealed-tube solid-state reaction at 320 °C for CoOBr, but because it is an oxyhalide, **CS signal is weak/neutral = 50** for MgBrCl solid-state kinetics.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

CS1 and CS4 directly show that 2D oxyhalides TiOCl and VOCl can be grown by vapor transport in a temperature gradient, including plate-like TiOCl crystals (CS1). However, no CS/SS/TS source directly reports MgBrCl or mixed Mg chloride/bromide CVT, so the route is speculative and composition control is a concern. SS/TS ampoule halide syntheses indicate that sealed high-temperature halide handling is feasible (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS2, TS4), but they do not prove congruent vapor transport of MgBrCl. Therefore, F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Mg²⁺, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure is a simple 2D MgBrCl sheet with edge-sharing MgBr₃Cl₃ octahedra, not a large multi-cation framework, and no evidence indicates a >40 atom unit cell. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is also not triggered because the target description specifies edge-sharing octahedra rather than coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing. Exact unit-cell size is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on C2 exact atom count; neutral source score = 50**, but structural simplicity supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgBrCl is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0070 eV/atom, comparable to stable/near-stable halide analogues in TS1–TS5. The strongest support comes from SS/TS halide ampoule and melt syntheses, especially Mg-containing Li₂MgBr₄ from LiBr/MgBr₂ (SS1/TS1) and mixed Br/Cl Sr halides from SrBr₂/SrCl₂ (TS5). The most critical enabling factor is use of anhydrous sealed-ampoule melt processing, which should overcome diffusion limitations and maintain halide chemical potentials, as demonstrated in SS1/TS1 and SS5/TS2. The most critical limiting factor is Br/Cl stoichiometry and ordering: TS5 shows mixed-halide systems can have multiple phase regions and require careful cooling/annealing. Overall, the target appears synthetically feasible, with the best probability via sealed-ampoule melt growth followed by slow cooling and low-temperature annealing.
