### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.017 eV/atom, which lies well below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold for highly synthesizable or metastably accessible compounds. TS strongly supports this assessment because TS1 has nearly the same hull energy, 0.0184 eV/atom, and TS2–TS5 are all low-hull phases that have reported syntheses. SS also supports feasibility because several structurally related layered/halide materials are near the hull and experimentally synthesized. CS is not very informative for TlF because the closest CS examples are mainly Pt/Bi oxides; insufficient evidence from CS, neutral source score 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because TS2 used slow evaporation of thallous fluoride in ethanol/water at ambient conditions, and SS3/TS4 used simple hot-solution cooling for a layered fluoride. The only major severity penalty is thallium toxicity, directly relevant because TS2 uses thallous fluoride. The remaining uncertainty is that CO₂ exclusion is recommended by **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly required by the successful TlF crystallization portion of TS2.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the route uses dissolved Tl⁺/F⁻ species rather than solid-state diffusion, consistent with TS2 and SS3/TS4 solution crystallizations. The main kinetic uncertainty is not diffusion but nucleation selectivity: TS2 shows an unintended carbonate-fluoride phase can appear in air, so controlling the desired TlF polymorph may be nontrivial. The score is therefore high but not perfect.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate solvothermal temperatures, supported by SS5 at 150 °C and TS1 at 503 K. It avoids extreme pressure but requires a sealed autoclave, slow cooling, filtration/washing, and toxic TlF, giving moderate severity. CS offers insufficient direct evidence for solvothermal TlF, although CS4 shows hydrothermal synthesis can grow crystals of heavy p-block oxides.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solvothermal kinetics should be good because SS5 demonstrates fluoride crystallization from methanol and TS1 demonstrates hydrothermal crystallization of Tl-containing compounds. Dissolution and recrystallization bypass slow cation diffusion. The uncertainty is whether solvothermal conditions stabilize the target monoclinic TlF sheet rather than a different TlF polymorph or hydrate/solvate.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because TS3 requires vacuum evaporation, low-temperature substrate control, and controlled heating. The route also involves toxic thallium fluoride and likely volatility control by **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS1/CS2 show deposition can access difficult phases, but those are oxide thin films and provide insufficient direct evidence for TlF.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor deposition may access metastable polymorphs, as TS3 shows for thallium halides under low-temperature deposition. However, composition control for TlF may be sensitive because fluorides can evaporate or decompose non-stoichiometrically **[Reasonable Inference]**. Crystallization may also be substrate-dependent and may require annealing, lowering the kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 requires sealed ampoules or inert/vacuum handling, supported by SS4/TS5 for thallium halide-related melt growth and SS1 for Tl₂O handling under Ar/vacuum. Although temperatures are not extreme, toxicity, volatility, and multistep sealing/melting/annealing create significant severity. The exact TlF melting/crystallization window is a **[Reasonable Inference]** and should be experimentally screened.

#### F3 — Route 4

Melt diffusion is fast, and SS4/TS5 show thallium halide-related crystals can be grown from stoichiometric melts. However, the target is a specific low-energy monoclinic 2D polymorph, and melt growth may favor the most stable bulk polymorph instead. Long annealing requirements in SS4/TS5 and SS1 suggest slow ordering or phase equilibration may be an issue.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, a small described crystallographic basis, and no stated complex mixed polyhedral network, so none of the amorphization triggers are clearly activated. SS1 and SS3 show related low-dimensional sheet materials can crystallize, and TS2/TS3 show thallium halides/fluorides can crystallize under mild or deposition conditions. CS provides insufficient evidence for TlF amorphization, so it is neutral as a source signal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target TlF phase is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strongly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.017 eV/atom. The best support comes from TS2, which directly involves thallous fluoride solution crystallization, and from SS3/TS4/SS5, which show mild solution or solvothermal routes can crystallize layered fluorides. The most critical enabling factor is the chemical simplicity of TlF and the ability to crystallize from solution, avoiding high-temperature diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and impurity control, especially carbonate incorporation shown by TS2 and possible formation of alternative TlF structures. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable if CO₂-free, low-temperature crystallization conditions are carefully controlled.
