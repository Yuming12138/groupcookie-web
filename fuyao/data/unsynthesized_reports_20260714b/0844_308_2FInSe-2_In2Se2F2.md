### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability metastability range of ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, including MnIn₂Se₄ at 0.0316 eV/atom and high-pressure InSe at 0.0367 eV/atom, have reported syntheses [TS-1, TS-2]. Structurally similar InSe phases are stable or nearly stable [SS-1, SS-2], supporting the In–Se framework. CS is less decisive because chemically similar oxyhalides span from stable Sn₂OF₂ to high-E_hull Cd oxyhalides [CS-1 to CS-5], so CS contributes mixed thermodynamic evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state

The severity score is limited mainly by vacuum sealing, volatile Se/F control, and toxic fluoride/selenide handling [SS-2, SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. The route is not high-pressure and does not require >1000 °C if adapted from sealed chalcogenide and fluoride analogues [SS-4, SS-5, CS-5]. Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from the close structural relationship to layered InSe, where In–Se bond lengths match the target well [SS-1, SS-2]. The main uncertainty is whether F can be incorporated into the layered In–Se framework without forming separate InF₃ or InSe phases; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for InSeF formation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT

The severity is moderate-to-high because CVT requires a sealed evacuated ampoule, transport agents, temperature gradients, and prior feed preparation [TS-1, TS-4]. CVT is supported for layered and thermodynamically similar indium chalcogenides [TS-1, TS-4], but composition control is a major concern because AlCl₃ or I₂ may exchange with F [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, vapor transport can grow high-quality crystals, but the target’s F content may not transport congruently. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a validated In–Se–F CVT agent.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solvothermal / fluoride-mineralizer

Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature and have precedent for oxyhalides at 210–250 °C [CS-2, CS-4, TS-3]. However, the target is an anhydrous selenide fluoride, whereas the analogues are oxide/hydroxyhalide phases [CS-2, CS-4, TS-3], so structural transferability is weak. Kinetics in solution may be favorable, but water and fluoride can drive hydrolysis or oxyfluoride formation [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore plausible for exploratory screening but less reliable than Route 1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film deposition + fluorination

Thin-film processing is plausible because the target and InSe analogues are 2D layered materials [SS-1, SS-2]. The severity arises from vacuum processing, reactive fluorination, and multi-step deposition/annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, fluorination of a preformed InSe film could bypass bulk diffusion, but excessive fluorination may convert the film to InF₃ or amorphize the layer [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct thin-film InSeF growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply [Target]. The described structure is simple, with one In, one Se, and one F site, and there is no indication of a >40 atom unit cell [Target]. The target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the structural glass-forming risk appears low [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact conventional cell size is not provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for hidden large-cell disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InSeF is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom [Target]. This metastability is still favorable, since thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.03–0.04 eV/atom have been synthesized [TS-1, TS-2, TS-5]. The strongest support comes from structurally similar layered InSe phases with very similar In–Se bonding [SS-1, SS-2], plus sealed-tube fluoride precedent from Sn₂OF₂ [CS-5]. The most important enabling factor is the simple single-cation In–Se–F chemistry and preservation of an InSe-like sheet motif [SS-1, SS-2]. The main limiting factor is fluorine chemical-potential control: too little F likely gives InSe, while too much F may yield InF₃ or related fluoride impurities [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful sealed-tube stoichiometry and phase-purity screening.
