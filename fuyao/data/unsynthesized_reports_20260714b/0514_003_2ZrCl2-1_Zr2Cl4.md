### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E above hull is 0.0290 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and therefore receives a high score. Direct trigonal ZrCl₂ is hull-stable in the reference set (CS-1, SS-1), showing that the ZrCl₂ composition itself is thermodynamically credible. Thermodynamic analogues with similar small positive hull energies are experimentally made, including ScCl at 0.021 eV/atom and YCl at 0.037 eV/atom (TS-2, TS-4). The uncertainty is that the target is a monoclinic polymorph, not the reported trigonal ground-state-like ZrCl₂, so it may require kinetic or polytype control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT / Vapor-Equilibration

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because direct ZrCl₂ CVT requires sealed Ta tubes, drybox handling, volatile ZrCl₄, and 4–10 week gradients at 600–750 °C (CS-1, SS-1). The route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, improving F2 relative to Sc/Y reduced chloride sintering routes (TS-2, TS-4). Kinetically, vapor transport is favorable because ZrCl₄ provides a volatile transport component and direct ZrCl₂ growth is known (CS-1). The main uncertainty is polytype control, since CS-1/SS-1 explicitly note multiple polytypes, twinning, intergrowths, and slow equilibration.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Reductive Halogenation

This route is penalized for controlled sealed/vacuum conditions, volatile ZrCl₄, air-sensitive chlorides, and likely multistep processing, consistent with reduced chloride syntheses in sealed Ta/Nb tubes (CS-1, TS-2, TS-4). Kinetics are less favorable because Zr metal is refractory and solid-state equilibration is slow, as analogous Sc/Y chloride syntheses require weeks to months (TS-2, TS-4). Halide vapor can partially assist transport, as indicated by ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ transport reactions (CS-1, SS-5). The uncertainty is whether a bulk equilibration route can selectively produce the monoclinic ZrCl₂ polytype rather than trigonal ZrCl₂, ZrCl₃, or cluster phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: AlCl₃ Flux

The flux route has mild temperature severity because ZrCl₃ analogues are made at 220–300 °C in aluminum halide melts (CS-2). It is still penalized for volatile/corrosive AlCl₃ and ZrCl₄, sealed ampoule handling, and moisture sensitivity (CS-2). Kinetically, melt-mediated transport should be strong because CS-2 describes homogeneous reduced-state solutions, but structural transferability is uncertain because the demonstrated product is ZrCl₃ rather than ZrCl₂. There is insufficient evidence from SS and TS that AlCl₃ flux stabilizes the target monoclinic layered ZrCl₂ phase, so the route remains exploratory.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: H₂-Mediated Disproportionation

This route is penalized for H₂ atmosphere, sealed Ta equipment, volatile/reactive chlorides, and multistep hydride/disproportionation handling, all of which appear in structurally and thermodynamically similar Zr chloride syntheses (SS-2, TS-1). Kinetics are only moderate because related reactions still require ~20 days at 750 °C (SS-2, TS-1). The strongest limitation is structural selectivity: the analogues form ternary cluster phases such as K₂Zr₇Cl₁₈ rather than binary layered ZrCl₂ (SS-2, TS-1). There is insufficient evidence from CS for this route as a direct binary ZrCl₂ synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so no cation-radius mismatch criterion is triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target structure is described as a two-dimensional sheet of edge-sharing ZrCl₆ octahedra, not a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is absent from the target description. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given; however, no CS/SS/TS evidence indicates a very large >40 atom target cell, so this check is treated as not triggered with some uncertainty. Overall, the layered binary structure should have low amorphization tendency, though stacking disorder and polytype intergrowth are still likely (CS-1, SS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target monoclinic P2₁/m ZrCl₂ is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the most stable ZrCl₂ form, because its E above hull is 0.0290 eV/atom while a trigonal ZrCl₂ analogue is reported at 0 eV/atom (target, CS-1). The synthesis support is strong chemically because ZrCl₂ itself has been made by CVT from ZrCl and ZrCl₄, and strong structurally because monoclinic P2₁/m ZrI₂ exists and is made by sealed-tube transport (CS-1, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is halide vapor transport using volatile ZrCl₄ or related Zr halide species, which allows equilibration and crystal growth below the melting point of Zr (CS-1, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/polytype control, since reduced Zr chlorides show substoichiometry, multiple polytypes, twinning, intergrowth, and slow equilibration (CS-1, SS-1). Overall, the target is feasible but requires careful sealed-tube vapor-transport optimization rather than a simple ceramic reaction.
