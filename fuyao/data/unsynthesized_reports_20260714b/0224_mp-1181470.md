### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the favorable threshold for synthesizable metastable intermetallics. CS1/SS2 list Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃ itself with E_hull = 0.0, while SS1/TS1 show a related Nd–Zn–Ge phase at E_hull ≈ 0.041 eV/atom and TS4 shows Tb₄Zn₅Ge₆ at E_hull ≈ 0.067 eV/atom. The main uncertainty is that the target structural description appears lower-coordinate than the CS1 Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃ description, suggesting possible polymorphism, disorder, or database inconsistency.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
SS1/TS1 provide mild conditions: 600 °C in evacuated quartz, pressed pellet, no high pressure. The deductions come from vacuum sealing, Zn volatility, and Gd air sensitivity; these are manageable but non-negligible. CS gives insufficient reliable precursor/condition detail for this exact solid-state route and is treated as neutral for the missing details.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Elemental Gd, Zn, and Ge are less reactive than salts or molecular precursors, so precursor activity is only moderate-low. However, SS1/TS1 demonstrate that rare-earth–Zn–Ge phases can form at 600 °C, implying Zn-assisted diffusion or favorable intermetallic formation. The uncertainty is whether the exact Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃ stoichiometry competes with Zn-rich Gd–Zn–Ge phases.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
TS4 shows arc melting under Ar plus long annealing in evacuated fused-silica tubes, which is standard but more severe than simple sintering. CS also mentions arc-melted and annealed samples for related phases. The major penalties are high-temperature melting, controlled atmosphere/vacuum anneal, multi-step processing, Zn volatility, and Gd air sensitivity.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
Arc melting improves homogeneity and overcomes initial diffusion barriers, as supported by TS4 and CS. The 400 h anneal in TS4 indicates slow ordering or phase equilibration can be important. The main uncertainty is Zn loss during arc melting, which can move the alloy away from the desired Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃ composition.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CS1–CS5/SS2 support Sn-flux growth for the R₂Zn₆Ge₃ family, including Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃, but do not provide a complete heating profile. The route avoids high pressure and likely avoids sustained ultra-high temperature, but sealed ampoules, volatile Zn, Gd air sensitivity, and decanting/cleaning make it moderately demanding. Because CS lacks explicit atmosphere and temperature, those details are partly Reasonable Inference.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Flux growth gives the best kinetic score because molten Sn provides high mobility and a low-barrier crystallization pathway. CS1/SS2 directly support crystallization of Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃-type material from Sn flux, making structural reconstruction risk low. The remaining uncertainty is Sn incorporation or stabilization of Sn-containing impurity phases, which CS characterization by EPMA/Mössbauer implies should be checked.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
SS5/TS2 show sealed Ta crucible induction melting at about 1100 °C, remelting, annealing, and cooling. This is a robust route for volatile-metal intermetallics but requires specialized Ta welding and high-temperature sealed processing. Its relevance is weaker than CS/SS2 because the analogue is Eu–Zn–Sn, not Gd–Zn–Ge.

**F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics.**  
Full melting and remelting improve homogenization, so precursor activity and diffusion are better than in simple solid-state sintering. However, the target structure is not directly demonstrated by SS5/TS2, and Ge substitution for Sn may change phase stability. The route is plausible but less directly supported than Sn flux.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers one structural-complexity check. A large Gd/Zn size mismatch also triggers a radius-mismatch check, although CS/SS/TS show rare-earth–Zn–Ge intermetallics can crystallize despite this mismatch. There is insufficient evidence that the unit cell contains more than 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Gd₂(Zn₂Ge)₃ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and CS1/SS2 list the same nominal Gd₂Zn₆Ge₃ composition as stable in the R₂Zn₆Ge₃ family. The analogue support is strong chemically and structurally from CS1–CS5/SS2, and solid-state/thermodynamic support from SS1/TS1 and TS4 shows rare-earth–Zn–Ge intermetallics are routinely accessible. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a liquid or semi-liquid transport pathway, especially Sn flux, which improves kinetics and supports single-crystal growth. The main limiting factor is composition control, especially Zn volatility and the possibility of competing Gd–Zn–Ge or Sn-containing phases. Overall, the target should be synthesizable with careful sealed-tube or flux methodology.
