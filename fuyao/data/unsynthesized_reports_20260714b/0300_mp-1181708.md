### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin. This is reinforced by CS/SS examples where cyanide salts with much higher calculated E_hull values were experimentally crystallized by mild solution methods, including K₂Hg(CN)₄ at 0.257 eV/atom (CS1/SS5), KHgC₂N₂I at 0.284 eV/atom (CS2), KMnAg₃(CN)₆ at 0.287 eV/atom (SS1), and K₂NaAg₃(CN)₆ at 0.207 eV/atom (SS2). TS analogues near 0.08–0.10 eV/atom are also synthesized by mild aqueous routes (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull energies for molecular/ionic cyanides may not fully capture hydration, solvation, or polymorph stability.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS1, CS2, SS4, and SS5 report room-temperature aqueous evaporation, and SS2 reports aqueous reaction/evaporation for a K/Ag cyanide framework. No high temperature, pressure, vacuum, or inert atmosphere is required in those analogues. The only major severity penalty is the use of toxic cyanide precursors, directly relevant because KCN is used in SS2 and TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for air sensitivity of KAu(CN)₂, so no air-sensitivity deduction is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because the reaction is solution-mediated, supported by aqueous crystallization in CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, SS4, and SS5. Precursor activity is high due to soluble KCN and formation of linear [Au(CN)₂]⁻, analogous to the [Ag(CN)₂]⁻ chemistry in SS1 and SS2. Structural reconstruction is minimal because the target can crystallize from molecular/ionic cyanometallate units rather than requiring bulk diffusion. The main uncertainty is the exact solubility window of AuCN in KCN solution, which is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses mild aqueous conditions supported by TS4, where KCN reacts with a noble-metal chloride complex in cold saturated aqueous solution. No high temperature, high pressure, or controlled atmosphere is required by TS4, TS1, or TS3. Deductions are applied for toxic KCN and the multi-step nature of cyanidation, filtration/chloride removal, and recrystallization, consistent with the multi-step aqueous processing in TS1–TS3. The principal uncertainty is the Au(III) to Au(I) conversion, which is inferred from gold cyanide chemistry rather than explicitly demonstrated by the provided sources.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution-phase diffusion and ligand exchange should be rapid, and TS4 supports cyanide substitution on noble-metal chloride complexes under mild aqueous conditions. However, Route 2 is kinetically less clean than Route 1 because the target linear Au(I) dicyanoaurate structure must form from a chloridoaurate precursor and may involve redox/ligand rearrangement. TS4 supports the general cyanometallate conversion but not the exact Au oxidation-state pathway. Thus the kinetics are good but not as robust as direct AuCN/KCN complexation.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is also mild because SS1 uses aqueous slow diffusion, followed by filtration, washing, and drying at 50 °C, with no special atmosphere or pressure. The process duration of 14 days in SS1 is long but not severe under the prescribed F2 deductions. A toxicity deduction is applied because the route uses KAu(CN)₂ or KCN-derived cyanide solutions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for volatile precursor loss in this route, so no sealed-system penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are favorable because crystallization occurs by slow solution diffusion, as demonstrated for structurally similar KMn[Ag(CN)₂]₃ in SS1. The slow supersaturation profile should improve ordering of the layered target phase and reduce amorphous precipitation. Structural reconstruction is minimal if using preformed KAu(CN)₂, analogous to the preformed KAg(CN)₂ building block in SS1. The remaining uncertainty is whether diffusion growth gives the simple KAu(CN)₂ phase or another solvated/hydrated polymorph, which is not resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a relatively simple ionic/molecular cyanometallate with linear CN units and K coordination; CS/SS analogues repeatedly form well-defined crystals from aqueous solution rather than amorphous solids, including large crystals in CS1/SS5 and good-quality crystals in SS2. The formula unit is small, and the provided target description does not show a large >40-atom cell or complex mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network. The ionic-radius criterion is not strongly applicable because Au is in a linear [Au(CN)₂]⁻ anion rather than acting as a conventional competing framework cation. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for the exact target’s amorphization behavior remains, but the analogue crystallization evidence supports low risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or very low-metastability phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and this is lower than many synthesized cyanide analogues reported in CS and SS. The CS and SS data strongly support solution synthesis: multiple K-containing cyanometallates crystallize from aqueous slow evaporation or diffusion under room-temperature to low-temperature conditions (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong preference of Au(I) for linear dicyanoaurate [Au(CN)₂]⁻ units, which closely parallels the Ag(I) linear cyanide chemistry in SS1 and SS2. The most important limiting factor is safe and precise cyanide chemistry: pH, free CN⁻ concentration, and avoidance of HCN are essential. A secondary limiting factor is controlling supersaturation so the trigonal layered crystalline phase forms instead of fine precipitate or alternate solvated forms.
