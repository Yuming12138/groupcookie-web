### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom cutoff in the required rubric. Similar Sr₂TaInO₆ polymorphs in CS/SS/TS have E_hull values from 0.0 to ~0.0419 eV/atom, supporting thermodynamic accessibility of the same composition and perovskite-derived framework [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. Thermodynamically similar compounds with E_hull around 0.054–0.067 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by ceramic routes, indicating that modest metastability is not prohibitive [TS2–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target C2/m oxygen-order/tilt variant is the exact room-temperature equilibrium polymorph or a nearby metastable distortion.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires high temperature above 1000 °C and multiple processing steps, so deductions are applied for both factors [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. No high pressure, inert/reducing atmosphere, sealed ampoule, or air-sensitive precursors are reported for the exact Sr₂TaInO₆ synthesis [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. The severity is therefore moderate rather than severe. The main uncertainty is whether reproducing the exact target C2/m variant requires a particular cooling rate, which is not specified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because the route uses refractory Ta/In oxides and long heat treatments, but the InTaO₄ intermediate provides a chemically matched In–Ta precursor [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. Intermittent regrinding is repeatedly reported, indicating diffusion and homogeneity are limiting but manageable [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. The structural pathway is favorable because the product is the same double-perovskite-derived Sr₂TaInO₆ network [CS1; SS2]. Remaining uncertainty is whether all polymorphs interconvert easily during cooling.

#### F2 — Route 2

The direct mixed-oxide route is inferred to need roughly the same final temperature as the demonstrated ceramic route, so it receives the same >1000 °C deduction [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Related direct solid-state routes use ambient pressure and air, so no atmosphere or pressure penalty is applied [CS5; SS5; TS3, TS4]. It is still multi-step because calcination, regrinding, pelletizing, and sintering are expected [TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is higher because no CS/SS/TS source directly confirms this route for Sr₂TaInO₆.

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct reaction from SrCO₃, In₂O₃, and Ta₂O₅ is expected to be slower than Route 1 because it lacks the InTaO₄ intermediate used in exact reports [CS1–CS4; Reasonable Inference]. Ta₂O₅ is a refractory oxide, and exact syntheses already require long heating even after forming InTaO₄ [CS1, CS2; SS2, SS3]. Complete reconstruction and B-site ordering into Sr₂TaInO₆ add kinetic barriers [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether extended milling or nanoscale precursors could substantially improve this route.

#### F2 — Route 3

The sol–gel route is inferred to require a final crystallization above 1000 °C because analogous Sr₂TaInO₆ ceramics crystallize at ~1200 °C [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. It is multi-step due to solution preparation, gelation, burnout, calcination, and final annealing [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and should use air for oxide formation, consistent with CS/SS/TS oxide syntheses [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. The main uncertainty is precursor handling, especially Ta alkoxide or tantalum complex stability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetically, sol–gel processing should improve precursor activity and cation homogeneity relative to mixed oxides [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates sol–gel Sr₂TaInO₆, so the evidence base is weaker and should be treated cautiously. The final perovskite crystallization still requires building an ordered Ta/In octahedral framework, which may be slow because ceramic analogues require prolonged firing [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. The route may reduce firing time but has uncertainty in avoiding amorphous or secondary tantalate phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The largest amorphization-related concern is the large cation size/coordination mismatch among Sr, In, and Ta, reflected in very different Sr–O, In–O, and Ta–O bond distances [target structure; CS1; SS2]. The target’s octahedral network is nevertheless simple corner-sharing TaO₆/InO₆, which lowers amorphization risk compared with mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing structures [target structure; CS1; SS2]. The provided data do not show a >40-atom unit cell, so that check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large-cell amorphization penalty. Overall amorphization risk is low to medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂TaInO₆ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a highly metastable material, because the target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and same-composition polymorphs in CS/SS/TS lie at 0.0–0.0419 eV/atom [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. The analogue support is unusually strong because multiple CS and SS entries are the same Sr₂TaInO₆ composition and perovskite-derived framework, including a very close monoclinic C2/m structural match [CS1; SS2]. The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated ceramic route using preformed InTaO₄, which directly addresses In/Ta mixing and B-site ordering [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1]. The most important limiting factor is slow solid-state diffusion and phase homogenization, evidenced by 5–10 day heating schedules and intermittent regrinding [CS1, CS2; SS2, SS3]. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible with standard ceramic equipment, but phase-pure synthesis will likely require patience and repeated grinding/XRD checks.
