### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, SS4/TS4, and SS5/TS5 include stable fluoride analogues with E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that alkali/heavy-metal fluoride frameworks can be thermodynamic ground states. CS data are mixed: CS1 is stable, while CS2 and CS5 have relatively high E_hull values but are still reported materials, so CS provides moderate rather than decisive thermodynamic support. Remaining uncertainty is chemical rather than thermodynamic: the Os–N–S ligand chemistry is not represented in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Hydrothermal / solvothermal

CS1 and SS5/TS5 support low-temperature hydrothermal fluoride synthesis at 100–200 °C, so temperature severity is low. CS2, CS5, and SS5/TS5 use HF media, which are synthetically effective but toxic and corrosive, causing the main F2 penalty. Kinetically, hydrothermal solution chemistry strongly improves diffusion and precursor activity, as shown by CS1 and SS5/TS5. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS hydrothermal analogue contains the Os–N–S unit, so structural reconstruction/ligand incorporation is only moderately scored.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Direct fluorination

SS1/TS1 and SS2/TS2 directly support direct fluorination of alkali transition-metal fluoride phases at 350 °C, showing that the temperature is not extreme. However, F2 atmosphere, volatile/toxic species, and moisture-sensitive fluoride products make F2 severe. Kinetics are weaker because the route begins from solids and requires formation of an ordered K–Os–F–N–S sheet, while SS1/TS1 and SS2/TS2 only demonstrate simpler MF6 fluoride frameworks. The absence of direct CS/SS/TS evidence for thiazyl incorporation during F2 fluorination is the major uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Solution crystallization

SS3/TS3 and SS4/TS4 show room-temperature crystallization of Cs2IrF6 and Rb2IrF6 from alkali carbonate plus H2[IrF6], giving strong support for mild solution crystallization of 5d fluorometallates. CS5 also supports slow evaporation of alkali transition-metal fluorides from HF-containing solution. F2 is favorable because no high temperature, pressure, or reactive gas is required, although toxic/specialized Os and NS precursors still penalize the route. F3 is highest for this route because soluble preformed fluorometallates minimize diffusion barriers; the main uncertainty is whether the Os–N–S motif can be installed without hydrolysis or competing Os fluoride crystallization.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: HF precipitation / slow diffusion

CS2 supports room-temperature precipitation and slow diffusion growth of fluoride crystals in aqueous HF, and CS5 supports slow evaporation of alkali transition-metal fluoride solutions. This gives mild F2 conditions, with deductions mainly for HF toxicity and probable precursor pre-synthesis. Kinetics are favorable because precipitation/diffusion occurs from dissolved complexes rather than refractory solids. The limiting uncertainty is again the N–S unit, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target combines a large K cation with a compact octahedral Os center, triggering the cation-radius mismatch criterion by standard ionic-size reasoning; analogous alkali/transition-metal fluoride structures in SS/TS show that such mismatch is common but can still crystallize. The target formula is small, and there is insufficient evidence that its unit cell exceeds 40 atoms. The structure has multiple F coordination environments and 2D sheets, but the target description does not explicitly show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The remaining uncertainty is that layered fluoride/thiazyl ordering may create local disorder not captured by the simple F4 checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KOsNSF5 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of alkali transition-metal fluoride synthesis, especially by solution crystallization and hydrothermal methods, but they do not directly validate the Os–N–S chemical motif. The most critical enabling factor is use of a preformed soluble Os fluorometallate, because SS3/TS3 and SS4/TS4 show that heavy 5d fluorometallates can crystallize at room temperature from solution. The most critical limiting factor is preserving and incorporating the N–S ligand under strongly fluorinating or HF-rich conditions. Overall, the target appears synthesizable, but the best strategy should avoid harsh gas fluorination and instead use controlled solution crystallization with high fluoride activity and slow growth.
