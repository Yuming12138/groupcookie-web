### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull = 0.034 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues include Bi₂O₃ polymorphs at E_hull = 0.0386, 0.0165, and 0.0508 eV/atom, and these have reported syntheses by HP or hydrothermal methods [TS1, TS2, TS5]. Chemically similar Bi₂O₃ entries also show that low-energy metastable polymorphs are experimentally accessible [CS1, CS3, CS5]. The uncertainty is polymorph-specific: low E_hull supports accessibility but does not guarantee that monoclinic P2/c ribbons are selected over other Bi₂O₃ polymorphs [Target, CS1–CS5].

#### F2: Condition Severity — Route 1

Hydrothermal synthesis uses only 240 °C, avoiding high-temperature and >5 GPa penalties [CS1, SS1, TS2]. However, it requires concentrated KOH, multiple additives, and careful ratio/redox control, so it is not a simple precipitation [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The F2 score is therefore high but not near-perfect. Exact duration is not reported, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for time severity.

#### F3: Reaction Kinetics — Route 1

Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O in concentrated KOH provides a reactive solution-phase Bi source, which should greatly reduce diffusion limitations [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The reported formation of single crystals at only 240 °C indicates favorable nucleation/growth kinetics for at least one metastable Bi₂O₃ polymorph [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction from the hydrothermal ε-Bi₂O₃ motif to the target P2/c 1D ribbon, which is not directly demonstrated [Target, CS1, SS1].

#### F2: Condition Severity — Route 2

HP Bi₂O₃ routes require 6–6.3 GPa and 850–900 °C, using multianvil or DIA-type equipment [CS2, CS3, CS5, SS5, TS1, TS5]. The temperature is below 1000 °C, but the pressure and multistep capsule/quench/release protocol impose a large penalty [CS3, CS5, TS1, TS5]. No reactive gas atmosphere is required, and Bi₂O₃ is a simple precursor [CS3, CS5, TS1, TS5]. The route is credible but operationally severe.

#### F3: Reaction Kinetics — Route 2

Because the precursor and product are both Bi₂O₃, the transformation is polymorphic rather than a multication diffusion reaction [CS2, CS3, CS5, TS1, TS5]. Reported HP analogues form within 10–30 min or 0.5 h, which supports rapid kinetics under pressure [CS3, CS5, TS1, TS5]. However, the target P2/c ribbon topology differs from the trigonal and hexagonal HP phases reported, so structural selectivity remains uncertain [Target, CS3, CS5, TS1, TS5].

#### F2: Condition Severity — Route 3

The oxidative solid-state route is experimentally simpler than high pressure and uses furnace temperatures above 800 °C [CS4]. Controlled oxygen flow is required, and graphite/O₂ conditions require care [CS4; Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids GPa pressure and complicated precursors, giving a good F2 score. The source does not report precise time or cooling rate, so insufficient evidence from CS for those severity elements.

#### F3: Reaction Kinetics — Route 3

Bi or Bi₂O₃ + C under O₂ can form a Bi₂O₃ polymorph above 800 °C [CS4], implying adequate mobility for phase formation. Still, oxide/metal solid-state precursors are less kinetically active than nitrate hydrothermal precursors [CS4 vs CS1]. The main kinetic and structural challenge is trapping the desired monoclinic P2/c phase rather than the reported tetragonal β-type polymorph [Target, CS4]. Insufficient evidence from TS for this oxidative route specifically, so TS is neutral for Route 3 kinetics.

#### F2: Condition Severity — Route 4

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, Bi, so cation-radius mismatch does not drive amorphization [Target]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a low-energy metastable Bi₂O₃ polymorph rather than a ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.034 eV/atom and many nearby Bi₂O₃ analogues are described as metastable polymorphs [Target, CS1, CS3, CS5, TS1, TS2, TS5]. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis well at the composition level, since multiple Bi₂O₃ polymorphs have reported syntheses [CS1–CS5], and the closest structural analogue is also hydrothermally prepared Bi₂O₃ [SS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the ability of Bi³⁺ lone-pair chemistry to stabilize multiple low-coordinate, low-dimensional Bi–O frameworks under non-equilibrium conditions [CS1, CS5, SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: hydrothermal, high-pressure, and oxidative routes each stabilize different Bi₂O₃ polymorphs, and none directly reports the monoclinic P2/c 1D ribbon target [CS1–CS5, SS1, TS1, TS5]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable in principle, but obtaining it phase-pure will likely require screening mineralizer chemistry, cooling rate, or pressure-temperature pathway.
