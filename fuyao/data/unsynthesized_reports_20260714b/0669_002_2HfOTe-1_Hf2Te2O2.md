### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0490 eV/atom, placing it at the top of the 90–100 scoring bin for ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. Chemically and structurally close ThTeO and ThOSe are reported at E_hull = 0 and share P4/nmm oxychalcogenide motifs [CS1, CS3, SS2, SS3]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈ 0.038–0.050 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by flux, hydrothermal, and solid-state methods [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that HfTeO is slightly metastable and Hf–Te–O competing phases are not explicitly listed in the references [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS on competing Hf–Te–O phases].

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct evacuated-tube solid-state reaction

The severity is moderate because the closest route requires vacuum sealing, slow heating, and high-temperature annealing [CS1, SS2]. The proposed 750–950 °C window avoids the >1000 °C deduction, but Te volatility and evacuated ampoule handling are unavoidable [CS1, CS3]. No high pressure is needed, consistent with ThTeO and ThOSe analogues [CS1, CS3]. Uncertainty is that HfO₂ may require higher temperature than ThO₂, possibly pushing the route above 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct evacuated-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetics are limited by refractory HfO₂ and metallic Hf diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The same structural family is strongly supported by ThTeO and ThOSe [CS1, CS3, SS2, SS3], which improves the structural reconstruction score. However, CS1 reports degradation and poor diffraction/phase-control issues for ThTeO-related samples, lowering the kinetic/phase-control confidence [CS1]. Overall, the route is plausible but may need extended annealing and repeated grinding [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux growth

Flux growth uses sealed ampoules and inert handling but can operate near 800 °C, avoiding very high-temperature penalties [TS1]. NaI/KI flux and iodine-assisted crystallization are supported by TS1 and SS1, while actinide oxychalcogenide flux/byproduct formation is supported by CS4. The method still carries penalties for volatile Te/I₂ species, multistep processing, and controlled atmosphere [CS4, TS1]. Uncertainty is that HfTeO solubility and stability in halide flux are not directly demonstrated [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux growth

The flux route improves mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, as small crystals of thermodynamically similar oxyselenides were grown in NaI/KI flux [TS1]. CS4 shows that oxychalcogenides can form in flux/transport environments, but also notes NpOSe was obtained as a byproduct, indicating uncertain phase selectivity [CS4]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target structure is known among analogues [CS1, SS2], but flux conditions may instead stabilize competing Hf tellurides/oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are better than Route 1, but phase control remains a major uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3: Metathesis

Metathesis benefits from lower temperature, as NpOI forms at 400 °C with optional 700 °C crystallinity improvement [CS2]. Severity remains moderate because the route requires air-free handling, volatile Hf halides, toxic/air-sensitive tellurides, and sealed evacuated ampoules [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is expected [CS2]. The main uncertainty is chemical compatibility of HfCl₄/HfO₂/Na₂Te mixtures and whether the salt byproduct can be removed without decomposing HfTeO [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Metathesis

The kinetic score is highest because reactive halides/tellurides can shorten diffusion lengths and salt formation can drive reaction [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. The NpOI analogue demonstrates that layered P4/nmm oxide-anion compounds can be made by SSM under sealed vacuum [CS2]. However, HfTeO is an oxide telluride rather than an oxide iodide, and no direct Hf–Te–O metathesis precedent is supplied [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Therefore the score is improved but capped due to extrapolation risk.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally simple: one cation, one chalcogen, one oxygen, and a P4/nmm layered motif [Target]. The absence of multiple cations avoids large cation radius mismatch [Target; Reasonable Inference], and the unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The one risk factor is mixed corner- and edge-sharing OHf₄ tetrahedral connectivity, explicitly described in the target structure [Target]. No CS/SS/TS source gives direct amorphization data, so those sources are neutral for this factor [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfTeO is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, just below the 0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability threshold [Target]. The chemical and structural analogue support is strong: ThTeO is same P4/nmm oxy-telluride family and was synthesized by evacuated-tube direct reaction [CS1, SS2]. Thermodynamic analogues show that compounds in the ~0.04–0.05 eV/atom range are experimentally accessible by solid-state or flux methods [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of directly analogous P4/nmm oxychalcogenides, especially ThTeO and ThOSe [CS1, CS3, SS2, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling Te activity and avoiding competing HfO₂/Hf–Te phases during reaction with a refractory Hf–O component [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues in CS1/CS4].
