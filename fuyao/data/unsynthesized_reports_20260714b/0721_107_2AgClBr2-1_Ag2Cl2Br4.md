### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0110 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Similar successful analogues also lie near this energy: CS1 is 0.016 eV/atom, CS4 is 0.0066 eV/atom, TS1 is 0.0114 eV/atom, and TS4 is 0.0158 eV/atom. This suggests the target is probably accessible as a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase, although CS/SS/TS do not prove it is the ground state.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, CVT/vapor transport.**  
SS and TS strongly support low-temperature sealed-ampoule halide transport: SS5/TS2 use 150→120 °C gradients, TS4 uses 423→398 K, and SS1/TS1 use 423–453 K halide transport. The condition score is penalized because vacuum/Ar handling, volatile halogens, and sealed ampoules are required, as in SS1, SS5, TS1, TS2, and TS4. Kinetically, vapor transport should crystallize low-dimensional halides efficiently, but mixed Br/Cl stoichiometry is likely narrow, and phase-control problems are documented in CS1, CS2, SS3, and TS1.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, hydrothermal/solvothermal.**  
CS1 directly supports Ag-containing halide-rich hydrothermal synthesis at 220 °C in concentrated HI, while SS3 supports bromine-containing sealed solvothermal synthesis at 180 °C. These are moderate-temperature but chemically severe conditions because they involve concentrated acids, halogens, sealed vessels, and often post-growth separation. Kinetics are favorable due to solution mobility, but phase purity is uncertain because CS1 and SS3 both report multiphase products or manual separation.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, sealed melt/solid-state.**  
CS2 supports evacuated ampoule halide synthesis at 550 °C with cooling-rate-sensitive phase selection, and CS4 supports sealed-ampoule Ag compound synthesis at 350–600 °C. The route is not thermally extreme, but volatile halogens and vacuum sealing remain significant severity factors. Kinetics should be acceptable because Ag halides are soft and relatively low melting [Reasonable Inference], but structural ordering into a 1D mixed-halide ribbon may require precise cooling, as suggested by CS2.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, solution precipitation.**  
SS2 shows halometallates can precipitate immediately and recrystallize from hot HBr/HCl mother liquor using simple glassware, so condition severity is low relative to sealed routes. However, CS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that AgBr₂Cl itself forms by ambient precipitation. The main uncertainty is that Ag⁺ may rapidly form simple AgCl/AgBr precipitates rather than the ordered 1D target, so the route receives an expert downward adjustment.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple one-cation chemistry, so cation-radius mismatch is not triggered. The reported structure is a 1D ribbon, not a large mixed-polyhedral network, and no evidence indicates >40 atoms per unit cell. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high amorphization risk, so the score is high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgBr₂Cl is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, comparable to several synthesized analogues in CS and TS. The strongest support comes from TS/SS low-temperature halide CVT and sealed-ampoule crystallization examples, especially TS4 and SS5/TS2; CS1 also supports Ag-containing halide-rich hydrothermal synthesis. The key enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility of halide species under sealed, halogen-rich conditions, which should allow crystallization without refractory diffusion barriers. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: AgCl, AgBr, or halogen-rich byproducts may form more readily than an ordered 1D AgBr₂Cl ribbon. Overall, the material is plausible to synthesize, but single-phase bulk powder may be harder than small crystals grown from vapor or halogen-rich solution.
