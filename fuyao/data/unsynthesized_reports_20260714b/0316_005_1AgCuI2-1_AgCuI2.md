**F1 Score: 95 / 100**

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0200 eV/atom places it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS gives especially strong support because several thermodynamically similar compounds are experimentally synthesized near the same hull energy, including CuHgSI at ~0.021 eV/atom [TS1], AgTl2I3 at ~0.027 eV/atom [TS2], and CuHgSCl at ~0.024 eV/atom [TS4]. CS is supportive but mixed because AgTl2I3 is close in hull energy [CS3], while Ag3SI polymorphs include higher-hull phases [CS4/CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific triclinic P1 AgCuI2 sheet is the experimentally accessible polymorph rather than a nearby competing AgI/CuI-derived arrangement.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 Solid-State

The solid-state route is directly supported by evacuated-tube iodide analogues: AgHgSI from AgI + HgS at 473 K [SS3], CuHgSI from CuI + HgS at 553 K [SS5/TS1], and CuHgSCl from CuX + HgS at 573 K [TS4]. F2 is reduced by the need for vacuum sealing, iodide retention, and likely regrinding/annealing, consistent with Ag3SI processing [CS5/SS2]. Kinetically, soft iodides should diffuse better than oxides, but long annealing in SS1 and SS5/TS1 indicates ordering can be slow. Uncertainty remains because no source directly reports AgI + CuI forming AgCuI2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis has the best kinetic outlook because AgTl2I3 forms from AgI/TlI in 25% HI at only 155–175 °C [CS3/TS2]. The route avoids bulk diffusion and provides high iodide activity, which is favorable for a ternary iodide framework [CS3/TS2]. F2 penalties come from corrosive/toxic HI, sealed autoclave pressure, and multistep heating/cooling [CS3/TS2]. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: AgTl2I3 had twinning and low yield of untwinned crystals [CS3/TS2], so AgCuI2 may require screening of HI concentration and cooling rate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 Melt / Bridgman

Melt growth is supported by Ag3SI growth from sealed Ag2S + AgI around 700 °C [CS4/SS4] and melt-anneal AgGaSe2 at 650 °C [CS2]. F2 is penalized for sealed-vacuum ampoule handling, volatile iodide retention, and multistep melt/anneal processing [CS2/CS4/SS4]. F3 benefits from excellent liquid-state diffusion, but structural selection is less certain because Ag3SI melt growth produced phase mixtures requiring crystal separation [CS4/SS4]. This route is attractive for single crystals only after a phase-pure composition/temperature window is identified.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 High Pressure

CS1 shows that chemically similar Ag-containing chalcogenide phases can be made at 20–40 kbar and 600–1000 °C with quenching. However, SS and TS provide stronger evidence that related iodide/chalcogenide halides can be made at ambient pressure in sealed tubes or hydrothermal systems [SS3/SS5/TS1/TS2]. F2 is not heavily penalized by the provided scoring rubric because the CS1 pressure is below the >5 GPa threshold, but practically the equipment is specialized. The key uncertainty is transferability: high pressure may be unnecessary and could favor a different dense polymorph rather than the 2D P1 sheet.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only the cation-radius/coordination mismatch check is clearly triggered: target Ag is 7-coordinate while Cu is 3-coordinate, indicating strongly different local preferences in the same framework. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral. There is also insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that AgCuI2 contains simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, so C3 is neutral. Thus the amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCuI2 is likely a **near-ground-state or weakly metastable** compound because its E_hull is only 0.020 eV/atom, similar to experimentally synthesized low-hull iodide analogues [TS1/TS2/TS4]. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis best through iodide-rich hydrothermal chemistry and evacuated-tube solid-state reaction, while high-pressure synthesis is less directly supported for this target [CS3/TS2/SS3/SS5/TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the soft, mobile Ag+/Cu+/I− chemistry, which allows low-temperature crystallization in analogues [SS3/SS5/TS1/CS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against AgI, CuI, and possible alternative Ag–Cu–I polymorphs; analogues show long ordering times, twinning, or phase mixtures [SS1/CS3/CS4]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, with the best first attempt being iodohydrothermal growth followed by evacuated-tube annealing if needed.
