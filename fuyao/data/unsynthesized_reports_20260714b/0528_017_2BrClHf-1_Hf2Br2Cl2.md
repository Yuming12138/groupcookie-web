### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0320 eV/atom, which places it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic bracket of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Chemically similar HfNBr phases include one ground-state α-HfNBr and one nearly stable β-HfNBr with E_hull ≈0.004 eV/atom, supporting that layered Hf halide-related phases can be thermodynamically accessible (CS-1, CS-2). Structurally similar ZrCl₂ is on the hull, also supporting the layered molybdenite-derived halide topology (SS-1). The uncertainty is that HfBrCl is a mixed-halide ordered compound, and CS/SS/TS do not directly report this exact mixed Cl/Br Hf(II) composition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, consistent with sealed-tube reduced halide syntheses at 600–900 °C in SS-1, TS-1, and TS-2. The main severity penalties come from air-sensitive/volatile halide precursors, sealed Ta-tube handling, and multi-step loading/sealing/heating/cooling procedures, all supported by SS-1 and TS-1/TS-2. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for this exact HfBrCl solid-state route, so CS is treated as neutral for Route 1 condition severity. The route is practical in a solid-state halide lab but not routine for open-furnace synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Hf metal is refractory and likely slow to equilibrate, analogous to reduced halide systems requiring days to weeks in TS-1, TS-2, and SS-1. Halide vapor or molten halide species may enhance transport, as implied by autogenous ZrCl₄ pressure and vapor transport in SS-1 and TS-3. Structural reconstruction is moderate rather than severe because SS-1 shows a closely related molybdenite-like metal halide sheet, but the target requires Cl/Br ordering that is not proven in CS/SS/TS. Therefore F3 is only moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses controlled vapor transport, and similar vapor/chemical transport methods are directly supported by SS-1, SS-2, TS-3, and TS-5. The temperature is moderate, but sealed ampoules, volatile halides, inert/vacuum handling, and multiple setup steps justify substantial deductions. SS gives the strongest support for the method because ZrCl₂ is structurally closest; TS supports the reduced-halide vapor-transport environment. CS is less directly applicable except for showing Hf-containing layered halide-related phases can be synthesized under specialized conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport improves kinetics by bypassing long-range solid-state diffusion, and several analogues grow by vapor/CVT routes, including ZrCl₂-like material and TiBr₂ (SS-1, TS-5). Direct crystallization of layered/reduced halides is supported by SS-1, SS-2, and TS-3, giving Route 2 the highest kinetic score. The limiting kinetic/compositional factor is simultaneous control of Cl and Br chemical potentials, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates ordered HfBrCl. Thus composition control, not crystallization itself, dominates the uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure synthesis is directly supported only by chemically similar HfNBr/HfNI nitride halides in CS-1. It requires 3–5 GPa, ~1000–1100 °C, capsules, BN crucibles, and quenching, which makes it more severe than ambient sealed-tube routes. Because the specified pressure is not above 5 GPa, the formal >5 GPa deduction is not applied, but the route remains operationally challenging. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure synthesis of pure mixed Hf halides, so their signal is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux/high-pressure treatment may accelerate ordering or stabilize a metastable layered polymorph, as CS-1 demonstrates for β-HfNBr. However, the CS-1 chemistry includes nitride halides rather than pure Hf mixed halides, so transferability is uncertain. If preformed HfCl₂/HfBr₂ or HfBrCl is used, structural reconstruction should be limited to halide ordering, which is favorable by [Reasonable Inference]. The need for prior precursor preparation and possible halide volatility under high T/P keep the F3 score moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch is absent. The described cell is small and layered, and the Hf coordination is consistently octahedral with edge-sharing HfBr₃Cl₃ units, so the three amorphization checks are not triggered. SS-1 reinforces that related molybdenite-like layered halides can crystallize rather than amorphize. CS and TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data and are treated as neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfBrCl is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0320 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from structurally similar molybdenite-like ZrCl₂, which is a layered edge-sharing metal halide synthesized by sealed-tube CVT/equilibration (SS-1). Chemically similar HfNBr phases show that layered Hf-halide frameworks are accessible, but their nitride chemistry makes them only partially transferable to pure HfBrCl (CS-1, CS-2). The most important enabling factor is the availability of volatile halide transport chemistry, which can overcome sluggish Hf solid-state diffusion (SS-1, TS-3, TS-5). The most important limiting factor is mixed Cl/Br stoichiometry and ordering, because CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate an ordered HfBrCl phase and volatile chloride/bromide chemical potentials may favor binary HfCl₂/HfBr₂ or intergrowths.
