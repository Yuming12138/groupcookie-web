### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore near the metastability threshold for experimentally accessible inorganic solids. Thermodynamically similar examples at comparable hull energies are reported as synthesizable, including Sr₂ZnS₃ at 0.040 eV/atom under high pressure (TS-1) and BaBrCl at 0.048 eV/atom by solid-state/melt cooling (TS-2). The uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not specify whether the decomposition products are simple binaries such as SrS/CaS/CaBr₂ or more complex competing phases.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires sealed vacuum or inert atmosphere handling, which is directly supported by Sr chalcogenide syntheses at 800 °C under vacuum in CS-1/CS-4 and sulfide halide syntheses in sealed ampoules in SS-3/SS-5. The temperature is not extreme, but multi-step grinding/reannealing is likely because CS-1/CS-4 required repeated cycles for phase purity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether SrCa₂(SBr)₂ can be made phase-pure without flux assistance.

#### F3 — Route 1

The SrS structural motif is highly transferable because SS-1 gives SrS₆ octahedra with Sr–S = 3.02 Å, essentially identical to the target’s 3.01 Å. However, forming the target requires simultaneous cation ordering and mixed S/Br anion ordering, which is more complex than forming binary SrS or SrBr₂ (SS-1, SS-2). CS-1/CS-4 show that related Sr chalcogenide solid-state syntheses can need repeated grinding, so kinetics are rated moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses a halide flux near 900 °C, supported by Ca₂CuBr₂O₂ flux growth in CS-2 and Sr₂CuO₂Br₂ melt growth in CS-5. It still requires dry/inert handling because bromide-containing and halide-rich analogues are moisture sensitive or handled in gloveboxes in CS-3, CS-5, SS-4, and TS-2. The condition severity remains moderate because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation.

#### F3 — Route 2

The flux route has the best kinetic outlook because molten CaBr₂/SrBr₂ can transport Ca/Sr/Br and promote layered crystal growth, as demonstrated for layered alkaline-earth oxybromides in CS-2 and CS-5. Controlled cooling from alkaline-earth halide melts is a proven strategy in SS-4 and TS-2. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates sulfide-bromide layered Sr–Ca ordering, so the structural reconstruction score is not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure route is penalized heavily because TS-1 explicitly identifies high-pressure equipment and high-temperature operation as necessary for a thermodynamically similar Sr sulfide. It also requires dry glovebox loading and specialized capsules, again supported by TS-1. This route is a contingency rather than the recommended first attempt because insufficient evidence from CS and SS indicates that high pressure is required for the target.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure may help stabilize a near-hull metastable Sr-containing sulfide, as in TS-1, but TS-1 also reports reproducibility and gradient issues. Diffusion may be adequate at 700–900 °C, yet capsule volume and short dwell times can limit equilibration. Therefore, kinetics are moderate: better for stabilizing metastable products, worse for phase-pure bulk scale-up.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The only triggered amorphization check is the Sr/Ca ionic radius mismatch, estimated at >15% for octahedral Sr²⁺ vs Ca²⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure has mixed edge/corner sharing but no face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered from the target description. The unit cell is not indicated to exceed 40 atoms, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests a large disordered cell; thus the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCa₂(SBr)₂ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom. The CS data support bromide-containing layered alkaline-earth phases and Sr chalcogenide solid-state synthesis, while SS data strongly support the SrS₆ local motif and sulfide-halide layered chemistry; TS data show that similar near-hull alkaline-earth sulfide/halide compounds can be synthesized but sometimes require careful cooling or high pressure. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a CaBr₂/SrBr₂ molten flux, which should improve ion transport and Br incorporation. The main limiting factor is phase competition against stable binary salts and mixed alkaline-earth halides/sulfides, especially if Sr/Ca or S/Br ordering is slow. Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with flux growth the best first route.
