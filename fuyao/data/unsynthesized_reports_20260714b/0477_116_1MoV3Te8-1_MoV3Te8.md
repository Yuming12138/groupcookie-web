### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0310 eV/atom falls in the high-synthesizability range of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Experimentally synthesized analogues in CS/SS/TS often have E_hull values from 0 to ~0.021 eV/atom [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5], so the target is plausibly metastable but accessible. The remaining uncertainty is that V₃MoTe₈ is not directly reported, and the convex-hull proximity does not guarantee cation ordering or kinetic accessibility; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for this exact composition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Direct solid-state synthesis receives a moderate-low F2 score because it requires high temperature, vacuum sealing, volatile Te management, and likely staged heating/regrinding. Similar routes use 1000 °C for 4–12 d [CS1–CS3], ~1000 K for 1 week [SS1, SS2, TS1], or 1173 K plus long annealing/quench [TS5]. The route is experimentally conventional, but sealed Te ampoules and multi-day high-temperature dwell impose safety and operational severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by elemental V and Mo powders, which are refractory and slow-diffusing [Reasonable Inference], although Te melt/vapor can assist reaction, as similar tellurides form far below the melting points of the highest-melting metals [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS2]. Structural reconstruction is not severe because the target shares layered MTe₆-like local coordination with VTe₂/MoTe₂ [SS1–SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether V and Mo order into the Pmm2 V₃MoTe₈ sheet rather than forming binary VTe₂/MoTe₂ domains.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT has a better F2 score because temperatures of 650–800 °C are lower than most direct solid-state routes, consistent with NbPdTe₅ at 700/750 °C [CS5, SS4, TS2]. The penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile Te/I₂, and toxic/corrosive halogen chemistry, all supported by I₂-assisted telluride syntheses [CS5, SS4, SS5, TS2, TS3]. It avoids high pressure and can be operationally simpler than repeated sintering.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves effective mass transport because I₂ was required for NbPdTe₅ crystal growth [CS5, SS4, TS2] and halogen transport is established for Mo tellurides [SS3]. It still faces composition-control uncertainty because V and Mo may have different halide transport rates [Reasonable Inference], and NbNiTe₅ showed non-ideal composition after CVT [SS5]. The structure is compatible with layered telluride growth, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for V/Mo ordering under CVT.

#### F2 — Route 3

Te-rich self-flux has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still requires vacuum-sealed Te at high temperature and additional separation/slow-cooling steps. Its basis is strong: Te-rich starting compositions are repeatedly used for related layered tellurides [CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5]. The limiting severity is the large Te excess, which raises vapor pressure and safety risk in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

The Te-rich route has better precursor activity than direct solid-state reaction because molten Te can serve as a self-flux [Reasonable Inference], and excess Te improved formation or growth in multiple analogues [CS1–CS3, CS5]. However, V/Mo diffusion and ordering remain challenging because the cations are refractory [Reasonable Inference]. Phase competition is a significant concern because impurity phases such as TaTe₂/TaTe₃/PdTe₂ or elemental Te were reported in related systems [CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has low amorphization tendency by the specified checks: V and Mo have similar telluride bond lengths in the target and in VTe₂/MoTe₂ analogues [SS1, SS3], the formula unit is small, and the target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. TS5 shows that some V–Te phases can have mixed connectivity [TS5], but that structure is not the target, so it is only a caution. CS/SS/TS contain insufficient direct amorphization data, so the F4 score relies mainly on the target’s structural simplicity and local-coordinate compatibility.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃MoTe₈ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0310 eV/atom rather than zero, but this value is within the experimentally accessible range seen for synthesized tellurides [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support sealed-tube telluride synthesis and halogen-assisted crystal growth, especially for layered Te-rich transition-metal compounds [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the close structural match to VTe₂/MoTe₂-type layered MTe₆ coordination, with V–Te and Mo–Te bond lengths matching known tellurides [SS1, SS3]. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: VTe₂, MoTe₂, V₃Te₄, or Te-rich binary/ternary impurity phases may compete, as impurity formation is repeatedly reported in analogues [CS1, CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS5]. The best practical route is likely I₂-assisted CVT because it combines lower temperature with proven crystal-growth capability in related tellurides [CS5, SS4, TS2, SS5].
