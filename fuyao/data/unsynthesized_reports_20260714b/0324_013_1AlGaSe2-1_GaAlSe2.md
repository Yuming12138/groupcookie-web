### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0220 eV/atom, which lies well below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold for high synthesizability. Thermodynamically similar compounds in TS1–TS5 have E_hull values around 0.016–0.032 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized, supporting the idea that this metastability range is accessible. The target is probably not a strict ground state, but the small hull distance indicates a realistic metastable or near-equilibrium phase. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS identifies the exact competing phases for GaAlSe2.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because sealed evacuated ampoules are repeatedly required for similar selenides in CS2, CS4, TS2, and TS3. The temperature is not extreme, since related Al/Ga/In selenides form around 700–900 °C in TS2/TS3 and CS2, but volatile Se requires sealed handling. The deductions mainly arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and multi-step grinding/annealing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS determines whether GaAlSe2 specifically requires Se excess.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate: elemental Ga and Se can react readily, but Al oxide skin may slow incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. Solid-state analogues often require long anneals, repeated firing, or homogenization, such as CS4 and TS3. The target layered Ga/Al ordering is a structural reconstruction from elemental/binary precursors, so the structural kinetics are less favorable than simple binary GaSe formation. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms whether Ga/Al cation ordering occurs rapidly.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires high-vacuum sealed ampoules and volatile/corrosive transport agents, as shown by SS1/SS2 for iodine and TS1 for AlCl3. The process is multi-step because a source charge must normally be prepared before transport, as explicitly described in TS1. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, making it less severe than melt or high-pressure routes. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives the exact optimal transport agent for GaAlSe2.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the best kinetic score because the closest structural analogue, GaSe, is directly grown by iodine CVT in SS1/SS2. TS1 also shows that layered ternary selenides can be grown by CVT with AlCl3, supporting vapor-transport crystallization of complex selenides. The uncertainty is cation-ratio control: Ga and Al may transport at different rates, and TS1 notes that transport conditions required optimization to avoid multiphase condensation. This makes composition control the main kinetic limitation.

#### F2 — Route 3

The melt route is harsher because it likely approaches or exceeds 1000 °C, similar to the 1150 °C melt treatment used for CuInSe2 in CS4. It also requires evacuated ampoules, staged heating, slow cooling, and annealing, as in CS4 and SS4. Volatile Se imposes overpressure and stoichiometry-control risks. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether GaAlSe2 melts congruently.

#### F3 — Route 3

The melt route benefits from fast liquid-phase mixing, supported by melt growth of CuInSe2 in CS4. However, crystallizing the desired layered ordered P3m1 phase may be difficult; CS3 shows that Ga–chalcogen phases can require extremely long annealing to reorganize into the desired ordered structure. Thus the diffusion component is strong, but the structural reconstruction component is uncertain. The route is plausible but not the most controlled.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis has the largest equipment penalty because CS5 uses 60–80 kbar and TS4 uses >20 kbar in a modified Belt apparatus. The route still avoids ultrahigh temperature, since CS5/TS4 use roughly 600–850 °C. Its severity is dominated by pressure, encapsulation, and precursor preparation. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that pressure is beneficial for GaAlSe2 specifically.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can accelerate transformations, as TS4 reports quantitative TlGaSe2 high-pressure conversion after 30 minutes. However, CS5 also reports pressure-induced decomposition in CuAlS2, so the same strategy may destabilize rather than stabilize the desired phase. Since GaAlSe2 is already close to the convex hull, pressure is a contingency route rather than a primary pathway. The main uncertainty is whether a high-pressure polymorph or decomposition product would form.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple small-cell layered tetrahedral structure with only corner-sharing tetrahedra, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face-sharing frameworks. The main triggered risk is Ga/Al size mismatch in tetrahedral coordination [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar GaSe sheets in SS1/SS2 are crystalline and experimentally grown, supporting low intrinsic amorphization tendency. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS quantifies disorder tolerance in GaAlSe2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaAlSe2 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom, within the range of experimentally accessible thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5. The structural evidence is strong because the target closely resembles layered GaSe, and GaSe is grown by CVT in SS1/SS2. Chemical evidence is moderately supportive because many Ga/Al/In/Cu selenides form by sealed-tube solid-state or melt methods in CS2–CS4, though no source directly reports GaAlSe2. The most critical enabling factor is the compatibility of the target with layered tetrahedral GaSe-like crystallization, supported by SS1. The most critical limiting factor is Ga/Al/Se stoichiometry and cation ordering, since ternary vapor transport can produce multiphase condensation or off-stoichiometry, as suggested by TS1 and the repeated firing needs in CS4.
