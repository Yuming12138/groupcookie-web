### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.044 eV/atom falls below 0.05 eV/atom, giving a high thermodynamic score. Chemically similar Cu₂OCl₂ has a low hull energy of 0.0216 eV/atom, and CuOCl is still experimentally synthesized despite 0.0793 eV/atom (CS1, CS5). Structurally similar Zn₂NCl and Zn₂NBr are calculated ground-state phases at 0 eV/atom (SS1, SS2), while thermodynamic analogues cluster around 0.03–0.06 eV/atom (TS1, TS3–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that Zn₂OCl₂ is not itself experimentally demonstrated in the provided data.

#### F2: Route 1 — Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis avoids very high temperature and applied high pressure, matching the mild-to-moderate conditions used for CuOCl and ZnIO₃(OH) (CS5, TS5). The main severity deduction is procedural: loading, heating, slow cooling, isolation, and washing constitute a multi-step process. There is insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal Zn oxychloride synthesis specifically, so SS is treated as neutral for this route. Overall severity is low to moderate.

#### F3: Route 1 — Hydrothermal

Reaction kinetics are favorable because aqueous/hydrothermal transport reduces diffusion barriers, as shown by CuOCl formation at 250 °C and Zn–O sheet crystallization at 230 °C (CS5, TS5). ZnCl₂ should provide a reactive soluble chloride source [Reasonable Inference], while ZnO can act as a solid oxide reservoir as in TS5 and by analogy to CuO in CS5. The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction into an anhydrous Zn₂OCl₂ layer rather than ZnO or hydrated zinc hydroxychlorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 2 — Sealed-Tube ZnCl₂ Flux

The sealed-tube flux route uses moderate temperatures but requires vacuum or dry inert atmosphere, as Zn₂NX analogues are prepared in sealed evacuated quartz tubes and handled with glovebox-type precautions (CS3, CS4, SS1, SS2). Volatile/hygroscopic ZnCl₂ and sealed ampoule operation add severity, and post-reaction washing to remove flux adds steps (CS3, CS4). SS4/SS5 also support the need for dry ampoules and inert atmospheres in halogenozincate reactions. Thus the route is feasible but operationally more demanding.

#### F3: Route 2 — Sealed-Tube ZnCl₂ Flux

Kinetics benefit strongly from molten ZnCl₂ flux, since excess ZnX₂ is explicitly advantageous for Zn₂NX single-crystal growth (CS4, SS1). The stoichiometric ZnO + ZnCl₂ reaction is direct [Reasonable Inference], but ordering into the target 2D Zn₂OCl₂ sheet may require partial reconstruction from ZnO-like and chloride-rich environments. Analogues require 20 h to 3 d holds, indicating that crystallization and defect/anion ordering are not instantaneous (CS3, CS4).

#### F2: Route 3 — Solution Crystallization

Room-temperature aqueous crystallization is very mild in TS1–TS3 and does not require high temperature, pressure, or inert atmosphere. The route still involves controlled hydrolysis, aging/evaporation, and isolation, so it receives a multi-step deduction. CS2 provides some chemical precedent for slow evaporation of a Cu–O–Cl-related phase, but the listed precursor details are not directly transferable to Zn₂OCl₂; therefore CS evidence is weak for this exact route.

#### F3: Route 3 — Solution Crystallization

Solution diffusion is excellent, and aqueous chloride phases crystallize readily in TS1–TS3. However, structural reconstruction into an anhydrous Zn₂OCl₂ layered solid is the major kinetic and phase-selectivity problem. TS2 and TS3 explicitly report difficulty obtaining single-phase products and simultaneous crystallization of multiple phases, which lowers confidence. Insufficient evidence from SS for room-temperature Zn₂OCl₂ solution crystallization is treated as neutral-to-negative for selectivity.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger. The described structure is a simple corner-sharing tetrahedral Zn–O–Cl sheet, and no edge/face-sharing coexistence is reported in the target description. The simple formula and 2D sheet motif suggest low amorphization tendency, although the exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given. CS5 and TS5 show that related 2D oxyhalide/oxoanion sheets can crystallize under hydrothermal conditions, supporting low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zn₂OCl₂ is likely a mildly metastable but accessible phase, because its E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, just inside the high-synthesizability range. The CS/SS/TS analogues collectively support synthesis: CS5 supports hydrothermal oxychloride formation, SS1/SS2 support Zn–Cl tetrahedral network chemistry, and TS5 supports hydrothermal crystallization of layered Zn–O compounds. The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive ZnCl₂ chemistry, either as an aqueous chloride source or as a molten self-flux, supported by ZnX₂-based syntheses in CS3/CS4/SS1/SS2. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity against ZnO, Zn(OH)₂, hydrated basic zinc chlorides, or chloride-rich salts [Reasonable Inference], especially because no source reports exact Zn₂OCl₂. The hydrothermal route is preferred because it balances low severity with good solution-mediated kinetics.
