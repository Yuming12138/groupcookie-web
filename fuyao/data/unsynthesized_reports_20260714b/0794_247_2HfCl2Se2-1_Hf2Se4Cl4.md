### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0210 eV/atom, which is in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, such as SrZrSe₃ at 0.027 eV/atom, EuZrSe₃ at 0.0247 eV/atom, Tl₂VNb₆Cl₁₈ at 0.0239 eV/atom, and Ce₃CuSnSe₇ at 0.0181 eV/atom, were all synthesized by conventional sealed-tube solid-state routes (TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Chemically close NbSe₂Cl₂ is on the hull and experimentally accessible by CVT (CS-2, SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that Hf(SeCl)₂ is slightly metastable rather than exactly on the hull, so competing Hf–Se and Hf–Cl phases may still form.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives deductions for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile halogen/chloride chemistry, and toxic/air-sensitive chlorine sources, all consistent with evacuated sealed-tube CVT used for NbSe₂Cl₂ and NbSeBr₃ (CS-2, SS-5, SS-4). The temperature itself is moderate, since close analogues form at 400–515 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS-2, SS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS for exact Hf–Se–Cl conditions, but TS-1 supports the need for chloride vapor-pressure control in related layered chlorides. The main uncertainty is ampoule pressure and chloride activity.

#### F3 — Route 1

CVT improves kinetics by transporting volatile halide species, as demonstrated for NbSe₂Cl₂ and related Nb halide chalcogenides (CS-2, SS-5, SS-4). The close structural match to NbSe₂Cl₂ and Nb(SBr)₂ supports feasible nucleation of the target layered motif (CS-2, SS-5, CS-3). However, Hf is refractory and may react slowly if introduced as metal **[Reasonable Inference]**, and NbX₂Y₂ analogues required weeks and careful gradients (CS-2, SS-5). Thus F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, because Nb–Se–Cl analogues were made near 477–530 °C in sealed tubes (SS-1, CS-4, SS-2). It still requires vacuum sealing, volatile chloride/chalcogen containment, and likely air-sensitive chloride precursors such as HfCl₄ **[Reasonable Inference]**, analogous to NbCl₄/NbCl₅ use in Nb–Se–Cl syntheses (SS-1, CS-4, SS-2). Multi-step burden is higher if HfSe₂ must be pre-synthesized or if regrinding/reannealing is required, as related selenide solid-state routes sometimes require reannealing (SS-3, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS specifically confirms HfSe₂ + HfCl₄ conversion to Hf(SeCl)₂.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state diffusion is likely the slowest route because Hf/HfSe₂ are refractory or dense precursors **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structurally related NbSeCl₃ was obtained only as a side product with inhomogeneous products and unidentified phases (SS-1), and Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ had poor reproducibility/narrow stability (CS-4, SS-2). Starting from HfSe₂ could reduce reconstruction relative to elements **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the final Se–Se bonded chloroselenide sheet still requires major rearrangement. Therefore F3 is the lowest among the three routes.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperature, supported by Nb(SBr)₂ formation at 500 °C in a sealed glass tube (CS-3). It is penalized for sealed volatile halide chemistry, likely vacuum/inert handling, flux removal, and toxicity if CdCl₂/Cd is used **[Reasonable Inference]**, based on Cd use in the Nb(SBr)₂ analogue (CS-3). Insufficient evidence from TS for flux-specific synthesis of Hf(SeCl)₂, but TS-1 supports volatile halide transport chemistry. The major condition risk is safe handling of volatile chlorides and toxic flux components.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux/mineralizer assistance should improve kinetics compared with direct solid-state reaction **[Reasonable Inference]**, and the closest same-space-group analogue Nb(SBr)₂ formed in only 2 days at 500 °C using Cd/Br₂ (CS-3). The structural reconstruction score is high because Nb(SBr)₂ shares the monoclinic C2/m layered arrangement, 8-coordinate metal center, chalcogen–chalcogen bond, and bridging halide environment with target Hf(SeCl)₂ (CS-3). The uncertainty is chemical transferability from Nb–S–Br to Hf–Se–Cl and the possibility of Cd/Hf chloride side reactions **[Reasonable Inference]**. This gives good kinetics but not the best overall route because F2 and chemical uncertainty remain significant.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any mixed-cation amorphization risk. The target structure is a simple layered HfSe₂Cl₂-type formula rather than a large cluster framework; close analogues NbSe₂Cl₂ and Nb(SBr)₂ are also layered/simple structures (CS-2, SS-5, CS-3). The target description does not indicate simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, unlike more complex cluster/ribbon systems in some references (CS-1, TS-4). The residual risk is not amorphization, but crystallizing the correct ordered layered polymorph.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(SeCl)₂ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.0210 eV/atom, but this is well within the range where crystalline compounds are commonly recoverable. The strongest support comes from NbSe₂Cl₂, a chemically and structurally close 2D selenide chloride synthesized by CVT (CS-2, SS-5), and Nb(SBr)₂, which shares the same monoclinic C2/m layered topology (CS-3). The most important enabling factor is halide-assisted transport, which can overcome slow Hf diffusion and promote crystal growth at moderate temperature, as seen in NbX₂Y₂ and related chloride systems (CS-2, SS-5, TS-1). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: related Nb–Se–Cl systems show polymorphism, narrow phase windows, side products, and poor reproducibility in some sealed-tube syntheses (CS-2, SS-5, SS-1, CS-4). Overall, the material is synthetically plausible, with CVT the most defensible first attempt.
