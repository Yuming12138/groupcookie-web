### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0360 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS-3 provides a closely thermodynamic analogue, Pd₂OCl₂, with E_hull = 0.03596 eV/atom and reported flux synthesis, supporting that oxyhalides at this metastability level can be isolated. CS evidence is mixed: CS-3–5 include stable E_hull = 0 compounds, while CS-1 has a higher E_hull = 0.174 eV/atom yet was synthesized by CVT. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the DFT hull captures the correct V/Os oxidation states and bromine chemical potential.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because CVT requires vacuum or sealed-tube handling, volatile Br₂, and toxic/air-sensitive halide precursors, all consistent with CS-1 and CS-3–5. Temperatures are moderate rather than extreme: CS-3–5 use 360–475 °C and SS-3 uses 550–650 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty applies. The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility and Br₂ pressure for Os-containing bromides, which is [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly documented in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

CVT provides high precursor activity through volatile bromide species, as demonstrated for NaW₂(OBr₃)₂ with Br₂ transport in CS-1 and related halide phases in CS-3–5. The structural reconstruction penalty is low because VOBr₂ in SS-1 already has the same type of VBr₄O₂ layer motif as the target. The limiting kinetic factor is incorporating Os into the same ordered sheet; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for V–Os cation ordering.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth avoids very high temperatures but still requires sealed conditions, bromine activity control, toxic Os reagents, and flux removal. SS-4 and TS-3 support flux growth of oxyhalide/thermodynamically similar phases, while CS-1 and CS-3–5 support bromide-rich sealed systems. The uncertainty is whether alkali bromide flux will dissolve enough OsO₂/vanadium oxide without forming competing alkali bromometalates.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, consistent with flux growth in SS-4 and TS-3. However, OsO₂ is expected to be refractory by physicochemical reasoning, and no CS/SS/TS source proves high solubility of Os oxides in bromide flux. Structural formation is plausible but not templated as directly as in Route 1, so F3 is lower.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest temperature and pressure conditions, supported by CS-2 HBr crystallization and TS-1/TS-5 low-temperature oxyhalide crystallization. It still requires corrosive HBr, possible sealed operation, and careful redox control; SS-1/SS-2 indicate vanadium oxyhalide solution work benefits from Ar/vacuum handling. The major uncertainty is not severity but phase selectivity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and low diffusion barriers, as shown by CS-2 and TS-1/TS-5. However, ordered 2D V–Os oxybromide sheet formation from solution is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction/ordering is the limiting term. This route may yield hydrated, molecular, or alkali-containing bromometalate phases rather than the target sheet.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple edge/corner-sharing octahedral sheet and no reported face-sharing, so the polyhedral-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered. The V/Os ionic radius mismatch is estimated below 15% for octahedral V/Os oxidation states by physicochemical inference, and the structural description suggests a relatively small ordered cell. The remaining uncertainty is that exact unit-cell atom count and oxidation-state assignment are not explicitly supplied by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS-1, which shows an almost directly matching VOBr₂ layer motif, and from CS-1/CS-3–5, which show that bromide-rich CVT can form layered oxyhalide/halide crystals. The most critical enabling factor is high bromine chemical potential in a sealed transport or flux environment, which should stabilize M–Br bonding and suppress oxide-only phases. The most critical limiting factor is controlled incorporation and ordering of Os with V in the same 2D oxybromide sheet; none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates this mixed V–Os chemistry. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sealed-tube optimization rather than a simple ceramic route.
