### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and scores in the 90–100 range. CS evidence is mostly supportive because CS1 and CS5 are on hull and CS4 is nearly on hull, although CS3 is high above hull and therefore contradictory. SS/TS evidence is strongly supportive because SS1/TS1, SS4/TS2, TS3, TS4, and TS5 include stable halide phases; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact ZnBrCl phase, but the thermodynamic signal is favorable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed handling, multi-step loading/sealing/heating, and moisture-sensitive halide precursors, all of which are consistent with CS1 and SS5. It is not penalized for high temperature or high pressure because analogous halide syntheses occur at 250–600 °C without high pressure (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS2, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is the vapor pressure of ZnCl2/ZnBr2 at the selected temperature, for which CS/SS/TS imply sealed systems but do not quantify losses.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because ZnCl2/ZnBr2 are low-melting halides and analogous halide melts crystallize at moderate temperature (SS1/TS1, SS2). SS5 directly supports Zn halide tetrahedral chemistry, while CS1 supports reactive ZnX2 flux behavior. The main uncertainty is whether Cl/Br ordering into the orthorhombic Pmc2_1 sheet occurs spontaneously or requires a narrow thermal window.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1, with additional flux-removal handling supported by CS1. CS1 explicitly used vacuum, glovebox handling, excess ZnX2 flux, and acetone washing; these justify deductions for controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile components, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive precursors. No CS/SS/TS evidence suggests extreme temperature or high pressure, so no such penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because excess ZnX2 provides a liquid medium and CS1 states that excess zinc halide is advantageous for single-crystal growth. SS1/TS1 and SS2 further support slow cooling from halide melts as a route to ordered halide crystals. The uncertainty is whether the flux composition biases toward ZnCl2-rich or ZnBr2-rich impurity phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is moderately severe because CS4 and TS4/TS5 require evacuated sealed ampoules, temperature gradients, and multi-step ampoule handling. TS3 shows sublimation growth can be mild in temperature, so no high-temperature penalty applies. The main risk is sealed-ampoule pressure and differential volatility of ZnCl2/ZnBr2, which is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly measured in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport can overcome diffusion limitations, as shown by CS4 and TS4/TS5. However, TS4/TS5 report phase-control difficulty, low yields, and sensitivity for some halide transport systems, so structural reconstruction and composition control are not as favorable as in self-flux growth. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct ZnBrCl vapor transport, so the score is conservative.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is mild in temperature because CS3 and SS3 demonstrate solution crystallization at ambient or unspecified mild conditions. It is still penalized for anhydrous/inert handling because Zn halides are moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/SS5 show dry handling is important in Zn halide chemistry. Direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for anhydrous ZnBrCl solution crystallization is insufficient.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution kinetics are favorable for ion transport, as solution crystallization avoids solid-state diffusion and is supported generally by CS3 and SS3. The structural pathway is weak, because CS3 gives molecular/ammine-type crystals and SS3 gives an aqueous Hg chloride, neither demonstrating an anhydrous 2D Zn mixed-halide network. The major uncertainty is solvate/hydrate formation rather than crystallization rate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation and a simple corner-sharing tetrahedral network, so none of the three amorphization checks is triggered by the target description. SS5 shows more complex corner/edge sharing in Li2ZnBr4, but the target itself is described only as corner-sharing ZnBr2Cl2 tetrahedra. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior of ZnBrCl, so the low-risk assignment is based primarily on the target’s simple connectivity and small chemistry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZnBrCl is likely a **ground-state** phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and this is consistent with multiple stable halide analogues in CS/SS/TS. The analogue support is good: CS1 directly supports ZnX2 flux chemistry, SS5 directly supports ZnCl2/ZnBr2 halogenozincate synthesis, and SS1/TS1 plus SS2 support slow cooling from halide melts. The most important enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility of ZnCl2/ZnBr2 molten/flux media, which should allow crystallization without refractory diffusion barriers. The key limiting factor is stoichiometry control under sealed conditions, because ZnCl2 and ZnBr2 are hygroscopic/volatile [Reasonable Inference] and Cl/Br ratio drift could produce competing zinc halide phases. Overall, self-flux growth is the most credible route to phase-pure or single-crystalline ZnBrCl.
