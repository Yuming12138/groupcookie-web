### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

CuHgBr₃ has E_hull = 0.0150 eV/atom, so it is close to the convex hull and falls in the highest scoring range. CS supports the plausibility of low-energy heavy-metal halides because TlAlBr/TlCuCl-related phases are stable or nearly stable (CS2, CS4), while SS/TS support it through synthesized Cu/Hg and Hg-halide/chalcogenide analogues at 0.010–0.031 eV/atom (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that 0.015 eV/atom may still correspond to a metastable phase rather than the true ground state, and the database energy does not by itself guarantee kinetic accessibility.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to 200–280 °C Hg–Br sealed-tube synthesis (CS5, SS3) and 553–573 K CuHgSX evacuated-tube synthesis (SS4, TS3, TS4), so no high-temperature deduction is needed. The main deductions are for vacuum, volatile HgBr₂/possible Br₂, and Hg toxicity; sealed-tube handling is directly supported by CS5/SS3 and SS4/TS3/TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact vapor pressure of CuHgBr₃ itself, so volatility control is inferred from HgBr₂ chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Halide precursors should be substantially more reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and related halides/chalcogenide-halides form by solid-state heating in sealed tubes (CS5, SS3, SS4, TS3, TS4). However, the target requires formation of a 2D Cu–Hg–Br framework from binary halides, so structural reconstruction is nontrivial. The long 6-day to 2-week anneals in CS5/SS3 and SS4/TS3/TS4 lower the kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature and external high pressure, consistent with hydrothermal CuHgSBr synthesis at 670 K for 24 h (SS1, TS5). The penalties come from sealed acid pressure, toxic mercury bromide, and multi-step hydrothermal handling. Insufficient evidence from CS for hydrothermal CuHgBr₃ specifically is noted; the main support is SS1/TS5.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrothermal HBr provides solution-mediated transport, so ion diffusion should be faster than in a dry powder compact [Reasonable Inference]. The strong positive evidence is that CuHgSBr crystals form in concentrated HBr/HX at 670 K with controlled cooling and no observed side products (SS1, TS5). The uncertainty is that the target lacks sulfide, so the solution speciation and nucleation pathway may differ from the CuHgSBr precedent.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires evacuated ampoules and volatile/toxic Hg halides, as indicated by Hg mixed-halide/chalcogenide gas-phase growth in evacuated quartz ampoules (SS5, TS1). The route is penalized for vacuum, volatile HgBr₂ or Br₂, toxicity, and the need for a thermal gradient. Insufficient evidence from CS for CuHgBr₃ CVT specifically makes this route more uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hg halide vapor transport is plausible because Hg-containing mixed halides/chalcogenides can be grown from the gas phase in evacuated ampoules (SS5, TS1). The limitation is that CuBr is less volatile than HgBr₂ [Reasonable Inference], which may cause composition gradients and off-stoichiometry. No SS/TS/CS source confirms direct crystallization of CuHgBr₃ from vapor, so crystallization-pathway confidence is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Low-temperature melt growth is supported by TlCuCl₃ Bridgman synthesis at 420–450 °C under Ar (CS4) and low-temperature heavy halide reactions at 150–200 °C (CS2). The route avoids extreme T/P but still requires sealed or controlled-atmosphere handling because HgBr₂ is volatile and toxic [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a direct CuHgBr₃ melt route leaves some uncertainty in the decomposition window.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

A molten halide environment should provide excellent mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and CS4 shows that a Cu-containing heavy-metal halide can be grown by Bridgman with controlled growth rate. The kinetic score is high, but not maximal, because composition control with volatile HgBr₂ is likely narrow [Reasonable Inference]. Phase selection may require slow cooling, as high-quality TlCuCl₃ crystals in CS4 required optimized temperature and growth rate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one triggered amorphization check due to the large Cu/Hg cation-size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference], while the target description does not indicate >40 atoms per cell or face-sharing plus edge/corner-sharing coexistence. SS and TS analogues show that Cu/Hg mixed-anion frameworks can crystallize successfully (SS1, SS4, TS3, TS5), reducing concern. Remaining uncertainty comes from the unusual 2D CuHgBr₃ sheet topology and possible metastability at only 0.015 eV/atom above hull.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuHgBr₃ is likely a **near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is only 0.015 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong overall: CS demonstrates accessible low-temperature heavy-metal halide chemistry, SS gives close Cu–Hg–halide/chalcogenide structural precedents, and TS shows that near-hull Cu/Hg halide-related phases are experimentally synthesizable. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of bromide/mercury-halide chemistry in sealed or hydrothermal environments, supported by CS5/SS3 and SS1/TS5. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under conditions where HgBr₂ and Br-containing species are volatile, toxic, and pressure-generating [Reasonable Inference]. Hydrothermal HBr synthesis is the best first route because it combines high transport kinetics with direct Cu–Hg–Br precedent from CuHgSBr (SS1, TS5).
