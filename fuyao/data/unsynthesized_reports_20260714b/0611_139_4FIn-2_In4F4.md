### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0370 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring band and is comparable to synthesizable metastable examples: AgO at 0.047 eV/atom (CS1), h-In₂O₃ at 0.0207 eV/atom (CS3), PbSnF₄ at 0.0318 eV/atom (TS2), and Sn₂F₃I at 0.0036 eV/atom (SS4). This supports a high but not perfect score because it is likely near-hull/metastable rather than strongly ground-state. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for the exact In–F phase diagram, so F1 is based mainly on the provided hull energy plus analogue metastability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Sealed solid-state synthesis uses moderate temperatures, consistent with Sn₂OF₂ at 200 °C in a sealed tube (SS5) and PbSnF₄ at 473–548 K in Au tubes under vacuum/Ar (TS2). The severity penalty is mainly from controlled dry/vacuum handling, sealed fluoride containment, and possible multi-step regrinding/annealing, all supported by TS2 and TS4. **CS gives insufficient direct fluoride solid-state signal**, so CS is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetically, binary fluorides can react at modest temperatures in sealed systems, as shown by SS5 and TS2. In metal should enable relatively fast transport because of its low melting point, but that is a [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the orthorhombic 1D ribbon, since TS2 reports reproducibility and phase competition issues in related fluorides.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Solvothermal conditions are mild in temperature because ASnF₃ analogues grow at 150 °C for 24 h (SS1, SS3). Deductions arise from sealed autoclave pressure, dry handling, multiple workup steps, and moisture-sensitive fluoride chemistry; SS1/SS3 explicitly note solvothermal autoclaves and phase/moisture issues. **TS gives only indirect support** through dry fluoride handling in TS2 and TS4, not through solvothermal synthesis.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution-mediated fluoride crystallization has favorable kinetics because it bypasses slow solid diffusion; SS1/SS3 and SS4 show low-dimensional fluoride crystals can form from solution or solvothermal media. This justifies the highest F3 among the routes. The uncertainty is chemical rather than kinetic: In(I) may disproportionate or oxidize, and direct InF solvothermal synthesis is not present in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Thin-film deposition avoids high temperature and high pressure, but CS4 shows vacuum deposition requires specialized vacuum conditions and gives phase mixtures. Fluorine incorporation would require volatile/corrosive F sources, which is a [Reasonable Inference]. **SS/TS provide insufficient direct vapor-deposition signal**, so the route is penalized for uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
CS4 supports the general idea that vacuum deposition can trap metastable phases, but it also reports formation of multiple phases including metals and oxides. For InF, composition control is expected to be difficult because F volatility and In-rich segregation are likely [Reasonable Inference]. Thus vapor deposition is plausible but less kinetically reliable than solvothermal or sealed solid-state synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is absent by definition. The described Pmma structure has one inequivalent In and F and a simple 1D ribbon, so the atom-count and mixed-connectivity amorphization checks are not triggered; this small-cell inference is based on the target description. Remaining uncertainty is that 1D materials can crystallize as small or defective domains, but the formal amorphization score is low-risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InF is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than an unambiguously ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.037 eV/atom. The analogue set supports synthesis of low-dimensional fluorides by solvothermal methods (SS1, SS3, SS4), sealed-tube reactions (SS5), and dry ampoule solid-state methods (TS2, SS2/TS1), but no reference directly demonstrates In(I) fluoride formation. The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature fluoride chemistry: structurally similar Sn(II) fluorides crystallize under mild conditions, and avoiding high temperature should help preserve a metastable In(I) phase. The most critical limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control of In(I), because In metal, InF₃, and possible competing In-rich or In(III) fluoride phases may coexist. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, but phase-pure bulk material will likely require careful sealed, dry, fluoride-rich, low-temperature conditions.
