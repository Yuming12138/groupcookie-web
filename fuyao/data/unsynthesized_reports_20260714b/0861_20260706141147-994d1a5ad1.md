### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is scored as a ground-state phase by the required rubric. This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues BaSn(GeO₃)₃, Ba₂MgGe₂O₇, and Ba₂ZnGe₂O₇, all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a mixed oxyselenide, while most SS/TS analogues are oxide germanates, so thermodynamic stability does not guarantee easy kinetic access [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed-tube flux

Route 1 inherits moderate condition severity from CS flux syntheses at 850 °C for 7 days in evacuated silica tubes (CS-1, CS-2). The major penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Se requiring containment, and toxic/air-sensitive Se/BaSe handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids >1000 °C treatment, which is favorable relative to oxide ceramic analogues reaching 1330 °C (SS-1, TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed-tube flux

Flux improves transport and is directly supported by NaCl flux examples in chemically similar systems (CS-1, CS-2). GeO₂-based Ba germanates are well established in SS/TS data, but the target requires Ge–Se incorporation and mixed O/Se ordering not demonstrated in those oxide analogues (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The kinetic score is therefore good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal / solvothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis uses lower temperatures than solid-state or flux routes, with CS examples at 500 K and 375 °C (CS-3, CS-5). It avoids vacuum and >1000 °C penalties, but receives deductions for sealed volatile Se chemistry, multistep handling, and toxic/air-sensitive selenide precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 shows that Ba mixed-anion compounds can require pressurized autoclave containment, but this is not >5 GPa and therefore is not penalized as high-pressure by the rubric.

#### F3 — Route 2 hydrothermal / solvothermal

Hydrothermal media can dissolve and transport Ba, Ge, and chalcogen species more effectively than dry powders [Reasonable Inference], consistent with crystal formation in CS-3 and CS-5. The route is kinetically attractive because it reduces solid-state diffusion barriers. The main uncertainty is redox control of selenium and the lack of a direct SS/TS hydrothermal Ba–Ge–Se–O analogue; CS-5 also warns of byproducts and low yields.

#### F2 — Route 3 sealed-tube solid-state

The route is supported indirectly by sealed ampoule syntheses for chemically similar oxyhalides/chalcogen-related materials (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4) and by ceramic Ba/Ge oxide syntheses (SS-1, TS-1). It is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Se, multistep prereaction/annealing, and toxic or air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids ultra-high temperature if preformed selenides are used, but this lower temperature may worsen kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 sealed-tube solid-state

The kinetics are the weakest because dry solid-state reaction must form a mixed-anion tetrahedral framework from oxide/selenide precursors. SS/TS oxide germanates often require high temperatures up to 1330 °C in air (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-5), while the Se-containing target cannot simply be treated under oxidizing air at those temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. Complete reconstruction and Se ordering reduce the kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 4 single-crystal flux growth after prereaction

Flux growth is supported by SS-2 and TS-2, but the target’s Se content requires sealed/inert adaptation [Reasonable Inference]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaGe₂SeO₄ is likely a **ground-state phase** because the target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS Ba–Ge oxide analogues that demonstrate stable Ba–Ge–O frameworks and from CS sealed-tube or hydrothermal mixed-anion syntheses that demonstrate practical access to complex oxyhalide/chalcogenide-oxide materials. The most critical enabling factor is the strong compatibility of Ba–O coordination and GeO₄ tetrahedral chemistry with known Ba germanate frameworks (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the correct selenium chemical potential and redox state so that Se enters as Ge–Se/Ba–Se rather than being lost, oxidized, or segregated as binary selenide [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, hydrothermal/solvothermal synthesis is the best first route for phase discovery, while sealed flux growth is the best follow-up for crystals.
