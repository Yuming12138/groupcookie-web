### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the upper edge of the most favorable scoring bin and suggests possible metastability rather than guaranteed ground-state stability. CS-1 and CS-2 show chemically similar Ag oxides with very low hull energies, supporting the idea that Ag-containing oxyanion frameworks can be stable or nearly stable. TS-2, TS-4, and TS-5 show experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues around 0.054–0.067 eV/atom, so the target’s 0.050 eV/atom is within an experimentally accessible metastability window. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity does not specify the exact competing phases in the Ti–Ag–Sb–O system.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature, vacuum, and GPa pressure, consistent with CS-2 hydrothermal synthesis at 230 °C. The main deductions are for sealed-vessel operation with volatile/decomposable Ag species and toxic Sb oxides [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1/CS-2 support hydrothermal Ag oxide formation, but neither source contains Ti, so precursor reactivity remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal solution transport gives favorable kinetics because CS-2 forms crystalline Ag–V–P oxide at 230 °C and CS-1 uses water as mineralizer. TiO2 is less reactive than V2O5, so hydrated TiO2 or peroxide-assisted Ti chemistry is recommended [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is the main kinetic penalty because no CS/SS/TS analogue gives the exact P1 Ti2AgSbO6 framework.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because sealed ampoules, intermediate grinding, and volatile Ag/Sb control are likely needed [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 and SS-3 show that structurally similar oxides can form at 700–850 °C or 800 °C, but SS-2/SS-3 also show multi-step heating and regrinding. The route is feasible but operationally less attractive than hydrothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state kinetics are limited by TiO2 diffusion and complete reconstruction from binary oxides [Reasonable Inference]. SS-3 used ball milling and calcination, showing that mechanical activation helps related oxide formation. However, Ag-containing phases may decompose or lose Ag during prolonged firing, so the kinetic score is only moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux synthesis uses moderate temperatures but likely requires sealed evacuated or controlled tubes, as in SS-4. Toxic Sb precursors and flux washing add practical complexity [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is comparable to sealed-tube solid-state synthesis, though the lower effective kinetic barrier makes it more promising.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux improves mass transport and crystal growth, which is supported by SS-4 for complex triclinic oxides. The liquid flux can reduce the impact of refractory TiO2 compared with dry ceramic routes [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no provided source demonstrates Ag–Ti–Sb oxide flux growth directly, so composition control may require screening.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 inherits the strongest chemical precedent from CS-1 but requires high-pressure O2 autoclave equipment. The rubric does not penalize MPa O2 as GPa pressure, but practical difficulty remains high because CS-1 used 40–55 MPa oxygen. Toxic Sb oxide handling and sealed high-pressure operation reduce the score.

#### F3 — Route 4

CS-1 directly supports high-O2 hydrothermal/mineralizer synthesis for Ag–transition-metal–pnictogen oxides, giving strong kinetic confidence. High oxygen pressure should suppress Ag reduction and can stabilize oxidized transition-metal oxide networks, as in CS-1. The main uncertainty is substitution of V/As chemistry by less soluble Ti/Sb chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has multiple cations with different coordination preferences: Ti forms TiO4 tetrahedra, Ag is T-shaped 3-coordinate, and Sb is see-saw-like 4-coordinate in the target description. That size/coordination mismatch increases the chance of local disorder or amorphous products [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target description reports a small, fully ordered P1 framework with corner-sharing TiO4 units and no edge/face-sharing complexity, so amorphization risk is not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2AgSbO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data support synthesis most strongly because Ag-containing oxyanion materials were made hydrothermally or under high oxygen pressure from simple oxides (CS-1, CS-2). The SS data support the broader feasibility of corner-sharing mixed-polyhedral oxides by ceramic or flux methods, but they are less chemically specific to Ag–Ti–Sb. The key enabling factor is enhanced mass transport under hydrothermal/mineralizer or flux conditions, which helps overcome TiO2 sluggishness. The key limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ag volatility/reduction and Sb oxidation state while crystallizing a low-symmetry P1 ordered phase.
