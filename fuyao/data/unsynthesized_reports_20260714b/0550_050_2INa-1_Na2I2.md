### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull = 0.042 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. CS2/TS1 show a LiI polymorph with E_hull ≈0.0289 eV/atom made by cryogenic vapor deposition, and SS1/TS3 show a low-hull iodide compound made by melt growth, supporting accessibility of low-hull halide phases. The uncertainty is that low hull does not guarantee room-temperature persistence, as CS1/SS4 and CS2/TS1 report metastable LiI modifications requiring low-temperature handling.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, cryogenic vapor deposition.**  
Condition severity is high because CS1/SS4 and CS2/TS1 require high vacuum, cryogenic substrates, dried precursors, and specialized in-situ/controlled handling; these justify the F2 deductions. Kinetically, this route is favorable because CS1/SS4 and CS2/TS1 directly demonstrate vapor condensation of alkali iodides into metastable polymorphs. The main uncertainty is structural transferability: those LiI analogues are hexagonal/wurtzite-like, while the target is tetragonal P4/nmm NaI.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, sealed melt growth.**  
SS1/TS3 and TS2 support sealed ampoule melt/slow-cooling of iodide/bromide halides under Ar or vacuum, so the route is experimentally straightforward but still penalized for sealed/controlled atmosphere and volatile halides. Kinetics are good in the molten state, but structural reconstruction is unfavorable because slow cooling generally promotes equilibrium phases, while CS1/CS2/TS1 imply metastable alkali iodide polymorphs need kinetic trapping. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly shows binary NaI P4/nmm can be obtained by melt cooling.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux/quench.**  
CS4/CS5, SS1/TS3, and TS2 show that halide/chalcogenide flux or melt methods can produce crystalline ionic compounds at moderate temperatures using sealed ampoules and slow cooling. The flux route improves diffusion and may allow lower-temperature nucleation, so F3 is slightly higher than direct melt. However, phase control is uncertain because CS4/CS5 report by-product/competing phase considerations in flux growth, and no source directly shows a tetragonal binary NaI polymorph from flux.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation and a simple binary composition, so cation-size disorder is not expected; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from the target description, with insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target-specific disorder. The target description reports edge and corner sharing, not face sharing, so the specified mixed vertex+edge+face amorphization trigger is not met. Remaining uncertainty is that low-temperature vapor deposition can produce small crystallites or strained material, as CS2/TS1 notes broadened diffraction lines for low-temperature alkali halide deposits.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target NaI phase is likely metastable rather than a robust ground-state bulk phase because its E_hull is positive at 0.042 eV/atom and because CS1/SS4 and CS2/TS1 show nonstandard alkali iodide polymorphs can require cryogenic deposition and may transform toward stable cubic phases. The CS/TS evidence is chemically strong for alkali iodide polymorph trapping, especially from LiI vapor-deposition analogues, while SS/TS evidence supports sealed halide melt/flux handling but not directly this P4/nmm binary NaI structure. The most critical enabling factor is kinetic control of nucleation, either by cryogenic vapor deposition or rapid quenching from a flux, supported by the low-temperature deposition behavior in CS1/SS4 and CS2/TS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with ordinary NaI polymorphs, particularly during warming or slow cooling, inferred from metastable LiI transformations in CS/TS. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires screening multiple kinetic-trapping conditions rather than a simple equilibrium solid-state reaction.
