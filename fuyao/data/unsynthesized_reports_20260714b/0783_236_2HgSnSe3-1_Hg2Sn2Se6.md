### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0410 eV/atom, placing it below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore in the 90–100 scoring band. This suggests the phase is close to thermodynamic stability and may be experimentally accessible as a near-ground-state or mildly metastable compound. Similar thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values of ~0.018–0.038 eV/atom were synthesized by direct solid-state methods (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5), supporting accessibility of slightly above-hull chalcogenides. The uncertainty is that E_hull does not guarantee selection of the specific P-31m layered polymorph over competing HgSe/SnSe₂/SnSe phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for vacuum sealing, volatile Hg/Se components, toxicity, and multi-step annealing. These penalties are supported by Hg-containing analogue syntheses in evacuated quartz containers and long annealing schedules (CS1, SS5, TS2). The route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which improves severity relative to direct fusion routes such as TS3. Remaining uncertainty is the optimal temperature window for preserving the layered P-31m phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

HgSe + Sn + Se should have better kinetic compatibility than refractory oxide precursors because the system contains soft chalcogenides and low-melting elements [Reasonable Inference], and HgSe-based syntheses are directly used in SS5 and TS2. Long 250 h anneals in SS5/TS2 indicate diffusion and ordering are not instantaneous, but are manageable. Structural reconstruction is the weak point because SS5 and TS2 are stannite-like/tetrahedral analogues, whereas the target contains SnSe₆ octahedra and Hg-containing trigonal pyramids. Thus, kinetics are moderate rather than easy.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses harsher temperatures, though still below the 1000 °C deduction threshold if limited to 850–950 °C. The need for evacuated ampoules is supported by CS1, TS3, and TS4, and volatile Hg/Se control follows from CS1, SS5, and TS2. Multi-step staged heating is justified by TS4 and by the need to avoid ampoule overpressure [Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is whether high temperature will decompose or transform the desired layered structure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

High-temperature direct fusion improves transport and can form multinary selenides, as shown by CS1, TS3, and TS4. However, CS1 required 4–5 weeks for Hg-containing chalcogenides, and TS3 reported a secondary GeSe₂ impurity, demonstrating phase-purity challenges. The target’s layered octahedral/pyramidal topology is structurally distinct from many SS/TS stannite analogues, so high-T synthesis may produce competing denser phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are adequate for reaction completion but uncertain for phase selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the same sealed-vacuum, volatile-component, toxicity, and multi-step penalties as Routes 1–2. Iodine transport is directly supported in related quaternary chalcogenides by TS4, and two-zone chalcogen control is supported by CS2. The added iodine does not increase formal high-pressure or >1000 °C severity, but it increases chemical complexity [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly confirms iodine transport for HgSnSe₃, making this an exploratory route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Iodine can enhance mass transport, as shown by TS4, and may improve crystallinity relative to simple solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, Hg and Sn iodides are plausible side products under transport conditions [Reasonable Inference], and no direct Hg–Sn–Se transport analogue is provided by CS/SS/TS. The phase-selectivity score is therefore lower than the transport advantage alone would suggest. This route is useful for crystal growth screening but not the safest first synthesis attempt.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a significant cation coordination mismatch: Hg forms HgHgSe₃ trigonal pyramids while Sn forms SnSe₆ octahedra, increasing disorder/amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The structure also contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, which can complicate crystallization relative to simple tetrahedral stannites. The likely small P-31m cell avoids the >40 atom penalty [Reasonable Inference]. The resulting risk is medium, consistent with the need for long anneals shown in SS5 and TS2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgSnSe₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.041 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support that Hg–Se–main-group chalcogenides can be made in sealed evacuated ampoules, especially using HgSe as a controlled Hg source (CS1, SS5, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the use of HgSe plus long low-to-moderate-temperature annealing, which helps retain Hg and allows slow cation/anion ordering (SS5, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: many close analogues are tetrahedral stannite-like phases, while the target requires a layered P-31m framework with SnSe₆ octahedra and Hg-containing trigonal pyramids. Therefore, the material is synthesizable with moderate difficulty, but phase-pure bulk synthesis will likely require careful temperature, stoichiometry, and annealing optimization.
