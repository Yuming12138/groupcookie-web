### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0240 eV/atom, which lies well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom “highly synthesizable/metastable” window. Similar low-hull phases are experimentally reported: InGaS3 at 0.0172 eV/atom by CVT (CS2, TS5), TlInS2 at 0.0155 eV/atom by Bridgman (SS4, TS4), and Li2In2GeS6 with low/zero effective ground-state offset by sealed-tube reaction (TS3). The main uncertainty is that low hull does not guarantee the specific P3m1 polymorph rather than a nearby In–Ga–S competing phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT

CVT conditions are moderately severe because analogues require evacuated ampoules, iodine, temperature gradients, and long runs (CS2, SS1, SS3, TS5). Kinetically, CVT is favorable because vapor transport bypasses slow bulk diffusion and has already crystallized layered GaS, ZnIn2S4, and InGaS3 analogues (SS1, SS3, CS2/TS5). The remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS2/TS5 demonstrate InGaS3 rather than InGaS2, so sulfur chemical potential must be tuned.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: sealed solid-state

Solid-state synthesis is supported by evacuated-tube sulfide reactions at 600–1000 °C and by long annealing protocols for phase-pure products (CS3, CS4, SS2, TS3). Its severity is moderate due to vacuum sealing and sulfur volatility, but it avoids iodine and specialized CVT gradient optimization. Kinetics are less favorable than CVT because the target layered P3m1 structure must form by solid-state diffusion and cation ordering, and analogues often need repeated heating or long annealing (CS3, SS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: melt/Bridgman

Melt growth is strongly supported for chalcogenide crystals such as CuInS2 by Bridgman from pure elements (CS1), and TlInS2 is reported by Bridgman (SS4, TS4). Kinetics are good because a melt provides high mobility, but conditions are severe due to sulfur vapor pressure, sealed ampoules, and probable temperatures around or above 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference supported by CS4/SS2 high-temperature sealed ampoules]. The key uncertainty is whether the target P3m1 phase crystallizes congruently or instead decomposes/peritectically forms another In–Ga–S phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: solvothermal + anneal

Solvothermal synthesis has mild conditions and highly reactive precursors, as shown for Cu2SnS3 from metal chlorides and thiourea at 453 K for 20 h (CS5). However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that solvothermal chemistry directly yields crystalline layered InGaS2, so the structural-reconstruction score is low. This route is better viewed as a way to prepare reactive amorphous/nanocrystalline In–Ga–S precursor powder for subsequent annealing rather than as a definitive direct synthesis route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only two cations and a small layered formula, so no large-cell amorphization penalty is evident from the target description. The main amorphization/ordering concern is the significant Ga³⁺/In³⁺ ionic size mismatch, which triggers one risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii]. Mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not described for the target, unlike more complex analogues with edge-sharing octahedra such as InGaS3 (CS2/TS5), so F4 remains favorable.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InGaS2 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0240 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is good: direct In–Ga–S CVT evidence exists for InGaS3 (CS2, TS5), and multiple layered sulfides are grown by CVT or sealed ampoule methods (SS1, SS3, CS3, CS4). The most important enabling factor is the soft chalcogenide chemistry: low-melting In/Ga and sulfur/iodine vapor transport allow crystallization without refractory diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1, SS1, SS3]. The main limiting factor is phase competition and sulfur chemical potential control, because the closest direct chemical analogue is InGaS3 rather than InGaS2 (CS2, TS5). Overall, CVT or carefully controlled sealed-ampoule annealing should be feasible, with CVT favored for single crystals and phase selection.
