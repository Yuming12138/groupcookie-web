### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0270 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score. Thermodynamic analogues include Hf2N2S at 0.021 eV/atom (TS1) and TaON at 0.0386 eV/atom (TS3), both in a similar metastable-but-accessible range. However, TS1 and TS3 also indicate that phases in this energy range can require careful kinetic control, so high thermodynamic plausibility does not guarantee easy synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from the very close α-HfNBr structural analogue synthesized simply from Hf + NH4Br at 760 °C (SS1). The major penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube handling and volatile/reactive bromide or azide components, as similar nitride halide syntheses use evacuated silica ampoules and NaN3/NH4X sources (CS1–3, SS1, TS2). Kinetically, direct reaction is limited by refractory Hf/Sc nitrides or oxides [Reasonable Inference], and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that Sc/O can be incorporated by a one-pot reaction.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Flux growth is strongly supported by CS1/CS3, where NaCl or excess metal chloride flux secures complete, fast reaction and single crystals, and by TS2, where NaBr flux gives bromide nitride single crystals at 900 °C. The route still requires sealed evacuated ampoules and toxic or moisture-sensitive reagents such as NaN3 and anhydrous bromides (CS1–3, TS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically, flux transport should improve cation/anion mobility relative to dry solid-state reaction, but exact Sc/Hf/O/N/Br ordering remains unproven by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

The topochemical route is attractive because α-HfNBr is nearly isostructural in local Hf–N–Br coordination to the target (SS1), and Hf2N2S can be made from α-HfNBr by topochemical reaction at 750 °C under vacuum (SS4/TS1). The severity is moderate because it needs vacuum sealing, pre-synthesis of α-HfNBr, and careful heating; SS4/TS1 explicitly show phase purity depends on rapid heating and temperature control. The uncertainty is high because Sc incorporation and oxygen incorporation require more structural modification than the demonstrated α-HfNBr → Hf2N2S transformation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

High-pressure nitride-halide synthesis is well documented for β-HfNBr, β-ZrNBr, and related β-MNCl phases at 3–5 GPa and 900–1200 °C (SS2, SS3, SS5). The rubric does not penalize pressure unless >5 GPa, but experimentally this route is challenging because it needs multianvil equipment, noble-metal capsules, BN crucibles, and quenching, all reported in SS2–SS5. The structural transferability is weaker because high pressure favors β 7-coordinate polymorphs, while the target is closer to the 6-coordinate α-HfNBr motif from SS1.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only Sc and Hf as cations, and their likely 6-coordinate radii are similar enough that a severe cation-size amorphization trigger is not expected [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct radius data. The formula contains 12 atoms, and the provided structure description does not prove a >40 atom unit cell, so the atom-count trigger is not applied. The target has corner and edge sharing but no stated face sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.027 eV/atom, similar to accessible metastable analogues such as Hf2N2S (TS1) and TaON (TS3). The structural analogue support is strong: α-HfNBr has nearly identical Hf–N and Hf–Br bond lengths and the same layered nitride bromide motif (SS1). The chemical/flux support is moderate, because CS1–3 and TS2 show that multicomponent nitride halides can be crystallized in evacuated ampoules using halide fluxes, but they do not contain the exact Sc–Hf–O–N–Br chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a reactive bromide/halide flux or topochemical environment that permits crystallization below the decomposition/volatilization range. The most critical limiting factor is ordered incorporation of Sc and O into a HfNBr-like layered framework without forming competing HfNBr, Sc2O3/ScN, HfO2, or binary bromides.
