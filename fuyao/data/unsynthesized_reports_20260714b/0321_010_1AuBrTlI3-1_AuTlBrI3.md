### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0080 eV/atom, which is strongly favorable under the provided rubric and comparable to near-hull analogues such as CS2, CS5, TS1, and TS3. SS2 and SS3 are 0D Indium-like iodide clusters with E_hull = 0, supporting the stability of similar discrete halide cluster motifs. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or I/Br site ordering.

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
CS2 demonstrates a mild hydrothermal condition, 160 °C for 3 h in concentrated HI, which strongly reduces thermal severity. The score is lowered for sealed acidic/volatile halide media and toxic Tl chemistry, both inferred from CS2 and general Tl/halide handling. TS provides insufficient useful hydrothermal-condition evidence for Tl–Au mixed halides and is treated as neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Solution transport in HI/HBr should make ion mobility high, supported by CS2’s short hydrothermal synthesis and SS4’s Au iodide hydrothermal crystallization. The 0D cluster target likely assembles from solvated Tl⁺ and Au–halide complexes rather than requiring long-range solid diffusion. The uncertainty is whether Au remains in the required oxidation state and whether Br statistically disorders or orders onto the single Br site.

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
SS2–SS3 and CS1 show sealed ampoule/CVT conditions at moderate temperatures, but they require vacuum/Ar handling, volatile iodine/halogen transport agents, and careful sealing. These conditions are more demanding than hydrothermal Route 1 but not extreme because no high pressure or >1000 °C heating is needed. TS4 supports gas-phase growth in evacuated ampoules but lacks detailed temperature information, so it is supportive only qualitatively.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport should enhance precursor activity because halogenated Au/Tl species can move through the ampoule, analogous to SS2–SS3 iodide cluster growth. Structural similarity is high because SS2–SS3 are Indium-like 0D iodide clusters, close to the target’s Indium-derived 0D description. The main kinetic risk is controlled incorporation of exactly one Br per Au center.

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
CS4, CS5, and TS5 show that sealed-tube halide solid-state or melt-assisted routes are feasible at moderate temperatures, but vacuum sealing and toxic/volatile halides produce severity deductions. Long anneals are sometimes required in TS3 and TS5, making the practical burden higher than Route 1. SS offers insufficient direct evidence for this exact solid-state route and is neutral-to-cautious.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Binary halides are more reactive than oxides, supported by TS1–TS5, but solid-state ordering of a 0D mixed-halide Au complex may be slower than solution or vapor assembly. TS3 and TS5 report very long anneals, indicating possible slow equilibration in related halide systems. Kinetics are therefore acceptable but less favorable than hydrothermal or CVT routes.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The large Tl/Au cation size difference triggers one amorphization-risk criterion by ionic-radius reasoning. However, the target is 0D with a small described formula unit and lacks mixed edge/vertex/face-sharing polyhedral networks, so only one of three checks is triggered. SS2–SS3 show discrete 0D halide clusters can crystallize, reducing concern despite cation-size mismatch.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlAuI₃Br is likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0080 eV/atom, comparable to several synthesizable near-hull mixed halides in CS and TS. The strongest support comes from SS2–SS3, which show 0D Indium-like iodide cluster phases can be grown by sealed halogen-rich ampoule methods, and from CS2/SS4, which support hydrothermal halide crystallization involving Tl or Au. The most critical enabling factor is the salt-like 0D halometallate character, which should allow solution or vapor assembly without extensive solid-state diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Au oxidation state and I:Br ordering while preventing volatile I/Br loss. Toxic Tl compounds and corrosive halogen media are significant practical but not fundamental barriers.
