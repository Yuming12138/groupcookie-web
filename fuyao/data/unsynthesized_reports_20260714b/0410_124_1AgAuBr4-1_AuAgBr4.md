### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category by the provided rubric [target]. Many close analogues are also on the hull, including TlFeBr4 [CS1], TlTlAl2Br8 [CS4], CuGaI4 [CS5], AuTeI [SS1/TS1], AuBr [SS4/TS3], AgHgSI [SS5/TS4], and CuBr2 [TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for Au–Br systems where phase transformations can be sluggish [SS4/TS3].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses mild temperatures comparable to CS4 and CS5, so no high-temperature penalty is applied [CS4/CS5]. It does require sealed vacuum or inert handling, volatile/decomposable Au bromide species, and moisture-sensitive/toxic bromide chemistry, so deductions are assigned for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed precursor, and hazardous/air-sensitive precursors [CS4/CS5; SS4/TS3]. CS provides strong evidence for low-T ampoule halide synthesis [CS4/CS5], SS/TS provide relevant Au–Br handling constraints [SS4/TS3], and no high-pressure GPa evidence is required.

#### F3 — Route 1

Halide precursors are expected to be more reactive than refractory oxides, and CS4 demonstrates direct ternary halide formation at 150–200 °C [CS4]. Diffusion is aided by soft/volatile halide chemistry and possible local melt/vapor transport, as implied by sealed halide melt growth in CS5 [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is assembling the target 2D Au–Ag–Br sheet from binary bromides and avoiding competing AuBr/Au phases, which is cautioned by AuBr sluggish phase behavior [SS4/TS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not extremely hot by the scoring rubric, but it uses Br2 in sealed evacuated tubes, which is explicitly associated with pressure-compensated ampoules in CS1 [CS1]. Br2 is volatile and toxic, and the evacuated sealed-tube condition triggers the controlled-atmosphere and volatile-precursor penalties [CS1/CS3]. The route is therefore operationally more hazardous than the F2 score alone suggests, although the rubric does not penalize sub-GPa autogenous pressure beyond volatile/sealed handling [CS1].

#### F3 — Route 2

The main kinetic weakness is elemental Au, because SS4/TS3 indicate that Au2Br6 preparation requires reactive gold powder and extended bromination conditions [SS4/TS3]. Br2 transport can improve bromination and mass transfer, as shown in Tl/Fe bromide formation [CS1/CS3], but the target requires full structural assembly from elemental or binary precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Au–Br phase transformations are reported as sluggish and not always reproducible, lowering the structural reconstruction score [SS4/TS3].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids vacuum and high-temperature penalties and uses only moderate hydrothermal temperatures inferred from the AuTeI hydrothermal precedent [SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. It still requires a sealed vessel with corrosive/volatile HBr and possibly Br2 or AuBr3, so deductions are applied for volatile sealed chemistry and toxic/corrosive precursors [CS1/CS3; SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is that SS1/TS1 do not provide exact temperature/time or demonstrate the Ag–Br analogue directly.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal mineralization should provide high precursor activity and fast transport relative to dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and SS1/TS1 show that a 2D Au-containing sheet phase can crystallize hydrothermally in hydroiodic acid. The structural match is favorable because both AuTeI and the target are low-dimensional Au-containing sheet compounds [SS1/TS1; target]. The uncertainty is AgBr’s low solubility and the need to maintain Au(III) rather than reducing to AuBr/Au [SS4/TS3; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures similar to CS2 and CS5, so no high-temperature penalty is applied [CS2/CS5]. It requires sealed vacuum/inert conditions, volatile/corrosive Au bromide chemistry, and multiple operations including flux separation, so deductions are applied for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed precursor, multistep processing, and hazardous/air-sensitive materials [CS2/CS5; SS4/TS3]. CS2 also indicates that flux products can require post-synthesis washing and separation [CS2].

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux and melt routes improve kinetics because they provide liquid-phase transport and slow crystallization, supported by AuCuSe4 flux growth and CuGaI4 slow-cooling melt growth [CS2/CS5]. Ion diffusion is therefore scored high despite Au and Ag metal melting points, because the relevant transport medium is a molten/flux halide rather than solid metal diffusion [CS2/CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is reduced because CS2 reports mixed phases and inability to obtain phase-pure AuCuSe4, indicating phase selection can be difficult in flux systems [CS2].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: the Ag–Br bonds are substantially longer than Au–Br bonds, consistent with a significant Ag/Au cation size mismatch [target; Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a >40-atom cell or coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral networks, so C2 and C3 are not triggered [target]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization tendency, so their source-level F4 scores are neutral at 50; the final F4 score is based on the target structure and ionic-size reasoning [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuAgBr4 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target]. The analogue set supports synthesis well: chemically similar bromides form in sealed halide reactions [CS4/CS5], structurally similar AuTeI forms as 2D hydrothermal plates [SS1/TS1], and Au–Br phases are experimentally accessible under dry/vacuum conditions [SS4/TS3]. The most important enabling factor is soft-halide chemistry, which permits low-temperature transport and crystallization rather than requiring high-temperature cation diffusion [CS4/CS5; SS1/TS1]. The most important limiting factor is Au–Br redox/phase control, because AuBr formation and Au bromide decomposition/phase sluggishness are documented for Au–Br analogues [SS4/TS3]. Overall, synthesis is plausible to likely, with hydrothermal HBr growth and low-temperature sealed-ampoule bromide reaction as the best practical routes.
