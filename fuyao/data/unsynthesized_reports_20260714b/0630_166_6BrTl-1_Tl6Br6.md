### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, which is within the low-energy metastability range and earns a high F1 score. [target data] Chemically identical or very close Tl halides have very low E_hull values: rocksalt TlBr has E_hull ≈ 0.0047 eV/atom and TlCl ≈ 0.0062 eV/atom. [CS1, CS2] Structurally and thermodynamically similar Tl halide compounds also cluster at low E_hull values, supporting the plausibility of isolating related phases. [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS5] The main uncertainty is not decomposition energy but polymorph competition with the known TlBr structure. [CS1, SS1, TS2]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum processing, volatile halide evaporation, multiple controlled steps, and Tl toxicity. [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4] It avoids high temperature and high pressure, which improves its severity score. [CS1, SS1] The major operational burden is cryogenic substrate control and in-situ monitoring, because related metastable phases are temperature-sensitive. [CS1, SS1, TS2]

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic advantage of Route 1 is that TlBr can be directly evaporated, so precursor vapor availability is strong. [CS1, SS1, TS2] The crystallization pathway is less favorable because the analogue reports metastable polymorphs that depend on low temperature and may disappear on warming. [CS1, SS1] Composition control is favorable because a single-source TlBr precursor should preserve 1:1 stoichiometry, although differential Tl/Br volatility remains possible. [Reasonable Inference from CS1]

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses mild temperatures of 120–200 °C and avoids high pressure above 5 GPa. [SS5, TS1, TS3] Deductions arise from sealed acidic/aqueous halide pressure and the high toxicity of thallium salts. [SS5, TS1, TS3] CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal binary TlBr P4/mmm, so CS is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated recrystallization avoids slow solid-state diffusion, and analogous Tl halides crystallize under these conditions. [SS5, TS1, TS3] Precursor activity is high in acidic halide media because Br⁻/HBr can act as a mineralizer for dissolution–reprecipitation. [Reasonable Inference from SS5/TS1/TS3] The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction: no source directly demonstrates that binary TlBr adopts the target P4/mmm layered polymorph hydrothermally. Insufficient direct CS evidence for this route is treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure but requires evacuated/sealed ampoules, volatile halide containment, multi-step thermal treatment, and toxic Tl-containing precursors. [SS2, SS3, TS4, TS5] Melt/Bridgman equipment is standard in halide crystal growth, but sealed ampoules impose safety and stoichiometry constraints. [SS2, SS3, TS4] The severity is therefore comparable to Route 1 despite higher temperature, because the formal penalty comes mainly from sealing/vacuum and toxicity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Melt growth has good kinetics because diffusion in the melt is fast and related Tl halide-containing crystals are grown from stoichiometric melts. [SS2, SS3, TS4] However, structural selection is poor for the target because the closest binary TlBr analogue is a different rocksalt polymorph, and low-temperature deposition—not melt equilibrium—is the demonstrated route to abnormal TlBr polymorphs. [CS1, SS1, TS2] Long annealing in melt-growth analogues may improve crystallinity but could also drive the system toward the thermodynamic polymorph rather than the desired layered metastable phase. [SS2, TS4; Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has no multi-cation radius mismatch because Tl is the only cation. [target description] The structure does have mixed polyhedral connectivity, including corner- and edge-sharing octahedra and square pyramids, which raises amorphization or disorder risk. [target description] There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered and the source signal is neutral 50. Overall, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target TlBr P4/mmm phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the dominant ground-state room-temperature bulk form, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.0210 eV/atom. [target data] The strongest direct support comes from chemically identical TlBr evaporation data showing that unusual TlBr polymorphs can be trapped under low-temperature vacuum deposition. [CS1, SS1, TS2] Hydrothermal synthesis is attractive because it offers mild conditions and good crystallinity for Tl halide materials, but its support is indirect for binary TlBr. [SS5, TS1, TS3] The most critical enabling factor is the small thermodynamic penalty combined with soft, polarizable Tl–Br bonding that allows multiple coordination environments. [target data + CS1/SS1] The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control against the known TlBr rocksalt structure and possible loss of the target layered ordering during warming or annealing. [CS1, SS1, TS2]
