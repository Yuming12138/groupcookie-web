### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the upper edge of the highest scoring range in the provided rubric. Chemically similar KBrO₄ with E_hull ≈0.033 eV/atom is experimentally crystallized by saturated-solution evaporation (CS1), and structurally similar KClO₄ has E_hull = 0 and is also crystallized by evaporation (CS2; SS1). Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, including KBrO₄ and KNO₃, are synthesized under mild conditions (TS1, TS2), supporting that slight metastability does not preclude synthesis. The remaining uncertainty is whether the tetragonal P4₂/nmc target is the thermodynamic room-temperature form or a metastable/high-temperature polymorph, because the closest KClO₄/KBrO₄ analogues listed are orthorhombic Pnma (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has minimal condition severity because KClO₄ and KBrO₄ analogues are crystallized from saturated solutions at room or constant temperature with no special pressure or atmosphere (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3; TS1). TS2 also supports simple saturated aqueous evaporation for an alkali oxoanion salt. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Route 1 requires any high-pressure, reactive-atmosphere, sealed, or multistep operation, so those source signals are scored as neutral-to-positive rather than penalizing. The main safety issue, perchlorate oxidizing behavior, is a practical handling concern [Reasonable Inference] but is not a severe synthesis-condition deduction under the given rubric.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is high because solution crystallization bypasses solid-state diffusion, as directly shown by KClO₄/KBrO₄/KNO₃ evaporation syntheses (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3; TS1, TS2). Precursor activity is effectively maximal when using dissolved Rb⁺ and ClO₄⁻ ions [Reasonable Inference], and analogous salts form crystals directly rather than requiring prolonged sintering (CS1, CS2; TS1, TS2). The only kinetic/phase-selection uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the target tetragonal polymorph, since the closest KClO₄/KBrO₄ reference structures are orthorhombic (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 also has low severity because neutralization and evaporation analogues occur in aqueous solution without high temperature, high pressure, or controlled atmosphere (SS2; TS3). The thallous perbromate route explicitly uses carbonate + dilute perbromic acid followed by slow evaporation at room temperature (TS3), and chlorate crystallization uses hydroxide + chloric acid in aqueous solution (SS2). There is insufficient evidence from CS that carbonate + perchloric acid is directly used for RbClO₄, so CS is neutral for this specific route. Perchloric acid introduces safety complexity [Reasonable Inference], but the physical synthesis conditions remain mild.

#### F3 — Route 2

The reaction kinetics are favorable because acid–base neutralization creates the target ions in solution directly, analogous to carbonate + perbromic acid for TlBrO₄ (TS3) and hydroxide + chloric acid for chlorate salts (SS2). Ion diffusion is not limiting in solution, and the slow evaporation step is well supported by KClO₄/KBrO₄ analogues (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3; TS1). The reconstruction score is slightly lower than Route 1 because the target salt is first chemically generated, then crystallized, and the tetragonal phase is not directly proven by the neutralization analogues. SS and TS provide useful support; CS provides indirect support only through final salt crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate condition severity because related alkali carbonate + oxoacid syntheses are performed in air at 573 K for 2 days (CS3, CS4), which is still mild compared with high-pressure or >1000 °C synthesis. However, this route is multistep because it involves neutralization, drying, and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. CS3/CS4 provide useful support for low-temperature carbonate + oxoacid processing, while SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for thermal RbClO₄ crystallization and are treated as neutral for this route. The route must avoid overheating because perchlorates may decompose under unsuitable thermal conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are acceptable because reactive carbonate/acid-derived residues should form RbClO₄ at low temperature [Reasonable Inference], and alkali carbonate + selenic acid analogues react at 573 K (CS3, CS4). Ion diffusion is much easier than in refractory oxide synthesis because the likely pathway involves ionic salts/residues rather than dense oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is only moderate because CS3/CS4 mention by-product/impurity issues in some alkali selenium oxide cases, implying phase control can be less clean than direct solution crystallization. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this thermal route and are neutral for this kinetic subfactor.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The amorphization risk is low because chemically and structurally similar alkali oxoanion salts crystallize directly from solution, including KClO₄ and KBrO₄ (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3; TS1). The target is a simple ionic perchlorate with isolated ClO₄ tetrahedra and one alkali cation sublattice, so it lacks the multi-cation disorder and complex mixed-connectivity frameworks that often promote amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any amorphous competing product in these perchlorate/perbromate/nitrate evaporation routes, so each source is positive or neutral rather than negative. The uncertainty is mainly polymorphic, not amorphous.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbClO₄ in the specified tetragonal P4₂/nmc structure is likely a slightly metastable or boundary-stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, while several comparable oxoanion salts with similar small E_hull values are experimentally accessible (CS1; TS1–TS3). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of crystalline alkali perchlorate/perhalate salts by slow evaporation, especially KClO₄ and KBrO₄ (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3; TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the solution accessibility of Rb⁺ and ClO₄⁻ ions, which allows crystallization without solid-state diffusion barriers, as shown by saturated-solution evaporation analogues (CS1, CS2; TS1, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the closest listed KClO₄/KBrO₄ analogues are orthorhombic Pnma, not tetragonal P4₂/nmc (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS3). Therefore, the material is highly synthesizable as RbClO₄, but obtaining and preserving the exact tetragonal polymorph may require temperature control, seeding, or quenching [Reasonable Inference].
