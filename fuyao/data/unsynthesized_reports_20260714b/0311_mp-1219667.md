### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability boundary in the requested rubric, so a score of 90 is justified. PuIn, the same Tetraauricupride/P4/mmm structural analogue, is on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS3, SS2), and PuGa₃ in the same Pu–Ga binary is low-energy at ~0.0219 eV/atom (SS4, TS1). TS also shows synthesizable thermodynamic neighbors at nonzero hull energies, such as UPdGa at ~0.0586 eV/atom and Lu₃Mn₃Ga₂Si at ~0.0718 eV/atom (TS2, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is whether PuGa at exactly 1:1 is a true equilibrium phase or a metastable phase competing with nearby Pu–Ga compositions.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is supported by arc-melted PuIn and PuGa₃ analogues (CS3, SS2, SS4, TS1). The main severity penalties are high local arc temperature and Pu toxicity/radioactivity, with Pu handling risk supported by glovebox/actinide synthesis descriptions (SS5). The route avoids high pressure and avoids long sealed annealing, making it less severe than Route 2. Uncertainty remains because the exact arc-melt temperature/time for PuGa is not specified in the references.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Arc melting gives excellent atomic mixing in a liquid alloy, consistent with PuIn/PuGa₃ alloying by arc melting (CS3, SS2, SS4, TS1). Dense elemental metals score only moderate precursor activity by the rubric, but Ga’s low melting point helps wet and alloy the charge [Reasonable Inference]. The same Tetraauricupride structure in PuIn strongly supports low structural reconstruction penalty (CS3, SS2). The main uncertainty is ordering into the exact 1:1 PuGa phase rather than forming nearby Pu–Ga phases.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 adds sealed or evacuated annealing to arc melting, as in UPdGa and PuNiGa-type analogues (TS2, SS3, TS3). This increases severity through vacuum/sealed-tube handling, multi-step processing, and possible Ga vapor management. Pu toxicity remains a major deduction, supported by SS5. The uncertainty is whether annealing is actually required for PuGa; if direct arc melting gives a single phase, Route 2 is unnecessarily severe.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Initial arc melting provides good mixing, but ordering/homogenization during annealing can be slow. Long anneals are explicitly reported for UPdGa at 600 °C for 2 weeks (TS2) and for Lu–Mn–Ga–Si phases at 1100 K for 120 h (TS5). Structural support from PuIn remains strong (CS3, SS2), but annealing implies slower kinetics than direct melt solidification. The uncertainty is the appropriate anneal temperature, because no direct PuGa anneal schedule is provided.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is based on Th–Ga direct synthesis in closed Ta crucibles at ~1800 °C for a few minutes (CS1, CS2). This imposes severe high-temperature and sealed-crucible requirements, and the target’s Pu component adds the actinide handling risk demonstrated in Pu/Am intermetallic work (SS5). The short dwell time is favorable, as reported in CS1–CS2. The uncertainty is whether Pu–Ga is compatible with the same Ta-crucible high-temperature approach without contamination or Ga loss.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The very high temperature and likely molten state make interdiffusion rapid, consistent with CS1–CS2 reporting few-minute syntheses for Th–Ga samples. Precursor activity remains moderate because elemental metals are used. The structural reconstruction score is lower than Route 1 because the Th–Ga analogues in CS1–CS2 are chemically similar but not the same Tetraauricupride/P4/mmm structure. The strongest structural evidence still comes from PuIn, not the Ta-crucible Th–Ga route (CS3, SS2).

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 is supported by PuGa₃ slow cooling after arc melting in the same binary system (SS4, TS1). Its severity is similar to Route 1: high melt/arc temperature and Pu hazard, but no high pressure or mandatory vacuum step. Controlled cooling adds operational complexity but does not trigger the rubric’s ≥3-step penalty. The uncertainty is the phase diagram window for 1:1 PuGa during slow cooling.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Melt growth benefits from liquid-state transport and is directly demonstrated for PuGa₃ in the Pu–Ga system (SS4, TS1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because PuIn is the same structure type as target PuGa (CS3, SS2). Dense elemental precursors limit the precursor-activity subscore, but not the practical melt kinetics. The uncertainty is whether slow cooling stabilizes PuGa or instead favors PuGa₃/other equilibrium phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, directly triggering C3 from the target description. A Pu–Ga metallic size mismatch likely triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference], while the simple P4/mmm Tetraauricupride cell does not trigger the >40 atom criterion, consistent with the simple PuIn structural analogue (CS3, SS2). Crystalline PuIn and PuGa₃ analogues indicate that crystallization is realistic rather than amorphization-dominated (CS3, SS2, SS4, TS1). The uncertainty is whether rapid quenching from arc melting could trap disorder or off-stoichiometry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PuGa at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is best viewed as borderline thermodynamically stable or low-energy metastable, rather than an unequivocal deep ground-state phase. The analogue support is strong: PuIn has the identical Tetraauricupride/P4/mmm structure (CS3, SS2), and PuGa₃ demonstrates that crystalline Pu–Ga intermetallics can be made from elemental Pu/Ga by arc melting and slow cooling (SS4, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is access to liquid-state alloying by arc melting or melt growth, which overcomes diffusion limitations and is repeatedly used for Pu/Ga/actinide intermetallics (CS3, SS2, SS4, SS5, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Pu–Ga binary, especially avoiding PuGa₃ or Pu-rich competing phases while controlling Ga loss [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control concerns in CS3/SS2/SS5 and PuGa₃ evidence in SS4/TS1]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but requires specialized radiological metallurgy capability.
