### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. SS/TS analogues HoSI, DySI, DySBr, GdSI, and MnSc2S4 are also on the hull [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5], supporting that related sulfide-halide or Sc-sulfide phases can be stable. CS provides additional but less direct support because several rare-earth sulfide chloride/nitride chloride analogues are also near or on the hull [CS1, CS3–CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that computational ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct mixed Br/I ordering **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The severity is moderate because SS/TS direct elemental sulfide iodide syntheses use 900 °C for several days in sealed silica ampoules [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. Vacuum sealing and volatile Br2/I2 introduce safety and stoichiometry-control penalties, consistent with DySBr/DySI sealed-ampoule synthesis [SS2–SS3, TS2–TS3]. There is insufficient evidence from CS for direct Sc–S–Br–I elemental synthesis, so CS is neutral for this exact route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The elemental route benefits from reactive halogens and sulfur, but Sc metal is diffusion-limited because it is refractory **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS analogues demonstrate that 2D MSX structures form at 750–900 °C despite long diffusion distances [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The main uncertainty is whether Br and I order into the Pmm2 target rather than forming separate ScSBr/ScSI or a disordered mixed-halide phase **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses the same severity class as DySBr/DySI synthesis: vacuum sealed quartz ampoule, 750–900 °C, and halogen reagents [SS2–SS3, TS2–TS3]. It avoids direct full reliance on elemental sulfur but still uses Br2/I2 and Sc metal, so volatile/toxic precursor deductions remain **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS gives insufficient evidence for this specific binary sulfide + Sc metal + Br/I route and is scored neutral.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Binary sulfide plus metal routes are proven for DySBr/DySI [SS2–SS3, TS2–TS3], which supports feasibility of forming layered sulfide halides from a preformed sesquisulfide. Kinetics may be limited by Sc2S3 stability and Sc metal diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**. The pathway is likely robust for phase formation but not necessarily for single-phase ordered Sc2S2IBr **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has moderate severity because it still requires evacuated sealed ampoules, but its projected temperature range overlaps CS flux routes at 850 °C and SS/TS layered sulfide-halide routes at 750–900 °C [CS1, CS3–CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. Anhydrous ScBr3/ScI3 are moisture-sensitive and may volatilize, giving handling penalties **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS provides useful evidence for metal-halide/flux use, while SS/TS provides the structural-family evidence.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux or excess metal halide can improve reaction completion and single-crystal formation in related sulfide halide systems [CS1, CS5]. Using ScBr3 and ScI3 fixes the Br/I supply and avoids metering liquid Br2, which may help stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates ScBr3/ScI3 + Sc2S3 metathesis to this ordered Pmm2 phase, so the structural reconstruction score is not maximal.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
CVT is the most severe route because the Sc sulfide CVT analogue uses 1050 °C for one month and iodine in an evacuated silica tube [SS5, TS5]. It requires a prior synthesis step plus transport growth, so multi-step and volatile halogen penalties apply. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth/purification option rather than the simplest synthesis route **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
CVT can improve Sc sulfide crystal quality, as shown for MnSc2S4 using iodine transport [SS5, TS5]. Vapor transport can overcome bulk diffusion limitations **[Reasonable Inference]**, giving a relatively good kinetic score despite severe conditions. However, I2 transport may alter the target Br/I ratio, so composition control is a major uncertainty **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Sc, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structure is described as a relatively simple 2D sheet of corner- and edge-sharing ScS4I2/ScS4Br2 octahedra, similar to SS/TS layered MSX analogues that are crystalline rather than amorphous [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. CS gives insufficient evidence for amorphization tendency in this exact Sc mixed-halide layered structure and is scored neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc2S2IBr is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple SS/TS analogues in the same sulfide-halide structural family are also hull-stable [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS: HoSI, DySI, DySBr, and GdSI are layered MSX phases made by sealed-ampoule solid-state reactions at 750–900 °C [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. CS supports the use of metal halides and fluxes in evacuated ampoules but is less structurally direct because the CS examples are mainly rare-earth sulfide chloride/nitride chloride compounds [CS1, CS3–CS5]. The key enabling factor is halide-assisted mass transport in sealed ampoules or flux, which is repeatedly useful in CS/SS/TS [CS1, CS5, SS2–SS3, TS2–TS3]. The key limiting factor is control of mixed Br/I stoichiometry and ordering in a volatile halogen environment **[Reasonable Inference]**.
