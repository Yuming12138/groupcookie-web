### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for high synthesizability, so F1 = 90. SS5 is especially encouraging because Lu₂CCl₂, the closest formula/structure analogue, is on the hull and experimentally synthesized. CS2, CS3, and CS4 also show chemically related chloride or nitride-halide phases at or near the hull, while TS1–TS5 show that phases around 0.07–0.12 eV/atom are experimentally accessible. The remaining uncertainty is that Ti₂CCl₂ may compete with TiC, TiCl₂/TiCl₃, and Cl-loss products, which is not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate high temperatures similar to CS2 and SS5, but requires sealed refractory containers, dry handling, and air-sensitive chloride reagents. The absence of high pressure keeps the severity moderate, unlike CS1/CS4 high-pressure routes. Volatile chloride chemistry and moisture sensitivity are the main deductions, supported by chloride-containing syntheses in CS2/CS3/SS5. TS gives insufficient condition-specific guidance for Ti₂CCl₂ and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by TiC and Ti metal, both refractory solid-state reactants, consistent with long sintering times in SS1, SS2, and SS4. Chloride flux or chloride vapor transport may improve transport, as seen in CS2 and SS5, but direct reconstruction from TiC/TiCl₂ to Ti₂CCl₂ still requires substantial rearrangement. The exact Lu₂CCl₂ structural analogue in SS5 improves confidence in the final structure. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ti chloride activity can be tuned without forming binary Ti chlorides.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C heating and avoids external pressure, making it less severe thermally than CS1/CS4 and SS3. It still requires reactive anhydrous chloride salts, controlled atmosphere, washing, and moisture exclusion, as implied by CS3 and SS5. The multi-step workup lowers the F2 score. The exact Lewis-acid chloride selection is [Reasonable Inference], not directly specified by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

This route receives the highest F3 because Ti₂AlC provides a preorganized Ti–C layered framework, and SS3 demonstrates topochemical reactivity in Ti–Al–C layered precursors. Molten chloride fluxes should improve diffusion and Cl incorporation, consistent with flux-assisted layered halide syntheses in CS1, CS4, and SS5. The main kinetic risk is incomplete conversion or residual parent phase, directly suggested by SS3, where Ti₅Al₂C₃ formed only as part of a multiphase mixture. Thus kinetics are favorable but phase purification remains nontrivial.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure chloride flux synthesis is the most severe route because CS1 and CS4 require 3–5 GPa, specialized capsules, controlled cooling, and quenching. The formal F2 table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, so the route is not deducted for pressure by the provided rule, but practically it remains challenging. Reactive fluxes and air-sensitive chloride handling cause additional deductions. TS provides insufficient route-specific support for high-pressure Ti–C–Cl chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 3

High-pressure fluxes can crystallize layered metal halides quickly, as shown by Zr/Hf nitride halides in CS1 and CS4. However, those analogues are nitride halides, not carbide chlorides, so transfer to Ti₂CCl₂ is only moderate. The flux may overcome TiC sluggishness, but the target may not need high pressure if the Lu₂CCl₂ analogue is accessible at ambient pressure in sealed Ta containers (SS5). The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the desired Ti₂CCl₂ sheet or instead favors denser Ti–C/Cl phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one cation, a small formula unit, and only edge/corner-sharing octahedra, so none of the three amorphization checks is triggered. SS5 shows an ordered Lu₂CCl₂ layered analogue, and SS1–SS4 show ordered Ti–C layered structures, supporting low amorphization tendency. CS2 shows even more complex carbide chloride networks can crystallize, further reducing amorphization concern. TS provides insufficient amorphization-specific evidence and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂CCl₂ is likely a slightly metastable or borderline-stable phase, since its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The best support comes from SS5, where Lu₂CCl₂ has the same M₂CCl₂ layered motif, and from SS3, where Ti–Al–C layered precursors show topochemical transformation capability. CS2 also supports carbide chloride formation from metal chloride/metal/carbon mixtures in sealed refractory containers, while CS1/CS4 support chloride-flux routes for layered metal halides. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high chlorine chemical potential while preserving the Ti₂C layer topology. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding TiC residues, TiClₓ phases, Cl loss, or incomplete Al removal in the topochemical route.
