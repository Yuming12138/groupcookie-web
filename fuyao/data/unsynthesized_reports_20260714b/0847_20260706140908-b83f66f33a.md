### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided thermodynamic criterion. TS contains several stable or near-stable Sb oxide analogues, including Sb₂O₅ at 0 eV/atom, LiSb₃O₈ at 0 eV/atom, BiSbO₄ at 0 eV/atom, and PbSb₂O₆ at 0 eV/atom, supporting the plausibility of stable Sb(V)-anion frameworks (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). CS gives less direct support because the chemically similar entries are mostly sulfides and one nitride analogue, so CS is positive for general solid-state accessibility but insufficient for direct Sb₃N₅ thermodynamics. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee a convenient kinetic pathway, especially for nitrogen-rich Sb(V) chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because CS-5 uses demanding but not >5 GPa conditions: 600 °C, 6 kbar NH₃, KNH₂, and 120 h. The kinetics are favorable compared with fully refractory solid-state routes because ammonothermal chemistry provides chemical transport and reactive nitrogen, as shown for the Cmc2₁ nitride analogue in CS-5. SS-1/TS-1 indicate that Sb(V) octahedral frameworks may need high anion chemical potential, which Route 1 provides via NH₃/amide chemistry. The main uncertainty is whether Sb dissolves and recrystallizes in the chosen ammonobasic medium without forming lower nitrides or metallic Sb.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has severe conditions because high-pressure synthesis above 5 GPa is proposed, analogous in spirit to pressure-stabilized dense anion frameworks in SS-1/TS-1 and high-pressure nitride chemistry in CS-5. Kinetics are acceptable because solid nitrogen donors can be highly reactive [Reasonable Inference], but the method involves full structural reconstruction and quenching. SS-1/TS-1 support the concept that high anion pressure can stabilize high-valent Sb frameworks, but they are oxide rather than nitride examples. The largest uncertainty is safe, controlled generation of nitrogen chemical potential without decomposing or overpressurizing the capsule.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because CS-3 demonstrates ball milling under Ar for 8 h as a relatively accessible route to a metastable SnS₂ polymorph. Mechanochemistry can increase precursor activity and shorten diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates mechanochemical Sb nitride synthesis, so direct evidence is insufficient from SS and TS. The post-anneal must crystallize an ordered orthorhombic Sb₃N₅ phase without nitrogen loss, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score. The route is attractive as a screening method but less reliable for phase-pure bulk crystals.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has relatively mild severity because thin-film deposition avoids high bulk pressure and can operate below 700 °C [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Sb–N thin-film growth, so the route is more speculative than Route 1. Kinetics benefit from short diffusion lengths in films, but crystallization and stoichiometry control are uncertain because reactive nitrogen incorporation and Sb volatility can narrow the process window [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best for exploratory phase mapping rather than immediate bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Sb, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The provided structural description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell, so the large-cell amorphization criterion is not triggered. The target contains corner-sharing SbN₆ octahedra and edge/corner-sharing NSb₄ units, but no face-sharing coexistence is stated, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not fully met. Remaining uncertainty is that nitride networks can still form amorphous intermediates during low-temperature synthesis, especially in mechanochemical or thin-film routes.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sb₃N₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-5, which demonstrates high-pressure ammonothermal nitride crystallization at 600 °C and 6 kbar NH₃, and from SS-1/TS-1, which show that high-valent Sb octahedral frameworks can be stabilized under high anion chemical potential. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a sufficiently high nitrogen chemical potential while allowing Sb–N transport and crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is the absence of a direct Sb₃N₅ synthesis analogue in CS/SS/TS, making competing lower antimony nitrides, metallic Sb, or amorphous Sb–N phases plausible. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require high-pressure NH₃ or another strongly nitriding environment rather than ordinary ambient-pressure solid-state firing.
