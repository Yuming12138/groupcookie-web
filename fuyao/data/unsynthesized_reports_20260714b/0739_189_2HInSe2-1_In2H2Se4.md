### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. Many SS/TS analogues are also on or near the convex hull, including AgIn(PSe₃)₂ at 0.0 eV/atom [SS5/TS1], HgIn₂Se₄ at 0.0 eV/atom [TS2], ZnIn₂Se₄ at 0.0 eV/atom [TS3], AgGaSe₂ at 0.0 eV/atom [TS4], and CdPSe₃ at 0.0 eV/atom [TS5]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for In–Se–H thermodynamics because the closest CS entries are mostly halide/ammonium or hydriodic-acid compounds rather than hydroselenide indium phases; CS is treated as neutral 50 for this sub-signal.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature because the proposed 300–450 °C hydroselenide-forming step is below 1000 °C **[Reasonable Inference]**, whereas many In–Se analogues tolerate higher sealed-tube temperatures [SS1, SS5, TS1–TS3]. The severity penalty comes from using vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile Se/H₂Se species, multiple handling/annealing steps, and toxic H₂Se/HSe⁻ chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS strongly support sealed vacuum chalcogenide synthesis [SS1, SS2, SS3, SS5, TS1–TS5], while CS gives insufficient evidence for direct InHSe₂ formation and is neutral for transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental In and Se are relatively reactive compared with refractory oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**, and elemental routes are common for similar selenides [SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS5]. However, direct formation of a hydrogenated layered phase from elements likely requires substantial structural reconstruction and H incorporation, which is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. Long annealing or repeated homogenization in analogues [SS1, SS4, TS2–TS5] lowers confidence in fast kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is low-temperature, so it avoids high-temperature deductions **[Reasonable Inference]**. It still requires sealed/inert conditions and volatile/toxic H₂Se or HSe⁻ species **[Reasonable Inference]**, so its condition-severity score remains moderate. SS/TS support layered In–Se precursors [SS2, SS5, TS1, TS5], but CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydro-selenating In–Se layers and is neutral for that factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

Starting from a layered In–Se precursor should reduce the reconstruction burden relative to elemental synthesis **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target and several analogues are layered [SS2, SS5, TS1, TS5], which supports a topochemical approach. The key uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS explicitly reports proton or hydroselenide insertion into an In–Se layer; this missing direct analogue prevents an even higher kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 3

The CVT route uses sealed vacuum conditions, volatile transport agents, and multi-step processing, as in MnIn₂Se₄ CVT [SS2]. The proposed low-temperature CVT avoids the high temperatures of the SS2 MnIn₂Se₄ case, which used 840–820 °C [SS2], but that lower temperature is an inference designed to retain H–Se bonds **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS provides insufficient useful CVT signal and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT can accelerate mass transport and crystal growth, as shown by MnIn₂Se₄ single-crystal growth using AlCl₃ under vacuum [SS2]. However, the target contains a chemically labile H–Se bond, and transport agents such as AlCl₃ or I₂ may form indium halides or remove hydrogen **[Reasonable Inference]**. Because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates hydroselenide retention during CVT, the route is kinetically plausible but chemically risky.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so the mixed-cation radius mismatch check is not triggered by the target description. The structure includes corner- and edge-sharing InSe₆ octahedra, but no face-sharing, so the specified mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not fully triggered. SS/TS analogues show many crystalline layered or framework selenides [SS2, SS5, TS1, TS5], supporting low amorphization tendency; CS gives insufficient direct structural signal for InHSe₂ and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InHSe₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The SS and TS analogues strongly support the accessibility of crystalline In–Se and layered selenide frameworks by sealed-tube, annealing, and CVT-type methods [SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5, TS1–TS5]. The CS analogues support general handling of H-containing salts and sealed/inert synthesis in limited ways [CS2, CS3, CS5], but they do not directly demonstrate indium hydroselenide chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the structural compatibility of layered In–Se frameworks with the target’s 2D InSe₆-sheet motif [SS2, SS5, TS1, TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is retention and controlled placement of H as an Se–H bond without forming H₂Se gas, elemental Se, In₂Se₃/InSe, or decomposed selenides **[Reasonable Inference]**.
