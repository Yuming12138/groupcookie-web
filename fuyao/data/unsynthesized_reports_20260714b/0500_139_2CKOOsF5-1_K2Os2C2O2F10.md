### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0130 eV/atom, which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues include phases with small positive hull energies that were synthesized, such as CsVOF3 at 0.026 eV/atom (TS1), LiCaUF8 at 0.025 eV/atom (TS2), and Cs2KMnF6/Cs2NaMnF6 near 0.044–0.046 eV/atom (TS3–TS5). This supports a high F1 score, although metastability is still possible because the target is not exactly on the hull.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — HF hydrothermal under CO

HF hydrothermal synthesis is strongly supported by CS1, CS2, CS4, SS2, SS3, and TS1, with successful crystallization at 100–350 °C. The severity score is reduced because HF is toxic, sealed vessels are required, and adding CO introduces a volatile/toxic reagent by **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are favorable because solution transport avoids slow solid-state diffusion, as shown by low-temperature hydrothermal analogues (CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1), but the absence of carbonyl examples in CS/SS/TS lowers confidence.

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Slow evaporation from preformed fluorocarbonylosmate

SS4 shows that heavy 5d fluorometallate salts such as Cs2IrF6 can crystallize by room-temperature solution evaporation from H2[IrF6], which supports low severity and excellent kinetic accessibility. The main deductions arise from needing a preformed Os carbonyl fluoride precursor and handling HF/CO/Os species, all of which are **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly shown in CS/SS/TS. If the [Os(CO)F5]− unit is stable in solution, structural reconstruction should be minimal, giving the best F3 score.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Direct fluorination plus carbonylation

SS1 and SS5 demonstrate that alkali Ru fluorides can be made by direct fluorination of RuCl3/alkali chloride mixtures at 350 °C, so an Os fluoride precursor route is plausible by analogy. However, SS1/SS5 also report moisture/temperature sensitivity and phase-control difficulty, and no CS/SS/TS source shows post-fluorination CO insertion. The kinetics and selectivity are therefore much poorer than solution routes, especially because carbonyl ligands are usually temperature-sensitive **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large alkali cation and a small octahedral Os-centered anion, a size mismatch also seen qualitatively in alkali fluoride frameworks such as KNaW(OF2)2 (CS4), Cs/RbVOF3 (SS2, SS3), and Cs/RbRuF6 (SS1, SS5). Only one amorphization trigger is confidently counted because CS/SS/TS do not provide the target cell atom count or direct mixed-connectivity evidence for KOsCOF5. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium, not prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KOsCOF5 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0130 eV/atom, and TS analogues with larger positive hull energies have been experimentally synthesized (TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS data strongly support formation of alkali transition-metal fluoride/oxyfluoride frameworks under HF hydrothermal, solution, or fluorination conditions (CS1, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride activity combined with low-temperature crystallization, because HF/solution media allow M–F frameworks to form without high-temperature diffusion limits (CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is the terminal Os–CO ligand: no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates carbonyl fluoride incorporation, so CO chemical potential and Os redox control must be optimized by **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible but will likely require molecular-precursor or low-temperature solution chemistry rather than conventional high-temperature ceramic synthesis.
