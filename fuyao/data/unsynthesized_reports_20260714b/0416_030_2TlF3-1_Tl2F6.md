### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.005 eV/atom, so it is very close to the convex hull and falls in the highest stability scoring range. TS gives strong supporting evidence because several thermodynamically similar fluorides with E_hull ≈ 0–0.02 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, including FeF₃, EuSnF₇, Cs₂NaTlF₆, and KFeF₄. SS also supports the local chemistry because Cs₂NaTlF₆ contains octahedral Tl–F bonds of 2.21 Å, close to the target Tl–F distance of 2.20 Å. CS is mixed: Tl₂SnF₆ is stable and made at 300 °C, but BiF₃ is listed with E_hull ≈0.268 eV/atom, showing that metastable fluorides can still be isolated.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is condition-severe because F₂ fluorination is repeatedly associated with high reactivity, special reactors, and sometimes hundreds of bar pressure in SS4, CS4, and CS5. It is not penalized for >1000 °C or GPa pressure because the relevant fluorination analogues operate near 300–450 °C in SS/TS2, SS4, and CS4. Kinetically, gas-phase fluorination should be active, but transforming TlF/Tl₂O₃ into the specific edge-sharing TlF₆ sheet is a significant reconstruction. The main uncertainty is whether direct fluorination gives the desired layered P-31m polymorph rather than another TlF₃ polymorph or decomposition product.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from low-temperature fluoride formation, supported by CS1 precipitation, SS1/TS1 soft chemistry, and TS5 HF hydrothermal synthesis. However, the use of HF, sealed hydrothermal conditions, and Tl toxicity keep the condition-severity score modest. Kinetically it is attractive because solution chemistry bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion, but the structural-reconstruction score is low because aqueous fluoride routes may yield hydrated or oxyfluoride thallium phases. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that binary anhydrous TlF₃ specifically can be crystallized from HF solution, so this route is treated as plausible but uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because dry sealed handling, toxic TlF₃, and possible F₂ buffering remain necessary; this is supported by sealed fluoride synthesis in CS3, SS5, and SS3/TS4. Its kinetic score is the best because starting from preformed TlF₃ removes the redox/stoichiometry problem and leaves mainly polymorph selection. The temperature window of 250–450 °C is supported by CS3 and SS5, which use 300–500 °C for Tl-containing fluorides. The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly identify the target TlF₃ P-31m polymorph, so phase screening by powder diffraction would be essential.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The described P-31m TlF₃ sheet appears structurally simple and contains edge-sharing TlF₆ octahedra without mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity. SS/TS analogues show that octahedral fluoride frameworks can crystallize, including FeF₃ and Cs₂NaTlF₆. The remaining uncertainty is polymorphism rather than intrinsic amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlF₃ with E_hull = 0.005 eV/atom is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase. The strongest support comes from TS and SS: thermodynamically similar fluorides near the hull are synthesized, and Tl(III)–F octahedra with nearly identical Tl–F distances appear in Cs₂NaTlF₆. The most enabling factor is the simple single-cation TlF₃ chemistry combined with a very low computed hull energy. The limiting factor is not thermodynamics but chemical handling: Tl compounds are highly toxic, Tl(III) fluorides require strongly oxidizing/dry fluoride conditions, and competing polymorphs or oxyfluorides may form. The best practical route is therefore to first obtain anhydrous TlF₃ and then use sealed-tube annealing/recrystallization to select the predicted trigonal layered phase.
