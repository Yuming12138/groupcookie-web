### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the boundary of the 90–100 bin specified in the prompt. Structurally identical F-43m ZnSO₄ has E_hull = 0.0383 eV/atom (SS3, TS2), and chemically related LiKSO₄ phases are very close to the hull, with E_hull ≈ 0.00008–0.0088 eV/atom (CS3, CS4). TS analogues show similar marginal metastability, including 0.0583 eV/atom for TS1 and 0.0383 eV/atom for TS2. Remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic Li₂SO₄ polymorph is stable at room temperature or only quenchable from elevated temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild initial conditions because aqueous solution growth and slow evaporation are repeatedly demonstrated for related Li/K sulfates and phosphates (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, SS2). The main severity deduction is the multi-step sequence needed to obtain an anhydrous cubic product **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are favorable because solution chemistry gives high precursor activity, but structural reconstruction/dehydration may be required to form F-43m Li₂SO₄, inferred from the anhydrous ZnSO₄ CVT analogue (SS3, TS2). Insufficient evidence from TS for direct aqueous Li₂SO₄ formation; TS is treated as neutral for the solution-specific route except where it supports sulfate aqueous chemistry generally.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 uses standard furnace processing analogous to CS5 and TS5, so temperature and equipment severity are moderate rather than high. Slow ramping is needed because gas evolution from carbonate/ammonium precursors is explicitly a concern in CS5 and TS5. Kinetics are acceptable for reactive salts, but repeated grinding and long firing in CS5/TS5 suggest that bulk solid-state ordering is slower than solution-derived routes. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct Li₂SO₄ solid-state synthesis; SS mainly informs the likely high-temperature anhydrous F-43m structural window through ZnSO₄ (SS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is structurally well motivated because F-43m ZnSO₄ was synthesized by Cl₂ chemical vapor transport at ≤660 °C (SS3, TS2). The severity score is much lower because Cl₂ is toxic/reactive and CVT requires sealed gradient equipment (SS3, TS2). Kinetics are uncertain: the crystallization pathway is plausible by analogy to ZnSO₄, but Li₂SO₄ volatility/transport chemistry is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so precursor vapor availability is penalized. Insufficient evidence from CS for Li sulfate CVT; CS is neutral for this route except for confirming sulfate/Li salt accessibility.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target framework is simple corner-sharing LiO₄/SO₄ tetrahedra, matching the corner-sharing motifs in LiKSO₄ analogues (CS3, CS4, SS1, SS2) and the F-43m ZnSO₄ analogue (SS3/TS2). Only one amorphization check is triggered: large cation size/charge contrast between Li⁺ and S⁶⁺ **[Reasonable Inference]**; CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii directly, so evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS for the numerical radius criterion. There is no evidence for edge/face-sharing complexity in the target, and no evidence of a very large unit cell from the target/SS3 description. Thus amorphization risk is low–medium, not severe.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Li₂SO₄ phase is likely marginally metastable rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, while thermodynamic analogues cluster near 0.04–0.06 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). The CS and SS data support synthesis strongly at the chemistry/framework level: Li/K sulfates form readily by aqueous evaporation (CS3, CS4, SS1, SS2), and the exact Cuprite-derived F-43m sulfate topology is experimentally accessible for ZnSO₄ by CVT (SS3, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of Li⁺/SO₄²⁻ salts in aqueous or moderately heated conditions, supported by CS1/CS3/CS4/SS1/SS2. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: the cubic anhydrous F-43m Li₂SO₄ structure may require dehydration and high-temperature quenching, which is inferred rather than directly proven by the reference set. Overall, the material is synthesizable with good probability, especially as a metastable/quenchable or high-temperature phase.
