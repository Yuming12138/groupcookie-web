### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability scoring range. Close analogues NbSeBr₃/Nb₄Se₄Br₁₂, Nb₃Br₈, and Nb₃I₈ are also reported on the hull (CS1/SS5/TS4; SS1/TS1; TS5), which supports the idea that Nb–chalcogen–halide and Nb trihalide layered/cluster phases can be thermodynamically stable. Remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or single-phase formation.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because NbSeBr₃ is synthesized at 500–460 °C and Nb₃Br₈ at 500–760 °C gradients (CS1/SS5/TS4; SS1/TS1). The deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Br₂/Se, and Br₂ toxicity/corrosivity, all consistent with sealed-tube halogen chemistry (CS1/SS5/TS4). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed for Nb₃SeBr₇; the only high-pressure example is chemically less relevant Mo–Br–O (CS3), so it is treated as non-transferable.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT

The kinetic score is moderate because refractory Nb metal is difficult to react, but vapor transport by bromine substantially improves mass transport [Reasonable Inference supported by CVT success in CS1/SS5/TS4 and SS1/TS1]. The target’s structure combines features of Nb₃Br₈-like 2D sheets and NbSeBr₃-like Nb–Se–Br bonding, reducing structural reconstruction relative to an unrelated phase (SS1/TS1; CS1/SS5/TS4). The main uncertainty is whether Nb₃SeBr₇ nucleates preferentially over NbSeBr₃ or Nb₃Br₈.

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

Direct solid-state synthesis uses standard evacuated silica/quartz tubes and 800–900 °C conditions based on Nb–Se and NbSeI analogues (CS4, CS5, SS4). Severity is increased by sealed-vacuum operation and volatile halogen/chalcogen components, but no >1000 °C condition is required in the proposed adaptation. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS indicates that direct solid-state synthesis specifically yields Nb₃SeBr₇, so this route is less preferred than CVT.

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

Kinetics are the weakest for direct solid-state synthesis because Nb is refractory and elemental pellet reactions are diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], while related Nb–Se reactions require 48 h at 800 °C and still show minor impurity/ordering issues (CS4, CS5). Building a 2D ordered Nb₃SeBr₇ sheet directly from elements requires substantial reconstruction. The route may still work because direct sealed-tube synthesis succeeds for NbSeI at 900 °C (SS4), but Br volatility makes stoichiometry more difficult [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Halide Flux

Flux growth avoids >1000 °C because the analogous K₄Nb₆Br₁₈ procedure uses 1063 K, about 790 °C (SS3/TS3). However, the route requires controlled Ar/vacuum containment, dried halide flux, multi-step handling, and likely air/moisture-sensitive bromide precursors (SS3/TS3; [Reasonable Inference]). The need for Nb or Ta tube containment increases practical difficulty relative to simple CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3: Halide Flux

Flux improves kinetics by enhancing mass transport and by using Nb₃Br₈, a structurally related 2D Nb–Br framework, as a precursor (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS3). Structural reconstruction should be lower than in direct solid-state synthesis because the Nb₃ sheet motif is partially preorganized. The main uncertainty is that no CS example directly shows Se incorporation using LiBr/KBr flux, so Se solubility and final stoichiometry control are uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation type, Nb, so no cation-radius disorder is expected [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. Its described bonding is primarily edge-sharing NbSeBr₅ octahedra, and the closest Nb₃Br₈ analogue is also a 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet (SS1/TS1), suggesting low topological frustration. Insufficient explicit CS/SS/TS evidence is available for target unit-cell atom count, but the 11-atom formula and simple P3m1 description suggest a small cell [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SeBr₇ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull, and closely related Nb–Se–Br and Nb–Br analogues are also on the hull (CS1/SS5/TS4; SS1/TS1). The synthesis evidence is strongest for CVT because an exact Nb–Se–Br analogue, NbSeBr₃, is made from Nb, Se, and Br₂ under sealed-tube temperature-gradient conditions (CS1/SS5/TS4). The most important enabling factor is bromine-assisted vapor transport, which can overcome slow Nb diffusion and produce single crystals at moderate temperatures (CS1/SS5/TS4; SS1/TS1). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among NbSeBr₃, Nb₃Br₈, NbSe₂-like phases, and the desired Nb₃SeBr₇ stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference supported by impurity/order issues in CS4/CS5 and multiple stable analogues in CS1/SS1/TS1]. Overall, the target appears **moderately to highly synthesizable**, with CVT as the preferred route.
