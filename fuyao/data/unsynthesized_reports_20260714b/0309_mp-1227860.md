### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high-synthesizability threshold used in the scoring rubric [Target]. Chemically similar BaCuP, BaIrP, BaPtP, and Ba₂PdP₃ analogues are calculated at or near the hull and have reported syntheses [CS-1 to CS-3, SS-4]. Thermodynamic analogues with even larger E_hull values, ~0.056–0.110 eV/atom, are experimentally reported [TS-1 to TS-5], supporting the plausibility of a slightly metastable BaPdP. Remaining uncertainty is whether BaPdP is a true ground-state polymorph or a metastable 1:1:1 phase competing with Ba₂PdP₃/BaPd₂P₂/PdP₂ [SS-4].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses temperatures up to 1000–1050 °C, Ar atmosphere, sealed quartz ampoule, alumina crucible, and repeated grinding/tempering [SS-4]. These are standard but not trivial conditions, and Ba/P handling plus sealed-tube P volatility add severity [SS-4; Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues use similar 900–1100 °C Ar solid-state conditions [CS-1 to CS-3], supporting transferability. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for this exact route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The route is kinetically moderate because elemental Pd is refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow [SS-4; Reasonable Inference]. However, successful Ba–Pd–P and Ba–Pt–P analogues form after annealing/grinding at 950–1050 °C [SS-4, CS-3], indicating that diffusion barriers can be overcome. The repeated cycles and reported impurities in Ba₂PdP₃ lower the kinetics score [SS-4].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Arc melting is supported by TS analogues made under Ar [TS-1, TS-2], but the local temperatures exceed ordinary furnace synthesis and likely exceed 1400 °C [TS-1, TS-2; Reasonable Inference]. Ba/P volatility is a major concern because Ba–Pd–P solid-state work uses sealed ampoules and P-containing systems require containment [SS-4]. CS/SS do not directly validate arc melting for BaPdP, so CS/SS evidence is neutral for the route-specific method but supportive for elemental reactivity.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Arc melting gives excellent homogenization and bypasses slow solid diffusion, as seen for thermodynamic intermetallic analogues [TS-1, TS-2]. The structural ordering risk is significant because the target is monoclinic P2₁/c [Target], and TS-1 reports phase coexistence for some related arc-melted pnictides. Post-annealing is therefore likely required [Reasonable Inference based on SS-4 and TS-1].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux routes require sealed tubes, controlled cooling, and flux removal, which add steps and handling complexity [TS-3, TS-5, SS-5]. Sn flux can operate near 880–950 °C [TS-3, TS-5], while Pd-rich flux may require ~1100 °C [SS-5]. The need for evacuated/sealed tubes and volatile P containment lowers the score [TS-3, TS-5, SS-5].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux synthesis improves mass transport and crystal growth, as demonstrated for LnCoP phosphides in Sn flux and Ba₂Pd₅Ge₄ in PdGe flux [TS-3, TS-5, SS-5]. This makes Route 3 kinetically attractive despite added processing steps. The uncertainty is whether BaPdP has a favorable crystallization window, because Ba–Pd–P competing phases are known [SS-4].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The large size mismatch between Ba and Pd triggers one amorphization/geometric-frustration criterion [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target has a crystalline P2₁/c description with well-defined Ba–Pd, Ba–P, Pd–P, and Pd–Pd distances [Target], so amorphization is not expected to dominate. CS/SS analogues are crystalline bulk phases made by solid-state routes [CS-1 to CS-3, SS-4], but explicit unit-cell atom count and mixed-connectivity criteria are insufficiently evidenced for the target.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaPdP is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from the exact Ba–Pd–P analogue Ba₂PdP₃ synthesized from elements in sealed Ar ampoules [SS-4] and from BaPtP/BaIrP/BaCuP analogues synthesized under similar Ar solid-state conditions [CS-1 to CS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is that Ba–Pd–P and Ba–T–P frameworks tolerate high-coordinate Ba and Pd–P bonding under ordinary solid-state temperatures [SS-4, CS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, since Ba₂PdP₃ synthesis reported impurities such as BaPd₂P₂ and PdP₂ [SS-4]. Overall, BaPdP should be experimentally reachable, but likely requires careful sealed-tube handling, repeated grinding, and phase-screening around the 1:1:1 composition [SS-4; Reasonable Inference].
