### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin and supports a high F1 score. CS contains several near-hull or hull-stable chlorides, including TlHgCl₃ at E_hull = 0 (CS-2), HgGaCl₄ at E_hull = 0 (CS-3), and Tl₂PdCl₄ at E_hull = 0 (CS-5). SS and TS also include multiple hull-stable halides, including AlBiTeCl₄ and Tl₂Hg₂PdCl₈ (SS-3/SS-5; TS-2/TS-3), so the thermodynamic analogue set supports synthesizability. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not prove kinetic accessibility or exclude hydration/volatilization during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 Solid-State

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/sealed handling because related halide solid-state syntheses use vacuum-sealed ampoules and glove-box handling (SS-4, TS-3). It is also penalized for toxicity and volatile chloride management because PbCl₂ is toxic and may redistribute in a sealed ampoule at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for this specific solid-state route and is neutral, score 50 from CS. The route is not severely penalized for temperature or pressure because SS-4 and TS-3 operate at moderate temperatures and no high pressure is required.

#### F3 — Route 1 Solid-State

Binary chlorides should react more readily than refractory oxides, giving a moderate precursor-activity score [Reasonable Inference]. Moderate-temperature sealed halide synthesis is supported by SS-4 and TS-3, so diffusion is not expected to be prohibitive. The limiting kinetic factor is structural reconstruction and Ag/Pb ordering into a 2D sheet, which is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS for AgPbCl₃. Remaining uncertainty is whether AgCl remains too inert under the selected temperature window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 Melt Growth

Melt growth has strong CS support from TlCuCl₃, TlHgCl₃, and TlCoCl₃ Bridgman/directed crystallization routes (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The severity score is reduced because these methods require sealed or controlled atmospheres, dehydration, thermal gradients, and slow translation/cooling (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). SS and TS provide insufficient direct support for melt growth of close analogues and are neutral, score 50 from SS and 50 from TS. The route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, which keeps F2 from being lower.

#### F3 — Route 2 Melt Growth

Kinetics are favorable because melt transport eliminates most solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 is particularly encouraging because TlHgCl₃, a 2D ternary chloride, is directionally crystallized at relatively low temperature. The structural reconstruction score is not perfect because the AgPbCl₃ phase may melt incongruently or segregate AgCl/PbCl₂ during cooling [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is lack of a provided AgCl–PbCl₂ phase diagram.

#### F2 — Route 3 Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal conditions are mild, with the key analogue Tl₂PdCl₄ made at 150 °C for 2 days in an evacuated water-containing ampoule (CS-5/SS-2/TS-1). The route is penalized for sealed/vacuum operation and multi-step ampoule preparation, which are explicitly described in CS-5/SS-2/TS-1. Toxic Pb precursor handling is an additional penalty [Reasonable Inference]. No deduction is applied for extreme temperature or high pressure because only autogenous hydrothermal pressure is expected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 Hydrothermal

Solution-mediated transport gives high precursor activity and avoids long-range solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The hydrothermal route is strongly supported by the repeated CS/SS/TS occurrence of Tl₂PdCl₄ synthesis from chloride salts in water (CS-5/SS-2/TS-1). The structural pathway remains uncertain because Tl₂PdCl₄ is 0D whereas the target AgPbCl₃ is 2D, so nucleation of the desired sheet is not guaranteed. AgCl’s low solubility is a kinetic risk unless chloride complexation is sufficient [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 Flux

Flux synthesis is supported by SS-3 and TS-2, where a complex chloride crystallizes from a Na[AlCl₄]/AlCl₃ melt at 130 °C under Ar in a sealed ampoule. The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed handling, multi-step flux separation, and toxic/corrosive chloride reagents [Reasonable Inference; handling complexity supported by SS-3/TS-2]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for molten chloride flux synthesis and is neutral, score 50 from CS. The route avoids high pressure and very high temperature.

#### F3 — Route 4 Flux

Molten chloride flux should provide high ion mobility and fast crystallization relative to direct solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. SS-3/TS-2 demonstrate that chloride fluxes can crystallize complex low-dimensional halides, supporting a high diffusion score. The structural score is limited because SS-3/TS-2 report low yield and hydrolysis/phase-control challenges. Flux incorporation or precipitation of binary chlorides remains an uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is relatively simple and crystalline, with no evidence of a very large unit cell from the target description, so C2 is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct atom-count evidence and are neutral for that sub-check. The target includes corner-sharing and edge-sharing motifs but no face-sharing motif, so the strict C3 criterion is not triggered by the given target description. A modest radius/coordination mismatch between Ag⁺ and Pb²⁺ is likely and triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgPbCl₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS set supports synthesis of chemically related ternary chlorides by melt growth, especially 2D TlHgCl₃ by directed crystallization (CS-2), while SS/TS support hydrothermal, flux, and sealed-ampoule routes for heavy-metal halides (SS-2/SS-3/SS-4; TS-1/TS-2/TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the use of chloride-rich sealed environments, which provide both chemical potential control and enough mobility for Ag/Pb/Cl ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against AgCl, PbCl₂, and possible off-stoichiometric chloride phases, especially under melt or flux conditions [Reasonable Inference; impurity/phase-control issues supported by SS-4 and SS-3/TS-2]. Overall, the target appears realistically synthesizable, with hydrothermal chloride-rich crystallization and Bridgman-type melt growth as the most promising complementary routes.
