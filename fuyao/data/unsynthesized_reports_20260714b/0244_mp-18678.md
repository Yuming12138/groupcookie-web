### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin specified in the prompt [Target]. CS/SS strongly support stability because Ce₂Rh₃Ge₅ and Dy₂Rh₃Ge₅ are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and close R₂Rh₃Ge₅ chemistry [CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-5]. TS analogues have E_hull values around 0.054–0.071 eV/atom and were experimentally synthesized, so TS supports accessibility of mildly metastable intermetallics [TS-1, TS-2, TS-4]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Nd₂Rh₃Ge₅ is a true low-temperature equilibrium phase or a slightly metastable member relative to competing Nd–Rh–Ge phases [Target].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild for intermetallic synthesis because the key reported germanide temperature is 1173 K / ~900 °C, below the >1000 °C deduction threshold [CS-1, CS-2, CS-5; SS-1, SS-5]. Argon and sealed quartz ampoules are standard in the CS/SS data, not extreme pressure or reactive-gas conditions [CS-1, CS-2; SS-1]. The main severity deduction is for air-sensitive Nd, inferred from the use of Ar handling for related rare-earth germanides [CS-2; SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Time is unspecified in CS/SS, so any long-duration severity is uncertain rather than directly scored.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The main kinetic limitation is refractory Rh, because elemental powders/filings are used and no highly reactive molecular precursor is reported [CS-2; SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. However, analogues form at 1173 K, far below the Rh melting point, indicating that solid-state interdiffusion is feasible in this chemical family [CS-2, CS-5; SS-5]. The structural reconstruction score is high because Ce₂Rh₃Ge₅ is the same orthorhombic Ibam R₂Rh₃Ge₅ type [CS-5; SS-5]. Uncertainty remains in whether Nd₂Rh₃Ge₅ competes with NdRhGe₂, which is also a stable Nd–Rh–Ge phase [CS-2; SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because arc/induction melting involves very high local temperatures and multiple operations, as in TS arc-melted intermetallics [TS-1, TS-2, TS-4]. Evacuated-tube annealing adds a controlled-atmosphere/vacuum penalty [TS-1, TS-2]. Ge loss during melting is a reasonable concern, although not explicitly reported in CS/SS for the target family [Reasonable Inference]. Nd air sensitivity again contributes a deduction [Reasonable Inference; TS-1 uses gettered Ar].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melting improves atomic contact and homogeneity compared with direct solid-state reaction, consistent with TS arc-melting protocols for intermetallics [TS-1, TS-2]. Post-annealing near 1173 K is compatible with R₂T₃Ge₅ formation conditions [CS-3, CS-5]. The structural pathway remains favorable because the target has a same-structure Ce analogue [CS-5; SS-5]. The uncertainty is whether the high-temperature melt first forms competing binaries or off-stoichiometric phases before annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is severe because TS-3 uses pressures up to 7.7 GPa and temperatures up to 1350 °C for RhGe. These conditions trigger the high-pressure and >1000 °C penalties [TS-3]. CS/SS provide insufficient evidence that Nd₂Rh₃Ge₅ requires high pressure, so the route is not preferred [CS-5; SS-5]. Air-sensitive Nd and multi-step high-pressure handling further reduce the score [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High pressure can rapidly form Rh–Ge phases from elemental powders in 5–30 min [TS-3], which supports some kinetic feasibility for Rh–Ge bonding. However, TS-3 concerns binary RhGe, not a ternary rare-earth R₂Rh₃Ge₅ framework, and CS/SS give insufficient evidence that high pressure helps form the Ibam target. Structural reconstruction is therefore uncertain and scored modestly. This route is best viewed as a contingency for metastable exploration, not the primary synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The size contrast between Nd and Rh/Ge is large, as reflected by long Nd–Rh/Nd–Ge distances versus shorter Rh–Ge/Ge–Ge contacts in the target and Ce analogue [Target; CS-5; SS-5]. That triggers one amorphization/complexity criterion. The data do not explicitly show a >40-atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence for the target, so C2 and C3 are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those checks. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with many crystalline analogues being reported [CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nd₂Rh₃Ge₅ is likely a **near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS evidence is strong: Ce₂Rh₃Ge₅ is a same-stoichiometry orthorhombic Ibam analogue, and Dy₂Rh₃Ge₅ is another R₂Rh₃Ge₅ analogue, although Dy adopts monoclinic C2/c [CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated 1173 K synthesis of closely related rare-earth Rh germanides from elemental precursors [CS-2, CS-5; SS-1, SS-5]. The main limiting factor is phase competition and lanthanide-size-dependent structural polymorphism, since related R₂Rh₃Ge₅ compounds can adopt different structures and some R₂T₃Ge₅ systems report phase-purity challenges [CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-5]. The best practical route is sealed-ampoule solid-state annealing under Ar, with regrinding/reannealing if XRD shows NdRhGe₂ or binary impurities [CS-2; SS-1; Reasonable Inference].
