### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.045 eV/atom, which falls within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈ 0.036–0.047 eV/atom are reported as experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4]. Chemically similar Nb–S/Se halides are also stable or nearly stable [CS1–CS3], which supports the chemical compatibility of Nb with mixed chalcogen/anion environments. Remaining uncertainty is that CS and TS do not directly prove the halide-free NbSeS₂ phase field, so the target may be metastable rather than a true equilibrium phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The severity is moderate because similar reactions use evacuated or sealed tubes, volatile chalcogens, and several-day to multi-week anneals [CS1, CS5, TS1–TS3]. The main kinetic penalty is refractory Nb diffusion, consistent with long high-temperature treatments in related chalcogenides [CS5, TS1, TS2]. Structural reconstruction is plausible because the target is layered and Nb–S/Se coordination resembles CS/SS analogues [CS3, SS1], but mixed S/Se ordering could require long annealing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a reported direct solid-state synthesis of NbSeS₂ itself, so Route 1 is plausible but not proven.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT / transport-assisted growth

CVT conditions are relatively mild in temperature but require vacuum sealing and volatile halogens, as shown for NbX₂Y₂ compounds at 400–515 °C over 3–4 weeks [CS3]. Kinetically, this route is favored because vapor transport bypasses much of the refractory Nb diffusion limitation, and layered Nb–S/Se halide crystals are directly reported [CS2, CS3]. The main uncertainty is chemical selectivity: the same halogen chemistry can stabilize Nb–S/Se–halide compounds rather than the halide-free target [CS1–CS3]. Thus, low transport-agent loading and starting from pre-reacted target-like powder are important [Reasonable Inference based on CS2, CS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure synthesis

The strongest structural evidence comes from NbS₃, a monoclinic P2₁/m 2D material with 8-coordinate Nb and comparable Nb–S distances synthesized at 700 °C and 2 GPa [SS1]. However, high-pressure equipment and possible >5 GPa operation, as used for NbPS/NbPSe-type compounds, make this route severe [SS4, TS5]. Kinetics may be acceptable because HPHT synthesis can form dense metastable structures quickly [SS1], but scale and phase-purity optimization are challenging. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS indicates whether high pressure is actually required for NbSeS₂, so this is best viewed as a contingency route for accessing the monoclinic topology.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: flux growth

Flux growth is supported by Nb–S framework crystallization in evacuated quartz tubes using excess alkali halide flux at 500–700 °C and slow cooling [SS2]. Kinetics are better than direct solid-state reaction because molten flux enhances mass transport [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. The limiting issue is chemical competition: CS analogues show Nb readily forms stable chalcogen-halide layered phases under halogen-rich conditions [CS1–CS3]. Therefore, this route is suitable for exploratory single-crystal growth but requires careful flux choice and post-growth phase identification.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Nb, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any mixed-cation disorder in the target. The described structure is not a very large complex cell, and the target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not the vertex + edge + face coexistence specified by the scoring rule. Structurally similar materials can be crystalline even with edge/corner-sharing Nb–S polyhedra [SS1, SS4], so amorphization tendency is low. The remaining uncertainty is whether S/Se disorder could broaden diffraction peaks, but this is a compositional-ordering issue rather than a strong amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbSeS₂ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.045 eV/atom and comparable thermodynamic analogues are experimentally accessible [TS1–TS4]. The CS data strongly supports Nb–S/Se layered chemistry and CVT/sealed-tube growth, while the SS data strongly supports the specific monoclinic P2₁/m-like 2D Nb–chalcogen topology [CS2, CS3, SS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube or CVT chemistry that maintains high S/Se chemical potential while enabling transport at moderate temperature [CS2, CS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with NbS₂/NbSe₂, NbS₃-like phases, and especially halide-containing Nb–S/Se phases if CVT or flux agents are used [CS1–CS3, SS1]. Overall, the target is realistically synthesizable, with CVT-assisted growth or carefully controlled sealed-tube reaction as the most practical starting points.
