### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0230 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability range for metastable or near-ground-state crystalline phases [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues at similar hull energies were experimentally synthesized, including Cu4Bi7S12 at 0.0293 eV/atom [TS1], BiS2 at 0.0185 eV/atom [TS2], and CsBi2CuS4 at 0.0234 eV/atom [TS5]. CS and SS confirm Bi–S layered chemistry is experimentally accessible, but they do not directly validate the exact hexagonal target polymorph; insufficient direct polymorph-stability evidence from CS/SS, neutral for that sub-claim [CS, SS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solid-state annealing

The route uses established evacuated sealed-tube conditions for Bi–S-containing materials [CS1, SS1, SS2, SS4], so the condition severity is moderate rather than extreme. Kinetics are helped by low-melting Bi and reactive sulfur [Reasonable Inference], but polymorph conversion from stable stibnite to the hexagonal sheeted target requires substantial reconstruction [Target vs CS1/SS1]. Remaining uncertainty is high because stable orthorhombic Bi2S3 may dominate at ambient pressure [CS1, SS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure quench

The condition severity is high because the closest Bi–S HP analogue required 5.5 GPa and up to 1200 °C [CS2, TS2]. Kinetics are favorable under HP/HT because reactions occur in 10–30 min with controlled cooling and quench [CS2, TS2]. The main uncertainty is the pressure window: 3.0 GPa gave no reaction and 7.5 GPa led to an unknown polymorph in the analogue [CS2, TS2], so target-phase reproducibility may be narrow.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Bridgman/melt growth

This route avoids high pressure and uses demonstrated sealed-ampoule melt growth for layered Bi chalcogenides [CS3] and Bi sulfide analogues [SS3]. Kinetics are favorable because melt growth minimizes long-range solid-state diffusion [CS3, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether cooling from the melt selects the hexagonal P-6m2 polymorph rather than stable orthorhombic Bi2S3 [Target, CS1, SS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT

CVT is well supported for layered sulfides and Bi-containing chalcogenides using halogen or halide transport agents [CS4, CS5, SS5, TS1, TS3]. Its lower growth temperature may help kinetic trapping of a metastable polymorph [Reasonable Inference], but composition and polymorph control are likely narrow because mixed products are reported in Cu–Bi–S CVT [SS5, TS1]. The route is also penalized by corrosive volatile transport agents [CS4, CS5, TS3; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Bi, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The target explicitly contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing BiS6 octahedra, triggering one amorphization/structural-frustration criterion [Target]. CS/SS/TS provide no direct evidence that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral [CS/SS/TS insufficient].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the ambient ground-state form, because its E_hull is 0.023 eV/atom [Target] while the known stibnite Bi2S3 analogue is at E_hull = 0 and crystallizes in orthorhombic Pnma [CS1, SS1]. The analogue set strongly supports the feasibility of Bi–S crystallization in sealed tubes, melt growth, CVT, and high-pressure synthesis [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the low hull energy combined with extensive precedent for layered Bi–chalcogenide crystal growth [Target, CS3, SS3, TS1–TS5]. The most important limiting factor is polymorph competition with ordinary stibnite Bi2S3 [CS1, SS1]. The best practical first attempt is sealed-ampoule melt/Bridgman growth with careful sulfur stoichiometry and cooling control [CS3, SS3; Reasonable Inference].
