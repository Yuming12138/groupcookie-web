### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the favorable cutoff and receives 90/100 by the required rubric. SS provides strong support because Sr(BrO₃)₂ and Mg(BrO₃)₂ are at 0 eV/atom (SS1, SS2), AgBrO₃ is nearly on the hull (SS3), and Ba(BrO₃)₂ is only 0.012 eV/atom above hull (SS5). CS also supports Pb halate stability through Pb(ClO₃)₂ at 0 eV/atom (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is that 0.050 eV/atom is borderline metastable rather than deeply stable.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild condition severity because SS1/SS2/TS1 show aqueous crystallization or slow evaporation at ambient or mild temperatures, with no high pressure or controlled atmosphere. The only significant F2 deduction is Pb toxicity [Reasonable Inference], while hydration sensitivity is suggested by CS1/SS5. Kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization avoids solid-state diffusion and because SS1/SS2/SS5 demonstrate structurally similar bromate frameworks. Uncertainty is whether Pb(BrO₃)₂ crystallizes as the target anhydrous C2/c form directly or first forms a hydrate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because CS1/SS1/SS5 use vacuum or dry handling, and CS1/SS5 show multi-step dehydration/extraction approaches for related halates. Kinetically, however, Route 2 is excellent because dehydration of a compositionally matching hydrate requires less structural reconstruction than starting from dense oxides. SS1 is particularly relevant because it mentions Pb(BrO₃)₂ in the anhydrous halate preparation series. The main uncertainty is the dehydration window: overheating may decompose bromate [Reasonable Inference], while under-drying may leave hydrate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal synthesis has moderate condition severity because CS2/CS5/TS4 require sealed Teflon-lined autoclaves, slow cooling, and multi-step workup. It is kinetically plausible because hydrothermal water transport promotes oxyanion crystal growth, as shown for iodates and selenites in CS2/CS3/CS5/TS4. However, SS indicates bromates can often be made under milder conditions, so hydrothermal treatment may be unnecessary. The largest uncertainty is sealed-vessel compatibility with oxidizing bromate chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Metathesis precipitation is mild because SS3 demonstrates AgBrO₃ formation from KBrO₃ and AgNO₃ at 25 °C, and SS2 supports aqueous evaporation. F2 is penalized for multiple purification/recrystallization steps and Pb toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are good because soluble salts react rapidly in water, but structural quality may require recrystallization and hydration control, as suggested by CS1/SS5. The uncertainty is solubility: Pb(BrO₃)₂ may not precipitate cleanly or may form hydrated intermediates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an oxyhalate salt with chemically distinct Pb²⁺ coordination and BrO₃⁻ trigonal pyramidal units, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned for large cation/coordination-center contrast [Reasonable Inference]. SS bromate analogues with similar BrO₃ units crystallize readily, including Sr(BrO₃)₂, Mg(BrO₃)₂, AgBrO₃, and Ba(BrO₃)₂ (SS1–SS5), which lowers the practical amorphization concern. The target description does not provide enough unit-cell atom count or mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing information to trigger C2 or C3; insufficient evidence from TS for these structural-risk checks. Remaining uncertainty concerns hydrate/disorder formation rather than true glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pb(O₃Br)₂ is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and many bromate/halate analogues are experimentally reported near or on the hull (SS1–SS5, CS1, TS1–TS2). The analogue support is unusually strong: SS1 explicitly discusses anhydrous Pb(BrO₃)₂ in the same halate preparation context, while CS1 supports the closely related Pb(ClO₃)₂ chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is solution mobility: aqueous crystallization or low-temperature dehydration avoids refractory solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated by SS1/SS2/TS1. The most critical limiting factor is hydration/dehydration control, since related halates require vacuum or glove-box handling to avoid rehydration (CS1, SS5). A secondary limitation is safety: Pb salts are toxic and bromates are oxidizing [Reasonable Inference].
