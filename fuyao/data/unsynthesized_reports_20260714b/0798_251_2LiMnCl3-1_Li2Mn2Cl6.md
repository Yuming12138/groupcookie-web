### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0330 eV/atom, which falls inside the high-probability ≤0.05 eV/atom band. CS and TS include synthesized Li₂CrCl₄ at E_hull ≈ 0.039 eV/atom, and SS includes synthesized Li₂MnBr₄ at E_hull ≈ 0.015 eV/atom, showing that comparable metastabilities are experimentally accessible. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific trigonal P-31m LiMnCl₃ polymorph is the experimentally selected phase rather than a competing Li–Mn chloride.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The route uses moderate temperatures, consistent with CS Li₂CrCl₄ at 400 °C and TS Li₂FeBr₄ at 723 K, but it requires vacuum/sealed tubes and dry handling, so F2 is penalized. Kinetics are acceptable because chlorides are reactive salts and analogues form at low temperature, but CS reports long 1-week treatments and phase-control difficulty, lowering the structural-reconstruction component. SS provides direct Mn-halide support through MnCl₂/MnBr₂ analogues, while solution-style TS evidence is insufficient for this route and treated as neutral where not relevant.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Melt growth has the same sealed-ampoule and moisture-sensitivity penalties as Route 1, but it has better kinetics because a molten chloride medium improves diffusion. CS directly supports this with Li₂CoCl₄ melt growth and NaScCl₄ Bridgman cooling, while SS supports Mn chloride melting for TlMnCl₃ and co-melting for Li₂MnBr₄. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS reports Mn-halide phase transformations and difficulty obtaining single crystals, so structural reconstruction is not scored near ideal.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
TS analogues show very mild aqueous crystallization for K–Pb and Na–Hg chlorides, so the formal F2 severity is low. However, CS provides insufficient evidence for aqueous Li–Mn chloride synthesis and is scored neutral at 50 as a source signal; SS instead points to anhydrous Mn chloride melt chemistry. The target is an anhydrous layered chloride, so hydrate formation and failure to isolate LiMnCl₃ are the main uncertainties, making this an exploratory rather than preferred route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one amorphization trigger: substantial Li⁺/Mn²⁺ size and coordination mismatch, which could slow ordering. The structure description does not indicate a large >40 atom cell or face/edge/vertex sharing coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS and SS show that mixed Li/transition-metal halide frameworks can crystallize, reducing concern about amorphization, but phase competition remains possible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiMnCl₃ at 0.033 eV/atom above hull is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase, based on the target hull energy and comparison to synthesized CS/TS analogues near 0.04 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports anhydrous LiCl + transition-metal chloride sealed-tube chemistry, while SS gives especially relevant Mn chloride and Mn bromide precedents. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical compatibility of chloride salts under sealed melt or low-temperature sintering conditions. The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because related Mn-halide and Li-transition-metal chloride systems show polymorphism, difficult crystallization, and slow transformations. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with melt/self-flux crystallization in a rigorously dry sealed ampoule as the best route.
