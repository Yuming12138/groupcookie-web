### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0350 eV/atom, which falls in the high-synthesizability range ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS analogues with comparable metastability, including Li₂CrCl₄ at 0.0394 eV/atom and Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.0287 eV/atom, are reported as synthesizable by ampoule solid-state routes (TS3, TS4). SS1/TS1 also shows NaHgCl₃ at 0.0552 eV/atom can crystallize from solution, suggesting slightly metastable layered halides are accessible. Remaining uncertainty is whether the P-31m LiCdCl₃ polymorph is the experimentally selected polymorph rather than another Li–Cd–Cl phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for evacuated/sealed ampoule requirements, volatile/toxic chloride handling, and multi-step drying/loading/heating. These penalties are supported by evacuated ampoule syntheses in CS5, TS3, and TS4 and by anhydrous halide handling in CS3–CS5. The temperature is moderate, with no evidence for >1000 °C or high pressure in relevant halide analogues. The main uncertainty is whether CdCl₂ vapor pressure demands a more robust ampoule protocol than the Zn/Cr/Fe analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halide precursors are much more reactive than refractory oxides, and analogous Li halides react at 400–450 °C in CS4, CS5, TS3, and TS4. Diffusion should be aided by partial melting or chloride mobility, similar to melt/ampoule halide routes in CS2, CS3, SS2, and SS4. Structural reconstruction is less certain because CS1 shows CdCl₆ edge-sharing but not the exact LiCdCl₃ sheet topology, and CS3/CS4 indicate some Li-containing halides can require long annealing.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because it uses a sealed evacuated or inert ampoule and slow cooling, as in CS2, SS2, and SS4. It avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but CdCl₂ toxicity/volatility and ampoule loading add practical risk [Reasonable Inference]. The score is not higher because single-crystal melt growth often requires careful temperature gradients and composition control, as implied by slow-cooling protocols in CS2 and SS2.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable because the reaction occurs through a melt or self-flux, directly supported by CS2, SS2, and SS4. Ion transport is expected to be rapid in the melt [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling gives the framework time to order. Structural success is plausible because related ternary halides crystallize from melts, but the exact LiCdCl₃ P-31m layered phase is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest condition severity because SS1/TS1 and TS2 use aqueous solution crystallization at room temperature or from hot water without special atmosphere or pressure. The main deduction is CdCl₂ toxicity and likely moisture sensitivity/hydrate control [Reasonable Inference], with moisture sensitivity of related halide crystals noted in SS4 and air instability in TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for direct aqueous LiCdCl₃ synthesis, so the mildness of the conditions does not guarantee phase selectivity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Precursor activity and ion transport are excellent in solution, as supported by aqueous crystallization in SS1/TS1 and TS2. The structural-reconstruction score is only moderate because solution chemistry may favor hydrates, chloro-complex salts, or different stoichiometries rather than anhydrous P-31m LiCdCl₃ [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest positive evidence is SS1/TS1, where a structurally similar 2D NaHgCl₃ phase crystallizes directly from aqueous chloride solution.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 shares the sealed-ampoule, volatile chloride, toxic precursor, and multi-step penalties of Routes 1 and 2. Flux use is supported indirectly by SS5, where HgCl₂ acts as a flux-like component, and by melt/ampoule routes in CS2, CS3, SS2, and SS4. No high-pressure or ultrahigh-temperature penalty applies. The uncertainty is whether flux separation can be done without dissolving or transforming LiCdCl₃ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux-assisted annealing should improve kinetics relative to direct solid-state synthesis because chloride fluxes enable mass transport [Reasonable Inference], consistent with melt-growth analogues in CS2, SS2, and SS4. Precursor activity is high because all components are halide salts, not refractory oxides. Structural ordering may still require extended annealing, as indicated by several-week to 20-month examples in CS3 and CS4.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant cation-size/coordination mismatch between Li and Cd, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference], and the target description explicitly gives distinct Li trigonal-planar and Cd octahedral coordination. However, the target does not have a large unit cell in the provided description and does not show vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence. Thus amorphization/glass formation risk is low–medium rather than high; the main risk is local disorder or polymorphism, not intrinsic amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiCdCl₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a highly metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.035 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well: CS1 confirms CdCl₆ edge-sharing chloride chemistry, SS1/TS1 supports layered A–M–Cl₃ solution crystallization, and CS3–CS5/TS3–TS4 support anhydrous Li-containing halides by sealed ampoule routes. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of chloride salts, especially in solution or molten chloride media. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: LiCl and CdCl₂ are hygroscopic/soluble, CdCl₂ is volatile and toxic, and hydrates or competing double-chloride phases may form. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with solution crystallization as the mild first attempt and sealed melt/ampoule synthesis as the more controlled backup.
