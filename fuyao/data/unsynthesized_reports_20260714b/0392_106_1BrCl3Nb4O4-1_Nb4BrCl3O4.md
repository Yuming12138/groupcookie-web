### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0270 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and justifying a high F1 score. TS strongly supports this because TiOCl has a similar E_hull of 0.0329 eV/atom and is experimentally grown by gas transport. CS also supports low-energy oxyhalide accessibility because Pr₃NbO₄Cl₆ has E_hull = 0.0192 eV/atom and is synthesized in a sealed quartz tube. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee a broad phase field, especially for mixed Br/Cl reduced Nb oxyhalides.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state route

For condition severity, CS supports sealed evacuated quartz synthesis at 950 °C for a niobium oxychloride, and TS/SS support vacuum-sealed reduced oxide reactions; these conditions are demanding but not extreme. The main penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile halides, multiple handling steps, and moisture-sensitive halide precursors. Kinetically, the route is less favorable because Nb₂O₅/NbO₂/Nb are refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow; CS reports phase-control problems for Pr₃NbO₄Cl₆. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct formation of Nb₄O₄BrCl₃ by simple oxide/halide sintering.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Sn-flux route

SS provides the strongest basis for this route: Nb₂O₂F₃, a close Nb–O–halide octahedral analogue, was made in Sn flux using Nb, SnO, and SnF₂ in welded Nb containers. The route receives severity deductions because SS required an Ar glovebox, welded Nb containers, and evacuated silica jackets. Kinetically, flux improves mass transport and can stabilize reduced Nb oxyhalide frameworks, but replacing F with larger Cl/Br is a significant extrapolation. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS exists for Sn-flux synthesis of mixed chloride/bromide Nb oxyhalides specifically.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Chemical vapor transport

TS and SS both support CVT for TiOCl, which is structurally and thermodynamically very relevant: it is a 2D oxychloride with edge/corner-sharing MO₄Cl₂ octahedra. This route avoids the worst solid-state diffusion penalties because volatile metal halides can transport material through the vapor phase. Severity remains moderate because sealed ampoules, halide vapor pressure, and toxic/moisture-sensitive halides are required. The largest uncertainty is whether Br and Cl incorporate in the desired 1:3 ratio rather than forming separate Nb oxychloride/oxybromide phases.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Molten-salt flux

SS supports halide mineralizer/flux use in niobium oxide synthesis through CaNb₂O₄ grown with CaCl₂·2H₂O at 1100 °C, and CS indicates KBr can assist crystal growth in related chalcogenide/oxide systems. The route is penalized for possible >1000 °C operation, sealed conditions, volatile halides, and multi-step flux removal. Kinetics should be better than dry solid-state synthesis but worse than CVT because Nb oxide dissolution and mixed-halide ordering remain uncertain. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for molten-salt growth of a TiOCl-like or Nb₄O₄BrCl₃-like layered oxyhalide.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Nb as a cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; this is based on the target composition, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient contradictory evidence. The formula unit has 12 atoms, and no >40-atom unit cell is indicated, so the large-cell amorphization criterion is not triggered. The target and SS/TS analogues involve edge and corner sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not triggered. The residual uncertainty is that low-symmetry monoclinic P2 and mixed Cl/Br ordering could still broaden diffraction peaks or yield stacking disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₄O₄BrCl₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom, and TS includes experimentally accessible TiOCl at a very similar E_hull. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: TS/SS TiOCl supports 2D oxychloride vapor-transport growth, SS Nb₂O₂F₃ supports reduced Nb oxyhalide formation under sealed flux conditions, and CS supports sealed-tube oxychloride chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is controlled halide chemical potential in a sealed environment, which can both transport Nb and stabilize terminal/bridging Nb–Cl/Br bonds. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of reduced Nb oxidation state and mixed Br/Cl stoichiometry without forming Nb oxides, Nb chlorides/bromides, or separate oxyhalide phases. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with CVT/halide vapor transport the most attractive first attempt.
