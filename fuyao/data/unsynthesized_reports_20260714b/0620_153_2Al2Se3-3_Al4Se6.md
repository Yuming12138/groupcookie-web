### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper boundary of the 90–100 rubric bin, so F1 = 90. TS analogues with similar thermodynamic placement, e.g. E_hull ≈ 0.054–0.065 eV/atom, are reported synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state reactions [TS1–TS4], supporting accessibility of slightly metastable chalcogenides. CS gives additional support because metastable Al₂S₃ polymorphs with much higher calculated hull energies are reported from quench synthesis [CS1, CS2]. The uncertainty is that the target’s exact monoclinic C2 Al₂Se₃ polymorph is not directly reported in the provided CS/SS/TS data.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses temperatures below or near 850 °C, which are not extreme and are directly comparable to 750 °C Al₂S₃ synthesis [CS1, CS2] and below the 1000 °C Mg–Al–Se synthesis [SS4]. The major severity penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile Se management, both strongly indicated by sealed ampoule use across SS and TS selenides [SS4, TS4, TS5]. The staged heat/hold/quench profile adds procedural complexity, as in Al₂S₃ synthesis [CS1, CS2]. The remaining uncertainty is whether elemental Al + Se directly selects the desired C2 layered polymorph rather than another Al₂Se₃ phase.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from low-melting Se and relatively non-refractory Al **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with successful Al/chalcogen sealed reactions [CS1, CS2, SS4]. However, complete formation from elements requires building the Al–Se framework from scratch, and long 7-day anneals in Al₂S₃ indicate sluggish ordering or polymorph selection [CS1, CS2]. The target contains edge- and corner-sharing AlSe₅ polyhedra, so structural reconstruction may be more demanding than forming simple tetrahedral AlSe₄ networks seen in many SS analogues [SS1, SS4, SS5]. Thus F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high temperatures above 1000 °C and uses the same general sealed-tube range as Al₂S₃ and BaAl₄Se₇ syntheses [CS1, CS2, SS1]. Vacuum sealing remains necessary because Al₂Se₃/selenides are handled in sealed tubes in SS analogues [SS1], and volatile Se control is common in selenide syntheses [SS4, TS4, TS5]. The use of preformed Al₂Se₃ reduces the number of chemical reaction steps **[Reasonable Inference]**, so the multi-step penalty is not applied. The main uncertainty is the inferred moisture sensitivity and polymorph selectivity of Al₂Se₃, which are not directly quantified by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

Using Al₂Se₃ as a same-composition precursor should reduce diffusion length and chemical reconstruction relative to elemental synthesis **[Reasonable Inference]**, and Al₂Se₃ is explicitly used as a precursor in BaAl₄Se₇ synthesis [SS1]. The thermal range is supported by Al₂S₃ and BaAl₄Se₇ conditions [CS1, CS2, SS1]. However, converting an existing Al₂Se₃ form into the target C2 layered polymorph may still require substantial rearrangement, especially because many related Al–Se materials contain AlSe₄ tetrahedra rather than AlSe₅ square pyramids [SS1, SS4, SS5]. Therefore Route 2 receives the highest F3 but not a near-perfect score.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses standard CVT temperatures similar to GaSe/GaS growth, 850/750 °C or 910/850 °C [CS3, CS5], so there is no high-temperature penalty. It does require evacuated ampoules, iodine, volatile Se, and a temperature gradient, all of which increase severity [CS3, CS5, TS4]. The method is multi-step by nature and uses hazardous iodine, so the condition severity remains comparable to Route 1. The largest uncertainty is that no provided source directly demonstrates Al₂Se₃ CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT has favorable crystallization potential for layered chalcogenides because GaSe/GaS single crystals are grown by iodine vapor transport [CS3–CS5]. However, Al transport as volatile iodide species is only a **[Reasonable Inference]**, and insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Al₂Se₃ vapor transport. Composition control may be more difficult than in bulk annealing because Se and iodine vapor pressures can shift stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**, and phase-control issues are noted in related vapor/iodine-assisted chalcogenide syntheses [CS5, TS4]. This justifies a lower F3 than Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Al, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise. The target description gives a small number of inequivalent sites, so the >40 atom unit-cell criterion is not triggered, though the exact conventional cell size is not provided. The mixed corner- and edge-sharing AlSe₅ square-pyramidal connectivity does trigger C3, and mixed connectivity in related selenides is associated with phase-control challenges [SS1, TS1–TS3]. The resulting F4 = 78 reflects low–medium risk rather than severe amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a borderline metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the edge of the high-synthesizability stability bin. The CS data strongly support sealed-tube Al–chalcogen polymorph control, especially via quenching in Al₂S₃ [CS1, CS2], while SS data show Al₂Se₃ is an accessible precursor in selenide solid-state chemistry [SS1]. TS data support that chalcogenides with similar thermodynamic metastability can be synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state routes [TS1–TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary composition and availability of Al₂Se₃ or Al + Se precursors [CS1, CS2, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is selective formation of the monoclinic C2 layered AlSe₅ polymorph rather than a competing Al₂Se₃ structure, since no provided source directly reports this exact target polymorph.
