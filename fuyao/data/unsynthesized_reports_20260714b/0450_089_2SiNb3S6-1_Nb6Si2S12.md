### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0370 eV/atom places it in the high synthesizability band of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS analogues with E_hull ≈0.030–0.047 eV/atom are reported as synthesized phases, so the thermodynamic penalty is not prohibitive [TS1–TS5]. CS and SS analogues are mostly stable Nb–S compounds, supporting the broader chemical space but not directly proving the Si analogue [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy can still correspond to a narrow metastable window or ordering-sensitive phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

The oxide/H₂S route is strongly supported for Nb₃VS₆ and Mn(NbS₂)₄ analogues, including 1000 °C air calcination, 1150 °C H₂S reduction, 800 °C annealing, and slow cooling [CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2]. Its F2 is lowered by high temperature, toxic H₂S, and multi-step processing [CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2]. Its F3 is limited because SiO₂ and Nb₂O₅ are refractory oxides and full oxide-to-sulfide reconstruction is required [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives insufficient evidence for this specific oxide-to-Nb–Si–S route [TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

The elemental sealed-tube route is supported by Nb₂PdS₅ synthesis from Nb, Pd, and S at 850 °C with Ar glovebox handling and quartz vacuum encapsulation [CS5], and by PbMo₆S₈ syntheses from elements under controlled sealed/inert conditions [TS3]. F2 is moderate because the route avoids H₂S and >1000 °C but still requires vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur control [CS5, TS3]. F3 is low because elemental Nb and Si are diffusion-limited and require complete reconstruction into the target framework [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives only partial support through sealed-tube NbS₂ chemistry, not direct elemental Nb–Si–S formation [SS4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

CVT is directly supported for MnNb₃S₆ using iodine vapor and a 1050 → 900 °C gradient for 15 days [CS2, SS5], and for NbS₂-related crystals using iodine in evacuated silica tubes [SS4]. F2 is penalized by >1000 °C hot-zone temperature, sealed iodine vapor, and the likely need for a pre-reacted feed [CS2, SS4, SS5]. F3 is moderate because vapor transport can overcome bulk diffusion, but composition control may be difficult; MnNb₃S₆ references report lower Mn content than starting powder and biphasic/intergrowth issues [CS2, SS5]. TS gives insufficient evidence for iodine transport behavior of Nb–Si–S [TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

The sulfide-precursor route has the best balance because NbS₂/NbSe₂ plus metals produced Nb₃SnS₆/Nb₃PbS₆-type phases at 700–900 °C over 1–4 weeks [CS3, SS3]. Its F2 is favorable because it avoids H₂S and >1000 °C, though sulfur volatility still motivates sealed-tube conditions [CS3, SS3, CS5]. Its F3 is stronger than elemental synthesis because Nb–S bonding is preformed in NbS₂, but long annealing indicates slow cation ordering and partial reconstruction [CS3, SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether Si can occupy the same structural role as Sn/Pb/Mn/V analogues without forming SiS₂ or Nb silicides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization/disorder trigger is the large Nb–Si cation size and bonding mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The provided target description does not establish a >40-atom unit cell or explicit mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so those checks are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS. Crystalline layered Nb–S frameworks are common across the analogues, which reduces concern for glass formation [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the orthorhombic Pmc2₁ ordering may require slow cooling or long annealing [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SiS₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deep ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom [target]. Similar TS compounds with E_hull values in the ~0.03–0.05 eV/atom range are experimentally accessible, so the thermodynamic penalty is acceptable [TS1–TS5]. The CS/SS data strongly support synthesis of Nb–S layered and Nb₃MS₆-like frameworks, especially using NbS₂-derived precursors [CS2, CS3, SS3, SS5]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of NbS₂ as a preformed Nb–S precursor, reducing the need to build the entire framework from refractory elemental Nb [CS2, CS3, SS3, SS5]. The most important limiting factor is Si incorporation and ordering, because no supplied analogue directly demonstrates Nb–Si–S formation and Si may compete through SiS₂, residual SiO₂, or Nb silicide formation [Reasonable Inference].
