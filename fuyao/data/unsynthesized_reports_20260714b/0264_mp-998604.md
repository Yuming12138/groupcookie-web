### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest scoring bin. TS provides useful support because several thermodynamically similar halides with E_hull values around 0.04–0.08 eV/atom are reported synthesized, including TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, and TS-5. CS and SS contain stable or near-stable halide analogues, but CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for the target’s exact thermodynamic stability, so they are treated as neutral-to-supportive rather than decisive. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.05 eV/atom may represent a metastable phase that requires kinetic trapping or careful solution conditions.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because the closest K/Pb chloride analogue is obtained by heating an aqueous KCl/PbCl₂ solution to boiling and slowly cooling, with no special atmosphere or pressure (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). TS-4 also supports mild aqueous crystallization for a thermodynamically similar chloride. The only formal severity deduction is PbCl₂ toxicity; CS/SS/TS do not indicate high pressure, high temperature, or vacuum for this route. Remaining uncertainty is whether RbPbCl₃ competes with other Rb–Pb–Cl phases under the same solution composition.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the reaction proceeds through solution, avoiding long-range solid-state diffusion; this is directly supported by slow-cooling crystallization in CS-1/SS-5/TS-3 and room-temperature aqueous crystallization in TS-4. Solution transport and lead chloride complexation are reasonable physicochemical mechanisms **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction is still not perfect because the target Cmcm RbPbCl₃ structure is not explicitly reported in the references, and K/Pb analogues may crystallize in related but different stoichiometries.

#### F2 — Route 2

Acidic chloride solution routes use moderate temperatures and standard glassware, supported by SS-1, SS-2, TS-2, and CS-2. The route is penalized for multiple operations and Pb toxicity, while concentrated HCl handling is a practical hazard not explicitly included in the F2 deduction table. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that concentrated HCl is optimal for RbPbCl₃ specifically, so the route is slightly less favorable than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics should be favorable because SS-1 reports immediate precipitation of a chloride double perovskite upon CsCl addition, and SS-2/TS-2 report simple mixing in concentrated HCl. High chloride activity should improve PbCl₂ dissolution through chloroplumbate complexes **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is speciation: concentrated HCl could stabilize molecular or lower-dimensional chloroplumbates rather than the target RbPbCl₃ framework **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis is more severe because TS-1 and TS-5 require evacuated sealed tubes/ampoules, and TS-5 uses glovebox preparation plus week-long heating. For RbPbCl₃, PbCl₂ toxicity and possible volatility require sealed handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS-3 shows that some Rb chloride syntheses can be much more demanding under high Cl₂ pressure, but that is tied to Pu oxidation-state control and is insufficient evidence for requiring similar conditions for Pb(II).

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary chlorides are more reactive than oxides, and TS-1/TS-5 demonstrate that halide solid-state routes can work. However, TS-5 required 1 week and reported phase-control difficulty, while TS-1 had a small impurity, so kinetics and phase purity are less favorable than solution routes. The target likely forms at moderate temperature, but solid-state diffusion and volatile PbCl₂ loss remain uncertainties **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large A/B cation size mismatch, as reflected by 8-coordinate Rb and octahedral Pb environments in the target description, so C1 is triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. C2 is not triggered because the Cmcm ABCl₃ cell is expected to be modest rather than >40 atoms, although CS/SS/TS do not directly provide the target cell atom count. C3 is not triggered because the target description mentions edge and corner sharing but not face sharing; related SS/TS analogues do show complex connectivity, but the scoring criterion is based on the target.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbPbCl₃ is likely near the metastability boundary rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom; TS analogues show that halides with similar hull energies can nevertheless be synthesized. The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports solution-based synthesis, especially because the closest K/Pb chloride analogue is crystallized by slow cooling from a boiling aqueous alkali chloride/PbCl₂ solution (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is high chloride activity in solution, which should solubilize PbCl₂ and promote direct crystallization of a chloroplumbate framework **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible Rb–Pb–Cl stoichiometries and hydration/moisture sensitivity, as related halides can decompose in air or moist air (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2). Overall, the best route is a mild aqueous slow-cooling crystallization with careful stoichiometry and humidity control.
