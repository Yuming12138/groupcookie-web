### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0350 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and giving a high thermodynamic score. TS analogues include synthesized phases with similar small positive hull energies, such as FeTaTe₃ at 0.020 eV/atom, Cr(RhTe₂)₂ at 0.034 eV/atom, U₂Te₅ at 0.046 eV/atom, and IrTe₂ at 0.038 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS and SS include several E_hull = 0 layered tellurides but do not directly establish the target’s stability, so they are supportive but not decisive (CS-1/CS-3, SS-1/SS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that 35 meV/atom may indicate a metastable ordered V/W derivative rather than a strict ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Direct solid-state synthesis uses standard evacuated silica/quartz tube equipment and elemental powders, which is well supported by CS-1, CS-2, and CS-3. The severity penalty comes from vacuum sealing, volatile Te, long heating, staged heating, and possible regrinding, all seen in CS-1 and CS-3. TS-1 and TS-3 show similar evacuated-tube telluride reactions but also include extra atmosphere or grinding steps, reinforcing moderate severity. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct synthesis of the exact ternary target, so SS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental W and V are refractory, so diffusion in a solid-state route is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1/CS-2/CS-3 require 4–12 days at ~1000 °C, and CS-1/CS-3 report regrinding/excess Te or impurity issues, supporting a low-to-moderate kinetic score. The structural similarity to WTe₂ helps because the target is molybdenite-derived and WTe₂-like (SS-1), but cation ordering of V/W is not directly demonstrated. Remaining uncertainty is whether Te-rich conditions are enough to equilibrate V and W sites.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

CVT avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, since WTe₂ CVT uses 820/700 °C for 8 days (SS-1) and U₂Te₅ uses 770–730 °C (TS-4). The severity penalty is mainly from vacuum ampoules, volatile Te/TeBr₄ or TeCl₄, halide toxicity/corrosivity, and multi-step setup (SS-1, TS-4). CS-4/CS-5 provide additional CVT-style conditions using TeBr₄, but their precursor description appears to be WTe₂-based rather than a direct Ta–Ir/Os synthesis, so I weight SS-1 more strongly. Remaining uncertainty is V transport and target stoichiometry under halogen transport.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT receives the best kinetic score because SS-1 demonstrates growth of structurally closest WTe₂ crystals by TeBr₄ transport at 820 °C, which bypasses bulk diffusion of refractory W. The target has similar layered 6-coordinate W–Te/V–Te geometry, so structural reconstruction should be modest relative to the WTe₂ analogue (SS-1). TS-4 shows another telluride can be transported with TeCl₄ in a similar temperature gradient, supporting generality. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether V and W form volatile halide species at compatible rates, affecting V:W = 1:3 control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Te flux growth is supported by TS-5 for IrTe₂, but it uses high temperatures up to 1160 °C, slow cooling, sealed evacuated tubes, and post-growth decanting/quenching. Large Te excess increases volatile-component and ampoule-pressure risk, and Te handling is toxic [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-3 support the usefulness of excess Te, but they also show phase-control issues. Insufficient evidence from SS for Te-flux growth of WTe₂-like VW₃Te₈, so SS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Molten Te flux should substantially improve mass transport compared with dry powder sintering, as TS-5 shows single-crystal IrTe₂ growth from Te-rich flux. Excess Te also improved related ternary tellurides in CS-1 and CS-3, supporting better reaction kinetics than stoichiometric solid-state firing. However, V and W solubilities and ordered VW₃Te₈ nucleation from Te flux are not directly reported, so the score remains below CVT. The route may favor binary WTe₂ or VTeₓ impurity crystals unless the cooling window is optimized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline layered molybdenite-derived compound with modest V–Te and W–Te bond-length differences in the target description, so severe size-mismatch amorphization is not expected [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 shows a closely related layered WTe₂ framework can crystallize well by CVT, which supports a low amorphization risk for a WTe₂-derived sheet. CS/TS provide little direct amorphization evidence, so those sources are treated as neutral for this sub-factor. The remaining uncertainty is whether V/W ordering causes stacking disorder or nanoscale phase separation rather than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VW₃Te₈ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.035 eV/atom, and TS analogues with similar hull energies have been synthesized (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar WTe₂, which is grown by TeBr₄ CVT under accessible sealed-tube conditions and shares layered 6-coordinate W–Te chemistry (SS-1). Chemically similar multinary tellurides are accessible by high-temperature evacuated-tube reactions, but CS-2 and CS-3 show that impurity phases are common. The key enabling factor is the availability of Te-rich sealed-tube/CVT chemistry that lowers kinetic barriers for refractory W-containing tellurides (SS-1, CS-1, CS-3). The main limiting factor is precise V/W/Te stoichiometry and cation ordering, which are not directly proven in the provided analogues and may compete with WTe₂ and VTeₓ formation.
