### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high-synthesizability threshold and comparable to synthesized analogues such as RuSr₂GdCu₂O₈ at 0.048 eV/atom [CS3], Cd₂Re₂O₇ at 0.0476 eV/atom [SS4], and TcPbO₃ at 0.064 eV/atom [TS1]. This suggests the target is probably accessible as either a near-ground-state or mildly metastable oxide. Uncertainty remains because the exact decomposition products and oxygen chemical potential dependence are not provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because Sr–Ru oxides require 1000–1050 °C, long holds, flowing O₂, and slow cooling [CS3], while Sr₂LnTaCu₂O₈ uses 1273–1373 K air firing with grinding/repressing [CS5]. Kinetics are the main weakness: RuO₂ and inferred IrO₂ are refractory noble-metal oxides, and CS3/CS5 show multi-step ceramic processing rather than rapid low-temperature formation. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct ambient-pressure synthesis of this exact Sr–Ru–Fe–Ir P1 structure, so kinetic confidence is limited.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed oxidizing ampoule

This route is conditionally useful for oxygen-potential control because RbOs₂O₆ required AgO-generated oxygen in a sealed quartz tube [SS2], and PbRe₂O₆ used evacuated ampoule synthesis [SS3]. However, lower temperature sealed synthesis worsens long-range Sr/Ru/Fe/Ir diffusion, and the target has more cations than the SS2/SS3 analogues. Insufficient evidence from CS for sealed-ampoule Sr–Ru–Fe–Ir synthesis, so this is a fallback route rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure synthesis

High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for structurally or thermodynamically similar noble-metal oxides: triclinic KOs₂O₆ at 3 GPa/900 °C [SS1], ReMnO₄ at 5.5 GPa/1473 K [TS2], and Sr/Ba–Os oxides under multianvil conditions [TS4]. The route is penalized because >5 GPa equipment is specialized and because phase fields can be narrow [SS1, TS2, TS4]. It may be the best rescue route if the P1 phase is metastable and not obtainable at ambient pressure.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Floating-zone growth

FZ receives the best kinetic score because molten-zone transport can overcome refractory cation diffusion, and CS1 directly demonstrates oxygen-pressure floating-zone growth of a Fe-containing complex oxide from a polycrystalline preform. The route is still challenging because CS1’s material is not Ru/Ir-containing, and congruent melting of SrRuFeIrO₆ is unknown. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for floating-zone growth of structurally similar noble-metal oxides, so the expert adjustment is negative.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Sr versus transition-metal size contrast triggers one amorphization/crystallization-risk criterion, consistent with the very different Sr–O and Ru/Fe/Ir–O distances in the target and with Sr–transition-metal frameworks in CS3/TS4. The unit cell appears small from the target P1 description, so C2 is not triggered. Mixed connectivity is possible by analogy to CS3/CS5, but insufficient evidence confirms simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing in the target, so C3 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrRuFeIrO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable oxide rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data support ambient-pressure Sr/Ru oxide synthesis most directly [CS3], while SS/TS data show that noble-metal oxides with similar thermodynamic positions often require oxygen control or high pressure [SS1, SS2, TS2, TS4]. The most important enabling factor is maintaining high oxygen chemical potential while giving enough thermal energy for Ru/Fe/Ir cation ordering. The most important limiting factor is slow solid-state diffusion and possible competition from simpler phases such as SrRuO₃, Fe oxides, IrO₂, or mixed Sr–Ru/Ir oxides, as impurity sensitivity is reported in related Sr–Ru and Sr–Ta–Cu systems [CS3, CS5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine.
