### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.036 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category for a 90–100 score by the required rubric. CS sources show many chemically related layered halide/chalcogenide compounds are synthesized at E_hull ≈ 0–0.008 eV/atom (CS1–CS5), and TS analogues include synthesized materials around 0.021–0.055 eV/atom (TS1, TS3, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that a small positive hull energy may still indicate metastability relative to MnCl₂/MnI₂ or other halide phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which is favorable relative to the rubric and consistent with sealed-tube analogues that form below 900 °C (CS3, CS5, SS2/TS2). Deductions are assigned for vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, volatile iodide control, and air-sensitive halide precursors, all supported by sealed and dry-inert methods in CS3, CS5, and SS2/TS2. The uncertainty is whether MnICl forms before melting or requires a narrow thermal window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halides provide better precursor activity than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and sealed halide reactions succeed in CS3, CS5, and SS2/TS2. Diffusion should be manageable because halide salts can soften or partially melt and because analogous halide reactions occur at moderate temperatures (CS3, SS2/TS2). Structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable because no source directly proves the Pmm2 MnICl sheet, and SS2/TS2 shows repeated annealing may be needed for Mn iodide phase purity.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses vacuum ampoules, volatile transport agents, and multiple operations, so the severity score is lower despite moderate temperatures. This is supported by evacuated gradient ampoules in CrSBr, NbSe₂Cl₂, and MnIn₂Se₄ (CS1, CS2, TS4). The uncertainty is that transport agents may change Cl:I activity and stabilize competing binary halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor availability is reasonable because I₂ and AlCl₃ are established transport agents in analogous systems (CS2, TS4). Crystallization by CVT is supported for layered materials (CS1, CS2, TS4), but mixed-halide composition control is likely the main kinetic/thermodynamic bottleneck [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-window sensitivity in related halide/chalcogenide systems is explicitly reported in CS2 and TS4.

#### F2 — Route 3

The melt/Bridgman route avoids high pressure and extreme temperature, but it requires dry handling, sealed ampoules, volatile iodide containment, and multiple thermal steps. These deductions are supported by Bridgman/annealing in SS2/TS2 and controlled sealed-ampoule halide crystallization in SS5. The uncertainty is ampoule compatibility with molten halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are favorable because molten halides improve mixing and diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and binary iodide growth/annealing is reported for TlMnI₃ (SS2/TS2). Slow cooling may help form ordered crystals, consistent with controlled cooling in SS5. The drawback is that melt growth can crystallize stable competing salts rather than the 2D MnICl phase if the mixed-halide phase has a narrow stability field [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Mn, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described structure is a simple 2D sheet of corner-sharing MnI₂Cl₂ tetrahedra, so it does not show the mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity associated with high amorphization tendency. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that MnICl specifically amorphizes; therefore CS, SS, and TS are neutral-to-supportive rather than directly diagnostic for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnICl is likely a **low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground state**, because its E_hull is positive but small at 0.036 eV/atom by the target data. The CS and TS analogues strongly support sealed-tube and CVT synthesis of layered halide/chalcogenide compounds (CS1–CS5, TS4), while SS/TS sources specifically support Mn–iodide binary-halide handling and long annealing strategies (SS2/TS2). The most important enabling factor is the use of anhydrous binary halides in sealed ampoules, which directly controls Mn, Cl, and I stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference supported by SS2/TS2]. The main limiting factor is mixed-halide phase control: MnCl₂/MnI₂ segregation, iodine volatility, or incorrect Cl:I chemical potential may prevent formation of the ordered Pmm2 sheet [Reasonable Inference, with phase sensitivity supported by CS2 and SS2/TS2]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry and annealing optimization.
