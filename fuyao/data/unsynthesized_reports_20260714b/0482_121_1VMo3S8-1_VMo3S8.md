### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.014 eV/atom lies well below 0.05 eV/atom, so it falls in the high-probability stability/metastability window by the required rubric. This is supported by synthesized analogues with similar or higher E_hull, including GaMo₄S₈ at about 0.042–0.047 eV/atom (CS2, CS3, SS4, SS5) and CdMo₆S₈ at about 0.035 eV/atom (TS5). MoS₂ analogues are at or near the hull and are readily synthesized or grown (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee the specific orthorhombic Pmm2 cation ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed-tube solid-state conditions, which are common in the reference sulfides (CS2, CS3, CS4, SS4, SS5). The main penalties are vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, sulfur volatility, and the need for grinding/refiring, all supported by evacuated silica-tube syntheses and repeated firings in CS3/SS4 and CS4. The temperature can likely be kept near 900–1000 °C based on GaV₄₋ₓMoₓS₈ (CS3, SS4), avoiding the >1000 °C deduction if optimized. Uncertainty remains because VMo₃S₈ itself may require a narrower sulfur chemical potential than the analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetic limitation for Route 1 is solid-state diffusion through refractory Mo and V powders; elemental Mo/V/S mixtures are used in CS3/SS4, but refractory metals are intrinsically slow to homogenize. Regrinding/refiring is therefore important, as directly reported for GaV₄₋ₓMoₓS₈ and Nb₂PdS₅-type syntheses (CS3, SS4, CS4). The structural reconstruction score is helped by the close molybdenite-like Mo–S environment of MoS₂ analogues (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether V and Mo order into the Pmm2 sheet rather than producing MoS₂ plus vanadium sulfides.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits high-temperature and vacuum-ampoule penalties from MoS₂ vapor transport, which uses 1040–1120 °C and evacuated quartz ampoules for 168–216 h (SS1, TS1). It also requires a temperature-gradient furnace, which is more specialized than the single-zone sealed-tube route (SS1, TS1). No high-pressure penalty is warranted because none of the vapor-transport references use high pressure (SS1, TS1). Optional iodine/halogen transport would add handling risk by **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the supplied MoS₂ source does not explicitly require it.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor growth can bypass slow bulk diffusion, and MoS₂ crystal growth by vapor transport is directly demonstrated (SS1, TS1). However, the target is ternary and requires V:Mo = 1:3 control; CS3/SS4 show that even related Ga–V–Mo–S materials require careful stoichiometry and excess Mo to suppress impurities. The crystallization pathway is plausible because the target is molybdenite-derived, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct VMo₃S₈ vapor growth. Thus kinetics for crystallization are favorable, while composition control is the limiting kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized by H₂S/H₂ atmosphere and multistep processing, both directly supported by CS5 and SS3. CS5 uses 1000 °C air heating, 1150 °C H₂S reduction, 800 °C annealing, and slow cooling, so T >1000 °C and multistep penalties apply. SS3 uses dry H₂ flow at 850–1050 °C and reports complex reduction behavior, reinforcing the controlled-atmosphere penalty. The route is feasible but operationally more hazardous than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Oxides may be more finely mixed and reactive than dense metals, which helps precursor activity, and CS5 demonstrates oxide-to-sulfide conversion. However, forming VMo₃S₈ from oxides requires complete framework reconstruction and precise reduction/sulfurization, which is kinetically demanding. SS3 shows that Mo sulfides under reducing conditions can pass through multiple intermediate phases, indicating substantial phase-control difficulty. The route may be useful for exploratory powder synthesis but is less ideal for phase-pure target preparation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline 2D molybdenite-derived sheet with simple V/Mo–S coordination and no evidence of very large unit-cell complexity in the target description. Structurally similar MoS₂ layered phases crystallize readily as ordered sheets (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4), and chemically similar layered sulfides are synthesized by standard annealing (CS1, CS4). The V/Mo cation-size mismatch is not expected to exceed the 15% threshold by physicochemical reasoning, but exact oxidation states are uncertain. Overall, amorphization is not the dominant risk; phase separation and cation ordering are more important.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VMo₃S₈ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.014 eV/atom, which is well within the synthesizable range and comparable to synthesized Mo sulfide analogues (CS2, CS3, SS4, SS5, TS5). The CS and SS data strongly support sealed-tube solid-state synthesis of related V/Mo/S and Mo–S frameworks, especially GaV₄₋ₓMoₓS₈ from elemental V/Mo/S-containing mixtures (CS3, SS4) and molybdenite-like MoS₂ sheets (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Mo–S layered framework chemistry, reflected by repeated successful synthesis of MoS₂ and Mo-rich sulfides (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). The main limiting factor is not thermodynamic stability but kinetic/phase control: V/Mo ordering must compete against formation of binary MoS₂ and vanadium sulfides, a concern inferred from impurity-suppression and repeated-firing requirements in CS3/SS4 and phase-complex reduction behavior in SS3. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with sealed-tube solid-state synthesis as the most defensible first route.
