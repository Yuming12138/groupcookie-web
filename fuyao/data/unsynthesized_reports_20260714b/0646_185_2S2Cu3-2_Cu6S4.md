### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0020 eV/atom, so it is essentially on the convex hull and falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS analogues are especially supportive because TS1–TS5 all have low E_hull values near 0.0015–0.0037 eV/atom and are reported as synthesizable. CS and SS also support accessibility through stable CuS and NaCu₄S₄ analogues with E_hull = 0.0 (CS3, SS3, SS4), although CS1 and SS1 show that nearby Cu-rich sulfides can be metastable. Remaining uncertainty is whether the exact P-3m1 binary Cu₃S₂ ordering is the stable low-temperature phase or a near-degenerate metastable polymorph.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state

F2 is moderate because CS3/SS4 require evacuated sealed silica tubes and sulfur vapor control, but only moderate temperatures of ~347–397 °C are needed. F3 is limited by the use of metallic Cu, whose bulk diffusion is slower than soluble or fluxed precursors, even though CS3/SS4 demonstrate CuS formation from elements. The target’s layered P-3m1 structure may require ordering beyond simple binary CuS formation, so longer annealing or regrinding may be needed [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS on direct binary Cu₃S₂ phase formation means Route 1 remains plausible but not guaranteed.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Hydrothermal / solvothermal

F2 is favorable because SS1, CS2, and TS2 use only 120–140 °C autoclave conditions, with no vacuum or high pressure beyond autogenous solvent pressure. F3 is strong because sulfide solution chemistry enables dissolution/reprecipitation, and SS1 shows local Cu coordination very similar to the target. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS1 forms NaCu₅S₃, so Na₂S may stabilize sodium copper sulfides rather than an alkali-free Cu–S binary. This is why the expert adjustment penalizes the otherwise high numerical route score.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Polysulfide flux

F2 is lower because SS3 requires vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive sulfide fluxes, slow cooling, and inert washing. F3 is high because flux transport should strongly enhance Cu/S mobility, and SS3 provides a P-3m1 Cu sulfide analogue with similar Cu–S distances. The strongest support is structural: SS3 contains trigonal-planar Cu–S motifs at 2.22 Å, matching the target’s Cu(2)–S length. The limiting uncertainty is alkali/Ba incorporation and the fact that SS3 explicitly notes phase sensitivity to flux choice.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: CVT / vapor phase

F2 is challenging because CVT generally requires pre-synthesis, sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile transport agents, and temperature-gradient control; TS1 gives only a brief CVT statement without conditions. F3 is moderate-to-low because sulfur volatility helps transport, but Cu:S stoichiometry control is likely narrow [Reasonable Inference]. TS1 supports that Cu sulfide-related single crystals can be grown by CVT, while CS4 supports vapor growth of sulfides but only for ZnS at ~1200 °C. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for binary Cu₃S₂ CVT makes this a secondary route.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Cu, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target is a simple binary P-3m1 layered structure rather than a large multi-cation framework, and its described connectivity is corner-sharing SCu₄ pyramids rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing. SS analogues show ordered crystalline Cu–S motifs with similar bond lengths (SS1, SS3, SS4), supporting low amorphization risk. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS directly addressing amorphization is treated as neutral, but the structural simplicity supports a high F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is very likely synthesizable as a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0020 eV/atom. CS and SS analogues strongly support Cu–S chemistry through direct sealed-tube synthesis, hydrothermal synthesis, and flux growth, especially CuS from Cu + S (CS3, SS4) and P-3m1 NaCu₄S₄ from sulfide flux (SS3). TS analogues reinforce that compounds at similar thermodynamic stability are experimentally accessible, but they are compositionally more complex than the target. The most critical enabling factor is high Cu–S chemical mobility, achieved either by sulfide solution chemistry or molten polysulfide flux. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among many competing Cu–S stoichiometries and possible alkali-stabilized side phases.
