### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues include ZrBr₂ at 0.0319 eV/atom and ZrI₂ essentially on the hull, showing that nearby reduced zirconium halide phases are accessible or nearly stable [TS-1, TS-3]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the ordered mixed I/Br compound is entropically stabilized, metastable, or competes with physical mixtures of ZrI₂ and ZrBr₂ [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for ZrIBr decomposition products].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized because ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ analogues require sealed Ta tubes, drybox handling, temperature gradients, and long high-temperature treatments [SS-1, SS-5]. The maximum temperature is moderate for solid-state chemistry, about 750–850 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied [SS-1, TS-3]. The main severity comes from volatile zirconium halides and sealed-vessel requirements [SS-1, SS-5].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
CVT is kinetically favorable relative to direct diffusion because volatile ZrI₄ transports zirconium iodide species in the successful ZrI₂ synthesis [SS-1, TS-3]. The structural reconstruction penalty is low because ZrI₂ is molybdenite-like and very close to the target ZrIBr sheet topology [SS-1]. The uncertainty is the I/Br ordering step, which is not directly demonstrated in the provided analogues [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for ordered mixed-halide ZrIBr].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct sealed reactions are less equipment-intensive than CVT in principle, and several chemically similar materials were made in sealed silica at ~800–900 °C [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5]. However, iodine/bromine volatility and evacuated sealed ampoules impose controlled-atmosphere and sealed-system penalties [CS-5, SS-3]. A Ta liner is likely needed for Zr halides, because ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ analogues use Ta containment [SS-1, SS-5; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Direct reaction from elemental Zr is kinetically poorer because Zr is refractory and diffusion through product layers is slow by physicochemical reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. Related direct syntheses often require several days to weeks [CS-3, CS-5, SS-3]. This route may still work because halogen vapor can partially transport material, but the closest Zr-halide analogues favor CVT rather than simple direct reaction [SS-1, SS-5].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux growth avoids very high temperatures, as related flux/sealed halide syntheses occur around 427–800 °C or 870–1100 K [CS-4, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5]. The route still requires drybox loading, sealed Ta containers, and volatile halide control [TS-2, TS-4, TS-5]. Additional steps such as slow cooling and flux removal justify the multi-step penalty [TS-2; flux removal is Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A molten halide flux should improve reaction kinetics and crystal growth, consistent with Nd bromosulfide crystallization in NdBr₃ flux and slow-cooled ternary halide syntheses [CS-4, TS-2]. The likely kinetic advantage is lower diffusion length and solution-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no provided source shows ZrIBr or a mixed Zr iodide-bromide grown from flux, so phase selectivity is uncertain [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple single-cation chemistry and a small ordered layered unit described by two Zr, two I, and two Br sites [Target]. Similar layered crystalline ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ phases exist, which supports a low amorphization tendency [SS-1, SS-5]. The main uncertainty is not amorphization but possible halide disorder or separation into ZrI₂/ZrBr₂ domains [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrIBr is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0210 eV/atom [Target]. The structural support is strong because molybdenite-like ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ analogues have been synthesized by sealed Ta transport/equilibration [SS-1, SS-5]. The thermodynamic support is also good because ZrBr₂ and ZrI₂ are low-hull thermodynamic analogues [TS-1, TS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is volatile zirconium-halide transport, which can equilibrate refractory Zr chemistry at 600–850 °C [SS-1, SS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is mixed I/Br stoichiometry and ordering, because the provided data demonstrate single-halide ZrX₂ phases but not the ordered mixed-halide ZrIBr phase [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for ordered ZrIBr].
