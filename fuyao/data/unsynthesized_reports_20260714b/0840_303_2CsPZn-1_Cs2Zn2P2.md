### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the calculated convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket. This is supported by several related stable phosphides/chalcogenides: BaCuP, NaBaP, K2NaInP2, K2NaGaP2, and KMgP are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 in the supplied data (CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). The closest chemical/structural analogue CsMnP has E_hull = 0.0415 eV/atom and was nevertheless synthesized (CS2), so the target being at 0.0 eV/atom is favorable. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee a wide synthesis window; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on Cs–Zn–P phase equilibria and competing binary phases, so that sub-aspect is neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The severity score is high because the route uses moderate temperatures, no external high pressure, and a direct elemental reaction, closely following CsMnP synthesis at 675–875 K under Ar (CS2). Deductions are mainly for volatile/air-sensitive Cs/P handling and the required sealed system, which are implicit in the use of sealed bombs and Ar in CS2 and sealed Nb ampoules in SS2/TS2. Kinetically, the route is favorable because CsMnP is isostructural and synthesized directly, minimizing structural uncertainty (CS2). The main uncertainty is that Zn may form stable competing Zn phosphides or volatilize under the same conditions [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly addresses Cs–Zn–P phase competition.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary-phosphide route

The binary route has more severe handling because Cs3P and Zn3P2 require prior preparation or procurement and sealed/inert manipulation [Reasonable Inference], although binary precursor synthesis is supported by KP/InP use in K2NaInP2 (SS1; TS1). The temperature can remain moderate because analogous binary-precursor reactions occur at 870–950 K in sealed vessels (CS5, SS1; TS1). Kinetically, preformed phosphides can improve local bonding and reduce P volatilization [Reasonable Inference], but the final P4/nmm layered framework must reconstruct from different binary structures. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms Cs3P + Zn3P2 reactivity toward CsZnP, so phase-selectivity confidence is lower.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-temperature sintering/annealing

High-temperature annealing is supported by KMgP and BaCuP syntheses at 800–1150 °C with inert/refractory containment and repeated thermal treatment (SS5; TS5; CS1). Its severity score is lower because directly using >1000 °C conditions risks Cs/Zn/P volatility [Reasonable Inference], even though the analogue methods themselves are experimentally established. Kinetics should be adequate at high temperature, and KMgP provides a close P4/nmm structural precedent (SS5; TS5). The uncertainty is that high-temperature protocols optimized for K/Mg or Ba/Cu analogues may overdrive CsZnP decomposition or component loss; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence exists for CsZnP stability above 800–1000 °C.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Flux/self-flux growth

Flux methods can improve kinetics by liquid-phase transport, and sodium flux plus slow cooling is explicitly used for BaCuN (CS4). Metal-rich sealed ampoule conditions can crystallize alkali/alkaline-earth pnictides, as NaBaP was obtained from a Na/Ba/Ga/P mixture in sealed Nb at 1275 K (CS3). However, the route has lower condition and phase-control confidence because CS3 reports NaBaP as a by-product and CS4 concerns nitrides rather than phosphides. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly supports flux growth of P4/nmm CsZnP, so the route is best viewed as exploratory crystal-growth rather than the preferred powder synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered P4/nmm AMX-type structure, and close analogues CsMnP and KMgP have similarly simple layered tetrahedral frameworks (CS2, SS5; TS5), which argues against strong amorphization tendency. One risk trigger is the large size/coordination mismatch between Cs and Zn, reflected by very different alkali–P and transition-metal–P bond lengths in CsMnP and target-like structures (CS2; [Reasonable Inference]). The target and close analogues use corner/edge-sharing tetrahedra without simultaneous face-sharing connectivity, reducing topological frustration (CS2, SS5; TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source reports amorphous by-products or glass formation for CsZnP specifically.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsZnP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically or structurally similar phosphides are also on the hull (CS1, CS3, SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5). The strongest synthesis support is the isostructural CsMnP analogue, which was directly synthesized from elements under Ar in sealed stainless-steel bombs at moderate temperature (CS2). The most critical enabling factor is structural transferability: CsMnP has the same P4/nmm layered motif and similar Cs–P coordination, making a direct elemental route highly credible (CS2). The main limiting factor is practical handling of volatile and air-sensitive Cs/P/Zn-containing mixtures in sealed vessels [Reasonable Inference], a concern reflected indirectly by the sealed-bomb/ampoule methods used throughout the analogues (CS2, SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). Overall, CsZnP should be synthesizable in a competent solid-state pnictide laboratory, with phase purity depending on careful stoichiometry and vapor-pressure control.
