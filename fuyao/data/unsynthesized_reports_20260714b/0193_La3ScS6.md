### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0130 eV/atom, well within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket [Target]. Chemically and structurally similar sulfides are often calculated on the hull, including CS mixed lanthanide sulfides and SS La₂S₃/Pr₂S₃/Sm₂S₃ analogues [CS1–CS5, SS1, SS2, SS5]. TS analogues have similarly low E_hull values, mostly 0–0.022 eV/atom, supporting the idea that low-energy sulfides can be made by conventional solid-state or flux methods [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee access to the specific ordered P2₁2₁2 La/Sc arrangement [Target].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The 1350 °C Ar route is directly supported by CS mixed sulfides synthesized from L₂S₃/L′₂S₃ with compression and heating in carbon crucibles [CS1–CS5]. The conditions are high-temperature and require controlled atmosphere but not high pressure, so severity is moderate rather than extreme [CS1–CS5]. The main uncertainty is sulfur retention and whether Sc₂S₃ reacts fast enough under the 4 h CS-style schedule [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary sulfide precursors provide a chemically compatible starting point, as in CS analogues [CS1–CS5]. However, La₃ScS₆ requires ordering of small Sc³⁺ into ScS₆ octahedra and larger La³⁺ into 7–8 coordinate sites [Target], so diffusion and reconstruction are not trivial. The CS analogues support mixed sulfide formation, but their rare-earth-only cation sets are not an exact structural match [CS1–CS5].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The 1000 °C sealed-ampoule route is less thermally severe than Route 1 and is explicitly described for CS analogues [CS1–CS4]. Vacuum sealing and possible volatile sulfur species add handling severity, as in LaS₂ sealed flux growth [TS2]. The uncertainty is whether the lower temperature can overcome Sc-containing diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The same sulfide precursors used in CS routes should be chemically suitable [CS1–CS4]. Kinetics are likely slower because the reaction is held at 1000 °C rather than 1350 °C, despite the longer two-day dwell [CS1–CS4]. This route may be useful as a sulfur-retentive anneal but may need repeated grinding or a higher-temperature follow-up [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux conditions are milder in temperature, typically 650–850 °C or 800 °C, and are well documented for La₂S₃/rare-earth sulfide systems [SS1, SS2, TS2, TS5]. However, sealed ampoules, flux handling, sulfur volatility, and washing steps make the route operationally nontrivial [SS1, SS2, TS2]. Direct CS support for a flux-made La–Sc ternary sulfide is insufficient, so the score is not raised further.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux should improve mass transport, consistent with rare-earth sulfide single-crystal growth in alkali halides [SS1, SS2, TS2, TS5]. This gives the best kinetic score despite uncertain target-phase selectivity. The limiting uncertainty is whether Sc₂S₃ dissolves/reacts adequately and whether the flux stabilizes La₃ScS₆ rather than La₂S₃, Sc₂S₃, LaS₂, or other competing sulfides [Reasonable Inference; SS1/SS2 note flux-dependent phase selection].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Direct sulfidation is supported by TS and SS precedent for making sulfides from oxides/carbonates under H₂S or CS₂/N₂ at high temperature [SS1, TS1, TS3, TS5]. It avoids sourcing Sc₂S₃ but introduces toxic gas handling and high-temperature reactive atmospheres [TS1, TS3]. No direct La₃ScS₆ oxide-sulfidation route is given, so this is a backup route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Oxide sulfidation has lower precursor activity for forming the target because it must remove oxygen and build a complex sulfide lattice [TS3]. TS3 shows long reaction times under H₂S, indicating moderate-to-slow kinetics. The route is penalized because Sc₂O₃ is expected to be difficult to sulfurize fully and residual oxysulfides could compete [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target structure is complex, with multiple inequivalent La, Sc, and S sites and mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedra [Target]. The La–Sc size mismatch is large, and the structure likely contains more than 40 atoms per unit cell [Target; Reasonable Inference]. These factors increase risk of sluggish crystallization, antisite disorder, or multiphase products even though thermodynamics are favorable.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La₃ScS₆ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0130 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS analogues, where mixed trivalent sulfides are made directly from L₂S₃/L′₂S₃ by solid-state sintering under Ar or vacuum [CS1–CS5]. SS and TS data add support that La–S frameworks and rare-earth sulfide motifs crystallize under flux or sealed-tube conditions [SS1, SS2, TS2, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high sulfur chemical potential while promoting cation diffusion, best addressed by sealed flux or binary-sulfide routes [CS1–CS5, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is the combination of La/Sc size mismatch, ordered ScS₆ octahedra, and complex mixed polyhedral connectivity, which may cause slow ordering or competing binary/ternary phases [Target; Reasonable Inference].
