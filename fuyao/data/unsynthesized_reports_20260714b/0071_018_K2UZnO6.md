### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the favorable upper boundary for near-ground-state synthesis [Target]. The closest thermodynamic analogue Sr2UZnO6 is experimentally synthesized despite E_hull = 0.054 eV/atom [TS1], supporting a high F1 score. CS also supports feasibility because U-containing perovskite-derived oxides with E_hull up to ~0.10 eV/atom were synthesized [CS2, CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target contains K and an apparent O–O unit, which may shift the accessible stability field relative to Sr-based analogues [Target, TS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 ceramic.**  
The ceramic route is well supported for Sr2UZnO6 and Sr2UNiO6 at 1150 °C in air with intermediate grinding [TS1, CS2], and K oxide synthesis with excess K2CO3 is supported by K2SrTa2O7 [CS3]. Severity is penalized for >1000 °C, multi-step firing, K volatility, and uranium toxicity [TS1, CS2, CS3]. Kinetics are only moderate because the target requires K/U/Zn/O ordering and a ZnO4–UO6 framework rather than the Sr2UZnO6 octahedral double perovskite [Target, TS1]. SS provides insufficient direct ceramic evidence for K–U–Zn and is treated as neutral 50 for the route-specific chemical transferability component [SS: insufficient evidence, neutral 50].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 flux.**  
Molten hydroxide flux growth is directly supported for K/U and Na/U oxides, including K2UO4 and Na4UO5 from KOH/NaOH in sealed Ag tubes [SS4]. Structurally related K/Na niobates and titanates also form from KOH/NaOH fluxes at 400–600 °C [SS1, SS2]. The route has lower thermal severity than ceramic synthesis but still needs sealed caustic flux and uranium handling [SS4]. The main uncertainty is Zn incorporation, because TS1 proves U–Zn oxide formation only in a ceramic Sr framework and not in a KOH flux [TS1, SS4].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal.**  
Thermodynamically similar alkali oxides/oxyfluorides can be crystallized hydrothermally at 150–230 °C in sealed Teflon systems [TS2, TS3]. The target’s short O–O distance suggests peroxide-like chemistry, so an alkaline oxidative hydrothermal route is chemically plausible by [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure. The low temperature gives mild F2 severity and solution transport gives favorable kinetics [TS2, TS3]. However, no CS or SS example directly demonstrates hydrothermal K–U–Zn oxide synthesis, so phase selectivity is the largest uncertainty [CS: insufficient evidence, neutral 50; SS: insufficient evidence, neutral 50 for hydrothermal K–U–Zn].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines large K+ coordination with smaller U/Zn coordination, triggering a cation-size mismatch risk by [Reasonable Inference] from the target coordination environments [Target]. The primitive composition is small and the connectivity is mainly corner-sharing UO6–ZnO4 rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing [Target], so amorphization risk is not severe. CS and TS analogues show crystalline perovskite-derived U oxides can be obtained despite size/coordination mismatch [TS1, CS2, CS4]. SS supports crystallinity in alkali uranium/alkali oxide flux products but not the exact U–Zn framework [SS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2UZnO6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and the closest Sr2UZnO6 analogue is synthesized at a very similar E_hull of 0.054 eV/atom [Target, TS1]. The CS/TS data strongly support U–Zn oxide framework formation by ceramic routes [TS1, CS2, CS4], while SS strongly supports alkali uranium oxide crystallization from molten hydroxide fluxes [SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high potassium activity while stabilizing U(VI), supported by K excess in K2SrTa2O7 synthesis [CS3] and sealed KOH flux for K/U oxides [SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: replacing Sr2+ with K+ and changing Zn coordination from ZnO6 to ZnO4 requires substantial structural reorganization [Target, TS1]. A peroxide-like O–O unit in the target may make low-temperature oxidative/hydrothermal synthesis attractive, but this is inferred rather than directly demonstrated by the reference set [Target; Reasonable Inference].
