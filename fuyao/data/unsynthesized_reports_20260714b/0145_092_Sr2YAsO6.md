### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score boundary in the supplied rubric. Thermodynamically similar Sr2YSbO6 and Ca2LaSbO6 have comparable E_hull values near 0.05 eV/atom and are reported synthesized by solid-state routes (TS-1, TS-2). The remaining uncertainty is that As-for-Sb substitution may alter decomposition pathways into arsenates; this concern is [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly shown in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
The ceramic route is supported by many analogues but requires >1000 °C and multiple regrind/firing cycles (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-2, SS-4, TS-1). It avoids high pressure and special atmosphere because analogues are made in air (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5). The main added penalties are As toxicity and volatility, which are [Reasonable Inference] not direct CS/SS/TS observations.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Solid-state kinetics are moderate because Sr2YSbO6 analogues need long sintering and pelletizing/regrinding, including 135 h in one report (CS-3, SS-1, TS-1). The same double-perovskite topology improves structural transferability (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4), but dense Y2O3 and carbonate/oxide precursors slow interdiffusion. Uncertainty remains over whether As accelerates or hinders crystallization relative to Sb; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
The sol-gel route is intended to lower the maximum temperature relative to ceramic analogues, but no CS/SS/TS example directly confirms this route for Sr2YAsO6 or Sr2YSbO6. It still requires multistep processing and toxic arsenic reagents, so its severity is not “easy.” The route is ambient-pressure and compatible with air/O2 based on the air synthesis of Sb analogues (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Sol-gel/Pechini chemistry should improve precursor activity and shorten diffusion paths compared with refractory oxide/carbonate ceramic mixtures; this is [Reasonable Inference]. The need for ordered A2BB′O6 crystallization still requires annealing, as inferred from repeated high-T treatments in analogues (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4, TS-1). Because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct wet-chemical evidence, the high kinetic score carries uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
High-pressure synthesis has a strong severity penalty because structurally/thermodynamically similar perovskites in the dataset require 100–120 kbar or 14 GPa apparatus (SS-3, TS-4). This route is not required by the closest Sr-Y-Sb analogues, which are ambient-pressure phases (CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, TS-1). It is therefore a fallback rather than preferred route.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
High pressure can promote dense octahedral frameworks, as seen in CaTiSiO6 and Ca2GeO4 high-pressure analogues (SS-3, TS-4). However, small capsule volumes, precursor preparation, and possible metastable polymorph competition reduce practical kinetic confidence. Direct evidence from CS for high-pressure Sr/Y/pnictogen double perovskites is insufficient, so the score is moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation-size disparity among Sr, Y, and As, which triggers one amorphization check by [Reasonable Inference] from ionic sizes and the supplied bond lengths. The target framework is mostly corner-sharing Y/As octahedra, matching ordered double-perovskite analogues rather than mixed edge/face networks (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4). Unit-cell atom count above 40 is not established by the supplied target description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that trigger.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2YAsO6 is best viewed as a borderline stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, while similar ~0.05 eV/atom double perovskites are experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-2). The CS and SS analogues strongly support formation of Sr2Y-based ordered double perovskites by ceramic routes, especially Sr2YSbO6 (CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural and chemical match to known Sr2YSbO6/Sr2LnSbO6 double perovskites (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2, SS-4, TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is replacing Sb with As, because As(V) oxide/arsenate chemistry introduces volatility, toxicity, and possible competing arsenate phases; this limitation is [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful As stoichiometry control and phase-purity optimization.
