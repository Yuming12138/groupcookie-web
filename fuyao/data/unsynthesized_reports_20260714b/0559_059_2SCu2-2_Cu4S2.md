### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.044 eV/atom [Target], placing it just inside the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom window. TS provides strong support because the closest thermodynamic analogues, Cu8GeS6 at 0.045 eV/atom and Ag3CuS2 at 0.046 eV/atom, have reported syntheses (TS1, TS3). SS also supports accessibility of similarly metastable chalcogenides, including AgCuS at 0.031 eV/atom and Cu8GeS6 at 0.045 eV/atom (SS2, SS5). CS is less specific because several chemically similar entries are not binary Cu–S or have questionable metadata, but it still shows sulfide/phosphide phases around 0.025–0.072 eV/atom can be synthesized (CS1, CS3, CS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile sulfur handling are required, as in SS2, SS3, SS5, TS1, and TS2. Its kinetic score is limited by starting from dense Cu metal, although analogues show Cu sulfide/chalcogenide formation at 300–500 °C, implying sulfur-assisted transport and favorable Cu mobility (SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2). The largest uncertainty is whether direct high-temperature formation followed by cooling gives the desired orthorhombic Pmma Cu2S rather than another Cu2S polymorph [Target, Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has lower F2 than Route 1 because it is explicitly multi-step: prepare Cu2S, grind, anneal, and screen cooling/quenching, consistent with the precursor strategy in SS2/TS2. Its kinetic score is better because preformed Cu2S already has the target composition and Cu–S framework, reducing reconstruction relative to elemental reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting issue is polymorph selectivity, since SS3 shows that related Cu sulfide phases can require specific quenching to obtain pure phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 scores best in F2 because SS1 and CS5 demonstrate low-temperature hydrothermal sulfide formation without vacuum or high pressure. It also has favorable kinetics because solution-mediated sulfide precipitation avoids bulk diffusion, as demonstrated by SS1 at 140 °C and CS5 at 120 °C. The main uncertainty is structural specificity: SS1 forms a structurally similar sodium copper sulfide and CS5 forms Ni3S4, but neither confirms the exact Pmma Cu2S polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Cu and S with a single cation type, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is absent [Target]. The target’s coordination description is simple, with two inequivalent two-coordinate Cu sites and one four-coordinate S site, giving no evidence for a >40-atom cell or highly mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not provide the target unit-cell size directly, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that subcheck; nevertheless the target description supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a ground-state phase, because its energy above hull is 0.044 eV/atom [Target]. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: TS and SS include several Cu sulfide or Cu-containing chalcogenide phases in the same metastability range that were synthesized in evacuated ampoules or hydrothermal conditions (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and soft bonding in Cu–S chemistry, inferred from low-temperature Cu sulfide/chalcogenide syntheses at 120–500 °C (SS1, SS2, CS5, TS1, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control, because Cu2S has multiple possible structures and SS3 shows related sulfide phases may need carefully controlled quenching for phase purity. Overall, the target appears synthesizable, but obtaining phase-pure orthorhombic Pmma Cu2S will likely require screening of temperature, sulfur chemical potential, and cooling rate.
