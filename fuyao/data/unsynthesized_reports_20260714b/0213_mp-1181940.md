### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies exactly at the highest-scoring threshold, so F1 is high but not perfect. **TS1** at 0.0552 eV/atom and **TS3–TS4** near 0.051–0.061 eV/atom show that phases in this metastability range can be experimentally obtained. **CS2** also has E_hull ≈ 0.0508 eV/atom and is synthesized, though its high-pressure requirement shows that a 0.05 eV/atom phase may need kinetic or thermodynamic assistance. **Insufficient evidence from SS** directly links the target’s 0.05 eV/atom metastability to the same CdCl2 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because **CS4**, **SS5/TS1**, and **TS3** demonstrate room-temperature or low-temperature aqueous crystallization without high pressure, high temperature, or vacuum. The only major deduction is toxicity of CdCl2, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** based on Cd chemistry rather than explicitly stated in CS/SS/TS. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** confirms whether the target anhydrous tetragonal polymorph survives solution growth, so no additional severity deduction is applied, but uncertainty remains.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization has favorable kinetics because soluble chloride precursors provide high activity, as supported by **CS4**, **SS5/TS1**, and **TS3**. Ion diffusion limitations are minimal in solution, which explains the high diffusion score. The structural reconstruction score is lower because **CS4** shows Cd–Cl layered chemistry but with CdCl6 octahedra, whereas the target requires corner-sharing CdCl4 tetrahedra. **TS3** warns that solution-grown halide systems can form persistent mixtures, adding uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized for vacuum/sealed-ampoule operation, volatile chloride handling, multi-step drying/loading/sealing/heating, and toxic Cd halide use. The need for sealed ampoules and vacuum is supported by **CS5**, **SS2–SS4**, and **TS2**. The temperature is moderate, not extreme, because analogous halide sublimation and transport examples occur around 90–475 °C in **CS5** and **SS2–SS4**. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** specifically proves CdCl2 vapor pressure conditions for this exact polymorph, so the route remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor recrystallization avoids slow solid-state diffusion, which is favorable and supported by successful halide sublimation/recrystallization in **CS5** and **TS2**. Precursor activity is high because volatile halides can redistribute and purify during sublimation, as seen in **CS5**. Structural reconstruction is moderately difficult because **TS2** reports polymorph behavior in BiBr3 and **CS5** required sublimation to improve crystal quality. The target’s small positive E_hull increases concern that vapor growth may crystallize a lower-energy CdCl2 polymorph instead.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the same main severity penalties as Route 2: vacuum ampoule, volatile chloride transport, multi-step operation, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. **SS2–SS4** explicitly support evacuated ampoules, 360–475 °C gradients, glovebox loading, and multiday heating. No high-pressure or >1000 °C deduction applies because **SS2–SS4** use moderate temperatures and ambient external pressure. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** confirms an optimal CdCl2 transport agent, so that part is inferred.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CVT gives high precursor mobility and avoids bulk diffusion, consistent with **SS2–SS4** chloride transport growth. The score is not higher because **SS2–SS4** report low yields, equilibrium mixtures, moisture sensitivity, and difficulties obtaining pure phases. Structurally, CVT is plausible for a 2D chloride phase, but **SS2–SS4** are chemically more complex than binary CdCl2. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target tetrahedral sheet is a kinetic polymorph accessible in the Cd–Cl binary phase space.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; this follows from the target formula, with **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** needed for this check. The described structure has simple CdCl4 corner-sharing tetrahedra and no mixed edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered. The formula and simple site description suggest the unit cell is not large, but exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS. Overall amorphization risk is low compared with complex multi-cation analogues in **SS2–SS4** and **TS3**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a strongly stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom from the prompt. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: **CS4** directly supports Cd–chloride solution crystallization, **SS5/TS1** supports mild crystallization of a 2D chloride sheet with very similar E_hull, and **CS5/TS2/SS2–SS4** support anhydrous halide vapor routes. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility of chloride species in solution or vapor, supported by mild crystallization in **CS4** and **SS5/TS1** and vapor transport in **CS5** and **SS2–SS4**. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because the target tetrahedral CdCl4 sheet differs from the octahedral Cd–Cl motifs in **CS4** and may compete with more stable CdCl2 arrangements. Overall, the material is synthesizable with a good chance of discovery-scale crystals, but bulk single-phase isolation may require careful chloride activity, hydration control, and cooling-rate screening.
