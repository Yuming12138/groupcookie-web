### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0480 eV/atom, placing it just inside the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom score bracket. Low-hull synthesized analogues exist in all three categories: TlCdI₃ at 0.0167 eV/atom (CS-1), CuTl₂I₃ at 0.00686 eV/atom (CS-3), CuHgSI at 0.02099 eV/atom (SS-5; TS-2), and CuHgSBr at 0.03085 eV/atom (TS-1). Thermodynamically, this suggests CuHgI₃ is at worst only mildly metastable; the remaining uncertainty is whether competing binary iodides or alternate Cu–Hg–I polymorphs are kinetically favored.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal HI

The low temperature and one-pot nature are strongly supported by CuTl₂I₃ in HI at 160 °C for 3 h (CS-3) and AgTl₂I₃ in HI at 155–175 °C (CS-5; TS-4), so condition severity is moderate rather than high. Deductions are mainly for sealed volatile/corrosive iodide chemistry and toxic Hg precursors, consistent with Hg-compound handling concerns in SS-2/SS-3 and HgI₂ sensitivity in SS-4. Kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal HI provides solution-mediated transport, but structural assembly of the exact Cu–Hg–I sheet is not directly demonstrated; this is the main uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 sealed-ampoule solid-state

The sealed-ampoule route is well supported by CuHgSI from CuI + HgS at 553 K for 2 weeks (SS-5; TS-2), CuHgSX at 573 K for 2 weeks (TS-3), and Hg₃Se₂I₂ from Hg chalcogenide + HgI₂ at 260 °C (SS-2). Condition severity is reduced by the need for vacuum sealing, toxic Hg compounds, and volatility management. Kinetics are moderate: binary iodides should be more reactive than oxides [Reasonable Inference], but 2-week anneals in CuHgSI/CuHgSX analogues (SS-5/TS-2; TS-3) indicate slow ordering or diffusion.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman

Melt growth is supported chemically by TlCdI₃ and TlFeI₃/TlMnI₃ analogues grown or prepared by Bridgman/melting plus annealing (CS-1; CS-2). However, the target includes HgI₂-like chemistry, and Hg–I systems often require sealed ampoules and show stability sensitivity (SS-2; SS-4), making this route more severe. Kinetically, a melt should be favorable, but phase control may be difficult because CS-2 reports repeated annealing for TlFeI₃ and because Hg/I volatility could shift stoichiometry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 solution crystallization

Solution crystallization has very mild conditions, supported by HgI₂ crystallization from saturated solution (SS-4) and NaHgCl₃ crystallization from aqueous HgCl₂ + NaCl at room temperature (TS-5). The main deduction is toxicity from Hg reagents and phase/light sensitivity of Hg iodide crystals (SS-2; SS-4). Kinetics of transport are excellent in solution, but there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that CuHgI₃ itself will nucleate from ambient solution rather than forming binary HgI₂, CuI, or soluble iodometallate salts.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has different cation sizes and coordination preferences, with Cu in distorted CuCuI₃ trigonal pyramids and Hg in HgI₆ octahedra, so one amorphization trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from the target structure]. The formula/cell description does not indicate a >40-atom cell, and the target description includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing; therefore C2 and C3 are not triggered. The remaining uncertainty is whether local Cu–Cu bonding and 2D sheet formation create kinetic disorder during rapid precipitation, especially in solution routes.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuHgI₃ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.0480 eV/atom, just below the 0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability threshold. The CS data strongly support iodide synthesis from binary iodides in HI or melt/anneal conditions (CS-1 to CS-5), while the SS/TS data strongly support Cu–Hg–halide/chalcogenide formation in sealed low-temperature environments (SS-5; TS-2; TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the softness and mobility of iodide chemistry: CuI/HgI₂ can react under relatively low-temperature hydrothermal or sealed-ampoule conditions rather than requiring refractory solid-state synthesis. The most critical limiting factor is Hg/I volatility and phase sensitivity, which can shift stoichiometry or favor competing HgI₂/CuI-related phases if temperature, iodide activity, and sealing are not controlled. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with hydrothermal HI crystallization being the most defensible first attempt.
