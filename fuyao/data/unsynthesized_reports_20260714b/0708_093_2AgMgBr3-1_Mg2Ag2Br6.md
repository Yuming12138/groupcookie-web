### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0110 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic range of ≤0.05 eV/atom. This is consistent with several CS/SS/TS analogues that are on or near the hull, including NaGaBr4 at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-2, SS-3), NaInBr4 at 0.0069 eV/atom (CS-1, TS-5), Li2MgBr4 at 0.0146 eV/atom (TS-2), and Li2ZnBr4 at 0.0172 eV/atom (SS-2, TS-1). The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not identify the exact competing decomposition phases for MgAgBr3; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on decomposition pathways, so the score is not set to 100.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires controlled Ar/vacuum ampoule handling and anhydrous halide reagents, as seen for NaInBr4/NaGaBr4 and Li2MgBr4 analogues (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). It does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, which is favorable relative to high-temperature ceramic syntheses. The deductions come from sealed volatile bromide handling, controlled atmosphere, and hygroscopic/air-sensitive precursors; CS/SS/TS repeatedly emphasize dry Ar, evacuated ampoules, and moisture-sensitive crystals or reagents (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2, SS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on the exact MgAgBr3 melting point, so the upper temperature is inferred.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary bromides are high-activity precursors compared with oxides, and analogous ternary bromides are formed directly by melting stoichiometric binary halides (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). Molten or partially molten reaction conditions strongly improve diffusion, supported by slow-cooling melt syntheses in CS-1, CS-2, and TS-2. The kinetic penalty is structural: the exact MgAgBr3 layered P-31m phase and Ag–Ag unit are not directly represented in the analogues; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct formation of the Ag–Ag trigonal-pyramidal sheet.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and no external high pressure, consistent with halogenozincate sintering below melting points (SS-2, SS-4, TS-1). However, the route requires dry Ar or vacuum, sealed ampoules, and likely long annealing/regrinding, all of which are supported by the multi-week tempering and oxygen-/water-free handling in SS-2, SS-4, and TS-1. It is therefore more operationally demanding than a one-pass melt growth route. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a specific successful low-temperature solid-state MgAgBr3 reaction, so this route is scored more conservatively.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Anhydrous binary bromides are still reasonably reactive precursors, as shown by Li2ZnBr4/Na2ZnBr4 synthesis from binary halides (SS-2, SS-4, TS-1). The main kinetic drawback is that multi-week tempering is reported for related bromides (SS-2, SS-4, TS-1), implying slow ordering or diffusion in the solid/partially molten state. Structural reconstruction from MgBr2 + AgBr into a layered MgAgBr3 sheet is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS, and TS-3 reports difficulty obtaining single crystals for Li2MnBr4, so the structural-kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate CVT temperatures and standard sealed ampoule/tube furnace setups, consistent with SS-1 and CS-3. The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic transport agents such as I2 or Br2, and the extra pre-reaction plus transport-growth steps. SS-1 specifically indicates air/moisture sensitivity and low-yield phase-control issues for CVT halide analogues, supporting a challenging classification. Insufficient evidence from TS for CVT of Mg-based simple bromides, so route-specific TS support is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport can bypass bulk diffusion limitations, and halide CVT growth is demonstrated in structurally/chemically related systems (SS-1, CS-3). However, the exact volatile Mg–Ag–Br transport species and the stability of MgAgBr3 under halogen transport conditions are not known from CS/SS/TS. Phase reconstruction at the cold end is the main uncertainty: SS-1 reports low yields and equilibrium mixtures, and CS/SS/TS provide no direct MgAgBr3 CVT analogue. Thus, kinetics are acceptable but not as reliable as melt growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target likely has a significant cation-size mismatch between Mg2+ and Ag+ [Reasonable Inference], which triggers one amorphization-risk check. The structure is a relatively simple crystalline halide sheet, and the target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell or face-sharing polyhedral network. CS/SS/TS analogues include many crystalline ternary bromides obtained by melt/solid-state methods (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2, SS-2), which supports low-to-medium rather than high amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide glass-forming data for Mg–Ag bromides.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgAgBr3 is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar bromides are stable or near-stable (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support binary-halide melt or melt-assisted synthesis under dry sealed-ampoule conditions, especially from the NaGaBr4/NaInBr4 and Li2MgBr4 precedents (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of anhydrous bromide melts, which allows ternary bromides to crystallize at moderate temperatures rather than requiring refractory ceramic conditions (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2, SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the exact P-31m layered MgAgBr3 structure with an Ag–Ag contact is not directly represented among the closest analogues, so competing MgBr2/AgBr or alternative ternary bromide phases may form [Reasonable Inference]. Moisture sensitivity and volatile bromide handling are secondary but important practical limitations, supported by dry Ar/vacuum handling requirements in CS-1, CS-2, TS-2, SS-2, and SS-4.
