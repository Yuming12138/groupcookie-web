### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it on the calculated convex hull and gives a very high F1 score **[Target]**. Thermodynamic analogues include multiple synthesized fluoride phases with E_hull = 0, such as CsSnF3, RbSnF3, Sn2OF2, and SnF2 (TS1/TS2/TS3/TS5). SS gives the same favorable signal because the highest-ranked structurally similar fluorides are experimentally synthesized and often stable (SS1/SS2/SS5). CS is less specific: CS3 involves indium oxide, not indium fluoride, and CS oxide routes include both stable and metastable cases, so CS provides only moderate supporting evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state comproportionation

The severity score is moderate because the route avoids high temperature and high pressure, but it likely needs evacuated or inert sealed ampoules, as fluoride analogues use sealed tubes or Ag ampoules (SS3/SS5/TS3). The kinetic score is moderate because In metal and InF3 must reconstruct into layered InF, although the low melting point of In and possible vapor/solid transport should help **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS5/TS3 support low-temperature sealed reactions for low-dimensional fluorides, but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates In + InF3 → InF; that is the main uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: solvothermal / solution crystallization

The severity score is high because structurally and thermodynamically similar ASnF3 phases are grown at only 150 °C for 24 h in methanol autoclaves (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2), and SnF2 crystals can grow from 50–25 °C aqueous solution (TS5). Kinetics are favorable because solution transport bypasses solid-state diffusion, and slow cooling is already demonstrated for fluoride crystal growth (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The uncertainty is redox: In(I) must be stabilized against disproportionation or oxidation, whereas the directly cited solution analogues are mainly Sn(II) fluorides (SS1/SS2/SS4/TS4/TS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: fluoride flux / molten-salt growth

The route benefits kinetically from molten-salt transport, and flux synthesis is shown to improve product purity in CS5, while closed-system fluoride reactions are demonstrated in SS3. However, severity is lower because the route requires sealed fluoride-compatible vessels, flux removal, and dry handling **[SS3; Reasonable Inference]**. The key uncertainty is competing alkali indium fluoride formation, inferred from ASnF3 formation from alkali fluorides and SnF2 in SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: thin-film reactive deposition

CS4 supports the general idea that vapor deposition at 350–400 °C can generate binary oxide phases, but it also reports simultaneous metal and oxide phases, indicating phase-control difficulty. For InF, reactive fluorination is more hazardous and compositionally sensitive than oxide deposition **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is therefore plausible mainly for screening or epitaxial stabilization, not as the best bulk synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered **[Target]**. The described PbO-like Pmmn binary sheet is structurally simple and does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell **[Target]**. It has edge- and corner-sharing FIn4 tetrahedra but no face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not triggered **[Target]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InF is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom **[Target]**. The strongest support comes from SS/TS fluoride analogues: low-dimensional Sn fluorides and alkali tin fluorides crystallize under mild solvothermal, solution, or sealed-tube conditions (SS1/SS2/SS4/SS5/TS1/TS2/TS3/TS4/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary composition and thermodynamic stability, which should reduce the need for extreme temperature or pressure **[Target; TS1/TS2/TS3/TS5]**. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining In in the +1 oxidation state while avoiding hydrolysis, oxidation, or competing InF3/In metal phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, low-temperature solvothermal crystallization is the best first attempt, with sealed-tube comproportionation as the most chemically direct backup.
