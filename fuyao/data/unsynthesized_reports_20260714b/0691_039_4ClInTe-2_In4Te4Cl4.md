### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which is very close to the convex hull and lies in the highest stability bin [target data]. The strongest analogues GaTeCl and InTeBr are calculated on the hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, SS-2, TS-4], and several thermodynamic analogues with small positive hull energies have been synthesized [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is that a 6 meV/atom metastability could correspond to a small competing phase field, but this is well within typical experimentally accessible metastability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from low temperature because GaTeCl forms at 400 °C for 7 days [CS-1/SS-2], and InTeBr forms around 520 °C by melt growth [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4]. The main severity penalties are evacuated ampoule/inert atmosphere and volatile/air-sensitive halide handling, both supported by GaTeCl’s evacuated ampoule and inert handling [CS-1/SS-2] and InTeBr’s hygroscopic handling concerns [CS-3]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for this exact chloride low-temperature route, so TS is neutral for chloride-specific volatility but supportive for sealed-ampoule chalcogenide synthesis generally [TS-1, TS-3].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics should be favorable because GaTeCl forms from chemically matched binary precursors rather than refractory oxides [CS-1/SS-2], and InTeBr uses the analogous InX₃ + In₂Te₃ chemistry [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4]. The halide precursor is expected to enhance mass transport through molten/vapor halide species [Reasonable Inference], consistent with moderate-temperature halide syntheses [CS-1, CS-3]. The main uncertainty is the 7-day annealing time for GaTeCl [CS-1], implying ordering and nucleation may still be slow.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures because InTeBr was grown from the melt at 520 °C [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4], far below the >1000 °C threshold. However, Bridgman growth requires sealed ampoules, controlled cooling/translation, and careful halide containment, as supported by InTeBr Bridgman growth [CS-3] and Bridgman conditions for AgIn₅Se₈ [TS-3]. CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct InTeCl melt-growth report, so chloride volatility and corrosivity are inferred rather than directly demonstrated.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt growth gives the best diffusion kinetics because the reacting charge is liquid, and the closest analogue InTeBr was successfully grown from the melt [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4]. The target’s InTe₃Cl tetrahedral sheet is structurally close to InTeBr and GaTeCl [CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, SS-2], so crystallization should not require a fundamentally different framework. The limiting uncertainty is possible incongruent melting or Cl loss; InTeBr already shows phase-control concerns from the melt [CS-3].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT avoids extreme pressure and can operate below 1000 °C for the proposed target [Reasonable Inference], but analogous RhTeCl CVT used a high-temperature gradient of 900→700 °C and chlorine chemistry [CS-2]. AlCl₃-assisted transport was necessary for difficult In-containing chalcogenides [SS-5/TS-2], which adds handling complexity and air sensitivity. CS supports Te–Cl transport [CS-2], SS/TS support In-chalcogenide transport [SS-5/TS-2], but no source proves InTeCl transport directly.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can overcome solid-state diffusion by transporting volatile halide complexes, as demonstrated for RhTeCl platelets [CS-2] and for difficult MnIn₂Te₄/MnIn₂Se₄ single-phase crystals using AlCl₃ transport [SS-5/TS-2]. However, vapor equilibria for In–Te–Cl may be narrow, and RhTeCl transport produced Te₄(AlCl₄)₂ as a byproduct [CS-2]. Thus kinetics may be good, but composition control is less certain than in the stoichiometric sealed-ampoule route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple single-cation layered compound with corner-sharing InTe₃Cl tetrahedra [target description], so amorphization risk from cation-size disorder and mixed polyhedral connectivity is low. InTeBr has mixed edge/corner-sharing tetrahedra [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4], but the target description specifically says corner-sharing tetrahedra, reducing structural frustration. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell atom count, so the atom-count check is treated using the simple formula and reported crystallographic simplicity.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InTeCl is likely experimentally accessible and may be either a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom [target data]. The analogue support is strong: GaTeCl gives a direct chloride/tetrahedral-sheet precedent [CS-1/SS-2], while InTeBr gives a direct In–Te–halide precursor and melt-growth precedent [CS-3/SS-1/TS-4]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of chemically matched binary precursors, especially InCl₃ and In₂Te₃, by analogy to GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ and InBr₃ + In₂Te₃ routes [CS-1, CS-3]. The most important limiting factor is control of chloride activity and moisture/volatility during sealed-ampoule synthesis [CS-1, CS-3, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, Route 1 is preferred because it most directly combines the GaTeCl chloride chemistry with the InTeBr indium telluride chemistry.
