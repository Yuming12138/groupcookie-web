### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.022 eV/atom, which is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range usually associated with experimentally accessible ground-state or low-metastability phases. Similar experimentally synthesized compounds include TiOCl at ~0.033 eV/atom [SS1, TS2] and Mg2BN2Br at ~0.030 eV/atom [CS5]. TS analogues around 0.03–0.05 eV/atom are also synthesized, although some require careful phase control [TS1, TS3, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee O/N/Br ordering.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT / Sealed-Ampoule Halide Transport

Route 1 benefits from the strongest structural precedent: TiOCl is a layered Ti oxyhalide synthesized by gas transport [SS1, TS2], and NbOBr2 is a layered bromide oxyhalide obtained in evacuated ampoules at 500 °C [SS2]. The severity score is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C conditions but requires vacuum/sealed halide handling and moisture-sensitive bromides [SS2, CS5]. Kinetics are favorable for layered crystal growth because halide transport can bypass bulk diffusion, but N incorporation lacks direct confirmation from CS/SS/TS. The main uncertainty is whether a Ti–N–O–Br sheet can crystallize before separating into TiN/TiO2/TiBrx phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-Temperature Solid-State Ampoule

This route is supported by nitride–bromide synthesis in sealed Nb ampoules for Mg2BN2Br [CS5] and by nitride/chloride sealed Ta ampoule chemistry for Sr2Cl2NCN [CS2]. Its severity is high because it requires sealed refractory-metal ampoules, inert handling, volatile bromide control, and likely repeated firings [CS5, TS5]. Kinetics are weaker than CVT because TiN and TiO2 are refractory and require long-range diffusion/reconstruction into a 2D layered phase [SS3, CS5]. The route is plausible for powder synthesis but less attractive for high-quality crystals.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solvothermal / Ammonothermal

Hydrothermal redox synthesis is supported for YFeO3Cl2 and SrV4O9 [CS3, TS3], indicating that low-temperature solution routes can access complex oxide/oxyhalide phases. Its condition severity is moderate because it avoids high temperature but uses sealed vessels, reducing conditions, and potentially NH3/amides/nitrides [CS3, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may be good because solution routes reduce diffusion limitations [TS3], but direct evidence for Ti–N–O–Br formation is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. This route is exploratory rather than primary.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Ti, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern; this is a favorable crystallization factor based on the target composition. The target structure is a relatively simple 2D sheet and does not show a large >40-atom cell; structurally similar TiOCl is also a simple layered phase [SS1]. The octahedra are described as edge- and corner-sharing, not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the amorphization tendency is low. Remaining uncertainty is O/N site disorder, which is not captured by the three F4 checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2NOBr2 is likely a low-metastability phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom and similar low-hull analogues are experimentally synthesized [SS1, CS5, TS2]. The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar TiOCl, which has the same layered Ti oxyhalide motif and is grown by gas transport [SS1, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or sealed-ampoule bromide chemistry, which can promote layered crystal growth at moderate temperature [SS1, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ti redox state, Br volatility, and N/O ordering; no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates the full Ti–N–O–Br chemistry. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful screening of bromine chemical potential and nitrogen source activity.
