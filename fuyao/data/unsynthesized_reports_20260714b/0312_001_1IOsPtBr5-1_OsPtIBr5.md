### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the predicted convex-hull ground state and supports a high F1 score. Similar stable halides exist in the references, including IrBr₃ [SS1, TS1], ReTeSeCl₄ [CS2], and Re–Te–Br [CS4, SS5, TS5]. CS is mixed because some chemically similar salts have high hull energies [CS3, CS5], so CS alone is not uniformly positive; SS/TS are more supportive.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct CVT route requires sealed Br₂/I₂ or halogen vapor, which is supported by IrBr₃/RuBr₃ syntheses in closed ampoules [SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3, TS4]. Temperatures are below 1000 °C, so there is no ultra-high-temperature penalty, but halogens are volatile/toxic and require sealed systems. Uncertainty is high because no reference directly demonstrates Os + Pt cohalogenation into a mixed-metal mixed-halide AlCl₃-derived phase.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Os/Pt metal halogenation [Reasonable Inference], even though Ir/Ru analogues show that halogen vapor transport can form late-transition-metal bromides [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because AlCl₃-type IrBr₃ contains edge-sharing octahedral sheets like the target [SS1, TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether Os/Pt and I/Br order rather than forming separate OsBr₃/PtBrₓ/I-rich phases.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed ampoules, matching CS examples at 300–550 °C [CS1, CS2, CS4]. The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile halogen sources, toxic/air-sensitive halides, and multistep annealing. SS/TS support sealed halide ampoules as standard for related frameworks [SS1, TS1], while insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Os/Pt/I/Br processing windows.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Binary halides should be more reactive than elemental Os/Pt [Reasonable Inference], and binary-halide routes are directly shown for Pd/Pb bromides [CS1] and Re halide systems [CS2, CS4]. The edge-sharing octahedral sheet motif is strongly supported by IrBr₃ [SS1, TS1]. Kinetic uncertainty remains from ordering of two metals and two halides in P1 symmetry, which may require slow cooling/annealing analogous to controlled slow-cooling programs [CS4, SS5, TS5].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Solution crystallization avoids high temperature and high pressure, supported by HBr evaporation for RbRe₃Br₁₀ [SS2, TS2] and cooling crystallization for Pb bromide salts [CS3]. However, concentrated HBr/HI, possible Br₂/I₂ buffers, and air-sensitive Os/Pt species impose safety and control penalties. Transferability is weaker because the target is anhydrous and neutral rather than an ionic solution-grown salt.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Precursor mobility and diffusion are favorable in solution, as shown by Re bromide crystallization from HBr [SS2, TS2]. The major kinetic/structural penalty is that solution routes in the references yield cluster/ionic or salt-like phases [SS2, TS2, CS3], not AlCl₃-derived neutral sheets. Thus, although nucleation may be fast, obtaining the target topology is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target uses only edge-sharing octahedra, and the AlCl₃-type analogue IrBr₃ also forms ordered edge-sharing sheets [SS1, TS1], supporting low amorphization risk. The Os/Pt cation size mismatch is expected to be small for late 5d metals in octahedral halide environments [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on the exact target unit-cell size and amorphization behavior, so uncertainty remains neutral for those sub-signals.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

OsPtIBr₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from AlCl₃-type IrBr₃, which has the same key edge-sharing octahedral sheet chemistry and is synthesized in sealed Br₂-containing ampoules [SS1, TS1]. Chemically similar sealed-ampoule halide syntheses at 300–550 °C further support a binary-halide or melt-anneal strategy [CS1, CS2, CS4]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high and controlled halogen chemical potential in a sealed ampoule, as repeatedly used for related bromides [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is achieving ordered Os/Pt and I/Br distribution without forming competing binary or ternary halides; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for this exact quaternary ordering.
