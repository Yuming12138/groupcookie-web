### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score [Target]. This is consistent with many CS/SS/TS analogues that are also on or extremely near the hull, including WTe2, Mo3Te4, NbIrTe4, ReSe2, and Re3Tl(Se2Cl)2 [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic, because a hull-stable phase can still be hard to isolate if competing Re–Te or Re–I phases form [SS3, TS3; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses no high pressure and temperatures mostly below or near 950 °C, which is supported by 900 °C Re chalcohalide synthesis and 600–1000 °C telluride sintering [SS3, CS1]. The severity penalty comes from vacuum ampoules, volatile iodine/tellurium components, multiple grind/anneal steps, and hazardous halide reagents [SS3, CS1, TS3; Reasonable Inference for iodine hazards]. The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility and iodine pressure at high temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Re, as solid-state diffusion through Re-rich telluride/iodide intermediates is expected slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS3 shows that Re chalcohalides can form under sealed-tube conditions, but it also reports difficult phase control and low yields, which reduces the kinetic score [SS3]. Structural similarity to Re3Tl(Se2Cl)2 improves the outlook because the target has the same Re-rich chalcohalide/layered motif and P-1 symmetry family [Target, SS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate thermal severity because Re telluride CVT is reported at 980 → 840 °C and WTe2-type CVT at 820 → 700 °C [SS5/TS4, SS1/TS1]. It still requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile halogen transport agents, and multiple setup steps [SS1, TS1, SS5/TS4]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is intrinsic to the recommended route [SS5/TS4].

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT receives the best kinetic score because halogen transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion of refractory Re, and iodine/bromine carriers are specifically reported for Re dichalcogenides [SS5/TS4]. Layered tellurides such as WTe2 and TaRhTe4 are also grown by CVT, supporting the crystallization pathway [SS1, TS1, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is composition control, because iodine is not merely a transport agent but a stoichiometric component of Re3Te4I [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The Te-rich flux route stays within the 400–950 °C range used for telluride melting reactions [CS5]. Its severity is still moderate because sealed Ar/vacuum ampoules, volatile Te/I species, slow cooling, and post-growth flux removal are required [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The route has less direct evidence for Re–Te–I than CVT or sealed solid-state synthesis, so the uncertainty is higher [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 3

A Te-rich liquid can improve contact and mass transport, and excess Te is beneficial in NbIrTe4 and Re chalcogenide transport contexts [SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4]. However, Re remains refractory and may not dissolve rapidly in Te-rich flux, lowering the diffusion score [Reasonable Inference]. The structural pathway is plausible for layered tellurides but not directly demonstrated for Re3Te4I [CS5; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one metal species, Re, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The formula has only 8 atoms per formula unit and no evidence is provided for a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing coexistence, so F4 is high [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for additional amorphization triggers]. The remaining uncertainty is that low-dimensional layered materials can form stacking disorder, but that is not the same as amorphization and is not directly quantified by the given checks [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re3Te4I is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The best support comes from structurally similar Re3Tl(Se2Cl)2, which demonstrates layered triclinic Re chalcohalide chemistry, and from Re dichalcogenide CVT using iodine/bromine carriers [SS3, SS5/TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, because iodine can both mobilize Re-containing species and supply the terminal Re–I bond environment [SS5/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding binary Re–Te phases, over-iodinated rhenium iodides, or iodine loss during high-temperature treatment [SS3, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible to likely, with CVT from a prereacted charge as the best first attempt [SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4].
