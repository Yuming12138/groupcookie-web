### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0320 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore scores highly. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, such as MnIn₂Te₄ at 0.0354 eV/atom and MnIn₂Se₄ at 0.0316 eV/atom, have reported syntheses (TS-1, TS-4). Chemically exact In₂Te₃ analogues with higher hull energies were also synthesized, including CS-1 at 0.067 eV/atom and CS-2 at 0.308 eV/atom, so the target’s lower hull is favorable. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: low hull supports synthesizability but does not identify the kinetic route to monoclinic Pc.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule melt + directional freezing

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it needs vacuum sealing, Te containment, and multi-step thermal processing, all directly reported for exact In₂Te₃ (CS-2/SS-4). The kinetics are favorable for diffusion because synthesis occurs from a melt above 667 °C (CS-2/SS-4), but structural selection is less favorable because In₂Te₃ has multiple polymorphs, including high-pressure trigonal and directional-freezing tetragonal forms (CS-1, CS-2). The main uncertainty is whether Pc is accessed by slow cooling, quenching, or post-annealing. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically distinguishes the Pc polymorph, so TS is neutral for the exact thermal window.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure conversion

High-pressure treatment is strongly supported for exact In₂Te₃ and related In–Te phases (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4), and for structurally/thermodynamically similar MnIn₂Te₄ (SS-1, TS-1). The formal F2 deduction is not as severe as ultra-high pressure because CS-1 uses 30 kbar = 3 GPa, below the >5 GPa rubric cutoff; nevertheless the practical difficulty is high due to specialized apparatus. Kinetically, short treatments at 400–500 °C for ≤4 h succeeded for high-pressure In₂Te₃ (CS-1), but structural reconstruction and metastability are significant risks. The key uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the target Pc sheet or instead converts it to denser octahedral phases like CS-1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Long sealed-tube annealing

This route is supported by related chalcogenides that require prolonged high-vacuum annealing, including InGaTe₂-type materials at 550–650 °C for several weeks (TS-3) and CdIn₂Te₄-type compounds requiring weeks at high temperature (SS-5). The conditions are not extreme in temperature or pressure, but vacuum sealing and Te volatility drive the F2 penalties. Kinetics benefit from low-melting In/Te, but slow ordering and competing phases lower F3, consistent with long anneals and phase-purity issues in TS-3/SS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS shows that long annealing alone selects monoclinic Pc In₂Te₃, so this route is less preferred than Route 1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Mechanochemical alloying + anneal

Mechanochemical In–Te alloying is directly supported for thermodynamically similar InTe using elemental powders under Ar (TS-2). This gives good precursor activation and relatively mild conditions, producing the best raw score. However, TS-2 also reports multiple InTe phases and minor In₂O₃, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score and creates uncertainty for phase-pure Pc In₂Te₃. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS confirms mechanochemical synthesis of exact In₂Te₃, so expert judgment ranks this behind the direct melt route despite the attractive raw score.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: CVT / vapor-assisted growth

CVT is supported for structurally and thermodynamically similar chalcogenides using AlCl₃ in sealed silica tubes (SS-2, TS-4). It is plausible for crystal growth of In₂Te₃ after preparing a polycrystalline feed, but no CS source reports binary In₂Te₃ CVT, so the transferability is weaker. Composition control is the main kinetic concern because volatile transport can fractionate In and Te species [Reasonable Inference], and exact In₂Te₃ already shows polymorph/ordering sensitivity (CS-2/SS-4). This route is best viewed as a secondary single-crystal optimization route, not the primary discovery synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, In, so the cation-radius-difference check is not triggered. The target structure includes corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, so the strict mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not triggered. The full unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS resolves this, so this check is treated as not triggered with some uncertainty. Overall amorphization risk is low, although Route 4 has a route-specific amorphous/multiphase risk because ball-milled InTe analogues showed multiple phases (TS-2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target monoclinic Pc In₂Te₃ is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than a clearly established ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.032 eV/atom and multiple other In₂Te₃ polymorphs are reported in CS-1 and CS-2. The analogue support is strong chemically because exact In₂Te₃ has been synthesized from elemental In and Te by both high-pressure and directional-freezing routes (CS-1, CS-2/SS-4). Structural support is moderate because related tetrahedral/telluride networks form by solid-state, CVT, and high-pressure methods (SS-1 to SS-5), but none exactly matches monoclinic Pc In₂Te₃. The most critical enabling factor is the low-melting, melt-processable In–Te binary chemistry, which allows homogenization and diffusion under sealed ampoule conditions (CS-2, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control: the target must be selected against trigonal, tetragonal, InTe, Te-rich, or In-rich competing phases (CS-1, CS-2, SS-4).
