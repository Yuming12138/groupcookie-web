### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0390 eV/atom, which falls below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore receives a high score of 94. CS includes chemically similar low-E_hull bromides such as Li₂ZnBr₄ at 0.017 eV/atom and Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.029 eV/atom (CS1, CS2), while SS includes several ground-state halometallates (SS1, SS2, SS4). TS also contains near-target metastable halides such as NaHgCl₃ at 0.055 eV/atom and Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.029 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). The uncertainty is that no source directly confirms LiCdBr₃ as an experimentally isolated phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state melt/anneal

The F2 score is reduced because evacuated ampoules and quenching are used for Li₂FeBr₄ (CS2, TS2), water-free Ar is required for Li₂ZnBr₄/Na₂ZnBr₄ (CS1, CS5), and cadmium bromide introduces toxicity and possible volatility [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, which improves the severity score. Kinetically, fused binary halides are favorable because Li₂FeBr₄ and Li₂MnBr₄ form from LiBr + MBr₂ melts (CS2, CS3), but long annealing and structural reorganization lower the reconstruction score. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition with other Li–Cd–Br stoichiometries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Melt growth / slow cooling

The F2 score is moderate because structurally similar halometallates are grown from sealed ampoules under dry Ar at 250–400 °C (SS1, SS4), and NaInBr₄ forms by slow cooling binary-component melts (CS4, SS5). It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, but still requires dry handling and sealed ampoules. F3 is strong because melt growth bypasses slow solid diffusion, as indicated by clear melt formation and slow cooling in SS1. The main uncertainty is whether the target layered CdBr₆ sheet nucleates instead of alternative cadmium bromide complexes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solution crystallization

The F2 score is high because TS analogues crystallize from aqueous solution at room temperature or by slow cooling from boiling water without special atmosphere (TS1, TS5). F3 is kinetically favorable due to solution mobility, but structural reconstruction is scored low because there is insufficient CS/SS evidence for anhydrous LiCdBr₃ crystallization from water. The key uncertainty is hydrate or solvated complex formation, which is a known risk for hygroscopic LiBr/CdBr₂ systems [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, despite high numerical condition favorability, this is not the preferred route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target likely has a significant Li⁺/Cd²⁺ radius mismatch, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s formula and described P-31m sheet imply a small unit cell rather than >40 atoms [Reasonable Inference from target structure], and the connectivity is dominated by edge-sharing CdBr₆ octahedra rather than a highly mixed vertex/edge/face network. CS/SS analogues commonly crystallize as ordered halometallates under melt or annealing conditions (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5), supporting a moderate-to-low amorphization risk. The uncertainty is that layered halides can form glassy or microcrystalline products if quenched too rapidly [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiCdBr₃ is likely a mildly metastable but accessible phase rather than a deep ground-state compound, based on its E_hull of 0.039 eV/atom. CS and SS analogues strongly support anhydrous sealed-ampoule melt chemistry for alkali/metal bromides, especially LiBr + MBr₂ systems such as Li₂FeBr₄ and Li₂MnBr₄ (CS2, CS3, TS2). TS provides useful thermodynamic context and shows that layered halometallates can crystallize near room temperature in some chloride systems (TS1), but this is less directly transferable to an anhydrous bromide. The most critical enabling factor is the low-melting, highly mobile binary bromide melt, which should accelerate ion transport and crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: LiCdBr₃ may compete with LiBr, CdBr₂, hydrated species, or other Li–Cd–Br stoichiometries if water content, stoichiometry, or cooling rate are not tightly controlled.
