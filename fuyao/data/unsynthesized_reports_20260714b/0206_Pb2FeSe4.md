### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. Chemically similar stable analogues include MnBi₂Se₄ and SiPb₂Se₄ with E_hull = 0 (**CS-1, CS-4**), while thermodynamic analogues YbCuPbSe₃ and K₂Bi₈Se₁₃ are also on the hull (**TS-1, TS-2**). The remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature decomposition or off-stoichiometry, which is not captured directly; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** for vapor-pressure-dependent stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate because the exact SiPb₂Se₄ analogue forms near 700 °C in vacuum-sealed Vycor (**CS-4**), and Pb–Se analogues use sealed fused silica tubes (**CS-2 / SS-2**). The kinetic score is only moderate because Fe metal is refractory and may react more slowly than Pb/Se **[Reasonable Inference]**, although FeSe₄ tetrahedra are known in CuFe₂InSe₄ (**SS-4**). This route is the simplest, but it may require repeated grinding/annealing; **insufficient evidence from TS** for direct FePb₂Se₄ formation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 halide-flux growth

Flux growth has similar atmosphere and sealed-tube penalties to Route 1, but better kinetics because a liquid flux improves transport. MnBi₂Se₄ was specifically isolated as good crystals using LiCl–RbCl flux cooled from 800 to 400 °C over 1–2 weeks (**CS-1 / TS-4**), and other low-dimensional selenides are accessible by flux methods (**TS-2, TS-5**). The main uncertainty is whether chloride flux changes Fe/Pb redox or incorporates chloride; **insufficient evidence from SS** for the exact target in chloride flux.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 chemical vapor transport

CVT conditions are supported by structurally similar layered Pb–Se material using iodine at 800–850 °C (**SS-1**) and Bi₃Se₄Br using a temperature gradient in sealed ampoules (**CS-3 / SS-5**). Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion, but composition control can be difficult for multicomponent Fe–Pb–Se systems **[Reasonable Inference]**. No thermodynamic analogue directly demonstrates Fe–Pb–Se CVT, so **insufficient evidence from TS** reduces confidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 melt/anneal

High-temperature melt/anneal is supported by Fe-containing CuFe₂InSe₄ synthesis and YbCuPbSe₃ synthesis in sealed quartz ampoules (**SS-4, SS-3 / TS-1**). The route improves diffusion but incurs higher severity due to >1000 °C operation and long annealing (**SS-3 / TS-1, SS-4**). CuFe₂InSe₄ contained 13.4% FeSe impurity (**SS-4**), indicating that Fe–Se binary competition may limit phase purity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines large Pb and smaller tetrahedral Fe cations, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target is crystalline P2₁/c and layered, and the formula size likely does not exceed 40 atoms per conventional cell **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the exact atom count is not provided; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** for a definitive cell-size trigger. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is not clearly described as vertex + edge + face sharing in the target, so that trigger is not applied.

Fe(PbSe₂)₂ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied energy above hull is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹.
