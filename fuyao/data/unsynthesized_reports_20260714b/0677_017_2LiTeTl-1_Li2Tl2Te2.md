### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0220 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range [target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, such as Na3Mn4Te6 at 0.021 eV/atom and RbAuTe at 0.024 eV/atom, are reported as synthesizable, although sometimes with phase-control difficulty [TS1, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that small positive E_hull suggests possible metastability rather than guaranteed ground-state behavior [target; TS1/TS5 comparison].

#### F2 — Route 1

The direct sealed-tube route uses moderate temperatures similar to 600 °C NaAuTe/KAuTe syntheses and 573–1023 K alkali telluride reactions [CS1, CS4, SS4]. The severity score is reduced by the need for vacuum/sealed-tube conditions, volatile Te/Tl containment, multiple thermal steps, and Li/Tl handling risk [SS4, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact LiTlTe decomposition temperature, so the proposed thermal window remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Li, Tl, and Te should react more readily than refractory oxides because Tl and Te are low-melting and alkali tellurides often form at moderate temperatures [CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3]. However, the structural transformation is not templated by a known LiTlTe precursor, and related tellurides need long anneals or controlled cooling [CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1]. Thus kinetics are moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Melt/Bridgman synthesis avoids extreme temperature and pressure, matching Tl–Te analogues grown near 550–573 K/°C-scale melt conditions and alkali telluride fusion at 600 °C [SS2, SS3, CS1]. The route still requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, slow cooling, and toxic/air-sensitive precursor management [SS2, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. Phase control is more severe than the temperature alone suggests because AgTlTe analogues show non-stoichiometry and polymorphic issues [SS3].

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth has favorable kinetics because diffusion occurs through a liquid or partially molten medium [SS2, SS3]. Slow cooling supports crystallization, but AgTl3Te2 and AgTlTe analogues reveal peritectic behavior, narrow composition ranges, and cracking/non-stoichiometry [SS2, SS3]. Therefore the kinetic score is higher than direct solid-state synthesis but limited by ordering and composition control.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses high but manageable temperatures around 750–850 °C, consistent with alkali polytelluride flux synthesis and Li-containing flux synthesis [TS2, CS5]. It requires high vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions, multi-step growth and flux separation, and air-sensitive/telluride handling [TS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a benign removable flux exists specifically for LiTlTe, so practical severity may be underestimated.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth gives the best kinetic score because molten alkali/polytelluride fluxes provide high precursor activity and rapid mass transport [TS2]. It may also support layered telluride crystallization, as seen for tetragonal KEu(CuTe2)2 [TS2]. The uncertainty is selectivity: fluxes can stabilize Te-rich or off-stoichiometric products, and no Li–Tl–Te flux analogue is given [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; TS2].

#### F2 — Route 4

The high-pressure route has moderate temperature but high equipment severity because TS5 uses a modified Belt apparatus at 40 kbar [TS5]. If pressure screening exceeds 5 GPa, the specified high-pressure penalty applies [TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Because the target is already close to the hull, high pressure is a contingency rather than the primary route [target; TS5].

#### F3 — Route 4

TS5 demonstrates that a related telluride can react rapidly at 500 °C under pressure, supporting acceptable kinetics [TS5]. High pressure may help suppress volatility and stabilize otherwise inaccessible phases [TS5; Reasonable Inference]. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that LiTlTe specifically requires or benefits from high-pressure stabilization.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Li/Tl cation size mismatch and mixed corner-/edge-sharing polyhedral connectivity, both of which increase disorder or amorphization risk [target; Reasonable Inference for ionic radii]. The unit cell is likely small because the structure description contains one Li, one Tl, and one Te site in P4/nmm, so the large-cell criterion is not triggered [target]. The main uncertainty is that amorphization is more relevant to rapid quench/thin-film routes than slow bulk crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiTlTe is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom [target]. The strongest support comes from sealed-tube and melt/flux synthesis of alkali tellurides and Tl–Te ternaries under moderate temperatures, especially NaAuTe/KAuTe, AgTl3Te2, AgTlTe, and alkali polytelluride flux products [CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility of low-melting Li/Tl/Te-containing mixtures, especially under melt or flux conditions [SS2, SS3, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, because related Tl–Te and alkali telluride systems show peritectic reactions, non-stoichiometry, air/moisture sensitivity, and competing phases [CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, SS4, TS1]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require sealed inert handling, slow cooling, and composition screening [CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS2].
