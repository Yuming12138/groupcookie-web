### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0480 eV/atom, placing it just inside the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom bin [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable metastabilities are experimentally synthesized, including Cu2FeSnS4 with E_hull 0.043 eV/atom (TS-1), EuCu2SnS4 with E_hull 0.033 eV/atom (TS-4), and Cu2CoSnS4 with E_hull 0.054 eV/atom (TS-5). The uncertainty is that an E_hull near 0.05 eV/atom may indicate a metastable polymorph rather than the ground state, and none of CS/SS/TS directly proves this exact P-31m CuSbS3 phase.

**F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube + CVT.**  
The route is conditionally severe because it uses vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile S/I2, and multistep transport, all supported by CVT analogues (CS-5, SS-5, TS-3). Kinetically, vapor transport improves diffusion and crystal growth, but analogues often require long times such as 300 h or several weeks (CS-5, TS-3), implying moderate ordering difficulty. The main uncertainty is whether iodine transport stabilizes the trigonal P-31m CuSbS3 polymorph or instead crystallizes common Cu–Sb–S competing phases [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F2/F3 — Route 2: hydrothermal/solvothermal.**  
This route has mild temperature severity because Cu-containing sulfides crystallize at 200 °C in hydrothermal/solvothermal conditions (SS-2, TS-5). Kinetics are favorable due to soluble chlorides, amine complexation, and sulfur donors, as demonstrated by chloride/thiocarbamide/ethylenediamine chemistry in SS-2 and chloride/diethylenetriamine chemistry in TS-5. The uncertainty is structural: the target has SbS6 octahedra and CuCuS3 trigonal pyramids, while SS-2/TS-5 analogues are tetrahedral stannite-type sulfides, so transferability is chemical rather than exact structural matching.

**F2/F3 — Route 3: supercritical NH3.**  
Supercritical NH3 synthesis is strongly supported for antimony sulfides with Ag/Sb/S networks at 160 °C for 4 days (SS-1, SS-3), but it requires high-pressure autoclave operation, NH3 handling, and drybox loading. Kinetics are likely favorable because polysulfide/NH3 media dissolve and recrystallize sulfide species, but reported phase mixtures and unidentified XRD peaks indicate phase-control risk (SS-1, SS-3). The biggest uncertainty is replacing Ag/alkali chalcogenometalates with a neutral CuSbS3 phase [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from SS].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is structurally simple in composition but contains different Cu and Sb coordination environments, which triggers the cation-size/coordination-mismatch check [Target; standard ionic-radius reasoning]. The target has corner-sharing Cu–Sb polyhedra and edge-sharing SbS6 octahedra, but no face sharing is reported, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for CuSbS3, so the F4 score remains moderately optimistic with residual uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuSbS3 with E_hull = 0.048 eV/atom is likely a low-lying metastable phase rather than a clearly robust ground-state phase [Target; thermodynamic comparison to TS-1/TS-5]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of chemically related sulfides by CVT, solvothermal, solid-state, and supercritical routes, but none directly demonstrates the exact trigonal P-31m CuSbS3 structure [CS-1/CS-5/SS-2/SS-5/TS-3/TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature solution or transport pathways that can crystallize metastable sulfides before equilibrium decomposition or conversion occurs (SS-2, TS-5, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against binary Cu–S, Sb–S, and other Cu–Sb–S polymorphs, since analogous systems report mixed phases, impurities, or narrow phase windows (CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with solvothermal screening preferred for accessibility and CVT recommended for crystal growth/phase confirmation.
