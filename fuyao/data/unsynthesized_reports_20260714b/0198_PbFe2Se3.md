### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high score. Chemically and thermodynamically similar selenides are frequently on the hull, including Tl₂Pd₄Se₆/Pt₂TlSe₃ in CS1/CS5, MnBi₂Se₄ in CS2, FeSe in SS1/TS1, and YbCuPbSe₃ in TS5. Uncertainty remains because DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of FeSe/PbSe binaries.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed-tube solid-state route uses moderate temperatures like Pb–Bi–Se synthesis at 773 K in CS4 and Pb-containing selenide synthesis in TS5, avoiding high pressure. Deductions are mainly due to vacuum sealing, volatile Se, staged processing, and toxic Pb/Se, all consistent with sealed ampoule requirements in CS4, SS3, and TS5. CS/SS/TS do not show a direct Fe₂PbSe₃ synthesis, so condition severity remains somewhat uncertain.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are moderate because Fe is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], while Pb and Se are reactive and can form low-melting/intermediate phases. FeSe₄ tetrahedral motifs are well supported by SS1/TS1, but target formation requires Pb ordering into a 1D monoclinic ribbon not present in simple FeSe. Long annealing and impurity reports in SS2/SS3 support a conservative kinetics score.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Melt homogenization has greater severity because SS2 and SS3 use high-temperature melting/annealing, with SS2 reaching 1500 K and SS3 using 1150 °C. The method still uses standard sealed quartz equipment, as in SS2/SS3, but volatility and ampoule safety are more severe than Route 1. CS1/CS5 support melting reactions for selenides, but their 400–950 °C range is milder than Fe-containing melt homogenization.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Melt processing improves homogenization, supported by SS2/SS3, but long annealing up to 30 days indicates slow ordering. FeSe impurity in SS2 and faint impurity lines in SS3 indicate competing phase formation. Thus kinetics are not poor, but structural ordering into the target ribbon phase may be slow.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth avoids >1000 °C conditions and is directly supported by CS2 at 800→400 °C and TS2 at 450–500 °C. It still requires sealed evacuated tubes, volatile Se control, flux handling, washing, and toxic Pb/Se, which justify moderate deductions. CS2 and TS2 show that such conditions are standard for complex selenide crystals.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux substantially improves kinetics by dissolving/reactivating solids, as demonstrated for MnBi₂Se₄ in CS2 and K/Nb/Ag/Se compounds in TS2. It is especially attractive for a 1D ribbon phase, because flux routes often favor crystal growth of low-dimensional chalcogenides, as in CS2/TS2. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, since TS2 reports low yields and precise thermal programming needs.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
CVT uses moderate temperatures and sealed ampoules, as shown for Bi₃Se₄Br in CS3. However, it introduces halogen transport chemistry and requires prior powder synthesis, so the process is multi-step and chemically more sensitive. There is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for direct CVT of Fe–Pb–Se, so SS/TS signal is neutral at 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
CS3 supports vapor growth of a 1D monoclinic selenide ribbon compound, but that system contains bromide as a structural component, unlike Fe₂PbSe₃. Fe and Pb may transport at different rates and form stable halides, making composition control difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore CVT is plausible mainly as a crystal-growth optimization after bulk synthesis, not as the first-choice preparation route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Fe/Pb cation size contrast [Reasonable Inference], which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. However, FeSe₄ tetrahedral frameworks crystallize readily in SS1/TS1, and Pb-containing selenides crystallize in CS4/TS5, reducing concern. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for unit-cell atom count >40 or face-sharing polyhedral connectivity in the target, so those checks are not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Fe₂PbSe₃ is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue set supports synthesis reasonably well: FeSe₄ tetrahedral motifs are strongly supported by SS1/TS1, Pb–Se sealed-tube chemistry is supported by CS4/TS5, and low-dimensional selenide crystal growth is supported by CS2/CS3/TS2. The most important enabling factor is the thermodynamic stability combined with chemically compatible Fe²⁺/Pb²⁺/Se²⁻ charge balance [Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition with FeSe and PbSe binaries, especially under sealed-tube or melt conditions [Reasonable Inference supported by impurity issues in SS2/SS3]. Overall, flux growth is the best route because it reduces diffusion limitations while allowing slow ordering into the 1D ribbon structure.
