### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the edge of the high-synthesizability stability bin. CS supports that related Sr–U oxides can be synthesized even with E_hull values up to ~0.139 eV/atom, although impurities are sometimes observed (CS-1, CS-5). TS provides strong thermodynamic similarity because several Ti/Nb perovskite analogues with E_hull ≈0.025–0.068 eV/atom are experimentally accessible (TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty comes from the target’s unusual SrUTi2O6 redox/coordination chemistry, which is not directly demonstrated in any CS/SS/TS source.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Ceramic solid-state

Condition severity is moderate because CS demonstrates Sr–U oxide synthesis in air at 900–1150 °C using standard ceramic methods and Pt crucibles (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). Kinetics are less favorable because TiO2/Ti2O3 diffusion and structural reconstruction into the low-symmetry P1 phase are likely slower than forming the simpler octahedral Sr2B′UO6 phases in CS. TS reduced titanates show that Ti redox chemistry often requires vacuum or reducing conditions, so pure air firing may overoxidize the target if Ti3+/U4+ is required (TS-2, TS-4). SS gives insufficient direct chemical evidence for Sr–U–Ti ceramic synthesis.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Controlled-atmosphere reduced solid-state

This route has harsher F2 conditions because TS and SS reduced perovskites use vacuum, Ar/H2, sealed tubes, Mo foil/crucibles, or evacuated capsules (TS-2, TS-3, TS-4; SS-2, SS-3). Kinetics remain moderate because oxide/suboxide precursors are less reactive, but the atmosphere is more chemically appropriate for reduced titanates. CS supports the Sr–U solid-state framework, while TS supports oxygen-potential control for Ti oxides; however, no source directly confirms the correct U/Ti valence combination in SrUTi2O6. The main uncertainty is whether the target decomposes into SrTiO3, UO2/U3O8, SrUO4, or Ti oxides under the required pO2.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: High-temperature sealed inert tube

SS and TS show that difficult perovskite-like Nb oxides can require 1500–1550 °C, sealed Nb tubes, Ar, and long annealing to achieve phase purity (SS-1, SS-4; TS-1). These conditions strongly penalize F2 but improve cation diffusion relative to lower-temperature routes. The route is less chemically certain because the high-temperature sealed-tube examples are Nb-based rather than U–Ti-based, and uranium compatibility with refractory ampoules is not directly documented in CS/SS/TS. It is best viewed as a fallback if lower-temperature routes fail kinetically.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Sol-gel / polymerized complex route

Sol-gel has the best F3 because soluble Sr/U/Ti precursors should give molecular-scale mixing and shorten diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], and CS directly supports uranium acetate as a usable U precursor (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). F2 is still moderate because a final high-temperature and possibly controlled-atmosphere anneal is likely needed, based on CS ceramic temperatures and TS reduced titanate requirements. The largest uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source explicitly reports a sol-gel synthesis of this Sr–U–Ti phase, so the route is extrapolated rather than directly evidenced. It is nevertheless recommended because the target has a low-symmetry mixed-polyhedral structure where cation homogeneity is critical.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large Sr site and much smaller Ti/U polyhedral sites, so cation size mismatch triggers C1 based on the target bond lengths. The target’s primitive description has only 10 atoms, so C2 is not triggered, and CS/SS/TS do not provide contrary evidence. C3 is triggered because the target has mixed UO5/TiO5/TiO4 polyhedra with corner and edge sharing, unlike the simpler mostly octahedral perovskite analogues in CS/SS/TS. This gives a medium amorphization/poor-crystallization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrUTi2O6 is likely a borderline stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability threshold. The CS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of Sr–U oxide solid-state synthesis, while TS/SS analogues support the broader feasibility of high-temperature titanate/perovskite oxide synthesis. The most important enabling factor is the availability of conventional oxide/carbonate/acetate routes that have already produced related Sr–U oxides at accessible temperatures. The most important limiting factor is redox control: the target stoichiometry and Ti/U bond environments may require a narrow oxygen fugacity not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. A sol-gel route followed by controlled-atmosphere annealing offers the best balance between homogeneity, diffusion, and redox tunability.
