### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0010 eV/atom, so it falls essentially on the convex hull and receives 98/100 by the provided rubric. Chemically similar CuAlCl₄/CuGaCl₄ phases are on or very near the hull (CS1–CS3), and thermodynamic analogues such as TlCuCl₃ are also near-hull (TS1), supporting high intrinsic stability. SS data are consistent but less chemically relevant, with several on-hull halides and one modestly metastable PdCl₂ (SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed near-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Ni/Cu ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to CS1 and CS3, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. It does require evacuated or Ar sealed ampoules, volatile chloride retention, and air/moisture-sensitive chloride precursors, which are supported by CS1/CS3 and TS1. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct NiCuCl₃ condition severity is treated as neutral, although SS5 supports the general evacuated-ampoule halide approach. The resulting severity score is 65/100.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chlorides are reactive at low temperature in chemically similar systems, as CuCl + GaCl₃/AlCl₃ react around 523–533 K (CS1, CS3). Diffusion is likely aided by halide mobility or partial melting, consistent with CS and SS5, so kinetics are better than conventional refractory solid-state oxide synthesis. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the target’s mixed NiCl₆/CuCl₃ layered framework, because CS analogues are mostly tetrahedral CuMCl₄ networks rather than Ni/Cu octahedral-pyramidal sheets. This gives 77/100.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains below 1000 °C, consistent with CS2/CS5 and TS1, but it has more operational penalties: sealed atmosphere, volatile chlorides, multi-step growth, and air-sensitive/toxic precursors. CS2/CS5 explicitly show cooling-rate-dependent melt products, which increases procedural control requirements. SS5 also supports melt/quench/anneal halide synthesis but does not remove these severity penalties. The score is 55/100.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth provides high cation mobility, supported by CuAlCl₄ melt growth in CS2/CS5 and TlCuCl₃ Bridgman growth in TS1. This improves precursor activity and diffusion relative to dry powder reaction. However, the crystallization pathway may be sensitive to cooling rate and polymorphism, as CS2/CS5 show quenching versus slow cooling can select different CuAlCl₄ phases. The kinetics score is therefore high but not perfect at 82/100.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT uses sealed evacuated ampoules and halogen/metal-chloride vapors, as shown in SS1/SS2 and TS2/TS3. It avoids extreme temperature or pressure but requires a transport agent, temperature gradient, and often a prior synthesis step. Toxic/corrosive halogen chemistry also applies by analogy to Br₂ transport in SS2 and sensitive halides in TS2/TS3. The severity score is 55/100.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport is plausible for halides because SS1 and SS2 show sublimation/CVT growth of Pd halides, while TS2/TS3 show sealed-ampoule vapor/gradient crystallization of thermodynamic halide analogues. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS for CVT of chemically similar Cu–Ni chlorides, so transferability is uncertain. Ternary composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because Cu and Ni chloride volatility may differ [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetics score is 60/100.

#### F2 — Route 4

Hydrothermal synthesis uses lower temperature, supported by SS4, and slow evaporation is very mild for CuBr₂ in TS4. Nevertheless, sealed ampoules, corrosive chloride media, redox control, and multi-step solution handling impose penalties. CS data do not directly support hydrothermal synthesis for CuAlCl₄/CuGaCl₄ analogues, so CS is neutral for this route. The severity score is 55/100.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution growth can bypass solid-state diffusion, and SS4 demonstrates hydrothermal chloride crystallization at 150 °C while TS4 demonstrates mild Cu-halide crystallization. The weakness is chemical: the target likely requires Cu⁺ stabilization alongside Ni²⁺ in chloride media, and aqueous/oxidizing environments can destabilize Cu⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. Because CS provides insufficient evidence for hydrothermal Cu–M–Cl formation, this route is exploratory. The kinetics score is 72/100.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target structure appears small-cell and crystalline, with defined NiCl₆ octahedra and CuCuCl₃ trigonal pyramids, so it does not obviously trigger the unit-cell-size amorphization criterion. It has corner- and edge-sharing but no reported face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity penalty is not triggered by the given criterion. The cation radius mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% for plausible Ni²⁺/Cu⁺ coordination environments [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly determine these target-specific checks, so they are neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NiCuCl₃ is likely a near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom, and similar Cu chloride phases in CS and TS are frequently on or near the hull (CS1–CS3, TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar sealed-ampoule Cu chloride syntheses at moderate temperatures, especially CuAlCl₄/CuGaCl₄ made from CuCl and metal chlorides (CS1–CS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of chloride precursors in sealed ampoules or melts, which allows low-temperature crystallization rather than refractory high-temperature diffusion (CS1–CS3, SS5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Cu⁺/Ni²⁺ stoichiometry and chlorine chemical potential without forming CuCl, NiCl₂, CuCl₂, or other competing binary/ternary chlorides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, with the best first attempt being a dry sealed-ampoule NiCl₂ + CuCl reaction followed by mild annealing.
