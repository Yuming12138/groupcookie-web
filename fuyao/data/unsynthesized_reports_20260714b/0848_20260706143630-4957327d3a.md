### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is strong evidence for a ground-state or near-ground-state phase by the provided criterion. Several close chemical analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Cd(BH₄)₂, LaAlH₆, NdAlH₆, PrAlH₆, and Zr(BH₄)₄ (CS-1/2/3/4/5). Thermodynamic analogues TiH₂ and ZrH₂ are also stable or nearly stable (TS-1/2). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic/phase-selectivity rather than thermodynamic.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Room-temperature mechanochemistry is well supported for chemically similar borohydrides and rare-earth complex hydrides (CS-1/2/3/4/5), so the condition severity is moderate rather than severe. The main F2 penalties are controlled H₂/Ar handling and air-sensitive precursors, both directly reflected in rare-earth alanate and borohydride syntheses (CS-1/2/3/4/5). Kinetics are favorable because metathesis avoids long-range diffusion, but structural reconstruction from simple BH₄⁻ salts to ordered LaB₂H₇ is nontrivial [Reasonable Inference]. Phase purity remains uncertain because rare-earth alanate analogues had low yields and impurities (CS-2/3/4).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Low-temperature milling is mild thermally and directly supported by Zr(BH₄)₄ synthesis at −30 °C (CS-5). The route is penalized for sealed/cold handling, multi-step aging, and air-sensitive reagents, all consistent with CS-5 and CS-1. Kinetics should be good for initial metathesis but slower for crystallization because CS-5 required 14–30 days of storage for crystal formation. Uncertainty is whether LaB₂H₇ is volatile/sublimable like Zr(BH₄)₄; CS gives a warning signal, but not direct proof for LaB₂H₇.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Hydrogenation is plausible only by extrapolation, because SS/TS hydride analogues show that metal hydrides can be formed but do not demonstrate B–H complex borohydride formation (SS-1/2; TS-1/2). The condition severity is higher due to H₂ pressure, sealed handling, and likely repeated processing [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are poor because bulk boron/boride conversion to B–H units requires major reconstruction [Reasonable Inference], and SS/TS analogues show long times or difficult phase control (SS-1/2; TS-1/2). Therefore this is a fallback exploratory route rather than the recommended route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The La/B size and bonding mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference], and mixed La–H/B–H coordination suggests local structural complexity consistent with complex hydrides (CS-1/2/5). However, the target does not clearly exceed 40 atoms per unit cell from the provided information, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell size. The target also lacks explicit mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity in its description, so C3 is not triggered despite analogous mixed sharing in LaAlH₆ and Zr(BH₄)₄ (CS-2/5; SS-5; TS-5). Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LaB₂H₇ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple chemical/thermodynamic hydride analogues are also on or near the hull (CS-1/2/3/4/5; TS-1/2). The strongest synthesis support comes from mechanochemical metathesis of metal chlorides with alkali borohydrides, which is repeatedly demonstrated for borohydrides and rare-earth complex hydrides (CS-1/2/3/4/5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive B–H precursors such as LiBH₄/NaBH₄ that can form B–H-containing products at room temperature without high-temperature diffusion (CS-1/5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: rare-earth complex hydride analogues show low yields, impurities, and partial decomposition during milling (CS-2/3/4). Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, but isolating a pure crystalline LaB₂H₇ phase will likely require careful low-temperature/inert mechanochemical optimization.
