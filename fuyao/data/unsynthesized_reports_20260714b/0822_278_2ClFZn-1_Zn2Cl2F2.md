### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, placing it in the high synthesizability band for thermodynamic stability. TS analogues with similar hull energies, e.g. Li₂CrCl₄ at 0.039 eV/atom and Na₂CrCl₄ at 0.040 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized phases (TS2, TS4). CS also shows chemically related metastable oxyhalides can be experimentally accessible even at higher E_hull values such as 0.079 and 0.103 eV/atom (CS1, CS3). The uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly confirms ZnClF or its monoclinic P2₁/m polymorph, so polymorph competition remains possible.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube anhydrous solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because the route requires dry Ar/vacuum, sealed ampoules, and anhydrous halides, similar to CS5, SS2, and TS2. The maximum temperature is modest, around 350–450 °C, consistent with 400 °C evacuated-tube synthesis in TS2 and 250–400 °C halide melt syntheses in SS1/SS3. Kinetically, binary halides should be more reactive than refractory oxides, but forming an ordered layered ZnCl₃F₃ octahedral sheet from ZnF₂/ZnCl₂ requires significant reconstruction. Long anneals in SS2 and TS2 indicate that solid-state halide ordering can be slow.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal / solvothermal route

Hydrothermal synthesis is mild in temperature and supported by CS1, CS4, and SS4, all of which form related layered oxyhalides or hydroxyhalides under sealed aqueous conditions. Kinetics should be favorable because solution transport bypasses solid-state diffusion, as shown by low-temperature crystallization in CS4 and SS4. The major limitation is chemical: the target is anhydrous ZnClF, while hydrothermal analogues incorporate O/OH, so competing Zn(OH)Cl, ZnO, or hydroxyfluoride phases are likely [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for hydrothermal Zn–Cl–F phase control, so the TS contribution is neutral for this route-specific uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: ZnCl₂-rich molten-salt / self-flux growth

This route has moderate severity because it requires sealed dry ampoules and volatile/corrosive ZnCl₂, consistent with inert/sealed halide synthesis in SS1, SS2, SS3, and TS5. Kinetically it is the strongest route: molten halide media in SS1/SS3/TS5 enable crystal growth at 250–450 °C, and a ZnCl₂-rich melt should enhance Zn/Cl/F mobility [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is well supported by SS1 and SS3 for halide crystal growth. The key uncertainty is whether ZnF₂ dissolves sufficiently in ZnCl₂ and whether the desired 1:1 Cl:F ordered layered phase nucleates instead of binary ZnF₂, ZnCl₂ adducts, or other zinc halides.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Zn²⁺, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; this follows from the target composition, while CS/SS/TS provide only indirect support through single-cation layered oxyhalides such as CS1, CS3, and SS4. The structure is described as a simple 2D sheet of edge-sharing ZnCl₃F₃ octahedra, not a large multi-cation framework, so amorphization tendency should be low. Edge-sharing connectivity alone is not considered high-risk under the specified criterion, and related edge-sharing octahedral compounds are reported in SS4 and TS4. Remaining uncertainty is exact unit-cell size and possible Cl/F disorder, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZnClF is likely a **near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, within the range where TS analogues are experimentally accessible (TS2, TS4). The CS data support layered metal halide/oxyhalide sheet formation under hydrothermal or sealed conditions (CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5), while SS and TS data strongly support anhydrous halide ampoule and melt-growth chemistry (SS1–SS3, TS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halide transport, especially via ZnCl₂-rich molten flux, inferred from halide melt growth analogues (SS1, SS3, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target requires ordered mixed Cl/F coordination around Zn, and no CS/SS/TS source directly confirms ZnClF. Avoiding water and OH incorporation is especially important if hydrothermal conditions are attempted, because the closest hydrothermal analogues are oxyhalides/hydroxyhalides rather than anhydrous halofluorides (CS1, CS4, SS4).
