### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest provided stability bin and supports a high F1 score. TS analogues with similar or slightly higher E_hull values, such as Ba3WO6 at 0.0675 eV/atom and Sr2YSbO6 at 0.0519 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized by ceramic routes (TS-1, TS-4). The main uncertainty is that the target may be metastable relative to other Ba–Ge–O phases, and SS Ba germanate analogues report parasitic phase formation unless composition is tuned (SS-1, SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1: Solid-state ceramic

The route uses temperatures above 1000 °C and repeated grinding/firing, consistent with TS ceramic methods requiring high temperatures and iterative processing (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). Air operation and ambient pressure are favorable because many Ba/Sr oxide analogues are made in air without high pressure (CS-4, TS-1, TS-4). The main severity penalties are high temperature, multi-step processing, and Ba precursor toxicity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Solid-state ceramic

Carbonate/oxide precursors are available and proven in Ba/Sr ceramic analogues (CS-5, TS-1, TS-2), but they are less reactive than molecular precursors. Long-range diffusion and full structural reconstruction are likely, and repeated firings are common in TS analogues to achieve homogeneity (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The uncertainty is whether Ba2GeO4 forms cleanly before competing Ba germanates crystallize [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/SS-3].

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux/self-flux

Flux/self-flux synthesis still requires high temperature and multiple operations, but it avoids high pressure and strongly reducing/reactive atmospheres, as shown for Ca2GeO4 and related germanates (SS-4). Slow cooling at 2.8–3 K h⁻¹ is reported for Ca2GeO4-type flux growth (SS-4), so temperature control is important but not extreme. Ba-containing fluxes or Ba salts add toxicity risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux/self-flux

Flux growth improves kinetics by providing a liquid transport medium, directly supported by Ca2GeO4 growth from melt/self-flux or CaCl2 flux (SS-4). The strongest transferability comes from the same A2GeO4/Pnma germanate structural family in SS-4. The main kinetic uncertainty is phase selectivity, because Ba germanate analogues require GeO2 or BaO/GeO2 excess to suppress parasitic phases (SS-1, SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 3: Melt/Czochralski

Melt growth is more severe because temperatures may approach or exceed 1400 °C and specialized crucibles/controlled pulling are needed, as in Ba2TiGe2O8 Czochralski growth using iridium/RF equipment (SS-2). However, ambient pressure and air/Ar atmospheres are feasible in related systems (CS-4, SS-2). The route is therefore challenging but credible.

#### F3 — Route 3: Melt/Czochralski

Liquid-state growth gives excellent diffusion and high precursor activity, and multiple Ba germanates are grown by Czochralski or melt techniques (SS-1–SS-3). Structural reconstruction from a melt can be efficient, but crystal growth requires avoiding parasitic phases and possibly thermal stress, as reported for Ba germanate analogues (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). This route is kinetically strong but experimentally more demanding.

#### F2 — Route 4: Sol–gel/Pechini

This route is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly reported in CS/SS/TS. It likely uses lower crystallization temperatures than conventional ceramic processing but still involves multiple steps and toxic Ba salts. Air processing is reasonable because TS/CS oxide analogues are commonly synthesized in air (CS-4, TS-1, TS-4).

#### F3 — Route 4: Sol–gel/Pechini

Sol–gel precursors give high precursor activity by the rubric and can mitigate the long homogenization seen in ceramic TS analogues (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). However, crystallization from an amorphous precursor into the ordered Pnma Ba2GeO4 framework may require careful annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The lack of direct CS/SS/TS sol–gel evidence lowers confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Ba-centered polyhedra with small GeO4 tetrahedra, indicating a large cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target also contains both corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, and the structurally similar Ca2GeO4 framework has comparable edge/corner connectivity (SS-4). The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms for Pnma A2GeO4 [Reasonable Inference], so the amorphization risk is medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba2GeO4 is likely a marginally metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom are experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-4). The synthesis case is well supported by SS data because Ca2GeO4 with the same A2GeO4 stoichiometry and Pnma-related structure is grown by flux/self-flux methods (SS-4), and Ba germanates are successfully grown from tuned melts (SS-1–SS-3). The most important enabling factor is use of a flux or liquid phase, which reduces diffusion barriers and allows slow crystallization into the desired framework (SS-4). The main limiting factor is phase competition in Ba–Ge–O melts, because Ba germanate analogues require GeO2 or BaO/GeO2 excess to avoid parasitic phases (SS-1, SS-3). Overall, synthesis is feasible, with flux/self-flux screening recommended before attempting large single-crystal melt growth.
