## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0190 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues TS1–TS5 also sit near the hull, with E_hull values around 0.0096–0.0272 eV/atom, supporting that low-energy iodide phases can be experimentally accessed. CS analogues include several stable or near-stable iodides, including CS1 and CS3 at E_hull = 0.0 and CS2/CS4 near the hull. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee this exact P-31m layered polymorph rather than another Sr–Ag–I arrangement.

### F2/F3 — Route 1: Concentrated-HI solution crystallization

The severity is low to moderate because CS1 and CS3 demonstrate ambient or near-ambient crystallization from concentrated HI, while CS4 uses only mild heating. The main deductions are chemical handling hazards from concentrated HI and multiple solution-processing steps; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that high pressure or vacuum is required for this route. Kinetically, solution chemistry is favorable because diffusion is liquid-mediated, and direct crystallization of iodide frameworks is supported by CS1/CS3/CS4. The main uncertainty is AgI solubility and whether the target layered phase nucleates instead of binary AgI/SrI2 or Ag-rich phases.

### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal synthesis in aqueous HI

Hydrothermal severity is moderate because TS1 and CS2 require sealed vessels with hot HI, and CS2 specifically used a vacuum-sealed ampoule and slow cooling. Temperatures remain modest, 150–175 °C in CS2/TS1, so no extreme thermal penalty applies. Kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal HI can dissolve/recrystallize Ag–I species, as shown in TS1, but phase control may be nontrivial because TS1 reports twinning and low yield of untwinned crystals. Insufficient evidence from SS specifically confirms hydrothermal formation of SrAgI3, so this route is a strong backup rather than the first choice.

### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The severity is higher than solution routes because SS2 and TS5 use evacuated sealed ampoules/tubes, repeated annealing, and careful thermal control. The maximum temperature is still moderate, based on SS2’s 373–583 K and TS5’s 650 °C Sr-halide precedent. Kinetically, binary iodides are more reactive than refractory oxides, but SS2 explicitly required intermediate grindings until single-phase material was obtained, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. The uncertainty is competing phase formation, especially because SS2’s closest Ag iodide analogue is CsAg2I3 rather than SrAgI3.

### F2/F3 — Route 4: Melt growth / Bridgman-type crystallization

Melt growth has moderate-to-high severity because SS1, TS2, and TS5 use sealed ampoules, dry/inert handling, and slow cooling. Kinetics can be good in a melt, as SS1 crystallized NaAlI4 from a melt at 573 K, and TS3 reports Bridgman-grown TlCdI3 single crystals. However, TS2 shows that long annealing and repeated processing may be required for phase purity in some thermodynamic analogues. The main uncertainty is whether SrAgI3 melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically forms binary iodides or Ag-rich phases.

### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple composition and likely small unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests a large-cell glass-forming tendency. C1 is triggered because Sr and Ag occupy very different coordination environments in the target, which can increase local structural mismatch. C3 is not triggered under the strict vertex + edge + face coexistence criterion because the target description includes corner and edge sharing but no face sharing. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with many CS/SS/TS iodides crystallizing readily as needles, octahedra, blocks, or single crystals.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrAgI3 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0190 eV/atom, placing it well inside the commonly synthesizable range. The CS evidence strongly supports iodide crystallization from concentrated HI, while TS1 gives particularly relevant support for Ag–I chemistry in aqueous HI. The most critical enabling factor is the use of iodide-rich, strongly acidic solution or hydrothermal media to mobilize AgI and suppress hydrolysis. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: SrI2, AgI, and possible Ag-rich ternary iodides may compete, especially if stoichiometry or iodide activity is not controlled. Overall, the target appears quite feasible, with the solution route preferred and hydrothermal synthesis as the most credible backup.
