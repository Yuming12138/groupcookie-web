### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring bin defined in the prompt [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues near this hull energy have reported syntheses, including Pr(O₂Br)₃ at 0.0528 eV/atom [TS-1], SCl₂ at 0.0534 eV/atom [TS-3], and KTiH₂S₂O₉ at 0.0583 eV/atom [TS-4]. CS analogues include several stable hull phases made by sublimation or mild routes, such as AsClF₈ and Ge(BrF₅)₂ at E_hull = 0 [CS-1, CS-3]. The uncertainty is that SF₄ is molecular and very volatile, so thermodynamic stability of the crystal does not guarantee ambient-temperature recoverability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 has no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty, which is favorable compared with TS high-temperature examples such as Cr₃(PO₄)₂ at 1300 °C [TS-2] or TiBr₂ at 900 °C [TS-5]. However, it requires vacuum/dry inert handling, sealed systems, and toxic volatile SF₄, consistent with specialized fluoride handling in CS-1 and CS-3 [CS-1, CS-3]. CS-1 specifically used a vacuum line with Monel/stainless valves and sealed quartz capillaries, supporting the deduction for specialized controlled-atmosphere work [CS-1]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for SF₄ volatility and is treated as neutral for this route [SS-1–SS-5].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics should be favorable because no long-range cation diffusion or solid-state reconstruction is needed; the target is a molecular zero-dimensional phase [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-3 show that chemically similar volatile fluorides can crystallize directly by sublimation/temperature gradient, including overnight single-crystal growth in CS-1 [CS-1, CS-3]. Composition control is strong when starting from purified SF₄ because the molecular stoichiometry is already fixed [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by cryogenic crystal nucleation and retention rather than diffusion [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high furnace temperatures but uses reactive fluorination chemistry, giving similar controlled-atmosphere and toxicity penalties to Route 1 [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The CS examples show that ClF₃, AsF₅, BrF₃, and GeF₄ can be handled in specialized setups, but they also demonstrate that reactive fluorides are not routine bench chemicals [CS-1, CS-3]. Multi-step processing is required because the product must be generated, purified, and then crystallized [Reasonable Inference]. TS does not provide a reliable direct route for preparing SF₄, so TS is neutral for the synthetic severity of this route [TS-3].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The final crystallization step is kinetically similar to Route 1, but the formation step introduces competing reaction pathways [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. Direct fluorination can produce higher fluorides or byproducts, so composition control is substantially worse than using preformed SF₄ [Reasonable Inference]. CS fluorination analogues show success with reactive fluorides, but they do not prove selective synthesis of SF₄ from sulfur, so the score is only moderate [CS-1, CS-3]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for selective sulfur fluorination and are neutral for this kinetic factor [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses low temperature but requires anhydrous HF and sealed PFA/Teflon apparatus, which is demanding [CS-2]. CS-2 explicitly reports an all-Teflon vacuum line, dry box, PFA tubes, anhydrous HF, and a one-month temperature-gradient crystallization, justifying deductions for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed media, multistep handling, and toxicity [CS-2]. The route is not high-temperature or high-pressure in the prompt’s >5 GPa sense [CS-2]. #### F4 — Amorphization tendency
