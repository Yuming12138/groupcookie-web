### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score boundary in the provided rubric. TS analogues with similar or slightly higher hull energies, including LaTiO₃ at 0.0676 eV/atom (TS1), YTiO₃ at 0.0580 eV/atom (TS2), and CaNbO₃ at 0.0589 eV/atom (TS4), are experimentally synthesized, supporting metastable accessibility. CS4 is especially encouraging because Ca₂CrOsO₆ is reported synthesized despite a high computed hull of 0.265 eV/atom. SS provides structural but not direct thermodynamic chemistry for Ca–Ti–Os; insufficient evidence from SS for stability prediction, neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is severe because it requires >1000 °C, vacuum/low-pO₂ sealed conditions, toxic Os handling, and multiple heat/regrind steps; these requirements are supported by TS1 for vacuum titanates and CS4 for sealed Os oxides. CS3 and TS1 show that repeated ceramic processing is common for complex oxides. The score is limited mainly by Os safety and redox control, not by pressure, because no high pressure is needed in CS/TS analogues.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are poor-to-moderate because CaCO₃/Ti₂O₃/OsO₂ are solid oxide/carbonate precursors, and CS3/TS1 show long annealing and regrinding are often needed. Refractory Ti/Os diffusion and reconstruction into a low-symmetry P1 network are expected to be slow. The remaining uncertainty is whether Ti₂O₃ and OsO₂ react cleanly before Os volatilization or Ti oxidation occurs.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 inherits the sealed ampoule and Os hazard directly from CS4, including glovebox loading and in-situ oxygen control. It is not high-pressure and can remain near 950–1050 °C like CS4, but the oxygen buffer adds operational complexity and risk. TS1 suggests low-pO₂ is needed for Ti³⁺ titanates, creating tension with the oxygen-buffer approach.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are slightly worse than Route 1 because Os metal is less chemically active than OsO₂, and CS4 requires careful sealed oxidation to incorporate Os. Complete reconstruction from metal/oxide mixtures into a triclinic Ca–Ti–Os oxide may require multiple firings. The main uncertainty is whether the oxygen buffer can be tuned finely enough to oxidize Os without overoxidizing Ti.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has the best severity score because a flux could keep synthesis at or below 1000 °C, reducing the temperature penalty. However, sealed low-pO₂ handling, toxic Os species, flux drying/loading, and post-reaction washing remain necessary; these are supported by CS4 and TS5. The source support is indirect: TS5 demonstrates sealed halide/oxide high-temperature growth, but not Ca–Ti–Os specifically.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux-assisted synthesis should improve precursor activity and diffusion relative to dry ceramic routes; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from flux/liquid transport and the sealed halide/oxide crystal-growth example in TS5. Slow cooling may help crystallize the low-symmetry P1 target rather than trapping amorphous or multiphase products. Uncertainty remains high because none of CS/SS/TS directly reports an osmium titanate flux synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large A-site/B-site cation size contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk check; similar large size contrasts are tolerated in crystalline CaNbO₃ (TS4) and Ca₂NiWO₆ (CS3). The unit cell appears small from the P1 formula description, and face-sharing is not reported, so only one trigger is counted. SS3 shows mixed edge/corner-sharing Ti oxide networks can crystallize, reducing concern, but SS gives insufficient direct evidence for the exact Ca–Ti–Os topology, neutral 50 for target-specific amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaTi₂OsO₆ is likely a borderline stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible. The strongest support comes from combining TS1 reduced titanate vacuum chemistry with CS4 sealed-ampoule Os oxide chemistry; no single analogue fully matches Ca–Ti–Os–O. The most critical enabling factor is precise oxygen fugacity control, because Ti³⁺/Os⁴⁺-type chemistry is plausible only under low-pO₂ conditions supported by TS1 and constrained by Os safety in CS4. The most critical limiting factor is Os volatility/toxicity, especially possible OsO₄ formation under oxidizing conditions, explicitly noted in CS4. A flux-assisted sealed route is the best practical starting point because it may lower kinetic barriers, but this is partly inferential rather than directly proven by the references.
