### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0480 eV/atom, so it lies just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability band specified in the rubric [Target]. Thermodynamically similar materials include metastable or near-metastable phases around 0.038–0.074 eV/atom that are experimentally accessible by CVT or sealed-tube methods [TS-1–TS-5]. This supports the view that Pb₂SI₂ is likely experimentally reachable, but not necessarily a robust ground-state phase [Target, TS-2, TS-4]. The uncertainty is that small errors in computed energies could move it slightly above the 0.05 eV/atom threshold [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The condition severity is moderate because comparable Pb/Bi chalcogenide reactions use evacuated or Ar-filled sealed tubes and multi-day heating, but not high pressure or >1000 °C conditions [CS-3, TS-4]. Toxic Pb compounds and volatile iodide/sulfur species require sealed-tube handling [Reasonable Inference from TS-2/TS-4 halide transport]. Kinetically, the single-cation Pb system is favorable, and PbI₂-like layered analogues provide a strong structural template [SS-1–SS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether solid-state diffusion can order S and I rather than producing PbS + PbI₂ [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues in CS-1, CS-4, TS-4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT / halide transport

The CVT route has moderate severity because analogues use evacuated quartz tubes, temperature gradients, volatile halogen species, and controlled heating but no high pressure [CS-2, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4]. Kinetics are favorable because gas-phase halide transport reduces diffusion limitations, as shown for BiSeI and Pb–S/Pb–Se analogues [CS-2, TS-2, TS-4]. The route is chemically well matched because layered chalcohalides such as BiSeI and Bi₂TeI are made by vapor transport [CS-2, TS-3]. The major uncertainty is phase selectivity, since Bi₂TeI synthesis produced multiple small/twinned phases in small amounts [TS-3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: PbI₂-rich melt/flux

The melt/flux route has higher condition severity because it requires vacuum sealing, volatile halide control, slow cooling, and likely post-growth flux removal or annealing [SS-5, CS-1, CS-4]. Kinetically, a molten PbI₂-rich phase should improve mass transport relative to dry powders [Reasonable Inference from melt growth in SS-5]. Structurally, PbI₂ and TlPbI₃ analogues support the Pb–I octahedral framework [SS-1–SS-5]. The uncertainty is sulfide solubility and the possibility that PbS remains undissolved or crystallizes separately [Reasonable Inference; long I₂-assisted treatment needed for Pb–S analogues in TS-4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: gel/solution diffusion

The gel route has very mild condition severity because PbI₂ platelets grow at room temperature from lead acetate and KI in silica gel under ordinary room conditions [SS-1, SS-2, SS-4]. However, the kinetic score is low for the actual target because SS only demonstrates PbI₂ polytype growth, not mixed Pb–S–I ordering [SS-1–SS-4]. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for solution/gel synthesis of Pb₂SI₂-like chalcohalides, so those sources are neutral for this route. The main uncertainty is whether slow sulfide release can avoid immediate PbS formation while still allowing the ordered Pb₂SI₂ layer to nucleate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, Pb, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The formula and single-sheet description imply a small crystallographic basis rather than a >40-atom complex cell [Target]. The amorphization risk is raised by coexistence of face-, edge-, and corner-sharing octahedra, which can create multiple local bonding motifs and kinetic traps [Target]. Similar layered iodide/chalcohalide analogues often show polytypism, twinning, or phase-control issues, supporting a medium rather than low structural-risk assessment [SS-1–SS-4, TS-3].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pb₂SI₂ is best viewed as a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a clearly stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0480 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CVT and sealed-tube analogues for layered chalcohalides and Pb–S/Pb–I systems [CS-2, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, SS-1–SS-5]. The most important enabling factor is the structural compatibility with PbI₂-like layered octahedral frameworks, especially the close Pb–I bond lengths and T-shaped iodide coordination [SS-1–SS-5, Target]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition between PbS, PbI₂, and possible iodine-rich or sulfur-rich byproducts [Reasonable Inference; phase-control problems are reported in CS-1, CS-4, TS-3, TS-4]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful halogen chemical-potential control and temperature-gradient growth rather than a simple one-shot powder reaction [CS-2, TS-2–TS-4].
