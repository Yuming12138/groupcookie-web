### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. CS analogues include stable or near-stable Cu–selenides such as Cu₂MnSnSe₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1), Cu₂ZnSnSe₄ near 0.005–0.009 eV/atom (CS-4, CS-5), and Cu₂CoSnSe₄ at 0.055 eV/atom (CS-2). SS and TS analogues also support low-hull Cu/chalcogen/halide chemistry, especially CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom (SS-5, TS-1) and CuSe₂Br at 0.0 eV/atom (SS-2), though SS-2 shows that thermodynamic score alone does not eliminate kinetic metastability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state / reactive halide annealing

The route is feasible because evacuated ampoule solid-state synthesis is common across chemically and thermodynamically similar Cu–chalcogenides (CS-2, CS-3, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5), and CuSe₂Br is directly made from CuBr + Se at 550 °C (SS-2). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, volatile halogen/chalcogen reagents, multistep annealing, and Br₂/Se handling risks, consistent with SS-2 and TS-3. Kinetically, binary halide/chalcogenide precursors should help, but long annealing in TS-4/TS-5 and phase sensitivity in SS-2 imply slow ordering and competing phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal / acidic HBr sealed-ampoule synthesis

This is the best-supported route because CuHgSBr, a Cu–S–Br analogue, forms quantitatively in concentrated HBr/HX at 670 K in sealed ampoules with controlled cooling (SS-5, TS-1). The conditions are moderate in temperature and avoid high vacuum, but the route still requires sealed corrosive HBr and careful autoclave practice (SS-5/TS-1). Kinetically, mineralizer-assisted transport should be superior to dry diffusion, but the target’s additional Se–Br and S/Se ordering requirements remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Low-temperature solvothermal bromination / crystal growth

This route is attractive for forming Se–Br bonds because Pd(SeBr₃)₂ is synthesized using Se + Br₂ solvothermally at 180 °C under N₂ (TS-3), and Cu–selenide particles can form solvothermally at 250 °C (CS-4). Its severity is penalized by Br₂, toxic solvent, inert handling, sealed ampoules, and post-reaction separation (TS-3). Kinetically it should be fast at the local bonding level, but TS-3 explicitly reports multiphase mixtures, making phase-pure Cu₃SeSBr₅ uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Chemical vapor transport / halogen-assisted transport growth

CVT is plausible because Cu₂MnSnSe₄ single crystals are grown by I₂ transport in evacuated silica tubes (CS-1), and I₂ accelerates related Cu–selenide solid-state syntheses (CS-3, TS-2). However, the route is downgraded because target stoichiometry contains volatile Br/S/Se, and the closest Cu–Se–Br analogue has a narrow cooling/decomposition window (SS-2). CVT is therefore better as a crystal-growth optimization after identifying a stable phase field rather than as the first discovery synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only Cu as the cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide no contrary evidence. The formula unit contains only 10 atoms, and the provided structure description does not prove a >40-atom cell, so C2 is treated as not triggered. The structure has edge-sharing CuSBr₃ motifs, but no explicit coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing in the target; SS-5/TS-1 show mixed connectivity can exist in related Cu–S–Br compounds, but evidence for amorphization risk is insufficient.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃SeSBr₅ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, just inside the high-stability threshold. The CS data strongly support general Cu–chalcogenide synthesis in evacuated ampoules, while SS/TS data provide more directly relevant Cu–Se–Br and Cu–S–Br chemistry, especially CuSe₂Br (SS-2) and CuHgSBr (SS-5, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high halide/chalcogen mobility under sealed HBr hydrothermal or halogen-rich ampoule conditions, as demonstrated by CuHgSBr hydrothermal crystallization (SS-5/TS-1) and CuSe₂Br formation from CuBr/Se (SS-2). The main limiting factor is Br/S/Se fugacity control: CuSe₂Br has cooling-rate sensitivity and decomposition behavior (SS-2), while bromoselenide solvothermal chemistry can yield multiphase products (TS-3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful bromine chemical-potential control and phase screening.
