### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the upper boundary of the most favorable score category. CS analogues include La₂MgTiO₆ at 0.008 eV/atom and La₂ZnTiO₆ at 0.043 eV/atom, both synthesized by ceramic routes [CS1, CS2]. TS analogues with similar E_hull values, including La₂ZnTiO₆ at 0.043 eV/atom and Gd₂ZnTiO₆ at 0.051 eV/atom, were experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS2]. The uncertainty is that La₂MgZrO₆ may compete with stable La₂Zr₂O₇ plus MgO because La–Zr oxide stability is strongly supported [SS3].

#### F2 — Route 1: solid-state route

Route 1 is penalized because La₂MgTiO₆ required 1450 °C and multiple 2–7 day heatings [CS1, SS1]. Similar perovskite-derived ceramics also used high temperatures around 1473–1773 K [CS2, SS2, TS1]. It avoids high pressure and reactive atmospheres, which improves feasibility [CS1–CS3]. The main uncertainty is whether Zr⁴⁺ substitution requires still higher temperature or longer dwell than Ti⁴⁺ analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: solid-state route

The kinetics are only moderate because oxide/carbonate reactions require long-range cation diffusion, and the Zr source is expected to be refractory [Reasonable Inference]. La₂MgTiO₆ offers strong structural transferability, with MgO₆ and TiO₆ corner-sharing octahedra analogous to target MgO₆/ZrO₆ [CS1, SS1]. However, repeated heating over days in CS1 indicates slow ordering and phase equilibration. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Mg–Zr ordering kinetics, so TS is neutral for that subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 2: sol-gel route

The sol-gel route avoids the highest ceramic temperatures because La₂Zr₂O₇ was formed by solution mixing and calcination at 1073 K [SS3]. It is multi-step, involving dissolution, pH adjustment, gelation, burn-out, and calcination [SS3]. It does not require high pressure, vacuum, or reducing conditions [SS3]. The uncertainty is whether the double perovskite La₂MgZrO₆ crystallizes at 1000–1200 °C or still requires higher-temperature ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: sol-gel route

Kinetics are favorable because soluble nitrates and citrate complexation provide molecular-scale cation mixing [SS3]. This is particularly important for Zr-containing oxides, where direct ZrO₂ diffusion can be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction from amorphous gel to perovskite is nontrivial, but SS3 shows that single-phase La–Zr oxide can be obtained by this approach. Insufficient direct CS evidence for sol-gel La₂MgZrO₆, so CS is supportive only by composition/structure, not by identical method.

#### F2 — Route 3: nitrate decomposition route

The nitrate decomposition route is supported by La₂MgRhO₆ synthesis using La and Mg nitrates, nitrate decomposition at ~500 °C, and subsequent calcination at 800–1100 °C [CS4]. It is less severe than the 1450 °C solid-state route [CS1] but still multi-step [CS4]. Oxygen flux was used in CS4, but the target has no redox-active cation, so air should be adequate [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether zirconyl nitrate decomposition gives sufficiently homogeneous Zr distribution without chelation.

#### F3 — Route 3: nitrate decomposition route

Precursor activity is high because nitrate decomposition creates fine mixed oxides [CS4]. Structural transferability is good because La₂MgRhO₆ is a La₂MgB O₆ perovskite-derived phase [CS4], although Rh chemistry is not identical to Zr chemistry. Zr may hydrolyze or form refractory ZrO₂-like domains during nitrate decomposition [Reasonable Inference based on SS3 use of citrate to manage Zr solution chemistry]. Thus kinetics are good but slightly worse than the citrate route.

#### F2 — Route 4: mechanochemical-assisted ceramic route

Mechanochemical assistance is inferred from wet milling and ceramic processing used for La₂ZnTiO₆ [CS2, SS2]. It still requires high-temperature sintering above 1000 °C, but the goal is to avoid the >1400 °C regime required in CS1 [Reasonable Inference]. It uses standard milling, drying, pressing, and firing steps, so the severity is moderate. Insufficient TS evidence specifically for mechanochemical activation, so TS is neutral for this route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 4: mechanochemical-assisted ceramic route

Milling improves precursor contact and defect density [Reasonable Inference], which should help the Zr diffusion problem. However, the route still begins from oxide/carbonate solids, so it is kinetically less favorable than nitrate/citrate methods [CS2 versus SS3]. Structural reconstruction should be manageable because the target matches the perovskite-derived framework of La₂MgTiO₆ [CS1, SS1]. The uncertainty is contamination or incomplete ordering after milling.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one major amorphization trigger: large A-site/B-site cation size contrast, which is inherent to La-based perovskites [Reasonable Inference] and reflected in long La–O versus shorter Mg–O/Zr–O distances in the target and analogues [CS1, SS1]. The target network is mostly corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face-sharing frameworks [CS1, SS1]. Unit-cell atom count above 40 is not established from the provided target description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, so this check is treated neutrally. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La₂MgZrO₆ is likely a **borderline metastable but accessible** phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable metastability cutoff. The CS and SS datasets strongly support the double-perovskite-derived framework: La₂MgTiO₆ is extremely close chemically and structurally, and La₂ZnTiO₆/Gd₂ZnTiO₆ show that related A₂BB′O₆ perovskites are routinely accessible [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust perovskite-derived corner-sharing octahedral topology, which is repeatedly synthesized in close analogues [CS1, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is Zr-related kinetic/phase competition, especially formation of La₂Zr₂O₇ or residual ZrO₂, because stable La–Zr oxides are documented [SS3]. The best practical route is therefore a citrate/Pechini route that maximizes La/Mg/Zr homogeneity before crystallization [SS3; Reasonable Inference].
