### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable metastability threshold and supports a high thermodynamic score [target data]. CS gives strong support because K–U perovskite-derived analogues are synthesized and lie on or near the hull, including K4CaU3O12 and K4SrU3O12 at E_hull = 0.0 and K4BaU3O12 at E_hull = 0.0357 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). TS also supports synthesizability because several thermodynamically similar oxides with E_hull around 0.054–0.069 eV/atom were experimentally synthesized (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that no exact K–U–V phase is reported in the reference set, so the target may be metastable rather than a robust ground state [insufficient exact-composition evidence from CS/SS/TS; exact-composition source score neutral 50].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because CS K–U analogues use simple air firing at 850 °C, Pt boats, and standard grinding rather than high pressure or vacuum (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The main penalties are multistep firing/regrinding, toxic uranium/vanadium handling, and likely K/V volatility [CS-1/2/5; CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide a better direct K–U–V ceramic condition, but SS/TS show that U-containing double perovskites sometimes need atmosphere control, which is a caution rather than a requirement for Route 1 (SS-1; TS-3).

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics of Route 1 are moderate because CS K–U analogues form by conventional solid-state synthesis at 850 °C but require 30 h and intermittent grinding (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). U3O8 is less reactive than soluble U salts or gel precursors, so precursor activity is not high [Reasonable Inference; U3O8 used in CS-1/2/5]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target and CS analogues are perovskite-derived, but U/V B-site ordering and phase competition may slow crystallization [target description; CS-1/2/5; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because controlled atmosphere, vacuum/O2-buffering, or sealing is introduced; such approaches are reported in SS/TS for similar oxide families (SS-2/TS-1; SS-5; TS-5). The temperature is still moderate, generally below 900 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied [CS-1/2/5; TS-5]. The route is penalized for sealed volatile chemistry and multistep processing, but not for high pressure because the references use sealed ampoules rather than GPa pressure (SS-2/TS-1; TS-5).

#### F3 — Route 2

The sealed/controlled route can improve kinetics by retaining volatile K/V species and potentially using V2O5/K–V oxide as a reactive transport medium [Reasonable Inference; TS-5 shows sealed Li–V oxide synthesis at 680 °C]. However, if the ampoule is sealed before full homogenization, the inability to regrind during reaction can hinder phase purity, which is why SS-2/TS-1 required repeated annealing and regrinding before sealing or between cycles. The structural pathway remains plausible because perovskite-derived and related oxides are accessible by bulk annealing in CS/SS/TS, but oxygen-potential tuning may be needed [CS-1/2/5; SS-1/TS-3; SS-5].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C conditions and high pressure, so its condition severity is moderate rather than challenging [Reasonable Inference anchored to CS-1/2/5 annealing temperatures]. The penalties come from multistep gel preparation/burnout/anneal, toxic U/V soluble precursors, and likely alkali/vanadium volatility during calcination [Reasonable Inference; K excess concern supported by CS-4]. Direct CS/SS/TS evidence for sol-gel synthesis is absent, so CS, SS, and TS are neutral 50 for direct route evidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Sol-gel/Pechini processing gives the best kinetic score because nitrate/acetate/citrate precursors can achieve molecular-scale K/U/V mixing before crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. U acetate precedent exists in U double-perovskite solid-state synthesis, but not as a sol-gel route (SS-1; TS-3). The uncertainty is that amorphous-to-perovskite crystallization may form potassium vanadates or uranium oxides first, and no CS/SS/TS entry directly validates this pathway for K–U–V oxides [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; source score neutral 50].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one clear amorphization risk: the large size/coordination contrast between K in KO12 cuboctahedra and U/V in octahedra [target description; Reasonable Inference]. The structure description does not indicate a >40 atom cell and does not report simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered [target description]. Perovskite-derived frameworks in CS and SS crystallize readily under ceramic conditions, which reduces concern about glass formation (CS-1/2/5; SS-1/3), but K/V-rich intermediates could still form amorphous or fluxy phases [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2UVO6 is likely a mildly metastable or threshold-stable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [target data]. The CS analogues provide strong support for synthesis because K–U perovskite-derived oxides form in air at 850 °C using simple oxide/carbonate ceramic routes (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The SS/TS analogues add confidence that U-containing and thermodynamically similar oxides can be made, although they also warn that oxygen potential and repeated annealing may matter (SS-1; TS-3; SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural and chemical match to known K–U perovskite-derived oxides [CS-1/2/5]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of K/V volatility and U/V oxidation state, for which the references provide only indirect guidance [CS-4; SS-1/TS-3; SS-5; Reasonable Inference].
