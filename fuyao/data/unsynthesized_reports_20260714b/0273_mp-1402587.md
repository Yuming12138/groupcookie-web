### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for a high F1 score. TS gives the strongest support: TS-1 has E_hull ≈0.0549 eV/atom and was synthesized by mild precipitation, while TS-2/TS-3/TS-5 show similar low-hull oxyhalide/selenium oxide phases can be made. SS also supports near-hull Se–O–Cl phases through SS-1 and SS-2. CS is less helpful because some chemically similar Cl/O phases are far above hull, so CS is treated as mixed rather than strongly supportive.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because CS-2/CS-4 and SS-3 support mild crystallization, but the target requires dry chlorinating conditions, volatile reagents, and toxic selenium/chloride chemistry. Kinetically it is favorable because molecular/solution precursors avoid solid-state diffusion, consistent with solution crystallization in CS-2 and SS-3. The uncertainty is phase stability: CS-1 notes instability and poor crystal growth for a related Cl/O compound, implying that careful low-temperature crystallization may be necessary.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more operationally demanding because SS-1/SS-2/TS-5 use sealed tubes, evacuated or HCl/Ar atmospheres, and long dwell times. Kinetically it is plausible because CVT transports volatile selenium oxide/chloride species efficiently, as shown by Se–O–Cl analogues in SS-1/SS-2/TS-5. The main uncertainty is whether the metal-free target can crystallize without Cu or another cation stabilizer; SS-1/SS-2 analogues are structurally relevant but not chemically identical.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 scores better on generic severity because TS-2/TS-3/TS-4 demonstrate hydrothermal oxyhalide synthesis at 200–280 °C without extreme pressure. Kinetics are good because solution/autoclave chemistry promotes dissolution–recrystallization, and TS-3 reports high-yield pure oxyhalide products. The main limitation is hydrolysis: no CS/SS/TS source directly confirms survival of terminal Se–Cl bonds in aqueous hydrothermal media, so a low-water strongly acidic variant is required by reasonable inference.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation type, Se, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The target formula and high-symmetry tetragonal I4/mmm description suggest a relatively simple periodic structure, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a >40-atom target cell. The described network has bridging oxygen and terminal chlorine but no explicit mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral topology, so the amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the high-synthesizability boundary. TS provides the strongest thermodynamic support, while SS provides the most relevant structural/chemical support through Se–O–Cl layered phases made by sealed-tube/CVT routes. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature molecular, solution, or vapor transport pathways that bypass slow solid-state diffusion, as supported by CS-2, SS-1/SS-2, SS-3, and TS-3. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require dry, chloride-rich, carefully sealed or strongly acidic conditions.
