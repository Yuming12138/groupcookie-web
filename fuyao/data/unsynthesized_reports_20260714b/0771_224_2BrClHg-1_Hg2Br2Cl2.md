### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category by the provided rubric. Structurally/thermodynamically related Hg halide-containing materials also include several E_hull = 0 or near-zero examples, such as HgBrI at 0.0105 eV/atom (CS2, SS1), Tl₂Hg₂PdCl₈ at 0 eV/atom (SS4, TS1), and Hg₃S₂I₂ / Hg₃Se₂I₂ at 0 eV/atom (CS5, TS2, TS5). The main uncertainty is that some chemically similar Hg–N–halide phases have high hull energies and handling instability (CS1, CS3), but those are less structurally direct than HgBrI (SS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on the exact experimental stability of HgClBr itself; the score relies primarily on the target hull value and the close HgBrI analogue.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses a direct solid-state halogen-exchange concept supported by HgBrI formation from iodine and mercurous halides (CS2, SS1). The severity penalty arises from sealed/vacuum ampoule practice common in related Hg-halide synthesis (CS4, CS5, SS2–SS4, TS1–TS5), plus volatile halogen handling [Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures are expected to be low-to-moderate, because related Hg halide systems form at or below 260–280 °C in several ampoule examples (CS5, TS1, TS2, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is the actual Br₂/Cl₂ activity required to form HgClBr without over-oxidation or separate HgX₂ phases.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

HgBrI formation by direct solid-state reaction demonstrates that molecular mixed mercury halides can form without extreme diffusion requirements (CS2, SS1). The target has only Hg as cation and forms zero-dimensional linear units, so structural reconstruction should be limited relative to extended frameworks [Reasonable Inference based on target and SS1]. Kinetics are therefore favorable, but solid halogen exchange may suffer from local stoichiometry gradients and incomplete conversion [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS on this exact exchange chemistry; TS contributes mainly general support that Hg halide-containing phases form at moderate temperature.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more operationally demanding because it likely requires drying, evacuated ampoule sealing, and a temperature gradient, as used in CVT/sublimation syntheses of Hg halide analogues (SS2, SS3, TS1, TS4). The temperature remains moderate compared with refractory ceramics, because Hg halide/chalcogenide ampoule reactions occur at 260–360 °C in several references (CS5, TS2, TS4, TS5). The volatility and toxicity of HgCl₂/HgBr₂ drive the main deductions [Reasonable Inference supported by repeated use of sealed ampoules in SS2–SS4 and TS1–TS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on a direct HgCl₂ + HgBr₂ vapor route to HgClBr, so the route is plausible but not confirmed.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport should have favorable mass transport because it avoids solid-state ion diffusion, and many Hg halide-containing crystals are grown from gas phase in evacuated ampoules (SS2, SS3, TS4). HgCl₂/HgBr₂ are chemically direct precursors, making precursor activity high [Reasonable Inference]. However, the same volatility that aids transport may also allow separate crystallization of HgCl₂ and HgBr₂ rather than ordered HgClBr [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide strong evidence for transportability of related compounds but insufficient direct evidence for the target heterohalide ordering.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 scores highest on condition severity because the relevant solution precedents use mild aqueous or methanolic conditions: Hg–Br precipitation at 18–80 °C (CS1), Hg–Cl precipitation under ice-water cooling (CS3), and slow evaporation at 293 K for a structurally similar Hg chloride molecular material (SS5). No vacuum, high pressure, or high temperature is required by those solution examples. The only major deduction is for toxic mercury salts, which are inherent to all routes and are present in the reference chemistry (CS1, CS3, SS5). Insufficient evidence from TS on solution routes; TS is neutral for this factor except for showing related phases are thermodynamically accessible.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization gives excellent precursor mixing and avoids solid-state diffusion, supported by rapid precipitation or week-scale crystallization in CS1, CS3, and SS5. Because the target is a small zero-dimensional molecular solid, assembly from solvated Hg–Cl/Br species should be kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic/phase uncertainty is solution speciation: excess halide may stabilize complex halomercurate ions or adduct salts rather than neutral HgClBr [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS support mild Hg-halide crystallization, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms neutral HgClBr as the solution product.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, a very small zero-dimensional formula unit, and no extended mixed polyhedral network, so none of the three amorphization triggers apply. The closest structural analogue HgBrI is also zero-dimensional with linear single-bonded halides (SS1), supporting low structural frustration. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphous byproducts for HgClBr, so their source-specific amorphization signal is neutral 50. Physicochemically, molecular Hg halides are more likely to crystallize or sublime than form persistent amorphous networks [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgClBr is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from HgBrI, which is chemically and structurally close: it is a zero-dimensional molecular mixed mercury halide with linear Hg–Br/I units and is reported synthetically accessible (CS2, SS1). Additional Hg halide-containing references show that mercury halide systems can be made by mild precipitation, slow evaporation, sealed ampoule reaction, and vapor transport (CS1, CS3, SS2–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the molecular, low-dimensional Hg–halide bonding, which should reduce diffusion and reconstruction barriers compared with extended frameworks (target; SS1). The most critical limiting factor is mixed-halide phase selectivity: HgCl₂, HgBr₂, mercurous halides, and halomercurate complexes are plausible competing products [Reasonable Inference], and no source directly reports the exact HgClBr synthesis.
