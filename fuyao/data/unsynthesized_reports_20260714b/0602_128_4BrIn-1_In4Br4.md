### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target lies at **0.0500 eV/atom above hull**, which falls at the top of the prompt’s 90–100 scoring bin and is close to experimentally realized thermodynamic analogues such as CrInBr₃ at 0.0499958 eV/atom (TS-2). Similar or less stable compounds in TS are reported as synthesized, including AgBrPbO at 0.0626 eV/atom (TS-1), Tl₂CS₃ at 0.0749 eV/atom (TS-3), and SnSe at 0.0876 eV/atom (TS-4). CS also shows a 1D InSe polymorph at 0.0367 eV/atom obtained by high-pressure treatment (CS-3), indicating that low-dimensional metastable In-based phases can be captured. Remaining uncertainty is whether the Pmm2 InBr polymorph is the equilibrium ambient-pressure polymorph or a kinetically stabilized variant; CS/SS/TS do not directly report this exact binary InBr phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high external pressure, consistent with halide ampoule syntheses at 250–450 °C in SS-5 and SS-1/TS-2. The score is reduced because the route requires a sealed evacuated ampoule, volatile Br₂, staged heating, and inert handling; vacuum ampoules are common in CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-5, and TS-2. The main uncertainty is pressure management from Br₂ vapor, which is inferred from halogen chemistry rather than explicitly reported for InBr in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental routes are kinetically feasible because CS-1/CS-5 and SS-5 show crystalline compounds can be made from elements in sealed ampoules. Diffusion should be relatively favorable because indium melts at low temperature and bromine is highly mobile/volatile [Reasonable Inference], while analogous halides form at moderate temperatures in SS-1/TS-2. The structural reconstruction score is only moderate because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates binary Pmm2 InBr ribbons; the closest support is 1D In-containing bromide ribbons in SS-1 and TS-2.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 also avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, and its 450–475 °C window is directly supported by SS-1, SS-3, and TS-2. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, air/moisture sensitivity of InBr₃-based bromides, use of volatile halide species, and multistep loading/heating/slow-cooling operations; these issues are explicitly reflected in SS-1 and TS-2. The severity is moderate rather than severe because no DAC, autoclave, plasma, or UHV equipment is required, unlike CS-3 and CS-4/SS-2.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The molten halide route has the best kinetic score because InBr₃ melt/flux chemistry provides high precursor activity and enhanced mass transport, as shown in SS-1, SS-3, and TS-2. Structurally similar 1D In-containing bromide ribbons are produced under nearly identical thermal conditions in SS-1 and TS-2, giving a strong transferability signal. Remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: ternary InMBr₃ analogues are stabilized by transition-metal octahedra, whereas the target is a binary InBr ribbon [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 operates at low substrate temperature and avoids high pressure, consistent with InCl deposition in CS-4/SS-2 and low-temperature halide evaporation in SS-4. However, the UHV/high-vacuum environment, effusion source, low-temperature substrate, and thermal cycling reduce the score substantially; CS-4/SS-2 explicitly describe effusion-cell/UHV deposition and narrow thermal phase behavior. The uncertainty is whether InBr has a usable vapor pressure and congruent evaporation window comparable to InCl, which is a reasonable but unproven extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor availability is plausible because InCl can be evaporated from powder under UHV (CS-4, SS-2), and simple halide salts can be vacuum-deposited (SS-4). The crystallization pathway is weak because CS-4/SS-2 indicate metastable InCl phases depend strongly on low-temperature deposition and thermal cycling, while SS-4 shows halide polymorphs can be temperature-sensitive. Composition control is moderate because the target is binary, but halide volatility and possible disproportionation/desorption create risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary InBr phase with one cation species and L-shaped 1D ribbon coordination, so it does not trigger the cation-radius mismatch check or mixed-polyhedral-connectivity check [Target description]. The structural description lists only two inequivalent In sites and one Br site, giving no evidence for a >40 atom unit cell [Target description]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization tendency of this exact InBr polymorph, so the low-risk score is based primarily on the target’s structural simplicity and on the fact that analogous crystalline halides are obtained in SS-1/TS-2 and CS-4/SS-2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a **borderline stable or mildly metastable phase**, because its energy above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold given in the prompt. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: CS supports indium low-dimensional compounds in ampoules, SS and TS strongly support 1D In-containing bromide ribbons from InBr₃ melts, and CS/SS support vapor access to indium halide polymorphs. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of **molten InBr₃ / halide flux chemistry**, which should enhance transport and crystallization at only ~450 °C, as shown by SS-1, SS-3, and TS-2. The main limiting factor is **phase selectivity and moisture/halide volatility control**, because bromide analogues require evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, and protective atmospheres (SS-1, TS-2). Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with Route 2 providing the best balance of thermodynamic access, kinetic mobility, and structural analogy.
