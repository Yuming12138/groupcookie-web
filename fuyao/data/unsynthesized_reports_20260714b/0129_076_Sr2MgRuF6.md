### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target], exactly at the rubric boundary for high thermodynamic accessibility. Similar fluorides in CS/SS/TS are often on or near the hull, e.g., K₂NaInF₆ is 0.0 eV/atom [CS1], Rb₂NaTiF₆ is 0.0 eV/atom [SS5], and K₂NaYF₆ is 0.032 eV/atom [TS5], supporting fluoride perovskite-derived stability. The remaining uncertainty is that 0.050 eV/atom may indicate a metastable polymorph rather than a true ground state [Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed solid-state.**  
Condition severity is favorable because analogous binary-fluoride routes use only 400–700 °C in Ni/Ag bombs under Ar [CS1, TS5] or 550–600 °C in closed Ag ampoules [SS1, SS5]. Kinetics are less favorable because binary fluorides and Ru-containing solids are expected to diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference], and analogous SS routes require long 10 d anneals [SS1, SS5]. Phase purity is uncertain because CS1 and SS1/SS5 mention difficulties or sensitivity to leaking/moisture for related fluoride products [CS1, SS1, SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal severity is moderate: temperatures are low, 180–350 °C, but HF/NH₄HF₂ and sealed pressure vessels introduce toxicity and containment penalties [CS3, CS4, TS2]. Kinetics score highest because HF-containing fluids enhance dissolution–reprecipitation and crystal growth, as seen for Ba₂TiOF₆, Rb₂NaAlF₆, and Na₃VF₆ [CS3, CS4, TS2]. The main uncertainty is selectivity: CS3 explicitly reports “very few crystals” and a competing BaTiF₆ phase, so Sr₂MgRuF₆ may also suffer competing fluoride/oxyfluoride formation [CS3].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, F₂ fluorination.**  
This route is penalized by reactive F₂, high-pressure fluorination, and multistep pre-fluorination/annealing, all directly reported for Rh/Pt fluoride analogues [CS2, CS5]. Kinetically, F₂ is highly effective for fluorination and can form complex fluorides from oxides/chlorides/metals at 390–500 °C [CS2, CS5, SS4]. The uncertainty is chemical transferability: the fluorination examples are Rh/Pt/Ce-rich systems, not Sr–Mg–Ru, so over-fluorination or incompatible Ru valence is possible [CS2, CS5, SS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains large Sr and small Mg/Ru coordination environments, reflected in a wide Sr–F range of 2.39–2.76 Å versus Mg–F 1.98–2.13 Å and Ru–F 2.06 Å [Target], so cation size mismatch is a real disorder/amorphization risk. However, related A₂BB′F₆ fluorides tolerate substantial cation-size contrast in ordered fluoride frameworks [CS1, SS1, SS5, TS5]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms or that it has simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so those checks are neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgRuF₆ is likely **borderline metastable** rather than a robust ground-state compound because its E above hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is reasonably strong for anhydrous fluoride framework formation: many CS/SS/TS A₂BB′F₆-type fluorides are made by sealed solid-state or hydrothermal methods [CS1, SS1, SS5, TS5, CS3, CS4, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of fluoride-rich, sealed environments that stabilize metal–fluoride frameworks and suppress hydrolysis/oxide formation [CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is Ru chemistry: the target’s Ru coordination/valence is unusual relative to the cited cubic Rh/Ti/In/Y analogues, so competing Ru fluorides, oxides, or metallic Ru may form [Target; CS2, SS1, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, hydrothermal HF/NH₄HF₂ synthesis is the best first attempt because it improves kinetic access at lower temperature, but sealed solid-state annealing should be used as a follow-up for phase equilibration [CS3, CS4, TS2, CS1, SS1].
