### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase rather than merely metastable [Target]. This is reinforced by chemically related Te–Br compounds that are also on the hull, including Pd–Te–Br and Re–Te–Br [CS1, CS4], and by structurally/thermodynamically related layered tellurides such as ZrGeTe₄ and HfGeTe₄ [SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3]. The uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee an easily accessible kinetic pathway, especially when Br₂ volatility and Zr diffusion are involved [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because related ampoule syntheses occur at 480–700 °C [CS1, CS4, SS2/TS1]. The main penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile/toxic Br₂ or ZrBr₄, which are directly indicated by Re–Te–Br and NbSeBr₃ bromine syntheses [CS4, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target decomposes or converts to ZrTe₃/ZrBr₄-rich products at the selected Br activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Zr has sluggish solid-state diffusion, and ZrTe₃ required 973 K for 3 days even without Br ordering [SS1]. However, the presence of Br₂/Te vapor can enhance transport, as bromide chalcogenides form at lower temperatures in sealed tubes [CS4, TS5]. The structural similarity to ZrTe₃ supports crystallization of a layered Zr–Te framework, but inserting Br into a specific C2/m sheet adds ordering difficulty [SS1, Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Flux growth uses standard sealed ampoules/furnaces rather than high pressure, as shown for ZrGeTe₄ and HfGeTe₄ [SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3]. It is penalized for multi-step handling: flux drying, inert loading, sealed heating, slow cooling, and flux removal are all reported in related flux syntheses [CS2, CS3, SS3/TS2]. Bromide source volatility and air sensitivity are inferred because the target needs Br and known bromide syntheses use Br₂ or bromides under sealed conditions [CS4, TS5, Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux growth is kinetically favorable for refractory-metal tellurides because ZrGeTe₄ and HfGeTe₄ form from elemental Zr/Hf in KCl flux at 973 K over 72 h [SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3]. The liquid/flux medium should improve mass transport and crystallinity relative to dry powders [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2]. The main uncertainty is bromine incorporation: the closest flux analogues are Zr/Hf–Ge–Te, not Zr–Te–Br, so Br activity must be tuned experimentally [insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The CVT/vapor-assisted route operates at moderate temperatures, comparable to the 500–460 °C gradient used for NbSeBr₃ and the 550 °C Re–Te–Br synthesis [TS5, CS4]. It requires vacuum-sealed tubes and Br₂, so penalties for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed precursor, and toxic/corrosive reagent apply [TS5, CS4]. It is less multi-step than flux growth because no flux removal is needed [TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Bromine vapor transport can improve reaction kinetics by moving material through volatile halide species, as demonstrated for bromide chalcogenide crystal growth [TS5]. This should help overcome Zr’s low solid-state mobility, but Zr may also form competing volatile or stable bromides [Reasonable Inference]. Because no direct Zr–Te–Br CVT analogue is supplied, nucleation of the desired 2D C2/m phase remains the central kinetic uncertainty [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so there is no multi-cation radius mismatch trigger [Target]. The described structure is a relatively simple 2D layered C2/m phase rather than a large, topologically frustrated framework, and the target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence [Target]. Related layered tellurides crystallize under ampoule or flux conditions, supporting low amorphization risk [SS1, SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3], but the exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, so that part retains minor uncertainty [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(TeBr)₂ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis outlook is favorable because chemically related Te–Br compounds form in sealed ampoules [CS1, CS4], structurally related Zr/Hf telluride sheets grow by solid-state or flux routes [SS1, SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3], and thermodynamic analogues include successful halogen chalcogenide CVT/ampoule syntheses [TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the strong structural match to layered ZrTe₃/ZrGeTe₄-type high-coordinate Zr–Te frameworks [SS1, SS3/TS2]. The most important limiting factor is bromine chemical-potential control, because Br₂/ZrBr₄ are volatile/corrosive and halogen-rich systems commonly show competing phases or narrow stoichiometry windows [CS4, TS5, SS2/TS1]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with best prospects via a carefully controlled sealed-tube vapor-assisted or CVT route.
