### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0360 eV/atom places it in the high-probability synthesizable range by the provided rubric. CS and TS analogues include several near-hull iodides: TlCdI₃ at 0.0167 eV/atom (CS1/TS5), AgTl₂I₃ at 0.0272 eV/atom (CS4/TS4), and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (TS1). SS analogues also include stable or nearly stable compounds, e.g., Ag₂HgSI₂ and Hg₃Se₂I₂ at E_hull = 0 (SS1/SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on the exact Ni–Ag–I phase diagram, so the score is high but not perfect.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state

The severity is moderate because analogous solid-state iodide reactions use evacuated sealed tubes and long heating, as in SS1, TS1, and TS3. CS5 shows that hygroscopic iodides may require dry glovebox handling and repeated annealing. Temperatures are expected to remain far below 1000 °C based on SS1/SS2/TS1/CS5, so high-temperature penalties do not apply. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact NiI₂ vapor pressure under the target conditions; volatile-iodide risk is assigned by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state

Binary iodides are more reactive than oxides and have successful precedent in CS5, SS1, TS1, and TS3. Diffusion is helped by soft iodide lattices and possible transient iodide mobility, as reactions occur at 120–573 K/°C-scale conditions in SS1/SS2/TS1/TS3. The main kinetic uncertainty is reconstructing from NiI₂ and AgI into a 2D ordered Ni/Ag framework; CS5 reports repeated annealing to obtain pure TlFeI₃, so structural ordering may be slow.

#### F2 — Route 2: hydrothermal HI

Hydrothermal HI avoids high temperatures and high pressure but requires sealed acidic conditions, as shown in CS3, CS4, and TS4. The route uses corrosive, volatile HI and toxic/air-sensitive metal iodides, giving moderate severity. TS2 shows hydrothermal halide synthesis may use sealed ampoules/autoclaves and controlled cooling. Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal synthesis of structurally close Ni–Ag iodide sheets; SS is therefore neutral for this route’s condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 2: hydrothermal HI

Hydrothermal transport gives high precursor activity because HI dissolves or transports iodide species, supported by AgTl₂I₃ in HI (CS4/TS4) and AgSe₃I in concentrated HI (CS3). Low-temperature solution growth minimizes bulk diffusion limitations. The limiting kinetic factor is phase selection: CS3 reports multiphase/intergrown hydrothermal products, and CS4/TS4 reports twinning/low yield of untwinned crystals. Insufficient evidence from SS on hydrothermal kinetics for close structural analogues, so SS is neutral here.

#### F2 — Route 3: melt/slow cooling

CS2 provides direct evidence for slow cooling of an iodide melt in sealed Pyrex under strict moisture exclusion, while CS1/TS5 supports Bridgman growth of TlCdI₃. CS5 supports melting/annealing binary iodide mixtures under Ar/dry conditions. The route is penalized for sealed volatile iodide conditions and air-sensitive precursors, not for extreme temperature. The exact melting behavior of NiAgI₃ is unknown from CS/SS/TS, so the upper synthesis temperature is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: melt/slow cooling

Melt growth has favorable kinetics because liquid iodide media provide rapid mass transport, consistent with CS2 and CS5. Slow cooling can directly produce crystals, as in CS2 and CS1/TS5. The structural reconstruction score is not perfect because incongruent melting is possible; CS5 explicitly notes incongruent melting for TlMnI₃ while still enabling Bridgman growth. Insufficient evidence from SS for melt growth of structurally close layered Ni–Ag iodides, so SS is supportive only indirectly.

#### F2 — Route 4: iodine-assisted CVT

SS4 and SS5 show that iodide crystals can be grown in sealed ampoules with I₂, Ar/vacuum, and temperature gradients. This route carries higher severity because it requires volatile iodine, vacuum/Ar ampoule handling, and gradient control. Temperatures remain moderate, so no high-temperature penalties apply. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for CVT growth of Ni–Ag iodides, so their route-specific condition signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4: iodine-assisted CVT

I₂ transport can generate mobile metal iodide species, supported by SS4/SS5, so precursor transport and diffusion are favorable. However, SS4/SS5 analogues are cluster/molecular iodides rather than layered NiAgI₃, so structural transferability is weaker. Composition control may be narrow because volatile iodine can shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS for this CVT pathway, so confidence is lower than Routes 2–3.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has a significant Ni²⁺/Ag⁺ size and coordination mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk check by [Reasonable Inference] from ionic radii and the target structure. The target description indicates corner-sharing and edge-sharing but not face-sharing, so the full mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing criterion is not triggered. The simple formula and trigonal P-31m description suggest a unit cell likely below 40 atoms, but CS/SS/TS do not give the target cell size; this is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and scored conservatively. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NiAgI₃ is likely a mildly metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.036 eV/atom, below the usual 0.05 eV/atom practical stability threshold. The CS and TS sets strongly support synthesis of related ternary iodides near the hull using sealed-tube, hydrothermal HI, and melt-growth routes, especially AgTl₂I₃ (CS4/TS4), TlCdI₃ (CS1/TS5), and TlFeI₃ (CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical softness of iodide systems, which allows crystallization at modest temperatures in HI solution, sealed ampoules, or melts (CS2/CS3/CS4/SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, because volatile iodine/HI conditions and AgI/NiI₂ reactivity mismatch can produce competing iodides or twinned/intergrown crystals, as seen in CS3 and CS4/TS4. Overall, the target should be synthetically accessible, with hydrothermal HI and slow-cooled melt routes being the most promising.
