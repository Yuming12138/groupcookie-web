### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.014 eV/atom [Target], which is well inside the commonly synthesizable metastability window and within the highest scoring bin. CS analogues include stable or nearly stable Cu chlorides with E_hull ≈ 0–0.0016 eV/atom [CS1–CS4], and TS analogues near the target show E_hull ≈ 0.008–0.031 eV/atom [TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. SS evidence is mixed because some structurally similar materials are high above hull [SS2, SS4, SS5], so SS gives insufficient consistent thermodynamic support and is treated as neutral-to-mixed rather than decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, comparable to 250–260 °C sealed ampoule routes for CuAlCl4/CuGaCl4 [CS1–CS4], so no high-temperature penalty applies. Vacuum or sealed dry ampoule handling is required, as in CS1–CS4, giving a controlled-atmosphere penalty. Volatile chloride retention is important because related chloride syntheses use sealed tubes [CS1–CS4], and CuCl2 volatility is a likely risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chlorides are reactive compared with refractory oxides, and CS analogues form at low temperature [CS1–CS4], supporting high precursor activity. The likely reaction path involves molten/volatile halide transport rather than slow diffusion through dense ceramics [Reasonable Inference from CS1–CS4]. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: CS analogues are tetrahedral Cu–Al/Ga chloride frameworks [CS1–CS4], while the target has Ag octahedra and Cu trigonal pyramids in sheets [Target].

#### F2 — Route 2

Temperature-gradient ampoule growth uses moderate temperatures similar to CS4 and SS1, so thermal severity is low. However, it requires vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile halide transport control [CS4, SS1, TS3]. It is also more operationally complex than a direct powder synthesis because prereaction, gradient growth, and crystal recovery are separate steps [CS4, SS1].

#### F3 — Route 2

Gradient growth can improve crystal quality because related chloride crystals deposit in colder zones over days [CS4] and phase-pure halides can be obtained under controlled gradients [SS1]. Kinetics are favorable for material transport but slow for ordering, as SS1 reports long growth times up to weeks. The route is therefore good for single crystals but less efficient for bulk powder [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal synthesis avoids high temperature and high external pressure, and TS2 shows Cu halides can crystallize hydrothermally in sealed ampoules/autoclaves. The penalties arise from sealed corrosive acid, multiple operational steps, and toxic/corrosive reagents [TS2, Reasonable Inference]. No CS source directly supports Ag–Cu hydrothermal chloride synthesis, so direct chemical evidence from CS is insufficient and neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution-mediated transport gives excellent ion mobility, and TS2 reports quantitative crystallization with no observed side products for Cu-containing halides. High chloride concentration should help dissolve Ag(I) and suppress Cu hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether the anhydrous AgCuCl3 sheet phase crystallizes instead of hydrated, basic, or simple AgCl/CuCl2 phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Slow evaporation has the mildest conditions and is supported by room-temperature crystallization of CuBr2 [TS4] and structurally similar halides [SS2, SS4, SS5]. It requires no furnace, vacuum, or high pressure. The remaining severity is chemical handling of CuCl2, HCl/brine, and Ag salts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution transport makes nucleation and growth kinetically easy, as shown by slow evaporation analogues [SS2, SS4, SS5, TS4]. However, direct evidence for an anhydrous Ag–Cu chloride from evaporation is absent in CS, so CS is insufficient and scored neutral for this route. The major kinetic/chemical uncertainty is keeping Ag in solution and maintaining the exact Ag:Cu:Cl ratio during evaporation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines a large Ag(I) chloride coordination environment with a smaller Cu-centered trigonal-pyramidal environment [Target], and the Ag/Cu size mismatch likely exceeds 15% [Reasonable Inference]. The structure also contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity between AgCl6 octahedra and CuCuCl3 trigonal pyramids [Target], which can increase defect or glassy disorder risk. The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms based on the simple AgCuCl3 formula and C2/m layered description [Target], limiting the amorphization penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCuCl3 is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.014 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS data: several chemically similar Cu chloride frameworks are made from binary chlorides in sealed ampoules at ~250 °C [CS1–CS4]. TS data also supports accessibility of low-hull Cu halide materials by sealed-tube, hydrothermal, or evaporation methods [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of chloride salts at moderate temperature in sealed systems [CS1–CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Ag/Cu/Cl stoichiometry and Cu(II) oxidation state while avoiding loss or segregation of volatile/hygroscopic chloride components [Reasonable Inference from sealed ampoule requirements in CS1–CS4].
