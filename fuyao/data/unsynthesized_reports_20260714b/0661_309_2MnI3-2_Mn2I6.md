### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.038 eV/atom, which lies below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold for high thermodynamic accessibility [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.017–0.031 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized in sealed or hydrothermal routes [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5], supporting that small positive hull energy does not preclude synthesis. The remaining uncertainty is that MnI3 is binary and redox-sensitive, whereas several TS analogues are ternary/quaternary compounds that may be chemically stabilized by additional cations [TS-3 vs Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, typically 250–350 °C by analogy to HfI4, W bromides, and TlMnI3 [CS-2, CS-3, TS-3], so no high-temperature penalty applies. It does require vacuum/sealed ampoule operation and volatile I2, as in TiI3 and HfI4 preparations [CS-1, CS-2]. Moisture/air sensitivity is expected because HfI4 and related iodide ampoule syntheses emphasize closed dry handling [CS-2, SS-4, TS-3].

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
MnI2 and I2 should react more readily than refractory oxide precursors, and iodine vapor transport can enhance mass transfer, as demonstrated for TiI3 and HfI4 [CS-1, CS-2]. The main kinetic uncertainty is reconstructing into a 2D MnI6 sheet rather than a 1D ribbon or lower iodide phase; TiI3 forms 1D ribbons [CS-1] and TlMnI3 forms MnI3 ribbons [TS-3], while the target is 2D [Target]. CS-1 reports mixed products, which reduces confidence in clean kinetic trapping of the desired phase.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Hydrothermal/solution iodide routes operate at relatively mild temperatures, e.g., 75–5 °C cooling for SnI3-type crystals [SS-1, SS-2], 155–175 °C for AgTl2I3 [TS-5], and 290 °C for PtI3 [CS-4]. The route still requires sealed vessels and volatile/corrosive HI/I2 [CS-4, TS-5]. No high-pressure >5 GPa or >1000 °C condition is involved [CS-4, TS-5].

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
Solution routes offer excellent mass transport, as shown by slow-cooling SnI3 crystallization [SS-1, SS-2] and hydrothermal AgTl2I3 formation [TS-5]. However, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that binary MnI3 can be crystallized from HI/I2; PtI3 has a narrow existence region and impurity issues [CS-4]. The likely Mn(III)/iodide redox conflict is a major [Reasonable Inference] uncertainty, so the kinetics/pathway score is reduced despite favorable transport.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The Bridgman/melt-anneal route is moderate in temperature because TlMnI3 is annealed at 350 °C [TS-3], and CuHgSX analogues form at 553–573 K [TS-2, TS-4]. It is penalized for dry/inert handling, sealed ampoules, volatile iodine, and multiple operations such as melting, powdering, annealing, and possibly Bridgman translation [TS-3]. These requirements make it more severe than simple hydrothermal crystallization but still not extreme.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
The strongest support is Mn-specific: TlMnI3 contains MnI6 octahedral ribbons and is made from MnI2-containing mixtures [TS-3]. The weakness is that TS-3 requires 3 weeks of annealing, and related Fe material required repeated powdering/annealing, indicating slow equilibration and phase-control challenges [TS-3]. The target 2D MnI3 sheet differs from the TlMnI3 1D ribbon, so structural reconstruction remains only moderately favorable [Target vs TS-3].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius-disorder check is not triggered [Target]. The structure has mixed corner- and edge-sharing MnI6 octahedra, triggering the connectivity complexity check [Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a large >40 atom unit cell, so C2 is treated as not triggered/neutral; overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.038 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is moderate-to-strong for making metal iodide frameworks in sealed ampoules, especially TiI3/HfI4 and Mn-containing TlMnI3 [CS-1, CS-2, TS-3], but no source directly demonstrates binary MnI3, so direct evidence is incomplete. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high iodine chemical potential in a dry sealed system, because I2-based sealed-tube chemistry is repeatedly successful for related iodides [CS-1, CS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase/redox control: MnI3 may compete with MnI2 + I2 or ternary Mn iodides, and similar triiodide systems show impurity or narrow phase fields [CS-1, CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful ampoule chemistry rather than a simple open solid-state reaction.
