### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0340 eV/atom, within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom range [Target]. Similar phases with E_hull values in the 0.01–0.035 eV/atom range are experimentally synthesized, including Cu2As4S3Cl2 [CS1, TS1], CdSbS2Cl/CdSbS2Br family members [CS2, CS4], and Cu3SbSe3 [TS4]. The phase is likely close to thermodynamic stability but not necessarily a strict ground state because it is above hull [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is the identity and kinetic accessibility of competing Ag–As–Se–Cl decomposition products, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild temperature and pressure because the closest analogue crystallizes at ambient temperature by solvent diffusion [CS1, TS1]. The deductions arise from N2/Schlenk handling, multi-step organometallic cluster/adduct preparation, and toxic As-containing precursors [CS1, TS1]. SS gives insufficient useful signal for this exact solvent-diffusion As–Se–Cl route. The main uncertainty is whether AgCl/Ag+ provides adequate solubility and controlled chloride activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because soluble As4S3·M(CO)5 and Cu chloride precursors form the analogous layered phase in only 12 h at ambient temperature [CS1, TS1]. The target’s 2D sheet topology and local coinage-metal chalcogen/chloride coordination are very similar to the analogue [CS1, TS1; Target]. The key uncertainty is whether the heavier Se and larger Ag substitution changes solution speciation enough to slow or redirect crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Solid-state sealed-ampoule synthesis avoids extreme temperature but requires vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic As/Se/Cl management, and likely grinding/annealing steps [CS2–CS4, TS4]. The 350–430 °C range is supported by chalcohalide analogues [CS2, CS4] and phase-pure Cu3SbSe3 at 350 °C [TS4]. CS/TS support the temperature window, while SS gives insufficient direct evidence for Ag–As–Se–Cl bulk formation. The main severity comes from ampoule safety and composition retention [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Bulk diffusion should be manageable because none of Ag, As, Se, or AgCl is highly refractory, and related quaternary chalcohalides form at moderate temperatures [CS2, CS4, TS4]. However, solid-state routes can require long annealing, as seen for CuClCu2TeS3 and Cu3SbSe3 [CS3, TS4]. Structural reconstruction from AgCl/As2Se3/As into a layered Ag2As4Se3Cl2 sheet is less direct than solution assembly [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is competing binary/ternary formation such as Ag2Se or As–Se phases, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence.

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal/solvothermal synthesis uses low temperatures, around 180–220 °C in Ag–Se–I and Pd–Se–Cl analogues [SS1, SS3]. It still requires sealed corrosive media and toxic As/Se/chloride components [SS1, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. The pressure is autogenous rather than high pressure, so no high-pressure penalty is applied [SS1]. CS/TS give insufficient direct support for hydrothermal As–Se–Cl synthesis, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution or melt transport should accelerate crystallization relative to dry solid-state reaction, as AgSe3I and Pd(Se3Cl)2 form under low-temperature hydro/solvothermal conditions [SS1, SS3]. Ag–Se–halide and Se–Cl motifs are well supported [SS1, SS3]. The weak point is As incorporation into the desired As4Se3-like cluster sheet, because SS does not provide direct As-bearing hydrothermal analogues. Phase separation is likely, consistent with multiphase/intergrown products in AgSe3I hydrothermal products [SS1].

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT/flux growth requires evacuated ampoules, volatile/corrosive transport agents, and multi-step handling [TS2, TS5, SS2]. Temperatures can plausibly be kept below 500 °C by analogy with halide-rich CVT and flux systems [TS5, SS2; Reasonable Inference], although TS2 demonstrates a higher-temperature CVT case for MnIn2Se4. The route is therefore chemically plausible but operationally more severe than ambient diffusion. The main uncertainty is transport-agent compatibility with As–Se–Cl stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor or flux transport reduces diffusion limitations, and CVT is established for layered selenides and halide-rich compounds [TS2, TS5]. Flux growth of Ag–Se chalcogenides in sealed tubes also supports the feasibility of mobile chalcogenide media [SS2]. However, phase selection may be difficult because transport agents can change chloride chemical potential or introduce iodine/aluminum contamination [TS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for Ag2As4Se3Cl2 CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains chemically distinct Ag and As cations with significantly different coordination preferences, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The formula unit has 11 atoms, and the provided structure description does not prove a >40-atom cell; therefore C2 is not triggered, with insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS. The structure is layered and mixed-coordinate, but face-sharing coexistence is not reported, so C3 is not triggered [Target]. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with many crystalline layered chalcohalide analogues [CS1, SS1, TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2As4Se3Cl2 is likely a near-stable metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground state because its E_hull is low but nonzero at 0.0340 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support is the chemically and thermodynamically similar Cu2As4S3Cl2, which has the same As4Q3Cl2-type layered chemistry and is experimentally accessible by ambient solution diffusion [CS1, TS1]. Structurally similar Ag–Se–halide and Se–Cl compounds support the plausibility of Ag–Se–halide coordination environments but do not directly prove As incorporation [SS1, SS3]. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature, solution-mediated assembly that avoids volatile As/Se/Cl loss and favors layered cluster formation [CS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among Ag–Se, As–Se, and chloride-rich competing phases, especially because several analogues report multiphase products, narrow cooling windows, or difficult phase purification [SS1, SS3, SS4, CS1].

|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile/toxic As, Se, and chloride-bearing components can cause stoichiometry drift or safety issues [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Use sealed ampoules for thermal routes as in chalcohalide syntheses [CS2–CS4, TS4]; use inert Schlenk conditions for solution route [CS1, TS1]. |
| 2 | All | Competing Ag–Se, As–Se, and AgCl-rich phases may form [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Screen chloride activity and Ag:As4Se3 ratio; slow diffusion or slow cooling improves selectivity in analogues [CS1, TS1, TS5]. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Solubility mismatch between As4Se3 species and AgCl may suppress crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium–High | Use organometallic As4Se3·M(CO)5 adducts by analogy to As4S3·M(CO)5 [CS1, TS1]; test mixed CH2Cl2/CH3CN/CH3OH solvent layering [CS1]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Precursor synthesis complexity for As4Se3·M(CO)5 [Reasonable Inference from As4S3·M(CO)5 complexity]. | Medium | Prepare and purify the As–Se cluster/adduct before crystallization, following the cluster-adduct strategy used for Cu2As4S3Cl2 [CS1, TS1]. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Slow ordering kinetics in dry solid-state reaction. | Medium | Use pre-synthesized As2Se3/As4Se3, fine grinding, and repeated annealing, following grinding/tempering approaches in CuClCu2TeS3 and long anneals in Cu3SbSe3 [CS3, TS4]. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Ampoule pressure from As/Se/Cl volatility. | High | Use small charges, thick-walled evacuated quartz ampoules, slow ramps, and temperatures near 350–430 °C as in related chalcohalides [CS2, CS4, TS4]. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Multiphase/intergrown crystals in hydrothermal products. | Medium–High | Use slow cooling and microscopic separation, as reported necessary/typical for Ag–Se–I hydrothermal products [SS1]. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Over-chlorination or dissolution by SeCl4/HCl mineralizer. | Medium | Screen chloride mineralizer concentration; Pd–Se–Cl synthesis highlights sensitivity to selenium/halide balance [SS3]. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Transport-agent contamination or incorrect halogen chemical potential. | Medium–High | Use low transport-agent loading, analogous to optimized AlCl3 CVT concentration in MnIn2Se4 [TS2]; compare AlCl3, I2, and SeCl4 only after bulk phase is identified [TS2, CS5, SS3]. |
| 10 | Route 4 | Crystal growth may be low-yield or narrow-window. | Medium | Employ slow gradients and extended growth times; flux analogues report low yields and need precise conditions [SS2], while halide CVT requires stoichiometric control [TS5]. |
