### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0250 eV/atom, which lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range for high synthesizability. TS analogues with similar small positive hull energies are experimentally accessible: SmZrF7 has E_hull ≈0.028 eV/atom and is made by direct solid-state reaction (TS1), and CoZrF6 has E_hull ≈0.020 eV/atom and is synthesized from CoF2/ZrF4 (TS2). CS also contains stable or near-stable complex fluorides, but many require harsh fluorination, so CS supports thermodynamic accessibility but not necessarily easy synthesis.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids F2 pressure and >1000 °C treatment, unlike CS1/CS3/CS4. The severity penalty mainly comes from volatile/moisture-sensitive fluoride handling and sealed-tube requirements, supported by TS2’s note on hygroscopicity and TS1/TS3/SS5 use of sealed Ni/Pt tubes. Insufficient evidence from CS for this mild sealed binary-fluoride route, so CS is neutral for severity.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluorides are reasonably reactive because TS2, TS3, and SS5 show direct formation of Zr/Zn or Zr/Ln fluorides from dried fluorides. The main kinetic uncertainty is conversion into the target edge-sharing layered P312 polymorph rather than the corner-sharing ReO3-derived ZnZrF6-like structure in TS2. Slow cooling and possible regrinding are inferred to help cation ordering and structural selection.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Reactive fluorination is much more severe because CS1 uses kbar F2 and CS3/CS4 use hundreds of atm F2 plus pre-fluorination and annealing. The route does not trigger the >5 GPa deduction under the provided rubric, but chemically it remains hazardous due to F2 toxicity and corrosion. TS gives insufficient evidence that such harsh F2 pressure is required for HfZnF6, so the route is viewed as a backup.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
F2 can overcome incomplete fluorination, as demonstrated for CS1–CS4, but those examples also show long times, phase sensitivity, and specialized handling. Starting from HfO2 would introduce slow fluorination kinetics because HfO2 is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. The route may produce fully fluorinated material but not necessarily the target polymorph.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Solvothermal synthesis has mild temperature and pressure compared with sealed-tube or F2-pressure synthesis, as SS2/SS3 use 150–200 °C in a Teflon-lined autoclave. The deductions are for multistep workup and toxic fluoride/HF chemistry. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for solvothermal Hf–Zn double fluoride synthesis, so their severity contribution is neutral.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solution precursors give favorable kinetics, supported by SS2/SS3 solvothermal ScF3 crystallization from soluble salts and NH4F. However, the structural pathway is highly uncertain because no CS/TS analogue shows solvothermal anhydrous HfZnF6 formation. Hf4+ hydrolysis and hydrated fluoro-hafnate formation are likely major competitors [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only HfF6 and ZnF6 octahedra with edge-sharing connectivity according to the target description, so the connectivity is not highly mixed. Hf4+ and Zn2+ octahedral radii are close enough that large cation-size mismatch should not drive glass formation [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for amorphization problems in this exact HfZnF6 phase; therefore the score is high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfZnF6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.025 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as SmZrF7 and CoZrF6 (TS1, TS2). The strongest synthesis support comes from TS2 and SS5, which demonstrate Zn–Zr fluoride chemistry using ZnF2/ZrF4-type binary fluorides, and from TS1/TS3 showing robust sealed-tube ZrF4 fluoride synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic and chemical compatibility of Hf4+/Zn2+ in fluoride octahedral frameworks [TS2, SS5, Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is polymorph control: the target is a 2D edge-sharing trigonal sheet, while the closest TS2 analogue CoZrF6/ZnZrF6 is a 3D corner-sharing ReO3-derived phase. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with sealed-tube binary-fluoride reaction the best evidence-weighted route.
