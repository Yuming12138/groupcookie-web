### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s **E above hull = 0.0500 eV/atom** places it at the favorable edge of metastability, so the assigned score is 90 by the required rubric. TS gives useful corroboration because thermodynamically similar compounds with hull energies around 0.058–0.079 eV/atom are experimentally made by high-temperature, flux, or vacuum routes (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS examples are mostly on-hull W oxides, suggesting W-containing multication oxides are accessible, but they do not reproduce the target’s Ta-W reduced structural chemistry (CS-1–CS-5). SS gives only partial signal because structural analogues include chemically different Ce/Ba/Nb/Ti oxides (SS-1–SS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

CS directly supports the soft-chemistry route: citrate resin formation, 120 °C drying, 600 °C decomposition, and 850–900 °C reducing firing are reported for NaLaFeWO6/NaNdFeWO6 (CS-1, CS-3). The condition severity is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but uses H2-containing gas and several processing steps (CS-1, CS-3). Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state synthesis because CS explicitly states the soft-chemistry route is designed to obtain reactive homogeneous precursors (CS-1, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is substantial for Ta incorporation and the unusual W coordination, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct synthesis precedent.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

CS supports ceramic synthesis of W-containing multication oxides at 800–1200 °C in air or forming gas (CS-2, CS-4), and NaLaMgWO6 at 1050 °C in air (CS-5). The condition score is lower than Route 1 because >1000 °C and reducing atmosphere are likely required, while high pressure is not supported or needed by CS (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). Kinetics are poorer because Ta2O5 and WO3/WO2 solid-state diffusion is expected to be slow; TS/SS examples of Nb/Ta oxides requiring 1450–1500 °C reinforce this concern (SS-3; TS-1, TS-4). The main uncertainty is whether the P1 target can form before decomposition or competing La/Sr tantalates/tungstates dominate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

TS and SS strongly support sealed high-temperature routes for reduced Nb/Ta oxides, including 1450 °C in sealed Ta tubes and 1500 °C in sealed Nb tubes (TS-4; TS-1; SS-3). The severity is high because the route uses >1400 °C, vacuum/sealed refractory tubes, and controlled cooling (TS-1, TS-4). Kinetics improve at high temperature, but refractory Ta/W cation diffusion and redox competition remain difficult, so F3 is not high despite harsh conditions. CS provides insufficient evidence for this route because chemically similar W oxides were not made in sealed Ta/Nb tubes (CS-1–CS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

SS and TS show that flux methods can grow structurally related oxides at 700–1000 °C with long dwell/cooling times (SS-1, SS-2; TS-2). This gives relatively mild thermal severity, but air-sensitive metal fluxes or sealed chloride fluxes add operational complexity, especially if Sr/Ba metal fluxes are used (SS-1). Kinetically, flux transport can help overcome slow diffusion, but there is insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of Sr-La-Ta-W oxides, and Ta/W oxide solubility in common fluxes is uncertain. Thus Route 4 is plausible for exploratory phase discovery or crystals, but less reliable than Route 1 for bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains large Sr/La cations and much smaller Ta/W cations, so the ionic radius mismatch check is triggered by physicochemical reasoning and by the very different coordination environments described for the target. The target P1 description lists one Sr, one La, one Ta, one W, and six O sites, so there is no evidence for a >40 atom unit cell; CS/SS/TS do not override this. Mixed corner/edge connectivity is present in the target, unlike the simpler corner-sharing octahedral networks in many CS analogues (CS-1–CS-5), increasing disorder/amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is whether the reported P1 structure is a small ordered cell or a simplified derivative of a larger disordered phase.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrLaTaWO6 is likely a **borderline metastable** phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its energy above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS analogues strongly support that multication W oxides can be synthesized by soft-chemistry or ceramic routes under reducing conditions, but they mostly contain octahedral W6+ networks rather than the target’s unusual TaO4/W low-coordinate environment (CS-1–CS-5). TS analogues show that phases with similar metastability can be synthesized, often with controlled oxygen potential or sealed high-temperature methods (TS-1–TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is **homogeneous precursor mixing plus controlled reducing atmosphere**, best captured by the citrate route in CS-1 and CS-3. The most critical limiting factor is **redox/phase control of W and Ta**, since over-reduction may form W metal/WO2 while under-reduction may favor conventional tungstates or tantalates.
