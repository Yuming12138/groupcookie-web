### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score by the given rubric. This is reinforced by several related bromide/chloride halides lying on or very near the hull: NaGaBr₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS2), RbHf₂Br₉ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS3), LuNb₆Cl₁₈ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS4), and NbBr₅ at ∼0.0006 eV/atom (SS1/TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for experimental LiNbBr₆ specifically, so source-specific experimental confirmation is neutral, but the computed ground-state result is strong.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to CS1/CS2, where alkali/metal bromides react or crystallize from melts at 250–400 °C. The main severity penalties come from the need for dry Ar or vacuum sealed ampoules and air/moisture-sensitive halide precursors, which are repeatedly noted for bromide/metal-halide analogues (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1). Insufficient evidence from TS beyond NbBr₅ sealed-ampoule behavior means TS contributes mainly a neutral-to-supportive signal rather than direct route confirmation.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is favorable because binary bromides form reactive melts at modest temperatures in CS1/CS2. NbBr₅ already contains NbBr₆ octahedral units and edge-sharing motifs (SS1/TS1), which reduces the structural reconstruction needed to form the LiNbBr₆ octahedral sheet. Uncertainty remains because the target requires Li/Nb ordering in an AlCl₃-derived 2D structure, and no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports that exact topology for LiNbBr₆.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits moderate temperature requirements from CVT analogues at 360–475 °C (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4, TS5). Its severity is higher than Route 1 because CVT requires an evacuated ampoule, thermal gradient, volatile halide transport, and air-sensitive precursors, while some analogues report difficult phase control and low yields (SS2/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS for CVT of simple Li/Nb bromides makes this route less directly supported.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport is plausible because halide octahedral materials can crystallize from vapor-phase sealed ampoule methods (SS2/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5), and NbBr₅ is compatible with sealed ampoule heating (SS1/TS1). However, LiBr is much less volatile than NbBr₅ [Reasonable Inference], so maintaining Li:Nb = 1:1 at the growth front may be challenging. The CVT analogues also report low yields or equilibrium mixtures (SS2/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5), lowering the kinetics/composition-control score.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses similar or slightly higher process complexity than Route 1 because it adds quench/anneal steps inspired by CaThBr₆ synthesis (CS5). Temperatures remain moderate compared with refractory solid-state synthesis, but sealed ampoules, volatile NbBr₅, and air-sensitive halides still drive deductions (CS5, SS1/TS1). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a flux-annealed LiNbBr₆ analogue means the route is plausible but not directly demonstrated.

#### F3 — Route 3

A LiBr-rich molten or flux-like environment should improve ion mobility, consistent with clear melt formation in AMBr₄ bromide syntheses (CS2) and binary-component melt growth (CS1). The post-annealing concept is supported by layered bromide CaThBr₆ synthesis, where high-temperature treatment followed by long low-temperature annealing is used (CS5). The uncertainty is that CS5 involves Ca/Th bromides with different coordination and oxidation chemistry, so the annealing benefit is inferred rather than proven for LiNbBr₆.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only edge-sharing octahedral connectivity according to the target description, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered. The unit cell is not indicated to exceed 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise, scored neutral. The Li⁺/Nb⁵⁺ size and charge mismatch triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference], but halide melt synthesis analogues crystallize ordered bromides despite cation mismatch (CS1/CS2), so the overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiNbBr₆ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically or thermodynamically related halides are also on or near the hull (CS2, CS3, CS4, SS1/TS1). The CS analogues strongly support low-temperature sealed-ampoule binary-halide melt synthesis, especially AMBr₄ compounds from alkali bromides plus metal bromides (CS1/CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a direct stoichiometric reaction, LiBr + NbBr₅ → LiNbBr₆, using reactive halide precursors [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/CS2 and SS1/TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling volatile, moisture-sensitive NbBr₅ in a sealed system while maintaining exact Li:Nb stoichiometry (SS1/TS1). Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with sealed-ampoule melt growth as the most credible first attempt.
