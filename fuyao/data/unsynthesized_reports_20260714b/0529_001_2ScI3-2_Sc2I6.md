### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0470 eV/atom, which lies just below the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore receives a high score of 92. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally accessible: ThPbI₆ at 0.033 eV/atom is synthesized by solid-state sintering (TS1), SrThBr₆ at 0.045 eV/atom is synthesized by sealed-tube sintering/annealing (TS3), and NaHgCl₃ at 0.055 eV/atom crystallizes from solution (TS4). CS and SS also show many stable layered or octahedral iodides at E_hull = 0, especially YI₃ and HfI₄ (CS1/SS1; CS5/SS2), but they do not directly prove the target Pmma polymorph stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly because it requires vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile I₂, both used in YI₃ and HfI₄ syntheses (CS1/SS1; CS5/SS2). It also likely requires staged heating and sublimation/purification, as reported for YI₃ and HfI₄, making it multi-step (CS1/SS1; CS5/SS2). The maximum likely temperature is below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1 and CS5/SS2]. Remaining uncertainty is the exact sublimation temperature of ScI₃ relative to YI₃/HfI₄.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental iodination benefits from highly reactive iodine vapor, and direct Y + I₂ and Hf + I₂ reactions are successful (CS1/SS1; CS5/SS2). However, Sc metal may have a passivating oxide layer and requires full conversion from dense metal to layered ScI₃, lowering precursor activity [Reasonable Inference]. Structural formation is plausible because YI₃ is a 2D triiodide made of edge-sharing YI₆ octahedra (CS1/SS1), but the target Pmma edge/corner-sharing sheet differs from the YI₃ R-3 structure.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses sealed evacuated tubes and volatile iodine/iodide transport species, matching TiI₃ CVT and HfI₄ sublimation references (CS2; CS5/SS2). The route avoids high pressure and avoids >1000 °C processing, since analogous CVT/sublimation occurs at 823–923 K for TiI₃ and ~150–250 °C for HfI₄ purification (CS2; CS5/SS2). It is still penalized for air/moisture-sensitive halides and sealed-tube iodine pressure. Insufficient evidence from TS for exact CVT conditions of ScI₃; TS mainly supports metastable halide accessibility rather than a vapor route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT/sublimation should have better kinetics because mass transport occurs through volatile iodide species rather than bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference from TiI₃ CVT in CS2 and HfI₄ sublimation in CS5/SS2]. The 2D octahedral nature of the target is supported by structurally similar YI₃ and BaUI₆ sheets (SS1; SS3), although those are not identical ScI₃ Pmma polymorphs. The major uncertainty is polymorph selection: vapor growth may crystallize a more stable BiI₃-type or other ScI₃ form rather than the desired Pmma sheet [Reasonable Inference; target E_hull = 0.047 eV/atom].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits sealed-ampoule and volatile halide requirements from La₂O₃ + AlBr₃ vapor transport routes (CS3/CS4). AlI₃ is expected to be air-sensitive and volatile, and the route requires precursor drying, sealing, reaction/transport, and cleanup [Reasonable Inference from CS3/CS4]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is required, consistent with LaBr₃/La₂Br₅ transport at 360–500 °C (CS3/CS4). Insufficient evidence from SS for oxide-to-ScI₃ metathesis specifically; SS mostly supports layered iodide structures and elemental/sublimation routes.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Sc₂O₃ is likely kinetically sluggish because strong Sc–O bonds must be converted into Sc–I bonds [Reasonable Inference]. Oxygen contamination is a known problem for rare-earth iodides, where oxyiodide formation is thermodynamically favored and purification by sublimation may be needed (CS1/SS1). Although La₂O₃ + AlBr₃ successfully yields LaBr₃ crystals (CS3), TiO₂/Ti/I₂ chemistry produced TiI₃ along with TiI₂ and Ti₂O₃, showing oxide routes can suffer mixed products (CS2). This makes Route 3 the least kinetically favorable.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger [target structure]. The target ScI₃ sheet contains edge- and corner-sharing ScI₆ octahedra but not face-sharing octahedra, so the specified mixed-connectivity trigger is not met [target structure]. The simple binary composition and layered octahedral motif resemble crystalline iodide analogues such as YI₃ (SS1), supporting low amorphization risk. The uncertainty is that metastable Pmma ordering may compete with alternative crystalline polymorphs rather than amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target ScI₃ phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than a strict ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.047 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support synthesis of metal iodides with octahedral coordination, especially YI₃ and HfI₄ by sealed-tube elemental reaction/sublimation (CS1/SS1; CS5/SS2), while TS analogues show that halides near 0.03–0.06 eV/atom can be experimentally obtained (TS1; TS3; TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the high volatility/transportability of iodide species, which allows CVT or sublimation growth rather than relying on slow solid-state diffusion (CS2; CS5/SS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selection: the desired Pmma 2D ScI₃ sheet may compete with a more stable layered triiodide polymorph, as the closest chemical analogue YI₃ adopts trigonal R-3 rather than Pmma (CS1/SS1). Moisture and oxygen exclusion are also essential because rare-earth/group-3 iodides are prone to oxyhalide contamination (CS1/SS1).
