### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0110 eV/atom, so it falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability regime. CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 give a very similar exact-composition HgBrI polymorph with E_hull ≈ 0.0105 eV/atom, reinforcing that the Hg–Br–I composition is near the convex hull. Several related Hg halide/chalcogenide phases are also on or near the hull in CS-2/CS-3/CS-4/TS-4/TS-5. The uncertainty is that a low E_hull does not identify which polymorph, 0D Cmc2₁ or 2D Pmm2, is kinetically favored.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is chemically simple because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 report direct formation of HgBrI from iodine and mercurous halides. However, the recommended practical implementation uses a sealed/vacuum ampoule because iodine is volatile and mercury halides are toxic; CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 do not specify atmosphere, so this part is [Reasonable Inference]. No high temperature or high pressure is indicated by CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. The severity score is reduced mainly by toxicity, volatility, and sealed handling.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Exact HgBrI formation from iodine plus mercurous halide in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 suggests favorable chemical kinetics. Soft Hg–halide bonding and possible iodine vapor transport should reduce diffusion barriers relative to refractory solid-state reactions; this interpretation is consistent with low-temperature sealed Hg iodide reactions in CS-3/CS-4/SS-3. The kinetic uncertainty is polymorph reconstruction, because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 report a molecular/0D HgBrI polymorph, while the target requires a 2D corner-sharing tetrahedral sheet.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses standard sealed-ampoule conditions similar to CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-4. It avoids extreme temperature and pressure, since related Hg iodide systems are synthesized at 260–320 °C in CS-3/CS-4/SS-3/SS-5. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile HgBr₂/HgI₂, toxicity, and multiple handling steps. The binary-halide route is therefore moderately severe but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

HgBr₂ and HgI₂ are reactive molecular halides, and related mercury halide precursors form complex Hg-containing phases in CS-2 and TS-4. Diffusion should be manageable because low-temperature sealed reactions are successful in CS-3/CS-4/SS-3. The main kinetic penalty is the need to order Br and I into the target HgBrI sheet and avoid the known 0D HgBrI polymorph reported in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT/vapor growth is supported for related Hg halide/chalcogenide materials in SS-4, TS-2, and TS-5. It requires evacuated quartz ampoules, a temperature gradient, and volatile toxic Hg/halide species, so the severity is comparable to Route 2. TS-5 demonstrates 500–540 °C gradient growth for Hg–S–Br–Cl, but lower temperatures are inferred for HgBrI due to higher volatility; this temperature adaptation is [Reasonable Inference]. No external high pressure is required in SS-4/TS-2/TS-5.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion, and SS-4/TS-2/TS-5 show that Hg halide-rich analogues can crystallize from the gas phase. However, mixed Br/I vapor composition may be difficult to control, and TS-5 reports polymorphism/statistical halogen occupation in related mixed-halide phases. The route may improve crystal quality but has a lower kinetic/composition-control score because no source directly demonstrates vapor-grown Pmm2 HgBrI.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Hg, so the cation-size-mismatch check is not triggered from the target composition. The target structure is a simple corner-sharing tetrahedral sheet, and no mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is reported for the target; SS-2 supports that corner-sharing Hg-halide sheets can exist. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct information on the exact target unit-cell atom count, but the reported single Hg, Br, and I sites indicate a small ordered structure rather than a large complex cell. Therefore amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgBrI Pmm2 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, and the exact HgBrI composition appears near the hull in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. The strongest support comes from CS-1/SS-1/TS-1, which directly report HgBrI formation by a simple solid-state reaction, although they report a different 0D polymorph. Structural support for the target’s 2D corner-sharing Hg-halide sheet comes from SS-2, where HgI₂ forms 2D sheets of corner-sharing HgI₄ tetrahedra. The critical enabling factor is the high chemical lability and volatility of Hg–halide species, which should allow low-temperature reaction and recrystallization. The critical limiting factor is polymorph control between molecular/0D HgBrI and the desired 2D Pmm2 sheet, compounded by Br/I volatility and toxic sealed-ampoule handling.
