### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, which lies at the favorable metastability threshold and receives a high score by the required rubric. The closest analogue Gd2ZnTiO6 has nearly the same E_hull, **0.0513 eV/atom**, and is reported as experimentally synthesized by solid-state sintering, showing that this energy range is practically accessible (CS1, SS3, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that Cu²⁺ substitution may introduce different electronic/Jahn–Teller distortions than Zn²⁺, which could change competing phase stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires high temperature above 1000 °C and multiple calcination/grinding steps, directly matching the Gd2ZnTiO6 route at 1323, 1423, and 1523 K in air (CS1, SS3, TS1). It avoids high pressure, vacuum, glovebox handling, toxic Os/Re chemistry, and sealed ampoules that appear in less relevant Os/Re analogues (CS2, CS3, CS5). The main uncertainty is whether CuO volatility or Cu-rich impurity formation becomes significant at the highest firing temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Oxide precursors provide robust but relatively slow kinetics because Gd2O3 and TiO2 ceramic reactions generally require high temperatures and repeated regrinding in the supplied titanate examples (CS1, SS2, SS5, TS3, TS5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because Gd2ZnTiO6 is essentially the same P2₁/c double-perovskite-derived framework with similar octahedral tilts (CS1, SS3, TS1). The remaining kinetic uncertainty is B-site ordering of Ti/Cu versus formation of Gd2Ti2O7 or Gd2CuO4 side phases [Reasonable Inference supported by separate titanate/cuprate analogues SS2 and SS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses multi-step solution processing, decomposition, calcination, and final annealing, so it incurs a multi-step penalty based on sol–gel Gd2CuO4 and citrate routes (SS4, CS4). It likely still needs a final temperature near or slightly above 1000 °C to crystallize the ordered P2₁/c phase, because exact Gd2ZnTiO6 requires 1250 °C by oxide reaction and citrate perovskites can require 1200 °C (CS1/SS3/TS1, CS4). Insufficient evidence from TS for direct sol–gel synthesis of a Gd–Ti–Cu P2₁/c phase, so this route is scored as plausible but not directly proven.

#### F3 — Route 2

Nitrate/sol–gel/citrate precursors are highly reactive and molecularly mixed, as shown by Gd2CuO4 sol–gel and citrate-resin perovskite precursor formation (SS4, CS4). This should reduce diffusion limitations compared with binary oxide mixing [Reasonable Inference], but the target still requires crystallization and B-site ordering into the P2₁/c double-perovskite framework, which is directly evidenced only for the Zn analogue under solid-state sintering (CS1/SS3/TS1). The uncertainty is whether the gel-derived powder crystallizes directly to Gd2TiCuO6 or first forms stable Gd2Ti2O7/Gd2CuO4 intermediates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has lower thermal severity but substantial pressure severity because TS4 uses 6 GPa in a cubic-anvil apparatus. There is insufficient evidence from CS and SS that high pressure is needed or beneficial for the exact Gd–Ti–Cu target, while the closest direct analogue Gd2ZnTiO6 is made at ambient pressure (CS1/SS3/TS1). Therefore, the route is penalized mainly for specialized high-pressure conditions rather than for temperature or atmosphere.

#### F3 — Route 3

The short high-pressure dwell in TS4 demonstrates that high pressure can process related oxide phases quickly, but it does not establish rapid formation of a multication ordered double perovskite. If a pre-reacted precursor is used, diffusion lengths may be reduced [Reasonable Inference], but B-site ordering and reconstruction may remain incomplete during a 0.5–2 h treatment. Because no CS/SS analogue directly supports high-pressure synthesis of Gd2TiCuO6, the kinetics score remains moderate-low.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains strongly size- and coordination-mismatched cations: large 8-coordinate Gd and smaller octahedral Ti/Cu, so one amorphization trigger is assigned (Target; analogous environments in CS1/SS3/SS4). However, the closest P2₁/c analogue has a compact 20-atom cell and a simple corner-sharing octahedral framework, reducing topological glass-forming tendency (CS1/SS3/TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether sol–gel powders transiently form amorphous intermediates, but these should crystallize under the proposed annealing conditions based on SS4 and CS4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Gd2TiCuO6 is best viewed as a **near-threshold metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than clearly on the convex hull. The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually supportive because the closest analogue, Gd2ZnTiO6, has the same P2₁/c double-perovskite-derived framework, nearly identical tilt geometry, similar E_hull, and an established air solid-state synthesis (CS1, SS3, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the charge- and size-compatible substitution of Zn²⁺ by Cu²⁺ in the Gd2MTiO6 framework [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/SS3/TS1 and target bonding]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering kinetics among Gd–Ti–O and Gd–Cu–O subphases, especially because Gd2Ti2O7 and Gd2CuO4 are stable accessible oxides in the supplied structural analogues (SS2, SS4). Overall, ambient-pressure synthesis is likely feasible, with sol–gel/citrate processing favored for kinetics and oxide ceramic sintering favored for direct structural precedent.
