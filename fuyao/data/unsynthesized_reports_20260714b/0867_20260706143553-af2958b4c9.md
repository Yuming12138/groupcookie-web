### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Ba₃P₁₅Cl has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase. Chemically close Ba–P–halide analogues Ba₂P₇Br and Ba₂P₇I are also on the hull, while Ba₂P₇Cl is essentially stable at 0.00078 eV/atom (CS). This strongly supports a high F1 score. SS/TS are much less chemically matched, so they are treated as neutral for Ba–P–Cl-specific thermodynamics.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 800 °C and no external high pressure, matching Ba₂P₇Cl/Br/I synthesis conditions (CS). The main severity penalties are evacuated ampoule operation, volatile P/halide containment, and air-sensitive Ba handling, all consistent with the glovebox/sealed-ampoule precedent (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS give generic support for inert/sealed synthesis but no specific Ba–P–Cl guidance, so their route-specific signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate because elemental Ba and red P are reactive enough for Ba₂P₇X formation at 1073 K, but the 140 h anneal indicates slow solid-state ordering (CS). The target’s trigonal Ba₃P₁₅Cl framework differs from monoclinic Ba₂P₇Cl, so substantial reconstruction of the P network is expected [Reasonable Inference]. The route is nevertheless plausible because the exact chemical system is already synthetically demonstrated (CS).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more procedurally severe because it likely requires prior BaP₂ synthesis before the final BaP₂/P/BaCl₂ reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS]. The BaP₁₀ precedent involves long sealed-ampoule heating at 1050 K with iodine transport, which adds volatility and impurity-control concerns (CS). No SS/TS analogue directly supports BaP₂-to-Ba₃P₁₅Cl conversion, so those sources are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

The BaP₂-assisted route has better kinetic prospects than the elemental route because a preformed Ba–P precursor should reduce long-range Ba/P interdiffusion and better match the desired polyphosphide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. BaP₁₀ synthesis from BaP₂ + P demonstrates that such precursors can form very P-rich Ba polyphosphides, but the 4-week duration shows slow reconstruction and ordering (CS). Chloride incorporation remains an uncertainty because BaP₁₀ is Cl-free (CS).

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure route benefits from relatively short synthesis times in the BaP₈ precedent, but it requires multianvil equipment, capsule preparation, and pressurization to 3 GPa (CS). The formal F2 rubric only penalizes pressures above 5 GPa, so the numeric score remains moderate; practically, it is still challenging [Reasonable Inference]. The closest Ba–P–Cl analogues do not require high pressure, so high pressure is not the preferred route (CS).

#### F3 — Route 3

BaP₈ formation at 3 GPa and 950–1000 °C in 1–2 h shows that high pressure can accelerate Ba polyphosphide formation (CS). However, BaP₈ is Cl-free, and the reported difficulty preparing some isostructural analogues suggests a narrow stability window (CS). For Ba₃P₁₅Cl, pressure may help densification but may also bias toward competing Ba–P phases rather than the desired chlorophosphide [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large polyphosphide framework and likely has a >40-atom crystallographic cell, so one amorphization/complexity trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference]. There is no mixed-cation radius mismatch because Ba is the only cation, and the target description only explicitly states face-sharing ClBa₆ octahedra rather than full vertex/edge/face coexistence. Therefore the amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₃P₁₅Cl is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar Ba₂P₇Cl/Br/I phases, which were synthesized as Ba–P–halide polyphosphides under sealed-ampoule solid-state conditions (CS). SS and TS analogues support the broader idea that air-sensitive Zintl-like solids can be made in sealed/inert environments, but they are chemically dissimilar and therefore only weakly informative. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated stability and synthesis of Ba–P–Cl chemistry near the same temperature range (CS). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Ba–P-rich compounds such as BaP₈, BaP₁₀, and Ba₂P₇Cl-like phases, especially because the target has a different Ba:P:Cl ratio and P-network topology [Reasonable Inference from CS].
