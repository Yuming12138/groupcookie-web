### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0290 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS analogues include experimentally synthesized chalcohalides with E_hull values from 0.0 to 0.037 eV/atom (CS1–CS5), and TS analogues cluster around 0.027–0.039 eV/atom while remaining experimentally accessible (TS1–TS5). SS analogues include low-hull Bi–Se–halides and Bi₂Se₃, supporting that low-dimensional Bi chalcogen-halide frameworks can be stable or mildly metastable (SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed S/Se/Cl and may compete with binary Bi₂S₃, Bi₂Se₃, and Bi–chalcogen halides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 sealed solid-state.**  
CS1/CS2/TS3 show moderate temperatures of 400–430 °C, which avoids high-temperature penalties. The deductions arise from evacuated sealed tubes, volatile S/Se/halide species, multi-step drybox/sealing/heating/cooling, and toxic or moisture-sensitive BiCl₃/Se precursors, all supported by CS1/CS2/TS3. Insufficient evidence from SS for this exact Cd-free solid-state target route, so SS is neutral for this route-specific severity.

**F3 — Route 1 sealed solid-state.**  
Reaction kinetics are favorable compared with refractory oxide syntheses because CS1/CS2/TS3 form related chalcohalides at 400–430 °C. The 4-day duration and slow ramps in CS2/TS3 imply that diffusion/order development is not instantaneous. The target’s 2D corner/edge-sharing Bi coordination is structurally compatible with CS/TS motifs, but S/Se ordering remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 CVT/vapor-assisted growth.**  
CS3, SS1, SS2, and TS1 all use evacuated ampoules and temperature gradients, so controlled atmosphere and sealed volatile transport are unavoidable. Temperatures of 550–600 °C are still moderate, so there is no >1000 °C penalty. Toxic/air-sensitive reagents such as S₂Cl₂ or BiCl₃ drive the safety deduction, as directly indicated by CS3 and implied by halide CVT in SS2/TS1.

**F3 — Route 2 CVT/vapor-assisted growth.**  
CVT improves kinetics by moving material through volatile halide/chalcogen species, supported by CS3 for InBi₂S₄Cl and SS1/SS2/TS1 for Bi–Se–halides. The target contains Bi, S, Se, and Cl, all capable of forming volatile halide/chalcogen-halide species under sealed-tube conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether transport conditions favor Bi₂SeSCl₂ rather than BiSeCl, BiSCl, Bi₂Se₃, or Bi₂S₃ impurities.

**F2 — Route 3 melt growth.**  
SS4 and CS4 show sealed ampoule melt/direct reactions below 800 K, so severity is moderate rather than extreme. The route still requires vacuum sealing and volatile chloride/chalcogen control, which leads to deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a true congruent melt of Bi₂SeSCl₂, so melt behavior is inferred rather than established.

**F3 — Route 3 melt growth.**  
Liquid or partially molten halide-containing mixtures should reduce diffusion barriers, consistent with SS4 slow-cooling from stoichiometric melts. However, SS4 reports possible minor side products, and CS4 uses preformed BiSCl, suggesting phase purity may depend strongly on precursor choice. Mixed S/Se/Cl ordering during cooling is plausible but not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 4 ionothermal.**  
CS5 demonstrates low-temperature synthesis at 165 °C, so thermal severity is low. Deductions arise from glovebox/dry handling, multi-step heating and washing, and air-sensitive/corrosive Lewis-acidic AlCl₃ ionic-liquid media. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for ionothermal synthesis of the target or close Bi₂SeSCl₂ analogues, so SS/TS are neutral for this route’s condition severity.

**F3 — Route 4 ionothermal.**  
CS5 supports high precursor activation and low diffusion barriers in Lewis-acidic ionic liquids. The weak point is structural transfer: CS5 contains AlCl₄-derived units and Br/Cl chemistry, not a simple neutral Bi₂SeSCl₂ sheet. Therefore the structural reconstruction score is low despite favorable low-temperature kinetics.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only Bi as a cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The described target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, and the formula is small; CS/SS/TS provide no evidence that the target necessarily has a >40-atom unit cell. The low amorphization risk is also consistent with many crystalline layered/ribbon Bi chalcogen-halide analogues in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Bi₂SeSCl₂ is most likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.029 eV/atom and TS analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized. The CS evidence strongly supports sealed-tube chalcohalide synthesis, while SS and TS support CVT and vapor-assisted growth for Bi–Se–halide frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of volatile/reactive Bi–halide and chalcogen-halide chemistry, which allows phase formation at 400–600 °C rather than requiring refractory diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Bi₂S₃, Bi₂Se₃, BiSCl/BiSeCl, and off-stoichiometric Bi–chalcogen halides, especially because S/Se ordering is not directly proven in the references. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible with careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry and slow transport or annealing.
