### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0410 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore merits a high score by the provided rubric. CS supports accessibility because PrCl₃ is on the hull and TbCl₃/ZrCl₃ are near-hull phases (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5). SS gives the strongest support because the closest DyCl₃ structural analogue is thermodynamically stable at 0.0 eV/atom (SS-2). TS supports that halides with similar thermodynamic profiles around 0.045–0.061 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized (TS-1/TS-4/TS-5), although those are not chemically identical to YCl₃.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The AlCl₃ CVT route is low temperature because Dy/Ln chloride transport used 250→160 °C and TbCl₃ used 580–670 K (SS-2, CS-5/SS-5/TS-3). Severity is nevertheless moderate because AlCl₃ is volatile and air-sensitive and the route needs sealed inert/vacuum ampoules, as implied by sealed ampoule/glovebox/protective gas handling in SS-2 and CS-3. CS and SS strongly support this route; TS provides additional support mainly through TbCl₃ CVT (TS-3), while other TS entries are less route-specific.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because AlCl₃-mediated transport avoids slow bulk diffusion through refractory Y₂O₃, as demonstrated for Dy/Ln chloride formation from Ln₂O₃ + AlCl₃ (SS-2). The structural reconstruction penalty is small because DyCl₃ has essentially the same 2D 8-coordinate motif as target YCl₃ (SS-2). Uncertainty remains because TbCl₃ forms different polymorphs at different deposition temperatures, showing that LnCl₃ polymorph selection is temperature-sensitive (CS-5/SS-5/TS-3).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The in-situ Al/Cl₂ route is plausible because PrCl₃ was made from Pr₂O₃, Al, and dried Cl₂ by aluminum chloride transport (CS-2/SS-4). Its score is reduced because Cl₂ is toxic/corrosive and requires sealed reactive-gas handling, while volatile AlCl₃ is produced in situ. TS gives insufficient evidence for Cl₂-based synthesis specifically and is treated as neutral 50 for that aspect.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics should be good because Cl₂/Al chlorination is chemically active and generates volatile AlCl₃ transport species, as in PrCl₃ synthesis (CS-2/SS-4). However, structural selectivity is weaker than Route 1 because the PrCl₃ analogue is hexagonal and 9-coordinate, unlike the 8-coordinate 2D target (CS-2/SS-4). The remaining uncertainty is whether YCl₃ will deposit in the desired Pmmn/Cmcm-like sheet form rather than a different LnCl₃ polymorph.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-tube annealing avoids oxide chlorination if anhydrous YCl₃ is available, but still requires dry sealed ampoules because related halides are moisture-sensitive and handled in Ar/vacuum systems (SS-2, TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). Temperatures are moderate compared with oxide ceramic synthesis, based on 500–750 °C sealed halide anneals in TS-2/TS-4/TS-5. The main severity comes from volatile/air-sensitive chlorides and possible precursor purification.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Starting from anhydrous YCl₃ gives high precursor activity because the target composition is already present [Reasonable Inference], and TS analogues demonstrate that binary halides can be reacted or ordered in sealed tubes (TS-1/TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). Kinetic risk remains because TS analogues sometimes require several weeks of annealing to achieve ordering (TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). Polymorph selection is uncertain because TbCl₃ shows deposition-temperature-dependent structures (CS-5/SS-5/TS-3).

**F2 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is feasible in principle for rare-earth trichlorides because TmCl₃-type phases were prepared at 7.5–25 kbar and 770–1420 K (SS-3). The route is penalized by high temperature, specialized high-pressure equipment, protective-gas handling, and sensitive anhydrous chloride precursors (SS-3). CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure YCl₃ synthesis and are scored neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
High temperature near liquidus can promote crystallization, as SS-3 explicitly notes heating rare-earth chlorides to the liquidus range for single-crystal preparation. However, the high-pressure analogue is structurally different from the target 2D sheet, whereas SS-2 is the closer target analogue. Therefore, kinetics may form a crystalline product, but not necessarily the target Pmmn phase.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple binary YCl₃ with one cation type, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The structurally closest DyCl₃ analogue is a well-defined crystalline 2D sheet with simple LnCl₃ stoichiometry (SS-2), supporting low amorphization risk. CS/SS/TS indicate polymorphism in TbCl₃ but not glass formation or amorphization (CS-5/SS-5/TS-3), so the main noncrystalline risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target YCl₃ phase is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong: the closest structural analogue DyCl₃ has the same 2D 8-coordinate sheet chemistry and was synthesized by Ln₂O₃ + AlCl₃ CVT (SS-2), while TbCl₃ and PrCl₃ confirm broader rare-earth chloride CVT feasibility (CS-2/CS-5/SS-5/TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is AlCl₃-mediated transport, which simultaneously chlorinates the oxide and enables low-temperature crystal growth (SS-2, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control, because TbCl₃ forms different polymorphs depending on deposition temperature and metastability (CS-5/SS-5/TS-3). Overall, synthesis is plausible and moderately favorable, especially via low-temperature AlCl₃-assisted CVT.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Moisture sensitivity and hydrolysis of rare-earth chlorides | High | Use protective gas, sealed ampoules, and air-free handling; SS-2 explicitly used protective gas and notes rapid decomposition of related products in air. |
| 2 | Route 1 | Incomplete conversion of Y₂O₃ to YCl₃ | Medium | Use excess anhydrous AlCl₃ and maintain transport gradient; Ln₂O₃ + AlCl₃ stoichiometry and transport were successful for Dy/Ln chloride analogues (SS-2). |
| 3 | Route 1 / Route 2 | Volatile AlCl₃/Al₂Cl₆ pressure and ampoule safety | High | Use sealed ampoules rated for halide vapor; LaBr₃ transport controlled excess Al₂Br₆ pressure in sealed tubes (CS-3), and Dy/Tb syntheses used ampoules/capillaries under protective atmosphere (SS-2, CS-5). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Cl₂ toxicity and corrosivity | High | Prefer Route 1 with preformed AlCl₃ when possible; if Route 2 is used, dry Cl₂ as in PrCl₃ synthesis and use compatible sealed gas-handling equipment (CS-2/SS-4). |
| 5 | All | Competing YCl₃ polymorph formation | High | Screen deposition/anneal temperature because TbCl₃ formed UCl₃-type at 580 K and PuBr₃-type at 670 K (CS-5/SS-5/TS-3). |
| 6 | Route 3 | Slow ordering or recrystallization kinetics | Medium | Use long low-temperature annealing; thermodynamic halide analogues required several weeks at 200–300 °C after high-temperature treatment (TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). |
| 7 | Route 3 | Oxychloride contamination from hydrated or impure YCl₃ | Medium | Use anhydrous, sublimed YCl₃ or prepare under chloride-rich conditions; SS-3 notes rare-earth trichloride precursor preparation required decomposition/sublimation to remove oxide chlorides. |
| 8 | Route 4 | High-pressure equipment and wrong polymorph selection | High | Use HP route only for exploratory polymorph screening; SS-3 shows HP rare-earth chloride synthesis is possible but tends toward high-pressure structures unlike the target. |
| 9 | Route 1 | Crystal growth difficulty / small crystals | Medium | Use a controlled two-zone gradient and multi-day transport; SS-2 used 250→160 °C for about three days, and CS-3 used two-zone transport for single-crystalline LaBr₃. |
| 10 | All | Stoichiometry control and chloride loss | Medium | Maintain chloride-rich sealed environment with excess AlCl₃ or direct YCl₃ vapor; sealed halide systems and controlled cooling/annealing are used in TS halide syntheses (TS-1/TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). |
