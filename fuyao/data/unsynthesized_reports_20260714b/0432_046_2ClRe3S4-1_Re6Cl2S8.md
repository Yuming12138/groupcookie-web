### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull = 0.0100 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window, so F1 is scored 95. This is reinforced by the closest Re–Se–Cl analogue having E_hull ≈ 0.0016 eV/atom (SS1 / TS1) and by several chemically similar sulfides being on or near the hull (CS1–CS5). The uncertainty is that a small positive E_hull can still indicate metastability with respect to competing Re sulfides/chlorides, but the energy is low enough to be experimentally accessible.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 inherits demanding conditions from SS1/TS1: dry inert grinding, vacuum sealing, 900 °C reaction, slow cooling, and quenching. These are not extreme-pressure conditions, but the use of sulfur and Re chloride in sealed ampoules creates volatility and safety penalties. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact Re3S4Cl solid-state conditions means the score relies most strongly on SS1/TS1.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Re metal **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the use of ReCl5 and sulfur should improve reactivity relative to pure dense metals, consistent with SS1/TS1. The target structure is closely related to the SS1/TS1 Re–Se–Cl sheet, which improves the structural reconstruction score. The main uncertainty is phase purity because SS1/TS1 reports difficult phase control and low-yield products.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures compared with high-temperature sintering, because CS1/CS4 show chlorine-assisted vapor transport at ~770→710 °C and CS2 shows halogen CVT at 1000–1273 K. The severity remains substantial because evacuated sealed ampoules and toxic/corrosive halogen transport agents are required (CS1/CS2/CS4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct CVT growth of the Re3S4Cl structure prevents a higher score.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
CVT improves kinetics by transporting reactive volatile species rather than relying purely on solid-state Re diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, which is supported by successful halogen transport of sulfides in CS1/CS2/CS4. Composition control is the main kinetic/processing concern because too much chlorine may form volatile rhenium chlorides or over-chlorinated products **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is still favored because the target contains chloride, making halogen chemical transport chemically compatible.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is more severe because it extrapolates from TS3’s ~1100 °C sealed-tube annealing for Mo6S6Br2 and from SS3–SS5/TS2/TS4 repeated high-temperature heat treatments. It requires vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/halide control, and multiple processing steps. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact Re sulfide chloride annealing route keeps the score conservative.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Using binary Re sulfides may reduce the reconstruction distance compared with elemental Re/S **[Reasonable Inference]**, but refractory Re-containing solids are likely slow to equilibrate. TS3 explicitly states annealing is required for a pure metal–sulfur–halide product, and SS3–SS5/TS2/TS4 show repeated heat treatments are often needed for cluster sulfides. The target’s 2D P-1 sheet is not the same as the trigonal Mo6S8-type analogues, so structural reconstruction remains uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Re, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any multi-cation disorder issue. The structure description gives edge- and corner-sharing square pyramids but no face-sharing, and the closest SS1/TS1 analogue is similarly based on edge/corner-sharing Re square pyramids. The formula is small, but exact unit-cell Z is not provided, so the atom-count criterion carries some uncertainty but no clear penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re3S4Cl is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0100 eV/atom, and the closest Re–chalcogenide–chloride analogue is also very close to the hull (SS1 / TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS1/TS1, which demonstrates a closely related triclinic P-1, 2D Re–chalcogenide–chloride sheet, while CS1/CS2/CS4 support halogen-assisted vapor transport as a practical crystal-growth route for sulfides. The most critical enabling factor is controlled halogen chemical potential, because chloride is both a required structural component and a plausible transport/mineralizing agent, as shown in CS1/CS4 and SS1/TS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among competing Re sulfides, Re chlorides, and possible over-chlorinated volatile species **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, but likely requires sealed-tube synthesis or CVT rather than open solid-state firing.
