### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff, so it is treated as near-ground-state or low-energy metastable rather than strongly unstable [Target]. CS is highly supportive because all five chemically similar rare-earth sulfides are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS1–CS5]. SS and TS are supportive but mixed because some analogues are stable while others sit at ~0.05–0.15 eV/atom [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Solid-state synthesis is penalized mainly for high temperature, vacuum/Ar control, sulfur volatility, and multi-step pressing/sealing/reannealing [CS2–CS5, TS1, TS3, TS4]. The route is chemically credible, but structural evidence from SS is insufficient for this sulfide-only route, so SS is neutral = 50 [SS1–SS5].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Binary sulfides give moderate precursor activity, and CS supports their use in rare-earth sulfide formation [CS2–CS5]. Kinetics are not prohibitive because Eu/Yb are not refractory cations, but target reconstruction from monoclinic CS analogues to Pnma YbEuS₃ adds uncertainty [Target, CS2–CS5, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth avoids the >1000 °C severity penalty in the proposed window, but still requires sealed tubes, controlled atmosphere, sulfur containment, and inert handling [CS1, SS2, SS4]. TS gives insufficient route-specific flux evidence and is assigned neutral = 50 [TS1–TS5].

**F3 — Route 2**  
Flux greatly improves ion mobility and crystal growth, with direct CS support from KI rare-earth sulfide flux and SS support from Eu-containing sulfide flux analogues [CS1, SS2, SS4]. The main kinetic uncertainty is phase selectivity because SS flux analogues report low yield and failed pure-phase attempts for LiEuAsS₃ [SS2, SS4].

**F2 — Route 3**  
CVT is penalized for sealed-vacuum operation, volatile transport species, and a required pre-synthesis step [SS1, Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient CVT-specific evidence for YbEuS₃, so CS = 50 and TS = 50 for this route [CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5].

**F3 — Route 3**  
SS1 supports single-crystal growth by chemical transport, but only for EuAsS and without detailed transport conditions [SS1]. Composition control is uncertain because the target lacks As and contains an S–S unit, making incongruent transport possible [Target, SS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized for >5 GPa operation, high temperature, capsule containment, and specialized equipment [SS5, TS2, TS4]. CS provides insufficient high-pressure evidence for chemically similar rare-earth sulfides, so CS is neutral = 50 [CS1–CS5].

**F3 — Route 4**  
High pressure can help stabilize dense chalcogen-rich phases, and YbSe₂ provides the closest Yb chalcogenide precedent [SS5, TS2]. However, TS4 shows that high-pressure sulfides can require phase recycling and polymorph control, so structural reconstruction is scored low [TS4].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is assigned because Eu/Yb size and valence mismatch are likely meaningful, while the target does not clearly exceed 40 atoms per cell or show vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence [Target, Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for YbEuS₃, so they are neutral = 50 for direct amorphization evidence [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YbEuS₃ is best interpreted as a low-energy metastable or borderline ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS set strongly supports rare-earth sulfide synthesis by conventional solid-state and flux methods, while the SS set supports Eu-containing sulfide crystal growth but often with As-containing frameworks [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur activity in a sealed flux so the S–S unit can form without sulfur loss [Target, CS1, SS2, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Eu/Yb sulfides and sulfur-rich polysulfide phases [SS2, SS4, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with KI/alkali flux growth as the best first attempt [CS1, SS2, SS4].
