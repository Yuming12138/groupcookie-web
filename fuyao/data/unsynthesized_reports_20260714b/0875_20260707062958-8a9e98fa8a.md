### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Several close SS/TS analogues are also on the hull, including Cs₂AgBiCl₆, Cs₃Sb₂Cl₉, Na₃InCl₆, and RbCdCl₃ (SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2, SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4). CS provides chemically related chlorides, but no direct K–Bi–Cl synthesis example for this exact stoichiometry, so exact phase confirmation from CS is insufficient evidence and treated as neutral for direct target existence.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses mild solution temperatures comparable to Cs₂AgBiCl₆ precipitation at 120 °C and K–Pb–Cl cooling crystallization from boiling solution (SS1/TS1, CS1). It avoids high pressure, high temperature, and specialized equipment, but BiCl₃/HCl handling is hazardous and moisture/hydrolysis control is needed [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for exact K₇Bi₃Cl₁₆ crystallization conditions means the score is not perfect.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because all ions are dissolved or complexed in chloride-rich solution; immediate precipitation is reported for Cs₂AgBiCl₆ after alkali chloride addition (SS1/TS1). Slow cooling crystallization also works for chemically similar K–metal chloride salts (CS1). The uncertainty is whether the complex R-3c K₇Bi₃Cl₁₆ ordering forms selectively rather than simpler K–Bi–Cl salts [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Slow evaporation is very mild and ambient-pressure, consistent with K₂ZnCl₄ and RbCdCl₃ single-crystal growth from aqueous solution (SS2, SS5/TS4). The main severity comes from corrosive concentrated HCl and BiCl₃ hydrolysis/toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct K₇Bi₃Cl₁₆ slow evaporation makes this a moderate-confidence route.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution mobility gives excellent kinetic access, and slow evaporation analogues show high-quality single-crystal formation from simple chloride salts (SS2, SS5/TS4). However, slower evaporation may also permit hydration, hydrolysis, or competing stoichiometries; related chlorides can decompose in moist air (CS5, SS3/TS2). The target’s many inequivalent sites make ordering slower and more sensitive than simpler analogues [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The sealed flux route avoids >1000 °C temperatures, unlike some high-temperature Bridgman halide examples, but it requires sealed ampoules and volatile BiCl₃ management [Reasonable Inference; TS5 shows sealed ampoule halide growth]. Melt growth is demonstrated for Na₃InCl₆ using a Bridgman furnace (SS4/TS3). The route is therefore more severe than solution synthesis but less extreme than high-pressure synthesis.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten chloride media should provide good mass transport, and melt/Bridgman growth is successful for Na₃InCl₆ (SS4/TS3). The drawback is phase control: Na₃InCl₆ has incongruent melting issues (SS4/TS3), and K₇Bi₃Cl₁₆ may have competing KCl/BiCl₃-derived phases [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is likely essential.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Anhydrous sealed-tube chlorination follows the severe but proven Pu chloride synthesis conditions using Cl₂ pressure, dry-box handling, autoclaves, and 350 °C holds (CS2, CS4). For Bi(III), the redox need for Cl₂ is weaker, so use of high Cl₂ pressure is optional and only partially transferable [Reasonable Inference]. Still, toxic gas, sealed pressure, and dry handling lower the score.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Anhydrous chlorides react at moderate temperature in CS2/CS4, so kinetics should be reasonable compared with oxide solid-state routes. However, CS2/CS4 require several days and careful atmosphere control, indicating nontrivial kinetics and phase control. The target’s complex framework likely needs slow annealing/cooling for K/Bi/Cl ordering [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a complex cell with six inequivalent K sites, three Bi sites, and nine Cl sites, and the structure contains corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. These features trigger all three amorphization/structural-complexity checks. The high F4 penalty does not mean the phase is thermodynamically unstable; rather, it indicates that kinetic trapping, disorder, or glassy/poorly crystalline products are plausible if crystallization is too fast [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₇Bi₃Cl₁₆ is likely a **ground-state phase** because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support alkali/metal chloride synthesis by solution, evaporation, precipitation, and melt routes, with the closest practical precedent being Bi-containing Cs₂AgBiCl₆ precipitation from hot concentrated HCl (SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is high chloride activity in solution, which should stabilize Bi–Cl coordination and suppress hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference; supported by concentrated HCl use in CS5, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target’s complex R-3c structure with many inequivalent sites and mixed polyhedral connectivity may compete with simpler K–Bi–Cl salts or hydrated/hydrolyzed phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is quite feasible, especially by hot HCl crystallization, but it will likely require careful stoichiometry and slow crystallization.
