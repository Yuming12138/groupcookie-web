### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.028 eV/atom falls in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom category, giving a high base score [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull distances are experimentally accessible: K₃NiO₂ at 0.035 eV/atom [TS1], RbNaO at 0.012 eV/atom [TS2], and KNaO at 0.015 eV/atom [TS3]. CS and TS therefore provide strong support, while SS provides moderate support because the structurally similar entries are not always chemically identical [SS1–SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that RbKO is metastable rather than exactly on the hull, so competing Rb₂O/K₂O or other alkali oxide phases may form [Target, TS2–TS3].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, around 350–500 °C, consistent with RbNaO/KNaO/RbLiO syntheses [CS1–CS3, SS4, TS2–TS3]. No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required in the relevant analogues [CS1–CS3]. The deductions are for sealed-system volatile/alkali oxide handling and air-sensitive Rb₂O/K₂O precursors, both supported by the repeated sealed Ar ampoule use and hygroscopicity notes [CS2, CS3, TS3]. Uncertainty is mainly whether RbKO adopts the target 2D polymorph rather than another mixed alkali oxide arrangement [CS1, CS3, SS4].

#### F3 — Route 1

The reaction kinetics should be favorable because the cations are large alkalis and analogues form at modest temperatures [CS1–CS3, SS4]. However, oxide powders are not as kinetically active as molecular nitrate/acetate routes, and alkali oxide handling limits intimate mixing [CS1–CS3]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the closest chemical analogues RbNaO/KNaO are Matlockite-type, while the target is layered P2₁/m; RbLiO gives better structural support as a 2D sheet compound [CS1–CS3, SS4]. Long anneals in RbLiO/KNaO indicate ordering may be slow [CS2, CS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains low in thermal severity because the closest 2D RbLiO analogue uses 773 K/500 °C for 30 d [CS2, SS4], and KNaO crystal growth uses 740 K/467 °C for up to 60 d [CS3, TS3]. It requires specialized but standard sealed Ag-bomb/quartz equipment, as used for RbLiO and other alkali oxides [CS2, CS5, SS4]. The main deductions are for volatile/sealed alkali oxide handling and air-sensitive precursors [CS2, CS3, TS3]. The route is not penalized for high pressure or extreme temperature because none is used in the cited analogues [CS2, CS5].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are slightly better than Route 1 for obtaining the target because RbLiO has a very similar 2D sheet motif with 3-coordinate Rb and was made by long Ag-bomb annealing [CS2, SS4]. Long annealing improves cation ordering and crystal growth, as shown by 30 d for RbLiO and up to 60 d for KNaO [CS2, CS3, TS3]. The precursor activity is still limited by use of binary oxides rather than molecular precursors [CS2, CS3]. The remaining uncertainty is whether Rb/K size similarity causes disorder or a different stacking variant [Reasonable Inference from target Rb/K mixed alkali topology].

#### F2 — Route 3

Direct oxidation benefits from low nominal temperatures, since Cs₃O was prepared by direct Cs + O₂ reaction and later heated to 170 °C [SS1]. However, it requires precise oxygen chemical potential control to avoid higher oxides, explicitly noted in the Cs oxide route [SS1]. It also uses extremely air-sensitive and volatile Rb/K metals, so sealed handling is mandatory [SS1, SS2]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this route to RbKO and are treated as neutral for route transferability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Metal + oxygen reactions are highly active kinetically, and alkali metals provide fast transport pathways [SS1]. The major kinetic and phase-control problem is not diffusion, but selective formation of the mixed RbKO phase rather than separate oxides, peroxides, superoxides, or suboxides; Cs₃O synthesis had to manage higher oxide decomposition [SS1]. There is insufficient CS/TS evidence for direct metal oxidation to RbKO, so the structural reconstruction score is low-neutral despite high chemical reactivity. This route is therefore plausible but less recommended than sealed binary-oxide annealing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has simple Rb/K/O chemistry and a small described crystallographic basis, so the >40 atom-count criterion is not triggered [Target]. Rb and K are chemically similar alkali cations, and the target bond lengths differ modestly, suggesting low size-frustration risk [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The connectivity is described as edge-sharing OK₃Rb₃ octahedra rather than mixed face/edge/corner sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not directly discuss amorphization in RbKO; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on amorphous competition, so those sources are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbKO is likely a **slightly metastable but experimentally accessible** phase because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom [Target], comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as K₃NiO₂ and KNaO [TS1, TS3]. The CS and TS datasets strongly support sealed solid-state alkali oxide synthesis, especially RbNaO and KNaO from binary oxides [CS1, CS3, TS2, TS3]. The SS dataset gives the key structural enabling analogy: RbLiO is a 2D sheet compound with 3-coordinate Rb and is made by long sealed Ag-bomb annealing [CS2, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is high alkali-ion mobility at moderate temperature in sealed inert systems [CS1–CS3, SS4]. The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control under extreme moisture sensitivity and possible Rb/K volatility or competing alkali oxide formation [CS2, CS3, SS1, TS3].
