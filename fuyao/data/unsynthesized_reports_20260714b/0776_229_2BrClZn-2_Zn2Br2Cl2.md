### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0110 eV/atom, so it lies in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic window by the required rubric. TS analogues are especially supportive because TS1–TS5 all have low E_hull values near 0.007–0.019 eV/atom and were synthesized by melt, solid-state, or solid-state/vapor-type routes. CS is somewhat mixed because CS3 has a very high E_hull, but CS1, CS4, and CS5 show stable or near-stable chemically related compounds. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed ZnBrCl phase is a low-temperature ordered mixed-halide phase or only metastable relative to disordered/binary zinc halides.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule mixed-halide melt/anneal

The temperature is moderate because TS2 and SS1/SS2 synthesize related halides well below 1000 °C. The severity penalty comes from controlled dry Ar/vacuum handling, sealed ampoules, and hygroscopic anhydrous halides, all consistent with TS2 and CS1. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed, so high-pressure severity is scored as not applicable. Uncertainty remains in the exact annealing temperature because no reference reports ZnBrCl directly.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule mixed-halide melt/anneal

Kinetics should be favorable because anhydrous halide melts are much more reactive than refractory oxides, as shown by TS2 and SS1/SS2. Diffusion is aided by partial or full melting, and analogues crystallize at modest temperatures rather than needing cation diffusion through refractory solids. The weaker part is structural reconstruction: target ZnBrCl needs ordered two-dimensional corner-sharing ZnBr₂Cl₂ tetrahedral sheets, while TS2/SS1/SS2 support tetrahedral halide chemistry but not this exact layered Pmm2 structure. Thus F3 is good but not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 2: self-flux / zinc-halide flux growth

CS1 directly supports ZnX₂ flux growth at 550–600 °C under vacuum, which is moderate in temperature but requires sealed tubes and glovebox handling. The route incurs deductions for vacuum/controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed-system chemistry, multiple procedural steps, and air-sensitive hygroscopic halides. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any need for high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. The route is operationally moderate rather than easy because flux removal and dry handling are essential.

#### F3 — Route 2: self-flux / zinc-halide flux growth

This route receives the highest kinetic score because CS1 explicitly states excess ZnX₂ flux is advantageous for growing single crystals. The liquid flux should reduce diffusion barriers and allow Zn–Cl/Br local coordination to equilibrate, consistent with SS1/SS2 halide melt crystallizations. Structural reconstruction is still a concern because none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrate ordered ZnBrCl sheets, but flux growth offers the best opportunity for halide ordering. Remaining uncertainty is whether flux conditions favor the mixed phase or simply recrystallize ZnCl₂/ZnBr₂-rich phases.

#### F2 — Route 3: closed-ampoule vapor transport / sublimation crystallization

CS4 and SS3 support sealed vacuum ampoule vapor/sublimation growth of halide materials at moderate temperatures, so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty is applied. The route is penalized for vacuum handling, volatile sealed ampoule operation, multi-step gradient control, and hygroscopic halide precursors. There is insufficient evidence from TS for exact vapor-transport conditions for ZnBrCl, so those details are inferred. The severity is similar to the other routes, but experimental sensitivity is higher.

#### F3 — Route 3: closed-ampoule vapor transport / sublimation crystallization

Vapor availability is plausible because SS3 demonstrates sublimation growth of volatile metal halides and CS4 demonstrates halide crystal formation in a sealed temperature gradient. Crystallization is only moderately favorable because no reference shows ZnCl₂/ZnBr₂ co-transport into a stoichiometric mixed zinc halide. Composition control is the main kinetic limitation: differential transport of chloride and bromide species could shift the product away from ZnBrCl. TS5 supports that mixed halides can form in solid-state halogen exchange, but it provides insufficient condition detail for robust scoring.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered from the target structure. The described structure is a simple two-dimensional sheet of corner-sharing ZnBr₂Cl₂ tetrahedra, with no reported edge/face-sharing coexistence and no evidence for a very large unit cell. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization evidence for ZnBrCl, so their source-specific scores are neutral for this factor. Based on the target topology, amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZnBrCl is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, which is comparable to many synthesized TS analogues. The strongest synthesis support comes from TS2, which directly involves anhydrous ZnBr₂/ZnCl₂ zinc-halide chemistry, and from CS1, which shows ZnX₂ flux methods are effective for zinc halide crystal growth. The most critical enabling factor is the use of molten zinc halides or self-flux to overcome diffusion barriers and allow halide ordering. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining exact mixed Cl/Br stoichiometry while avoiding hygroscopic degradation, volatilization, or crystallization of binary ZnCl₂/ZnBr₂-rich phases. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with flux growth the best first route.
