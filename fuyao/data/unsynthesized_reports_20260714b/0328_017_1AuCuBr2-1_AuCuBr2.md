### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0180 eV/atom, which is within the normally synthesizable near-hull range, giving 95/100 by the required rubric. This is reinforced by TS1, where AuBr has E_hull = 0.0178 eV/atom, and by TS3/TS4 CuHgSX analogues at ~0.021–0.024 eV/atom. CS also supports the conclusion because several chemically similar halide/chalcohalide phases are at or near the hull, including CS1 at 0.0, CS3 at 0.0128, and CS5 at 0.0041 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that near-hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or room-temperature persistence, as SS1–SS3 show for Au halides.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 sealed solid-state.**  
The severity score is reduced mainly because vacuum sealed tubes are required, consistent with TS3/TS4 and CS2/CS4, and because AuBr/Au2Br6 handling is multi-step and phase-sensitive in SS1/SS2. Kinetics are moderate: CuX halides react successfully at modest temperature in TS3/TS4, but AuBr formation/decomposition is sluggish and requires long times in SS1/SS2. Structurally, target AuCuBr2 differs from the 1D AuBr analogues in SS1/SS2, so ordering into a 2D sheet likely requires reconstruction. CS provides general sealed-tube support, but insufficient evidence from CS for Au–Cu ordering specifically, so that part is neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 CVT/vapor transport.**  
The CVT route is plausible because SS3 reports AuCl single-crystal vapor transport, SS1/SS2 report AuBr formation from Au2Br6 under dry/vacuum conditions, CS3 reports CVT crystal growth, and TS5 reports gas-phase growth in evacuated ampoules. The route is penalized for halogen/vacuum handling, volatile precursors, and toxic Br2 or Au bromides, all supported by SS1–SS3 and CS4. Kinetically, vapor availability is acceptable for Au halides but composition control is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates simultaneous Au and Cu bromide transport into a ternary phase. The key uncertainty is transport-rate mismatch between Au bromide species and CuBr [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal HBr.**  
This route receives the best severity score because SS4/TS2 demonstrate CuHgSBr formation at ~397 °C in concentrated HBr without ultra-high temperature, high pressure, or vacuum; the main penalties are corrosive/volatile HBr and hazardous Au bromides. Kinetics are favorable because the HBr medium provides solution-phase transport, and SS4/TS2 report quantitative reaction/no observed side products for a Cu-containing bromide framework. The structural transfer is not exact, because CuHgSBr contains Hg/S as well as Cu/Br, while the target is AuCuBr2; this is the main uncertainty. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Au–Cu–Br formation, so its hydrothermal relevance is neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 molten CuBr flux.**  
The flux route is supported by CS5 and SS5, where sealed-ampoule melt/Bridgman methods yield crystalline halide/chalcohalide materials, and by CS4 where bromide solid-state synthesis occurs at elevated temperature in sealed tubes. Severity is moderate because evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile bromides, and hazardous Au/Br species are expected. Kinetically, a liquid halide flux should enhance diffusion, but phase purity may be difficult because SS5 reports phase mixtures and SS1/SS2 report AuBr phase-control problems. No TS source directly supports flux growth of AuCuBr2, so that specific transfer is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has chemically distinct Au and Cu sites with different bond lengths, which suggests some size/coordination mismatch and triggers C1 [target description; Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing octahedra but not face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered under the required strict criterion. There is no evidence that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2, treated neutral. Crystalline analogues in SS1/SS2 and SS4/TS2 show that related halides can crystallize, but sluggish AuBr transitions in SS1/SS2 keep the F4 score at 80 rather than >90.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuCuBr2 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0180 eV/atom, comparable to synthesizable TS analogues such as AuBr and CuHgSX phases. The analogue support is good thermodynamically and chemically, but incomplete structurally: AuBr analogues strongly support Au–Br chemistry (SS1–SS3), while Cu bromide framework analogues support Cu–Br synthesis routes (SS4/TS2–TS4), yet no provided source directly reports Au–Cu–Br ternary formation. The most important enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature bromide chemistry in sealed tubes or HBr media, which avoids high-temperature decomposition and is supported by SS4/TS2 and TS3/TS4. The most important limiting factor is Au halide redox/phase instability, since SS1–SS3 show sluggish, metastable, or disproportionating Au halide behavior. Overall, the target appears synthesizable, with hydrothermal HBr or carefully controlled sealed-ampoule routes being the most credible starting points.
