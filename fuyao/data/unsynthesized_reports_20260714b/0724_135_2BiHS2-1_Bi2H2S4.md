### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, so it falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability stability range [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues also cluster close to the hull, with examples near 0.0058–0.012 eV/atom [TS1, TS3, TS5], and structurally similar sulfides include multiple hull-stable examples [SS1–SS4]. CS is less decisive because one chemically similar material has E_hull ≈0.104 eV/atom while others are stable or nearly stable [CS1–CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that convex-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility of a protonated sulfide sheet [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity penalty because it uses H₂S/HS⁻ chemistry and sealed vessels, both directly supported by the hydrothermal Rb₂Sb₄S₇ route [SS1]. The temperature itself is mild, matching 140–160 °C analogues [SS1, TS1, CS1], so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty is applied. CS provides useful signal that H-containing heavy-element phases crystallize under sealed aqueous conditions [CS1, CS2]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for the exact H–S bond retention in BiHS₂, so TS contributes neutral-to-supportive but not definitive evidence.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is favorable because solution/hydrothermal media provide high precursor mobility, as seen in low-temperature sulfide crystallization [SS1, TS1]. The target requires forming a BiS₆ layered sheet with S–H termination, which is a reconstruction from Bi₂S₃ but is chemically plausible in HS⁻ media [SS1, Reasonable Inference]. CS supports solution crystallization of H-containing phases but does not provide direct Bi–S chemistry, so CS is supportive for hydrogen incorporation but insufficient for BiHS₂-specific kinetics [CS1, CS2].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature because similar sealed-tube Bi sulfides form at 923 K and related chalcogenides at 400–430 °C [SS2, TS5]. It still requires vacuum sealing and volatile S/H₂S-containing species, which adds severity [SS2, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for solid-state synthesis of a Bi–S–H phase and is therefore treated as neutral for this route. The main uncertainty is whether H can be retained as an S–H bond at 300–450 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state Bi₂S₃-based synthesis has precedent for Bi sulfides [SS2], but protonated sulfide formation likely has slower ordering kinetics than solution growth [Reasonable Inference]. Thermodynamic analogues show multiday ampoule reactions and sometimes impurity control requirements [TS2, TS5], supporting only moderate kinetics. SS2 supports formation of Bi–S frameworks but not hydrogen incorporation, so the structural-reconstruction score is limited. CS is insufficient for solid-state BiHS₂ kinetics and is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures similar to flux-grown sulfide analogues at 500 °C [SS3, SS5]. The penalties come from vacuum/N₂ handling, sulfide flux air sensitivity, volatile sulfur/hydrosulfide species, and multistep flux removal [SS3, SS5]. TS supports sealed-tube sulfide chemistry under controlled conditions [TS2, TS5], but does not specifically support hydrosulfide flux protonation. CS provides insufficient evidence for alkali hydrosulfide flux growth and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth is kinetically favorable because it reduces diffusion limitations and has produced complex sulfide crystals in sealed ampoules [SS3, SS5]. However, the target has no alkali cation, so avoiding alkali incorporation or removing it by post-growth protonation is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives strong support for sulfide framework crystallization [SS3, SS5], while TS supports moderate-temperature sealed chalcogenide synthesis [TS5]. CS is insufficient for this flux mechanism and is neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation type, Bi, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The described structure has one Bi site, one H site, and two S sites, suggesting a relatively simple cell rather than a >40 atom complex framework, though the exact cell contents are not explicitly stated [Target]. Mixed corner- and edge-sharing BiS₆ octahedra are present [Target], which raises amorphization or defect risk slightly because mixed connectivity can complicate ordering [Reasonable Inference]. SS analogues with mixed polyhedral sharing can still crystallize successfully [SS2, SS4, SS5], so the risk is not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiHS₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar sulfides and thermodynamically similar chalcogenides that crystallize in sealed hydrothermal, solvothermal, flux, or ampoule conditions [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most enabling factor is the low-temperature hydrosulfide chemistry, which can simultaneously supply sulfide and protonated sulfur while avoiding H loss [SS1, Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is retention and ordering of the S–H sublattice without disproportionating to Bi₂S₃ plus H₂S or forming alkali-containing sulfide salts [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, hydrothermal hydrosulfide synthesis is the most plausible route, while solid-state and flux approaches are secondary screening routes.
