### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0090 eV/atom is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so the thermodynamic score is high. TS strongly supports this because several thermodynamic analogues are low-hull or on-hull, including BaAl₄Se₇ at 0.0186 eV/atom (TS-1), GaAlCl₄ at 0.0048 eV/atom (TS-3), and AlBi₂Se₂BrCl₄ at 0.0074 eV/atom (TS-5). SS also supports stable Al–Se tetrahedral frameworks, with LiAlSe₂, HgAl₂Se₄, and Mg₂Al₂Se₅ on the hull (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4). CS is mixed because GaTeCl is stable and highly relevant (CS-5), but several chemically ranked analogues are not close synthetic models or have high computed hull energies, so CS contributes weaker thermodynamic confidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the direct GaTeCl analogue uses only 400 °C and 7 days (CS-5), but it requires evacuated ampoules and moisture-sensitive/volatile chloride reagents (CS-5). The kinetics are favorable because Al₂Se₃ provides preformed Al–Se bonding, and AlCl₃ can act as a reactive chloride source/mineralizer [Reasonable Inference from CS-5, SS-1, SS-2]. The major uncertainty is whether AlSeCl forms cleanly rather than disproportionating to Al₂Se₃ + AlCl₃-rich phases, since no exact AlSeCl synthesis is provided. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact binary-precursor route, so TS is treated as neutral for the detailed route chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has slightly better F2 because it operates at low temperature and avoids high-temperature solid diffusion, as shown by 130 °C AlCl₃/NaCl molten-salt synthesis (SS-5) and 165 °C ionothermal synthesis (TS-5). Its F3 is high because molten or ionothermal halide media provide high ion mobility and can crystallize halogenated chalcogenide frameworks in days (SS-5, TS-5). The limiting uncertainty is structural transferability: SS-5 and TS-5 involve Bi-containing chalcogenide-halide frameworks rather than the simple AlSeCl tetrahedral sheet. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for molten-salt synthesis of AlSeCl specifically, so CS is neutral for the flux route except for the general chloride-chalcogenide feasibility implied by GaTeCl (CS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because direct elemental solid-state syntheses of related chalcogenides often require high temperature and long annealing, such as 800–850 °C for 2–4 weeks (CS-3), 800 °C for 10 days (SS-2), or 1000 °C for 12 h (SS-4). Its kinetics are weaker because elemental Al may have oxide passivation and the reaction must construct the Al–Se–Cl sheet from scratch [Reasonable Inference]. The route is still plausible because direct sealed-tube elemental reactions are repeatedly successful for chalcogenides (CS-3, SS-2, SS-4), but volatile Se/AlCl₃ and chlorine chemical potential make stoichiometry control difficult. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for this exact elemental Al–Se–Cl route, so TS is neutral for the route-specific kinetic assessment.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple corner-sharing AlSe₃Cl tetrahedral sheet, which is less prone to amorphization than structures with mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing networks [Reasonable Inference from target description]. SS analogues show that AlSe₄ tetrahedral frameworks crystallize readily in several compounds (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, SS-4), supporting low amorphization risk. CS-5 also supports crystallization of a closely related layered GaTeCl tetrahedral framework. Direct evidence from CS/SS/TS on target unit-cell atom count is insufficient, but the target composition and simple Pmn2₁ description suggest a small cell, so no C2 penalty is applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlSeCl is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0090 eV/atom, and TS analogues show several similarly low-hull compounds are experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). The strongest direct support comes from GaTeCl, which has the same type of layered MChCl tetrahedral chemistry and is synthesized from GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ at 400 °C (CS-5). Structurally, many Al–Se tetrahedral frameworks are known from sealed-tube solid-state synthesis (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, SS-4), which supports the Al–Se bonding motif. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficient chloride chemical potential while avoiding hydrolysis, best achieved by dry AlCl₃-containing sealed or molten-salt media (CS-5, SS-5, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is volatile/corrosive AlCl₃ and possible competing Al₂Se₃/AlCl₃-derived phases, requiring tight stoichiometry and ampoule control.
