### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase. This is strongly supported by analogous stable compounds: chemically similar Te–Br and Te–chalcogen phases are on the hull in CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, and CS-5, and structurally/thermodynamically similar layered Hf/Zr/Ta tellurides are also at or near the hull in SS-1/TS-1, SS-4/TS-4, TS-5, and SS-5. Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee a broad synthesis window, as SS-1/TS-1 reports side products when stoichiometry deviates.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high external pressure, which is favorable; comparable direct syntheses occur at 480–550 °C in CS-3 and SS-1/TS-1. The main severity penalties come from vacuum sealed ampoules, volatile Br₂ or HfBr₄, and toxic/corrosive brominating reagents, all inferred from Br₂ use in CS-3 and SS-2/TS-2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an air-stable open-vessel synthesis, so vacuum/sealing is treated as necessary.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The refractory nature of Hf makes direct solid-state diffusion slow [Reasonable Inference], although halogen-assisted reactions in CS-3 and SS-1/TS-1 show that metal–chalcogen–halogen phases can form well below metal melting points. The target has structural similarity to HfGeTe₄ with 8-coordinate Hf and comparable Hf–Te distances (SS-4/TS-4), which improves the reconstruction score. However, long anneals in CS-1 and SS-1/TS-1 suggest that ordering and phase equilibration are not instantaneous.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT uses moderate temperatures, supported by 500–460 °C Br₂ CVT in SS-2/TS-2 and 215–195 °C Hf–Te–Cl vapor growth in SS-3/TS-3. The severity remains moderate because it requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile halide precursors, dry handling, and a controlled thermal gradient. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports ambient-pressure open-system CVT for such bromides, so sealed conditions are assumed.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because volatile halide species can transport Hf/Te/Br and reduce reliance on bulk diffusion, consistent with SS-2/TS-2 and SS-3/TS-3. Hf-specific vapor chemistry is supported by the Hf(Te₂Cl₃)₂ analogue in SS-3/TS-3. The main uncertainty is structural selectivity: SS-3/TS-3 gives a 1D chloride-rich Hf–Te–Cl phase rather than the exact 2D bromide target.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth operates at accessible temperatures comparable to HfGeTe₄ and ZrGeTe₄ syntheses at 973 K in SS-4/TS-4 and TS-5. It still requires sealed vacuum ampoules, volatile bromine chemistry, and post-growth flux removal, as flux workflows in CS-2, CS-4, and SS-4/TS-4 involve multiple steps. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms water stability of Hf(TeBr)₂, so flux washing is an added risk.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth improves kinetics by dissolving/reacting refractory Hf and Te species, as demonstrated by HfGeTe₄ formation in KCl flux (SS-4/TS-4). The layered Hf/Zr telluride analogues in SS-4/TS-4 and TS-5 are structurally encouraging because they contain 8-coordinate Hf/Zr and Te–Te units. The uncertainty is Br incorporation: no provided flux analogue directly demonstrates Hf–Te–Br formation in molten bromide/chloride flux.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered from the target description. The target structure is ordered and relatively simple, and the description does not indicate a >40-atom cell or complex coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests amorphous competing products; most analogues crystallize as sheets, ribbons, or clusters under sealed-tube/flux/CVT conditions.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(TeBr)₂ is likely a **ground-state crystalline phase**, because the target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and many chemically/structurally similar phases are also on the hull in CS/SS/TS. The analogue support is good but not perfect: CS-3 and SS-2/TS-2 support Br₂-based chalcogenide halide synthesis, while SS-3/TS-3 supports Hf–Te–halide vapor chemistry, but none reports the exact Hf–Te–Br layered phase. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport/reactivity, because Br₂/HfBr₄ chemistry can overcome the slow diffusion expected for refractory Hf. The most critical limiting factor is bromine chemical potential: too little Br may yield Hf–Te binaries, while too much Br may stabilize HfBr₄/TeBr₄-rich molecular or cluster phases. Overall, the target is experimentally plausible, with CVT/vapor-phase crystallization as the most rational first route.
