### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0320 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic window of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamic analogues near this hull energy include Cu₃SbSe₃ at 0.0346 eV/atom [TS4], CrRh₂Se₄ at 0.0432 eV/atom [TS2], and FeRh₂Se₄ at 0.0450 eV/atom [TS3], supporting plausible metastable or near-ground-state synthesis. CS evidence is mixed: CS4 is on-hull, CS1 is low-hull, but CS2 is very high-hull and chemically less relevant to extended chalcogenide sheets [CS1, CS2, CS4]. Insufficient evidence from SS alone on target thermodynamics beyond showing related omega-like structures can be stable or low-hull [SS2–SS4].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile chalcogens, repeated firings, and hazardous Se/S handling, all of which are directly or indirectly supported by sealed-tube chalcogenide syntheses [SS1, TS1–TS3, CS5]. The temperature is moderate to high but not extreme, since MnSe₂ and mixed Rh selenides are made around 520–1080 °C [SS1, TS3]. The main uncertainty is whether the target can form below 1000 °C without decomposing into binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Mn–Pt–S–Se solid-state synthesis; CS5 only supports Mn sulfide solid-state chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by Pt diffusion and the need for Mn/Pt ordering, consistent with multication selenides requiring multiple firings and grinding [TS2, TS3]. Structural motifs are favorable because CrS₂, CrSe₂, and PtSe₂ share layered edge-sharing octahedral connectivity [SS2–SS5]. However, MnSe-related systems have phase transformations and phase-control issues [SS1, TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS for comparable 2D Mn/Pt mixed chalcogenide kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth uses a sealed vacuum ampoule, volatile chalcogens, and multistep handling, as in CrSe₂ flux growth with KCl/LiCl [SS3]. The maximum temperature is moderate, around 780 °C in the analogue [SS3], so no >1000 °C deduction is applied. The route is experimentally more controlled than simple solid-state because salts must be dried and removed [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for molten-salt growth of chemically similar Mn–Pt–S–Se phases.

#### F3 — Route 2

The flux route improves kinetics because molten salt enhances mass transport and allowed phase-pure CrSe₂ single crystals at 780 °C [SS3]. The structural match is strong because CrSe₂/CrS₂ are omega-like 2D edge-sharing octahedral materials [SS2, SS3]. The penalty comes from mixed cation and mixed anion ordering, which the binary CrSe₂ analogue does not address [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides general support for multication selenide formation but not specifically flux-grown Mn/Pt ordering [TS2, TS3].

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT/mineralizer growth requires an evacuated ampoule, volatile I₂, and volatile S/Se, which increases severity [CS1, SS2]. Temperatures can remain below 1000 °C by analogy to iodine-assisted crystallization and sealed-tube chalcogenide growth [SS2, TS2, TS3]. The route likely requires a pre-reacted source, making it multistep [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for iodine transport specifically; TS supports sealed-tube multication chalcogenide synthesis but not transport chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetics are favorable because vapor transport bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion and iodine is known to promote crystallization in a chalcogenide reaction [SS2] and to transport Se/I-containing material in a thermal gradient [CS1]. This is especially useful for growing crystalline layered products rather than merely sintered powders [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting uncertainty is co-transport of Mn, Pt, S, and Se without segregation into MnI₂, Pt metal, PtSe₂, or binary chalcogenides [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct quaternary Mn–Pt–S–Se CVT.

#### F2 — Route 4

The solution/sonochemical route has mild initial conditions, supported by PtSe₂ sonochemical synthesis at 35 °C followed by 500 °C treatment [SS4, SS5]. The final chalcogenizing anneal still requires inert/vacuum handling and volatile S/Se containment [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1]. It uses multiple wet-chemical steps and potentially toxic/corrosive precursors such as H₂PtCl₆ and Se [SS4]. CS3 shows solution routes for molecular Mo–S–N–Cl clusters but does not support direct formation of extended Mn/Pt chalcogenide sheets.

#### F3 — Route 4

Precursor activity is high because solution processing can intimately mix Mn, Pt, S, and Se at nanoscale [Reasonable Inference], and PtSe₂ can form through sonochemical processing [SS4]. #### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains distinct Mn and Pt octahedra; the likely cation size/chemistry mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The target and close SS analogues are dominated by edge-sharing octahedral sheets, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed vertex/edge/face frameworks [SS2–SS5]. The unit-cell atom-count criterion cannot be conclusively assessed from CS/SS/TS, so it is treated as non-triggering but uncertain. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂PtSeS₅ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0320 eV/atom, comparable to synthesizable thermodynamic analogues in TS2–TS4. The strongest support comes from structurally similar 2D omega-like edge-sharing octahedral chalcogenides, especially CrS₂, CrSe₂, and PtSe₂ [SS2–SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the shared layered edge-sharing octahedral motif, which suggests a realistic crystallization pathway [SS2–SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering: Mn/Pt cation ordering and S/Se anion control may be difficult, as multication selenides often need repeated firings and phase-purity optimization [TS2, TS3]. The best practical route is iodine-assisted sealed-tube growth after pre-reaction, because it combines solid-state accessibility with enhanced crystallization and mass transport [CS1, SS2].
