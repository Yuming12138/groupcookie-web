### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, placing it in the high-probability synthesis range under the requested rubric. The closest structural analogue ZrCl₂ is reported at E_hull = 0 in SS1, which supports the plausibility of a group-4 layered dichloride. TS2 has a thermodynamically similar reduced halide with E_hull ≈ 0.0455 eV/atom, close to the target; CS provides insufficient direct HfCl₂ evidence and is treated as neutral.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids high pressure and avoids >1000 °C temperatures, consistent with SS1 where ZrCl₂ is made at 600–750 °C. The main penalties are sealed reactive chloride handling, volatile HfCl₄ inferred from ZrCl₄ autogenous pressure in SS1, and multi-step Ta/silica tube preparation from SS1. The uncertainty is whether HfCl₄ transport chemistry reproduces ZrCl₄ transport without forming other Hf chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic score is moderate because SS1 directly demonstrates layered ZrCl₂ crystallization by sealed-tube CVT, but the same source reports slow 4–10 week equilibration, substoichiometry, polytypes, and intergrowths. SS4/TS3 further show reduced metal-halide transport can be slow and low-yield. The vapor pathway is enabling, but composition control is likely narrow [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS4/TS3].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Metallothermic reduction uses moderate temperatures of 600–900 °C and multi-day holds supported by SS2, SS5, and TS1, so it avoids high-pressure and ultrahigh-temperature penalties. It still requires air-sensitive alkali metals, anhydrous chlorides, sealed Ta ampoules, and inert handling, all indicated by SS2/SS5/TS1. The main uncertainty is whether alkali reduction cleanly stops at HfCl₂ rather than producing Hf metal or ternary alkali hafnium chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
SS2/SS5/TS1 show MCl₂ products can form by Li/Na/K reduction in days, which supports reasonable precursor reactivity. However, those data are for rare-earth dichlorides, not layered HfCl₂, so the structural reconstruction score is lower. Hf metal/chloride redox chemistry may be kinetically and thermodynamically less forgiving than lanthanide chloride reduction [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Direct sealed-tube equilibration uses similar temperature and equipment demands to Route 1, based mainly on SS1 and TS2. It avoids external high pressure but still requires sealed Ta tubing, anhydrous chlorides, volatile chloride pressure, and likely multi-step handling. The severity is therefore similar to Route 1.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The strongest favorable point is that SS1 shows the same family of ZrCl₂ phases can be reached by isothermal equilibration. The unfavorable point is that Hf metal is refractory and solid-state diffusion can be slow, while SS1 and SS4/TS3 both emphasize long reaction times in reduced halide systems. It may form the correct composition, but crystal quality and phase purity may be worse than in a deliberate CVT gradient [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple binary with one cation and edge-sharing HfCl₆ octahedra, so it does not trigger the cation-radius, large-cell, or mixed-connectivity checks. SS1 supports that an analogous layered group-4 dichloride can form a crystalline phase rather than amorphizing. Remaining uncertainty is mostly polytypism/disorder rather than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfCl₂ at E_hull = 0.044 eV/atom is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a highly unstable compound. The best support comes from SS1, because ZrCl₂ is the closest structural and chemical analogue and is made by sealed-tube CVT/isothermal equilibration in the same layered MCl₂ family. TS analogues support the general feasibility of reduced metal halides near this thermodynamic stability, while CS offers limited direct signal because the chemically similar set is dominated by Tb chloride/chalcogenide chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is sealed halide vapor transport with controlled Hf/HfCl₄ chemical potential. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among low-valent Hf chlorides, polytypes, and possible off-stoichiometric or oxychloride impurities.
