### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the high-score cutoff and is consistent with isolable metastable phases **[Target]**. Thermodynamic analogues include CrInBr₃ with E_hull = 0.0499958 eV/atom and successful synthesis by molten-salt ampoule methods [TS-2], and AgBrPbO at 0.0626 eV/atom synthesized by controlled solid-state sintering [TS-1]. CS also includes a 1D InSe polymorph at 0.0367 eV/atom made under high pressure [CS-3], suggesting that low-dimensional In compounds near this metastability scale can exist. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact binary Pmmn InBr polymorph is not directly reported in the sources, so polymorph competition remains possible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, consistent with sealed halide syntheses at 250–450 °C [SS-4] and bromide ampoule syntheses at 450 °C [TS-2]. The main penalties are vacuum/sealed operation and volatile/toxic bromine **[Reasonable Inference]**, with sealed ampoules supported by Ga iodide and In–Br analogue syntheses [SS-4, SS-1, TS-2]. CS supports evacuated ampoule solid-state chemistry for In compounds [CS-1, CS-5], but gives no direct InBr elemental bromination route, so **insufficient evidence from CS** for exact route reliability. Overall severity is moderate rather than extreme because no high pressure or >1000 °C step is required [SS-1, SS-4, TS-2].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct elemental reaction should be kinetically active because Br₂ reacts readily with metals **[Reasonable Inference]**, and Ga + I₂ halide formation occurs in sealed ampoules [SS-4]. Diffusion barriers should be low because In is low-melting and halogen transport is vapor-assisted **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with low-temperature halide formation [SS-4, TS-2]. The uncertainty is structural: the exact Pmmn ribbon must nucleate from the melt/vapor reaction, and no CS/SS/TS source confirms direct binary InBr crystallization. Therefore kinetics are good but not as reliable as the InBr₃ flux/comproportionation route.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is directly aligned with 450–475 °C InBr₃ molten-salt syntheses [SS-1, SS-3, TS-2], so temperature severity is low. Penalties arise from vacuum ampoules, volatile/corrosive InBr₃, precursor purification, and air/moisture sensitivity, all indicated for In–Br ternary bromides [SS-1, TS-2]. This route avoids high pressure, unlike the high-pressure 1D InSe polymorph route [CS-3]. The condition score is therefore lower than Route 1 because the precursor-handling burden is higher, even though the chemical pathway is better supported.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The InBr₃ melt is an active transport/reaction medium in MnInBr₃, CrInBr₃, and Zr(InBr₃)₂ syntheses [SS-1, SS-3, TS-2]. Since those analogues form at only 450–475 °C, ion diffusion should be flux-mediated rather than solid-state-limited [SS-1, SS-3, TS-2]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because MnInBr₃ and CrInBr₃ contain 1D bromide ribbons with 2-coordinate In–Br units [SS-1, TS-2], close to the target’s 1D InBr ribbon description. The remaining uncertainty is that removing transition-metal octahedra to obtain binary Pmmn InBr is a compositional simplification but not a directly demonstrated topology-preserving transformation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperature, following InCl deposition at −228 °C to room temperature [CS-4, SS-2] and low-temperature TlBr deposition [SS-5]. However, it requires UHV/vacuum deposition equipment and careful vapor-pressure/substrate-temperature control [CS-4, SS-2]. The InCl analogue also requires phase-transition thermal cycling, which adds processing complexity [CS-4, SS-2]. SS/CS support thin-film halide deposition, but TS provides no direct thin-film signal for InBr, so **insufficient evidence from TS** for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor availability is plausible because InCl and TlBr-like halides are evaporated/deposited in the analogues [CS-4, SS-2, SS-5]. Crystallization is less favorable because InCl forms metastable/amorphous deposits and requires narrow low-temperature crystallization/annealing windows [CS-4, SS-2]. Composition control may be challenging due to volatility and possible halogen loss **[Reasonable Inference]**, with phase-control difficulty supported by InCl metastable phase behavior [CS-4, SS-2]. Thus Route 3 is useful for exploratory thin films but weaker for bulk target synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a binary compound with one cation and simple L-shaped two-coordinate In–Br ribbons **[Target]**, so C1 and C3 are not triggered. No source reports a unit cell atom count >40 for the target; therefore C2 is not triggered, but **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for exact unit-cell-size-based amorphization. Structurally similar 1D bromide systems can crystallize as needles/ribbons under slow cooling [SS-1, TS-2], supporting low amorphization tendency. The main uncertainty is polymorph selection rather than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target InBr Pmmn phase is best viewed as a borderline metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff but not clearly a deep ground state **[Target]**. The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar In–Br ribbon compounds synthesized from InBr₃ melts at 450 °C, especially MnInBr₃ and CrInBr₃ [SS-1, TS-2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of an InBr₃ molten-salt/comproportionation chemistry that can transport bromide and indium at moderate temperature [SS-1, SS-3, TS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is control of In(I) stoichiometry and avoidance of moisture/Br-rich side phases, because related In–Br compounds are extremely air/humidity sensitive and require sealed ampoules [SS-1, TS-2]. Thin-film deposition is plausible but less reliable because InCl analogues show metastable phase windows and demanding UHV conditions [CS-4, SS-2].
