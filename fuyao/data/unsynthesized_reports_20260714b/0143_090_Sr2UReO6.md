### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom falls at the high end of the most favorable rubric bin, so it is not predicted as a deeply metastable phase. CS analogues Ba₂UNiO₆, Ba₂UZnO₆, Ba₂UMgO₆, and Ba₂UCdO₆ are on the hull, and Ba₂UCuO₆ is only slightly above hull, supporting the general stability of U-containing double perovskites (CS-1–CS-5). SS/TS include synthesized perovskites with similar or higher E_hull values, such as CaNbO₃ and LaTiO₃, indicating that ~0.05 eV/atom phases can be experimentally accessible (SS-3; TS-1, TS-5). The main uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity does not directly encode Re redox chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: controlled-atmosphere ceramic route

The route is penalized for >1000 °C treatment, controlled atmosphere, multiple grinding/annealing steps, and toxic U/Re precursors; these conditions are consistent with CS ceramic syntheses requiring 900–1100 °C and long/repeated firing, and SS/TS reduced perovskites requiring vacuum or Ar-sealed systems (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5; SS-1–SS-4; TS-1–TS-2). Kinetically, dry oxide/carbonate reaction is slow because U/Re oxides are refractory and B-site ordering in a double perovskite requires long-range cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues show success by repeated grinding, but also imply slow reaction and phase-purity challenges (CS-1, CS-2). The uncertainty is whether Sr₂UReO₆ can be obtained before Re oxidation or competing Sr–U/Sr–Re oxides dominate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux route

The flux route receives the best score because CS-3 directly demonstrates U perovskite-related crystal growth from molten carbonate flux at 950–1150 °C with a 48 h hold and slow cooling. Flux-assisted transport improves kinetics relative to dry ceramic reaction, so precursor activity and diffusion scores are higher than for Route 1 (CS-3). However, the target likely requires lower oxygen fugacity than the air-grown CS-3 Ba₂MUO₆ analogues, so adapting the flux to sealed inert or self-buffered conditions is a significant inferred modification [Reasonable Inference; SS-1/SS-2/TS-2]. The principal uncertainty is whether carbonate flux is too oxidizing for Re(IV)/U(IV), making halide flux under Ar a safer but less directly evidenced variant.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure route

High-pressure synthesis is heavily penalized because SS-5 uses ~80 kbar and specialized belt apparatus, and the proposed route would similarly require >5 GPa. The kinetic score is moderate because high pressure can stabilize dense perovskite frameworks, but short HP dwell times may not allow full U/Re ordering [Reasonable Inference; SS-5]. SS/TS high-temperature sealed-tube niobate routes show that reduced perovskites can require stringent conditions and phase windows (SS-3, SS-4; TS-1). The uncertainty is that no CS analogue indicates high pressure is necessary for U double perovskites, so this route is best viewed as a fallback for metastable stabilization.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization risk: large cation size mismatch between Sr and the U/Re B-site cations, consistent with the distorted Sr coordination and tilted octahedra described for the target [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii and target structure]. The target does not show mixed edge/face/vertex octahedral connectivity; it is described as corner-sharing UO₆/ReO₆ octahedra, which lowers amorphization risk. The provided structure does not establish a unit cell above 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered. Overall, crystallization should be feasible, but cation ordering and tilt accommodation may require slow cooling or repeated annealing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂UReO₆ is likely a mildly metastable or borderline-stable oxide rather than a deeply unstable phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and synthesized SS/TS analogues exist in the same or higher metastability range (SS-3; TS-1, TS-5). CS data strongly support the existence and synthesis of U-containing double perovskite oxides, but the CS analogues mostly involve Ba and divalent transition metals under air, so they do not fully resolve Sr/Re redox issues (CS-1–CS-5). The most enabling factor is the robust perovskite-derived framework chemistry and the availability of flux/solid-state routes for U oxide perovskites (CS-1–CS-3). The most limiting factor is maintaining the correct U/Re oxidation states while avoiding volatile or competing Re oxides [Reasonable Inference, supported indirectly by SS/TS controlled-atmosphere reduced oxide syntheses]. The best practical approach is a low-pO₂ flux-assisted route, followed by a controlled-atmosphere ceramic route if powders rather than crystals are acceptable.
