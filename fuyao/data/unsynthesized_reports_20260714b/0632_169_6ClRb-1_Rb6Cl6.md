### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, placing it inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. Chemically and thermodynamically similar alkali halides such as RbBr and CsBr have low E_hull values and are experimentally accessed as polymorphs by low-temperature evaporation (CS1, CS2, TS1). SS provides stable Rb/Cs chloride-containing structures, but insufficient direct evidence that binary tetragonal RbCl is a stable bulk phase; SS is therefore treated as neutral for exact thermodynamic accessibility.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature, high pressure, toxic reagents, and multi-reagent chemistry, which is favorable (CS1, CS2, TS1). The main deduction is the need for controlled vacuum deposition and low-temperature substrate handling (CS1, CS2, TS1). The severity is therefore moderate rather than easy because specialized deposition and cryogenic/low-temperature control are required.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Vapor availability is strong because RbCl and related alkali halides are directly evaporated in the reference methods (CS1, CS2, TS1). The crystallization pathway is the limiting kinetic factor because the desired phase is likely an abnormal/metastable alkali-halide polymorph, and the references explicitly describe temperature-sensitive polymorph formation and phase-control difficulty (CS1, CS2, TS1). Composition control is comparatively favorable because single-source RbCl evaporation should maintain the 1:1 ratio [Reasonable Inference from CS/TS single-halide evaporation basis].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures similar to structurally related Rb/Cs chloride syntheses at 300–400 °C and Rb-containing ampoule reactions near 350 °C (SS1, SS3, SS5, TS4). It requires inert or vacuum sealed handling and several operational steps—drying, loading, sealing, annealing, and cooling—so deductions are applied for controlled atmosphere and multistep processing (SS1, SS5, TS4). No high pressure or highly toxic reagent is required.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
RbCl is not a refractory precursor, and related alkali chloride solids can be processed at moderate temperatures, which supports reasonable diffusion and rearrangement kinetics (SS1, SS3, SS5). However, the target 2D tetragonal structure differs significantly from normal dense alkali-halide coordination motifs, so structural reconstruction and metastable phase retention are difficult [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/CS2/TS1 phase-control warnings]. Remaining uncertainty is high because no SS example demonstrates this exact binary RbCl polymorph from bulk annealing.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, so there is no mixed-cation ionic-radius mismatch trigger [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The provided structure description does not indicate a >40-atom cell and does not show vertex + edge + face-sharing coexistence, so the listed amorphization checks are not triggered. CS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for this exact target, while SS weakly supports crystallization of related Rb/Cs chloride networks under dry controlled conditions (SS1, SS3, SS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target RbCl polymorph is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a robust bulk ground state because its E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom and the normal alkali-halide polymorph landscape is known to be temperature-sensitive (target data; CS1, CS2, TS1). The CS and TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through low-temperature evaporation, where chemically similar RbBr/CsBr and RbCl-containing alkali halide sets are handled under vacuum and can form abnormal structures (CS1, CS2, TS1). SS supports the practical handling of dry RbCl/Rb–Cl systems in ampoules, but it does not directly prove that the binary tetragonal RbCl sheet phase can be obtained in bulk (SS1, SS3, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the low E_hull combined with low-temperature kinetic trapping during deposition (target data; CS1/CS2/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the material may relax to the conventional dense RbCl structure unless deposition temperature, rate, and warm-up are tightly controlled (CS1, CS2, TS1).
