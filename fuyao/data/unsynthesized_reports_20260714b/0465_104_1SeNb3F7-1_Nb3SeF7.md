### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0040 eV/atom, so it is effectively on or very near the convex hull and receives 96/100 by the requested rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low hull values are experimentally accessible, including VNbF6 [TS1], FeNbF6 [TS2], CaTiF5 [TS3], and NaZr2TiF11 [TS4]. CS and SS support that low-hull Nb fluoride frameworks are synthesizable [CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2], but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for Se-containing niobium fluorides, so Se-specific thermodynamic confidence remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 500–575 °C, matching Nb fluoride analogue conditions rather than extreme temperatures [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2]. It still requires sealed tubes because volatile/moisture-sensitive fluorides are used in the analogues [CS1–CS3, SS1–SS2], and Se volatility adds further sealed-system need **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is penalized for sealed volatile chemistry, air-sensitive fluorides, and multiple handling/heating steps, giving 70/100.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

NbF4/NbF5 are relatively reactive fluoride precursors, as shown by NbMF6 formation near 500–540 °C [CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2]. However, Nb metal is refractory and the target requires a different 2D Nb–Se–F sheet rather than the corner-sharing NbMF6 framework in the closest analogues [CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2]. Long anneal times in analogues [CS1, CS3, SS2] indicate that ordering and diffusion may be slow, so F3 is moderate at 52/100.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, but it requires welded Nb/Ta containers, evacuated jackets, and flux handling, as in Nb oxyfluoride flux syntheses [SS3, SS4, TS5]. SnF2 is a fluoride flux/fluorinating reagent in the analogues [SS3, SS4, TS5], but its volatility/reactivity and the need for sealed equipment increase severity. The score is 55/100 because equipment and processing are more demanding than simple sealed-tube sintering.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth improves kinetic access by dissolving and transporting species, and reduced Nb oxyfluorides have been synthesized in Sn/SnF2 flux [SS3, SS4, TS5]. This supports a higher precursor activity and diffusion score than dry solid-state reaction. The uncertainty is that no source demonstrates Se incorporation in Sn/SnF2 flux; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Nb–Se–F flux crystallization limits the structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the lowest nominal temperature, based on NH4HF2 reactions at 380–450 °C [CS4]. It is still severe chemically because NH4HF2 generates reactive HF/NH3-type conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS4 requires sealed Monel ampoules [CS4]. The route is penalized for reactive fluorination chemistry, sealed volatile species, multi-step precursor preparation, and hazardous reagents.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

NH4HF2 is an active fluorinating reagent for Hf/Zr systems [CS4], so anion incorporation could be fast. However, CS4 products are ammoniated metal fluorides, not anhydrous Nb–Se–F sheets, and the reported reaction time is up to 4 weeks [CS4], indicating slow solid-state evolution. Because SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this chemical route, the target-specific kinetics remain uncertain and the route scores 53/100.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Nb as the cation, so cation-size disorder is absent **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structure is described as a crystallographically ordered 2D P3m1 sheet with three F sites and one Se site, and no evidence is given for a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral complexity. CS/SS/TS analogues include many crystalline fluoride frameworks [CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2], but they do not directly test Nb–Se–F amorphization; remaining uncertainty is mainly from mixed-anion ordering.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb3SeF7 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0040 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are reported as synthesizable [TS1–TS4]. The CS/SS/TS data strongly support sealed-tube synthesis of Nb-containing fluorides at 500–540 °C [CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2], but they provide insufficient direct evidence for selenium-containing niobium fluorides. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive Nb fluoride chemistry in sealed tubes, especially NbF4/NbF5 routes under dry Ar [CS2, CS3, SS2, TS1, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling the mixed Nb–Se–F stoichiometry while avoiding Se loss, NbF5 volatilization, and competing Nb fluorides or Nb selenides **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but not routine, with the direct sealed-tube route being the best first attempt.
