### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0030 eV/atom, so it is essentially on the convex hull and falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability category. Thermodynamically similar halides include SrBr₂ with E_hull ≈0.0012 eV/atom (TS1), SrI₂ with E_hull ≈0.0096 eV/atom (TS2), Sr–Br–Cl mixed halide with E_hull ≈0.006 eV/atom (TS3), and high-pressure BaI₂ with E_hull ≈0.0032 eV/atom (TS4). Remaining uncertainty is structural: low hull energy supports existence but does not guarantee the trigonal omega-derived polymorph is the phase selected by ambient synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 inherits moderate condition severity from CS1, SS3/SS5, TS2, and TS3: sealed ampoules, dry glovebox handling, high-temperature treatment, annealing, and slow cooling are all required. Kinetics are favorable because CaBr₂/CaI₂ are halide salts and may partially melt or sinter effectively, analogous to melt-assisted alkaline-earth halide routes in CS1 and SS3/SS5. The uncertainty is whether Br/I ordering into the P3m1 layered octahedral structure competes successfully against simpler CaBr₂/CaI₂ solid solutions or binary halide segregation.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Elemental routes are supported by SS4, CS4, and CS5, but Br₂/I₂ volatility and Ca metal air sensitivity increase severity. Kinetics can be fast due to reactive halogen vapor, but complete reconstruction from elements lowers the structural-reconstruction score. The main uncertainty is safe control of halogen pressure and avoidance of off-stoichiometry during prereaction.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Solution/cocrystallization is supported by SS1/TS1 and SS5 and offers excellent initial mixing. However, SS1/TS1 explicitly warn of hydration, multiple modifications, and unstable crystals for SrBr₂, which is a strong negative signal for anhydrous CaIBr. The route is therefore plausible as a precursor-mixing or crystal-growth strategy, but final sealed annealing is likely required.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
TS4 demonstrates that thermodynamically similar halide polymorphs can be stabilized by 3 GPa high-pressure/high-temperature growth, slow cooling, and quenching. This route receives a relatively good formal F2 score because the specified rubric penalizes only pressure >5 GPa, but experimentally it is more specialized than Route 1. The uncertainty is high because TS4 concerns BaI₂, not mixed CaBrI, and high-pressure stabilization could favor denser non-layered phases instead.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The described P3m1 CaIBr sheet uses edge-sharing CaI₃Br₃ octahedra and a small formula, so neither large-cell complexity nor mixed edge/vertex/face connectivity is indicated. The remaining uncertainty is stacking or anion-order disorder, but that is more a crystallographic ordering issue than an amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaIBr is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0030 eV/atom, comparable to thermodynamically similar synthesized halides in TS1–TS4. The strongest synthesis support comes from alkaline-earth mixed-halide solid-state routes using sealed ampoules, annealing, and slow cooling, especially CS1, SS3/SS5, TS2, and TS3. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and melt-assisted reactivity of alkaline-earth halide salts, which should allow Br/I redistribution and ordering. The main limiting factor is phase selection: the target is a trigonal layered octahedral CaIBr structure, while many Sr/Ba analogues adopt different higher-coordinate halide frameworks. Therefore, the material appears synthesizable, but phase-pure preparation will likely require careful stoichiometry, anhydrous handling, and slow cooling/annealing.
