### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bracket [Target]. Chemically close ZrNBr is also on hull [CS2], ZrNCl is essentially on hull [CS1], and related ZrBr is on hull [SS5, TS2], supporting that layered early-transition-metal nitride/bromide or bromide phases can be thermodynamically accessible. SS and TS provide useful stability signals through on-hull ZrBr and UNCl analogues [SS1, SS5, TS1, TS2], while TS nitride-only analogues such as HfN and PaN confirm stable metal nitride bonding but provide less direct halide chemistry [TS3, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee that the P-3m1 stacking is kinetically selected over other Ti–N–Br polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because CS2 requires 1000–1100 °C, high-pressure multianvil operation, NH4Br flux, controlled cooling, and quenching for ZrNBr [CS2]. CS1 independently shows that layered Zr/Hf nitride halides may require 3 GPa, 900–1200 °C, NH4Cl flux, and special capsules [CS1]. SS and TS high-pressure nitride analogues support the idea that pressure can stabilize dense nitride frameworks, but they provide insufficient direct bromide-flux evidence for Ti2NBr2, so their route-specific signal is weaker/neutral relative to CS [SS4, TS4]. The main uncertainty is whether Ti2NBr2 forms at the same 3–5 GPa window or requires a different pressure because Ti is smaller than Zr/Hf [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The NH4Br flux should improve kinetics relative to dry TiN because ZrNBr is made from ZrN using NH4Br flux under high pressure [CS2]. However, TiN is refractory and structurally different from the layered Ti2NBr2 product, so some reconstructive transformation is required [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives the strongest kinetic signal through the successful short 1–1.5 h ZrNBr synthesis [CS2], while SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ti–N–Br flux kinetics and are treated as neutral for this specific factor. Phase purity remains uncertain because CS2 reports β-phases with narrow stability windows and moderate yields [CS2].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids GPa pressure and high temperature because TiNCl CVT occurs at 400–440 °C [SS2]. It is still penalized for NH3, vacuum-sealed tube operation, volatile Ti halide/NH4Br chemistry, and multiple processing steps, all analogous to TiNCl ammonolysis/CVT [SS2]. CS2 supports use of NH4Br in nitride bromide chemistry, but CS does not directly demonstrate ambient-pressure TiNBr formation and is partly insufficient for this route [CS2]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for ammonolysis/CVT of Ti bromide nitride and is scored neutral for route-specific feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

SS2 provides strong Ti-specific kinetic support because TiCl4 reacts with NH3 and the product crystallizes by CVT over 3–7 days [SS2]. Replacing Cl with Br is chemically plausible because ZrNBr exists and NH4Br is a successful flux/halide source [CS2], but this is still an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is the key kinetic risk because volatile TiBr4/NH4Br and competing TiN/TiBrx phases may shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference based on volatile sealed-tube halide behavior in SS2 and phase-control difficulty in CS2]. TS provides insufficient evidence for this vapor-transport bromide chemistry and is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate-to-high temperatures around 750–900 °C, consistent with sealed ZrBr synthesis at 800–850 °C [SS5, TS2] and Y nitride chloride sealed ampoule reactions at 800–900 °C [CS3]. It avoids external high pressure but requires sealed ampoules, drybox handling, and volatile/corrosive bromide control [SS5, TS2, CS3]. CS supports sealed nitride-halide solid-state chemistry through Y2NCl3 but not Ti–N–Br specifically [CS3]. The uncertainty is compatibility of Ti bromides with ampoule materials and whether sufficient nitride-halide diffusion occurs without a high-pressure flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solid-state/transport kinetics are slower because TiN and Ti metal are refractory, while volatile Ti bromide transport only partially compensates [Reasonable Inference based on ZrBr sealed transport in SS5/TS2]. ZrBr formation requires a slow 1–2 week temperature ramp and prolonged heating, indicating that layered bromide ordering can be slow [SS5, TS2]. CS3 also shows long nitride chloride ampoule schedules, up to days/weeks, for moisture-sensitive rare-earth nitride chlorides [CS3]. SS/TS support layered bromide transport but not nitrogen incorporation into Ti2NBr2, so there is significant uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple single-cation chemistry, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The described P-3m1 layered sheet contains TiBr3N3 octahedra with edge and corner sharing, but no face-sharing coexistence is reported [Target]. The target is two-dimensional, which can increase stacking disorder risk, as seen in layered ZrBr sample quality/stacking sensitivity [SS5, TS2], but this is not one of the three specified amorphization triggers. Therefore, the intrinsic amorphization score is high, with the main risk being stacking disorder rather than glass formation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2NBr2 is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar ZrNBr, which is an on-hull layered nitride bromide prepared by high-pressure NH4Br flux [CS2], and from TiNCl ammonolysis/CVT, which proves Ti nitride halide chemistry is experimentally accessible [SS2]. The most important enabling factor is reactive halide flux or vapor transport, because NH4X flux/CVT enables crystallization of layered nitride halides in Zr/Hf and Ti chloride systems [CS1, CS2, SS2]. The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under volatile bromide chemistry, since Zr/Hf β-nitride halides show narrow windows/moderate yields [CS1, CS2] and sealed ZrBr reactions require slow, carefully controlled schedules [SS5, TS2]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires specialized handling and optimization rather than a simple ceramic firing.
