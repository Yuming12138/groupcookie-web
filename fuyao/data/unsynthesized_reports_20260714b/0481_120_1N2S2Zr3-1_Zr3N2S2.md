### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, placing it just inside the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin. Chemically close Zr2N2S phases are stable or nearly stable, with E_hull values of 0 and 0.0108 eV/atom (CS1, CS2), and structurally similar ZrN is stable (SS1). Thermodynamic analogues include synthesizable phases around 0.02–0.07 eV/atom, including Hf2N2S and Zr3N4/Hf3N4 (TS1, TS2, TS5), so a metastable but accessible target is plausible. Remaining uncertainty is that no source directly reports Zr3N2S2, and its S-rich stoichiometry may compete with Zr2N2S, ZrN, or ZrS2 **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses relatively mild temperatures, 750–850 °C, consistent with Zr2N2S metathesis at 800 °C (CS1, CS2) and Hf2N2S topochemical synthesis at 750 °C (TS5). The score is penalized for vacuum sealed-tube work, multi-step precursor handling/heating/washing, and air-sensitive alkali sulfide/nitride-halide reagents, all reflected in CS1/CS2/TS5. It is still less severe than high-pressure methods used for nitride halides or high-pressure nitrides (CS4, CS5; TS1, TS2). Remaining uncertainty is the need for stoichiometric modification beyond the demonstrated 2:1 ZrNCl:A2S route **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1

Metathesis is kinetically favorable because formation of alkali halide byproducts drives reaction and enables transport, as shown for Zr2N2S from ZrNCl + A2S (CS1, CS2). The layered Zr–N precursor relation should reduce reconstruction compared with direct ZrN/ZrS2 reaction **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the target’s Zr3N2S2 formula and mixed ZrS3N3/ZrN6 topology are not identical to the Zr2N2S analogues (CS1, CS2). Phase control remains a concern because CS2 reports polymorphs, intercalated phases, ZrO2 impurities, and stoichiometry/temperature sensitivity. Thus kinetics are moderate-good, not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 also avoids high pressure and stays below 1000 °C, matching the NaCl flux synthesis of Zr2N2Se at 900 °C (CS3; SS5). It is penalized for evacuated sealed tubes, volatile sulfur chemical potential control **[Reasonable Inference]**, multi-step flux washing, and sulfide handling. Equipment is simpler than high-pressure syntheses, as Zr2N2Se only requires evacuated silica tubes (CS3; SS5). The main uncertainty is whether sulfur behaves analogously to selenium in this flux system.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are limited by ZrN, which is refractory and remains as a minor impurity in the Zr2N2Se flux analogue (CS3; SS5). Flux improves reaction rate and crystal growth at 900 °C (CS3; SS5), but conversion to the more complex Zr3N2S2 sheet requires significant reconstruction from ZrN and ZrS2 **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structurally similar ZrN has strong Zr–N octahedral networks (SS1), which can be beneficial locally but slow to reorganize. Therefore F3 is moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized because thermodynamic analogues Zr3N4/Hf3N4 require up to 18 GPa and 3000 K in laser-heated DAC experiments (TS1, TS2), and nitride halide polymorphs require 3–5 GPa at 900–1100 °C (CS4, CS5). Even a milder 3–8 GPa screen requires specialized capsules, heaters, pressure media, and controlled quench. This route is much more severe than the ambient-pressure metathesis and flux routes. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that high pressure is necessary for Zr3N2S2; therefore high-pressure relevance is partly speculative **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can promote formation of metastable nitrides, as seen for Zr3N4/Hf3N4 (TS1, TS2), but those are dense nitride phases rather than layered nitride sulfides. Structurally similar binary nitrides require high temperatures and long homogenization/sintering to equilibrate (SS1, SS3), suggesting cation diffusion is difficult. Pressure may suppress sulfur volatility **[Reasonable Inference]**, but it may also favor dense ZrN/Zr3N4-type phases over a 2D target **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thus kinetics and structural selectivity are only moderate to poor.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so cation-size disorder is absent, which reduces amorphization risk. The unit cell is not indicated to be large, so the >40 atom criterion is not triggered. The only triggered factor is mixed polyhedral connectivity, because the target includes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing NZr6 polyhedra plus mixed ZrS3N3 and ZrN6 environments; similar complex connectivity appears in crystalline analogues such as Zr2N2S and Zr3N4 (CS1; TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether the unusual Zr3N2S2 sheet topology crystallizes cleanly or forms stacking/intergrowth disorder **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr3(NS)2 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, just below the favorable 0.05 eV/atom threshold. The synthesis is strongly supported by chemically close Zr2N2S analogues made by ZrNCl/alkali-sulfide metathesis (CS1, CS2; SS2, SS4), while thermodynamic analogues show that Zr/Hf nitrides and nitride sulfides slightly above hull can still be synthesized (TS1, TS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is use of reactive halide–sulfide metathesis/topochemistry to bypass slow diffusion in refractory ZrN-like solids, supported by CS1/CS2 and TS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the known Zr–N–S system readily forms Zr2N2S polymorphs and related phases depending on stoichiometry and temperature (CS1, CS2), so targeting Zr3N2S2 will likely require careful Zr:S:N chemical-potential control. Overall, the best route is experimentally feasible in a solid-state chemistry lab but likely requires optimization rather than one-shot synthesis.
