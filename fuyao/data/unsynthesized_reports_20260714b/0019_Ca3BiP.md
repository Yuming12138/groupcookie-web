### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

#### Route 1: Direct Sealed-Tube Elemental Solid-State Reaction

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Direct high-temperature solid-state synthesis is strongly supported by cubic anti-perovskite analogues Ca3AuN and Sr3BiN, and by alkaline-earth pnictide/Zintl syntheses in sealed Nb/Ta or inert setups. [CS2, CS3, SS5, TS2] |
| Precursors | Ca metal shot/pieces, Bi metal pieces/powder, red P powder; recommended ≥99.9% purity and handling in Ar glovebox. [CS2, CS3, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Temperature program | Preheat at ~200 °C for ~5 h, then ramp ~100–200 °C/h to 950–1050 °C, hold 24–48 h, cool slowly at ~5–10 K/h; optional regrind/reanneal if impurity phases remain. [CS1, CS2, CS3, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Atmosphere | Sealed Nb/Ta tube under Ar, preferably jacketed in evacuated silica; N2 is not recommended because the target contains P rather than N. [CS2, CS3, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Pressure | Ambient pressure inside sealed metal tube, with autogenous P vapor pressure. [CS1, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Additional conditions | Use slight P excess, ~1–3%, to compensate volatility; avoid direct contact with silica because Ca is reactive at high temperature. [CS1, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Transferability to target | Strong structural transferability because Ca3AuN and Sr3BiN share cubic Pm-3m anti-perovskite-like coordination, while Ca/Bi/P requires replacing nitride chemistry with phosphide chemistry. [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference] |
| Estimated difficulty | Challenging because sealed handling, P volatility, Ca air sensitivity, and competing Ca–Bi/Ca–P binaries are likely. [CS1, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |

#### Route 2: Ca–Bi Intermetallic / Melt-Bead Phosphidation

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Sr3BiN was obtained by reacting A3Bi melt beads with nitrogen, so a Ca-rich Ca–Bi precursor followed by phosphidation is a close process analogue. [CS3, Reasonable Inference] |
| Precursors | Ca metal + Bi metal to form a Ca-rich Ca–Bi alloy or melt bead, then red P or Ca3P2/P mixture as phosphidizing source. [CS3, CS1, Reasonable Inference] |
| Temperature program | Step 1: alloy Ca and Bi under Ar by arc melting or sealed-tube prereaction; Step 2: grind with P source, preheat ~200 °C, then anneal ~800–950 °C for 24–72 h; Step 3: slow cool or reanneal after grinding. [CS3, CS1, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Atmosphere | Ar glovebox handling; sealed Nb/Ta tube under Ar or evacuated jacket. [CS3, SS5, TS2] |
| Pressure | Ambient sealed-tube pressure with autogenous P vapor. [CS1, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |
| Additional conditions | Slight P excess and staged heating are recommended to reduce P loss and avoid rapid pressure buildup. [CS1, Reasonable Inference] |
| Transferability to target | This route preserves a Ca–Bi framework before P insertion, which is more analogous to A3E melt-bead nitridation than direct elemental reaction. [CS3, Reasonable Inference] |
| Estimated difficulty | Moderate–Challenging because the route improves framework formation but adds a multistep alloying/phosphidation sequence. [CS3, TS2, Reasonable Inference] |

#### Route 3: Flux-Assisted Crystal Growth

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Pb/Sn/Mg-Bi flux growth is repeatedly used for Bi/Sb-containing thermodynamic and structural analogues. [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3, TS5] |
| Precursors | Ca metal, Bi metal, red P, and Pb or Sn flux; Bi-rich self-flux is possible but less directly supported. [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3, TS5, Reasonable Inference] |
| Temperature program | Heat sealed charge to ~900–1000 °C, hold ~20–24 h, cool slowly to ~500–700 °C at ~3–5 °C/h, then decant/centrifuge flux. [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS5] |
| Atmosphere | Sealed evacuated silica ampoule with Nb/Ta liner, or sealed Nb/Ta tube under Ar. [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference] |
| Pressure | Ambient sealed ampoule pressure with autogenous vapor pressure from P and flux. [TS5, Reasonable Inference] |
| Additional conditions | Flux-to-charge ratio should be high, and Ca contact with silica should be avoided using a metal liner. [SS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference] |
| Transferability to target | Flux growth can improve diffusion and crystal quality, but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Ca3BiP anti-perovskite flux growth. [SS1, TS1, TS5, insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS] |
| Estimated difficulty | Moderate–Challenging because flux improves kinetics but complicates composition control and flux removal. [SS1, TS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference] |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Ca3BiP has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold in the requested rubric. [Target] TS analogues include experimentally synthesized phases around similar metastability, including 0.057 eV/atom and 0.0395 eV/atom examples, which supports access to near-hull phases. [TS1, TS5] CS evidence is contradictory because Ca3PbN is high above hull while Ca3AuN and Sr3BiN are near/on hull, so CS is supportive for structure but mixed for thermodynamic robustness. [CS1, CS2, CS3]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C operation, controlled sealed atmosphere, volatile P, and air-sensitive Ca. [CS1, CS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference] It avoids high pressure and is procedurally simpler than flux or two-step phosphidation. [CS2, CS3] Remaining uncertainty is the exact temperature needed to order Ca3BiP without decomposing into Ca–Bi or Ca–P binaries. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Direct elemental reaction has moderate precursor activity because Ca/Bi/P are more reactive than refractory oxides but still require solid-state ordering. [CS2, CS3, TS2, Reasonable Inference] Kinetics are helped by low-melting Bi and Ca-near-melting conditions, while the anti-perovskite structural precedent improves the reconstruction score. [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference] Long anneals in related materials suggest ordering may still be slow. [CS1, TS2]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids >1000 °C phosphidation but is still penalized for sealed controlled atmosphere, volatile P, Ca air sensitivity, and multistep processing. [CS1, CS3, TS2] The extra prereaction step is justified by CS3, where A3Bi melt beads are used before nitrogen insertion. [CS3] The main uncertainty is whether a Ca-rich Ca–Bi precursor can be made without locking into competing binary phases. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 2**  
The Ca–Bi precursor should improve kinetics by shortening the pathway to the Ca/Bi framework of the target. [CS3, Reasonable Inference] This route receives the highest structural-reconstruction score because it mirrors the A3E-to-anti-perovskite logic used for Sr3BiN. [CS3] The uncertainty is that phosphidation may not behave like nitridation, especially because P is larger and more volatile than N. [CS1, CS3, Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth avoids very high temperature but requires sealed conditions, volatile P control, flux separation, and air-sensitive Ca handling. [SS1, TS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference] Pb flux also introduces toxicity and possible contamination risk. [SS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference] Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms flux growth of Ca3BiP specifically, so that uncertainty is retained. [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux routes score well for diffusion because Bi/Sb analogues crystallize from molten flux with slow cooling and centrifugation. [SS1, TS1, TS5] The structural-reconstruction score is lower than Route 2 because flux analogues are mostly different Zintl or layered pnictides rather than cubic anti-perovskites. [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS5] Composition control may be narrow because Ca, P, and flux metals can form competing phases. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a compact cubic Pm-3m anti-perovskite cell and no large multi-site unit cell, which lowers amorphization risk. [Target, CS2, CS3] The target connectivity is corner/face-sharing without the more complex vertex+edge+face coexistence seen in some SS/TS pnictides. [Target, SS1, TS5] Ionic-radius evidence is not directly supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that specific source signal is treated as insufficient and neutral 50, while the final F4 relies mainly on the target structure. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, Target]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca3BiP is most likely a near-hull metastable or borderline stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. [Target] The strongest support comes from CS2 and CS3, which show cubic Pm-3m anti-perovskite-like phases can be synthesized under high-temperature controlled-atmosphere conditions. [CS2, CS3] The most enabling factor is the simple five-atom cubic anti-perovskite framework, which should reduce amorphization and reconstruction penalties. [Target, CS2, CS3] The most limiting factor is stoichiometry control under P vapor pressure while maintaining Ca-rich chemistry. [CS1, TS2, Reasonable Inference] Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible but likely requires sealed-tube optimization rather than open crucible reaction. [CS1, SS5, TS2, Reasonable Inference]
