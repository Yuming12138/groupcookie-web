### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom is at the high end of the “highly synthesizable” bin, giving 90/100. CS gives favorable evidence because NaBa₂O₆I and NaSr₂O₆I are on the hull (CS-3, CS-5), while Na₂SnTeO₆ is also close to the hull at 0.0386 eV/atom (CS-1). TS gives particularly relevant support because several thermodynamic analogues lie near 0.04–0.07 eV/atom and have reported syntheses, including ZnIO₃(OH), Ag₂TeO₃, ZnSeO₃, and CdO₄I (TS-2 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that SS includes some iodate-like phases with higher hull energies, e.g., NaIO₄-related material at 0.362 eV/atom (SS-4), so metastability is plausible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

The ceramic route is moderate because CS-3/CS-5 demonstrate synthesis at only 650 °C in air, with no high pressure or controlled atmosphere. The score is reduced because CS-3/CS-5 require repeated heat/cool cycles and washing, and iodine volatilization at 650 °C is a [Reasonable Inference] concern. Pb precursor toxicity is also [Reasonable Inference], not directly present in CS-3/CS-5. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this exact ceramic Na–Pb–I–O route, so their direct condition signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are reasonably favorable because CS-3/CS-5 show Na–M–I–O perovskite-derived phases form at 650 °C, aided by NaF mineralizer. However, the target is triclinic P1 with Pb, not the Ba/Sr perovskite-derived phases of CS-3/CS-5, so full structural transfer is uncertain. Repeated firings in CS-3/CS-5 imply that ordering or diffusion may require multiple cycles. Insufficient direct kinetic evidence from SS/TS for ceramic Pb iodate formation, so their direct signal is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Hydrothermal synthesis is mild in temperature: SS-1/TS-1 use 200 °C, SS-3/TS-5 use 230 °C, SS-4 uses 170 °C, and TS-2 uses 200 °C. The main severity deductions are sealed autoclave operation with iodine oxyacids/iodates and toxic Pb precursors, the latter being [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure beyond autogenous water vapor pressure is required in SS-1/SS-4/TS-1, and no reactive gas atmosphere is reported in SS-1/SS-3/TS-2/TS-5. The uncertainty is that hydrothermal conditions may produce hydrated lead iodates rather than the anhydrous target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrothermal kinetics score high because SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, and TS-5 use soluble or reactive iodate/periodate precursors and form crystalline iodate materials at 170–230 °C. Slow cooling in SS-1, SS-3, TS-2, and TS-5 supports crystal growth rather than amorphous precipitation. Structural reconstruction is less certain because the closest hydrothermal examples are Li/Zn, K/Bi, Cd, Zn, or Na iodates, not Na–Pb–I–O specifically. SS-1 and TS-5 also show phase sensitivity or competing analog formation, reducing confidence.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The high-pressure route is penalized heavily because CS-1 uses 7 GPa/950 °C and TS-3 uses 15–70 kbar. Specialized high-pressure equipment is required in CS-1 and TS-3. Additional deductions come from multi-step preparation/quenching and toxic Pb precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for high-pressure synthesis of structurally similar iodates, so SS contributes neutral signal.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High-pressure kinetics are moderate: TS-3 reports direct oxide reactions in 20–60 min under high pressure, and CS-1 reports transformation of Na₂MTeO₆ at 7 GPa/950 °C. High pressure can stabilize dense frameworks and promote phase formation, but oxide/periodate precursors are less kinetically active than hydrothermal solution precursors. Quench retention is supported by CS-1, but no direct Na–Pb–I–O high-pressure example is available. Therefore, the route is credible but not preferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically different cations and mixed coordination environments, with Na in 4–6 coordination and Pb in a low-coordinate environment, so C1 is triggered by [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s described P1 structure appears to have 10 atoms per formula-level cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a larger unit cell. Mixed polyhedral sharing is not explicitly described for the target, so C3 is not confidently triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral. The resulting score of 80 reflects low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂PbO₆I is likely a near-threshold metastable or barely stable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the upper limit of the most favorable stability bin. CS analogues strongly support the existence of Na–I–O perovskite-derived oxides by ceramic synthesis, especially NaBa₂O₆I and NaSr₂O₆I (CS-3, CS-5), while SS/TS analogues strongly support hydrothermal formation of low-symmetry iodate frameworks at 170–230 °C (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive iodine oxyacid/periodate chemistry under hydrothermal or oxidizing ceramic conditions, supported by SS-1/SS-3/SS-4/TS-1/TS-5 and CS-3/CS-5. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Pb may form competing Pb iodates, oxides, or hydrated/basic phases, and SS-1/TS-5 show that iodate hydrothermal systems can be condition-sensitive. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with hydrothermal screening recommended first and ceramic synthesis as a strong backup.
