### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target thermodynamic data. Chemically and thermodynamically similar sulfides such as Na₂Ga₂GeS₆, Na₂Zn(GeS₃)₂, Na₂In₂SiS₆, CdGa₂S₄, ZnGa₂S₄, and BaGa₄S₇ are also stable or nearly stable (CS1, CS4, CS5; TS1–TS5). The main uncertainty is not thermodynamic existence but whether the Ca/Na/Ga ordering in Pbca can be kinetically accessed without forming simpler Na–Ga–S or Ca–Ga–S byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The sealed sulfide route is supported by solid-state syntheses of Na and Li gallium sulfides in evacuated/sealed tubes at 550–950 °C (CS1, CS2; SS1; TS4). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, sulfide/alkali volatility, and air-sensitive sulfide handling, all of which are present or implied in the analogues (CS1, CS2; SS1). Kinetically, binary sulfides are more reactive than refractory oxides, but long holds and possible regrinding are still needed, as reported for analogous sulfides (CS1, CS4, CS5; SS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The sulfurization/flux route is strongly supported by Na quaternary sulfides synthesized using CS₂ vapor, N₂/vacuum, sealed tubes, molten sodium thiosulfate flux, intermittent grinding, and slow cooling (CS1, CS4, CS5; TS4, TS5). The condition penalty is driven by toxic CS₂, sealed sulfur-containing systems, and multistep processing (CS1, CS4, CS5). Its kinetics score is the highest because flux/reactive sulfurization should enhance mass transport and sulfur chemical potential control relative to dry binary sulfide sintering [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/CS4/CS5 flux and CS₂ routes].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT is supported for structurally and thermodynamically similar Ga sulfides such as CdGa₂S₄ grown in evacuated ampoules with transport agents at 580–840 °C (SS2; TS1). However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Na–Ca–Ga–S transport chemistry, so composition control is uncertain and scored lower. The route is best viewed as a recrystallization/single-crystal growth route after bulk phase formation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Bridgman growth is supported by BaGa₄S₇, a Ga–S framework compound grown from polycrystalline feed at 1130–1140 °C in evacuated ampoules with controlled translation (SS3; TS2). The severity score is lower because it exceeds 1000 °C and requires controlled ampoule growth (SS3; TS2). The main uncertainty is whether NaCaGa₃S₆ melts congruently and whether Na/S loss can be suppressed at melt-growth temperatures [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has multiple distinct cation coordination environments—NaS₅, CaS₆, and three GaS₄ sites—which indicates a complex ordered sulfide lattice and a large cation-size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The Pbca structure description includes many inequivalent sites, implying a large unit cell, which raises crystallization/ordering demands [Reasonable Inference]. The target shows corner and edge sharing but no stated face sharing, so the strongest amorphization criterion is not fully triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaCa(GaS₂)₃ is likely a true ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar sulfides are also on or near the convex hull (CS1, CS4, CS5; TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for sealed-tube or flux/reactive sulfurization methods because multiple Na-containing quaternary sulfides were synthesized at 550–850 °C under CS₂/N₂/vacuum with slow cooling (CS1, CS4, CS5; TS4, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur chemical potential while allowing Na/Ga/Ca cation ordering, which is supported by CS₂ sulfurization, sealed tubes, and flux variants in the analogues (CS1, CS4, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Na/S volatility and possible competition from simpler Na–Ga–S or Ca–Ga–S phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears moderately to highly synthesizable, with the best route being reactive sulfurization or sodium-flux-assisted solid-state synthesis.

| 4 | Route 1 | Slow solid-state diffusion and cation ordering in a multi-cation Pbca structure | Medium | Use fine powders, pelletization, 48–96 h anneals, and regrinding; long solid-state holds are common in analogous sulfide syntheses (CS1, CS2; SS1). |
| 5 | Route 2 | CS₂ toxicity and sulfurization control | High | Use a controlled tube-furnace setup, gas scrubbing, and sealed or well-ventilated sulfurization protocols; CS₂ sulfurization is explicitly used in Na quaternary sulfide synthesis (CS1, CS4, CS5; TS4, TS5). |
| 6 | Route 2 | Flux incorporation or Na overactivity from sodium thiosulfate | Medium | Adjust Na stoichiometry and wash/remove soluble flux products; sodium thiosulfate flux is reported for related Na sulfides, but exact target stoichiometry requires optimization (CS1, CS5; TS5) [Reasonable Inference]. |

| 8 | Route 3 | Narrow composition control during vapor transport | Medium–High | Employ small temperature gradients and sulfur buffering; CVT analogues use controlled gradients in evacuated ampoules (SS2; TS1). |
