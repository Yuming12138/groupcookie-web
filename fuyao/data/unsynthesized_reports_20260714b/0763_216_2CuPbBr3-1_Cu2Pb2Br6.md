### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0100 eV/atom is very close to the convex hull, so the base thermodynamic score is high [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues have similarly low hull energies, including CsBi2Br7 at 0.0104 eV/atom, RbPb2Br5 at 0.0080 eV/atom, and TlPbI3 at 0.0114 eV/atom [TS2/TS5/TS4]. Structural analogues also support near-hull layered/halide phases [SS1–SS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that chemical analogues include both stable examples and a higher-energy Tl–Fe–Br phase at 0.1269 eV/atom [CS1], so thermodynamic proximity alone does not guarantee phase-selective synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is not temperature-severe because the direct Pb–transition-metal bromide analogue was annealed at 300 °C after melting/quenching [CS5/SS3/TS3], and other bromide analogues use 523 K to 700 °C [SS1/CS1]. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile halide chemistry, multi-step melt/quench/anneal operations, and toxic Pb precursors [CS5/SS3/TS3; CS1]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed for CuPbBr3, so high-pressure severity is not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary bromides are chemically active relative to oxides and are directly used in the closest Pb–transition-metal bromide analogue [CS5/SS3/TS3]. Diffusion is likely manageable because halide phases can react or partially melt at modest temperatures, consistent with low-temperature bromide syntheses [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. The kinetic uncertainty is structural: the target is a trigonal 2D Cu–Pb sheet, whereas Pd(PbBr3)2 is monoclinic with different transition-metal coordination [CS5/SS3/TS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses mild temperatures comparable to 150–200 °C TlAl/Ga halide synthesis [CS2] and 523 K Bi-bromide synthesis [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. Severity arises from vacuum sealing, inert handling, volatile/moisture-sensitive halides, and toxic Pb content [CS2; SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any need for high pressure, so pressure severity remains zero.

#### F3 — Route 2

Low-temperature sealed-ampoule crystallization is kinetically favorable for soft halides, as RbBi2Br7/CsBi2Br7 form by heating to only 523 K followed by slow cooling [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. Controlled cooling can help crystallinity and ordering [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. The limitation is phase purity: those same analogues report impurity phases such as BiBr3 or M3Bi2Br9 and narrow stability windows [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth occurs at moderate temperatures in the Cu-containing AuCuSe4 analogue, specifically 310 °C for 4 d under vacuum [CS4]. The route still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoules, slow cooling, flux removal, and toxic Pb handling [CS4; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure flux growth, so no high-pressure penalty is used.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth should improve kinetics because liquid/flux transport reduces long-range solid diffusion barriers [CS4]. Slow cooling is a known strategy for halide crystallization and defect reduction in structurally/thermodynamically similar bromides [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. The main kinetic risk is phase selectivity: CS4 reports mixed phases in flux growth, and SS1/SS2 also report impurity phases in slow-cooled bromides.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 has mild nominal conditions because TS5 describes cooling crystallization from aqueous salts and SS4 describes room-temperature gel growth of lead iodide. However, this route is only weakly supported for CuPbBr3 specifically, because neither TS5 nor SS4 includes Cu(I)–Pb mixed bromide chemistry. Inert/redox control is a [Reasonable Inference], not directly established by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution kinetics are potentially fast because ions diffuse through solution rather than through a solid lattice [TS5/SS4]. However, structural reconstruction/selectivity is poor: TS5 notes that a related lead bromide cannot be washed without decomposition and contains excess ammonium bromide. Cu(I) solubility and oxidation introduce additional uncertainty not resolved by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant cation-size/coordination mismatch because Cu is 4-coordinate while Pb is 6-coordinate in the reported structure [Target], which triggers one amorphization check by ionic-size contrast [Reasonable Inference]. The cell appears simple and does not meet the >40 atom criterion based on the formula/space-group description [Target]. The structure reports edge-sharing PbBr6 octahedra but not simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing coexistence [Target], so the overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuPbBr3 is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0100 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from thermodynamic and structural analogues that are near-hull bromide/lead-halide phases synthesized by sealed ampoule, slow cooling, or melt/anneal methods [SS1–SS3; TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is soft halide chemistry: binary bromides and flux-like melts can react at a few hundred °C rather than requiring refractory diffusion [CS5/SS3/TS3; SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity, because close bromide analogues report impurity phases, narrow stability windows, or mixed flux products [SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2; CS4]. A sealed, dry, slow-cooled ampoule synthesis using CuBr and PbBr2 is therefore the most defensible starting point.
