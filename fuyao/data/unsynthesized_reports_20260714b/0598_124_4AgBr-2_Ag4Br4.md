### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0050 eV/atom, which is within the highly synthesizable range for inorganic phases. CS supports this because chemically similar binaries include stable or near-stable phases, such as CuSe and CuS at 0 eV/atom [CS1, CS4] and CdS at 0.0027 eV/atom [CS3]. TS is especially supportive because thermodynamic analogues occupy a similar small positive hull-energy range, e.g., 0.0053–0.0139 eV/atom [TS1–TS5]. SS is mixed: some structurally similar Ag-halide phases are stable [SS3, SS5], while others are significantly metastable [SS1, SS2], so the uncertainty is mainly polymorph selection rather than composition stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule reaction

F2 is reduced because this route requires an evacuated sealed ampoule, analogous to CS1, CS4, SS2, and SS5, and because Br₂ is volatile/corrosive **[Reasonable Inference]**. F3 is moderate: the chemical reaction Ag + Br₂ should be strongly driven **[Reasonable Inference]**, but Ag metal is a dense precursor and direct solid-state analogues often require long annealing or repeated processing [CS1, SS2]. The biggest uncertainty is structural reconstruction, since the desired orthorhombic 2D polymorph is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-temperature precipitation / chemical-bath route

F2 is favorable because the main synthesis can occur at low temperature and ambient pressure, similar in spirit to CdS chemical bath deposition at 80 °C [CS3]. A deduction is retained for optional vacuum/inert annealing because Ag-halide analogues commonly use evacuated sealed treatment [SS2, SS3, SS5]. F3 is favorable because soluble Ag⁺ and Br⁻ precursors should form AgBr rapidly **[Reasonable Inference]**, but crystallizing the specific orthorhombic 2D polymorph may require careful aging/annealing; CS3 also warns that chemical bath products can contain mixed phases or impurities.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Melt/quench or Bridgman-type growth

F2 is moderate because the temperature is not extreme, but sealed evacuated ampoule processing and possible halide volatility are required, as in Ag₃SI melt growth [SS1] and Ag-halide solid-state syntheses [SS2, SS5]. F3 is relatively strong because a melt removes long-range diffusion limitations and SS1 demonstrates melt growth of an Ag-halide/chalcogenide analogue. The uncertainty is that slow cooling from melt may favor the common equilibrium AgBr polymorph rather than the orthorhombic layered polymorph **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Vapor transport / gas-phase growth

F2 is lowered by the need for an evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile transport conditions, consistent with TS5. F3 is moderate because TS5 shows gas-phase growth can work for halide-containing crystalline solids, but direct evidence for AgBr vapor transport or for preserving the target polymorph is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. Composition control is plausible for a binary compound **[Reasonable Inference]**, but Br activity and transport additive concentration may strongly affect defects and phase selection.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple binary composition with one cation type, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger. The described structure contains inequivalent Ag and Br sites but no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct target-cell information, so they are neutral for the formal F4 checks; the low amorphization risk is inferred primarily from the target structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target AgBr phase is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable polymorph because its hull energy is only 0.0050 eV/atom. CS and TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of low-hull soft-anion binaries and halide/chalcogenide materials, while SS analogues show that Ag-halide frameworks can be synthesized but may require careful thermal history. The most critical enabling factor is the very simple Ag–Br binary chemistry and the availability of low-temperature solution precipitation, which can bypass slow solid-state diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph competition: common AgBr structures may form preferentially, and the orthorhombic 2D P2₁2₁2 phase may require kinetic trapping, mild annealing, or seeding. Overall, the material is quite synthesizable in composition, but phase-pure access to the specified polymorph is only moderately certain.
