### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom is exactly at the high-stability/metastability boundary, so it receives 90/100 by the provided rubric. CS analogues include Sr2LiReO6 and Sr2YOsO6 at 0–0.011 eV/atom, showing that closely related Sr–5d oxides can be thermodynamically robust (CS-1 to CS-3). TS analogues lie near the same metastability range, e.g., Sr2UZnO6 at 0.054 eV/atom and Eu3OsO7 at 0.069 eV/atom, supporting synthesizability of phases slightly above hull (TS-1, TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry is exactly NaSr2OsO6 with Os(VII), so decomposition into Na/Sr osmates remains possible.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is penalized because CS-3 requires sealed-tube Ar synthesis, long heat treatment, and intermittent regrinding for Sr–Os oxides. CS-4 and TS-3 show that sealed tubes and controlled atmospheres are often used for Re/Os oxide chemistry, supporting a volatility/oxygen-control penalty. The route avoids >1000 °C if run near CS-3 conditions, but toxic Os reagents and reactive peroxides are significant [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for this specific ambient-pressure Sr–Na–Os solid-state route; SS is scored neutral, 50, for route-specific severity transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS-3’s ~100 h treatment and intermittent regrinding indicate slow bulk diffusion for Sr–Os oxide formation. TS-3 similarly reports repeated regrinding and annealing until single phase for Re oxides, which supports a low-to-moderate kinetics score. The target’s P1 OsO5/SrO6 topology differs from the more regular OsO6 perovskite-derived CS-3 analogue, so structural reconstruction is expected to be harder. SS provides little direct support for this solid-state route, so insufficient evidence from SS is treated as neutral, 50, in the qualitative source balance.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

CS-5 and TS-4 show that NaOH/H2O flux growth can be done at 600 °C for 12 h in silver tubes, so temperature severity is low. The main penalties are the multi-step flux process, corrosive/toxic reagents, and containment needed for Na/Os chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. No external high pressure or reactive gas is required in CS-5/TS-4, improving the score. SS gives no useful signal for hydroxide flux synthesis of Sr–Na–Os oxides; insufficient evidence from SS, neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because CS-5 and TS-4 demonstrate crystallization of Na/Ru and Os oxides at only 600 °C, far below temperatures needed for many solid-state reactions. The liquid NaOH medium improves mass transport and can dissolve/react oxides that would otherwise diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS-5 and TS-4 are not Sr–Na–Os phases, so the desired P1 NaSr2OsO6 may compete with SrOsO3/Sr2OsO4-type or Ln3OsO7-like motifs [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS are strong for this route; SS is insufficient and neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

SS-1 directly supports a high-pressure alkali-superoxide route at 20 kbar and 1000 °C in an Au capsule. Under the given rubric, 20 kbar is below the >5 GPa high-pressure deduction threshold, but practically it is still specialized equipment. The route is penalized for sealed reactive oxidants, multi-step capsule handling, and air-sensitive/toxic reagents, consistent with SS-1’s glovebox and KO2 use. CS/TS give limited direct evidence for this specific high-pressure route; insufficient evidence from CS and TS is treated as neutral, 50, for route severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

SS-1 shows rapid formation of K3NaRe2O9 in 60 min at 1000 °C, indicating that high pressure plus superoxide chemistry can overcome slow diffusion for alkali/high-valent 5d oxides. This supports a good kinetics score despite refractory Os [Reasonable Inference]. However, SS-1 also reports hygroscopic crystals and mixed products, implying difficult phase control and possible metastability. CS/TS do not directly validate high-pressure synthesis for NaSr2OsO6, so their route-specific kinetic signal is insufficient and neutral, 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations and bond lengths, with large Na/Sr–O coordination environments and short Os–O bonds, so cation-size/field-strength mismatch is significant; CS-3 shows similar Sr–O vs Os–O contrast in Sr2YOsO6. The target also has mixed SrO6 and OsO5 polyhedra with corner and edge sharing, which increases topological complexity relative to cubic CS-1/CS-2 perovskites. The atom-count criterion cannot be confirmed from CS/SS/TS, so it is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral, 50. Overall the structural complexity suggests medium amorphization/disorder risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaSr2OsO6 is likely a borderline metastable but accessible phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and comparable TS analogues are experimentally known at similar energies above hull. CS evidence strongly supports Sr–Os oxide formation by sealed solid-state synthesis, while CS-5 and TS-4 strongly support hydroxide-flux growth of Na/Ru and Os oxides. The most critical enabling factor is use of a flux or internally oxidizing sealed environment to provide sufficient oxygen chemical potential and mass transport for high-valent Os. The most critical limiting factor is phase/oxidation-state control: formal Os(VII), Na volatility, and possible formation of competing Sr–Os or Na–Os oxides may narrow the synthesis window. Overall, the best experimental first attempt is NaOH/H2O flux in Ag tubes, followed by sealed solid-state peroxide synthesis if flux chemistry gives competing phases.
