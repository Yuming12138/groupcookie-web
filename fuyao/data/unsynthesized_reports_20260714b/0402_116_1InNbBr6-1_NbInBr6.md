### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0060 eV/atom, placing it essentially on the thermodynamic hull and within the 90–100 scoring range. This is reinforced by nearby bromide analogues with very low hull energies, including Zr(InBr₃)₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-2), NbBr₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-3), InGaBr₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (SS-5), and MnInBr₃ at 0.0041 eV/atom (TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or stability against moisture, which is a repeated issue in bromide analogues (CS-2, TS-4, TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, around 450–500 °C, matching InBr₃ melt syntheses in CS-2 and TS-5. It is penalized for vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile/air-sensitive bromide precursors, because InBr₃ and metal bromide systems require dry or sealed conditions in CS-2, SS-5, and TS-5. No high-pressure or very-high-temperature penalty applies.

#### F3 — Route 1

The flux route has favorable kinetics because InBr₃ can serve as both reagent and molten medium, as shown for Zr(InBr₃)₂ in CS-2 and MnInBr₃ in TS-5. The limiting kinetic factor is oxidation/reaction of Nb metal, which is refractory and may retain passivating oxide **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction is moderate because In–transition-metal bromide frameworks are known (CS-2, TS-5), but the exact NbInBr₆ 2D P312 sheet is not directly represented.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is also moderate in severity because CVT analogues operate at relatively low temperatures, such as 200–250 °C in CS-1 and 150–180 °C in SS-1/TS-1. The route is penalized for vacuum ampoules, volatile bromides, and air-sensitive precursors, all of which are typical in the referenced CVT bromide syntheses (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1, TS-4). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT has the best kinetic score because volatile halides can transport and crystallize low-dimensional bromide phases directly, as demonstrated by CS-1, SS-1/TS-1, and TS-4. Composition control is the main uncertainty: SS-1/TS-1 notes byproducts and metastable or low-yield phases for some halide complexes. For NbInBr₆, maintaining the correct Nb/In ratio and Nb valence during transport is likely the critical kinetic/compositional issue **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis is more severe because it may require multiple operations: precursor preparation, grinding, sealing, heating, and controlled cooling, as seen in CS-4/SS-4 and TS-2. The route still uses only moderate temperatures, roughly 250–400 °C, consistent with SS-5 and TS-2. Penalties arise from air-sensitive/volatile bromides and the need for sealed vacuum or dry Ar ampoules.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary bromides are reactive relative to oxides, and NbBr₄/NbBr₅ already contain NbBr₆ octahedral motifs (CS-3, CS-4), which helps. However, direct solid-state mixing lacks the liquid/vapor transport benefits of Routes 1 and 2, so diffusion and phase segregation are more serious **[Reasonable Inference]**. Phase impurities are plausible because CS-4/SS-4 reports black crystals along with impurity-containing powder, and TS-2 reports multiple W bromide side products.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple edge-sharing octahedral network, not mixed edge/vertex/face connectivity, according to the target structure description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on the target unit-cell atom count or ionic-radius mismatch, so those source categories are treated as neutral for those subchecks. Physicochemically, octahedral Nb/In bromide coordination and low-dimensional halide crystallization should favor ordered crystalline phases over amorphous products if moisture and stoichiometry are controlled **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbInBr₆ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom, and many similar bromides in CS/SS/TS are on or very close to the hull. The analogue support is fairly strong: CS-2 and TS-5 support InBr₃-based molten/flux synthesis of transition-metal indium bromides, while CS-1, SS-1/TS-1, and TS-4 support CVT growth of low-dimensional bromide crystals. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of bromide species, allowing low-temperature assembly in flux or vapor transport conditions. The main limiting factor is Nb redox/stoichiometry control, because NbBr₄ and NbBr₅ form stable competing octahedral bromide motifs (CS-3, CS-4). Moisture sensitivity is also a universal practical challenge across the analogous bromide systems (CS-2, TS-4, TS-5).
