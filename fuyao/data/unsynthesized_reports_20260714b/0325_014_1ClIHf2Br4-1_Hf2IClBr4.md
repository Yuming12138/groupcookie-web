### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, placing it in the strongest stability category (Target). CS/SS/TS reinforce that related halide and chalcogenide-halide compounds can be experimentally accessible at or near the convex hull: CS1 and CS3 are 0.0 eV/atom; SS1, SS3, SS4, SS5 are 0.0 eV/atom; TS1–TS5 are 0.0 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee formation of the exact mixed-anion ordered Hf₂IBr₄Cl phase; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact mixed Cl/Br/I ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state comproportionation

The route inherits high-temperature sealed-tube severity from reduced bromide analogues that require 850–1050 °C, long anneals, dry inert atmospheres, and Ta/Nb containers (SS4, SS5; TS3, TS4, TS5). The proposed Hf route avoids >1000 °C if optimized, but still requires volatile Hf halides and strict exclusion of moisture [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide strong evidence for sealed-tube feasibility but insufficient direct evidence that Hf mixed trihalide stoichiometry will form without competing binary/ternary halides.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state comproportionation

Kinetics are penalized because Hf metal is refractory and solid-state diffusion is likely slow [Reasonable Inference], analogous to long rare-earth bromide reactions in SS1, SS4, SS5 and TS1, TS3–TS5. Halide volatility and possible molten/vapor transport may partially compensate, as moderate-temperature halide reactions occur in CS1 and CS5 and transport routes in SS2/SS3. Structural reconstruction from Hf metal plus HfX₄ to a 2D edge-sharing sheet remains a major kinetic uncertainty; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient exact structural-transformation evidence for Hf₂IBr₄Cl.

#### F2 — Route 2: chemical vapor transport

CVT severity is moderate because analogous bromide systems crystallize at only 200–500 °C in sealed ampoules (SS2, SS3; TS2), much milder than direct rare-earth bromide reactions (SS4, SS5; TS3–TS5). However, it still requires vacuum sealing, volatile/corrosive halides, and controlled two-zone temperatures, all supported by SS2/SS3/TS2. The main uncertainty is whether Hf(III)-like reduced chemistry can be maintained in a vapor transport environment; CS/SS/TS do not directly prove this.

#### F3 — Route 2: chemical vapor transport

Vapor availability is relatively favorable because halide transport using Al₂Br₆/AlBr₃ is directly demonstrated for structurally similar bromides (SS2, SS3; TS2). Crystallization in a cooler zone is also supported by SS2 and SS3, but the target requires simultaneous Hf reduction and three-anion ordering, which likely narrows the composition window [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for controlling Cl/Br/I ratios during transport, so composition control is the limiting kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 3: molten halide flux

Flux synthesis has moderate severity because it can operate below 700 °C, comparable to the 450 °C bromide-rich sealed reaction in CS5 and the moderate-temperature halide transport conditions in SS2/SS3. It still requires sealed tubes, volatile halides, and moisture-sensitive fluxes [Reasonable Inference], so the F2 score remains similar to CVT. CS/SS/TS do not directly report a Hf mixed-halide flux route, so this is an extrapolated route.

#### F3 — Route 3: molten halide flux

The flux route improves precursor activity relative to dry solid-state reaction because molten halides can enhance mass transport [Reasonable Inference], consistent with lower-temperature halide-mediated crystallization in CS5, SS2, and SS3. The high melting point of Hf still imposes a diffusion/reduction barrier, and the target sheet requires ordered assembly of HfX₆ octahedra. The largest uncertainty is whether the flux will dissolve/react Hf sufficiently without incorporating alkali or forming simpler Hf halides; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on this point.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation element, Hf, so cation radius mismatch does not appear to trigger amorphization based on the provided target description; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for cation disorder in the target. The target structure is described as edge-sharing HfIBr₄Cl octahedra rather than a coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low from the target description. The uncertainty is that mixed anion disorder among Cl/Br/I could broaden local environments [Reasonable Inference], but that is not one of the formal F4 triggers and is not directly quantified by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂IBr₄Cl is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target), and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also on the hull, including CS1, CS3, SS1, SS3–SS5, and TS1–TS5. The strongest synthesis support comes from sealed halide chemistry: direct reduced bromide syntheses in Ta/Nb tubes are well represented in SS4, SS5, TS3–TS5, while lower-temperature halide vapor/flux crystallization is supported by SS2, SS3, TS2, and CS5. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical activity of volatile/molten halides in sealed systems, which can lower kinetic barriers relative to refractory Hf metal [Reasonable Inference from CS5, SS2, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the exact Cl:Br:I ratio and Hf reduced oxidation state without forming competing HfCl₄/HfBr₄/HfI₄-derived phases or binary Hf halides [Reasonable Inference; phase competition is common in SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4, TS5]. Overall, the material is plausible but likely requires careful sealed-tube screening and slow cooling.
