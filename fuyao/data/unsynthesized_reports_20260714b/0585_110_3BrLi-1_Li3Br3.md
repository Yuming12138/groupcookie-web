### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.025 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score by the provided rubric. CS2 reports a chemically similar LiI polymorph with E_hull ≈0.0289 eV/atom, while SS1–SS3 and TS1–TS4 contain bromide analogues with E_hull values near 0–0.021 eV/atom, supporting that near-hull metastable halide frameworks are synthetically plausible. However, CS1’s stable LiI analogue and SS/TS ternary bromides do not prove the pure LiBr P4/mmm phase is a ground state; uncertainty remains about its finite-temperature stability.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized because CS1–CS2 require high vacuum, cryogenic substrates, evaporation hardware, and anhydrous precursor handling. CS1 explicitly includes drying at 200 °C under vacuum, evaporation at 430 °C, high vacuum below 10⁻⁶ mbar, and −196 °C substrate cooling. The maximum thermal load is not high, but the vacuum/cryogenic requirements make the route experimentally challenging.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The vapor availability is strong because CS1 directly evaporates LiBr-containing materials and CS2 evaporates alkali halides. Crystallization is moderately favorable because CS1–CS2 show metastable alkali-halide structures can be trapped by low-temperature condensation, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact tetragonal P4/mmm LiBr polymorph. Composition control is comparatively favorable because the target is binary LiBr, though deposition rate and re-evaporation must be controlled.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
SS1–SS2 and TS1/TS4 demonstrate sealed-ampoule melt growth of LiBr-containing bromides using anhydrous halides, evacuated quartz ampoules, glovebox handling, and controlled cooling. The route avoids >1000 °C conditions and high pressure, but it requires sealed systems and moisture exclusion. Volatility/corrosivity of molten bromides and ampoule compatibility remain practical concerns.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Melt processing gives excellent ion mobility, as supported by SS1–SS2/TS1/TS4 where LiBr + MgBr₂ melts crystallize Li–Mg–Br phases. The limiting kinetic/structural issue is that pure LiBr is expected to crystallize its stable rocksalt polymorph unless the tetragonal structure is kinetically trapped; SS/TS analogues do not directly solve this for binary LiBr. Therefore, reaction kinetics are good for crystallization but only moderate for obtaining the desired polymorph.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 uses relatively mild annealing temperatures inferred from SS3–SS5/TS2/TS5 bromide anneals and TS3 dry LiBr solid-state chemistry. It is still penalized for dry/inert or sealed conditions, likely multi-step handling, and moisture sensitivity. Insufficient evidence from CS for a direct LiBr solid-state polymorph conversion means the route is conditionally plausible but not strongly supported.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
TS3 confirms LiBr can participate in dry solid-state halide reactions, but a one-component LiBr polymorphic conversion has low chemical driving force. Solid-state diffusion may be adequate for Li⁺ at moderate temperatures, but reconstructing the Li–Br framework into a layered P4/mmm sheet is likely difficult. TS3’s report of complex temperature-dependent phase behavior in a Li–Br material reinforces the risk of narrow stability windows.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only Li as the cation, so there is no multi-cation radius mismatch trigger. The described structure has two inequivalent Li and Br sites and does not appear to exceed a 40-atom unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise. The target has corner and edge sharing but no face-sharing coexistence, and SS1–SS2/TS1/TS4 show related Li–Br polyhedral networks crystallize successfully, so amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.025 eV/atom and CS1–CS2 show alkali-halide metastable polymorphs can require low-temperature deposition. The strongest support comes from CS1–CS2, which directly demonstrate vapor deposition of LiBr/LiI-type alkali halides under cryogenic high-vacuum conditions; SS1–SS2 and TS1/TS4 support the feasibility of Li–Br octahedral/square-pyramidal frameworks but in ternary bromides. The critical enabling factor is kinetic trapping at low temperature before LiBr relaxes to its stable ambient structure, as shown for related alkali halide modifications in CS1–CS2. The critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: bulk melt or anneal routes are likely to favor ordinary rocksalt LiBr unless cooling, substrate constraint, or quenching captures the P4/mmm arrangement. Overall, the phase is synthesizable in principle, with thin-film/cryogenic deposition clearly favored over equilibrium bulk synthesis.
