### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability threshold in the requested rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar or slightly higher E_hull are experimentally synthesized, including KFeBr₃ at 0.0516 eV/atom [TS1], CsCrBr₃ at 0.0667 eV/atom [TS2], and CsCrI₃ at 0.0566 eV/atom [TS3]. Chemical analogues RbVBr₃ are essentially on the hull and are grown successfully [CS1–CS3]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the P6₃cm polymorph of RbMnBr₃ is the experimentally preferred polymorph or a near-degenerate metastable variant.

#### F2 — Route 1, Bridgman / melt growth

Route 1 requires evacuated sealed ampoules and high-temperature molten bromide handling, both supported by RbVBr₃ and CsCrBr₃ procedures [CS1–CS3, TS2]. Temperatures are moderate for solid-state chemistry, likely below 1000 °C based on Rb₃V₂Br₉ at 700 °C and CsCrBr₃ at 750 °C [CS5, TS2]. The main severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile bromides, multi-step crystal growth, and air/moisture sensitivity [CS2, CS5, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from SS for the exact Bridgman temperature of RbMnBr₃, so SS is treated as neutral for this route-specific temperature estimate.

#### F3 — Route 1, Bridgman / melt growth

Kinetics are favorable because melt growth bypasses long-range solid diffusion, as shown for RbVBr₃ and CsCrBr₃ [CS1–CS3, TS2]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because P6₃cm RbVBr₃ is a very close ABBr₃ analogue with face-sharing MBr₆ octahedra [CS3]. The uncertainty is the Mn²⁺-specific phase field, since CS analogues use V and TS analogues use Cr/Fe rather than Mn [CS1–CS3, TS1, TS2]. SS provides useful Mn–Br molten-salt evidence through Cs₃MnBr₅ but not the exact ABBr₃ stoichiometry [SS3].

#### F2 — Route 2, direct solid-state annealing

Direct annealing uses simpler equipment than Bridgman, as KFeBr₃ is made from KBr + FeBr₂ in evacuated quartz ampoules at 873 K [TS1]. Structurally similar halides are also made in evacuated glass ampoules with intermediate grindings [SS1, SS2]. Severity remains moderate because sealed evacuated tubes, bromide volatility, air sensitivity, and repeated grinding/annealing are likely required [SS1, SS2, TS1]. CS evidence for a direct low-temperature RbMnBr₃ solid-state route is insufficient, so CS is neutral for the exact annealing window.

#### F3 — Route 2, direct solid-state annealing

Binary bromides are more reactive than oxides and the analogous KFeBr₃ reaction is straightforward [TS1]. However, solid-state halide analogues sometimes need long annealing times up to weeks and intermediate grindings to reach single phase [SS1, SS2]. Structural assembly from separate RbBr and MnBr₂ is more demanding than crystallization from a melt [Reasonable Inference], so kinetics are lower than Route 1. The main uncertainty is whether RbMnBr₃ competes strongly with Rb-rich manganese bromides like Cs₃MnBr₅-type phases [SS3].

#### F2 — Route 3, molten-salt / self-flux

Molten-salt synthesis is supported directly for Mn bromides by Cs₃MnBr₅ from CsBr/MnBr₂ at 460–800 °C [SS3]. Rb-containing bromide melts are also supported by Rb₃V₂Br₉ at 700 °C under dry N₂/He [CS5]. Severity is reduced by moderate temperature but increased by sealed/inert handling, volatile bromides, and flux/crystal separation [SS3, CS5]. TS evidence supports melt growth of ABBr₃ bromides but not Rb/Mn specifically [TS2].

#### F3 — Route 3, molten-salt / self-flux

Molten salt improves ion transport and nucleation control, as seen for Cs₃MnBr₅ and Rb₃V₂Br₉ syntheses [SS3, CS5]. Kinetic barriers should be lower than direct solid-state annealing because the reacting medium is liquid [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting kinetic issue is phase selectivity: excess alkali bromide can favor A₃MBr₅-type bromomanganates, as shown by Cs₃MnBr₅ [SS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS to define the exact RbBr–MnBr₂ flux composition window, so composition screening is required.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a very large alkali cation site with small MnBr₆ octahedra, similar to RbVBr₃ frameworks where Rb is 12-coordinate and V is octahedral [CS1–CS3]. Mixed face/corner/edge-sharing connectivity increases disorder or polymorph risk, and similar mixed connectivity appears in RbVBr₃ and structural halide analogues [CS3, SS1, SS2]. The target is nevertheless an ionic crystalline halide family with many experimentally synthesized analogues [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3], so amorphization risk is medium rather than prohibitive. Exact unit-cell atom count is not fully constrained by the provided CS/SS/TS data, so that check is treated conservatively.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbMnBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state or low-metastability phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and comparable thermodynamic analogues are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS3]. The strongest support comes from chemically similar RbVBr₃, including a P6₃cm polymorph grown by Bridgman [CS3], and from ABBr₃ bromide analogues made from alkali bromide + divalent metal bromide melts [TS1, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and self-fluxing character of molten bromide salts, which supports crystallization at moderate temperatures [CS5, SS3, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the RbBr–MnBr₂ system, because Mn bromides can form other stoichiometries such as A₃MnBr₅-type compounds [SS3]. Overall, synthesis is feasible, with Bridgman or controlled molten-salt crystallization preferred over simple solid-state annealing.
