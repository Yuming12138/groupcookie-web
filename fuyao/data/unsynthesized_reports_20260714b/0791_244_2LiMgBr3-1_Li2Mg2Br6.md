### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0410 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range, so a score of 92 is assigned. This is consistent with chemically and thermodynamically similar halides with low E_hull values, including Li₂MgBr₄ at 0.0146 eV/atom (CS-1, SS-1, TS-5), Li₆MgBr₈ at 0.0199 eV/atom (TS-4), Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.0287 eV/atom (CS-2, TS-1), and Li₂ZnBr₄ at 0.0172 eV/atom (CS-3, TS-2). The remaining uncertainty is that 0.041 eV/atom indicates slight metastability rather than a clear ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives F2 = 65 because it avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, matching the moderate sealed-ampoule melt conditions used for Li–Mg–Br analogues (CS-1, TS-4, TS-5). Deductions are applied for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation and air/moisture-sensitive anhydrous bromides, both supported by evacuated quartz ampoule and anhydrous precursor requirements (CS-1, SS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The uncertainty is the exact melting/crystallization temperature of LiMgBr₃, which is not given in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 receives F3 = 82 because molten LiBr/MgBr₂ should have high ionic mobility, and Li–Mg–Br analogues are synthesized by direct melting and slow cooling (CS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The main kinetic penalty is structural selection: the target P-31m layered LiMgBr₃ differs from reported Cmmm Li₂MgBr₄ and cubic Li₆MgBr₈, so crystallization may favor competing phases (target; CS-1; TS-4/TS-5). The uncertainty is whether LiMgBr₃ has a stable primary crystallization field.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 receives F2 = 55 because annealing/quenching is more procedural than the direct melt route, similar to Li₂FeBr₄ preparation by fusing, annealing 5 days at 723 K, and quenching (CS-2, TS-1). Long tempering under dry/inert conditions is also supported by Li₂ZnBr₄/Na₂ZnBr₄-type syntheses requiring oxygen- and water-free Ar and several weeks (CS-3, SS-2, SS-3, TS-2). The route is not extreme in temperature or pressure, but sealed dry-bromide handling and multistep processing reduce the score.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 receives F3 = 75 because binary bromide precursors are more reactive than refractory oxide precursors, and analogous bromides form under moderate annealing conditions (CS-2, TS-1, CS-3, TS-2). However, solid-state or partially molten annealing may require cation ordering and framework rearrangement into the layered P-31m phase, which is not directly demonstrated by the analogues (target; CS-1; TS-5). The uncertainty is whether quenching helps preserve LiMgBr₃ or instead traps competing disordered phases.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 receives F2 = 55 because it uses the same sealed dry bromide environment as Route 1, but adds crystal-growth complexity such as composition/cooling-rate screening and possible flux separation [Reasonable Inference]. The need for evacuated ampoules and Ar glovebox handling is directly supported by Li–Mg–Br melt-growth analogues (CS-1, SS-1, TS-4, TS-5). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is applied because analogous bromides are made by moderate-temperature ampoule methods (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 receives F3 = 83 because liquid/flux growth gives excellent ionic mobility, and the same LiBr/MgBr₂ system has demonstrated melt crystallization behavior (CS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The structural-reconstruction score is modest rather than high because the known Li–Mg–Br products are Li₂MgBr₄ and Li₆MgBr₈, not LiMgBr₃ (CS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The uncertainty is whether the target layered phase can nucleate before more stable neighboring compositions crystallize.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

F4 = 80 because only one amorphization trigger is clearly assigned: Li and Mg have different preferred coordination environments in the target, with Li trigonal planar and Mg octahedral (target description). The target does not appear to have a large unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing network from the provided structure description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so their source-specific contribution is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiMgBr₃ is likely a **slightly metastable but accessible** phase because its E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, below the common 0.05 eV/atom practical metastability threshold. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of sealed-ampoule bromide synthesis, especially because Li₂MgBr₄ and Li₆MgBr₈ are made directly from LiBr + MgBr₂ under melt-growth conditions (CS-1, SS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The most important enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of molten/annealed bromide salts, which should allow Li/Mg/Br equilibration without extreme temperatures (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1, TS-5). The most important limiting factor is phase competition within the LiBr–MgBr₂ system, since known Li–Mg–Br compounds with different stoichiometries form from the same precursors (CS-1, TS-4, TS-5). Overall, the target is plausible but will probably require careful stoichiometry, sealed dry handling, and cooling-rate optimization.
