### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the prompt’s ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score boundary. CS and SS strongly support stability because same-composition Sr₂ZnWO₆ entries lie very close to the hull, 0.0048–0.0066 eV/atom [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. TS gives mixed but generally supportive evidence because thermodynamically similar oxides around 0.044–0.068 eV/atom are experimentally accessible, though some require special processing [TS1, TS2, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the exact triclinic P-1 polymorph is thermodynamic or metastable relative to the monoclinic/tetragonal Sr₂ZnWO₆ forms reported in CS/SS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Condition severity is moderate because the same compound is made in air at ambient pressure using standard furnaces, mortars, and crucibles [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The main penalties are the >1000 °C firing and multi-step grinding/heating cycles [CS1, CS2]. Kinetics are moderate rather than easy because SrCO₃/ZnO/WO₃ are stable powders and long annealing is required [CS1, CS2], but the desired double-perovskite framework is directly accessible [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: CS/SS confirm Sr₂ZnWO₆ but not specifically the target P-1 symmetry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

The sol–gel route has similar final thermal severity because the final crystalline product should still require annealing near the CS/SS window of 1000–1100 °C [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. Kinetics may improve because solution precursors give shorter diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS example directly proves Sr₂ZnWO₆ sol–gel synthesis, so the structural-reconstruction component is penalized. This route is attractive if ceramic mixing leaves SrWO₄ or unreacted ZnO/WO₃ impurities [Reasonable Inference; impurity issues in related systems from CS5, SS5]. The main uncertainty is avoiding tungstate precipitation and maintaining exact Sr:Zn:W stoichiometry in solution.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

High-pressure treatment is more severe because TS uses specialized cubic-anvil equipment and capsules for thermodynamically similar oxide synthesis [TS1, TS2]. It is not required by the direct CS/SS Sr₂ZnWO₆ evidence [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2], so it is best treated as a backup polymorph-stabilization method. Kinetically, using pre-reacted Sr₂ZnWO₆ should avoid complete reconstruction [CS1, CS2], but short HP holds may not equilibrate cation ordering or tilt distortions [Reasonable Inference from TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether pressure favors the desired P-1 distortion or instead reverts to another perovskite polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with substantially different size/charge preferences, Sr²⁺ on the A site and Zn²⁺/W⁶⁺ on octahedral B sites, so one amorphization trigger is counted [Reasonable Inference; coordination environments supported by target description and CS/SS]. However, the structure is a robust corner-sharing oxide perovskite-derived network, and direct CS/SS analogues crystallize well by ceramic routes [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The unit cell is not evidently >40 atoms based on compact Sr₂ZnWO₆/Sr₄Zn₂W₂O₁₂ descriptions [CS1, SS2], and the target B-site octahedra are corner-sharing rather than mixed edge/face connected [target description; CS1, CS2]. The residual risk is mostly formation of poorly ordered or multiphase ceramics rather than a persistent amorphous phase.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂ZnWO₆ in the target P-1 form is likely near-stable or mildly metastable, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom while same-composition monoclinic/tetragonal Sr₂ZnWO₆ analogues are much closer to the hull [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The CS and SS evidence strongly supports synthesis of the Sr₂ZnWO₆ double-perovskite composition by ambient-pressure air firing, but does not uniquely confirm the triclinic P-1 polymorph [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Sr₂BWO₆ double-perovskite chemistry, where W⁶⁺/Zn²⁺ charge contrast favors B-site ordering and corner-sharing octahedral connectivity [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and impurity control, especially avoiding SrWO₄/Sr₂WO₅-type competing phases and ensuring sufficient Zn/W ordering [CS5, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is quite synthesizable as Sr₂ZnWO₆, with moderate uncertainty about isolating the exact P-1 distortion.
