### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high-synthesizability threshold in the required rubric. CS supports this because CS-1 is synthesized at E_hull ≈ 0.0589 eV/atom and several CS analogues are on or near the hull; SS supports this because SS-5 is synthesized at E_hull ≈ 0.0698 eV/atom and SS-2/SS-4 are near the hull; TS strongly supports this because the thermodynamic-neighbor set contains synthesized phases at ~0.06–0.10 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom may correspond to a metastable rather than true ground-state phase.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The ceramic route is supported by SS-4, where BiTaO4 forms at 1200 °C for 24 h, and by CS-3/CS-4, where Na-containing complex oxides are made at 800–1200 °C. The severity is penalized for likely high temperature, multistep grinding/annealing, and Na/Te volatility management; alkali excess in CS-3/CS-4 and SS-3 supports that stoichiometry loss is a real issue. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for Te volatility in this exact phase, so that penalty is partly [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binary oxide/carbonate ceramic synthesis has slow kinetics because Ta2O5 is refractory [Reasonable Inference], although SS-4 shows Bi–Ta oxide formation is achievable. TS-2 and CS-3/CS-4 indicate that long heating and repeated grinding can be necessary for multication oxides. The low-symmetry P1 target likely requires more complete cation rearrangement than simple BiTaO4, increasing kinetic uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Sealed-tube processing is directly supported by SS-1, SS-5/TS-1, and CS-1, so it is plausible for retaining volatile components. The route avoids >1000 °C in the proposed adaptation, but vacuum sealing and volatile-component containment increase severity. The main uncertainty is tube compatibility and vapor pressure for Na–Te–Bi oxide mixtures, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The sealed environment can preserve stoichiometry and may enable vapor/low-melting transport [Reasonable Inference], consistent with pure Bi4TaO8Cl formation in sealed tubes (SS-5/TS-1). However, Ta diffusion remains slow, and no analogue proves that Na–Bi–Ta–Te ordering occurs below 900 °C. Thus kinetics are only slightly better than the ceramic route.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The reactive-precursor route avoids the highest temperatures and high pressure, making conditions milder than Route 1 or 2. TS-2 supports use of Bi nitrate in oxide synthesis, while CS/SS/TS do not directly support full sol-gel processing, so the method is inferential. The penalties come from multistep processing, toxic Te sources, and residual volatile Na/Te control.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Soluble or molecular precursors should give high precursor activity and shorter diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], addressing the regrinding/long-calcination issues seen in TS-2 and CS-3/CS-4. The limiting factor remains crystallization into the ordered triclinic P1 structure, not simply mixing. Because no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates the exact sol-gel route, the high kinetic score is moderated.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains chemically and size-diverse cations, especially large Na/Bi and small Ta, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description lists one Na, one Ta, one Bi, one Te, and six inequivalent oxygen sites, suggesting the primitive P1 cell may be small, so C2 is not triggered unless Z > 4; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target Z. C3 is not triggered because the target description does not show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaTaBiTeO6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues with comparable or larger E_hull values are experimentally synthesized. The analogue support is good for Bi–Ta oxide frameworks from SS-4 and for Na-containing ceramic oxides from CS-3/CS-4, but weak for the full Na–Bi–Ta–Te–O composition because none of CS/SS/TS includes the same Te-containing chemistry. The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for Bi–Ta–O phase formation and tolerance of metastability around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom in SS/TS. The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of Na and Te volatility/redox while achieving diffusion through refractory Ta–O chemistry. A reactive precursor route is therefore preferred, with sealed-tube synthesis as a backup if volatile loss prevents phase formation.
