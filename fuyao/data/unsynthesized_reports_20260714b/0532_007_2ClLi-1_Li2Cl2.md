### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0300 eV/atom, which lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range assigned 90–100. TS provides strong support because several thermodynamically similar halides with E_hull ≈0.0199–0.0394 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by sealed chloride/halide routes (TS-1–TS-5). SS also supports feasibility because Li₆CoCl₈ has a similar E_hull of 0.0309 eV/atom and is synthesized from LiCl/CoCl₂ at 400 °C (SS-5). CS is less directly useful for binary LiCl polymorph stability; insufficient evidence from CS, neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses sealed dry Ar/vacuum handling, matching the requirements of Li-chloride analogues made in sealed ampoules or evacuated Pyrex tubes (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5; TS-1/TS-2). It avoids >1000 °C temperatures and high pressure, unlike harsher CS examples such as ScCl at 960 °C for two months in tantalum tubing (CS-2). The main uncertainty is whether heating above the LiCl melt stabilizes the desired P4/nmm polymorph or simply recrystallizes rock-salt LiCl [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the target contains only Li and Cl, and molten/soft halide salts allow rapid ion transport; similar Li-chloride materials are made at moderate temperatures from anhydrous salts (SS-2–SS-5; TS-1–TS-3). The limiting kinetic factor is not diffusion but polymorph selection: the layered P4/nmm arrangement requires structural reconstruction relative to ordinary LiCl [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5 and TS-3 show that related chloride systems can require many samples, careful phase identification, or have distorted competing phases, so the structural-reconstruction subscore is lowered.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has more operational severity than Route 1 because it adds a flux component, drying, sealing, slow cooling, and flux removal [Reasonable Inference from SS-3 and TS-4]. SS-2 and SS-3 show that alkali lithium chlorides require dry argon, vacuum sealing, and controlled heating/cooling, so controlled atmosphere and sealed-system deductions apply. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is needed because the relevant SS flux examples operate near 400 °C or 573–673 K (SS-2, SS-3).

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves kinetics because molten chloride media reduce diffusion barriers, as demonstrated by RbLiCl₂ and CsLi₂Cl₃ growth from LiCl-containing chloride melts (SS-2, SS-3). TS-4 also supports controlled cooling from halide melts as an effective route to Li-containing halide crystals. The uncertainty is chemical selectivity: the same fluxes that improve kinetics can form ternary phases, directly shown by SS-2 and SS-3, so pure binary tetragonal LiCl may be difficult.

#### F2 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition avoids high bulk temperatures and high pressure [Reasonable Inference], but it requires high vacuum or inert atmosphere, and CS/SS/TS provide no direct thin-film precedent. The need for moisture exclusion is supported indirectly by Li-chloride analogues that require glovebox, Schlenk, dry argon, or evacuated ampoules (SS-2, SS-5; TS-1/TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for actual LiCl P4/nmm deposition makes the severity score uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3

The film route may be kinetically favorable because short diffusion lengths and substrate templating can stabilize metastable phases [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct crystallization of a tetragonal LiCl film, so the crystallization pathway score is moderate rather than high. Composition control is simpler for binary LiCl than for multi-cation chlorides [Reasonable Inference], but LiCl volatility and moisture sensitivity remain practical concerns.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius-difference check is not triggered [Target]. The reported structure consists of LiCl sheets with edge- and corner-sharing square pyramids but no face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered [Target]. SS/TS analogues with edge/corner-sharing LiCl₆ or LiCl₄/ClLi₆ motifs crystallize successfully (SS-4/SS-5; TS-1/TS-2), supporting a low amorphization risk; CS provides insufficient evidence and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **metastable** LiCl polymorph rather than the ground-state rock-salt form, because its E_hull is 0.0300 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom [Target]. Thermodynamics are favorable: TS shows several halide analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally accessible by sealed ampoule or melt methods (TS-1–TS-5). The strongest evidence comes from SS and TS Li-chloride analogues using anhydrous LiCl, sealed ampoules, vacuum/dry Ar, and moderate temperatures (SS-2–SS-5; TS-1–TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the very low E_hull combined with fast ion transport in molten or soft halide media [Target; SS-2/SS-3; TS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph competition with stable LiCl and possible reversion to rock-salt LiCl during cooling or annealing [Reasonable Inference; phase-control challenges in SS-2/SS-5/TS-3 support the concern].
