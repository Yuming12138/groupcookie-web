### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0170 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so it is likely experimentally accessible as a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase. CS analogues include near-hull heavy-metal halides such as Tl₄HgBr₆ at 0.0133 eV/atom (CS3), TlCdI₃ at 0.0167 eV/atom (CS4), and AgTl₂I₃ at 0.0272 eV/atom (CS5). SS/TS analogues also cluster near the hull, including HgBrI at 0.0105 eV/atom (SS1/TS2) and Hg₃S₂ICl at 0.0139 eV/atom (SS3/TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not identify the exact competing phases in the Ag–Hg–Br system.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state

The moderate F2 score reflects the need for vacuum-sealed ampoules and toxic HgBr₂, consistent with sealed Hg-halide reactions in CS1, SS2, TS4, and TS5. Temperatures are not severe because related Hg-halide systems form at 200–280 °C (CS1) or 260 °C (SS2). Kinetics are favorable relative to oxides because binary bromides are reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the long holds in CS1 and TS4/TS5 suggest slow ordering or crystal growth can still be limiting. The main uncertainty is whether AgBr and HgBr₂ react cleanly before HgBr₂ redistribution or sublimation causes stoichiometry gradients.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal HBr

This route has the best F2 because it uses low temperature and avoids vacuum ampoule synthesis, with direct analogues at 120–200 °C in hydrohalic acid media (CS3) and 155–175 °C in 25% HI (CS5). Its F3 is high because solution transport should overcome solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, and both Ag halides and Hg halides can form halometallate complexes in concentrated hydrogen halide media **[Reasonable Inference]**. Phase control is not perfect: CS3 notes that some targeted halide compositions can yield different phases, and CS5 reports twinning/low yield for untwinned crystals. Direct evidence for AgHgBr₃ in HBr is absent, so the route is strongly plausible but not guaranteed.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT / sublimation-assisted growth

The F2 penalty is larger because CVT requires vacuum ampoules, volatile Hg halides, toxic precursors, and temperature gradients, all supported by SS3, SS4, and TS1. Kinetically, vapor transport is attractive because SS3/SS4/TS1 demonstrate gas-phase growth of Hg-halide-containing phases. However, SS4 reports polymorphous modifications and halogen-site disorder, so phase selection may be narrow. The greatest uncertainty is Ag transport, since AgBr is much less volatile than HgBr₂ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Halide melt / Bridgman

Melt growth has favorable transport kinetics because molten halides provide high mobility **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS4 shows Bridgman growth for a chemically similar heavy-metal halide. The condition score is reduced because sealed ampoules, volatile HgBr₂, and crystal-growth temperature gradients are expected **[Reasonable Inference]**, while sealed/vacuum halide synthesis is common in CS1, CS2, SS2, TS4, and TS5. The route is less certain because CS4 is iodide-based and does not include Hg volatility. It may be better for single crystals after the phase field is established by hydrothermal or solid-state screening.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple AgHgBr₃ composition and no evidence of a very large unit cell, so the atom-count trigger is not activated **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target structure includes corner-sharing and edge-sharing polyhedra, but face sharing is not reported in the target description, so the strict mixed-connectivity trigger is not activated. Ag⁺/Hg²⁺ size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% in soft bromide coordination **[Reasonable Inference]**, but CS/SS/TS do not directly provide ionic radii. The amorphization risk is therefore low, though layered phases can still show stacking disorder **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgHgBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0170 eV/atom, and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also near the hull (CS3, CS4, CS5, SS1/TS2, SS3/TS1). The analogue support is strong for Hg-halide sealed-ampoule chemistry (CS1, SS2, SS4, TS4, TS5) and especially strong for low-temperature hydrothermal halide crystallization involving Hg(II) halides and monovalent halides (CS3, CS5). The most critical enabling factor is high halide mobility, either through acidic solution complexation in HBr or through volatile HgBr₂ in sealed ampoules **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Ag:Hg:Br stoichiometry while avoiding HgBr₂ loss, competing binary bromides, or alternative bromometallate phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target is experimentally plausible, with hydrothermal HBr screening recommended first and sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis as a robust backup.
