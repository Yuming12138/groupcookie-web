### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 scoring band by the provided rubric. This is strongly supported by thermodynamic analogues: TS-1, TS-2, and TS-4 are also on the hull, while TS-3 and TS-5 are within 0.01 eV/atom. Chemically similar materials include both stable and moderately metastable phases, with CS-2 and CS-5 at E_hull = 0 but CS-1 at 0.146 eV/atom, so CS support is positive but less uniform. Remaining uncertainty is that computational ground-state stability does not ensure kinetic accessibility, especially for a quaternary-like ordering problem reduced here to ternary Nb–Si–Se.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because CS-1/CS-2 show 800 °C, 48 h, evacuated sealed-tube pellet reactions, and CS-5 shows selenides can form at 650 °C for 2 days. The main F2 penalties are vacuum, volatile Se requiring sealing, and toxic Se handling, all consistent with the sealed selenide practices in CS/SS/TS. Kinetics are less favorable because Nb and Si are refractory/low-activity elemental precursors [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1/CS-2/CS-4 report impurity or phase-control challenges in related Nb–Se systems. The route is nevertheless plausible because many chemical analogues are made by essentially this method.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-temperature staged direct synthesis

This route has higher severity because it deliberately uses >1000 °C conditions, supported by CS-3, CS-4, SS-1, and TS-5. Staged heating is justified by CS-4 and by the need to avoid rapid Se volatilization and ampoule overpressure [Reasonable Inference], but it adds process complexity. Kinetically, higher temperature helps diffusion of Nb and Si, but the target still requires ordered incorporation of Si into a layered Nb–Se framework. The main uncertainty is whether high temperature stabilizes the desired Nb₃SiSe₆ phase or instead favors binary NbSe₂/Nb₃Se₄ or silicide/selenide impurities.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Chemical vapor transport / vapor-assisted crystal growth

Vapor transport is well supported for structurally and thermodynamically similar layered NbSe₂ materials at 750–950 °C (SS-3, SS-4, TS-1, TS-3), and TS-4 shows CVT can form a complex 2D Nb–chalcogenide using Br₂. F2 is penalized by vacuum ampoule use, volatile/toxic halogen transport agents, Se volatility, and multiple procedural steps. F3 is better than bulk routes because vapor transport can bypass some solid-state diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], but composition control is a serious concern because Si transport chemistry is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS. The strongest uncertainty is whether Nb, Si, and Se transport congruently enough to yield stoichiometric Nb₃SiSe₆ rather than NbSe₂-rich crystals.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a likely cation size mismatch between Nb and Si, triggering C1 by physicochemical reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for this criterion is treated as neutral 50. Mixed connectivity is plausible because CS-1 through CS-4 and TS-4 show complex edge/corner/face-sharing Nb–Se networks, but the target description does not explicitly state such connectivity, so C3 is not confidently triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SiSe₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several CS/SS/TS analogues are also stable or near-stable. The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong for Nb–Se layered frameworks and ternary Nb–M–Se compounds, especially from sealed-tube solid-state reactions and vapor transport routes (CS-1–CS-5; SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust formation chemistry of layered Nb–Se networks under evacuated sealed-tube or CVT conditions. The most critical limiting factor is the absence of direct Si-containing analogue evidence, making Si incorporation, stoichiometry control, and competing binary/ternary phases the main uncertainties. Overall, the material appears synthesizable with moderate difficulty, with CVT or vapor-assisted growth offering the best balance between crystallinity and kinetic access.
