### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.036 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable metastability range ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. The closest SnO analogue is experimentally synthesized even though it is higher in energy, E_hull = 0.125 eV/atom, which strongly supports accessibility of the target’s lower-energy SnO polymorph [CS1/SS1/TS2]. TS analogues near the same thermodynamic regime, including E_hull ≈ 0.018–0.050 eV/atom, are also experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS3, TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic accessibility does not guarantee selection of the Pmn2₁ polymorph over other SnO polymorphs [Target, CS1/SS1/TS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild thermal severity because the closest SnO analogue forms at 95 °C and ambient pressure using standard solution equipment [CS1/SS1/TS2]. The main F2 penalty is procedural complexity: dissolution, pH adjustment, aging, filtration, washing, and drying [CS1/SS1/TS2]. Kinetics are favorable because solution precipitation bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion [CS1/SS1/TS2]. The principal uncertainty is phase selectivity, because the analogue is described as metastable and sensitive to pH/pressure deviations [CS1/SS1/TS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal/alkaline crystallization has modest severity because analogous hydrothermal oxides crystallize at 503 K in Teflon-lined vessels and Sn(II) hydroxostannate chemistry occurs near 65 °C under N₂ [SS3/TS1, SS4]. The penalties are Sn(II) oxidation sensitivity and multi-step solution preparation [SS4, CS1]. Kinetics should be good because solution/hydrothermal transport enables crystallization at low temperature [SS3/TS1]. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Pmn2₁ SnO from hydrothermal conditions, so this route may yield hydroxides, SnO₂, or other SnO polymorphs [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Mechanochemistry is low-temperature but requires controlled N₂/H₂ atmosphere in the closest mechanochemical oxide analogue [CS4]. It is kinetically plausible because high-energy milling can transform oxide polymorphs at room temperature [CS4]. However, structural reconstruction of SnO sheets may compete with amorphization, oxidation, or formation of a different SnO polymorph [CS4; CS1/SS1/TS2]. Evidence is indirect because no source reports mechanochemical Pmn2₁ SnO [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Thin-film PVD has moderate condition severity because oxide formation by metal evaporation occurs at 350–400 °C under 10⁻⁵ Torr in the analogue [CS2]. Kinetic stabilization of metastable oxides is plausible, but CS2 also reports multiple phases including metals and oxides, indicating poor phase selectivity [CS2]. Composition control for SnO is likely narrow because oxygen chemical potential must avoid both metallic Sn and SnO₂ [Reasonable Inference, supported by CS2 mixed-phase behavior]. This route is more suitable for exploratory thin films than bulk powders [CS2; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Sn, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The structure is a simple 2D SnO sheet with T-shaped Sn and trigonal O environments, and there is no evidence of a large >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network [Target]. The closest analogue has edge/corner-sharing tetrahedral descriptions but not simultaneous face-sharing connectivity, so amorphization risk remains low [CS1/SS1/TS2]. The remaining uncertainty is that metastable polymorph targeting may still cause disorder even if the intrinsic amorphization indicators are favorable [CS1/SS1/TS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable SnO polymorph rather than a strict ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.036 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is strong: the closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic reference is also orthorhombic 2D SnO and is experimentally synthesized by low-temperature precipitation [CS1/SS1/TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated Sn(II) chemistry, which allows SnO to crystallize at only 95 °C without solid-state diffusion limitations [CS1/SS1/TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and oxidation-state control: red SnO is described as metastable, pH-sensitive, and pressure-sensitive, while Sn(II) hydroxostannate chemistry is prone to oxidation/decomposition [CS1/SS1/TS2; SS4]. Overall, synthesis is quite plausible, but obtaining the exact Pmn2₁ phase may require careful pH, redox, and gentle post-synthesis handling [CS1/SS1/TS2; Reasonable Inference].
