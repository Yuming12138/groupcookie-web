### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0380 eV/atom, which falls in the high-synthesizability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Similar above-hull phases have been synthesized, including BiSeI at 0.0128 eV/atom (CS1), MnIn₂Se₄ at 0.0316 eV/atom (TS1), and InSe high-pressure polymorph at 0.0367 eV/atom (TS2). Several chemically similar halide chalcogenides are computed on the hull, including SnICl, GaTeCl, InTeBr, and RhTeCl (CS2–CS5), which supports the feasibility of p-block chalcogenide halides. The remaining uncertainty is that 0.038 eV/atom may indicate a metastable polytype rather than a ground-state phase.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT severity

Route 1 requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and temperature gradient, as directly shown for BiSeI CVT (CS1), ZnIn₂S₄ iodine-aided CVT (SS2/TS3), and MnIn₂Se₄ CVT under high vacuum (TS1). The need to control volatile iodine/chalcogen species adds severity, consistent with sealed ampoule methods in CS1 and SS2/TS3. No high pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required, so the route is moderate rather than extreme. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact InSI vapor transport window, so the score is penalized.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT kinetics

CVT can bypass slow bulk diffusion because volatile iodine-containing species transport material through the gas phase, supported by iodine-aided ZnIn₂S₄ synthesis (SS2/TS3) and AlCl₃-assisted MnIn₂Se₄ transport (TS1). BiSeI demonstrates that heavy chalcogenide iodides can crystallize by CVT (CS1). The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the exact trigonal P3m1 InSI sheet rather than a competing In–S or In–I phase. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an identical octahedral InS₃I₃ CVT product.

#### F2 — Route 2: binary sealed-ampoule severity

Route 2 is less severe than CVT because it can be executed as a direct binary precursor anneal without a long transport gradient. GaTeCl synthesis from GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ at 400 °C for 7 days in evacuated ampoules supports this strategy (CS3), while InTeBr chemistry involving InBr₃ + In₂Te₃ supports analogous In–halide/In–chalcogenide reactivity (CS4/SS1). Vacuum sealing and moisture-sensitive halides remain necessary, as in CS3 and CS4/SS1. Insufficient evidence from TS for a direct InSI binary-anneal route is noted; TS mainly supports thermodynamic plausibility rather than this specific precursor set.

#### F3 — Route 2: binary sealed-ampoule kinetics

In₂S₃ + InI₃ is a chemically activated pair because InI₃ can provide mobile iodide and indium at moderate temperature; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ forming GaTeCl (CS3). The cation diffusion burden is small because indium is not refractory and related halide/chalcogenide systems react at 400–520 °C (CS3, CS4/SS1). Structural reconstruction is still required because the target uses edge-sharing InS₃I₃ octahedra, whereas CS3/CS4 analogues are not identical. The route receives the best balance of manageable conditions and good chemical reactivity.

#### F2 — Route 3: melt / Bridgman severity

InTeBr was grown from the melt at 520 °C using a Bridgman arrangement (CS4/SS1), supporting a melt-growth route for In–chalcogen–halide crystals. However, InSI contains iodine and sulfur, both more volatile than Br/Te under many sealed-tube conditions, so ampoule pressure and stoichiometry control are more severe by [Reasonable Inference]. The route also requires multistep prereaction and slow crystal growth. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for congruent melting of InSI, making the route more uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3: melt / Bridgman kinetics

Melt growth offers the fastest atomic mobility and therefore favorable kinetics, as demonstrated by InTeBr crystal growth from the melt (CS4/SS1). The crystallization pathway may be efficient if InSI has a stable melt or self-flux region, but no direct phase diagram is supplied. If InSI melts incongruently, the pathway may instead produce In₂S₃, InI₃, or other iodide/sulfide phases; analogous phase-control issues occur in BiSeI due to incongruent melting (CS1) and in Tl/Hg/In sulfides with competing phases (SS4). Thus kinetics are high, but phase selection is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so the cation-size-mismatch criterion is not triggered. The described structure is a simple two-dimensional sheet of edge-sharing InS₃I₃ octahedra, not a large multi-cation framework, and no evidence suggests a >40 atom unit cell. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is not reported; the target is described as edge-sharing octahedra only. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for amorphous InSI formation, so the amorphization risk is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InSI is likely a mildly metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.0380 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized above-hull analogues such as MnIn₂Se₄ and InSe polymorphs (TS1, TS2). The CS set strongly supports chalcogenide-halide synthesis in sealed ampoules, especially BiSeI by CVT and InTeBr by melt growth (CS1, CS4), while SS/TS support layered In–chalcogen frameworks accessible by CVT or ampoule methods (SS2, TS1/TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high chemical mobility of iodine/indium iodide species, which should allow reaction at moderate temperature by analogy to halide-assisted syntheses (CS3, CS4, SS2/TS3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control under volatile iodine/sulfur conditions, as related systems show mixed phases, incongruent melting, or sensitivity to reaction conditions (CS1, CS2, SS4, TS5). Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with sealed-ampoule binary annealing as the safest first attempt.
