### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly into the highest rubric band of ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. CS/SS analogues TbB12, DyB12, TmB12, and YB12 are reported with E_hull = 0, supporting the inherent stability of the MB12 structure for several related cations [CS/SS]. TS analogues ThB12 and HfB12 are near the target’s thermodynamic range and are synthesized under high pressure, suggesting the target is accessible but likely not a trivially stable ambient-pressure phase [TS].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because CS/SS solid-state analogues require 1500 °C and vacuum, which triggers both high-temperature deductions and the controlled-atmosphere deduction [CS/SS]. Additional processing such as pressing, sintering, and surface-layer removal is supported by CS/SS and makes the route moderately multi-step [CS/SS]. The main uncertainty is that PrB12 itself is not directly reported by this route, and the oxide precursor identity in the CS/SS entries is chemically inconsistent, so this route is partly inferred [CS/SS; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Oxide + B reactions have limited precursor activity because rare-earth oxides and boron are refractory, and TS rare-earth boride preparations required high vacuum and temperatures up to 2100 °C [TS]. Diffusion of Pr itself is not the worst case because Pr is not a high-melting transition metal, but constructing the B12 cage network from oxide + B is a large structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether liquid Pr or sub-boride intermediates form and accelerate the reaction under B-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is severely penalized because CS/SS/TS analogues use 65 kbar pressure, which exceeds the >5 GPa high-pressure threshold [CS/SS/TS]. The route also uses temperatures above 1000 °C and likely above 1400 °C if conditions similar to ThB12 are required [TS]. The core synthesis is otherwise simple, because the cited HP routes mostly involve mixing elemental precursors and heating under pressure [CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental Pr + B should be kinetically more direct than oxide reduction because no oxygen removal step is needed [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS high-pressure analogues form MB12 products in relatively short holds of 5–75 min, supporting acceptable kinetics once high pressure and temperature are applied [CS/SS/TS]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is formation of the extended B12 framework from elemental B, which may compete with PrB6 or PrB4 formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized for very high temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum processing, and multiple operations, all of which are present in the TS ZrB12 floating-zone route [TS]. It avoids high pressure but requires specialized image-furnace or floating-zone equipment, so it remains challenging [TS]. The major uncertainty is whether PrB12 melts congruently or can sustain a stable molten zone, which is not established in CS/SS/TS [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A molten-zone route should provide fast mass transport, and TS ZrB12 growth at 5 mm/h supports efficient crystallization once the proper phase field is accessed [TS]. Using preformed lower borides plus B should reduce reconstruction relative to oxide + B, but ZrB12 required careful composition control due to incongruent melting and decomposition [TS]. For PrB12, the phase diagram and melt behavior are not provided, so transferability is uncertain [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The conventional Fm-3m MB12 cell contains 52 atoms, so the atom-count check is triggered [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS analogues show the same ordered cubic MB12 topology, so the framework is not an obviously glass-forming mixed-polyhedral system [CS/SS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PrB12 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. CS/SS analogues strongly support the structural and chemical plausibility of the Fm-3m MB12 framework, especially for rare-earth/Y dodecaborides [CS/SS]. TS analogues indicate that MB12 phases with similar thermodynamic penalty, such as HfB12 and ThB12, may require high pressure for reliable formation [TS]. The most important enabling factor is the robust B12 cage framework that is repeatedly observed across chemically and structurally similar MB12 compounds [CS/SS/TS]. The most important limiting factor is competition with lower praseodymium borides and the need to stabilize a B-rich phase at or near the metastability limit [TS; Reasonable Inference].
