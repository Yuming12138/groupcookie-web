### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-scoring threshold in the required rubric, so a score of 90 is assigned from the target data. TS provides useful support because similar low-dimensional chalcogen/cluster compounds with E_hull values near 0.05–0.10 eV/atom have reported syntheses, including Pd2Se12Cl4 and Tl2CS3 polymorphs (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). CS is partly contradictory because chlorometallate salts have very high computed hull energies yet are experimentally accessible, implying computed hulls for molecular salts may be imperfect but not necessarily fatal (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). SS gives moderate support through synthesized structurally similar chalcogen-rich compounds, though several require sealed systems or show phase-control issues (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild conditions because CS ammonium chlorometallates form by solution mixing, evaporation, and drying under air or mild heating (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5), while TS Tl2CS3 forms by aqueous precipitation, filtration, washing, and vacuum drying (TS-2, TS-4). The main severity deductions arise from toxic sulfide/polysulfide reagents and the practical need to limit oxidation under N2/Ar, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** from sulfide chemistry rather than explicitly stated in CS/TS. Kinetics are favorable because solution-phase ligand exchange/precipitation avoids long-range solid diffusion, consistent with the ease of crystallization or precipitation in CS and TS analogues (CS-1, CS-5, TS-2, TS-4). The uncertainty is that none of CS/TS directly shows conversion to a Pt–S/S–S/N cluster, so Pt redox/speciation could redirect the reaction to PtS, PtS2, or Pt chloride/polysulfide side products.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because SS/TS chalcogen-rich Pd(Se3Cl)2 requires sealed ampoules/autoclaves, vacuum, 180 °C, and two weeks (SS-2, TS-1). The route benefits kinetically from liquid/melt or solvothermal chalcogen media, as shown by Pd metal reacting at only 180 °C in selenium/SeX4 melt (SS-2, TS-1). However, the target requires retention or incorporation of N-containing clusters, and SS/TS sealed chalcogen analogues do not directly demonstrate that; this is the main structural-reconstruction uncertainty. Phase purity is also a concern because SS-2/TS-1 report difficulty obtaining phase-pure products and sensitivity to excess chalcogen.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by CS AuPS4 direct solid-state synthesis at 600 °C in evacuated ampoules and SS Pd sulfide flux synthesis at 650 °C for 10 d under vacuum (CS-3, SS-5). The severity is moderate-to-high because sealed vacuum, volatile sulfur/N sources, controlled heating/cooling, and flux removal are needed, similar to SS-5 and CS-4. Kinetics are less favorable than solution synthesis because Pt metal is refractory, although sulfur-rich flux can partially overcome this as inferred from SS-5. The largest uncertainty is that N-containing precursors may decompose or be expelled at 450–650 °C before the desired zero-dimensional PtS6N2 cluster assembles **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is zero-dimensional and molecular/cluster-like, so extended mixed polyhedral connectivity is absent according to the target structure. The atom count implied by the described clusters is below the >40 trigger, and there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch because Pt is the only metal cation. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for PtS6N2, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral with respect to amorphization mechanisms; the score is based primarily on the target structural description. Remaining uncertainty concerns possible disorder of N-containing clusters, but this is more a crystallization/occupancy issue than a glass-forming mixed-network issue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PtS6N2 is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS and TS strongly support that zero-dimensional molecular salts and cluster compounds can be synthesized by mild precipitation/evaporation, while SS/TS support that square-planar late-transition-metal chalcogen complexes can form in chalcogen-rich sealed systems. The most important enabling factor is the zero-dimensional cluster nature: such phases often crystallize from solution or low-temperature chalcogen-rich media before extended binary sulfides dominate, as suggested by CS/TS precipitation analogues. The most critical limiting factor is Pt–S redox/speciation control, because sulfide can reduce Pt and precipitate simple PtS/PtS2 instead of forming the desired Pt–S/S–S molecular cluster **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the material is plausible to synthesize, with Route 1 as the best first attempt and Route 2 as a backup crystal-growth route.
