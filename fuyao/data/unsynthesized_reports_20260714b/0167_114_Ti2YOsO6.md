### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the provided ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bracket. Synthesized CS analogues include stable or near-hull oxides such as CaAlTaO5 at 0 eV/atom and La2Ti3Ag2O10 at 0.0597 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-3), while SS and TS analogues include YTiO3 and SmTiO3 near 0.048–0.058 eV/atom (SS-3, SS-5, TS-1). The remaining uncertainty is that the target contains Os, and no direct Y–Ti–Os–O analogue is provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits conventional high-temperature solid-state requirements from CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5, where multication oxides require 900–1773 K/°C-scale firing and sometimes multiple heating steps. Vacuum/Ar control is supported by SS-1, SS-3, TS-2, and TS-3, and is likely needed because rare-earth titanates require oxygen-stoichiometry control (SS-2, SS-4, TS-1). The major uncertainty is Os volatility/toxicity, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence, so that penalty is based on [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is low because the likely precursors are refractory oxides/metals, similar to oxide precursors used in CS-4/CS-5 and SS-3. Multiple firings and regrinding are common in SmTiO3 and LaTiO3 analogues (SS-3, TS-2), indicating slow diffusion and phase-purity challenges. The triclinic target is structurally less direct than perovskite YTiO3 analogues (SS-5, TS-1), so structural reconstruction is expected to be substantial [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is severe because structurally similar rare-earth titanates can require arc melting, floating-zone growth, or melt growth under purified Ar or Ar/H2 (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1). Temperatures can exceed 1400 °C in melt-growth analogues (SS-4), and feed preparation plus growth plus annealing constitute a multistep route. Os volatility at such temperatures is a significant inferred risk, but direct CS/SS/TS evidence for Os in floating-zone growth is insufficient.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt or floating-zone methods improve mass transport compared with purely solid-state diffusion, as shown by YTiO3 floating-zone growth and rare-earth titanate melt growth (SS-4, SS-5, TS-1). However, the target is not the same Pnma perovskite structure as YTiO3, and Os incorporation into a homogeneous melt/zone is not established by CS/SS/TS. Therefore, the kinetics are somewhat better than Route 1 for diffusion but still limited by phase selection and volatility.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids the very high bulk temperatures used in CS/SS/TS solid-state and melt routes, so it receives a higher condition-severity score. However, it still requires vacuum/controlled oxygen pressure and toxic Os-containing targets or sources [Reasonable Inference]. Because no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports thin-film growth, the thin-film precedent itself is neutral 50 from the source data.

#### F3 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition can bypass some bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], which is why Route 3 scores better than bulk routes in kinetics. The limiting kinetic issues are crystallization of the triclinic phase and precise Y:Ti:Os:O stoichiometry, especially because Ti oxygen stoichiometry is sensitive in SS-2, SS-4, TS-1, and TS-2. The lack of direct thin-film analogue in CS/SS/TS remains the main uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Y and Ti/Os polyhedra, so one amorphization trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from target bond lengths and comparison to rare-earth titanate size mismatch in SS-5/TS-1]. The formula and P1 description suggest a small primitive cell rather than >40 atoms, so the cell-size trigger is not assigned. The target description does not explicitly show simultaneous edge/face/vertex-sharing coexistence, and CS/SS/TS do not provide exact connectivity evidence for YTi2OsO6, so the connectivity trigger is not assigned.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YTi2OsO6 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a clearly robust ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. CS, SS, and TS analogues support the general feasibility of synthesizing rare-earth/titanium multication oxides near this energy range, especially by high-temperature solid-state, vacuum/Ar, or reducing routes (CS-4/CS-5, SS-3/SS-5, TS-1/TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is that similar rare-earth titanate and perovskite-derived oxides can be made when oxygen stoichiometry is carefully controlled (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is Os chemistry: Os oxidation state, volatility, toxicity, and possible OsO4 formation are not directly addressed by CS/SS/TS and must be managed by low-pO2, sealed, or thin-film approaches [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine.
