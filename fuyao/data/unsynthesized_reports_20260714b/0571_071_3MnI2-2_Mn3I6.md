### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s **E_hull = 0.0050 eV/atom**, so it is very close to the convex hull and receives a high thermodynamic score. Comparable low-hull analogues exist: Nb₃I₈ is on the hull in CS1, TiI₃ is on the hull in CS2, MoSe₂ has E_hull ≈0.0033 eV/atom in CS5, and MnSe has E_hull ≈0.0035 eV/atom in SS1/TS1. However, low hull energy does not guarantee the specific monoclinic C2 tetrahedral polymorph will be isolated, because SS2/TS2 demonstrate Mn–I chemistry can favor different coordination motifs.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderate because CS1/CS2/CS3 all use sealed evacuated ampoules, which are standard but require vacuum handling. The main severity penalties are volatile I₂ and sealed-tube pressure risk, supported by iodine use in CS1/CS2. The temperature is below 1000 °C by inference from CS1/CS2/CS3 ranges, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Remaining uncertainty is the exact pressure developed by I₂/MnI₂ vapor during staged heating.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are acceptable but not ideal because elemental Mn must react fully with iodine; CS3 shows elemental Mn can react in evacuated tubes, while CS1/CS2 show halogen vapor improves reactivity. Diffusion should be easier than refractory oxide synthesis because iodine vapor can transport material, as implied by CS1/CS2. The major kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the target tetrahedral sheet rather than another MnI₂ polymorph; SS2/TS2 give Mn–I evidence but with octahedral MnI₆ coordination.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it requires a controlled temperature gradient, vacuum ampoule sealing, and iodine loading, all supported by CS1/CS2/CS5. CS1 uses vacuum 10⁻⁵ Torr, CS2 uses an evacuated quartz tube, and CS5 uses two-zone furnace vapor transport. The route remains below high-pressure and >1000 °C thresholds if adapted to MnI₂, but iodine volatility remains a central hazard. Insufficient evidence from SS for CVT of binary MnI₂ specifically; SS only supports MnI₂ as a precursor in annealed iodide chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT has favorable transport kinetics because CS1/CS2 demonstrate halide materials can be grown or purified via vapor transport. Crystallization is plausible for a 2D halide because CS1 involves a 2D iodide and CS5 involves layered transport-grown chalcogenides. Composition control is less certain: CS2 reports TiI₃ appeared with TiI₂ and Ti₂O₃, showing that transport reactions can produce mixed phases. Insufficient direct evidence from TS for MnI₂ CVT; TS2 supports MnI₂ stability in iodide reactions but not vapor transport.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses dry/inert handling and sealed annealing, consistent with SS2/TS2’s TlMnI₃ preparation from MnI₂ under Ar with glovebox handling. It avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but it is penalized for multi-step preparation and iodine/anhydrous iodide sensitivity. If true melt growth is attempted, the higher temperature remains below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], so no high-temperature penalty is assigned. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct MnI₂ melt growth; CS supports sealed iodide reactions generally.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Route 3 has the best kinetics because the feedstock is already MnI₂, avoiding long-range cation/anion stoichiometric assembly from elements. SS2/TS2 show MnI₂ can participate in iodide melting/annealing at 250–350 °C, supporting good chemical compatibility. The limiting kinetic factor is polymorph conversion: same composition helps, but the target’s edge/corner-sharing tetrahedral MnI₄ sheets may require careful nucleation and annealing. SS2/TS2’s need for weeks of annealing and sometimes repeated powdering indicates ordering can be slow in related iodides.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Mn, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered. The described structure contains edge- and corner-sharing tetrahedra, but no face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity check as defined is not triggered. The unit cell size is not indicated to exceed 40 atoms, so that check is neutral/negative. Remaining uncertainty is that layered iodides can be soft and defect-prone, but CS/SS/TS do not provide enough evidence to assign a high amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0050 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports sealed-tube and vapor-transport synthesis of iodides and layered compounds, while SS/TS provide direct Mn–I relevance through MnI₂-containing TlMnI₃ synthesis. The most important enabling factor is the availability of chemically matched MnI₂ feedstock and iodine-vapor/sealed-ampoule methods, supported by SS2/TS2 and CS1/CS2. The main limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the target has tetrahedral MnI₄ sheets, whereas the closest Mn–I analogue in SS2/TS2 contains octahedral MnI₆ ribbons. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful iodine chemical potential control and thermal screening.
