### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high. This is reinforced by TS analogues, where NaAl(PS₃)₂, LiAl(PS₃)₂, GaPS₄, AlPS₄, and AgAlS₂ are all reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [TS-1–TS-5]. SS data also supports stable crystalline Al/Ga sulfide frameworks [SS-2–SS-5]. The uncertainty is that H-containing CS analogues include unstable or air-sensitive salts, so thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic or environmental stability [CS-2, CS-5].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed-tube sulfide synthesis conditions similar to Li/Na Al thiophosphates at 1023 K and evacuated/Ar handling [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]. The severity comes from needing a sealed system and volatile/toxic hydrosulfide or H₂S sources [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids external high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, which keeps the score moderate. Direct CS evidence for solid-state Al–S–H synthesis is insufficient, so the volatile-H handling penalty is retained as a conservative adjustment [CS].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state Al sulfides form at moderate high temperatures in several analogues, showing that Al–S diffusion and ordering are feasible [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]. However, the target requires H retention and conversion to a hydrosulfide-containing AlS₆ sheet, which is a larger structural change than forming an anhydrous thiophosphate framework [Reasonable Inference]. This lowers the structural-reconstruction component. CS provides insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Al–S–H solid-state formation, so it does not improve the score [CS].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is not high-temperature or externally high-pressure in its preferred implementation, and CS-2 shows autoclave crystallization at only 373 K for 48 h [CS-2]. If ammonothermal conditions are needed, CS-1 shows that H-bearing frameworks can require demanding 550 °C and 6 kbar NH₃ conditions [CS-1]. The score is reduced mainly for reactive/controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile hydrosulfide/H₂S components, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents [CS-1, CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct solvothermal AlHS₂ evidence, so the route remains moderate rather than easy [SS, TS].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solvothermal chemistry should provide high precursor activity because Al and HS⁻ species can react through dissolved or solvated intermediates [CS-1, CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. This route also avoids the slow diffusion limitations of dense solid-state sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is crystallizing the exact 2D AlHS₂ sheet rather than amorphous Al sulfide/hydroxide or soluble Al complexes [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS support the target-like AlS₆ geometry but not the solvothermal pathway directly [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT is supported for a 2D sulfide analogue, GaPS₄, grown with iodine in a 650–600 °C gradient [SS-4, TS-3]. The route requires sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/iodine/H₂S species, and multi-step crystal-growth handling [SS-4, TS-3; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids external high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures. The main penalty is chemical hazard and the uncertainty of retaining S–H bonds at CVT temperatures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can grow crystalline layered sulfides, as shown by GaPS₄ [SS-4, TS-3]. Vapor transport may improve mass transport compared with solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. However, Al–H–S vapor speciation is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, and H loss as H₂S is plausible at 500–700 °C [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are acceptable for sulfide crystallization but uncertain for hydrosulfide stoichiometry.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

High-pressure routes are directly supported by structurally similar AgAlS₂-II and AlPS₄ examples [SS-1, SS-5, TS-4]. Although the proposed 2–5 GPa window does not trigger the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, the route still requires specialized pressure apparatus, capsule handling, controlled atmosphere, and volatile hydrosulfide containment [SS-1, SS-5, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore lower than sealed-tube or solvothermal routes. CS gives insufficient direct high-pressure Al–S–H evidence [CS].

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Pressure can assist dense sulfide network formation and may retain volatile H/S species [SS-1, SS-5, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. However, high-pressure AlPS₄ synthesis reports low crystal quality and phase-control challenges [SS-5, TS-4]. The target’s layered hydrosulfide structure may require careful quenching to preserve H positions [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics and phase evolution are moderate but not clearly favorable.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one framework metal cation, Al, so there is no large cation radius mismatch trigger [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The described formula and sheet motif do not indicate a >40-atom unit cell, and the connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing [Target]. SS/TS analogues show that layered Al–S frameworks can crystallize successfully [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]. The main uncertainty is not amorphization from cation disorder, but possible H/S disorder or partial dehydrogenation, which is not captured by the F4 rubric [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlHS₂ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many thermodynamically similar Al/Ga sulfides are also on the hull [TS-1–TS-5]. The SS/TS analogues strongly support the crystallization of layered or low-dimensional Al–S frameworks with Al–S bond lengths closely matching the target [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]. The CS analogues support the broader idea that H can be incorporated as a covalently bound anion proton in extended frameworks under autoclave conditions, but they do not directly establish Al–S–H chemistry [CS-1, CS-2]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high HS⁻/H₂S chemical potential at moderate temperature to preserve the S–H bond [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is avoiding competing anhydrous Al sulfides, hydroxides, or amorphous Al–S–H products caused by H/S volatility and moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].
