### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the upper boundary of the 90–100 scoring bracket specified by the prompt [Target]. Closely related PbI₂ polytypes in CS and SS have very low E_hull values around 0.0008–0.0019 eV/atom, showing that the PbI₂ layered family is thermodynamically accessible [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. TS analogues show metastable heavy halides with E_hull values up to ~0.062 eV/atom can still be experimentally synthesized, but they are chemically less direct than CS/SS [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the R-3m nine-sheet stacking is a true equilibrium polytype or a low-energy metastable stacking variant.

#### F2 — Route 1, gel diffusion

CS and SS give direct evidence for room-temperature PbI₂ gel growth under ordinary room conditions with only test tubes/U-tubes and no pressure or controlled atmosphere [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. The main deductions are the multi-stage gel/diffusion/crystal-growth procedure and the toxicity of Pb²⁺ precursors [CS3; SS2; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for this exact gel route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 severity. The mild temperature and ambient pressure make this the least severe synthesis route.

#### F3 — Route 1, gel diffusion

The use of soluble lead acetate and KI gives high precursor activity, and the gel medium provides diffusion-controlled reaction without requiring high-temperature solid-state diffusion [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. Structural reconstruction is minimal because all closest CS/SS PbI₂ references share edge-sharing PbI₆ layers and differ mainly in stacking periodicity [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the desired nine-layer R-3m polytype rather than a more common PbI₂ stacking sequence. TS provides insufficient direct kinetic evidence for gel-grown PbI₂ and is therefore neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule melt growth

SS5 supports iodide melt growth in evacuated quartz ampoules, with high temperature, slow cooling, and annealing; TS2–TS4 also support sealed-ampoule halide processing. The severity score is reduced by vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile iodine/iodide species, multistep thermal processing, and Pb/I toxicity [SS5; TS2–TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is moderate relative to oxide ceramics and does not trigger the >1000 °C deduction [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for melt growth of these exact PbI₂ polytypes and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule melt growth

Kinetically, direct PbI₂ melting should be favorable because the starting feedstock already has the target composition and liquid-phase crystallization avoids slow diffusion [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. SS5 reports high-quality single crystals for a related Pb iodide by controlled melt growth, supporting good crystallization kinetics. However, exact polytype selection from the melt may be less controllable than slow gel growth, and TS4 indicates some halide systems require quenching/tempering to stabilize specific polymorphs. Thus the kinetic score is high but below the gel route.

#### F2 — Route 3, vapor transport / sublimation

TS1 and TS5 support evacuated-tube sublimation or vapor transport for related heavy halides, but no direct PbI₂ vapor-growth example appears in CS or SS, so those sources are insufficient/neutral for this route. The route requires vacuum/sealed quartz, volatile iodide/iodine species, multistep loading/growth/recovery, and toxic Pb/I compounds [TS1; TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures remain moderate and far below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature deduction is applied [TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The route is experimentally feasible but less straightforward than gel growth.

#### F3 — Route 3, vapor transport / sublimation

TS1 demonstrates that BiBr₃ can be purified by sublimation, and TS5 demonstrates gas-phase growth of a layered bismuth telluroiodide, supporting the general vapor availability of heavy halides. However, TS5 also reports very small crystal amounts, multiple phases, and twinning, indicating crystallization and phase-control challenges. PbI₂ stoichiometry may be sensitive to iodine vapor pressure, so composition control is only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Because direct CS/SS PbI₂ vapor-growth evidence is absent, uncertainty remains significant.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Pb as the cation, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is a layered network of edge-sharing PbI₆ octahedra without mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk [Target]. The inferred nine-sheet PbI₂ stacking repeat is not expected to exceed 40 atoms for the primitive polytype repeat, so the unit-cell-size trigger is unlikely [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is crystallographic setting/conventional-cell choice, but the chemistry is simple and strongly crystalline in CS/SS references.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a low-energy metastable PbI₂ polytype rather than a strongly unstable compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while closely related PbI₂ polytypes are essentially on-hull [Target; CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. CS and SS provide very strong support because they describe nearly identical PbI₂ layered structures with the same PbI₆ edge-sharing octahedra and successful mild gel synthesis [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. TS provides weaker but consistent evidence that low-energy heavy halide metastable phases can be synthesized in sealed or vapor routes [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the high solution reactivity and slow diffusion in silica gel, which allows PbI₂ platelets to crystallize under ambient conditions [CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4]. The main limiting factor is polytype/stacking control: references explicitly indicate multiple PbI₂ polytypes and the need for controlled gel conditions to target specific stacking sequences [CS3; SS2]. Overall, synthesis is highly feasible, especially as crystals, but phase-pure isolation of the exact R-3m nine-sheet variant may require screening pH, concentration, temperature, and growth time.
