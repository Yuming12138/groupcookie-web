### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.043 eV/atom lies below 0.05 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. This is consistent with thermodynamic analogues: CoTe₂ is 0.0192 eV/atom in TS1, pyrite-like CoTe₂ is 0.0236 eV/atom in TS3, and Co₃Se₄ is 0.0458 eV/atom in TS4. CS also supports near-hull Co–Te/Ni–Te phases, including CoTe₂ at 0.019–0.024 eV/atom and NiTe₂ at 0 eV/atom (CS1 / CS2 / CS4). Remaining uncertainty is that Co₂Te₃ is not the same stoichiometry as the closest synthesized CoTe₂ analogue, so phase competition may still occur [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures and standard evacuated quartz tubes, directly supported by CoTe₂ synthesis at 600 °C for one month under vacuum (CS2 / SS1 / TS1) and NiTe₂ synthesis at 800 °C in vacuum-sealed quartz tubes (CS4). The main severity penalties arise from vacuum sealing, Te volatility, and long/multi-step annealing. CS, SS, and TS all support sealed-tube feasibility; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact safe Te excess, so that parameter is a [Reasonable Inference]. Overall severity is moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are moderate because elemental Co is not highly reactive, but Te can provide liquid/vapor-assisted transport at the synthesis temperatures [Reasonable Inference, with elemental synthesis supported by CS2 / SS1 / TS1]. Long annealing in CoTe₂ and NiTe₂ analogues indicates slow homogenization/order formation (CS2 / SS1 / TS1; CS4). Structural similarity to 2D CoTe₂ sheets supports transferability (SS1), but the different Co₂Te₃ stoichiometry and mixed polyhedral connectivity increase reconstruction difficulty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for dense CoTe₂/FeTe₂/NiTe₂ pyrite-like phases at 60–89 kbar and up to 1200–1600 °C (CS1 / CS3 / CS5 / SS3 / TS3). These conditions require specialized multi-anvil/tetrahedral-anvil equipment and rapid quenching, producing a large severity penalty. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that Co₂Te₃ specifically requires high pressure; therefore high pressure is treated as a fallback rather than preferred route. The route is challenging mainly because of pressure and apparatus demands.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-pressure analogues react over short 0.5–6 h times, indicating that HPHT conditions can overcome diffusion barriers (CS1 / CS3 / CS5 / SS3 / TS3). However, the structural target is layered P-6m2 rather than the pyrite-like structures often stabilized under pressure (CS1 / SS3 / TS3), so the crystallization pathway is uncertain. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows no direct HPHT Co₂Te₃ preparation. Therefore kinetics are acceptable but structural selectivity is a concern.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT uses standard sealed silica tubes and furnace gradients, supported by CoGeTe growth at 700 °C under <10⁻⁴ Torr with iodine (SS2) and CoSb₃ recrystallization with iodine/chlorine near 1123 K (SS5). The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Te/I₂, and hazardous halogen transport agents. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct binary Co₂Te₃ CVT, so exact transport loading and gradient must be inferred. Conditions are moderate, but chemical handling risk is higher than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport can improve crystallinity and bypass some bulk diffusion limitations, as shown by CoGeTe single-crystal growth using iodine (SS2). CoSb₃ CVT/recrystallization further supports halogen-assisted growth of Co-centered octahedral frameworks (SS5). The limiting factor is composition control: Co–Te phase equilibria are noted as difficult/narrow in the CoTe₂/Ni–Co–Te system (CS2 / SS1 / TS1), and vapor transport may fractionate volatile Te relative to Co [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are fair, but stoichiometric selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Co, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide no contradictory multication evidence for the binary target. The formula and described sheet are small, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a >40 atom target unit cell, so that check is not triggered. Mixed face/edge/corner-sharing octahedra are explicitly present in the target and are also found in Co₃Se₄-like octahedral connectivity (TS4), so one trigger is assigned. This gives a low-to-medium amorphization risk rather than a high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Co₂Te₃ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than an unambiguous ground state because its E_hull is 0.043 eV/atom, below the common metastability threshold but not zero. The strongest support comes from the same binary Co–Te analogue CoTe₂, which is synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods and is chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close (CS2 / SS1 / TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of Te-rich sealed-tube chemistry, where Te mobility at 600–800 °C can promote formation of layered Co–Te frameworks [Reasonable Inference supported by CS2 / SS1 / TS1 and CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Co–Te compositions, since the CoTe₂/Ni–Co–Te references emphasize homogeneity ranges and phase-control difficulty (CS2 / SS1 / TS1). If ambient-pressure synthesis gives CoTe₂/CoTe impurities, high-pressure quenching or CVT can be used as secondary strategies, but both introduce additional selectivity challenges (CS1 / SS3 / TS3; SS2 / SS5).
