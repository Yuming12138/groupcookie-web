### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.024 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic range, and thermodynamically similar tellurides with E_hull ≈ 0.017–0.037 eV/atom have reported syntheses (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The closest chemical/structural reference, orthorhombic AgTlTe, is a ground-state phase with E_hull = 0.0, strongly supporting the Ag–Tl–Te composition but also indicating the tetragonal P4/nmm target is likely a higher-energy polymorph (CS-1, SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is whether the 24 meV/atom tetragonal phase is quenchable in bulk or requires epitaxial/kinetic stabilization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses mild temperature, 570 K, and simple sealed-tube annealing, which is directly supported for Ag–Tl–chalcogen compounds (CS-5, SS-2). Deductions arise from the evacuated ampoule and Tl/Te sealed-system requirement, plus Tl toxicity, while high pressure and high temperature are not required (CS-5, SS-2). Insufficient evidence from TS for this specific low-temperature AgTlTe anneal route, so TS is neutral, 50, for route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because thallium chalcogenide plus Ag plus chalcogen reacted at only 570 K in the TlAgSe/TlAgTe family (CS-5, SS-2). The main kinetic/structural uncertainty is polymorph selection: the known exact AgTlTe analogue is orthorhombic Pnma, not target P4/nmm (CS-1, SS-1). Thus diffusion is likely manageable, but reconstructive ordering into tetragonal layers is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is directly supported by exact AgTlTe Bridgman growth under vacuum in sealed quartz ampoules with controlled cooling (CS-1, SS-1). It is more severe than Route 1 because it requires melt homogenization, slow cooling, and tight sealed-ampoule control, and the source notes Ag deficiency and polymorphic/cracking issues (CS-1, SS-1). TS gives only partial general support from melt/ampoule syntheses of thermodynamically similar chalcogenides, not direct AgTlTe polymorph control (TS-4, TS-5).

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth gives excellent reaction kinetics because liquid transport bypasses solid-state diffusion barriers, and both exact AgTlTe and related AgTl₃Te₂ have been grown from melts or Tl–Te-rich melts (CS-1, SS-1, SS-3). However, the same evidence shows the route tends to yield known Pnma AgTlTe and suffers composition/polymorph issues, limiting the structural-reconstruction score (CS-1, SS-1). The uncertainty is whether cooling-rate control can trap P4/nmm instead of equilibrating to Pnma [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate thermal severity because Tl₂Q flux syntheses use 723–773 K and AgTl₃Te₂ uses 550 °C, both below 1000 °C (CS-4, SS-4, TS-1, SS-3). The route still requires evacuated sealed tubes, toxic Tl-containing fluxes, long slow cooling, and likely flux separation, all documented in Tl–chalcogen flux work (CS-4, SS-4, TS-1). CS, SS, and TS all provide useful flux or telluride-melt signal, but none proves the target P4/nmm polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth offers strong kinetic advantages through solution-mediated transport and slow crystal growth, as shown by Tl–Hg–Te flux crystals and AgTl₃Te₂ slow-cooling synthesis (CS-4, SS-4, TS-1, SS-3). The risk is phase selectivity: Tl-rich flux can favor Tl-rich phases such as AgTl₃Te₂ rather than stoichiometric AgTlTe (SS-3). The structural score is therefore moderate despite favorable mass transport.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film synthesis is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS, so CS, SS, and TS are each neutral, 50, as direct evidence sources. The assigned severity reflects [Reasonable Inference] from high-vacuum deposition, toxic/volatile Tl, and likely post-deposition annealing. It avoids high temperature and high mechanical pressure, but the lack of precedent makes it operationally uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 4

Thin-film kinetics may help bypass bulk equilibrium and stabilize a layered P4/nmm polymorph [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS provide no direct crystallization pathway evidence. Composition control is likely difficult because Ag-deficient AgTlTe was reported in melt-grown samples and Tl/Te volatility requires sealed or overpressure conditions (CS-1, SS-1, CS-4, TS-1). Thus the F3 score is substantially lower than for bulk/flux routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The simple AgTlTe composition and layered crystalline target suggest low-to-medium amorphization risk, but the Ag/Tl cation size mismatch triggers one risk criterion [Reasonable Inference supported by distinct Ag/Tl coordination environments in CS-1/SS-1]. The target description includes face- and edge-sharing tetrahedra but not simultaneous vertex + edge + face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a >40-atom target unit cell, so that source signal is neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgTlTe in the target tetragonal P4/nmm structure is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the bulk ground state, because exact-composition orthorhombic AgTlTe is reported on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 while the target is 0.024 eV/atom above hull (CS-1, SS-1). The CS and SS analogues strongly support that Ag–Tl–Te chemistry is experimentally accessible, especially via sealed ampoule annealing and Bridgman growth (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-2). TS analogues support the broader idea that low-E_hull Tl/Ag/telluride-like materials can be made under sealed-tube, flux, or melt conditions (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature chalcogenide reactivity in sealed evacuated tubes, which may allow kinetic trapping before conversion to the orthorhombic phase (CS-5, SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, especially the reported Ag deficiency and polymorphic issues in AgTlTe growth (CS-1, SS-1).
