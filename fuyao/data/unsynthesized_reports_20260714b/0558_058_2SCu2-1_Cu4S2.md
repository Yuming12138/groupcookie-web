### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.012 eV/atom, which falls in the 90–100 range for thermodynamic stability [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low hull energies are synthesizable by standard or solvothermal/flux methods: AgCuS at 0.0306 eV/atom (TS1), Ag₃CuS₂ at 0.0388–0.0462 eV/atom (TS2 / TS3), and KAg₃S₂ at 0.00925 eV/atom (TS5). CS evidence is less clean because CS1 is chemically close but has inconsistent precursor metadata; therefore CS is supportive for Cu–chalcogenide feasibility but only moderate for this factor. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.012 eV/atom can still correspond to a metastable polymorph rather than the ambient Cu₂S ground state [Reasonable Inference].

**Source signal scores for F1:** CS = 65, SS = 80, TS = 90.

---

#### F2 — Condition severity

**Route 1:** Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis uses moderate temperatures comparable to 300–500 °C in AgCuS/Cu₂S preparation (SS2 / TS1), so no high-temperature penalty is needed. The main penalties are vacuum sealed ampules and volatile sulfur, both directly motivated by evacuated ampule routes in SS2 / TS1, SS4 / TS2, and SS5 / TS3. Multi-step grinding/annealing is likely if phase purity is poor, consistent with long annealing/mechanical alloying in SS5 / TS3. Insufficient direct CS condition evidence for binary tetragonal Cu₂S; CS1 is chemically close but has unreliable precursor details, so CS is neutral here.

**Route 2:** Hydrothermal synthesis is mild because NaCu₅S₃ forms at 413 K in water (SS1), and Ni₃S₄ forms at 120 °C in a Teflon-lined autoclave (CS5). No vacuum, elemental sulfur vapor, or high pressure beyond autogenous solvent pressure is required in these analogues (SS1; CS5). The only practical severity penalty is sulfide/H₂S hazard from Na₂S [Reasonable Inference based on Na₂S use in SS1 and CS5]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for ordinary hydrothermal Cu₂S, so TS is neutral-to-moderate.

**Route 3:** Flux/melt synthesis requires 700–800 °C under Ar for CsCu₃S₂ (SS3), which is not extreme but is more severe than hydrothermal conditions. The route involves excess sulfur, Ar control, and leaching/washing after cooling, all described in SS3. Sulfur volatility is also supported indirectly by the frequent use of evacuated sealed tubes for sulfides in SS2 / TS1 and SS4 / TS2. CS gives insufficient specific flux evidence, so CS is neutral for Route 3 severity.

---

#### F3 — Reaction kinetics

**Route 1:** Solid-state kinetics are moderate because Cu metal is less reactive than solution precursors, but sulfur reacts readily and Cu₂S can be made at 500 °C in evacuated quartz (SS2 / TS1). The need for 50 h, 2 weeks, or longer anneals in related Ag/Cu sulfides indicates ordering and phase homogenization can be slow (SS2 / TS1; SS5 / TS3). The target’s simple binary composition lowers reconstruction complexity relative to ternaries [Reasonable Inference from target data], but polymorph selection remains uncertain.

**Route 2:** Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because sulfide species and copper sources can react by dissolution–precipitation rather than long-range solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1 and CS5]. SS1 demonstrates Cu–S framework formation at only 413 K over 5 days, and CS5 demonstrates sulfide nanoparticle formation at 120 °C over 12 h. The kinetic uncertainty is not reaction completion but obtaining the exact tetragonal layered Cu₂S polymorph rather than a common Cu₂₋ₓS phase [Reasonable Inference].

**Route 3:** Flux kinetics are favorable because SS3 reports Cu–S crystals from a melt/flux reaction after only 1.5–4 h at 700–800 °C. Flux/liquid transport reduces diffusion barriers compared with dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by SS3]. The limiting kinetic/structural issue is that the same flux chemistry can incorporate alkali cations and stabilize ternary phases such as CsCu₃S₂ (SS3), so transfer to binary Cu₂S is not guaranteed.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Cu, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [target data]. The described structure is simple—one Cu site, one S site, two-coordinate Cu, four-coordinate S, and one Cu₂S sheet—so there is no evidence for a large >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [target data]. SS/TS analogues include some structurally complex ternaries, but those complexities are not present in the target, so they do not increase the target’s amorphization score penalty. Remaining uncertainty is the exact conventional-cell atom count, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence and are treated neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state Cu₂S polymorph because its E_hull is only 0.012 eV/atom [target data]. The analogue evidence strongly supports formation of Cu–S and Ag/Cu–S phases by sealed-tube, hydrothermal, and flux routes: SS1 supports hydrothermal Cu–S formation, SS2 / TS1 support Cu₂S-based sealed-tube chemistry, and SS3 supports flux growth of Cu–S frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of sulfide chemistry, especially under hydrothermal or sulfur-rich flux conditions (SS1; CS5; SS3). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, because related Ag/Cu sulfides show impurity or phase-control challenges (SS2 / TS1; SS5 / TS3), and Cu₂₋ₓS phases can vary in copper content [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible, with hydrothermal screening followed by low-temperature annealing being the most attractive strategy.
