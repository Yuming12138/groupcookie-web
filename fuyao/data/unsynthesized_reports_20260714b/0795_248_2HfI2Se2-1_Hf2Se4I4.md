### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0040 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull and falls in the highest stability scoring range. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values near 0–0.009 eV/atom have reported syntheses, including MnBiSe₂I at 0.0058 eV/atom [TS1], MnSbSe₂Br at 0.0077 eV/atom [TS2], and AgVP₂Se₆ at 0.0090 eV/atom [TS5]. However, TS1 explicitly reports that no phase-pure product could be obtained, so low E_hull supports existence but does not guarantee clean synthesis [TS1].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature because analogous sealed-tube chalcogenide-halides form at 550–900 °C or 1073 K [CS1/CS2/CS5/SS4]. The main severity comes from sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile I₂/Se, and toxic/corrosive handling [CS1/CS2/SS2]. CS1 and SS4 also show slow cooling, reheating, or reannealing, so multi-step control is likely needed.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Hf is refractory, so solid-state diffusion from Hf metal is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. Related direct syntheses often require long dwell times, such as two weeks for Nb–Se–I [CS1] or five days for Hf-containing selenide [SS4], supporting a low kinetic score. The target’s layered Hf–Se–I framework must form from elemental components, so structural reconstruction is substantial [target structure; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses moderate absolute temperatures but still requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile halogen species, gradients, and long durations [CS3/SS2/SS5]. CS3 reports 3–4 weeks with a controlled gradient, and SS2 reports 15 days in a 500–460 °C gradient. These conditions are not extreme but demand careful ampoule and temperature-gradient control.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport improves mass transport relative to static bulk diffusion because volatile halogen species carry material through the ampoule [CS3/SS2/SS5]. The uncertainty is Hf-specific: none of the provided CS/SS/TS examples demonstrate Hf–Se–I CVT directly, so the availability of a suitable volatile Hf iodide/selenoiodide transport species is inferred rather than proven. Layered chalcogenide-halide analogues support crystallization by CVT [CS3], giving a moderate kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 3

The topochemical iodination route keeps the second step at lower temperature, but it is multi-step because HfSe₂ must be prepared or sourced before iodination [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires vacuum sealing and volatile iodine, as in Nb–Se–I and iodine transport analogues [CS1/CS2/SS5]. The severity is therefore similar to Route 2, despite potentially lower reaction temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Using pre-formed HfSe₂ should reduce the need for long-range Hf diffusion and preserve part of the layered Hf–Se framework [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the best kinetic score among the three routes. The uncertainty is that the target has Hf coordinated to both Se and I in an 8-coordinate environment, so the conversion may not be fully topotactic and could instead produce HfSe₂ plus HfI₄ or iodine-rich byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation-size disorder is not expected [target structure]. The reported structure is a crystalline 2D sheet with simple Hf–Se/I coordination, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network is stated for the target [target structure]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a large unit cell or mixed polyhedral connectivity in Hf(SeI)₂, so amorphization risk is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(SeI)₂ is likely thermodynamically accessible or only very weakly metastable because its E_hull is 0.0040 eV/atom, within the range of experimentally reported thermodynamic analogues [TS1/TS2/TS5]. The CS and SS datasets strongly support sealed-tube chalcogenide-halide chemistry, especially for Se–I/Br systems and low-dimensional structures [CS1–CS3/SS1/SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed ampoules or CVT conditions to maintain iodine and selenium chemical potentials [CS1/CS3/SS2/SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity, because several close analogues report impurities, difficult phase-pure preparation, or sensitivity to stoichiometry and thermal history [CS1/CS3/TS1/SS4]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful iodine activity control and repeated optimization.
