### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be thermodynamically stable by the supplied target data. The closest same-composition AgTlSe reference also has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), and several related Ag selenides are stable or nearly stable, including Ag₈SnSe₆ and RbAg₃Se₂ (SS2/TS2 and SS4/TS4). The main uncertainty is not bulk thermodynamic existence of AgTlSe, but whether the tetragonal P4/nmm polymorph is the experimentally accessible ground-state form rather than another low-energy polymorph, since the reported AgTlSe analogue is orthorhombic Pnma (CS1 / SS1 / TS1 vs Target).

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is low-temperature and simple because the same-composition AgTlSe was prepared by annealing at 570 K in evacuated sealed silica tubes (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The severity deductions come mainly from vacuum/sealed-tube operation and the toxic/volatile nature of Tl/Se chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS1 / SS1 / TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact annealing duration; this uncertainty is handled by recommending a conservative 1–7 day anneal.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because the reported AgTlSe route succeeds at only 570 K, suggesting high chalcogenide reactivity and short diffusion distances or liquid/defect-assisted transport (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Ag metal is less reactive than soluble salts or organometallics, so precursor activity is not scored near 100 [Reasonable Inference]. The largest kinetic uncertainty is structural/polymorph conversion, because the direct reference structure is Pnma, whereas the target is P4/nmm (CS1 / SS1 / TS1 vs Target).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

The melt-growth route is supported by AgTlTe grown in a sealed evacuated quartz ampoule with slow cooling (CS2). It is more severe than Route 1 because CS2 describes multiple operational steps and composition/polymorph issues, even though the absolute temperatures are still modest. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS specifically supports AgTlSe melt growth, so those source categories are neutral for this route, source score 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt growth should improve diffusion and homogenization relative to solid-state reaction because transport occurs through a liquid or partially liquid phase [Reasonable Inference from CS2]. However, CS2 reports Ag deficiency and polymorphic transformations/cracking in AgTlTe, which lowers the structural reconstruction and composition-control confidence. For the target P4/nmm polymorph, the route may favor larger crystals but not necessarily the desired polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures of 723–773 K in sealed evacuated tubes for Tl–Hg–Se layered crystals (CS3 / SS5 / TS5), so it avoids extreme temperature and high pressure. The deductions arise from vacuum sealing, toxic Tl-containing flux, sealed volatile chalcogenide chemistry, and multi-step slow-cooling/flux-removal operations (CS3 / SS5 / TS5). The route is experimentally more involved than Route 1 despite being chemically plausible.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because fluxes can dissolve reagents and support crystal growth at temperatures far below refractory solid-state regimes, as shown for Tl₂Hg₃Q₄ crystals grown over 14 days in Tl₂Q flux (CS3 / SS5 / TS5). The target’s two-dimensional sheet character is compatible with layered chalcogenide flux growth, but no direct AgTlSe flux synthesis is supplied. Remaining uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the tetragonal P4/nmm sheet or instead yields the known orthorhombic AgTlSe polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple AgTlSe composition, so a very large unit cell is not expected [Reasonable Inference from Target], but CS/SS/TS do not provide the exact target cell atom count and are neutral on that specific check, score 50. The Ag and Tl cations differ significantly in size [Reasonable Inference], and the target AgSe₄ tetrahedra show mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity (Target), which raises disorder/amorphization risk. The risk is still moderate rather than high because many related Ag/Tl/Se crystalline phases are experimentally reported (CS1–CS5 / SS1–SS5 / TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgTlSe is very likely synthesizable as a crystalline compound because the target is on the hull and the same nominal AgTlSe composition has already been prepared in evacuated sealed tubes (Target; CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The key uncertainty is whether the tetragonal P4/nmm layered polymorph is the ground-state experimental phase or a polymorph competing with the reported orthorhombic Pnma AgTlSe phase (Target vs CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The strongest enabling factor is the low-temperature reactivity of Tl–Ag–Se chemistry, demonstrated by AgTlSe formation at 570 K (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The strongest limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, reinforced by AgTlTe melt-growth problems such as Ag deficiency and polymorphic transformations (CS2). Overall, Route 1 should be attempted first for phase formation, while flux or controlled cooling may be needed if single crystals or the tetragonal polymorph are specifically required.
