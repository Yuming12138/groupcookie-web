### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0360 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window [Target]. Similar Tl-halide analogues are experimentally reported with low E_hull values, including AgTl₂I₃ at 0.0272 eV/atom [CS-5, SS-5, TS-1] and Tl₄HgBr₆ at 0.0133 eV/atom [TS-3]. CS, SS, and TS all provide positive thermodynamic signal; none contradicts target feasibility on energetic grounds [CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3]. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.036 eV/atom may still indicate metastability relative to binary TlI/TlBr mixtures [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal condition severity

Hydrothermal analogues use only 120–200 °C, so temperature severity is low [CS-1, CS-5, TS-3]. The main penalties are sealed acidic conditions and Tl toxicity, because HI/HBr media are corrosive and Tl compounds are hazardous [CS-1, CS-5, TS-3; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from SS for direct hydrothermal synthesis of the exact target, so SS is neutral for this route-specific factor. Overall severity is moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics

Hydrothermal formation of Tl iodide analogues in 3 h to 2 days indicates favorable transport and nucleation in halogen-acid media [CS-1, CS-5]. Low-pressure hydrothermal Tl–Hg bromides also give well-crystallized samples at 120–200 °C [TS-3]. The kinetic uncertainty is I/Br ordering into the monoclinic 2D sheet rather than crystallization of separate TlI/TlBr binary phases [Target, SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the score is high but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed-ampoule solid-state condition severity

Sealed-ampoule syntheses of halides are well supported by CS and TS analogues at 260–470 °C [CS-3, TS-2, TS-5]. The required vacuum ampoule and volatile halide retention impose moderate penalties [CS-3, TS-2, TS-5]. Tl toxicity adds another unavoidable penalty [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that this exact Tl₂IBr phase forms by direct solid-state reaction, so the route remains less preferred than hydrothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed-ampoule solid-state kinetics

Binary halides are more reactive than oxides, and Tl-halide-related compounds form at moderate temperatures in sealed ampoules [CS-3, TS-2, TS-5]. However, several TS analogues require 6–7 days of annealing, implying that phase homogenization and ordering are not instantaneous [TS-2, TS-5]. The target’s layered Cm structure likely requires partial reconstruction from TlI/TlBr binaries [Target, SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. This gives moderate-to-good kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 melt / Bridgman condition severity

Melt and Bridgman analogues require evacuated sealed ampoules, controlled gradients, and often extended annealing [SS-2, SS-3, SS-4]. The absolute temperatures are not extreme, because Tl₆SI₄ growth is reported at 600–650 °C [SS-3]. The condition score is reduced by vacuum sealing, volatile halide retention, multiple growth steps, and Tl toxicity [SS-2, SS-3, SS-4; Reasonable Inference]. This route is practical in a crystal-growth laboratory but not the simplest synthesis route.

#### F3 — Route 3 melt / Bridgman kinetics

Molten halides have high ionic mobility, and structurally similar Tl halide/chalcogenide phases have been crystallized by Bridgman or melt growth [SS-2, SS-3, SS-4]. That gives favorable diffusion and precursor activity. The limiting kinetic issue is not diffusion but selecting the monoclinic Tl₂IBr sheet rather than binary TlI/TlBr or a disordered mixed-halide solid solution [Target, SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are good for crystal growth but phase selection remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4 thin-film evaporation condition severity

TlBr/TlI-type halides can be evaporated under vacuum onto low-temperature substrates [SS-1]. Vacuum, halide volatility, toxic Tl vapor, and multi-step deposition/annealing reduce the score [SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS and TS for vapor-deposited ternary Tl–I–Br phases, so those sources are neutral for this route. This makes the method exploratory rather than recommended.

#### F3 — Route 4 thin-film kinetics

The vapor availability of TlBr/TlI is supported by the evaporation precedent [SS-1]. However, SS-1 focuses on binary halides and metastable low-temperature polymorph behavior, not ternary layered Tl₂IBr crystallization [SS-1]. Composition control is likely difficult because I and Br fluxes must be balanced precisely [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient CS/TS support lowers confidence in the crystallization pathway.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Tl as the cation, so the cation-radius-mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing ITl₅ square pyramids but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not fully triggered [Target]. The exact unit-cell atom count is not given, and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct evidence for this sub-check, so that component has residual uncertainty and is treated cautiously. Overall amorphization risk is low because many chemically and structurally related Tl halides are crystalline under accessible conditions [CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-3].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl₂IBr is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom, below the usual 0.05 eV/atom practical synthesizability threshold [Target]. The CS, SS, and TS analogues strongly support synthesis of Tl-halide and mixed-halide frameworks by hydrothermal, sealed-ampoule, and melt-growth methods [CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halogen-acid hydrothermal chemistry, which can dissolve and recrystallize Tl halides without requiring high-temperature diffusion [CS-1, CS-5, TS-3]. The most important limiting factor is I/Br ordering and avoidance of competing binary TlI/TlBr or other mixed-halide phases [Target, SS-1; Reasonable Inference]. The best practical route is therefore hydrothermal crystallization in mixed HI/HBr, followed by careful stoichiometry and slow cooling control [CS-1, CS-5, TS-3; Reasonable Inference].
