### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0280 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window [Target]. TS gives strong corroboration because CuHgSBr, CuHgSCl, PdCl₂, and CuHgSI analogues with E_hull ≈0.021–0.032 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS4]. CS and SS also support stability because chemically/structurally related PdBr₂, PtI₂, and Te₃(PdBr)₄ are reported as crystalline phases on the hull [SS1, SS2, CS1]. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not prove the specific ordered mixed-halide arrangement is the equilibrium product rather than a Br/I-disordered or phase-separated mixture [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures near 350–430 °C, supported by 700 K sealed-tube Te₃(PdBr)₄ synthesis [CS1, SS4] and lower-temperature evacuated-tube CuHgSX syntheses [TS2, TS4]. The main severity deductions are for vacuum/sealed conditions and volatile halide activity, both supported by the use of sealed ampoules for PdBr₂, PtI₂, and Pd–Br–Te chemistry [SS1, SS2, CS1]. There is insufficient evidence from TS that PdIBr itself needs a special atmosphere beyond sealed/vacuum handling, so no additional penalty was applied.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binary Pd halides provide preformed Pd–Br/Pd–I bonding motifs, and PdBr₂ has square-planar Pd–Br coordination similar to the target [SS1]. PtI₂ provides an especially close layered rectangular see-saw analogue with M–I distances near the target Pd–I distances [SS2]. The kinetic uncertainty is mixed-halide ordering: CS1 reports stoichiometry sensitivity for a Pd–Br sheet compound, implying that PdIBr may also require careful Br:I control [CS1, SS4].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
CVT avoids extremely high temperatures but requires evacuated quartz ampoules, halogen transport agents, and controlled gradients, as shown for PdBr₂ CVT at 700 °C with Br₂ [SS1] and RhTeCl CVT at 900→700 °C [CS5]. The use of Br₂/I₂ causes volatile and toxic precursor penalties [SS1, SS2]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for PdIBr-specific CVT conditions, so this route is scored by analogy rather than direct precedent.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
PdBr₂ is demonstrably vapor-transportable [SS1], and layered halide crystals can grow by CVT [CS5], supporting the crystallization pathway. However, mixed I/Br composition control is less certain because the sources show binary PdBr₂ transport [SS1] and iodine-rich Pt iodide hydrothermal growth [SS2], not mixed PdIBr vapor growth. Phase sensitivity in Pd–Br–Te chemistry [CS1] further justifies a lower composition-control score.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis operates at moderate temperature, with Pt iodide growth at 420 °C [SS2] and CuHgSBr at 670 K / ~397 °C [TS1]. The route is penalized for corrosive volatile halide media and multi-step sealed-autoclave processing, consistent with thick-walled ampoules/autoclaves in SS2 and TS1. There is insufficient evidence from CS that PdIBr forms in simple aqueous precipitation, despite SnICl doing so [CS2], so the route remains challenging.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal/solution media should give high halide mobility, supported by Pt iodide hydrothermal crystal growth [SS2], CuHgSBr hydrothermal synthesis [TS1], and SnICl precipitation from solution [CS2]. The structural reconstruction score is limited because SS2 describes Pt iodide hydrothermal reactions as complex and defect-prone, which may also occur for Pd mixed halides [SS2]. There is insufficient direct evidence from TS for hydrothermal Pd mixed-halide ordering, so uncertainty remains high.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Pd, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered [Target]. The described structure is a simple 2D PdIBr sheet with Pd coordinated to two I and two Br atoms and no reported mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [Target]. SS supports low amorphization risk because close binary halides PdBr₂ and PtI₂ crystallize as ordered low-dimensional structures [SS1, SS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PdIBr is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom [Target], and TS analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally accessible [TS1–TS4]. The analogue support is strong structurally: PdBr₂ supplies square-planar Pd–Br motifs [SS1], PtI₂ supplies a very similar layered iodide framework [SS2], and Pd–Br-containing 2D sheets form in sealed tubes [CS1, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of preformed binary Pd halides, allowing low-temperature halide exchange rather than high-temperature elemental diffusion [SS1, CS1]. The most critical limiting factor is Br:I stoichiometry and ordering, because related Pd–Br sheet chemistry is sensitive to exact stoichiometry [CS1, SS4] and mixed-halide vapor/solution equilibria are likely narrow [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, with sealed-tube solid-state reaction being the most robust first attempt.
