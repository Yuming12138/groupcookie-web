Chemically similar iodate/perovskite-derived compounds are reported with E_hull = 0 and successful ceramic synthesis [CS-1, CS-2], while thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.056–0.069 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized [TS-1 to TS-5]. SS analogues support general ordered oxide framework stability, but insufficient evidence from SS exists for iodine-containing thermodynamics, so SS is treated as neutral for iodine-specific stability.

**Source scoring:** CS = 90, SS = 50 for iodine-specific stability, TS = 85.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LaO₆I is likely a borderline ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest evidence for synthesis comes from chemically similar IO₆-containing alkaline-earth iodates that form at 650 °C in air by ceramic methods with fluoride mineralizer [CS-1, CS-2]. Thermodynamic analogues show that materials with comparable slightly positive hull energies can be synthesized by solid-state or flux methods [TS-1 to TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature mineralized/flux-assisted reaction chemistry, which can preserve high-valent iodine while enabling crystallization [CS-1, CS-2, TS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling iodine volatility/oxidation state and avoiding competing Sr–La oxides or alkali-contaminated phases [Reasonable Inference].
