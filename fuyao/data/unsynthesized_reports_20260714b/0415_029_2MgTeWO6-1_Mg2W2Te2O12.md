### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0280 eV/atom lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom regime, so it is expected to be experimentally accessible as a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase. CS/SS/TS all contain experimentally synthesized analogues with similar or slightly lower hull energies: MgTeMoO₆ at 0.0114 eV/atom (CS1, SS1, TS5), MnTeMoO₆ at 0.0254 eV/atom (TS3), and KSbMo₂O₈ at 0.0201 eV/atom (TS1). The main uncertainty is whether W substitution shifts phase competition toward MgWO₄ + TeO₂/Te oxides, which is not directly resolved by the sources.

#### F2 — Route 1 Flux

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures around 750 °C, air atmosphere, and no high pressure, directly supported by MgTeMoO₆ synthesis (CS1/SS1/TS5). Deductions are assigned for multistep flux handling, slow cooling, and TeO₂ toxicity/volatility risk, with volatility concerns supported indirectly by SS3. The severity is therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1 Flux

The kinetic advantage is strong structural transferability: MgTeMoO₆ is the closest analogue and has nearly identical layered topology (CS1/SS1/TS5). The liquid flux should reduce diffusion limitations compared with bulk ceramic routes, as shown by MgTeMoO₆ crystallizing from TeO₂–MoO₃ flux at 750 °C (CS1/SS1/TS5). The remaining kinetic limitation is dissolution/reaction of MgO and formation of the correct W tetrahedral framework [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 Sealed-Tube Solid-State

The solid-state route operates at moderate temperatures, supported by Zn/MnTeMoO₆ synthesis at 823–873 K and RCrTeO₆ synthesis up to 850 °C (SS3, CS3). Severity increases because SS3 explicitly used evacuated sealed silica tubes to prevent volatilization, and the method required repeated grinding/pelletizing. Thus, the route is feasible but experimentally more demanding than open-air ceramic or hydrothermal routes.

#### F3 — Route 2 Sealed-Tube Solid-State

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because MgO and WO₃ are stable oxides and W diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS3’s repeated 12 h × 3 h heating/grinding sequence indicates that ordering in ATeMoO₆ phases can require multiple cycles. Structural precedent is good, but reaction completion and phase purity will likely be slower than in flux.

#### F2 — Route 3 Ceramic MgWO₄ + TeO₂

The ceramic route is operationally simple and based on CdTeMoO₆/CoTeMoO₆ synthesis at 600 °C from TeO₂ plus preformed molybdate (SS2). It avoids sealed ampoules in the reference, but SS3 shows volatilization can be significant in related Te/Mo oxides, so a volatility deduction is retained. If MgWO₄ must be presynthesized, the overall route becomes multistep [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 Ceramic MgWO₄ + TeO₂

Using MgWO₄ may help preorganize Mg–W–O chemistry, analogous to CdMoO₄/CoMoO₄ precursors in SS2. However, MgWO₄ is also a stable competing phase and may resist reconstruction into layered MgWTeO₆ [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are therefore moderate-to-poor despite the simple setup.

#### F2 — Route 4 Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal analogues were synthesized under mild conditions at 230 °C for 2–3 days in Teflon-lined autoclaves (CS2, CS4/CS5). No high pressure beyond autogenous pressure, no special atmosphere, and simple mixing/heating/cooling are reported in those sources. The main severity deduction is TeO₂ toxicity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4 Hydrothermal

Solution-mediated transport can accelerate crystallization at low temperature, as shown by hydrothermal Te/Mo/W oxide formation (CS2, CS4/CS5). However, no source demonstrates the MgTeMoO₆/MgWTeO₆ layered P2₁2₁2 structure hydrothermally; the hydrothermal examples have different cations and frameworks (CS2, CS4/CS5). The route is therefore kinetically plausible but structurally uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has cations with distinct coordination environments—MgO₆ octahedra, WO₄ tetrahedra, and Te see-saw units—which creates some local ordering complexity [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. However, the closest analogues crystallize as layered ordered oxides by flux or solid-state methods rather than amorphizing (CS1/SS1/SS3). The target connectivity is mainly corner-sharing, not mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, lowering amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgWTeO₆ is likely a **low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase**, because its E_hull is only 0.0280 eV/atom and comparable experimental analogues exist at similar hull energies (TS1, TS3, TS5). The strongest evidence comes from MgTeMoO₆, which is nearly isostructural and chemically identical except for Mo → W substitution, and was successfully grown by TeO₂–MoO₃ flux (CS1, SS1, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a TeO₂–WO₃ flux or liquid transport medium that can overcome MgO/WO₃ solid-state diffusion limits [CS1/SS1/TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control involving volatile TeO₂/WO₃ and stable MgWO₄-like tungstate byproducts [SS3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with flux growth being the most chemically defensible first route even if hydrothermal conditions are milder.
