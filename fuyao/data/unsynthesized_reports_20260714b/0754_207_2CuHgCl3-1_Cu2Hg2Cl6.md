### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and supporting a high thermodynamic stability score. Chemically and structurally related TlHgCl3 is exactly on the hull in the reference data (CS4, SS4), and several Hg-halide thermodynamic analogues are also near-hull, e.g., HgBrI at 0.0105 eV/atom and Hg3S2ICl at 0.0139 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). This supports the idea that Hg-halide frameworks can be stable or nearly stable, though insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact CuHgCl3 phase diagram.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule melt / directed crystallization

The severity is moderate because the route uses only 250–330 °C, far below 1000 °C, but requires vacuum/Ar, sealed tubes, toxic HgCl2, and air-sensitive CuCl; controlled atmospheres and purification are common in TlCuCl3 and TlHgCl3 syntheses (CS1, CS4, SS4). Kinetics are favorable because halide melts/transport media allow reaction at low temperatures, as shown by TlHgCl3 directed crystallization at 553 K and TlCuCl3 Bridgman growth at 450 °C (CS4, SS4, CS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether CuHgCl3 melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically; insufficient evidence from TS for congruent melting of the target.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: low-temperature solid-state annealing

This route is less severe than melt growth in equipment complexity but still needs evacuated ampoules and toxic/volatile HgCl2, consistent with CuHgSCl and Hg/Tl/Pd chloride syntheses in evacuated tubes (SS3, SS1). Kinetics are acceptable but slower because solid-state analogues require 2 weeks or even 8 weeks (SS3, SS1), indicating that long-range ordering and phase equilibration may be limiting. The route is credible because Cu and Hg coexist in a structurally similar halide/chalcogenide (SS3), but the absence of sulfide in CuHgCl3 changes the bonding network [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: chloride-rich solution crystallization

Condition severity is low to moderate because temperatures are mild and no high pressure is needed, as supported by NaHgCl3 crystallization from aqueous solution at room temperature (SS5) and HgGaCl4 solution synthesis at 80 °C (CS3). Kinetics are strong because solution transport bypasses solid-state diffusion, but structural selectivity is uncertain: CS3 yields a zero-dimensional HgGaCl4 cluster material, whereas the target is a 2D CuHgCl3 sheet. The main uncertainty is CuCl solubility and Cu(I) stability in chloride media; this is [Reasonable Inference], with insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for CuHgCl3 solution crystallization.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT / sublimation-gradient crystallization

CVT has moderate severity because it uses vacuum ampoules, volatile HgCl2, toxic precursors, and a temperature gradient, as used in Hg chalcogen-halide thermodynamic analogues (TS2, TS3). Kinetics may be adequate for Hg transport because TS2/TS3 report gas-phase Hg-halide crystal growth, but CuCl transport is less certain [Reasonable Inference]. Phase control is a concern because TS3 reports polymorphous modifications and statistical halogen occupation in related systems, suggesting narrow crystallization windows.

#### F4 — Amorphization/ordering tendency

The target’s simple formula and likely small cell reduce amorphization risk; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40 atom target cell. However, the target combines Cu-centered trigonal pyramids, Cu–Cu bonding, HgCl6 octahedra, corner-sharing, and edge-sharing, which increases ordering complexity; related Hg chloride sheets also show varied coordination environments (CS4, SS4, SS5). The resulting F4 score is medium, not low, because cation-size/coordination mismatch and mixed connectivity may slow crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuHgCl3 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom, well within the range commonly accessible by standard synthesis. The CS and SS data strongly support Hg chloride framework formation, especially through TlHgCl3 and NaHgCl3 analogues (CS4, SS4, SS5), while TS data supports the broader stability and ampoule/CVT accessibility of Hg halides (TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of metal chlorides at low temperature, demonstrated by directed crystallization and evacuated-ampoule syntheses (CS4, SS1, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Cu(I)/Hg(II)/Cl stoichiometry while preventing HgCl2 volatility, Cu(I) oxidation, and competing molecular chloride complexes [Reasonable Inference supported by controlled atmosphere and sealed-ampoule requirements in CS1, CS3, SS1, TS2]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with sealed-ampoule directed crystallization being the most reliable first experimental route.
