### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.028 eV/atom, which is below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold for high synthesizability [Target]. Chemically similar KCr₅S₈ and RbCr₅S₈ are calculated on the hull, supporting stability of Cr-rich sulfide frameworks [CS1, CS2]. Thermodynamic analogues also lie near the hull, typically ~0.035–0.060 eV/atom [TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact SrCr₄S₇ phase may compete with SrS plus binary Cr sulfides, which is inferred from the existence of stable Cr₂S₃/Cr₅S₈ analogues [SS3, SS4; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the closest analogues require ~1000–1100 °C and a dry H₂S stream [CS1, CS2]. H₂S is both reactive and toxic, increasing experimental severity [CS1, CS2]. The route avoids high pressure and is essentially one-step in the analogue reports, which keeps the score from being lower [CS1, CS2]. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact H₂S-stream route to SrCr₄S₇, so TS contributes neutral confidence rather than direct severity evidence.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic advantage is that H₂S can convert Cr to sulfide at high sulfur activity, as shown for alkali thiochromates [CS1, CS2]. The kinetic limitation is refractory Cr powder and the need to form a complex Sr–Cr–S framework rather than a simple binary sulfide [CS1, CS2; Target]. Structural similarity to KCr₅S₈ supports framework transferability [SS5], but Sr²⁺ incorporation changes charge balance and stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct Sr-containing analogue evidence from CS/SS/TS keeps the kinetics score moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2

Sealed-tube synthesis uses vacuum and volatile sulfur, both explicitly present in similar Cr sulfide syntheses [SS1, SS2, TS1–TS4]. Multi-step grinding, annealing, and slow cooling are common in these analogues [SS1, SS2, TS3, TS4]. SrS or Sr metal adds moisture/air-sensitivity by reasonable chemical inference, not directly from the provided sources [Reasonable Inference]. This route is less hazardous than flowing H₂S but more sensitive to sealed-tube pressure and stoichiometry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental/sulfide sealed-tube routes suffer from slow solid-state diffusion, especially with Cr [SS1, SS2, TS4]. Analogues often require several days, repeated grinding, homogenization, or annealing to reach single phase [SS1, SS2, TS3, TS4]. The target’s multiple inequivalent Cr and S sites imply significant ordering requirements [Target]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS for SrCr₄S₇ sealed-tube formation makes this route plausible but not strongly supported.

#### F2 — Route 3

The oxide-sulfidation route avoids >1000 °C conditions because analogues are sulfidized at ~750 °C and annealed up to ~850 °C [CS3, CS5, TS5]. However, it requires toxic H₂S, sulfur-containing sealed anneals, and multiple processing steps [CS3, CS5, TS5]. Oxidized Sr–Cr precursors such as SrCrO₄ are inferred, not directly reported for the target [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, severity is moderate to high.

#### F3 — Route 3

Oxide precursors can improve cation homogeneity, and H₂S provides a strong sulfidation driving force [CS3, CS5, TS5]. The drawback is complete oxide-to-sulfide reconstruction, which can be slow and may trap intermediate oxysulfides or binary sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. The analogous V–Ni sulfides require long annealing up to two weeks, indicating nontrivial kinetics [CS3, CS5, TS5]. Insufficient direct Sr–Cr oxide-sulfidation evidence from CS/SS/TS keeps the route uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is severely penalized because the relevant Cr₅S₈ analogue requires 65–89 kbar and 1200 °C [SS3]. The specialized multi-anvil/DAC-type equipment and quench protocol make the route experimentally demanding [SS3]. The target itself has low E_hull, so high pressure is not clearly necessary [Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for high-pressure SrCr₄S₇ synthesis makes this a backup route.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can accelerate densification and stabilize dense Cr sulfide frameworks, as shown for Cr₅S₈ [SS3]. The short 2 h reaction time in SS3 suggests favorable kinetics once pressure and temperature are applied [SS3]. However, forming SrCr₄S₇ adds Sr ordering and stoichiometry constraints absent in binary Cr₅S₈ [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are moderate, but equipment severity dominates.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large mismatch between highly coordinated Sr and octahedral Cr environments [Target; Reasonable Inference]. It also contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing CrS₆ octahedra, a structural complexity known from the target description and related analogues [Target; CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS5]. The many inequivalent sites in P2₁/c imply a large and highly ordered unit cell [Target; Reasonable Inference]. This raises the risk of poorly crystalline or multiphase products unless long annealing or high sulfur activity is used [SS1, SS2, CS3, CS5, TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCr₄S₇ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from chemically and structurally similar Cr-rich sulfides such as KCr₅S₈/RbCr₅S₈, which form by direct H₂S sulfidation and contain similar CrS₆ networks [CS1, CS2, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur chemical potential while allowing Cr sulfide framework formation, best achieved by dry H₂S or sealed sulfur annealing [CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5, TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: SrS, Cr₂S₃, Cr₅S₈, or other Cr sulfides may compete, and the target has complex cation/anion ordering [SS3, SS4, Target; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires careful atmosphere and stoichiometry optimization rather than a simple one-shot ceramic firing.
