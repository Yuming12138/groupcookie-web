### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase [Target]. This is reinforced by close analogues: Ta₃SBr₇ is nearly on-hull at 0.0049 eV/atom [CS2/SS1/TS1], Nb₃TeCl₇ is on-hull [TS5], and several Ta chloride analogues are on-hull [CS1, SS2/TS2]. Remaining uncertainty is that convex-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of volatile competing phases; CS/SS/TS specifically warn about impurity/byproduct formation in halide systems [CS2/SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, consistent with Ta/Nb chloride analogues made at 600–800 °C [CS1, SS2/TS2, TS5]. The severity deductions come from sealed dry/vacuum or Ar conditions, volatile TaCl₅/S, and air-sensitive TaCl₅, all supported by TaCl₅ handling requirements in halide syntheses [CS1, SS2/TS2]. The main uncertainty is whether Ta₃SCl₇ prefers the lower Ta₃SBr₇ temperature regime [CS2/SS1/TS1] or the higher Nb₃TeCl₇ regime [TS5].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are helped by reactive TaCl₅ and volatile chloride transport, as in Nb₃TeCl₇ and Ta chloride cluster syntheses [TS5, CS1]. They are hindered by refractory Ta metal and likely need extended annealing, as Ta₃SBr₇ and Nb₃TeCl₇ require days to weeks [CS2/SS1/TS1, TS5]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target matches known M₃QX₇ layered motifs [CS2/SS1/TS1, TS5], but pure-phase ordering may still be slow.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 benefits from the exact Ta₃SBr₇ elemental route at only 550 °C [CS2/SS1/TS1]. However, it requires sealed tubes, volatile halogen dosing, toxic/reactive chlorine chemistry, and likely multiple handling/purification steps; Ta₃SBr₇ itself required purified reagents and sealed evacuated tubes [CS2/SS1/TS1]. The uncertainty is whether Cl₂ gives cleaner reaction due to higher reactivity or worse overchlorination to TaCl₅-rich products [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Elemental Ta is kinetically sluggish, and Ta₃SBr₇ synthesis required long reaction times plus further processing [CS2/SS1/TS1]. Halogen vapor improves reaction transport, but byproduct TaBr₅ in the bromide analogue indicates that analogous chloride synthesis may suffer volatile TaCl₅ formation and stoichiometric drift [CS2/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The structural match is excellent, but phase purity is the key kinetic/chemical risk.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
CVT/gradient growth avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, matching layered chloride transport at 600–750 °C [SS3/TS3] and Ta₃SBr₇ gradient growth near 500 °C [CS2/SS1/TS1]. Deductions arise from sealed-tube operation, volatile chloride transport species, air-sensitive chlorides, and multi-step pre-synthesis/growth workflow [CS2/SS1/TS1, SS3/TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an already-demonstrated Ta₃SCl₇-specific transport window, so the route is plausible but less certain.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Gas-phase transport can bypass some bulk diffusion limitations, as shown for reduced layered chlorides such as ZrCl₂ [SS3/TS3]. Still, CVT analogues require long times and can suffer polytypes/intergrowths or small crystals [SS3/TS3, CS2/SS1/TS1]. Thus, kinetics are acceptable for crystal growth but not necessarily efficient for bulk yield.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a single cation species and a simple layered edge-sharing TaSCl₅ octahedral network, reducing glass-forming or amorphization risk [Target]. The closely related Ta₃SBr₇ and Nb₃TeCl₇ layered structures are crystalline and experimentally synthesized [CS2/SS1/TS1, TS5]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct target-specific unit-cell atom count data, so that portion relies mainly on the target structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃SCl₇ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is strong: Ta₃SBr₇ directly validates the Ta₃S X₇ layered motif [CS2/SS1/TS1], and Nb₃TeCl₇ validates a closely related M₃QCl₇ layered chloride synthesized in quantitative yield [TS5]. The most important enabling factor is volatile chloride chemistry, especially TaCl₅-mediated transport/reactivity, which allows formation of refractory Ta compounds at moderate temperatures [CS1, TS5, SS2/TS2]. The most important limiting factor is control of volatile Cl/S chemical potentials and suppression of competing tantalum halides or sulfides, as byproduct halides and purity problems are reported in close halide analogues [CS2/SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2]. Overall, the material should be synthesizable, but careful sealed-ampoule technique is essential.
