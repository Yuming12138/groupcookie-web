### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0250 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic range and justifying a score of 95 [Target]. Chemically similar and thermodynamically similar halides are often stable or only mildly metastable: SnICl and Pd₄Br₄Te₃ are on the hull (CS-1, CS-2), while WOI₃, WCl₆(Te)₂, and Bi(TeBr₂)₂ are near 0.024–0.033 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). Structural analogues provide less direct evidence because many are molecular clusters rather than Co-based mixed halide sheets; therefore, **insufficient evidence from SS** for direct CoIBr thermodynamic stability, treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable relative to high-pressure TlFeBr₄ synthesis in SS-5. However, it requires dry/vacuum ampoule handling and volatile halogen control, consistent with sealed ampoule methods in CS-2, SS-4, TS-1, and TS-4. The main severity penalties are vacuum, sealed volatile components, multistep dry handling, and toxic/air-sensitive halide reagents [SS-2, SS-4, TS-4].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The use of CoBr₂/CoI₂ should provide high precursor activity compared with refractory oxides because both are ionic halide salts [Reasonable Inference]. Moderate-temperature halide syntheses in SS-4 and TS-1 suggest that halide mobility or vapor-assisted transport can occur well below cation melting points. The uncertainty is whether CoBr₂ and CoI₂ will order into the orthorhombic CoIBr sheet rather than segregating into binary halides; phase sensitivity in CS-1 and CS-2 motivates the moderate structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses autogenous hydrothermal pressure, but not multi-GPa pressure, so the high-pressure penalty does not apply; TS-5 demonstrates hydrothermal halide crystallization in sealed ampoules/autoclaves. The penalties arise from sealed volatile acids, corrosion/toxicity of HBr/HI, and multistep ampoule/autoclave handling [TS-5, SS-3]. Compared with sealed dry CVT, it is less demanding in atmosphere control because vacuum or inert gas is not necessarily required during reaction [TS-5].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis receives the best kinetic score because soluble cobalt halides and concentrated hydrohalic acids should provide high ion mobility [Reasonable Inference]. TS-5 supports fast crystallization in concentrated HX over 24 h, and SS-3 supports iodide crystallization from HI solution. The main uncertainty is chemical selectivity: hydrated cobalt halides, Co–halide complexes, or oxidation of iodide could compete, consistent with phase sensitivity in CS-1 and moisture/air sensitivity in SS-3.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from moderate temperatures like those used for Ga/In tellurium iodides in SS-4 and WOI₃ in TS-1. It nevertheless requires an evacuated ampoule, volatile halogen transport agents, and dry handling, producing the same main severity penalties as Route 1 [SS-4, TS-1, TS-4]. It is less severe than the RhTeCl high-temperature CVT example in CS-3 but still operationally nontrivial because of halogen pressure and toxicity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport is kinetically favorable for crystal growth because reactive halogen vapors can transport material and bypass slow bulk diffusion, as shown by CVT analogues in CS-3, SS-4, TS-1, and TS-4. The pathway should allow direct assembly of low-dimensional halide motifs [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting uncertainty is composition control: mixed Br/I volatility and chemical potentials may drift along the ampoule, and analogous halide systems report byproducts or yield sensitivity (CS-3, SS-2, TS-4).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Target]. Its described framework is simple corner-sharing CoI₂Br₂ tetrahedra without mixed edge/face connectivity, reducing topological frustration [Target]. CS/SS/TS analogues show many low-dimensional halides can crystallize as single crystals, but they do not directly determine the target’s amorphization risk; where not directly informative, evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient and treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoIBr is likely a **mildly metastable but experimentally accessible** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0250 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from CS and TS: chemically similar mixed halides and thermodynamic analogues are frequently stable or near-stable and are synthesized by solution, sealed-tube, hydrothermal, or CVT methods (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the high chemical mobility of halides, especially under hydrothermal or halogen-vapor conditions, as demonstrated by concentrated HX hydrothermal synthesis in TS-5 and ampoule/CVT halide growth in SS-4 and TS-1. The most critical limiting factor is **Br/I stoichiometry and hydration control**, because mixed-halide systems show phase sensitivity in CS-1/CS-2 and moisture-sensitive halide chemistry is noted in SS-2/SS-4. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with Route 2 best for rapid screening and Route 3 best for single-crystal growth if hydration becomes problematic.
