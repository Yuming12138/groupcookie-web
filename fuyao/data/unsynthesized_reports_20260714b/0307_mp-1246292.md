### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff, so it is likely synthesizable but may be metastable rather than a robust ground state. CS supports this because chemically similar Cu₃P phases with E_hull values of ~0.025 and ~0.059 eV/atom are reported with high-confidence solid-state syntheses (CS-1, CS-2). SS and TS strengthen the case because several Cu–As analogues with E_hull from 0 to ~0.038 eV/atom are synthesized by direct or sealed solid-state routes (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Cu₄As competes with other binary Cu–As phases not listed in the reference set.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for sealed-vacuum/inert conditions, volatile/toxic As, and multi-step processing. These penalties are supported by sealed ampoule and glovebox methods in Cu–As analogues (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-5) and by slow sealed pnictide synthesis in Cu₃P (CS-1). The temperature itself is moderate relative to high-pressure or ultra-high-temperature syntheses; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any need for pressure. The uncertainty is the exact As vapor pressure at the needed formation temperature, which may force lower-temperature or staged reaction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Cu and As are acceptable but not highly activated precursors; this is consistent with elemental-powder Cu–As analogues requiring heating and tempering (SS-2, SS-4; TS-5). Diffusion is manageable because related arsenides form around 850–950 °C (SS-2, SS-4; TS-2), but the target’s cubic P2₁3 structure is not directly matched by the analogues. Regrinding and tempering in SS-5 indicate that competing phases and incomplete reaction are realistic concerns. Thus Route 1 has moderate kinetics but significant ordering uncertainty.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 still requires sealed inert/vacuum handling and toxic arsenide precursors, but it reduces the hazard of free elemental As compared with Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support is SS-5, where Cu₃As is explicitly used as a precursor in evacuated quartz for Ca(Cu₂As)₂. Multi-step processing is unavoidable if Cu₃As must be prepared first or if regrinding/reannealing is needed, as reported in SS-5. No CS/SS/TS source indicates high pressure, so pressure severity is low.

#### F3: Route 2 kinetics

Using Cu₃As gives a chemically preorganized Cu–As precursor, which should improve kinetics relative to starting from separate Cu and As [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5 directly supports Cu₃As as a reactive precursor for Cu–As frameworks, though it also reports impurity formation requiring reprocessing. Diffusion should be moderate because only Cu redistribution and Cu–As ordering are needed rather than complete pnictogen incorporation from vapor. The main uncertainty is whether the P2₁3 Cu₄As phase is the equilibrium low-temperature phase or requires a narrow annealing/quench window.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Melt-assisted synthesis is more severe because it may require temperatures near or above 1000 °C, sealed metal liners, and strict control of volatile As [Reasonable Inference]. TS-3 supports arc melting plus long annealing for Cu intermetallics, and CS-4 supports sealed-tube intermetallic homogenization, but neither directly addresses volatile As in a binary Cu–As melt. The route avoids high pressure but adds containment and safety complexity. Therefore it is kinetically attractive but operationally challenging.

#### F3: Route 3 kinetics

Melt or partial-melt reaction should strongly improve homogenization and reduce diffusion limitations, as reflected by arc-melted UCu₅Sn in TS-3. Long post-annealing is still likely needed for ordering, also supported by the one-week anneal in TS-3. Structural reconstruction may be incomplete if the target is metastable and forms only on cooling or at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore high in kinetic potential but uncertain in phase selectivity.

#### F2: Route 4 condition severity

CVT/vapor-assisted growth needs evacuated two-zone tubes, volatile transport species, and toxic As-containing charges. CS-5 supports I₂-assisted CVT in evacuated silica tubes, while CS-3 supports controlled vapor-pressure intermetallic synthesis, but neither directly reports Cu₄As. The method is not high-pressure or ultra-high-temperature, but the chemical transport environment adds risk of side reactions. Thus severity is moderate-challenging.

#### F3: Route 4 kinetics

Vapor transport can bypass slow solid-state diffusion and promote crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS-5 and CS-3 using two-zone vapor methods. However, phase predictability is weak: CS-5 explicitly reports Ni₃Sn as an unexpected byproduct during an attempted ternary synthesis, showing that CVT can select unintended phases. For Cu₄As, iodine may form copper iodides or alter As chemical potential [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best for crystal growth after the bulk phase field is known.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one metal/cation species, Cu, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any mixed-cation amorphization problem. The target structure contains corner- and face-sharing AsCu₁₂ cuboctahedra, but not the full vertex/edge/face coexistence criterion; CS-5 and other analogues show complex cuboctahedral sharing, but that is not direct evidence for amorphization. The main structural issue is not amorphization but crystalline phase competition and ordering, as seen in SS-5. Therefore F4 is high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₄As is likely a borderline metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS contains successfully synthesized analogues in the ~0.03–0.06 eV/atom range (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: CS supports sealed solid-state pnictide chemistry (CS-1, CS-2), while SS/TS provide direct Cu–As solid-state precedents (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of Cu₃As or related Cu–As binary precursors, directly supported by SS-5, which should reduce As volatility and improve Cu–As homogeneity. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition/stoichiometry control, because SS-5 reports impurity formation and reprocessing, and CS-5 shows that vapor methods can produce unintended phases. Overall, Cu₄As should be synthesizable in a competent solid-state pnictide laboratory, with Route 2 as the most defensible first attempt.
