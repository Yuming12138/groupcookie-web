### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold specified in the scoring rubric, so the base thermodynamic score is high. TS-1 and TS-2 are especially relevant because they are Sb-containing double perovskites with E_hull ≈0.052 and ≈0.050 eV/atom, respectively, and both have reported solid-state syntheses. The remaining uncertainty is that K2TaSbO6 may be metastable with respect to KTaO3, KSbO3, Ta2O5, or antimonate byproducts by [Reasonable Inference], but the hull energy is low enough to justify experimental pursuit.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard ceramic conditions closely matching CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, TS-1, and TS-2, all of which use air firing, oxides/carbonates, and repeated grinding. The penalties arise from >1000 °C firing, multi-step calcination/regrinding, and likely volatile K/Sb loss; Sb volatility concerns are supported by SS-4, and antimony excess in some syntheses is supported by TS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly quantifies K volatility in K2TaSbO6, so that part is treated as [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Ceramic synthesis is structurally credible because TS-1 and CS-4 are Fm-3m double perovskites, and SS-2/TS-2 show related perovskite-derived oxides can form through solid-state reaction. However, Ta2O5 is refractory and likely slow to react by [Reasonable Inference], and TS-1’s 135 h sintering indicates slow ordering/phase formation for Sb double perovskites. The kinetic score is therefore moderate-low despite strong structural analogy.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The sol-gel route inherits multi-step processing from CS-4, including solution preparation, evaporation, resin formation, decomposition, and final firing. It still likely needs near-1000–1100 °C crystallization because CS-4 used 1100 °C and TS-1 used ~1090–1100 °C. The route avoids high pressure and reactive gas, but volatile K/Sb retention and Sb toxicity remain concerns by [Reasonable Inference] and by Sb-related handling implications from SS-4/TS-4.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CS-4 provides the strongest kinetic support: citrate/nitrate solutions give random cation distribution at the atomic level, which directly addresses Ta/Sb/K mixing. The target’s Fm-3m double-perovskite topology is close to CS-4 Ba2CoMoO6 and TS-1 Sr2YSbO6, so structural reconstruction is not expected to be severe. Remaining uncertainty is Ta chemistry in solution, because CS-4 did not include Ta; soluble Ta precursors are therefore [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

TS-3 shows high-pressure alkali antimonate perovskite synthesis requires 10.5 GPa, 1150 °C, Au capsule sealing, quenching, and slow decompression. Applying that style to K2TaSbO6 incurs heavy penalties for high pressure, high temperature, capsule processing, and multi-step workup. This route is feasible only in specialized HPHT facilities.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

HPHT can force denser perovskite frameworks, as TS-3 demonstrates for NaSbO3. However, K2TaSbO6 is a quaternary ordered double perovskite, so it requires both framework formation and Ta/Sb ordering, which TS-3 does not directly demonstrate. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that HPHT improves Ta/Sb ordering specifically, so the kinetic score remains moderate.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Hydrothermal synthesis avoids high firing temperature and can reduce K/Sb volatilization, which is a major severity advantage by [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5 and TS-5 support hydrothermal oxide synthesis generally, but TS-5 also shows that hydrothermal conditions can become severe at 750 °C and 1000 bar. For the proposed lower-temperature alkaline route, the main penalties are corrosive/toxic reagents and likely multistep washing/annealing.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Solution-mediated transport can improve precursor activity and ion mobility by [Reasonable Inference], and hydrothermal oxide crystallization is supported by SS-5 and TS-5. However, SS-5 concerns ilmenite-type alkali oxides rather than cubic Fm-3m double perovskites, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports hydrothermal B-site ordered K2TaSbO6 formation. The structural-reconstruction uncertainty is therefore substantial.

#### F4 — Amorphization/disorder tendency

The target has a large size/coordination contrast between K–O cuboctahedra and Ta/Sb octahedra, as shown by K–O = 2.84 Å versus Ta–O/Sb–O ≈2.00–2.02 Å in the target description. The target also has mixed connectivity: Ta/Sb octahedra share corners, K polyhedra share faces, and O-centered environments include corner/edge/face-sharing motifs. The simple cubic Fm-3m description avoids a large-cell penalty, but cation disorder or amorphous intermediate formation remains a medium risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2TaSbO6 is likely a low-energy metastable or barely-on-hull-accessible phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The best support comes from TS-1 and CS-4, which show cubic Fm-3m double perovskites can be synthesized near similar structural/thermodynamic regimes, while TS-2 supports Sb-containing double-perovskite formation by oxide/carbonate routes. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match to known ordered double perovskites, especially the un-tilted Fm-3m framework in TS-1 and CS-4. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of K volatility, Sb oxidation/volatility, and sluggish Ta diffusion by [Reasonable Inference]. A citrate/nitrate sol-gel route is therefore preferred because it maximizes cation homogeneity while staying within synthesis conditions demonstrated by CS-4.
