### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

HoOBr has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high-feasibility boundary in the provided rubric [Target]. Multiple chemically and structurally analogous LnOBr phases are computed/reported at E_hull = 0.0, including LuOBr, LaOBr, NdOBr, YbOBr, GdOBr, and SmOBr [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-4]. TS supports that a related oxyhalide with similar metastability, YOCl at E_hull ≈ 0.0468 eV/atom, is synthetically accessible [TS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom may indicate slight metastability, so phase purity may depend on bromine chemical potential and atmosphere [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

The best-supported hydrate route uses only 450–500 °C for Eu–Lu and Y oxybromides, so it avoids high-temperature and high-pressure penalties [CS-3, SS-4]. The process is multi-step—dissolution, evaporation/crystallization, and decomposition—and uses corrosive HBr, so those deductions are applied [CS-3, CS-4]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for this hydrate route, so it is treated as neutral for this route-specific severity assessment. The main uncertainty is whether Ho hydrate decomposition exactly mirrors the reported Eu–Lu group average without requiring additional annealing [CS-3, SS-4].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because the reactive bromide hydrate decomposes to LnOBr in 40–60 min, which is much faster than typical refractory solid-state ceramic synthesis [CS-3, SS-4]. The low temperature relative to refractory oxide diffusion suggests a local decomposition mechanism rather than long-range cation diffusion [CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is modest because the product is the common Matlockite LnOBr motif seen across Nd, Sm, Gd, Lu, and Yb analogues [CS-1, CS-3–CS-5, SS-1–SS-4]. Uncertainty remains around hydrate stoichiometry and water partial pressure, which can affect oxybromide versus bromide/oxide impurities [CS-4; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The NH₄Br/oxide route uses moderate temperatures: LaOBr was treated at 400 and 900 °C, while GdOBr-related materials used 450 °C plus 850–1000 °C recrystallization [CS-2, CS-5, SS-3]. No high pressure is indicated, and atmospheres are ordinary inert N₂/Ar rather than H₂/NH₃/vacuum [CS-2, CS-5, SS-5, TS-1]. A deduction is applied for volatile ammonium halide behavior and possible need for sealed/controlled conditions, supported by YOX ammonium halide ampoule chemistry [SS-5, TS-1]. The uncertainty is whether HoOBr needs only a short LaOBr-type treatment or a higher-temperature GdOBr-type recrystallization step [CS-2, CS-5].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

NH₄Br activates the oxide and provides bromide chemical potential, but Ho₂O₃ remains a refractory oxide reactant, so precursor activity is lower than the hydrate route [CS-2, CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. Flux recrystallization with REBr₃/KBr improves diffusion and crystallinity by analogy to GdOBr-related phosphors [CS-5, SS-3]. Structural formation is supported by multiple Matlockite LnOBr analogues [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-4], but ammonium halide chemistry can involve intermediate ammonium hexahalometallates and stoichiometry-sensitive products [SS-5, TS-1]. Thus kinetics are good but less direct than hydrate decomposition.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth temperatures of 850–900 °C for SmOBr/LaOBr and 850–1000 °C for GdOBr-related recrystallization avoid >1000 °C penalties [SS-1, CS-5, SS-3]. However, the route requires anhydrous HoBr₃ preparation/drying, dry-box handling, slow cooling, and flux removal, so multi-step and air/moisture-sensitive precursor penalties apply [SS-1]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for HoOBr flux growth and is neutral here. The principal uncertainty is whether HoBr₃/KBr flux composition must be optimized to prevent halide-rich residues [SS-1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux transport should produce favorable ion mobility, as SmOBr and LaOBr single crystals grew from rare-earth bromide flux under slow cooling [SS-1]. GdOBr-related phases also used REBr₃/KBr flux recrystallization, supporting the kinetic benefit of molten halide media [CS-5, SS-3]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because SmOBr is nearly identical structurally to HoOBr in the Matlockite P4/nmm family [SS-1, Target]. The limitation is that slow cooling and crystal habit control make the process less kinetically simple than hydrate decomposition [SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

The sealed high-temperature LuOBr analogue uses 1273 K and 1073 K long heat treatments in a sealed Ta container under Ar handling [CS-1, SS-2]. Although the temperature is only about 1000 °C, the route uses air-sensitive lanthanide metal/tribromide, sealed containment, and multiple processing steps [CS-1, SS-2]. TS provides insufficient direct support for this sealed metal/tribromide route and is neutral. The severity score is therefore moderate-low despite avoiding high pressure [CS-1, SS-2].

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

The LuOBr route required 3 days at 1273 K plus 10 days at 1073 K, implying slow equilibration compared with the hydrate route [CS-1, SS-2]. LuOBr was obtained as a by-product at about 35% yield, indicating poor phase-selective kinetics and competing reactions [CS-1, SS-2]. Structural similarity to HoOBr is strong because LuOBr is a close LnOBr analogue [CS-1, SS-2], but synthesis transferability is weakened by non-targeted formation. The route is therefore feasible but not recommended.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

HoOBr is a simple single-cation oxybromide, so the cation-radius mismatch check does not apply [Target]. The Matlockite P4/nmm LnOBr structure is a small layered unit rather than a large complex cell, based on the analogous LnOBr descriptions [Target, CS-1, SS-1]. The target has corner- and edge-sharing OHo₄ tetrahedra but no reported face sharing, so the mixed connectivity criterion requiring vertex + edge + face coexistence is not triggered [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is minimal because the structural description is explicit [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HoOBr is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, while many LnOBr analogues lie on the hull [Target, CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-4]. The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis: NdOBr, SmOBr, GdOBr, YbOBr, LuOBr, and LaOBr all adopt closely related Matlockite/P4/nmm oxybromide structures and have reported synthetic routes [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-4]. The most enabling factor is the direct rare-earth bromide hydrate decomposition route, which explicitly covers Eu–Lu and Y and therefore includes Ho by lanthanide-series transfer [CS-3, SS-4]. The main limiting factor is bromide/water chemical-potential control: HBr concentration, hydrate composition, and decomposition atmosphere must be controlled to avoid HoBr₃, Ho₂O₃, or bromine-deficient phases [CS-3, CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, HoOBr should be highly synthesizable, especially as a powder by controlled hydrate decomposition.
