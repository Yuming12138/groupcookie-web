### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.045 eV/atom, placing it within the high-probability synthesizable range by the required scoring rubric [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, including ZnIn2S4 at ~0.060 eV/atom and MnIn2Se4 at ~0.032 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target may be metastable with respect to binary In2S3/InCl3 or other In–S–Cl phases, and CS data are mixed because some chemically similar compounds have high computed E_hull yet are reported experimentally [CS1, CS3].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and dry handling, matching GaTeCl synthesis in evacuated glass ampoules under inert handling [CS4]. The proposed temperature is moderate because the closest chalcohalide analogue GaTeCl forms at 400 °C [CS4], and InTeBr growth occurs at 520 °C [SS2]. The main severity penalties are volatile/moisture-sensitive halide handling and vacuum sealing, as also seen for indium bromide sublimation [SS1].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The kinetics are favorable because InCl3 is a reactive halide and the stoichiometric reaction with In2S3 avoids multiple cations [Reasonable Inference]. The 2D layered target resembles GaTeCl and InTeBr chalcohalide frameworks, lowering the structural reconstruction burden [CS4, SS2]. Uncertainty remains because sulfide chemistry may be less mobile than telluride/bromide analogues and may require longer annealing [TS2, TS3].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
CVT requires vacuum ampoules, transport agents, and multiple operations, as in ZnIn2S4 and MnIn2Se4 syntheses [SS3, TS1, TS5]. It avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but it adds hazard and complexity through I2 or AlCl3 and volatile halide species [SS3, TS5]. The severity is therefore higher than Route 1 despite strong crystal-growth precedent.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
CVT has excellent mass transport because vapor-phase species bypass solid diffusion, as demonstrated for layered chalcogenides [SS3, TS1, TS5]. It is especially plausible for InSCl because indium halides are volatile and sublimation growth is documented for related indium bromides [SS1]. The uncertainty is the transport chemistry: too high a chloride or iodine activity may favor InCl3/InI species rather than stoichiometric InSCl [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Solvothermal synthesis is low-temperature, supported by indium chalcogenide formation at 185–200 °C [CS3]. However, it uses sealed autoclaves and potentially corrosive chloride/amine/sulfide media, giving nontrivial handling penalties [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS for solvothermal InSCl formation, so the route is not scored as easy overall.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Solution precursors are highly reactive and diffusion limitations are minimal, consistent with solvothermal indium chalcogenide synthesis [CS3]. The limiting kinetic issue is structural selectivity: no provided source shows the target’s layered InSCl topology forming from solution, and solution reactions may instead produce In2S3 or hydrolysis products [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetic access to “some product” is easy, but kinetic access to the specific crystalline phase is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, so radius mismatch-driven glass formation is not expected [Target]. The described structure contains corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so it does not meet the full mixed-connectivity trigger defined in the prompt [Target]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not give a direct amorphization signal for InSCl, so that subfactor retains some uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InSCl is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a strongly unstable phase, because its E_hull is only 0.045 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from close chalcohalide analogues: GaTeCl is synthesized by sealed-ampoule annealing from GaCl3/Ga2Te3 [CS4], and InTeBr is grown from InBr3/In2Te3 [SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a chemically matched, stoichiometric precursor pair, InCl3 + In2S3, which can in principle deliver the exact In:S:Cl ratio [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is chloride volatility and moisture sensitivity, which can shift stoichiometry or produce secondary indium halide/sulfide phases [SS1, CS4, TS5]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis being the most credible first attempt.
