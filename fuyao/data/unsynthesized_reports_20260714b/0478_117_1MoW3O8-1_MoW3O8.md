### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score. This is consistent with several thermodynamic analogues that also have E_hull = 0 (TS1–TS5), and with at least one chemically similar tungstate, MnWO4, also at E_hull = 0 (CS5). The main uncertainty is that several structurally similar reduced Mo oxides in SS have relatively high E_hull values (SS1–SS4), indicating that structural similarity alone does not guarantee easy equilibrium synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1: Evacuated sealed-tube solid-state route

Route 1 is moderately severe because it likely requires 900–1200 °C and vacuum sealing, as seen in CS1, CS4, SS1, and SS3. Volatile high-valent oxides such as WO3/MoO3 motivate sealed ampoules, consistent with the sealed-tube practice in CS1/CS4/SS1/SS3, although volatility for the exact target is inferred rather than directly reported. The route avoids high pressure and exotic equipment, but oxygen fugacity control is critical.

#### F3 — Route 1: Evacuated sealed-tube solid-state route

The kinetics are difficult because W and Mo are refractory, and oxide/metal redox mixtures can react slowly [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in related systems—2 d at 900 °C in CS4 and 7 d at 1200 °C in SS1/SS3—support a low-to-moderate kinetic score. The target’s edge-sharing W/Mo sheet likely requires substantial reconstruction from simple WO2/MoO2 or WO3/MoO3/metal mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux growth

Flux growth has somewhat harsher procedural complexity than direct solid-state synthesis because slow cooling and flux removal are required, as shown by SS2 and CS5. The thermal severity is moderate to high: SS2 uses 850 °C for 2 weeks, while CS5 uses 1050 °C and controlled cooling. Vacuum sealing and possible volatile oxide containment reduce the F2 score.

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux growth

The flux route has better kinetics than dry solid-state synthesis because molten Sn or tungstate flux can enhance mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. SS2 shows that reduced Mo oxide crystals can be grown from Sn flux at 850 °C, and CS5 shows tungstate crystals can be grown from oxide flux near 1050 °C. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether W and Mo will order into the exact 3:1 W3MoO8 sheet rather than forming binary WO2/MoO2 or mixed Mo–W oxide impurities.

#### F2 — Route 3: High-pressure reducing synthesis

CS2 directly supports a high-pressure route for chemically similar W oxides at 50 kbar and 1570 K under graphite-reducing conditions. The formal severity score is not extremely low because 50 kbar is approximately 5 GPa and the rubric penalizes only >5 GPa, but in practical terms this route is specialized. The main advantage is that high pressure can suppress volatile loss and stabilize dense reduced oxide frameworks [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: High-pressure reducing synthesis

High pressure and high temperature can accelerate densification and crystallization, but W/Mo cation diffusion remains intrinsically slow due to refractory bonding [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 confirms that W oxide phases can be made under these conditions, but it does not prove the specific W3MoO8 topology. Therefore, the kinetic score is moderate-low despite the favorable thermodynamics.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has low amorphization tendency because W and Mo are chemically and size-compatible, and the target W–O/Mo–O bond lengths are very similar. The structure description reports primarily edge-sharing W/MoO6 pentagonal pyramids, not highly mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity. The largest uncertainty is the actual crystallographic Z and unit-cell atom count, but the formula and Pmm2 sheet description do not suggest an exceptionally large cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

W3MoO8 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support using sealed, low-oxygen-fugacity or reducing conditions for W/Mo oxides, while TS analogues support the general plausibility of stable complex oxides but are less chemically specific. The most critical enabling factor is the close chemical compatibility of W and Mo in similar oxygen coordination environments, which should favor cation mixing/order in a reduced oxide sheet. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: refractory W/Mo oxides and metals diffuse slowly, and related SS analogues often require long high-temperature treatments or show phase-control issues. Overall, the material is feasible but will likely need tight oxygen fugacity and stoichiometry control.
