### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the 90–100 scoring bin. CS evidence is supportive because chemically similar oxides CS-1 and CS-5 are on or near the hull. TS evidence is also supportive because experimentally reported analogues with E_hull ≈0.059–0.096 eV/atom exist by flux, solid-state, hydrothermal, and high-pressure routes (TS-1 to TS-5). SS evidence is less useful because several structurally similar entries have much higher hull energies, so SS is treated as neutral/weak for thermodynamic prediction.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

For Route 1, condition severity is moderate: CS-1 and CS-5 show related oxides can be made in air below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature/high-pressure penalty applies. The main deductions are repeated calcination/grinding and toxic Bi/Te precursors. Kinetically, nitrates and Te(OH)₆ are reactive according to CS-1/CS-5, but the target’s low-symmetry P1 structure differs from the perovskite-derived CS analogues, lowering the structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 but better kinetics because CS-3 and CS-4 explicitly use citrate/nitrate chemistry to produce homogeneous multication oxide precursors. The low diffusion length is a strong enabling factor for Li/Sr/Bi/Te ordering. The uncertainty is that CS-3/CS-4 required reducing atmospheres for Fe/W chemistry, whereas the target likely needs oxidizing conditions for Bi/Te; this atmosphere change is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild thermal severity because SS-2 and TS-3 demonstrate hydrothermal crystallization at ~200–220 °C. However, F3 is limited by structural uncertainty: hydrothermal chemistry may produce hydroxides, oxyhydroxides, or hydrated tellurates rather than the anhydrous LiSrBiTeO₆ phase. SS-2 and TS-3 support Bi and Li oxoanion solution chemistry, but insufficient evidence from CS exists for hydrothermal Li–Sr–Bi–Te quaternary oxide synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 benefits kinetically from flux transport, supported by Bi₂O₃ flux growth of PdBi₂O₄ in TS-5 and sealed oxide flux growth in TS-1. Its condition severity is worse because flux growth is multi-step and may require sealing or covered crucibles to control Li/Te volatility. The largest uncertainty is stoichiometry: Bi₂O₃-rich flux could stabilize Bi-rich impurities instead of the desired 1:1:1:1 Li/Sr/Bi/Te phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization risk is the large cation-size and coordination mismatch between Li and Sr/Bi/Te, supported by the target bond lengths and by the need for ordered frameworks in CS-5. The target does not clearly exceed 40 atoms per cell from the provided information, and mixed edge/face/vertex sharing is not explicitly stated. Therefore only one F4 trigger is assigned, giving a low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiSrBiTeO₆ is likely a marginally metastable or barely stable phase rather than a robust deep ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The chemically similar data support synthesis most strongly: CS-1 demonstrates air synthesis of tellurate oxides, and CS-5 demonstrates Li-containing ordered oxides from nitrate/oxide precursors. Thermodynamic analogues show that phases in the same metastability range can be synthesized, but TS-2 and TS-4 warn that phase competition and special conditions may occur. The most critical enabling factor is homogeneous multication mixing, best addressed by a citrate/Pechini route. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the correct Li/Sr/Bi/Te/O stoichiometry while preventing Li/Te loss and competing Bi–Te–O or Sr–Te–O phase formation.
