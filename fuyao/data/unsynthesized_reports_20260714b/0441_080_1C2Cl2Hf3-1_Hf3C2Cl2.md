### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is 100 by the provided rubric. Several analogous materials in CS, SS, and TS are also on or near the convex hull: ThNCl and ThNBr have E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, CS2), Hf2N2S has one stable polymorph at E_hull = 0.0 and one near-hull polymorph at 0.021 eV/atom (CS3, CS4), and Ta3AlC2/Hf2SnC analogues are stable (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for layered chloride carbides **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C if limited to ~800–950 °C, but it requires sealed/vacuum or Ar handling, which is supported by sealed Hf2N2S and Hf2SnC syntheses (CS3, CS4, SS3 / TS3). The volatile chloride source HfCl4 and moisture-sensitive chloride handling are **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with moisture exclusion emphasized for Th nitride halides (CS1, CS2). The main uncertainty is whether direct reaction gives the target rather than HfC + Hf chlorides or overchlorinated products **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is low because Hf metal and HfC are refractory and likely diffusion-limited **[Reasonable Inference]**; this is consistent with long annealing times for Hf2SnC-type phases (SS3 / TS3) and high-temperature carbide synthesis for TaC (SS2 / TS2). Direct assembly requires substantial structural reconstruction into a 2D Hf3C2Cl2 sheet, unlike topochemical retention of a parent lattice. Chloride vapor transport may help, but there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that this exact chlorocarbide forms by direct solid-state reaction.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because flux methods are well supported for layered carbide growth at high temperature under Ar (SS1 / TS1), but the adapted chloride-flux route likely requires sealed handling and volatile chloride reagents **[Reasonable Inference]**. It may exceed 1000 °C depending on optimization, although a chloride flux could lower the required temperature relative to Ta3AlC2 synthesis at 1400 °C (SS1 / TS1). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a chloride flux directly yielding Hf3C2Cl2, so this deduction remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve mass transport, as Al/Sn melt improves Ta3AlC2 crystal formation (SS1 / TS1), but HfC remains refractory and slow to equilibrate **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target shares layered carbide motifs with Ta3AlC2 and Hf2SnC (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3), supporting moderate structural compatibility. The major uncertainty is maintaining Cl activity without dissolving/decomposing the carbide framework **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is favored because Hf2N2S topochemical synthesis occurs at 750 °C in sealed vacuum tubes, and rapid heating helps avoid mixed phases (CS3, CS4). It still requires controlled atmosphere, volatile/moisture-sensitive chlorides, and multiple steps, including parent carbide synthesis and post-reaction cleanup **[Reasonable Inference]**. Moisture sensitivity is plausible because related nitride halides require moisture exclusion (CS1, CS2).

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic score is highest because a layered parent carbide can preserve the Hf–C scaffold while the A-layer is removed/replaced by Cl **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural similarity to MAX/H-phase carbides is supported by Ta3AlC2 and Hf2SnC analogues with comparable M–C distances (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is whether a stable Hf3C2Cl2 termination can be achieved without forming disordered MXene-like products or amorphous chlorinated carbides **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure HfNBr synthesis uses 3–5 GPa and 1000–1100 °C in Pt/Au capsules with halide fluxes (CS5), so the route is experimentally demanding. The scoring rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, but specialized multianvil equipment remains a practical challenge from CS5. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS that high pressure is necessary for the carbide chloride target, making this a backup rather than preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure and halide fluxes can crystallize layered Hf nitride halides (CS5), giving moderate kinetic support. However, carbide formation from HfC/Hf/halide mixtures is less directly supported, and HfC diffusion remains difficult **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route may be better for single-crystal growth after phase discovery than for initial bulk synthesis **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The formula is small and likely does not exceed 40 atoms per primitive cell **[Reasonable Inference]**. Mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing connectivity is explicitly present in the target description, so one amorphization trigger applies; this creates low–medium risk, especially during aggressive chlorination **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf3(CCl)2 / Hf3C2Cl2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS data strongly supports Hf–halide layered chemistry and topochemical transformations in sealed/vacuum systems (CS3–CS5), while the SS/TS data supports layered Hf/Ta carbide frameworks with similar metal–carbon bonding (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is using a preformed layered Hf–C parent so that the Hf–C framework does not need to be built by slow diffusion from refractory HfC. The most critical limiting factor is controlling chloride chemical potential: too little Cl may leave parent carbide or deintercalated intermediates, while too much Cl may form HfCl4 or amorphous chlorinated carbon/carbide products **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the best-supported route is topochemical chlorination/A-layer replacement under sealed, dry conditions.
