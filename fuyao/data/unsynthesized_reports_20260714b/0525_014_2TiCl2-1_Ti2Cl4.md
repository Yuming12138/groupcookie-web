### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the top of the scoring range. CS provides strong support because ZrCl₂ is on hull and structurally/chemically close to layered TiCl₂ (CS-2), while TiBr₂ is a near-hull Ti dihalide analogue (CS-1). SS and TS also support stability because several similar reduced/layered chlorides are on hull, including ZrCl₂, Nb₃TeCl₇, K₂Zr₇Cl₁₈, and Sc₇Cl₁₀ (SS-1/SS-2/SS-4; TS-1/TS-2/TS-3/TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic existence, but whether the monoclinic P2₁/m polytype is the experimentally accessible polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from the direct TiBr₂ vapor-phase precedent, where elemental Ti and halogen vapor produced a layered TiX₂ phase (CS-1). The severity is moderate because temperatures are below 1000 °C and no high pressure is required, but toxic/volatile chlorine sources and strict exclusion of moisture are required by analogy to volatile halide syntheses (CS-1/CS-2). Kinetics are favorable for transport/deposition, but composition control is the limiting issue because TiBr₂ required rapid cooling to avoid back-reaction (CS-1), and ZrCl₂ analogues show phase/intergrowth sensitivity (CS-2/SS-2/TS-2). SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for TiCl₂ vapor synthesis specifically, so their route-specific signal is treated as neutral to mildly supportive via ZrCl₂.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is strongly supported by ZrCl₂ CVT, which is both chemically and structurally close to target TiCl₂ and uses sealed Ta tubes, volatile tetrachloride transport, and 600–750 °C gradients (CS-2/SS-2/TS-2). Severity is higher than Route 1 because the route requires sealed refractory-metal tubes, drybox handling, long gradients, and volatile chloride pressure control (CS-2/SS-2/TS-2). Kinetics are acceptable for crystal growth because CVT is proven for ZrCl₂, but the 4–10 week duration and reported polytypes/intergrowths indicate slow ordering and narrow phase stability (CS-2/SS-2/TS-2). The main uncertainty is whether TiCl₂ survives TiCl₄-rich vapor without disproportionation or overchlorination.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is a chemically natural reduced-halide strategy, supported by Sc + ScCl₃ reactions forming reduced scandium chlorides in sealed Ta tubes (SS-3/TS-5) and by TiCl₃-containing chloride synthesis in Nb ampoules (SS-5/TS-4). Severity is significant because sealed refractory-metal containers, air-free loading, long heating, and volatile halide containment are required (SS-3/SS-5/TS-4/TS-5). Kinetics are likely slower than vapor routes because Ti metal diffusion and TiCl₃ reduction/reconstruction are required, consistent with Sc chloride syntheses taking weeks to months (SS-3/TS-5). CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Ti + TiCl₃ → TiCl₂, so the route remains a reasonable inference rather than a demonstrated analogue.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary with one cation and edge-sharing TiCl₆ octahedra, so the three amorphization checks are not triggered. CS supports this low-risk assignment because TiBr₂ and ZrCl₂ are also two-dimensional edge-sharing halide frameworks (CS-1/CS-2). SS/TS include some complex chlorides with mixed connectivity and large cells, but those are not representative of the target’s simple TiCl₂ topology; for amorphization, their signal is therefore weaker or neutral. The remaining uncertainty is polytype disorder rather than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiCl₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS: TiBr₂ demonstrates that Ti(II) dihalide layered phases can be made by vapor-phase halogen transport, and ZrCl₂ demonstrates that layered MCl₂ chlorides can be made by CVT (CS-1/CS-2). SS and TS reinforce the feasibility of reduced layered chlorides, especially ZrCl₂, but also warn that phase control, polytypes, and long equilibration are common issues (SS-2/TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a controlled, Ti-rich chlorine chemical potential so Ti(II) is stabilized. The most critical limiting factor is volatile chloride chemistry: excess Cl₂/TiCl₄ can drive TiCl₃/TiCl₄ formation, while insufficient transport may leave unreacted Ti or poorly crystalline material.
