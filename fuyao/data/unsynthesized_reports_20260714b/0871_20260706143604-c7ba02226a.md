### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported as exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which supports a high thermodynamic stability score. This is reinforced by multiple similar alkali chalcogenides at E_hull = 0.0, including Rb₂GeS₃ (SS1, TS1), Cs₂GeSe₃ (SS2, TS2), Cs₂Sb₄S₈ (CS3), and Rb₂Ag₂GeS₄ (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the structural description contains an unusually short Cs–Cs distance, so the computational structure should be checked before experimental commitment [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The sealed-tube route is not extreme in temperature because the closest Rb₂GeS₃ analogue is made at 1073 K, about 800 °C, below the >1000 °C penalty threshold (SS1, TS1). However, evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive alkali sulfide precursors impose substantial severity penalties (SS1, TS1; CS3). The uncertainty is whether Cs₂S can be purchased and used directly or must be prepared, as Rb₂S was prepared from elements in liquid ammonia in the analogue (SS1, TS1).

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Ge is less reactive than soluble Ge species, but alkali sulfide and sulfur can promote sulfide transport in a sealed tube [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1]. The GeS₄ tetrahedral motif already exists in the closest analogue, reducing structural reconstruction difficulty (SS1, TS1). The target’s different stoichiometry and 2D sheet topology mean diffusion and phase selection may still require slow cooling and stoichiometric screening [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The solvothermal route operates at mild temperatures, with analogues at 120 °C, 140–150 °C, and 170 °C (TS5, CS1, CS2, SS2/TS2). Severity arises instead from sealed autoclaves/ampoules, toxic H₂S or thiol mineralizers, and multistep preparation/isolation (CS1, SS2/TS2, TS5). If a benign sulfide mineralizer can replace H₂S/thiols, severity would improve, but the provided data do not prove that for the exact Cs–Ge–S target.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
This route has the best kinetic outlook because solution chemistry avoids long-range solid-state diffusion and has precedent for alkali chalcogenogermanates at low temperature (SS2/TS2, TS5). Basic sulfide/amine/thiol media should solubilize Ge/S species and allow GeS₄ network assembly [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS2/TS2, TS5]. The uncertainty is that solvothermal routes may produce competing molecular thiogermanates, solvates, or different dimensionalities [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The flux route benefits from low temperature, since Cs–Sb–S crystals form at 280 °C in Cs₂S flux (CS3). It still requires vacuum sealing, sulfur vapor containment, glovebox handling of Cs₂S, and post-reaction flux removal (CS3). The uncertainty is compatibility of Cs₂S/S flux with Ge source and the silica/Pyrex container [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux growth is kinetically favorable because a molten or highly reactive Cs₂S/S medium can transport chalcogenide species and grow crystals at low temperature (CS3). The method should aid crystallization of a layered sulfide, but the closest flux precedent is Cs–Sb–S rather than Cs–Ge–S, so topology transfer is indirect (CS3; [Reasonable Inference]). Slow cooling is likely essential for obtaining ordered 2D sheets rather than glassy sulfide material [Reasonable Inference from CS3].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains very different cation sizes, Cs and tetrahedral Ge, which increases glass-forming or disorder risk in sulfide networks [Reasonable Inference]. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing GeS₄ tetrahedra, which can complicate crystallization relative to a single-connectivity framework. However, similar alkali chalcogenides with complex coordination crystallize successfully in SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, and CS3, so the risk is medium rather than prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase according to its reported E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related alkali chalcogenides in CS/SS/TS are also on or near the hull. The strongest support comes from Rb₂GeS₃, which shares alkali–Ge–S chemistry and GeS₄ tetrahedra and is experimentally accessible by sealed-tube synthesis (SS1, TS1), plus Cs₂GeSe₃ solvothermal synthesis showing that Cs–Ge–chalcogen frameworks can crystallize at low temperature (SS2, TS2). The most enabling factor is the robust GeS₄ tetrahedral chemistry, repeatedly observed in structurally related compounds (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). The most limiting factor is phase selectivity: Cs–Ge–S systems may form alternative Cs₂GeS₃-, GeS₂-, or polysulfide-rich phases unless sulfur chemical potential and Cs:Ge ratio are tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference]. The anomalous reported Cs–Cs distance and formal charge mismatch should be verified by independent structure relaxation or diffraction comparison before large-scale synthesis [Reasonable Inference].
