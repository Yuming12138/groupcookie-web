### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom, placing it in the high-stability/metastability-accessible range by the provided rubric. The closest NaHgCl₃ analogue is experimentally crystallized even though its E_hull is 0.055 eV/atom (CS1 / SS2 / TS1), which supports the idea that NaHgBr₃ at 0.037 eV/atom should be accessible. Structurally related Hg halides with E_hull ≈ 0–0.011 eV/atom are synthesized by solution or cooling crystallization (SS3 / SS4), and thermodynamic analogues around 0.02–0.03 eV/atom are experimentally accessible (TS2 / TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that the target P-31m polymorph may be metastable relative to another Na–Hg–Br phase even if the composition is accessible **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solution crystallization

The condition severity is low because NaHgCl₃ forms from aqueous HgCl₂/NaCl at room temperature with no special atmosphere (CS1 / SS2 / TS1), and Cs–Hg–I crystals form from solution at room temperature after stirring and volatilization (SS3). The only major F2 deduction is Hg toxicity, inferred from use of Hg halides in CS1 / SS2 / TS1 / SS3 and standard chemical safety knowledge **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics should be favorable because ions are solvated and mobile; the target’s chemical and structural similarity to NaHgCl₃ gives strong support for nucleation of a related layered Na–Hg–halide phase (CS1 / SS2 / TS1). Uncertainty remains over whether bromide stabilizes the trigonal P-31m polymorph or instead yields another mercurate bromide hydrate/solvate **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule melt growth

The route is more severe than solution growth because dry Ar or sealed ampoule conditions are recommended by analogy to NaAlI₄ and AInI₄ melt growth (CS4 / CS5), and HgBr₂ volatility makes sealing prudent **[Reasonable Inference]**. The temperature is still moderate, since related halometallates form from melts near 300 °C or 573 K (CS4 / CS5). Kinetics are good if a molten HgBr₂-rich flux forms, but direct evidence for NaHgBr₃ melt growth is absent; SS provides insufficient melt-specific evidence and is treated neutrally for this route. The main uncertainty is whether melt conditions favor decomposition, volatilization, or a different Na–Hg–Br stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The sealed-tube route is supported by Hg-containing halide syntheses in evacuated ampoules at moderate temperature, including Rb–Hg–Pd bromide/chloride phases at 280 °C (SS1) and CuHgSI at 553 K for 2 weeks (TS4). F2 deductions come from vacuum/sealed operation, volatile HgBr₂, and Hg toxicity, all consistent with sealed Hg halide chemistry in SS1 and TS4 plus physicochemical knowledge **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are less favorable than solution or melt routes because NaBr is a solid ionic salt and long anneals are common in the analogues (SS1 / TS4). The uncertainty is whether vapor-assisted HgBr₂ transport sufficiently overcomes NaBr diffusion limitations **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple composition and apparently small crystallographic description, so no >40-atom unit-cell risk is evident from the target structure; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a large-cell amorphization issue and are neutral for that check. The target contains very different cation coordination environments, NaBr₆ octahedra and 3-coordinate HgBr₃ units, which likely creates size/coordination mismatch and triggers one amorphization-risk criterion **[Reasonable Inference]**. The connectivity described for the target is not a complex coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that such mixed connectivity applies to the target. Overall amorphization risk is low to medium, not a dominant barrier.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaHgBr₃ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom rather than zero, but this is well within the range commonly accessible by crystallization. The support from analogues is strong: NaHgCl₃, the direct chloride analogue, is synthesized at room temperature from aqueous HgCl₂ and NaCl and appears in CS, SS, and TS as the closest recurring reference (CS1 / SS2 / TS1). The most critical enabling factor is solution-phase complexation and crystallization of Hg–halide species, which avoids slow solid-state diffusion and is demonstrated in NaHgCl₃ and Cs–Hg–I systems (CS1 / SS2 / TS1 / SS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible Na–Hg–Br stoichiometries, hydrates, or molecular HgBr₂-rich crystals **[Reasonable Inference]**. Mercury toxicity and HgBr₂ volatility are significant practical constraints for all non-solution or heated routes **[Reasonable Inference]**.
