### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase rather than a highly metastable phase (Target). The closest chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar NbS3 analogue also has E_hull = 0.0 (CS1/SS1/TS1), and several Nb–S-containing analogues are also on the hull (SS2/SS3; TS2/TS3/TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or polymorph selectivity, especially because the closest exact analogue used high pressure (CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1, high-pressure synthesis

The reference NbS3 analogue is synthesized at 700 °C and 2 GPa for 30 min (CS1/SS1/TS1), so temperature is moderate and pressure is below the scoring threshold of >5 GPa. The main severity comes from sulfur volatility and the need for a contained high-pressure assembly [Reasonable Inference; CS1/SS1/TS1 uses S in a cubic-anvil cell]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available on the gas atmosphere inside the high-pressure assembly.

#### F3 — Route 1, high-pressure synthesis

The exact analogue forms quickly at 700 °C under 2 GPa (CS1/SS1/TS1), which strongly supports kinetic accessibility under pressure. However, Nb metal is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], so precursor activity and cation diffusion are not ideal. Structural reconstruction is scored high because the analogue is essentially the same binary NbS3 framework (CS1/SS1/TS1; Target).

#### F2 — Route 2, sealed-tube solid-state route

Related Nb–S phases are synthesized in evacuated silica/quartz tubes at 850 °C or 1000–1075 K, often with grinding or long heating (SS2/SS3; TS2/TS3). The route avoids high pressure but requires vacuum sealing and control of sulfur vapor pressure, which is a significant hazard and stoichiometry issue [Reasonable Inference from S volatility; SS/TS sealed-tube data]. It is less severe in equipment than Route 1 but more prolonged.

#### F3 — Route 2, sealed-tube solid-state route

Solid-state diffusion is likely slow because Nb is refractory [Reasonable Inference], consistent with related analogues requiring 10–60 days or repeated heating/grinding (SS2/TS2/TS5). The lack of a direct ambient-pressure NbS3 reference lowers confidence, because the most direct NbS3 analogue is high-pressure (CS1/SS1/TS1). The route may be feasible for bulk material but carries higher risk of NbS2, elemental S, or other Nb sulfide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3, CVT route

CVT analogues use evacuated quartz ampoules, temperature gradients, and transport agents such as I2 or (NH4)2PbCl6 (CS4/CS5). Severity is increased by vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/iodine, corrosive or toxic transport agents, and multi-step setup (CS4/CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS confirms CVT for the exact binary NbS3 target.

#### F3 — Route 3, CVT route

CVT improves mass transport by forming volatile species, and layered Nb sulfide crystals are successfully grown this way (CS4/CS5). This compensates partly for poor Nb solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is phase selection: NbS2 is directly supported by CS4/CS5, while this exact NbS3 polymorph is not.

#### F2 — Route 4, flux/mineralizer route

Flux analogues use evacuated quartz tubes at 500–700 °C for about a week with slow cooling (SS4/TS4), so temperature severity is modest. Vacuum sealing and sulfur volatility still impose penalties [Reasonable Inference; SS4/TS4 evacuated tubes]. Toxic TlCl is used in the reference flux chemistry (SS4/TS4), but a target route can reasonably use alkali chloride flux instead [Reasonable Inference], so toxic precursor penalty is not applied.

#### F3 — Route 4, flux/mineralizer route

Flux growth can lower kinetic barriers by enhancing dissolution and transport [Reasonable Inference], and related Nb–S frameworks crystallize from halide fluxes (SS4/TS4). However, the reference contains P/Tl/alkali components and is structurally related rather than chemically identical (SS4/TS4). The uncertainty is whether a binary NbS3 phase will crystallize rather than flux-stabilized ternary or chloride-containing byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Nb as the cation, so the cation-size-mismatch amorphization criterion is not triggered (Target). The target description shows ordered inequivalent Nb and S sites but does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a large-cell amorphization penalty. The target has edge/corner-sharing motifs but no reported simultaneous face-sharing network, giving a low amorphization risk (Target; CS1/SS1/TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target), and the closest NbS3 analogue is also on the hull (CS1/SS1/TS1). The CS/SS/TS support is unusually strong for the high-pressure route because the top chemical, structural, and thermodynamic match is essentially NbS3 synthesized at 700 °C and 2 GPa (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the direct precedent for forming a very similar monoclinic 2D NbS3 framework under experimentally specified conditions (CS1/SS1/TS1). The main limiting factor is that the best-supported route requires specialized high-pressure equipment, while ambient-pressure routes are only indirectly supported by related Nb sulfides and may favor competing NbS2 or other Nb–S phases (CS4/CS5; SS2/SS3; TS5). Overall, synthesis is feasible but route-sensitive, with high-pressure synthesis being the most reliable path.
