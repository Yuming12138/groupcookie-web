### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.014 eV/atom, which lies well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range, so the rubric supports a 95/100 score [target]. The closest Re–Se–Cl analogue is nearly stable at 0.0016 eV/atom [SS1/TS1], and several thermodynamic comparators are also near hull at ~0.017–0.029 eV/atom [TS2, TS3, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or a wide phase field for the exact Br stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C operation if patterned after the 900 °C Re–Se–Cl analogue [SS1/TS1]. However, it requires vacuum-sealed fused silica, dry inert grinding, volatile Se/Br-containing species, and multiple thermal/cooling steps [SS1/TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether ReBr5/ReBr3 gives the right Br activity without decomposing or forming binary Re bromides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Re powder and solid-state diffusion, even though SS1/TS1 demonstrates that a closely related Re–Se–halide framework can form at 900 °C [SS1/TS1]. The structural reconstruction score is helped by the near-isostructural Re3Tl(Se2Cl)2 sheet motif [SS1/TS1], but lowered because SS1/TS1 reports low yields and multiple products. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Re–Br solid-state kinetics, so CS is treated as neutral for this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT is more severe thermally because Re selenide transport uses ~1080→1000 °C for selenides [SS2]. It also requires evacuated sealed tubes and toxic volatile bromine transport chemistry [SS2, CS1]. The process is nevertheless standard for chalcogenide crystal growth and does not require high pressure [SS2, CS1].

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by forming volatile Re/Se/Br-containing transport species rather than depending entirely on solid-state Re diffusion [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. ReSe2 is directly reported to grow by Br/I CVT, and the target already contains Br, making the pathway chemically well matched [SS2; target]. The key uncertainty is composition control: too much Br may over-transport or stabilize other Re bromoselenides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth can run at lower peak temperatures, as CS3 used 800→400 °C slow cooling for selenide crystals [CS3]. It still requires sealed evacuated tubes because Se and Br species are volatile, and flux removal/slow cooling adds process complexity [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from TS for halide-flux synthesis of Re3Se4Br, so TS is neutral for this route’s condition estimate.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to dry powders, consistent with CS3 isolating selenide single crystals by low-temperature flux [CS3]. However, the analogues are not Re–Se–Br, and alkali/halide fluxes may introduce competing alkali rhenium bromoselenides [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore kinetics are rated moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, Re, so cation size mismatch is not a concern [target]. Its formula is small, and no provided source indicates a very large target unit cell, but it does contain mixed edge- and corner-sharing square pyramids, which increases structural frustration/amorphization risk slightly [target]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on amorphous formation tendencies, so those sources are neutral for F4 beyond structural comparison.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re3Se4Br is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.014 eV/atom [target]. The strongest support comes from the structurally and thermodynamically close Re3Tl(Se2Cl)2 analogue, which shares triclinic P-1 layered Re–Se–halide square-pyramidal chemistry [SS1/TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/mineralization, because ReSe2 is explicitly grown by Br/I CVT and the target itself incorporates Br [SS2; target]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the closest Re–Se–Cl analogue required controlled ampoule conditions and had difficult phase control/low yields [SS1/TS1]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires careful Br and Se chemical-potential tuning rather than a simple one-shot ceramic reaction [SS1/TS1, SS2, CS1; Reasonable Inference].
