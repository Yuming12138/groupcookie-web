### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high end of the most favorable scoring bin. CS contains several E_hull = 0 synthesized analogues (CS1–CS4), while SS and TS include experimentally synthesized phases with E_hull values of roughly 0.03–0.13 eV/atom (SS2–SS5, TS1–TS5). This supports likely metastable-but-accessible behavior rather than severe instability. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact Sr–La–Ir–Te–O competing phase landscape is not provided.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits moderate severity from SS2–SS4, which use 1000–1050 °C, vacuum, quartz tubes, and 24–48 h reactions. For the target, a ≤1000 °C sealed/inert approach is preferred to limit Te loss [Reasonable Inference], but controlled atmosphere and sealed-tube handling still penalize the route. Insufficient evidence from CS for Ir redox control, so CS is neutral for that sub-factor. The route is feasible but operationally less forgiving than simple air firing.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are weak because SrCO₃/La₂O₃/IrO₂/TeO₂ require long-range diffusion and full reconstruction into a P1 mixed-polyhedral structure [Reasonable Inference]. SS2–SS4 demonstrate that related low-symmetry La–Re/Os oxides can form by solid-state reaction, but only after high-temperature sealed-tube treatment. TS1 similarly shows staged firing and inert atmosphere for Re double perovskites. The main uncertainty is whether Ir/Te ordering occurs before competing binary or ternary oxides form.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C because CS2/CS3 achieve related ordered oxides at 850–900 °C. It still requires multi-step resin preparation and controlled final atmosphere, directly supported by CS2/CS3. Te volatility and Ir/Te toxicity are [Reasonable Inference], because no direct Ir–Te soft-chemistry source is given in CS/SS/TS. Overall condition severity is moderate.

#### F3 — Route 2

CS2/CS3 provide strong evidence that citrate/nitrate processing improves cation homogeneity and precursor reactivity. This is especially valuable for SrLaIrTeO₆ because the target contains four cations and low-symmetry coordination environments. The route still requires crystallization from an amorphous/oxide precursor into a complex ordered phase, so structural reconstruction is not trivial. Insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS for citrate synthesis of Ir–Te oxides, so that uncertainty remains.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux synthesis can operate below 900 °C by inference from CS1 and TS2/TS5, but sealed ampoules, washing, and controlled atmosphere add complexity. CS1 specifically uses fluxing agents and sealed silica ampoules; TS2 uses sealed molybdenum/protection tubes and post-washing. For the target, flux compatibility is uncertain because chloride or molybdate fluxes could introduce impurity phases [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the route is not harsher thermally but is chemically more complicated.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth, as supported by CS1 and TS2/TS5. TS5 is particularly relevant because it shows that fused-salt/electrochemical methods can access phases not obtained by direct solid-state synthesis. However, no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates Sr–La–Ir–Te oxide flux growth, so transferability is uncertain. Kinetics are better than ceramic solid state but somewhat less predictable than citrate soft chemistry.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Sr/La and Ir/Te, and the target description shows multiple coordination environments and corner/edge-sharing oxygen polyhedra. Similar low-symmetry, mixed-connectivity oxides appear in SS2–SS4 and TS2, indicating crystallization is possible but not necessarily easy. The primitive formula has only 10 atoms, and no >40-atom unit cell is stated, so C2 is not triggered. Overall amorphization/poor-crystallinity risk is medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrLaIrTeO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The analogue set supports synthesis reasonably well: CS4 supports Te-containing ordered oxides, CS2/CS3 support soft-chemistry formation of multication perovskite-related oxides, and SS2–SS4/TS1 support low-symmetry complex oxides under sealed or inert conditions. The most important enabling factor is homogeneous cation mixing, best addressed by citrate/nitrate soft chemistry. The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of Te volatility and Ir/Te oxidation state. Overall, the target is experimentally plausible but likely requires atmosphere and stoichiometry optimization.
