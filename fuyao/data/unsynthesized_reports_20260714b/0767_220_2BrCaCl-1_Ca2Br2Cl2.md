### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0050 eV/atom places it very close to the convex hull, so it is likely thermodynamically accessible or only weakly metastable. SS and TS provide strong support because SrBr₂ and Sr₅(Br₄Cl)₂ analogues have very low hull energies around 0.001–0.006 eV/atom (SS-1; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). CS also supports mixed alkaline-earth halide feasibility through BaBrCl at 0.048 eV/atom, although LiNBr at high hull energy is chemically less transferable and gives contradictory signal (CS-1; CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: the computed trigonal omega-derived CaClBr may compete with other Ca–Cl–Br arrangements.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because analogous BaBrCl and Sr mixed halides require high-temperature treatment, inert/vacuum handling, and controlled slow cooling (CS-4; SS-3; TS-2). The target likely needs sealed or very dry handling because CaCl₂/CaBr₂ are hygroscopic and molten halides can lose halogen in open systems [Reasonable Inference]. No source suggests high pressure, and temperatures should remain below 1000 °C by analogy to BaBrCl/Sr halide synthesis and lower Ca-halide melting temperatures (CS-4; SS-3; TS-2; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable compared with oxide ceramics because the binary halides can melt or soften near the synthesis temperature, improving diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. BaBrCl and Sr₅(Br₄Cl)₂ were formed by high-temperature mixing and slow cooling of binary halides, showing that alkaline-earth halide sublattices can order on laboratory timescales (CS-4; SS-3; TS-2). The main kinetic uncertainty is reconstructing into the trigonal 2D omega-derived layer rather than the cotunnite/tetragonal structures seen in Ba/Sr analogues (CS-4; SS-3; SS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still requires sealed ampoules, dry atmosphere, and multi-step loading/heating/cooling. Flux growth and evacuated ampoules are well supported by CS-5 and TS-4, while Ta/quartz assemblies are supported by SS-2 and SS-5. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which keeps the severity moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux/self-flux growth should improve kinetics by providing a liquid medium and better ion mobility; this inference is supported by CsCl-assisted single-crystal growth in CS-5 and Bridgman growth of BaZnCl₄ in TS-4. Slow cooling gives time for anion ordering, consistent with BaBrCl and Sr–Br–Cl synthesis protocols (CS-4; SS-3; TS-2). The uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the desired trigonal CaClBr phase or instead promotes known binary halides or different mixed-halide polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has low thermal severity because SrBr₂ crystallization from alcohol occurs at mild temperature under dry air (SS-1; TS-1). It is still penalized for multi-step solvent drying, crystallization, isolation, and possible desolvation [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for solution growth of mixed CaClBr, so CS is neutral, score 50, for this route’s condition precedent.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution chemistry gives excellent ionic mobility, and SrBr₂ alcohol crystallization demonstrates that alkaline-earth halides can crystallize from anhydrous alcohol (SS-1; TS-1). However, the same SrBr₂ precedent reports multiple modifications, hydration tendency, and unstable small crystals, which strongly reduces confidence in direct formation of anhydrous trigonal CaClBr (SS-1; TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS for this route; CS is neutral, score 50, for kinetics of solution-grown Ca mixed halides.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ca²⁺, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description indicates edge-sharing CaBr₃Cl₃ octahedra in a simple 2D sheet, with no evidence of a very large unit cell or mixed edge/vertex/face-sharing coexistence. CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly crystalline halides and oxyhalides made by conventional methods, supporting low amorphization tendency, but they do not directly prove the target’s glass-forming tendency; where direct evidence is lacking, the source signal is treated as neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaClBr is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its computed E_hull is only 0.005 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar Sr/Ba alkaline-earth halides that form under dry sealed high-temperature conditions with slow cooling (SS-3; SS-4; TS-2; CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the use of molten/fluxed halide chemistry, which should provide fast ion transport and allow Cl/Br ordering during slow cooling, as seen for BaBrCl and Sr mixed halides (CS-4; SS-3; TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selection: the target trigonal omega-derived 2D structure differs from the cotunnite/tetragonal structures of the closest mixed-halide analogues, so competing CaCl₂/CaBr₂ or alternative CaClBr polymorphs may form. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with best practical odds via sealed-ampoule self-flux or carefully cooled melt synthesis.
