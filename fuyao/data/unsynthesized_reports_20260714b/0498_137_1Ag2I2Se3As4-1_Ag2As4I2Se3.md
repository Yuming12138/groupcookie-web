### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0280 eV/atom, which lies in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score. This is comparable to the closest Cu2As4S3Cl2 analogue with E_hull = 0.0286 eV/atom (CS1, TS3), and to other thermodynamically similar phases near 0.018–0.028 eV/atom (TS1, TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy supports synthesizability but does not identify the kinetic route or rule out competing Ag–Se–I/As–Se phases.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Solution diffusion

The condition severity is moderate because the route is low temperature and ambient pressure, but requires N2/Schlenk handling, preformed As–chalcogen organometallic adducts, and layered crystallization (CS1, TS3). Kinetics are favorable because soluble precursors and diffusion crystallization avoid bulk diffusion, and the closest analogue has nearly identical 2D As4Q3–metal halide sheet chemistry (CS1, TS3). The key uncertainty is whether As4Se3 adducts are as accessible/soluble as As4S3 adducts and whether AgI can be introduced without immediate precipitation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Hydrothermal HI

The hydrothermal route has moderate severity: 220 °C is mild, but concentrated HI in sealed tubes/autoclaves creates pressure and corrosion hazards (SS2, SS4, TS5). Kinetics are favorable for Ag–Se–I because AgSe3I repeatedly forms as a major hydrothermal product with Ag–Se and Ag–I distances close to the target (SS2, TS5). The limitation is structural: SS2/TS5 contain Ag–Se–I but no As, so As–Se sheet assembly is inferred rather than directly demonstrated; insufficient evidence from CS for this route.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Solid-state ampoule

The sealed-ampoule route is more severe due to vacuum sealing, volatile Se/I/As species, and long annealing/grinding cycles, although the recommended temperatures are not extreme (CS2, SS5, TS1, TS2). Kinetically, solid-state diffusion is slower than solution/hydrothermal transport, but Ag/As/Se/I are not refractory and sealed ampoule syntheses are common for related selenides and chalcohalides (CS2, CS3, SS5). The main uncertainty is whether direct heating gives the desired 2D Ag2As4Se3I2 sheet rather than binary AgI, Ag2Se, or As2Se3 **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: CVT

CVT has moderate-to-high severity because evacuated sealed ampoules, halide vapor species, and temperature gradients must be controlled (TS4). Kinetically, vapor transport can be excellent and is supported by layered Bi–Te–Br chalcohalide CVT and iodine-assisted selenide transport (TS4, CS3). However, no CS/SS/TS example directly shows Ag–As–Se–I transport growth, so phase selectivity and transport chemistry remain uncertain; insufficient evidence from SS for this route.

#### F2/F3 Route 5: Flux

Flux growth has significant handling complexity because SS1 uses preformed polychalcogenide flux, evacuated tubes, slow thermal programs, and flux removal. Kinetics are favorable because molten flux reduces diffusion limitations and can grow Ag chalcogenide crystals at 450–500 °C (SS1). Phase control is the weakness: SS1 reports low yields and precise flux dependence, and there is insufficient evidence from CS that the As–Se–I sheet will crystallize from flux rather than competing phases.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically distinct Ag+ coordination and covalent As–Se units, giving a meaningful cation size/chemistry mismatch and one amorphization trigger **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target description does not indicate a very large unit cell or explicit coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those triggers, so they are treated as not triggered/neutral. The 2D layered nature may support crystallization if grown slowly, but it may also promote stacking disorder **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2As4Se3I2 is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the chemically and thermodynamically similar Cu2As4S3Cl2, which has the same type of 2D As4Q3–metal halide sheet and nearly identical hull energy (CS1, TS3). Structurally similar AgSe3I provides strong local Ag–Se–I bonding evidence, especially for Ag–I distances near 2.87 Å (SS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature transport/crystallization that preserves As–Se cluster motifs while allowing Ag–I coordination to assemble. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among AgI/Ag–Se phases and arsenic selenides, especially if AgI precipitates or volatile iodine/selenium activity is not controlled.
