### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the phase is predicted to be a ground-state compound. Similar stable or near-stable layered halides include ZrCl₂ with E_hull = 0 in SS/TS and ZrI₂ near the hull in TS (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). CS also includes stable bromide lower halides such as Pr₂Br₅ and La₂Br₅/LaBr₂, but those are rare-earth systems and less chemically direct (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or the exact monoclinic polytype.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: HfBr₄/Hf CVT

Condition severity is significant because close analogues require sealed Ta tubes, drybox handling, volatile halide transport, and long gradient anneals (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). The temperature is not extreme relative to refractory ceramic synthesis, but the sealed halide chemistry and air sensitivity reduce F2. Kinetics are favorable compared with ordinary Hf solid-state diffusion because volatile halide transport can shuttle material, as shown for ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ (TS-5, SS-1, TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether HfBr₂ has the same vapor-transport window as ZrI₂; this is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly demonstrated.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct Vapor-Phase Hf + Br₂

This route benefits from a simpler apparatus and shorter high-temperature exposure than Route 1, following TiBr₂ from Ti + Br₂ at 900 °C in Ar (SS-3). F2 is penalized for toxic volatile Br₂, multi-step handling, and moisture-sensitive bromide products (SS-3). Kinetically, Br₂ vapor is highly reactive, but Hf metal is refractory and over-bromination to volatile HfBr₄ is likely by [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is larger than for Route 1 because the structural analogue TiBr₂ is not monoclinic P2₁/m molybdenite-like.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Metallothermic Reduction

Metallothermic reduction is supported for lower bromides/chlorides by PrBr₃ + Li and rare-earth trichloride + Li/Na routes (CS-1, SS-5). The conditions are comparable to Route 1 in atmosphere and sealed-tube demands but involve byproduct salts and more complicated redox control. Kinetics may be helped by reactive alkali metals and possible molten halide phases, but structural reconstruction into the exact HfBr₂ layered phase is not directly evidenced. TS provides insufficient evidence for HfBr₂ metallothermic reduction specifically and is treated as neutral for that route-specific claim.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

HfBr₂ is a simple single-cation layered binary halide, so there is no cation radius mismatch trigger. The target description indicates a simple layered sheet with one Hf site and two Br sites rather than a large complex framework. Structurally similar ZrCl₂, LaBr₂, and ZrI₂ are crystalline layered materials made by high-temperature transport routes, supporting low amorphization risk (SS-1, SS-2, TS-5). The uncertainty is mainly polytypism/intergrowth rather than true amorphization, as seen in ZrCl₂ (SS-1, TS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfBr₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from group-IV layered dihalide analogues, especially monoclinic molybdenite-like ZrI₂ made by ZrI₄ + Zr CVT and ZrCl₂ made by sealed-tube halide transport (TS-5, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is volatile halide transport, which can bypass slow diffusion of refractory Hf metal and promote layered crystal growth. The most critical limiting factor is bromine chemical potential control: too oxidizing/Br-rich conditions may favor HfBr₄ or higher bromides, while too reducing conditions may leave Hf metal or subhalides by [Reasonable Inference], consistent with phase-control issues in ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ analogues (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). Overall, synthesis is feasible but requires expert sealed-tube halide handling.
