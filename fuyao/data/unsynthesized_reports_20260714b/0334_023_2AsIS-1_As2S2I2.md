### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives 98/100. CS supports this because several close chemical analogues are also on the hull, including SnICl, GaTeCl, and Ga₂TeS₂ (CS1, CS3, CS5). TS also supports high stability because the closest thermodynamic analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 phases such as K₃BiAs₆Se₁₂, TeI, TeITe, TlSbS₂, and CuBrSe₃ (TS1–TS5). SS is somewhat mixed because several structural analogues are slightly metastable, e.g., Sn₂SbS₂I₃ at 0.0889 eV/atom and TeI at 0.0066 eV/atom (SS1, SS5), but this does not contradict the target’s predicted ground-state status.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because hydrothermal TeI analogues require 195–280 °C, 7–10 days, acidic HI, and autoclaves, but not high pressure above 5 GPa or >1000 °C temperatures (SS5, TS2, TS3). The main severity penalties are volatile iodine/HI, toxic arsenic precursors, and multi-step sealed-vessel operation. Kinetically, hydrothermal solution transport should help form low-dimensional iodide ribbons, as demonstrated for TeI/subiodides (SS5, TS2, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that CS provides insufficient direct evidence for As–S–I hydrothermal formation, and As₂S₃ may dissolve slowly.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 solution precipitation

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because CS1 demonstrates a closely related 1D edge-sharing iodide/halide ribbon can form at 293 K by HI addition in aqueous solution. Kinetics are favorable because solution chemistry avoids long-range solid-state diffusion, and CS2 supports slow crystallization/evaporation for halogen-rich compounds. The key uncertainty is chemical transferability: SnCl₂/HI precipitation in CS1 is not the same as As₂S₃/AsI₃/HI chemistry, and SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for ambient As–S–I precipitation, so those sources are neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 sealed ampoule

Route 3 is more severe because evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile iodide species, toxic arsenic compounds, and multi-step heating/cooling are needed, analogous to CS3, CS5, and TS4. The temperature can likely remain moderate, around 250–400 °C, by analogy to GaTeCl at 400 °C (CS3) and CVT/iodide analogues at 290–430 °C (SS1, TS5). Kinetics are acceptable if As₂S₃ and AsI₃ are used instead of dense elements, but complete reconstruction into the 1D ribbon may require long annealing. SS gives insufficient direct evidence for this exact direct ampoule route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 CVT

Route 4 has challenging condition severity due to vacuum sealing, iodine transport, toxic arsenic species, and a pre-reaction or feed preparation step. Kinetics are strong because SS1 shows iodine CVT can grow low-dimensional pnictogen/chalcogenide iodide crystals, and TS5 shows similar chemical transport at only 340–290 °C. The structural transferability is good but not perfect, since the closest SS1 analogue contains Sn/Sb/S/I rather than only As/S/I. CS provides insufficient direct CVT evidence and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally simple: it contains one cationic framework element, As, one S site, and one I site, so cation-radius mismatch and large-cell complexity are not triggered. The structure is described as edge-sharing AsS₃I₂ square pyramids, with no reported coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization evidence, so they are neutral for this specific factor, but the low-dimensional crystalline analogues in SS5/TS2/TS3 suggest ribbon crystallization is feasible. Remaining uncertainty is possible glass formation in arsenic chalcogenide chemistry, especially if quenched rather than slowly crystallized.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically or thermodynamically related chalcogenide/iodide materials are also on or near the hull (CS1, CS3, CS5, TS1–TS5). The analogues support synthesis best through low-temperature iodide solution chemistry and hydrothermal HI chemistry: CS1 is the closest motif for room-temperature HI precipitation, while SS5/TS2/TS3 strongly support HI-mediated growth of 1D iodide ribbons. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of soft, polarizable As–S/I bonding and mobile iodide species, which should allow crystallization at relatively low temperature [Reasonable Inference] and is consistent with CS1, SS5, TS2, and TS3. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control, especially loss or excess of iodine and hydrolysis/volatility of AsI₃; CS1 explicitly reports phase sensitivity to HI quantity, and SS5/TS2/TS3 note narrow conditions for iodide phases. Overall, synthesis is quite plausible, with Route 2 as the fastest screening route and Route 1 or Route 4 as better crystal-growth backups.
