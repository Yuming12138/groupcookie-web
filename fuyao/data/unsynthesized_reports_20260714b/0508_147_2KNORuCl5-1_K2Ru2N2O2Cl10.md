### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. Related stable halide/oxychloride materials appear in CS and TS, including CS1–CS3 at E_hull = 0 and TS2–TS4 at or near hull stability [CS1–CS3, TS2–TS4]. SS1/TS1 is also very close to hull, indicating that pentachloro oxo/hydrate frameworks can be thermodynamically accessible [SS1/TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee that the nitrosyl oxidation state/speciation is the easiest experimental product; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ru–NO redox selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because SS1/TS1 and SS4 use dissolution in HCl and evaporation without high temperature, high pressure, or controlled atmosphere. CS5/SS2 specifically supports K–Ru–Cl–O crystallization from 50% HCl on a water bath. The only major severity penalty is chemical hazard from concentrated HCl and nitrosylating species, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because nitrosyl reagents are not specified in CS/SS/TS. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether an inert atmosphere is needed; it is scored as not required because the closest acid-evaporation analogues do not report one [SS1/TS1, SS4, CS5/SS2].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization should have favorable kinetics because related Rh/Fe/Ru chloride salts crystallize directly from acid solution [SS1/TS1, SS4, CS5/SS2]. Diffusion limitations are minimal relative to solid-state synthesis because ions are solvated before nucleation [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS1 and SS4 procedures]. Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-favorable because the Ru–Cl octahedral motif and K–Cl coordination are close to CS5/SS2, but the target’s linear Ru–N–O unit is not demonstrated in the analogues. Therefore, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for nitrosyl ligand installation limits the F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits sealed-ampoule and volatile reagent penalties from SS5, where SOCl₂ was used in an evacuated ampoule at 150 °C. It is not severe thermally because SS5 operates at 150 °C, far below 1000 °C. However, volatile SOCl₂/NOCl and toxic nitrosylating conditions make it more hazardous than Route 1 [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. TS5 shows that sealed halogen chemistry can become demanding when high chlorine pressure is needed, although Route 2 is not assumed to require TS5-level 525 °C/6–8 atm conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

SS5 shows that a sealed SOCl₂ medium can promote chloride/oxychloride transformations efficiently at 150 °C. This supports high precursor activity and low diffusion barriers for chloride-rich solvothermal chemistry [SS5]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether KRuNOCl5 nucleates instead of molecular Ru nitrosyl chlorides, oxo species, or alkali chloride byproducts; insufficient evidence from CS/TS for this exact pathway. Thus F3 is high but below Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses sealed-tube/flux procedures similar to CS1–CS3, which require vacuum sealing, long holds, slow cooling, and flux removal. Although proposed temperatures are below 1000 °C, the sealed volatile nitrosyl/chloride environment adds hazard [CS1–CS3; Reasonable Inference]. TS5 demonstrates that high-temperature metal chloride synthesis can require pressure control to prevent dissociation, warning against overly hot conditions [TS5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports using molten chloride flux specifically for KRuNOCl5, so severity remains challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux transport can improve ion mobility, as CS1–CS3 demonstrate for K-containing solids grown at 480–650 °C with slow cooling. However, the target contains a nitrosyl ligand likely sensitive to high temperature, and none of CS1–CS3 contains NO or chloride nitrosyl chemistry. Structural reconstruction from a molten chloride mixture into a 2D nitrosyl chlororuthenate sheet is therefore uncertain. The route is kinetically plausible only if a preformed nitrosyl ruthenate survives the flux, which is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes and coordination environments: large 11-coordinate K and octahedral Ru, so one amorphization trigger is assigned from the target structure. The formula unit is small, and no evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40-atom target cell, so the atom-count trigger is not assigned. The target is a 2D sheet, but coexistence of vertex, edge, and face-sharing polyhedra is not explicitly established; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that trigger. The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KRuNOCl5 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stabilized phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related halometalates are on or near the hull [CS1–CS3, SS1/TS1, TS2–TS4]. The best experimental support comes from acid slow-evaporation analogues: K–Ru–Cl–O crystallizes from 50% HCl [CS5/SS2], and structurally/thermodynamically similar pentachloro Rh/Fe salts crystallize from HCl by evaporation [SS1/TS1, SS4]. The key enabling factor is high chloride activity in solution, which stabilizes pentachlorometalate coordination and enables slow crystal growth [CS5/SS2, SS1/TS1]. The key limiting factor is nitrosyl speciation: CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for forming or preserving the Ru–NO unit, so redox and NO+/NOCl control will determine success. Overall, synthesis should be feasible, with Route 1 strongly preferred over sealed high-temperature routes.
