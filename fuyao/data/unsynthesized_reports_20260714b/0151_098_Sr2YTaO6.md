### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the highest-stability scoring threshold, so F1 = 90. Exact or very close Sr₂YTaO₆ entries are reported with E_hull = 0.0173 eV/atom in CS-2/SS-1 and 0.0 eV/atom in CS-1/SS-2, supporting strong thermodynamic accessibility, although CS-1/SS-2 has a Nb/Ta inconsistency in its synthesis snippet. TS analogues have E_hull values around 0.05–0.08 eV/atom and are still synthesized by ceramic routes (TS-1 to TS-5), supporting that phases in this metastability range can be accessible. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific C2/m polymorph is the equilibrium low-temperature form or a metastable distortion relative to P2₁/c/I4/m Sr₂YTaO₆ variants.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 1400 °C for 3 days with intermittent regrinding, directly reported for Sr₂YTaO₆ (CS-2; SS-1), giving a high-temperature and multi-step penalty. No high pressure, inert/vacuum atmosphere, or specialized equipment is reported in CS-2/SS-1, so no additional severity deductions are applied. The main uncertainty is whether the target C2/m polymorph needs a modified cooling profile or anneal, which is not specified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate-low because CS-2/SS-1 requires long firing and intermittent regrinding, indicating sluggish solid-state diffusion and ordering. YTaO₄ provides a chemically preorganized Y–Ta oxide precursor [Reasonable Inference], making it kinetically better than separate Y₂O₃ + Ta₂O₅. The uncertainty is whether the target C2/m form requires lower-temperature ordering, oxygen stoichiometry control, or quenching, none of which is addressed directly in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by Sr₂HoTaO₆ and related A₂HoTaO₆ ceramic syntheses in air at 1280–1330 °C with calcination/sintering steps (SS-3; SS-4). It receives deductions for T > 1000 °C and multi-step ceramic processing, but no deductions for high pressure or controlled atmosphere because SS-3/SS-4 use air and standard equipment. Uncertainty comes from extrapolating Ho³⁺ to Y³⁺ and from the more refractory separate Y₂O₃/Ta₂O₅ precursors.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetics are less favorable than Route 1 because Y₂O₃ and Ta₂O₅ are separate refractory oxides requiring complete reconstruction into Sr₂YTaO₆ [Reasonable Inference]. SS-3/SS-4 show the precursor family can work for Sr₂HoTaO₆, but CS-2/SS-1’s use of YTaO₄ suggests pre-reacted B-site precursors may be preferred for Sr₂YTaO₆. Remaining uncertainty is how much longer firing or regrinding is required to achieve phase-pure and B-site-ordered Sr₂YTaO₆.

#### F2 — Route 3

The Pechini route is multi-step and in the closest Sr₂LuNbO₆ analogue requires final firing at 1773 K for 60 h (CS-3; SS-5), so high-temperature and multi-step deductions apply. If TaCl₅ or Ta alkoxide is used, moisture sensitivity/corrosivity adds a precursor-handling penalty [Reasonable Inference based on NbCl₅ use in CS-3/SS-5]. No high pressure or special atmosphere is reported in CS-3/SS-5.

#### F3 — Route 3

Pechini chemistry gives high precursor activity and atomic-scale mixing, supported by CS-3/SS-5 using nitric solution, citric acid, and ethylene glycol. However, CS-3/SS-5 still requires 1773 K for 60 h, showing that crystallization and ordering of Sr₂LnB⁵⁺O₆ double perovskites remain kinetically demanding. The main uncertainty is Ta solution chemistry, because the provided Pechini analogue uses NbCl₅ rather than a Ta precursor.

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux growth is based on KLn₂NbO₆ C2/m phases grown in sealed silver tubes with KOH/KF flux at 500–600 °C (CS-4; CS-5), but direct Sr₂YTaO₆ evidence is lacking. The route is penalized for sealed volatile/corrosive flux handling and multi-step crystal isolation. It may avoid extreme firing temperatures, but if higher-temperature chloride/borate flux is needed for Sr–Y–Ta oxides, severity increases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux can improve diffusion by liquid-phase transport, as demonstrated by low-temperature KLn₂NbO₆ crystal growth (CS-4; CS-5). However, CS-4/CS-5 also report narrow phase windows, decomposition sensitivity, and unsuccessful alternative synthesis methods, implying phase selection can be difficult. Since Sr₂YTaO₆ is chemically different from KLn₂NbO₆, the structural reconstruction score is limited and the route remains uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization trigger is counted: large cation size/coordination contrast between Sr²⁺ and Y³⁺/Ta⁵⁺ [Reasonable Inference from target structure and double-perovskite chemistry]. The target octahedral framework is corner-sharing only, matching the relatively simple connectivity described for CS-2/SS-1, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a unit cell above 40 atoms for the specific C2/m target, so C2 is treated as not triggered/neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂YTaO₆ is likely synthesizable, but the specific C2/m target is best viewed as a near-ground-state or mildly metastable polymorph because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and other Sr₂YTaO₆ polymorphs appear at 0–0.017 eV/atom in CS-1/SS-2 and CS-2/SS-1. The CS and SS evidence is very strong for forming the Sr₂YTaO₆ composition by high-temperature solid-state synthesis, especially the exact SrCO₃ + YTaO₄ route in CS-2/SS-1. TS evidence is more general: it shows oxides with similar thermodynamic metastability can be synthesized, but it does not directly establish Sr₂YTaO₆ chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a direct exact-composition solid-state synthesis precedent. The main limiting factor is polymorph/order control, since the target C2/m symmetry differs from the reported I4/m and P2₁/c-like Sr₂YTaO₆ variants.
