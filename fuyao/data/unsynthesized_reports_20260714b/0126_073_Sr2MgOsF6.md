### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring bin by the provided rubric. CS and SS analogues are mostly stable or near-stable, including CS4, SS1, SS2, SS4, and SS5 with E_hull = 0, supporting feasibility of related fluoride frameworks. TS analogues show that synthesized materials can occur at higher metastability, such as TS1 at 0.091 eV/atom and TS4 at 0.096 eV/atom, so the target’s 0.050 eV/atom is plausible. Remaining uncertainty comes from the unusual Os coordination and formal redox balance, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The route uses moderate temperatures comparable to CS5/SS2/SS3/TS4 at 550–600 °C and CS3 at 600–750 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Deductions are applied for sealed volatile fluoride chemistry and toxic/air-sensitive Os fluoride handling by [Reasonable Inference], supported indirectly by CS3, TS1, and TS2 requiring dry boxes/sealed tubes for sensitive fluorides. A multi-step penalty is applied because CS3 required two-step reaction and several annealings, and target optimization would likely require regrinding/reannealing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Os fluoride sealed-tube behavior.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Binary fluoride precursors are supported by CS3–CS5 and SS2–SS4, but MgF₂/SrF₂ are refractory and likely diffuse slowly by [Reasonable Inference]. TS1 reports phase-control difficulties for AVF₃ fluorides, including oxidation/disproportionation and impurities, suggesting that pure-phase fluoride solid-state synthesis can be kinetically difficult. CS3 also reports traces of starting/intermediate phases and need for several annealings. Therefore kinetics are scored low–moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2: HF hydrothermal

CS1/CS2 and TS5 show that fluoride crystals can be made at 180–350 °C in sealed HF-containing vessels, so temperature severity is low. Deductions are applied for sealed volatile/corrosive media and toxic HF/Os chemistry; CS1/CS2 used sealed Pt tubes and TS5 used sealed autoclaves. No GPa high-pressure penalty applies because hydrothermal autogenous pressures are far below 5 GPa even when CS2 reports 750–1350 bar. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS remains for Os speciation under HF hydrothermal conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2: HF hydrothermal

Hydrothermal media provide liquid-phase transport, which TS5 demonstrates at 180 °C and CS1/CS2 at 350 °C. This strongly improves ion diffusion relative to solid-state reaction, and TS5 shows simple mixing/stirring/sealing can yield single crystals under acidic fluoride conditions. However, CS1/CS2 report “very few crystals,” indicating that yield and crystal growth may be poor for some fluoride systems. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because the target is not directly represented among CS/SS/TS hydrothermal examples.

#### F2 — Route 3: flux growth

SS1 and SS5 show flux growth of structurally similar fluorides at ~780–800 °C under Ar with slow cooling, so no >1000 °C penalty applies. A sealed/volatile precursor penalty is applied by [Reasonable Inference] because Os fluorides may volatilize and must be retained. A toxic/air-sensitive precursor penalty is also applied due to Os fluoride and fluoride flux handling. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for osmium retention in molten chloride/fluoride fluxes.

#### F3 — Route 3: flux growth

SS1 and SS5 support flux-enhanced crystallization and slow cooling of transition-metal fluorides, improving precursor activity and structural ordering relative to dry solid-state synthesis. The ion diffusion score is moderate because flux helps transport but Os solubility/volatility is not evidenced in CS/SS/TS. Structural reconstruction is scored moderate–good because flux growth can produce ordered crystalline fluorides, but the target’s monoclinic Sr–Mg–Os ordering is more specific than the Ti fluoride analogues in SS1/SS5. Remaining uncertainty is primarily Os chemistry and phase competition.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target likely has a large cation-size mismatch between Sr²⁺, Mg²⁺, and Os species, triggering C1 by [Reasonable Inference]. C2 is not triggered because only the 10-atom formula is given and no >40 atom unit cell is specified; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a larger cell. C3 is not triggered because the target description does not explicitly report coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, unlike some CS/SS analogues that mention mixed sharing. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgOsF₆ is likely a marginally metastable or near-threshold phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the upper limit of the most favorable stability bin. CS and SS analogues strongly support the broader feasibility of complex fluoride synthesis by sealed solid-state, flux, and hydrothermal methods, while TS analogues show that phases with comparable or higher metastability can still be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride chemical potential with liquid-mediated or sealed-vessel transport, supported by CS1/CS2 and TS5 for HF routes and by SS1/SS5 for flux routes. The most critical limiting factor is osmium chemistry: volatility, toxicity, redox ambiguity, and lack of direct Os–fluoride analogue evidence in CS/SS/TS. Overall, synthesis is plausible but would require careful sealed-system design and phase-purity screening.
