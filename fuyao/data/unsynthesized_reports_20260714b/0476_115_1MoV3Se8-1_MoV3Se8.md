### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.024 eV/atom, which falls in the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom category. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values, including NbSe₂ polymorphs at 0.0195–0.0253 eV/atom, have reported syntheses in evacuated tubes or CVT routes (TS1, TS2, TS4). Structurally similar MoSe₂ phases also lie very close to the hull and are synthetically accessible (SS4, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull supports thermodynamic accessibility but does not guarantee the specific V/Mo ordering in orthorhombic Pmm2.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate-to-high temperatures but generally below 1000 °C, consistent with 600 °C NbCrSe₅, 650 °C V₄GaSe₈, and 800 °C Nb₄CrSe₈/Nb₃VSe₆ (CS1, CS3, CS2, CS5). The main deductions are vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile/toxic Se, both strongly supported by the fact that essentially all related solid-state selenides are made in evacuated quartz/silica tubes (CS1–CS5, TS1–TS4). TS3 shows that prereaction and grinding may be needed, but this is not necessarily a mandatory ≥3-step route. The remaining uncertainty is whether Mo’s refractory nature forces temperatures closer to 1000 °C.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental V and Mo powders are less reactive than solution precursors, and Mo/V diffusion is expected to be slow due to refractory metallic precursors [Reasonable Inference]. However, the CS and TS analogues show that sealed-tube Se chemistry enables formation of complex transition-metal selenides at 600–1000 °C (CS1–CS5, TS1–TS4). Structural reconstruction is aided by the target’s molybdenite-derived sheet motif, which resembles MoSe₂/NbSe₂ layered materials (SS4, SS5, TS1, TS4). Kinetic uncertainty remains substantial because exact V₃MoSe₈ cation ordering is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has higher severity because CVT requires vacuum ampoules, halogen transport agents, volatile Se, and often a two-zone temperature gradient (SS2, SS5, TS2). Some MoSe₂ growth conditions exceed 1000 °C, giving an additional penalty (SS5). I₂/Br₂ introduce toxicity and corrosion risks, as iodine is used in NbCrSe₅ crystal growth and transport reactions, while bromine is used in related CVT synthesis (CS1, SS2, TS2). The route is feasible but more operationally demanding than direct sintering.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves mass transport through volatile metal halide/chalcogen species, and layered MoSe₂/NbSe₂-type materials are established CVT products (SS5, TS2). Structurally similar complex layered selenides also form by CVT with long growth times (SS2). The limiting kinetic/compositional factor is that V and Mo may have different transport efficiencies, making V:Mo = 3:1 control difficult [Reasonable Inference]; TS2 explicitly shows transport-agent dependence for different metals/chalcogenides. Thus kinetics for crystallization are better than Route 1, but stoichiometry control is worse.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The microwave step itself is mild for MoSe₂, using 450 W for 15 min in ethylene glycol (SS4). However, adapting it to V₃MoSe₈ likely requires post-annealing in an evacuated Se-containing tube, which reintroduces vacuum, Se volatility, and multistep processing [Reasonable Inference based on CS1–CS5 and TS1–TS4]. NaBH₄/Se handling also adds chemical hazard [Reasonable Inference; NaBH₄ and Se are listed in SS4]. The route’s condition severity is therefore moderate rather than mild.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution mixing and reductive selenization should provide high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths, supported by rapid MoSe₂ formation in SS4. However, the target is not binary MoSe₂; it requires ordered V/Mo incorporation into a Pmm2 molybdenite-derived lattice. There is insufficient evidence from CS and TS for solution synthesis of this exact kind of ordered mixed-metal selenide, and SS4 only supports MoSe₂. The structural-ordering uncertainty substantially lowers confidence despite favorable initial kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has similar V–Se and Mo–Se bond lengths, reducing the likelihood of strong local strain from cation-size mismatch; this is inferred from the target structural data and is consistent with layered V/Nb/Mo selenide analogues tolerating related octahedral/pyramidal coordination (SS4, SS5, TS1, TS3). The formula unit is small, and the described connectivity is mostly edge-sharing rather than a highly frustrated mixture of vertex/edge/face sharing. Some analogues have more complex mixed connectivity (SS1, TS3), but that complexity is not explicitly present in the target. Therefore amorphization risk is low, with the caveat that nanoscale solution routes could still yield amorphous precursors.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃MoSe₈ is likely a mildly metastable but accessible phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.024 eV/atom. The CS and TS analogues strongly support sealed-tube synthesis of transition-metal selenides with similar thermodynamic metastability and layered motifs, while SS analogues support the molybdenite-derived MoSe₂/NbSe₂ structural chemistry. The most important enabling factor is the broad success of evacuated quartz/silica tube selenide chemistry at 600–1000 °C, which should maintain Se activity and allow layered M–Se frameworks to form (CS1–CS5, TS1–TS4). The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control: V, Mo, and Se may form competing binaries such as VSe₂/MoSe₂ or other V–Se/Mo–Se phases, and several analogues report impurity traces, repeated annealing, or polymorph sensitivity (CS2, CS5, TS1, TS3). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best first attempts using direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction followed by CVT optimization if single crystals are desired.
