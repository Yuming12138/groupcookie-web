### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the 90–100 rubric bin, so a conservative score of 90 is assigned. CS strongly supports stability because Ba₂BiTaO₆ polymorphs are reported at 0–0.014 eV/atom (CS-2/3/4), and SS also supports stability through Ba₂BiTaO₆ and Sr₂TaInO₆ analogues near the hull (SS-1/2/3/4/5). TS analogues cluster around similar metastability, ~0.042–0.067 eV/atom, showing that phases in this energetic regime can be synthesized (TS-1/2/4/5). Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom may indicate a metastable polymorph rather than a true ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct Ceramic

CS and SS show that Ba₂BiTaO₆ is accessible by conventional ceramic methods from carbonate/oxide precursors, with no special atmosphere reported (CS-2/3/4; SS-1/2/3). The main severity comes from high-temperature processing and repeated grinding, because Sr₂TaInO₆ uses 1200 °C heating for 5 days with intermittent regrinding (SS-4/5; TS-5). Bi₂O₃ volatility is not explicitly discussed in CS/SS/TS, so that deduction is a [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Sr₂TaBiO₆; TS is used only for analogous high-T oxide processing and is otherwise neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct Ceramic

The reaction uses low-reactivity oxide/carbonate precursors, especially refractory Ta₂O₅, which is consistent with long reaction times in Sr₂TaInO₆ analogues (SS-4/5; TS-5). The ceramic route requires full formation and ordering of a double perovskite from binary precursors, so the structural reconstruction score is modest. CS provides favorable chemical evidence for Ba₂BiTaO₆ but does not provide kinetic details such as temperature/time for that synthesis (CS-2/3/4), so CS is only partially informative. Insufficient evidence from TS for Bi-containing kinetics; TS is neutral beyond showing typical tantalate high-T processing.

#### F2 — Route 2: BiTaO₄-Assisted Ceramic

The two-step route inherits the high-temperature and multi-step burden from the InTaO₄-assisted Sr₂TaInO₆ synthesis (SS-4/5; TS-5). It remains ambient-pressure and air-compatible, consistent with Sr₂TaInO₆ analogues (SS-4/5; TS-5). BiTaO₄ itself is proposed by analogy rather than directly given in CS/SS/TS, so that part is [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports the Bi–Ta–O ceramic chemistry through Ba₂BiTaO₆ but does not explicitly mention BiTaO₄ intermediates (CS-2/3/4).

#### F3 — Route 2: BiTaO₄-Assisted Ceramic

Using a pre-reacted Bi–Ta oxide should improve cation mixing relative to separate Bi₂O₃ and Ta₂O₅ [Reasonable Inference]. This inference is supported by the explicit use of InTaO₄ as an intermediate for Sr₂TaInO₆ synthesis (SS-4/5; TS-5). Diffusion is still limited by Sr incorporation and B-site ordering at high temperature, so the improvement is moderate rather than decisive. Insufficient evidence from CS for an intermediate-assisted route, so CS is neutral for this kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 3: Hydroxide / Alkali Flux

CS provides direct evidence that NaOH/H₂O flux can crystallize complex tilted oxide phases at only 600 °C for 24 h in silver tubes (CS-1/5). This avoids the >1000 °C penalty of ceramic routes, giving the route a high condition-severity score. However, it is still operationally more complex than simple ceramic heating because it requires corrosive flux handling, sealed or compatible containers, and post-reaction washing (CS-1/5). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Sr–Bi–Ta flux synthesis, so they are neutral for the flux-specific condition score.

#### F3 — Route 3: Hydroxide / Alkali Flux

The molten flux can strongly improve precursor activity by dissolving oxide species, as shown for NaLn₂OsO₆ hydroxide flux growth (CS-1/5). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Ta₂O₅ and Bi₂O₃ dissolve and recombine into Sr₂TaBiO₆ rather than competing Sr/Bi tantalates or Na/K-containing phases [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction in flux is plausible because CS flux analogues form tilted octahedral oxides, but no direct Sr₂TaBiO₆ flux analogue is provided (CS-1/5). SS and TS are insufficient for this specific flux pathway and are treated as neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large mismatch among Sr, Bi, and Ta cation sizes, reflected in distinct Sr–O, Bi–O, and Ta–O bond lengths in the target structure description; this triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target connectivity is predominantly corner-sharing TaO₆/BiO₆ octahedra, and no edge/face-sharing coexistence is described, so C3 is not triggered. The unit cell atom count is not explicitly supplied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for >40 atoms means C2 is treated as neutral/not triggered. Overall, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂TaBiO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest supporting analogues are Ba₂BiTaO₆, which shares Bi/Ta double-perovskite chemistry and is made by conventional ceramic methods (CS-2/3/4; SS-1/2/3), and Sr₂TaInO₆, which shares Sr/Ta tilted double-perovskite structure and is made at 1200 °C with regrinding (SS-4/5; TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust TaO₆–BiO₆ corner-sharing double-perovskite framework, which is directly represented in Ba₂BiTaO₆ analogues (CS-2/3/4; SS-1/2/3). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: Ta₂O₅ is refractory and Sr-based tilted double perovskites require long, high-temperature reaction or a flux route to overcome diffusion limits (SS-4/5; TS-5). Flux synthesis could reduce thermal severity, but its transferability to Sr–Bi–Ta chemistry is less directly proven than the ceramic route (CS-1/5).
