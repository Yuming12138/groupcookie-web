### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff, so it is near-hull but not deeply stable [Target]. CS and SS analogues include many stable or nearly stable alkali/protonated phosphate or oxyanion phases, while TS analogues intentionally cluster around similar thermodynamic distances, especially TS1–TS3. The remaining uncertainty is that small positive hull energy can still reflect metastability or missing competing hydrate/acid phosphate phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 receives no severity deductions because CS1, SS2/TS5, TS1, and TS3 demonstrate ambient-to-65 °C aqueous crystallization without high pressure, reactive gas, or specialized apparatus. The source conflict is that CS1 also notes phase-control issues for some compositions, but that affects F3 more than condition severity. Insufficient evidence from CS4/SS1 for MOCVD is treated as neutral 50 and not used.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Aqueous carbonates and phosphorus oxyacids are highly active precursors in CS1 and SS2/TS5, and solution transport minimizes kinetic barriers. Structural assembly is less certain because the target requires both mixed Cs/K cation ordering and retention of H₂P₂O₇ units [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS1’s phase-window sensitivity lowers the structural-reconstruction component.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Diffusion growth uses mild conditions, as TS4 and CS5 operate at ambient pressure and low temperature. A −10 deduction is applied because setting up mother liquor, diffusion medium/antisolvent, growth, and isolation is a multi-step process. Methanol volatility is flagged as a safety issue but not counted as a volatile precursor under the scoring rule.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Diffusion routes are kinetically favorable for ordering because transport occurs through solution or gel, as in TS4 and CS5. Slow growth may improve single-crystal quality and mixed-cation ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the target nucleates before competing single-alkali acid phosphates.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Hydrothermal synthesis is moderate-severity because TS2 and CS3 require Teflon-lined autoclaves, elevated temperature, and controlled cooling, but not extreme temperature or >5 GPa pressure. A −10 multi-step deduction is applied for loading, sealed heating, slow cooling, and recovery. There is insufficient evidence from TS for any need for reactive gas, so that source is neutral for atmosphere severity.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Hydrothermal media provide good dissolution and recrystallization kinetics, supported by TS2 and CS3. The pathway is less certain for this target because hot water can hydrolyze pyrophosphate, while the target requires protonated pyrophosphate connectivity [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Acidic pH control from CS3 partly mitigates this risk.

**F2 — Route 4**  
The low-temperature melt route avoids >1000 °C conditions, but SS3 requires humidity protection and P₄O₁₀ handling, causing deductions for controlled atmosphere and air-sensitive precursor use. The route also needs several processing steps, so a multi-step deduction is applied. SS4 and SS5 show phosphate framework synthesis at higher temperature, but they are not direct evidence for retaining a protonated target.

**F3 — Route 4**  
SS3 supports fast reaction kinetics through liquid formation at 80–100 °C, which improves ion transport. However, SS3 also reports glass formation risk, and the target’s hydrogen pyrophosphate framework is more likely to dehydrate or disorder under melt conditions [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic term.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a large Cs/K size contrast, supported by Cs–O distances in CS4/SS4/SS5 and shorter K–O distances in CS2/SS3/TS4. The likely >40 atom unit cell adds configurational complexity [Target; Reasonable Inference], while the target’s reported connectivity is mainly corner-sharing, so the mixed edge/face-sharing check is not triggered. Exact unit-cell atom count is not directly supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that specific source signal is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a near-hull, mildly metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis from aqueous acidic media: CS1, SS2/TS5, TS1, and TS3 all show mild solution crystallization of related hydrogen phosphate or condensed phosphate materials. The most important enabling factor is high precursor mobility and acid–base reactivity in water, which should allow Cs⁺, K⁺, and hydrogen pyrophosphate units to crystallize without high-temperature diffusion barriers. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: CS1 reports phase-control difficulties, SS3 reports glass risk in melt chemistry, and the target requires mixed Cs/K ordering plus protonated pyrophosphate retention. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with aqueous slow evaporation or diffusion growth preferred over hydrothermal or melt routes.
