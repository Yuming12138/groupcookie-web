### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score. CS analogues are mostly thermodynamically accessible, with CS2–CS5 at E_hull = 0 and CS1 nearly stable at 0.005 eV/atom. SS/TS analogues also include multiple E_hull = 0 phases, especially Mo–S cluster compounds in SS2–SS4 and TS1–TS5. The remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS gives a direct Re–S–I phase-equilibrium experiment, so volatile iodine could still narrow the practical synthesis window.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires sealed vacuum ampoules and volatile S/I control, which is supported by SS1 sealed fused silica ampoules and CS2 evacuated silica tubes. The recommended 800–900 °C avoids the >1000 °C penalty, but the use of I2/Re iodides and multistep heating/regrinding imposes substantial condition severity [Reasonable Inference from SS3 repeated cycles]. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically on iodine-containing Re sulfides, so TS contributes mainly a neutral-to-general solid-state signal rather than exact conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are the major weakness of direct solid-state synthesis because Re metal is refractory and likely diffuses slowly [Reasonable Inference]. SS1 demonstrates that Re chalcogenide-halide phases can form at 900 °C, but it also reports low yields and difficult phase control, implying sluggish or selective crystallization. Repeated heating/regrinding protocols in SS3/TS2 support using multistep processing but also indicate kinetic difficulty. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for dry Re–S–I diffusion, so it is treated as neutral for this route’s kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT/self-transport uses controlled vacuum and volatile halogens, so deductions for sealed reactive/vacuum conditions and volatile/toxic reagents apply. CS1 and CS2 directly show halogen-assisted transport in sealed ampoules, while CS4 supports vapor transport for related sulfides. The temperature can likely be kept below or near 950 °C [Reasonable Inference], avoiding the >1000 °C deduction. SS1 reinforces the need for sealed ampoules and controlled cooling in the closest Re chalcogenide-halide chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best kinetics because volatile Re–I/S–I species can bypass slow Re solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/CS2 halogen CVT]. Using a pre-reacted feed reduces structural reconstruction relative to starting from elements. The main uncertainty is the stability window of Re3S4I under iodine vapor: too much iodine could favor binary rhenium iodides or over-transport [Reasonable Inference]. SS1’s close structural match makes the recrystallization pathway plausible, but it also warns that phase yields may be low.

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route has similar safety and containment penalties to Route 2 because it still uses sealed ampoules and volatile iodine/sulfur. Its lower proposed temperature range is favorable [Reasonable Inference], and SS1’s use of halides in Re chalcogenide-halide synthesis supports the idea of a halide-rich medium. However, because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports iodide flux synthesis of Re3S4I, uncertainty remains high. TS provides insufficient specific flux evidence and is scored neutral for flux condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Molten iodide flux should improve mass transport compared with dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], but Re solubility in alkali iodide flux is uncertain. SS1 supports halide involvement in Re chalcogenide-halide formation, and CS5 supports melt/slow-cooling synthesis of layered chalcogenides, but neither directly proves iodide-flux growth for Re–S–I. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target layered sheet may crystallize from a flux, but competing alkali intercalation or Re sulfide phases are possible [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is intermediate between direct solid-state and CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, Re, so cation-size disorder is not a concern. The described P-1 cell has multiple inequivalent sites but not an obviously large >40-atom cell, and the polyhedra are edge- and corner-sharing rather than face/edge/corner mixed. SS1 and SS2 show analogous square-pyramidal chalcogenide frameworks can crystallize, supporting low amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is that layered low-symmetry phases can form stacking faults, but that is more a crystallinity/phase-purity issue than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re3S4I is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS1, which is a structurally very close triclinic P-1 Re chalcogenide-halide layered compound synthesized in sealed ampoules, and from CS1/CS2/CS4, which show that halogen-assisted vapor transport is effective for chemically related sulfides. The most critical enabling factor is iodine/halogen transport, which can mobilize refractory rhenium and promote crystal growth. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under volatile sulfur and iodine chemical potentials. Overall, the target appears synthesizable, with the highest probability route being iodine-assisted CVT or self-transport from a pre-reacted feed.
