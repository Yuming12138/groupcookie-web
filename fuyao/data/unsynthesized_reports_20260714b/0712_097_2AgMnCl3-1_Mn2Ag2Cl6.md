### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and likely a ground-state phase. Stable halide analogues at E_hull = 0 appear in CS2, CS5, SS1–SS5, and TS1–TS5, supporting that low-dimensional halides can be thermodynamically stable. The strongest evidence is the target’s own hull energy; CS gives only indirect chemical support, while SS/TS provide stronger support through stable Mn chloride and low-dimensional halide analogues. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of hydrated/competing chloride phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: molten chloride synthesis

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because SS1/TS1 require evacuated sealed quartz ampoules, precursor drying, and ~500 °C melting, but not high pressure or extreme temperature. Kinetically, molten/soft chloride media should enhance ion transport, and TlMnCl₃ provides a close AMnCl₃ structural precedent with MnCl₆ octahedra (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS1/TS1 report slow transformations and metastability between related phases, so MnAgCl₃ may require careful cooling and storage.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: low-temperature sealed solid-state annealing

Route 2 is not thermally severe because CS2, CS5, and SS5/TS5 show halide materials can form at 120–260 °C. However, vacuum sealing and dehydration still penalize F2, and the kinetics may be slow, as SS5/TS5 required 4 months at 120 °C for Ag₂HgSI₂. This route may preserve volatile components better than Route 1, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that MnAgCl₃ will form rapidly or phase-pure at low temperature.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT

CVT has good crystallization potential because CS1 and SS2–SS4/TS2–TS4 demonstrate halide crystal growth in sealed evacuated ampoules with temperature gradients. Its F2 score is lower because transport agents and volatile halide species create sealed-ampoule and toxicity/air-sensitivity issues. Kinetically, vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion, but SS2–SS4/TS2–TS4 report low yields, equilibrium mixtures, twinning, and moisture sensitivity, so phase-pure MnAgCl₃ growth is uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: solution evaporation

Solution evaporation has mild nominal conditions, supported only indirectly by CS4, where Cu chloride salts crystallized from concentrated HCl or alcohol chloride media. It scores well on precursor mixing and diffusion because solution chemistry enables high mobility, but it is structurally risky for anhydrous MnAgCl₃. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports solution growth of Mn–Ag–Cl anhydrous phases; hydration, AgCl precipitation, or formation of chloroargentate salts are major uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is triggered: the Mn²⁺/Ag⁺ cation size mismatch exceeds 15% [Reasonable Inference]. The target is a simple 5-atom formula and the supplied structure description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell or face-sharing connectivity. CS3 shows an amorphous-to-crystalline transition in VOCl₃, but that is a molecular oxychloride and provides weak signal; SS1/TS1 phase transformations in TlMnCl₃ provide a more relevant warning that halide frameworks can be structurally labile.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnAgCl₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS1/TS1, where TlMnCl₃, an AMnCl₃ manganese chloride analogue with MnCl₆ octahedra, forms by sealed-ampoule molten chloride synthesis. CS2, CS5, and SS5/TS5 further show that chloride/halide compounds can form at relatively low temperatures in sealed ampoules, but these are less structurally specific. The most critical enabling factor is the use of dry, chloride-rich sealed conditions that suppress hydrolysis and allow halide mobility. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: related halides show transformations, low yields, or sensitivity to moisture and stoichiometry in SS1/TS1 and SS2–SS5/TS2–TS5.
