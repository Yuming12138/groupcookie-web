### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.048 eV/atom falls in the highest rubric bin, so the base thermodynamic score is high. Experimentally realized thermodynamic analogues include NaBeSb at 0.0484 eV/atom (TS5), NaCu3Te2 at 0.0572 eV/atom (TS1), and Ag3SI at 0.0668 eV/atom (TS2), showing that nearby metastable phases can be isolated. CS analogues such as LiCdAs, NaLiTe, NaAuTe, NaCdAs, and KLiTe are on-hull (CS1–CS5), but insufficient evidence from CS proves LiAgTe is a true ground state. SS analogues show stable Ag chalcogenide frameworks (SS1–SS5), but insufficient evidence from SS resolves competing Li–Ag–Te phases.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because NaAuTe is synthesized at 600 °C (CS3, SS2), KCu3Te2 at 700 °C (TS3), and NaCu3Te2 at 700–750 °C (TS1). The main severity deductions are the sealed system needed for volatile Li/Te [Reasonable Inference from alkali telluride sealed ampoules in CS3, TS1] and air-sensitive/toxic precursors [Reasonable Inference supported by moisture sensitivity in CS5, TS1, TS3]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is needed, based on CS3 and TS3. Remaining uncertainty is whether LiAgTe decomposes or loses Li before ordering, for which insufficient evidence from SS is available.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Elemental Ag is less reactive than preformed tellurides, so precursor activity is only moderate [Reasonable Inference], although Ag powder is successfully used in Ag chalcogenide syntheses (SS1, SS5). Diffusion should be manageable because analogous alkali/coinage tellurides form at 600–750 °C (CS3, TS1, TS3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because P4/nmm KLiTe and P4/nmm NaMnSb are accessible by solid-state routes (CS5, TS4). The uncertainty is the absence of an exact LiAgTe synthesis precedent, so SS gives insufficient direct evidence for this structure.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 benefits from lower temperature, since KLiTe and NaLiTe binary telluride anneals occur at 673–773 K, about 400–500 °C (CS2, CS5). The route is penalized for air-sensitive Li2Te/alkali tellurides (CS2, CS5), sealed handling (CS2, CS5), and likely precursor preparation steps [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, consistent with CS2 and CS5. The largest uncertainty is whether Ag2Te reacts cleanly with Li2Te rather than segregating Ag-rich tellurides; insufficient evidence from TS addresses Li2Te + Ag2Te specifically.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Preformed tellurides provide better local chemical matching than elemental Ag/Te [Reasonable Inference], and Li2Te is directly validated for KLiTe/NaLiTe (CS2, CS5). The structural transfer is strong because KLiTe is P4/nmm and synthesized from binary tellurides (CS5). The long annealing times of 2–7 days indicate diffusion/order kinetics are not instantaneous (CS2, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is Ag incorporation into the Li–Te layered motif, for which CS gives strong Li–Te evidence but insufficient exact Li–Ag–Te evidence.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Supercritical ethylenediamine routes operate at low temperature, 300–340 °C, for structurally similar Ag chalcogenides (SS1, SS5). However, they require sealed tubes, autoclave operation, multiple handling/washing steps, and air-sensitive/reactive Li sources [Reasonable Inference from SS1, SS5 and CS5]. The rubric’s >5 GPa penalty does not apply because 2400 psi is far below 5 GPa (SS1, SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports this as a standard route for LiAgTe.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Solvothermal mineralization can make Ag chalcogenides at lower temperature than bulk routes, supporting high transport kinetics (SS1, SS5). The drawback is structural selectivity: RbAg3Te2 and RbAg5Se3 are Ag-rich frameworks, not 1:1:1 P4/nmm phases (SS1, SS5). Phase competition is a known issue in these routes, including mixed products and yield variation (SS1, SS5). Therefore, kinetics may be good but the pathway may not select the target phase.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a crystalline layered P4/nmm ternary with a small likely unit cell, similar to KLiTe and NaMnSb P4/nmm analogues (CS5, TS4). The main amorphization risk comes from the large Li/Ag cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], which can increase local strain. The target description contains face- and corner-sharing cuboctahedra but not simultaneous edge/face/corner sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully activated. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any tendency of LiAgTe to form glassy or amorphous products.
