### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0040 eV/atom, so it lies well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom “highly synthesizable” window. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable low hull energies are synthesized by CVT or melt routes, e.g. Cd2AsCl2 at 0.0027 eV/atom (TS1), Hg/S/Br/Cl at 0.00145 eV/atom (TS3), and NaInBr4 at 0.0069 eV/atom (TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy does not specify which polymorph or kinetic pathway will form first; CS2/CS4 show cooling rate can select different CuAlCl4 polymorphs.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 because the required temperatures are low, comparable to 250–260 °C CuAlCl4/CuGaCl4 syntheses (CS1, CS3, CS4), but vacuum/inert sealing and volatile halide containment are needed, as in CS2–CS4 and TS1. Kinetics are favorable because halide salts are reactive and may form liquid/volatile phases at low temperature, consistent with rapid melt reactions in CS2/CS4. The uncertainty is structural: CS analogues are CuMCl4 tetrahedral frameworks, not CuCdCl3 sheets, so reconstruction/order formation may be nontrivial.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because CVT requires vacuum sealing, a temperature gradient, multi-day growth, and handling volatile/toxic halides; these complications are explicitly present in SS1/SS2 and TS1. Kinetically, vapor transport can overcome solid-state diffusion and produce crystals, as shown for layered halide analogues in SS1/SS2 and Cd chloride thermodynamic analogues in TS1. The main uncertainty is phase purity, since SS1/SS2 report low yields, equilibrium mixtures, and difficult separation for related halide CVT products.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest conditions because solution crystallization of Cd/Hg chloride analogues occurs from solution, even at room temperature in SS3 and without complex equipment in CS5. Kinetics are strong because solvated ions assemble without bulk diffusion barriers, but structural reconstruction is scored lower because the target’s Cu–Cd layered sheet is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. The key uncertainty is Cu(I) chemistry in solution: CuCl may require high chloride activity and oxygen exclusion to avoid oxidation or precipitation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 inherits moderate severity from sealed melt handling and possible quenching/annealing steps, as used for CuAlCl4 polymorph control in CS2/CS4. Kinetics are favorable in a molten halide medium, but phase selection may be sensitive to cooling rate, since CS2/CS4 explicitly show rapid quenching versus slow cooling gives different CuAlCl4 phases. This route is therefore useful for polymorph screening but less certain as a direct route to the target structure.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines small/low-coordinate Cu and larger octahedral Cd sites, so the cation-size mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference based on target coordination]. The structure also has mixed connectivity: corner-sharing Cu–Cd polyhedra and edge-sharing CdCl6 octahedra, which raises disorder/glass risk. However, the formula is simple and no evidence indicates a very large unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom target cell, so C2 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuCdCl3 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.004 eV/atom. The CS data strongly supports Cu(I) chloride chemistry in sealed low-temperature ampoules using CuCl, while CS5/SS3 supports solution crystallization of related MCl3 chlorides; TS data supports that low-hull Cd/halide phases can be crystallized under moderate conditions. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of halide salts, especially in solution, melt, or vapor-transport environments, as shown across CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3, and TS1. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Cu(I)/Cd(II) stoichiometry and selecting the layered CuCdCl3 topology rather than binary chlorides or alternative ternary chlorometallates. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the solution route easiest if Cu(I) can be stabilized, and sealed-ampoule melt/CVT routes serving as robust backup routes.
