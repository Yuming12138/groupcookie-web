### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0180 eV/atom, placing it well within the usually synthesizable near-hull regime [Target]. Similar materials also lie close to the hull: CS-1 is 0.0286 eV/atom, SS-2/TS-2 is 0.0161 eV/atom, and TS-1/SS-3 is 0.0178 eV/atom. This supports a high thermodynamic score, though the target is slightly metastable rather than guaranteed ground-state [Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists to determine the exact decomposition products, so that part is treated neutrally.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
CS-1 demonstrates ambient-temperature synthesis, which strongly lowers condition severity. However, CS-1 also requires Schlenk/N2 handling, organometallic As4S3·M(CO)5 precursors, controlled solvent layering, and specific molar ratios. The analogous Se/Br route likely inherits these handling issues [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not directly inform this exact solution-diffusion route for Ag–As–Se–Br, so the strongest signal is CS-1, with insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
CS-1 indicates that soluble As4S3·M(CO)5 adducts can deliver an As4Q3 framework by solution diffusion at ambient temperature, implying favorable kinetic access. The target’s topology is extremely close to CS-1, so structural reconstruction should be limited. The remaining uncertainty is whether As4Se3·M(CO)5 has comparable solubility/stability and whether AgBr is sufficiently labile; this is a Reasonable Inference rather than directly proven.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
SS-5 and CS-3 support sealed evacuated ampoule synthesis at 550–600 °C, which is not thermally extreme. The main deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile As/Se/Br chemistry, toxicity, and possible multi-step grinding/annealing as seen in CS-3. CS-2/4/5 further support evacuated ampoule methods for selenides but do not specifically prove Ag–As–Se–Br phase formation, so that aspect remains uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
SS-5 shows that a coinage-metal selenide bromide sheet can form at 550 °C from CuBr and Se, and CS-3 shows that related chalcogenide halides can form after grinding and annealing. Kinetics should be better than refractory oxide synthesis because Ag/As/Se/Br components are relatively soft and volatile [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting kinetic factor is assembling the specific As4Se3 sheet while avoiding binaries such as AgBr, Ag2Se, and As–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
SS-2/TS-2 demonstrates Ag–Se–I hydrothermal synthesis at 220 °C for 5 days, so the thermal severity is low. The penalties arise from autoclave/sealed acid handling, concentrated hydrohalic acid, toxic As/Se components, and multiphase product separation. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal As–Se–Br synthesis, so the route is plausible but less directly supported than Route 1.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal media can transport Ag–Se–halide species efficiently, as SS-2/TS-2 repeatedly obtained AgSe3I as a major product. This supports good ion mobility at low temperature. However, SS-2/TS-2 also reports at least two phases, contaminants, and intergrowths, and no As analogue is provided, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity are only moderate.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
TS-5 supports low-temperature CVT/halide transport in evacuated ampoules, avoiding high-temperature penalties. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile halide transport agents, glovebox handling, and toxic As/Se chemistry [TS-5; Reasonable Inference]. SS-1/TS-4 supports flux growth under vacuum but does not directly address bromide transport for Ag–As–Se–Br, so that signal is partial.

**F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics.**  
Vapor/halide transport should provide high precursor mobility, as TS-5 shows bromide chalcogenide growth in a temperature gradient and SS-1/TS-4 shows Ag–Se crystal growth from chalcogen-rich media. The structural match is weaker than CS-1 because TS-5 is Bi–Te–Br rather than Ag–As–Se–Br. Phase control is also only moderate because TS-5 reports by-products and sensitivity to stoichiometry.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines Ag-centered tetrahedra and As–Se units, giving a significant cation size/coordination mismatch [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The structure contains edge- and corner-sharing tetrahedra but not face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered [Target]. The unit-cell atom count cannot be confirmed from CS/SS/TS, so C2 is scored as not triggered/neutral rather than penalized.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2As4Se3Br2 is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.0180 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from CS-1, which is a very close Cu/S/Cl analogue made by ambient solution diffusion and possessing essentially the same layered coinage-metal As–chalcogen halide architecture. SS-5 and SS-2/TS-2 independently support the feasibility of Ag/Cu–Se–halide tetrahedral chemistry, but they also warn that cooling rate and multiphase products can be critical. The most important enabling factor is the availability of a preorganized As4Se3 molecular/cage precursor analogous to the As4S3 adduct in CS-1. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity against AgBr, Ag2Se, and binary As–Se phases, especially if using high-temperature ampoule routes.
