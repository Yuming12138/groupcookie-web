### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.037 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom band and therefore receives a high score. TS directly supports this assessment because several thermodynamically similar halides around 0.033–0.061 eV/atom are reported as synthesized phases, including ThPbI₆ and alkaline-earth thorium bromides (TS-1 to TS-4). CS also supports feasibility because chemically related layered chalcogenide-halides have very low hull energies and known syntheses, such as MnBiSe₂I and Tl₂Cu₂Hf₃Se₈ (CS-2, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee access to the specific S/Se/I anion-ordered phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile components, multistep thermal processing, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. These penalties are supported by CS-2 and CS-3, which use evacuated ampoules for chalcogenide/iodide or Hf–Se synthesis, and by SS-1, which requires a closed, moisture-excluded system for HfI₄. There is no high-pressure or >1000 °C requirement based on the closest analogues, so severity is challenging but not extreme. Insufficient evidence from TS for exact processing conditions of Hf–S–Se–I; neutral TS process-condition signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct solid-state reaction is kinetically limited because Hf metal is refractory and dense [Reasonable Inference], while CS-3’s Hf selenide synthesis still requires 800 °C for 5 days. HfI₄ provides a structurally relevant and reactive iodide source, supported by SS-1, but complete mixing of Hf/S/Se/I in the target sheet may require long diffusion distances. CS-2 shows a related Mn–Bi–Se–I phase can form at 600 °C over 7 days, but also reports phase purity difficulty, which reduces confidence. TS gives thermodynamic similarity but insufficient kinetic detail for the exact target; neutral TS kinetic signal 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 because it requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile iodine/HfI₄ transport species, and careful moisture exclusion. SS-1 and SS-3 directly support these requirements for HfI₄ and ThI₄ vapor/distillation syntheses. The temperature remains moderate, but the need for a thermal gradient and pressure-safe sealed ampoule makes it nontrivial. CS provides evidence that S/Se/I compounds tolerate sealed ampoule synthesis (CS-2), but not direct evidence for Hf–S–Se–I vapor transport.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport improves kinetics by moving Hf as volatile iodide species, as demonstrated for HfI₄ and ThI₄ analogues (SS-1, SS-3). The target’s HfI₄-derived octahedral framework makes structural reconstruction less severe than for an unrelated structure. However, S and Se incorporation into the transport-grown iodide framework is not directly established by SS, and CS only supports bulk sealed-ampoule chalcogenide-halide formation. This route is therefore kinetically favorable but chemically uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 also requires vacuum/inert handling, sealed ampoules, volatile halide/chalcogen components, and multistep preparation. CS-1 supports the use of alkali halide media and slow cooling in layered chalcogenide-halide synthesis, while SS-4 supports Hf halide formation in sealed alkali-halide-containing systems. The conditions are milder in temperature than CS-1 but still require careful air/moisture exclusion. TS provides no specific flux-synthesis evidence for Hf₂SeSI₄; neutral TS route-condition signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux-assisted synthesis scores best kinetically because molten/excess halide media should enhance precursor activity and ion transport [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS-1 and SS-4. Hf halide structural chemistry is accessible at relatively low temperatures in sealed ampoules, as shown by SS-4, and HfI₄ provides the closest local motif from SS-1. The major uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the desired mixed S/Se/I anion order rather than producing HfI₄, HfS₂, HfSe₂, or alkali hafnium iodides. CS-2 warns that related selenide iodides can suffer persistent impurity phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern. The described structure is an ordered 2D sheet of edge-sharing HfSeSI₄ octahedra, and the closest structural analogue HfI₄ also has edge-sharing HfI₆ units (SS-1), suggesting low topological frustration. CS materials include more complex mixed polyhedral networks, but those are not structurally decisive for this target, so insufficient evidence from CS for amorphization tendency; neutral score 50. The remaining uncertainty is whether S/Se/I anion disorder could broaden crystallization or lead to stacking disorder rather than a fully amorphous product.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂SeSI₄ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase, because its E_hull is only 0.037 eV/atom and TS contains experimentally realized halide analogues in a similar thermodynamic range. The strongest support comes from SS-1, since HfI₄ has very similar edge-sharing Hf–I octahedral connectivity, and from CS-2/CS-3, which show that mixed selenide iodides and Hf selenides are accessible in sealed ampoules. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of volatile/reactive HfI₄ or iodine transport chemistry, which can overcome otherwise slow Hf diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing HfI₄, HfS₂, HfSe₂, and off-stoichiometric iodide/chalcogenide phases are plausible. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with best prospects via a sealed iodide-flux or HfI₄-mineralized route.
