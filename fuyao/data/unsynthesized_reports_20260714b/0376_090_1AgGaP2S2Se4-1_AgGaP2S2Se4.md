### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.005 eV/atom, which is essentially on the convex hull and should be accessible under equilibrium or near-equilibrium annealing conditions [Target]. Very close analogues AgGa(PSe₃)₂, AgVP₂Se₆, AgIn(PSe₃)₂, and SnPSe₃ are also low-hull or on-hull phases [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. CS entries have broad chemical similarity but do not directly represent layered Ag–Ga chalcophosphates; insufficient evidence from CS for F1 beyond neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
Condition severity is moderate because the method requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile P/Se/S management, but it avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C [SS2, TS2, SS5]. Kinetics are acceptable because close analogues form at 600–750 °C from elements [SS2, TS1, TS2], and the target’s framework is the same layered P₂X₆ motif [SS2, TS2]. The main uncertainty is mixed S/Se ordering and possible competing Ag/Ga sulfide/selenide or P–chalcogen binary phases, which are not directly addressed in SS/TS for this exact composition.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Bridgman/melt growth.**  
The Bridgman route is more severe than Route 1 because it involves controlled cooling and post-annealing, as reported for AgGa(PSe₃)₂ and AgIn(PSe₃)₂ analogues [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS5]. Kinetically, partial melting should improve homogenization and crystal growth [SS2, TS1], but phase control may be complicated by incongruent melting/metastability reported in related systems [SS1, TS5]. Mixed S/Se volatility makes this route less robust than for pure selenides [Reasonable Inference; no direct SS/TS mixed-anion Bridgman example].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 CVT.**  
CVT has useful kinetic advantages because iodine transport enables vapor-phase redistribution and single-crystal growth in SnPSe₃ and sulfide chalcogenides [TS4, CS5]. Its severity remains moderate-to-high due to iodine handling, sealed ampoules, thermal-gradient control, and likely composition drift between S and Se [TS4, CS5, Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient evidence for direct CVT of AgGa(PSe₃)₂-type compounds, so the route is plausible but less proven than direct ampoule annealing.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains distinct Ag and Ga octahedral sites with significantly different bond lengths, matching the known Ag/Ga size contrast in AgGa(PSe₃)₂ [Target; SS2, TS1], so one amorphization trigger is assigned. The layered framework contains corner-sharing tetrahedra and edge-sharing octahedra but not face sharing, as in the close analogues [Target; SS2, TS2]. The exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS for the mixed-anion target; therefore that check is treated as not triggered with uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgGaP₂(Se₂S)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.005 eV/atom [Target], and close Ag–Ga–P–Se and Ag–V–P–Se analogues are also low-hull or experimentally synthesized phases [SS2, TS1, TS2]. The strongest support comes from structural and thermodynamic similarity: AgGa(PSe₃)₂ has the same Ag/Ga/P₂Se₆ layered framework [SS2, TS1], and AgVP₂Se₆ shares monoclinic C2 layered topology [TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the well-established evacuated-ampoule chemistry of metal chalcophosphates from elemental metals, P, and chalcogens [SS2, SS4, SS5, TS2]. The main limiting factor is mixed S/Se stoichiometry and anion ordering, because none of the provided SS/TS references directly demonstrate AgGaP₂Se₄S₂ synthesis. Overall, synthesis should be feasible with careful sealed-tube handling and long annealing.
