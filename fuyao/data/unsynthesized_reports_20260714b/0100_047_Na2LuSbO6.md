### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the favorable cutoff for the 90–100 score bin. TS contains several thermodynamically similar materials with E_hull values around 0.055–0.092 eV/atom that have reported syntheses, including Na₃Cu₂SbO₆ and NaSbO₃, supporting the plausibility of synthesizing a slightly metastable sodium antimonate. CS contains several stable NaLnGeO₄ analogues and a near-ground-state La₂LiSbO₆ antimonate, supporting oxide stability. SS provides structurally similar stable sodium oxides/tellurates, but insufficient evidence from SS alone to determine the target thermodynamic stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses conventional ceramic conditions similar to CS La₂LiSbO₆, SS NaMgLaTeO₆, and TS Na₃Cu₂SbO₆. The main penalties are high final temperature above 1000 °C, multiple grinding/firing steps, and toxic Sb precursors. No high pressure or reducing/inert atmosphere is required, which keeps severity moderate. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS determines whether sealed containment is mandatory, so only a partial volatility penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CS and SS show that nitrate/oxide ceramic routes can form related perovskite-derived oxides within hours, which supports reasonable precursor activity. TS shows that Na–Sb oxides may require calcination, regrinding, firing, quenching, and annealing, indicating nontrivial diffusion and phase-control kinetics. Lu₂O₃ is refractory by physicochemical reasoning, so reaction kinetics are slower than for nitrate-only systems. The structural similarity to perovskite-derived antimonates in CS supports a moderate-to-good reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, high pressure above 5 GPa, and reactive gas atmospheres. CS supercritical hydrothermal synthesis is operationally demanding, but the scoring table only penalizes high pressure above 5 GPa, which does not apply. The route is penalized for multiple operations and hazardous concentrated NaOH/Sb chemistry. SS provides insufficient evidence for hydrothermal synthesis of this target class, so it is neutral with respect to this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CS and TS strongly support hydrothermal Na–Ln oxide crystallization, including NaLuGeO₄ at 524 °C and NaEuGeO₄ at 230 °C. The liquid/mineralizer pathway should reduce the diffusion barrier relative to solid-state Lu₂O₃ reaction. However, the available hydrothermal analogues are germanates, not antimonates, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity are uncertain. Therefore the kinetics score is good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized heavily because TS NaSbO₃ requires 10.5 GPa and 1150 °C in an Au capsule. The method is also multistep and uses toxic Sb sources. CS and SS do not provide useful support for high-pressure synthesis of Na₂LuSbO₆ specifically; therefore evidence from CS and SS is insufficient and scored neutral for this route’s necessity. HPHT is feasible but operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

TS demonstrates that high pressure can drive dense sodium antimonate perovskite formation, supporting the general kinetic pathway. A pre-reacted precursor should improve reaction homogeneity, as in TS NaSbO₃. However, HPHT conversion may not be necessary for Na₂LuSbO₆, and the target triclinic P-1 structure is not proven to be a high-pressure polymorph from CS/SS/TS. Thus the route has acceptable reaction kinetics but poor practical attractiveness.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, Na⁺, Lu³⁺, and Sb⁵⁺, with distinct coordination preferences, so cation-size/coordination mismatch is a real disorder risk. The target also has mixed connectivity, including corner-sharing LuO₆/SbO₆ octahedra and edge/corner-sharing oxygen-centered polyhedra, which increases the chance of local disorder or competing amorphous products. The provided structure does not prove a unit cell larger than 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is whether the reported short O–O distance in the target description reflects a true peroxide-like contact or a structural artifact.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂LuSbO₆ is likely a borderline metastable but synthesizable oxide, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and TS includes successfully synthesized sodium antimonates with similar or slightly higher metastabilities. The CS data strongly supports Na–Lu oxide crystallization under hydrothermal conditions and rare-earth antimonate formation by ceramic routes, while TS directly supports sodium antimonate synthesis but also warns about phase control and Na/Sb volatility. The most enabling factor is the existence of robust octahedral Lu³⁺/Sb⁵⁺ oxide chemistry and transferable Na–Ln hydrothermal mineralization. The most limiting factor is competition among sodium antimonates, Lu antimonates, and Na-deficient phases during heating or crystallization. Overall, Route 2 is the best exploratory route, with Route 1 as the most practical bulk-powder backup.
