### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high. This is consistent with several stable Ag-containing analogues in the data, including EuAg4Sb2 with E_hull = 0.0 in CS3/TS4, AgInTe2 with E_hull = 0.0 in CS4, and Ag8SnSe6 with E_hull = 0.0 in SS4/TS1. Some chemically or structurally related phases are metastable but still synthesizable, such as AgTe3 at 0.0186 eV/atom in CS2 and Ag3SI at 0.0668–0.1164 eV/atom in SS2/SS3, which reinforces that a hull-stable Ag2Te target should be feasible. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target tetragonal P4₂12 phase may not be the only accessible Ag2Te polymorph under laboratory thermal histories [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The severity score is moderate because the route uses vacuum and sealed ampoules, both supported by SS4/TS1, SS5, CS4, and TS2, and Te volatility requires sealed handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids extreme pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, which keeps F2 from being too low. Kinetically, elemental Ag and Te are simple but not highly reactive molecular precursors; long anneals in SS4/TS1 and CS3/TS4 suggest ordering and diffusion may be slow. The main uncertainty is whether the tetragonal sheet structure forms directly or requires a melt/anneal pathway.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Melt synthesis followed by controlled quench/anneal

Route 2 has harsher thermal conditions because CS4-type fusion reaches 1050 °C under vacuum and uses quenching, giving deductions for high temperature, vacuum, volatile Te, and multi-step processing. Its kinetic score is better than Route 1 because melting should homogenize Ag and Te and reduce solid-state diffusion limitations; this inference is supported by CS4 for elemental fusion and SS2 for sealed-ampoule melt growth. The main risk is phase selection during cooling, because CS4 and SS3 show quenching/annealing can strongly affect chalcogenide phase identity. The target’s zero hull energy makes the route promising, but polymorph competition remains uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure/high-temperature reaction

CS2 provides very strong chemical precedent for Ag + Te reacting under pressure, but it concerns AgTe3 rather than Ag2Te. The F2 rubric does not heavily penalize 1–2 GPa pressure because the specified high-pressure deduction begins at >5 GPa; nevertheless, the route is experimentally challenging because CS2 requires a modified Belt apparatus and BN capsule. Kinetically, CS2 shows rapid Ag–Te reaction in 30 min under pressure, but phase transferability is weak because CS2 describes pressure-dependent metastability and decomposition behavior for AgTe3. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports the need for pressure to make the target Ag2Te phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film evaporation/annealing

CS5 supports vacuum evaporation and subsequent heating for related chalcogenide systems including Ag and Te, so the route is plausible. F2 is moderate because high vacuum, multi-step deposition/annealing, and Te volatility all add control requirements. F3 is moderate because Ag and Te are evaporable, but Te loss and narrow composition control can produce defect or off-stoichiometric films; CS5 explicitly notes defect/disordered phases and narrow phase ranges in related systems. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS confirms direct crystallization of tetragonal Ag2Te films.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains one cation species, Ag, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description is structurally simple, with one Ag site and one Te site in a 2D Ag2Te sheet, and does not indicate a large >40-atom unit cell; explicit atom count is not provided, so this is partly inferred. Mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity is not described for the target, unlike several analogues where such connectivity is explicitly noted in CS4, CS5, and TS2. Thus amorphization tendency is scored low, although thin-film deposition could still produce amorphous material without annealing, as suggested by CS5.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Ag2Te phase is likely thermodynamically stable rather than merely metastable because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of Ag chalcogenides and Ag tellurides by sealed-tube solid-state, melt/quench, and high-pressure routes, with the closest chemical evidence being Ag + Te high-pressure synthesis in CS2 and Ag–Te-containing fusion in CS4. The most critical enabling factor is the chemical simplicity of the binary Ag–Te system combined with the target’s hull stability. The main limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, especially because Te is volatile and Ag chalcogenides in SS3, SS4/TS1, and CS4 often require careful cooling, quenching, or long annealing. Overall, the material should be synthesizable, with melt homogenization plus low-temperature annealing being the most practical route.
