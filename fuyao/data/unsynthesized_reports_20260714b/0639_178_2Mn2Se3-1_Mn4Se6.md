### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0050 eV/atom, placing it well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window [Target]. Chemically similar MnSe and Cr₃Se₄ are also near-hull at about 0.0035 and 0.0051 eV/atom [CS4, CS3], and thermodynamic analogues cluster near the hull [TS1/TS2/TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee that the Pmc2₁ layered polymorph is the kinetically favored product under ambient-pressure synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct solid-state

The condition severity is moderate because analogous MnSe₂ and related selenides form at 520–850 °C in evacuated sealed tubes [CS1/SS1, SS4], avoiding high pressure or >1000 °C conditions. The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, repeated firing, and toxic selenium handling [CS1/SS1, SS2/SS4]. The main uncertainty is whether a single anneal is sufficient or whether multiple regrind/anneal cycles are needed, as MnSe₂ required multiple firings [CS1/SS1].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state

Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Mn is a dense metal precursor and the target structure requires reconstruction from elements [CS1/SS1 + Reasonable Inference]. Long reaction times and repeated firings in analogues indicate slow diffusion/order development [CS1/SS1, SS2/SS4]. The score is not lower because Se vapor/liquid transport inside sealed tubes can assist reaction at temperatures below the Mn melting point [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: CVT / mineralizer growth

CVT avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, since analogous selenides use 600–650 °C gradients or 600 °C iodine-assisted sealed tubes [SS3/TS4, SS5]. Severity remains moderate because the method requires vacuum ampoules, volatile iodine/selenium, and a multi-step source-growth process [SS3/TS4, SS5]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS exists for CVT growth of binary Mn₂Se₃, so the route is extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 2: CVT / mineralizer growth

Kinetics are favorable relative to bulk solid-state synthesis because halogen transport can bypass slow solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated by bromine/iodine-assisted growth of structurally similar selenides [SS3/TS4, SS5]. Structural reconstruction is still uncertain because the exact Pmc2₁ Mn₂Se₃ phase is not reported among the CVT analogues [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The route receives the best score because it combines moderate temperature with enhanced mass transport and crystal-growth selectivity [SS3/TS4, SS5 + Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Flux growth

Flux growth uses non-extreme temperatures around 780 °C and vacuum sealed tubes, as shown for two-dimensional CrSe₂ [CS5/TS3]. Deductions come from vacuum, volatile Se, multi-step binary precursor/flux handling, and toxic/corrosive materials [CS5/TS3]. The remaining uncertainty is whether Mn₂Se₃ dissolves and recrystallizes sufficiently in KCl/LiCl without chloride or Bi contamination [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

The molten salt flux should improve kinetics and reduce diffusion barriers, consistent with phase-pure single-crystal CrSe₂ growth in LiCl/KCl [CS5/TS3]. The target is also two-dimensional [Target], which supports using a slow-cooling crystallization route, but the target’s Mn₂Se₃ stoichiometry differs from CrSe₂ [Target vs CS5/TS3]. Therefore, flux growth is promising but may require substantial optimization of selenium chemical potential [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4: High-pressure synthesis

This route is severe because the chemically similar Cr₅Se₈ analogue required 65–89 kbar and 1200 °C [CS2]. It also requires specialized multi-anvil/DAC-type equipment, encapsulation, and quenching [CS2]. The target’s low E_hull [Target] and the existence of ambient sealed-tube Mn–Se syntheses [CS1/CS4] make high pressure a fallback rather than a first choice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4: High-pressure synthesis

High temperature and pressure can accelerate reaction and stabilize dense/new phases, as shown for Cr₅Se₈ [CS2]. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target layered Pmc2₁ Mn₂Se₃ specifically benefits from high pressure. The kinetic score is moderate because reaction completion may be fast, but phase selectivity is uncertain [CS2 + Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only Mn as a cation, so cation-size disorder is not a concern [Target]. The target description reports edge- and corner/edge-sharing MnSe₅ trigonal bipyramids but no coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met [Target]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large >40-atom target unit cell, so this check is scored as not triggered but with some uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂Se₃ with E_hull = 0.005 eV/atom is very likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase [Target]. The CS data strongly supports Mn–Se chemical accessibility by sealed-tube reaction and precipitation routes [CS1/CS4], while SS and TS data support crystal growth of low-dimensional selenides by CVT, iodine assistance, and flux methods [SS3/SS5, CS5/TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the very small energy above hull combined with established sealed-tube selenide chemistry [Target, CS1/SS1]. The main limiting factor is phase competition within Mn–Se chemistry, especially among MnSe, Mn₂Se₃, and MnSe₂-like compositions, which requires tight selenium chemical-potential control [CS1/CS4 + Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with CVT/mineralizer growth or flux growth preferred if phase-pure crystals are desired.
