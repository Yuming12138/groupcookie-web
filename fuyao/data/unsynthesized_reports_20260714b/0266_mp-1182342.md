### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the boundary of the highest stability bin in the scoring rubric [Target]. Structurally similar borates such as Ca4GdB3O10, Ca5(BO3)3F, Ba2Ca(BO3)2, and Sr3B2O6 are calculated on the hull, supporting the stability of related BO3-based frameworks [SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.052–0.067 eV/atom have reported syntheses, showing that phases slightly above the hull can be experimentally accessible [TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. The main uncertainty is whether BaNaY(BO3)2 is an equilibrium phase or a metastable phase requiring kinetic trapping [Target; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 solid-state ceramic.**  
Condition severity is moderate because related Ba2Ca(BO3)2 requires high-temperature ceramic treatment and regrinding, but no high pressure or special atmosphere [SS4]. Kinetics are less favorable than flux or nitrate routes because Y2O3 is refractory and cation ordering among Ba/Na/Y requires diffusion through a solid matrix [SS4, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. However, the structural match to Ba2Ca(BO3)2 is strong, since both contain Ba–O polyhedra and trigonal BO3 units [Target, SS4]. Remaining uncertainty is the competition with binary/ternary borates such as Ba borates, Na borates, and YBO3 [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS product-map evidence, neutral 50].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 flux/self-flux.**  
Flux growth is directly supported by Na–La borate and Ca borate analogues, which use H3BO3/Na2CO3 or borate-rich melts with slow cooling [CS5, SS3, SS2]. The flux lowers diffusion barriers and should help form ordered multication borates [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The penalty comes from high temperature, slow cooling, washing/isolation, and likely Na/B volatility [CS5, TS4]. The uncertainty is the exact flux composition window needed to stabilize BaNaY(BO3)2 rather than other Na–Y or Ba borates [CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 nitrate/citrate.**  
This route earns the best severity score because TS3 shows Ba borate synthesis from nitrate precursors at 600/800 °C, suggesting lower crystallization temperature than conventional ceramic routes [TS3]. Nitrate/citrate chemistry should provide superior cation homogeneity and shorter diffusion lengths [TS3; Reasonable Inference]. The limiting kinetic issue is crystallizing an ordered R-3 phase from an amorphous or nanocrystalline precursor, which may require annealing close to 900–1000 °C [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Direct evidence for Ba–Na–Y nitrate/citrate borate synthesis is absent, so this route contains more extrapolation than Routes 1–2 [insufficient direct CS/SS evidence, neutral 50].

**F2/F3 — Route 4 melt growth.**  
Melt growth is plausible because Ca4GdB3O10 grows congruently by Czochralski at 1480 °C [SS1]. It is more severe because temperatures may exceed 1400 °C and specialized Pt/Ir crucibles or pulling equipment are needed [SS1]. Transport kinetics in the liquid are favorable, but Na/B volatilization and uncertain congruent melting lower feasibility [SS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/TS that BaNaY(BO3)2 melts congruently, so this route should be considered exploratory [insufficient evidence from CS/TS, neutral 50].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation size/charge contrast among Ba2+, Na+, Y3+, and B3+, so amorphous borate glass formation is a plausible risk [Target; Reasonable Inference]. However, structurally similar BO3 compounds are crystalline and experimentally synthesized by ceramic, flux, and melt routes [SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5]. The target description does not prove a very large unit cell or complex mixed edge/face/vertex sharing network, so only one amorphization trigger is assigned [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is that excess B2O3 or Na–borate flux can vitrify during cooling if crystallization is too fast or composition drifts [CS5, SS2; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaNaY(BO3)2 is likely **borderline ground-state / weakly metastable**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS and SS data strongly support the general accessibility of Ba-containing borates, Na–rare-earth borates, and BO3-based crystalline frameworks by solid-state or flux methods [CS4, CS5, SS2, SS4]. The TS data shows that phases in the same metastability range can be synthesized, although some require careful phase control or yield mixtures [TS1, TS2, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a reactive nitrate/citrate or borate flux medium to overcome cation diffusion and ordering barriers [TS3, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Na/B stoichiometry while avoiding competing borate phases or glassy flux residues [CS4, CS5, TS4; Reasonable Inference].
