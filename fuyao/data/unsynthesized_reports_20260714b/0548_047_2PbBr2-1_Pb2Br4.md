### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom range. TS gives a close thermodynamic analogue, BiBr, with E_hull = 0.0387 eV/atom and successful CVT synthesis (TS-1), while SS/CS show layered PbI₂ analogues very near the hull and experimentally grown by mild gel methods (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). CS and TS also include bromides with E_hull near 0.02–0.06 eV/atom that were synthesized in ampoules or solid-state methods (CS-1, TS-2, TS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to determine whether the Pmmn PbBr₂ target is the thermodynamic ground state rather than a low-energy metastable polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high severity score because the closest PbI₂ gel analogues use room temperature, ambient pressure, ordinary atmosphere, and simple test-tube equipment (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-5). The only significant deduction is the toxicity of Pb salts, which is inherent to all Pb-containing routes. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Br⁻ substitution requires inert atmosphere or sealed vessels, so no atmosphere/volatility deduction is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because soluble lead acetate and halide salts react through diffusion in a gel, as demonstrated for PbI₂ crystal growth (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). The structural analogue is strong: PbI₂ sheets contain edge-sharing PbI₆ units and T-shaped iodide coordination, resembling the target’s layered PbBr₂ network. The remaining uncertainty is that PbBr₂ Pmmn pentagonal-pyramidal coordination is not identical to trigonal/hexagonal PbI₂ octahedral coordination, so polymorph selection is not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is mild because the structurally similar RbPb₂Br₅ was made by cooling an aqueous solution (SS-3), implying no high pressure, high temperature, or sealed ampoule requirement. The main penalty is the toxicity of PbBr₂/Pb²⁺ salts. Insufficient evidence from SS-3 on exact temperature/time/equipment means the method is scored conservatively but still mild.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution crystallization gives high precursor activity and minimal diffusion barriers, supported by the aqueous cooling route for a Pb–Br-containing compound (SS-3). Chemical transferability to PbBr₂ is good because PbBr₂ is explicitly a component salt in the analogue (SS-3). The structural transferability is weaker than Route 1 because RbPb₂Br₅ is ternary and not a simple layered PbX₂ sheet compound.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because Br₂ is volatile/toxic and requires sealed ampoule handling; closed Ar-filled tubes and evacuated ampoules are used for BiBr₃ (CS-1, TS-2), and sealed ampoules with controlled Ar pressure are used for BiBr (TS-1). The method does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. The main penalties are sealed atmosphere, volatile halogen, multistep handling, and toxic precursors.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics should be acceptable because Br₂ is highly reactive and analogous Bi + Br₂ chemistry produces BiBr₃ directly (CS-1, TS-2). The low-temperature CVT synthesis of BiBr at 272 °C also shows that bromide transport can be effective at modest temperatures (TS-1). However, complete crystallization from elements into a specific Pmmn PbBr₂ polymorph is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses standard evacuated ampoule conditions similar to bromide/halide solid-state syntheses at 523 K or 400–470 °C (TS-3, TS-4). It avoids elemental Br₂ if commercial PbBr₂ is used, but dry/vacuum ampoule handling is still needed and PbBr₂ is toxic. Bromide volatility at elevated temperature is a **[Reasonable Inference]**, so a sealed-system deduction is retained.

#### F3 — Route 4

Kinetics are favorable because the starting material already has the target stoichiometry, requiring only recrystallization or polymorphic transformation **[Reasonable Inference]**. Analogous halide ampoule syntheses use slow cooling and annealing to obtain crystalline phases (TS-3, TS-4). The uncertainty is that slow cooling may favor the stable PbBr₂ polymorph rather than the target low-energy Pmmn phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation radius mismatch does not apply; this assessment comes from the target formula and structure. The reported structure is simple layered PbBr₂ with edge-sharing PbBr₆ units, not a mixed network of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedra. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for glass formation tendencies in PbBr₂ specifically, but structurally similar crystalline PbI₂ polytypes readily form crystals under mild conditions (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5), supporting low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than an assured ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom, below the common metastability threshold but not zero. The strongest support comes from SS and CS PbI₂ analogues, which show that layered PbX₂ frameworks can crystallize easily from gel media at room temperature (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). TS provides additional confidence that bromide phases with similar small positive hull energies can be isolated by sealed ampoule or vapor transport methods (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, polarizable Pb–halide chemistry that permits low-temperature solution or gel crystallization, supported by PbI₂ gel growth (CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: PbI₂ analogues show polytypism (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5), and CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate the exact orthorhombic Pmmn PbBr₂ phase.
