### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom [Target], which lies at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom category in the provided rubric. The closest chemical/structural analogue K2AuBi is stable on the hull and experimentally synthesized [CS1/SS1], while K2AuSb is also on the hull and synthesized in the same Cmcm structure family [SS3]. TS shows that phases with similar or even higher E_hull values, such as Cs2Se at 0.075 eV/atom and Rb2S at 0.0876 eV/atom, can be synthesized [TS1/TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that Ag substitution may shift competing equilibria in the K–Ag–Bi system, and TS does not directly sample K–Ag–Bi chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature and high pressure because K2AuBi was made at 700 °C in a sealed Nb ampoule [CS1/SS1]. The main severity penalties come from volatile/air-sensitive K, multistep thermal processing, and the need for dry inert handling [CS1/SS1/SS3]. This route is moderately demanding but well precedented for the same structure family [CS1/SS1]. TS is only indirectly supportive because it confirms sealed elemental reactions for alkali pnictides but not this exact Cmcm phase [TS2/TS3].

#### F3 — Route 1

K and Bi will be molten at 700 °C, improving reaction kinetics relative to a fully refractory solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and K2AuBi synthesis proves such a route works even with Au [CS1/SS1]. The target is structurally very close to K2AuBi, so the structural reconstruction burden should be low [Target; CS1/SS1]. The uncertainty is whether Ag forms competing binaries or segregates, because direct K2AgBi synthesis is not reported in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses high-vacuum welded-container synthesis analogous to K4ZnBi2 [CS2/SS2], so it is penalized for vacuum, sealed volatile K, and air-sensitive reagents. It is less multistep than Route 1 because CS2/SS2 describes a single stoichiometric reaction. The condition window is less well defined because CS2/SS2 gives no exact temperature/time, so the route is less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Stoichiometric elemental reactions are simple but may suffer from contact and diffusion limitations [CS2/SS2; Reasonable Inference]. K and Bi melting should improve transport, while Ag remains a relatively dense metallic phase until near 962 °C [Reasonable Inference]. Structural transfer is only partial because K4ZnBi2 is not Cmcm K2MBi, so the route receives a lower structural reconstruction score than Route 1 [CS2/SS2].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized most strongly because K self-flux uses excess reactive/volatile K and requires high-vacuum distillation after crystallization [SS4/SS5]. The temperature itself is moderate, around 950 K / 677 °C in the analogues [SS4/SS5]. The method is operationally more complex than stoichiometric direct reaction, but it may be valuable for crystal growth.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth improves precursor activity and diffusion by using a molten K-rich medium [SS4/SS5; Reasonable Inference]. K-rich routes have successfully formed K–coinage-metal–pnictogen frameworks such as K5CuAs2 and K5AuAs2 [SS4/SS5]. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: flux conditions may favor K-rich competing phases rather than stoichiometric K2AgBi [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has strong size/coordination contrast between large K environments and compact Ag–Bi bonding, and the structure includes mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity [Target]. These features increase the chance of disorder, glassy intermediates, or competing local arrangements [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS/SS analogues with related K–pnictogen frameworks are crystalline and experimentally accessible [CS1/SS1/SS3], reducing concern. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for amorphization in this structure type, so it is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2AgBi is likely a marginally metastable or borderline-stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target], while the closest K2AuBi analogue is a ground-state phase [CS1/SS1]. The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis because K2AuBi has the same Cmcm structure type and very similar K–Bi and M–Bi coordination [CS1/SS1], and K2AuSb shows the same structural family is experimentally accessible [SS3]. TS evidence is supportive only at the general level: compounds with comparable E_hull values can be synthesized by sealed direct reactions, but TS does not directly address K–Ag–Bi chemistry [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a direct isostructural K2AuBi synthesis route in welded Nb ampoules [CS1/SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control involving volatile, air-sensitive K and possible competing K–Bi or Ag–Bi phases [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed ampoule requirements in CS1/SS1/CS2/SS2].
