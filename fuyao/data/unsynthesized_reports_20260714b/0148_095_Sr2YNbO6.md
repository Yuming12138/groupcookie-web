### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. The identical Sr₂YNbO₆ composition in CS1/SS1 is reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. TS analogues with E_hull values around 0.054–0.067 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, showing that slightly positive-hull oxide phases can still be made under ceramic conditions (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether the triclinic P-1 polymorph is the equilibrium form or a slightly metastable distortion relative to the monoclinic P2₁/c phase in CS1/SS1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild synthesis severity because the identical compound is prepared at 1573 K without reported special atmosphere, high pressure, or exotic equipment (CS1, SS1). The kinetic score is moderate rather than high because YNbO₄ is a stable oxide precursor and Y/Nb diffusion in ceramic solids is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS1/SS1 directly demonstrate that this precursor pair forms Sr₂YNbO₆, so the route is experimentally well supported. The remaining uncertainty is the missing reported hold time in CS1/SS1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 uses common oxide/carbonate precursors and ambient-pressure furnace conditions, consistent with related Sr niobate and double-perovskite syntheses (CS5, TS1). Its F2 score is lower than Route 1 because it likely requires at least calcination, regrinding, pelletizing, and final firing, as in CS5 and TS1. Its F3 score is the lowest because starting from separate Y₂O₃ and Nb₂O₅ requires long-range diffusion and full formation of the ordered Sr₂YNbO₆ framework [Reasonable Inference supported by multi-step behavior in CS2/CS5]. The uncertainty is whether intermediate YNbO₄ formation helps the reaction or traps the system in secondary phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has greater condition severity because SS2 reports a Pechini route for Sr₂LuNbO₆ requiring organic processing, burn-out, and firing at 1773 K for 60 h. Its kinetic score is better than Route 2 because Pechini chemistry gives more homogeneous cation mixing, as shown by the polymeric complex route in SS2. The route is still not kinetically trivial because crystallization of ordered double perovskites from amorphous precursors can require high-temperature annealing, and SS2 used long firing. The main uncertainty is Nb precursor chemistry, since NbCl₅ is moisture-sensitive and solution speciation can complicate stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger because the Sr/Y/Nb cations differ substantially in size and charge [Reasonable Inference], and the structure contains clearly distinct A-site and B-site cations (Target, CS1/SS1). The ordered double-perovskite framework is nevertheless based on corner-sharing octahedra, which is a robust oxide connectivity motif in the CS/SS analogues (CS1, SS1–SS5). There is insufficient evidence from TS specifically on glass-forming or amorphization behavior, so TS is neutral for this factor. The remaining uncertainty is whether the triclinic distortion increases configurational complexity relative to the monoclinic analogue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂YNbO₆ is likely synthesizable as a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable oxide phase, because the target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom and the identical composition in CS1/SS1 is reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis because they include the same Sr₂YNbO₆ chemistry and nearly the same ordered double-perovskite structure, while TS provides broader support that related positive-hull niobate oxides are experimentally accessible. The most important enabling factor is the availability of a direct precursor route using preformed YNbO₄ plus SrCO₃, which reduces cation-distribution complexity relative to separate Y₂O₃/Nb₂O₅ oxides (CS1, SS1). The main limiting factor is slow high-temperature ordering and diffusion in a refractory Y/Nb oxide framework, especially if the desired product is specifically the triclinic P-1 polymorph rather than the more commonly reported monoclinic P2₁/c phase [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1]. Overall, the material should be accessible by conventional ceramic synthesis with careful grinding, pelletization, and annealing.
