### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0190 eV/atom, which is well within the generally synthesizable metastability window and scores 95/100 by the required rubric. Exact-composition InGaTe₂ appears in the chemically and thermodynamically similar sets with low hull energy and experimental synthesis by direct solid-state reaction (CS1; TS2). Structurally similar tellurides such as GaTe and AgIn₅Te₈ also have low hull energies and experimental routes (SS1; SS2). Remaining uncertainty is that CS1/TS2 report a tetragonal InGaTe₂ polymorph, not the target trigonal P3m1 polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate because CS1/TS2 use only 550–650 °C but require high vacuum, sealed ampoules, and several-week anneals. Kinetics are helped by low-melting In and Ga and by the demonstrated exact-composition synthesis (CS1; TS2), but phase ordering is slow and phase purity is nontrivial according to CS1/TS2. Structurally, the target’s tetrahedral layered motif is supported by SS1/SS2, but the exact known InGaTe₂ analogue is tetragonal, so polymorph selection is uncertain. Insufficient evidence from SS alone proves P3m1 InGaTe₂ can be obtained by this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Melt growth / Bridgman

Melt growth has more severe thermal demands because SS2 uses 1050 °C direct fusion and CS4 uses up to 1150 °C, although GaTe melts/anneals in evacuated ampoules at 1200 K in SS1. Kinetics are favorable because liquid homogenization reduces cation diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and related tellurides crystallize from melts (CS4; SS1; SS2). The limitation is that controlled cooling may favor the most stable or most easily nucleated polymorph, likely the known tetragonal InGaTe₂ seen in CS1/TS2 rather than the trigonal sheet phase. Insufficient evidence from TS distinguishes whether melt cooling favors the target polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT

CVT is supported for related tellurides: iodine-assisted growth of CuGaTe₂ at 650–840 °C for two weeks is reported in CS3, and AlCl₃-assisted CVT improved single-phase crystal growth for Mn–In chalcogenides in SS4. The severity score is reduced by the need for sealed evacuated tubes, corrosive/volatile transport agents, and multistep feed preparation. Kinetics for crystal growth are plausible, but composition control is risky because CS3 reports spurious phase formation and narrow stability issues. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows CVT stabilizes P3m1 InGaTe₂ rather than merely recrystallizing another polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Mechanochemical alloying + anneal

Mechanochemical synthesis has mild temperature requirements and TS3 shows that InTe can form from elemental In and Te powders under Ar at room temperature. Kinetics are promising because ball milling increases precursor activity and shortens diffusion distances [TS3; Reasonable Inference]. However, TS3 also reports multiple InTe phases and minor oxide, suggesting phase selectivity is not automatic. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS proves ternary InGaTe₂, especially the P3m1 polymorph, forms mechanochemically.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: High-pressure polymorph conversion

High-pressure conversion is directly supported for several similar tellurides: MnIn₂Te₄-II at 1.5 GPa and 1073 K (SS3; TS1), MnGa₂Te₄-II at 15–30 kbar and 800 °C with quenching (TS4), and HgIn₂Te₄ at 65 kbar and 400 °C (SS5). The numerical score is competitive because polymorph conversion from a preformed precursor can be kinetically efficient. The major physicochemical concern is that pressure usually favors denser structures, whereas the target is a 2D sheet; this conflicts with the target topology and reduces practical confidence [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS1 and TS4 dense HP phases]. Insufficient evidence from CS shows InGaTe₂ requires or benefits from high pressure.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant In/Ga size mismatch, giving one amorphization/structural-disorder trigger [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii]. The small formula and corner-sharing tetrahedral network reduce amorphization risk relative to large, mixed-connectivity frameworks; target description does not indicate edge/face-sharing coexistence. SS1 shows layered GaTe with similar tetrahedral sheet motifs can crystallize, supporting low-to-medium amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is possible cation disorder between In and Ga during rapid cooling, which CS1/TS2 phase-control comments indirectly support.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target InGaTe₂ with E_hull = 0.019 eV/atom is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than clearly proven ground-state, because exact-composition InGaTe₂ is reported experimentally in a different tetragonal structure (CS1; TS2). The analogue support is strong chemically, since the exact composition is experimentally accessible by sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction (CS1; TS2), and it is also reasonable structurally because layered/tetrahedral tellurides such as GaTe and AgIn₅Te₈ are known (SS1; SS2). The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature, sealed-ampoule chemistry of soft In/Ga/Te elements, which allows long annealing without extreme equipment (CS1; TS2). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the target trigonal P3m1 sheet must be stabilized against the known tetragonal InGaTe₂ polymorph and other binary/ternary tellurides (CS1; TS2). Slow annealing, careful Te stoichiometry, and cooling/quench screening are therefore essential.
