### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0090 eV/atom, which is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom highly synthesizable range specified in the rubric. The closest Sn–F–I analogue Sn₂F₃I is also near the hull at 0.0036 eV/atom (SS1, TS1), supporting that Sn–F–I chemistry can form thermodynamically accessible crystalline phases. Related tin(II) halides such as SnBr₂ are also near-hull (SS4, TS3). CS provides insufficient direct Sn–F–I thermodynamic evidence, so its signal is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because Sn₂F₃I is made from hot aqueous SnF₂ + HI followed by cooling, with no high pressure, vacuum, or extreme temperature (SS1, TS1). SnF₂ itself crystallizes by cooling from 50 to 25 °C (SS2), reinforcing mild condition severity. The only significant penalty is chemical handling: HI is corrosive and Sn(II) salts are oxidation/hydrolysis-prone [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether SnIF needs more acidic/iodide-rich conditions than Sn₂F₃I.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The solution route should have fast kinetics because the closest analogue uses dissolved SnF₂ and HI (SS1/TS1), avoiding solid-state diffusion. Structural assembly is favorable because Sn₂F₃I already contains 2D Sn–F motifs and iodide-containing Sn chemistry (SS1/TS1). The uncertainty is stoichiometric selectivity: Sn₂F₃I is F-rich relative to SnIF, so the reaction may need higher iodide activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Room-temperature precipitation is mild because SnICl is formed at 293 K by dropwise HI addition to aqueous SnCl₂ (SS3). No high pressure, high temperature, or specialized equipment is reported for SnICl (SS3), and Sn₂F₃I also forms under simple aqueous conditions (SS1/TS1). The severity penalty again comes mainly from HI and Sn(II) chemical sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Precipitation/metathesis should be kinetically favorable because SnICl forms at room temperature from aqueous species (SS3), and Sn₂F₃I forms from aqueous SnF₂/HI (SS1/TS1). However, SS3 explicitly indicates phase outcome depends on HI amount, with mixed phases possible. The target may similarly compete with Sn₂F₃I, SnF₂, or SnI₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The sealed-tube solid-state route is more demanding than solution routes because it requires dry grinding and vacuum/Ar ampoule sealing, similar to binary-halide ampoule syntheses (TS2, TS4). The temperature can likely remain modest around 150–220 °C, supported by Sn₂OF₂ formation at 200 °C in a sealed tube (SS5). Volatile halide retention and air-sensitive Sn(II) halides justify deductions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are better than typical oxide solid-state reactions because SnF₂/SnI₂ are soft halides, and analogous halide materials are synthesized from binary halides in evacuated ampoules (TS2, TS4). Sn₂OF₂ formation at 200 °C/24 h from SnO + SnF₂ suggests Sn(II) fluoride-containing phases can form at low temperature (SS5). The uncertainty is whether SnF₂ and SnI₂ interdiffuse and order into the 2D SnIF phase rather than segregating [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Vapor transport requires sealed vacuum/Ar ampoules and temperature gradients, as shown for SnBr₂ (SS4, TS3) and vacuum sublimation for GeF₂ (TS5). Even if a lower temperature than SnBr₂ is chosen, vapor methods impose sealed-system and composition-control complexity. The route therefore receives substantial condition penalties. CS offers insufficient direct vapor evidence for SnIF; neutral CS signal.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Vapor availability is plausible because SnBr₂ can be transported (SS4/TS3) and GeF₂ can sublime under vacuum (TS5). However, F- and I-containing tin species likely have different volatilities, making Sn:F:I control difficult [Reasonable Inference]. No provided source confirms direct vapor crystallization of SnIF, so the crystallization pathway is uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 5 condition severity

Hydrothermal/sealed-solution growth is moderately mild: CS2 uses 210 °C sealed-tube conditions for CdOCl crystals, and SS5 uses 200 °C sealed-tube synthesis for Sn₂OF₂. For SnIF, lower temperatures are recommended to limit halide volatility and Sn(II) decomposition [Reasonable Inference]. The penalties come from sealed volatile HI/iodide medium and toxic/corrosive precursors.

#### F3 — Route 5 reaction kinetics

Solution-mediated transport should be kinetically favorable, as shown by aqueous Sn₂F₃I formation (SS1/TS1) and hydrothermal CdOCl crystal growth (CS2). The sealed environment may improve crystallinity and retain volatile iodide [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is hydrolysis/oxide impurity formation, since CS hydrothermal evidence is oxyhalide-based rather than Sn–F–I-specific.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SnIF is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0090 eV/atom, and the closest Sn–F–I analogue Sn₂F₃I is also nearly on the hull (SS1, TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/TS, especially direct aqueous SnF₂ + HI crystallization of Sn₂F₃I and room-temperature SnICl precipitation from SnCl₂ + HI (SS1, SS3, TS1). CS evidence is weaker because most chemically similar examples are oxyhalides or unrelated deposition cases, so CS is mostly neutral except for supporting general sealed-solution/hydrothermal feasibility (CS2). The key enabling factor is the high reactivity of Sn(II) halides in mild aqueous solution, allowing crystallization without high-temperature diffusion (SS1–SS3). The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: SnF₂ + HI is known to produce F-rich Sn₂F₃I, so SnIF may require careful iodide activity and stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1]. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with solution crystallization the preferred first attempt.
