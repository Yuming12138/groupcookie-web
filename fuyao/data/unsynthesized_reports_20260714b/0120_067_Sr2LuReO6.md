### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold specified in the rubric. CS analogues Ba₂CaOsO₆, Ba₂CaReO₆, and Ba₂YReO₆ are at or essentially on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS #1–4), while TS analogues with similar or slightly higher metastability, including LiLa₂ReO₆ at 0.062 eV/atom and YTiO₃ at 0.058 eV/atom, are synthesized (TS #1, TS #5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee the specific monoclinic polymorph, but the thermodynamic penalty is low.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits high-temperature, controlled-atmosphere, multi-step features from Ba₂YReO₆/LiLa₂ReO₆ synthesis in Ar and Ba₂CaReO₆ sealed/evacuated routes (CS #2–4; SS #1–2; TS #1). Re/ReO₃ handling and possible Re oxide volatility impose additional severity, consistent with the need for sealed or controlled conditions in Re/Os analogues (CS #1–4). Insufficient evidence from TS for the exact Sr₂LuReO₆ temperature window, so the score includes uncertainty and is penalized conservatively.

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

The route uses refractory Lu₂O₃ and Re-containing powders, and analogous Re double perovskites require multi-stage firing and regrinding (CS #4; SS #1–2; TS #1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target and analogues are all perovskite-derived double perovskites (CS #2–4; SS #1–3), but B-site ordering and cation diffusion are slow in ceramic routes. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a low-temperature reactive precursor set such as nitrates/sol-gel, so precursor activity is scored low-to-moderate.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from the closest Sr-based structural analogue, Sr₂YOsO₆, synthesized at only 950–960 °C in sealed quartz under Ar (SS #3), avoiding >1000 °C and high pressure. However, sealed-tube handling, oxidizing SrO₂, toxic Re oxide, and multiple regrinding/firing operations add moderate severity (SS #3; CS #2–4). Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for the exact SrO₂/ReO₂ route to Re(V), so oxygen-potential control remains an uncertainty.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

SrO₂ may improve reactivity and oxygen supply relative to SrCO₃, supported by the successful Sr₂YOsO₆ route using SrO₂ (SS #3). The long ~100 h reaction and intermittent regrinding in SS #3 indicate slow diffusion/order kinetics, especially relevant for Lu/Re B-site ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The close structural match to the target improves the structural reconstruction score, but Re–Os substitution and Re(V) stabilization are not directly proven by SS #3.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

The flux route has mild temperature and ambient pressure because Ba₂NaOsO₆ was grown at 600 °C for 24 h in air (CS #5). Severity comes instead from caustic hydroxide fluxes, toxic Re/Os-type precursors, washing steps, and likely covered containment to suppress volatile high-valent Re species [Reasonable Inference from CS #1–5]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for hydroxide flux growth of Sr₂LuReO₆ specifically, so the route is scored with moderate uncertainty.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

Molten hydroxide flux can greatly improve cation transport, as shown by single-crystal growth of Ba₂NaOsO₆ at 600 °C (CS #5), so ion diffusion is less limiting than in dry ceramic routes. However, the specific target includes Lu³⁺ and Re⁵⁺ in a tilted Sr double perovskite, while the flux example is Ba/Na/Os and cubic (CS #5), so structural selectivity and Lu dissolution are uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for analogous flux growth lowers the confidence.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has strong cation size/coordination contrast among Sr, Lu, and Re, supported by the target bond lengths and standard ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target structure is a crystalline corner-sharing double perovskite; the supplied target description does not show mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity or a >40-atom unit cell. CS/SS analogues demonstrate that similarly distorted perovskite-derived oxides crystallize successfully (SS #2–3; TS #1), so amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LuReO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable threshold, and TS analogues with similar metastability are synthesized (TS #1, TS #3, TS #5). The CS and SS data strongly support the existence of Re/Os double perovskite chemistry under solid-state conditions, especially Ba₂YReO₆, Ba₂CaReO₆, LiLa₂ReO₆, and Sr₂YOsO₆ (CS #2–4; SS #1–3). The most critical enabling factor is the robust double-perovskite framework tolerance for ReO₆–B′O₆ corner-sharing networks, shown across CS/SS analogues. The most critical limiting factor is redox/volatility control for Re(V), because Re/Os analogues use sealed, inert, or carefully controlled conditions and sometimes suffer volatile metal oxide loss (CS #1–4; SS #3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful oxygen chemical potential and repeated annealing or a flux-mediated route.
