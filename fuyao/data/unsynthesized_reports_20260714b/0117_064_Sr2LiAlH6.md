### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is exactly at the threshold where the required rubric assigns 90–100, so a conservative score of 90 is used. Chemically similar hydrides in CS1–CS5 are mostly reported at E_hull = 0.0, supporting the feasibility of related hydride phases. Structurally similar SrMgH₄ and Sr₂Mg₃H₁₀ are at or very near the hull (SS2, SS4), while thermodynamic analogues up to ~0.08 eV/atom are synthesized under pressure (TS1–TS4). The uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee isolation of the specific low-symmetry LiSr₂AlH₆ phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because CS1 required high H₂ pressure and strict Ar handling, while SS2/SS4 also required high-pressure H₂ autoclaves and glovebox handling. The proposed temperature is moderate, so no high-temperature deduction applies, but sealed H₂ operation and air-sensitive hydrides are significant hazards. The route is chemically well motivated by CS1, but its operational difficulty remains high. Insufficient evidence from TS for a lower-pressure Li–Sr–Al hydride route; TS is therefore neutral for lowering this severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory solid-state chemistry because LiAlH₄/SrH₂-type hydrides are reactive and CS1 formed K₂LiAlH₆ at only 320–330 °C. Sr hydrides can also form by autoclave hydrogenation in SS2/SS4, supporting cation/hydride mobility under H₂. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural ordering: LiSr₂AlH₆ is triclinic P1, whereas CS1 is trigonal and SS2/SS4 are higher-symmetry Sr–Mg hydrides. TS provides insufficient specific kinetic evidence for Li–Sr–Al–H and is treated as neutral here.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids GPa pressure and very high temperature, following CS5 where ball milling plus 50 bar H₂ annealing at 315 °C gave a complex hydride. It still requires controlled Ar/H₂ atmospheres and air-sensitive hydride precursors, supported by CS5 and CS1. The main severity advantage is that it uses common mechanochemical/autoclave equipment rather than multi-anvil equipment. SS and TS do not directly demonstrate mechanochemical Li–Sr–Al hydride synthesis, so their signal is weaker for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Mechanochemical activation gives the best kinetic score because CS5 shows ball milling can form a multication hydride after a relatively low-temperature anneal. CS1 also supports Li–Al hydride formation in the same temperature range, suggesting Li/Al/H rearrangement is plausible. The uncertainty is phase purity: CS5 still reported impurities such as MgNi₂ and NaMgH₃, so competing phases are likely. The low-symmetry target may require optimization of milling energy, anneal time, and H₂ pressure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is operationally challenging because SS1/SS3 require multi-anvil cells, BN/graphite assemblies, controlled heating, and pressure release. Although the proposed pressure is below the rubric’s >5 GPa deduction, it is still specialized. Air-sensitive binary hydrides and multistep capsule preparation are directly supported by SS1/SS3. TS1–TS4 reinforce that thermodynamically similar hydrides often need high-pressure apparatus.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can overcome kinetic barriers and stabilize dense hydride phases, as shown for Eu–Mg hydrides in SS1/SS3 and Mg-based thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS3. However, SS1/SS3 explicitly report multiple ternary phases, pressure/temperature gradients, and difficulty obtaining pure phases, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. For LiSr₂AlH₆, the route may stabilize the target but may also trap mixtures or metastable polymorphs. CS provides insufficient evidence that a multi-anvil route is needed for Li–Al complex hydrides, since CS1 succeeded in an autoclave.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The cation size/charge contrast among Li, Sr, and Al is large [Reasonable Inference], so one amorphization/structural-disorder trigger is counted. The target description does not provide evidence for a >40-atom unit cell, and it does not explicitly describe simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so those checks are not triggered. CS1, SS2, and SS4 show mixed-cation hydrides with varied coordination can crystallize, which reduces concern. Remaining uncertainty is that P1 symmetry may reflect many local distortions that could broaden diffraction peaks or cause partial disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiSr₂AlH₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable hydride rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The synthesis case is reasonably supported: CS1 directly supports Li–Al complex hydride formation under high H₂ pressure, while SS2/SS4 support Sr hydride frameworks under autoclave H₂ conditions. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high hydrogen chemical potential during formation, as shown by CS1, CS5, SS2, and SS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and low-symmetry ordering, because analogous Sr/Mg and Eu/Mg hydride systems often show impurities or multiple phases (SS1–SS4). The best practical route is mechanochemical activation followed by H₂ annealing, because it offers good kinetics without requiring GPa pressure.
