### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.043 eV/atom is below 0.05 eV/atom, so it lies in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic bracket by the required rubric. Exact-composition CsBr is very close to the hull in the reference data, E_hull = 0.0051 eV/atom (CS1, SS1), and thermodynamically similar RbBr is also modestly metastable at E_hull = 0.0316 eV/atom (TS1). The remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: CS/SS/TS support CsBr chemistry strongly, but they do not directly prove the P4/mmm 2D CsBr phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires vacuum and cryogenic/low-temperature substrate control, which is explicitly used for CsBr and related alkali halides (CS1, SS1, TS1). It does not require high temperature or high pressure, which keeps severity moderate. The main severity penalty comes from vacuum operation and the multi-step deposition/warming procedure (CS1/SS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no toxic precursor penalty for CsBr itself.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CsBr vapor availability is excellent because CsBr is directly evaporated in the exact-composition analogue (CS1, SS1). Crystallization is less certain because the references describe temperature-sensitive abnormal phases and phase transitions upon warming (CS2, CS4, SS2, TS1). Composition control is easier than in multication halides because the target is binary [Reasonable Inference], but polymorph control is the kinetic bottleneck.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and standard ampoule methods, supported by bromide and halide analogues made in evacuated or Ar-sealed ampoules (SS4, SS5, TS5). It is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed-system operation, and multiple handling/heating/cooling steps. It avoids extreme pressure and very high temperature, unlike the ammonothermal high-pressure analogues in CS3/TS4, which are not necessary for CsBr.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Binary halide precursors are generally reactive in ampoule synthesis, and the reference Cs–Ag–Br/Cs–Pb–Br systems use simple halides effectively (SS4, SS5, TS5). Diffusion should be favorable because alkali halides are not refractory and analogous reactions occur at moderate temperatures (SS4, TS5). The low structural-reconstruction score reflects that bulk recrystallization is likely to return normal CsBr polymorphs rather than the target 2D P4/mmm polymorph; exact CsBr evidence reports cubic rocksalt, not the target (CS1, SS1).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check does not trigger. The described P4/mmm structure appears to have a small number of inequivalent sites rather than a >40 atom cell [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The polyhedral network includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered; however, low-temperature deposited alkali halides can still show diffuse or metastable patterns, so experimental nanocrystallinity remains possible (CS4/SS2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CsBr P4/mmm phase is likely a metastable polymorph rather than the bulk ground state because its E_hull is 0.043 eV/atom and exact CsBr references report a different cubic rocksalt structure (CS1, SS1). The CS and SS evidence strongly support the chemical feasibility of CsBr preparation and strongly support low-temperature vapor deposition as a route to nonstandard alkali-halide polymorphs (CS1, SS1, TS1). The TS evidence supports the idea that similarly metastable alkali halides can be accessed by controlled low-temperature deposition (TS1). The most critical enabling factor is kinetic trapping during low-temperature deposition on an amorphous or weakly interacting substrate (CS1, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is selective stabilization of the 2D tetragonal P4/mmm polymorph rather than relaxation to ordinary cubic CsBr during warming or crystallization (CS1, SS1, CS4, SS2, TS1).
