### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the predicted convex hull and giving F1 = 98. Stable analogues with E_hull = 0 include CuS [CS3; SS4; TS2], NaCu₄S₄ [SS3; TS1], Cu₁₂Sb₄S₁₃ [TS3], and Cu₄TiS₄ [TS5], so CS/SS/TS collectively support that Cu-containing sulfides can be thermodynamically stable. The main uncertainty is that a zero calculated hull energy does not guarantee a wide experimental stability field, especially for Cu–S phases with competing compositions [SS2; SS3]. CS1 gives insufficient useful signal because its synthesis details appear to describe FeSe₂ rather than Cu₉S₅, so CS1 is treated as neutral 50 for transferability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

F2 is moderate because the route uses vacuum-sealed tubes and volatile sulfur, both directly indicated by CuS synthesis [CS3; SS4; TS2]. The temperature is not severe because CuS was synthesized at 620–670 K, far below 1000 °C [CS3; SS4; TS2]. F3 is only moderate because dense Cu metal has limited solid-state reactivity, although sulfur melt/vapor transport improves kinetics as shown by direct CuS formation [CS3; SS4; TS2]. Remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: Cu–S has several stable competing binaries, and analogues such as AgCuS report impurity formation [SS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary-sulfide annealing

F2 is better than direct elemental reaction because the base route can avoid free volatile sulfur by using Cu₂S + CuS, while still using a sealed evacuated ampoule as in AgCuS synthesis [SS2]. F3 is favorable because AgCuS formed from binary sulfides at only 300 °C over 50 h [SS2], and Cu₈GeS₆ formed from binary Cu₂X-type precursors at 500 °C [SS5]. The structural reconstruction score is high but not perfect because the target P-6m2 sheet is not identical to CuS/covellite or AgCuS structures [SS2; SS4; TS2]. The main uncertainty is whether Cu₂S + CuS equilibrates to the target rather than simply remaining a two-phase mixture [Reasonable Inference from phase competition in SS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / solvothermal synthesis

F2 is high because hydrothermal analogues use mild temperatures of 120 °C for Ni₃S₄ [CS2] and 413 K for NaCu₅S₃ [SS1], without vacuum or high pressure beyond autogenous autoclave pressure [CS2; SS1]. F3 is also high because solution-mediated sulfide transport overcomes slow solid-state diffusion, and NaCu₅S₃ demonstrates formation of Cu–S frameworks from Cu powder and Na₂S·9H₂O [SS1]. The key uncertainty is chemical specificity: the closest Cu–S hydrothermal analogue includes Na and shows limited atmospheric stability [SS1]. Therefore, the hydrothermal score is adjusted downward in the final expert score despite excellent mild-condition metrics.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Alkali/polysulfide flux growth

F2 is lower because flux growth uses vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, air/moisture-sensitive sulfides, slow cooling, and flux-removal steps [SS3; TS1]. F3 is strong because molten polysulfide flux enhances diffusion and crystallization, and NaCu₄S₄ was grown at 500 °C over 4 d [SS3; TS1]. The limiting uncertainty is that NaCu₄S₄ synthesis required a specific Na/Ba/S flux and reportedly was not obtained from other flux reactions [SS3; TS1], suggesting narrow phase selectivity. For the binary target, alkali incorporation from flux is a major risk [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS1 composition].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Cu, so there is no cation radius mismatch trigger; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct target-specific ionic-radius evidence, so this subcheck is neutral 50 from source evidence and resolved by target composition. The P-6m2 target description is structurally simple, with two inequivalent Cu sites and one S site, so the >40 atom-cell trigger is not supported by the target description; CS/SS/TS do not directly provide the target atom count, so they are neutral 50 for this subcheck. The target reports corner-sharing SCu₄ trigonal pyramids rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, unlike some more complex analogues [SS3; SS4; TS1; TS2]. Thus F4 = 95, with low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Cu-containing sulfide analogues in CS/SS/TS are also on the hull [CS3; SS3; SS4; TS1; TS2; TS3; TS5]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well: direct Cu–S sealed-tube synthesis is strongly supported by CuS [CS3; SS4; TS2], hydrothermal assembly of related Cu–S frameworks is supported by NaCu₅S₃ [SS1], and binary-sulfide annealing is supported by AgCuS [SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of sulfide chemistry at low to moderate temperature, especially via hydrothermal solution transport or polysulfide/volatile sulfur transport [CS2; SS1; SS3; TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among nearby Cu–S compositions and possible impurity persistence, as seen for AgCuS with elemental Ag impurity and flux-specific NaCu₄S₄ formation [SS2; SS3; TS1]. Overall, the material is highly plausible experimentally, but pure-phase isolation may require careful Cu:S chemical potential control.

| 1 | All | Competing Cu–S phases such as CuS/Cu₂S may form instead of the target Cu₃S₂ composition. | High | Use preformed Cu₂S + CuS at exact stoichiometry as in binary-sulfide routes [SS2], or tune sulfur activity in sealed tubes as done for CuS [CS3; SS4; TS2]. |
| 2 | Route 1 | Sulfur volatility and pressure buildup in sealed ampoules. | High | Use evacuated silica/vitreous silica tubes and staged heating; sulfur delivery by inclined/constricted tube is supported for CuS [CS3; SS4; TS2]. |
| 3 | Route 1 / Route 2 | Slow ordering or incomplete interdiffusion of Cu–S phases. | Medium | Use fine grinding, pelletizing, intermediate grinding, and reannealing; analogous long anneals/regrinding were used for Cu–Sb–S and Cu–Ti–S phases [TS3; TS5]. |
| 4 | Route 3 | Na incorporation from Na₂S mineralizer, producing Na–Cu–S phases rather than binary Cu–S. | High | Reduce Na₂S concentration, use soluble Cu salts with controlled sulfide release, or replace Na₂S partly with thiourea/thioacetamide by [Reasonable Inference]; Na incorporation risk is indicated by NaCu₅S₃ formation [SS1]. |
| 5 | Route 3 | Air/solution instability of low-temperature Cu sulfide crystals. | Medium | Wash and dry under N₂ or inert atmosphere; limited air stability was reported for NaCu₅S₃ crystals [SS1]. |
| 6 | Route 4 | Alkali or Ba contamination from polysulfide flux. | High | Use minimal flux, screen alkali-free or removable fluxes, and wash under N₂; Na/Ba/S flux and inert washing were used for NaCu₄S₄ [SS3; TS1]. |
| 7 | Route 4 | Narrow crystallization window / flux specificity. | Medium–High | Reproduce slow cooling at ~4 °C h⁻¹ and 500 °C/4 d first, then vary flux ratios systematically; NaCu₄S₄ could not be obtained from other flux reactions [SS3; TS1]. |
| 8 | All | Stoichiometry control of Cu:S = 3:2. | High | Use high-purity precursors and sealed systems; direct CuS synthesis used high-purity Cu/S in evacuated tubes [CS3; SS4; TS2], and AgCuS used stoichiometric binary sulfides in evacuated ampoules [SS2]. |
