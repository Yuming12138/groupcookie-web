### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. Similar stable phases are abundant: Cd3PCl3 and Cd3AsCl3 are E_hull = 0 in CS-1 and CS-4, and Zr/Hf Hg chalcogenide chlorides are E_hull = 0 in SS-1/TS-1 and SS-4/TS-3. This supports a high score, but calculated hull stability does not guarantee that the P-31m polytype is kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 CVT

CVT analogues use moderate temperatures, sealed ampoules, vacuum, and gradients: 693 K/613–653 K for Cd3AsCl3 (CS-4), 693 K/673 K for Cd2AsCl2 (SS-5), and 360–475 °C for related 2D halides (SS-1, TS-1, TS-3). The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile halide transport, and Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any need for high pressure or >1000 °C.

#### F3 — Route 1 CVT

The kinetics are favorable because volatile halide species can transport material and grow crystals, as shown by Cd chloride CVT in CS-1, CS-4, and SS-5. Structurally similar 2D halide/chalcogenide sheets also form by CVT in SS-1 and TS-1. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS-1/TS-3/TS-5 report low yields and equilibrium mixtures in related systems, so pure AgCdCl3 may require screening.

#### F2 — Route 2 Melt/Solid-State

Melt growth is supported by TlCuCl3 synthesis at 450 °C under Ar with dehydration and Bridgman control (CS-2), and sealed vacuum solid-state synthesis is supported by CS-5 and TS-2. The route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure, but requires sealed/controlled atmosphere, drying, slow cooling, and toxic CdCl2 handling [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether AgCdCl3 melts congruently.

#### F3 — Route 2 Melt/Solid-State

If a chloride melt or partial melt forms, cation diffusion should be rapid [Reasonable Inference], and CS-2 demonstrates that ternary chlorides can crystallize from molten binary chlorides. However, structural ordering into the 2D AgCdCl3 P-31m sheet is not directly demonstrated in the analogues. Long time/temperature control in TS-2 and CS-5 suggests that crystal growth and phase purity may be slower than the simple chemistry implies.

#### F2 — Route 3 Solution

The solution route scores well because SS-2 reports room-temperature aqueous crystallization of a structurally similar 2D chloride, and CS-3 reports low-temperature solution crystallization at 80 °C followed by room-temperature growth. It avoids high temperature, vacuum, and sealed volatile halide hazards. The main deduction is toxicity of Cd salts [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/TS supports direct AgCdCl3 solution synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3 Solution

Solution kinetics are potentially excellent because dissolved ions and chloride complexes remove solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], consistent with SS-2 room-temperature crystallization. The weakness is structural reconstruction/phase selectivity: Ag(I) chloride solubility is limited unless excess Cl⁻ forms chloroargentate complexes [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderated despite mild conditions.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one significant amorphization trigger: Ag⁺ and Cd²⁺ likely differ in ionic radius by more than 15% depending coordination [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a large >40-atom unit cell, and it describes edge-sharing CdCl6 octahedra without simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS modifies this factor, so the assessment relies mainly on the target structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCdCl3 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically or thermodynamically similar halides are also on or near the hull (CS-1, CS-4, SS-1/TS-1, TS-2). The analogue support is strongest for sealed-ampoule CVT and moderate-temperature halide growth, because Cd chloride frameworks and 2D halide-related structures are repeatedly synthesized this way (CS-4, SS-5, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high chloride mobility/volatility or solution complexation, which can overcome solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-4, SS-2, TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially loss or excess CdCl2 in high-temperature routes and AgCl insolubility/speciation in solution routes [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is feasible, with solution crystallization worth screening first and CVT as the more evidence-backed fallback.
