### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the favorable threshold in the provided scoring table. Chemically similar oxides with E_hull values from 0.0 to ~0.026 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized [CS1–CS5], and thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.054–0.069 eV/atom are also synthesized [TS1–TS5]. This supports a high but not perfect score because the target is likely near the metastability boundary rather than an unambiguous ground state. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not capture oxygen redox/peroxide stability, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, ceramic route

The ceramic route has mild equipment demands because many analogues use furnaces, crucibles, air/O2, grinding, and sintering rather than high pressure [CS1, CS2, CS4, SS3, TS2, TS5]. The main severity penalty is multi-step processing, directly supported by repeated grinding/cycles in CS1, CS2, CS4, and TS4. Kinetics are weaker because oxide/carbonate diffusion is slow, and CS5 specifically reports that traditional solid-state synthesis of related Ln2MgIrO6 phases produced impurities/unreacted MgO. Uncertainty remains whether the target P1 phase can form before Bi loss or oxygen redox changes occur.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, nitrate/citrate sol–gel route

This route retains benign ambient pressure and moderate annealing temperatures while improving precursor homogeneity; nitrate decomposition and multistep calcination are supported by La2MgRhO6 [CS4] and Bi4Ti3O12 [TS4]. Its high F2 score reflects no high pressure, no sealed tube, and no required reducing atmosphere. Its F3 score is higher than Route 1 because nitrates/gel precursors are much more reactive, but ordering Sr/Mg/La/Bi into a low-symmetry P1 oxide still likely requires long annealing. The largest uncertainty is whether the target’s possible O–O/peroxide-like feature survives the final anneal.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, hydroxide flux route

Flux methods are well supported for Mg–lanthanide double perovskite growth at 700 °C [CS5] and La/Gd oxide crystallization in NaOH/KOH at 400–600 °C [SS4, SS5]. F2 is penalized for sealed silver-tube handling and post-growth flux removal, both explicitly present in CS5/SS5. F3 is favorable because the molten flux reduces diffusion barriers and can crystallize complex oxides far below refractory oxide melting points. The key uncertainty is compositional transferability: CS/SS/TS do not directly show Sr–Mg–La–Bi hydroxide flux synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, high-pressure route

High-pressure synthesis is structurally motivated by BiScO3, which requires 6 GPa and 1413 K [SS2]. F2 is strongly penalized because SS2 uses specialized belt-type high-pressure equipment and >5 GPa pressure. F3 is moderate because high pressure can stabilize distorted Bi oxide frameworks, but precursor diffusion and multication ordering remain nontrivial. This route is best viewed as a backup if ambient-pressure ceramic/flux routes fail.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a large alkaline-earth cation, small Mg, La, Bi, and six inequivalent oxygen sites, so cation-size mismatch is a real disorder/amorphization risk; analogous materials show very different coordination environments for large A-site and small B-site cations [CS4, CS5, TS2]. However, the target primitive description contains only 10 atoms, so the unit-cell-size trigger is not met. Mixed edge/face/vertex sharing is seen in some analogues [SS3, TS1], but the target description does not explicitly show all three, so that trigger is not applied. Overall F4 is low–medium risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrMgLaBiO6 is most likely a marginally metastable or near-threshold phase rather than a robust deep ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and TS analogues strongly support experimental access to multication oxides in this stability range by ceramic or nitrate-assisted methods [CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5], while SS2 warns that some distorted Bi oxides may need high pressure. The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive nitrate/gel chemistry to homogenize Sr, Mg, La, and Bi before crystallization [CS4, TS4]. The most important limiting factor is oxygen/Bi redox control, especially because the target structure contains a short O–O distance of 1.52 Å that may indicate peroxide-like bonding; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct precedent for that feature. A low-temperature flux route is a strong secondary option because it may reduce Bi volatility and preserve unusual oxygen configurations [CS5, SS4, SS5].
