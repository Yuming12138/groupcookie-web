### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0300 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability metastability window, giving 95/100 by the provided rubric. TS gives especially relevant support because AgTl2I3 has E_hull = 0.0272 eV/atom and was synthesized, and K6Cd4Sn3Se13 has E_hull = 0.0306 eV/atom and was synthesized (TS-2, TS-5). CS and SS also show many iodides at or near the hull, including NaInI4, KGaI4, TiI3, and CdI2 polytypes (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-4/SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target’s precise V2+/Ag+ ordering is not experimentally validated by the references.

#### F2 — Route 1, melt/Bridgman

The temperature severity is moderate because analogous ternary iodides crystallize between roughly 250–400 °C for TlFeI3/TlMnI3 and NaInI4, and KGaI4 is grown from 573 K (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5/SS-1/TS-1). The main deductions come from sealed dry handling, volatile iodide/I2 risk, air-sensitive V iodides, and likely multi-step grinding/annealing; moisture exclusion is explicitly emphasized for KGaI4 and TlFeI3/TlMnI3 analogues (CS-2, CS-5/SS-1/TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact melting behavior of VAgI3, so the temperature program is partly **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1, melt/Bridgman

Binary iodide melts are kinetically favorable compared with oxide solid-state reactions, as shown by successful NaInI4/KGaI4 slow-cooling and TlM I3 melt/anneal syntheses (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5/SS-1/TS-1). The diffusion score is high because a molten iodide medium can bypass refractory V metal diffusion, but only if VI2 or reactive vanadium iodide forms; this is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target’s 2D VI6/AgI3 framework is not identical to the 1D TlM I3 ribbons or AInI4/AGaI4 frameworks, although layered octahedral iodide sheets are supported by CdI2 analogues (SS-4/SS-5).

#### F2 — Route 2, CVT

CVT uses sealed evacuated ampoules and I2 transport, which directly triggers vacuum/controlled atmosphere and volatile precursor deductions (CS-4, SS-2, SS-3). The temperatures are below 1000 °C in the proposed adaptation, consistent with 400→275 °C NbOI3 and 500→180 °C Ta iodide synthesis, though TiI3 used a higher 923→823 K gradient (CS-4, SS-3, SS-2). Toxic/air-sensitive handling is likely for iodine and vanadium iodides, consistent with dry-box harvesting in Ta iodide chemistry (SS-3).

#### F3 — Route 2, CVT

Iodine-assisted vapor transport is well established for transition-metal iodides in the references, including TiI3, NbOI3, and Ta iodides (SS-2, CS-4, SS-3). However, TiI3 was obtained with TiI2 and Ti2O3 impurities, showing poor phase selectivity in at least one structurally similar case (SS-2). Composition control for VAgI3 is uncertain because AgI transport is less directly evidenced than transition-metal iodide transport, and the Ag-containing thermodynamic analogue used hydrothermal HI instead of CVT (TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3, HI solvothermal

The route is mild in temperature because AgTl2I3 was synthesized at 155–175 °C in aqueous HI (TS-2). It still requires a sealed corrosion-resistant pressure vessel and uses corrosive HI plus potentially air-sensitive VI2, causing deductions for volatile/sealed medium and hazardous precursors (TS-2 plus **[Reasonable Inference]** for VI2). There is insufficient evidence from CS and SS for hydrothermal V–Ag iodide synthesis, so the low-temperature severity does not guarantee feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 3, HI solvothermal

The solution pathway has favorable mass transport and strong Ag–I precedent because AgTl2I3 forms from TlI + AgI in HI (TS-2). However, no provided CS/SS/TS source demonstrates aqueous low-valent vanadium iodide framework formation, and V2+ oxidation/hydrolysis could block the target; that limitation is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore, despite high precursor mobility, structural assembly and redox control are the dominant kinetic uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger from cation size/coordination mismatch between V and Ag, based on the charge-balanced V2+/Ag+ assignment **[Reasonable Inference]**. The unit cell described for trigonal P-31m VAgI3 appears simple rather than >40 atoms, and the target description contains edge and corner sharing but no face sharing. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the target’s amorphization tendency, although layered CdI2 analogues show that 2D iodides can crystallize readily (SS-4/SS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VAgI3 is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.0300 eV/atom, but that value lies within a commonly synthesizable metastability window. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of ternary and layered iodides by sealed-ampoule melt growth, Bridgman growth, CVT, and HI hydrothermal routes, with the strongest practical support coming from melt-grown binary iodide mixtures (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of iodide melts or iodide-rich solutions, which allows crystallization at relatively low temperatures compared with oxide ceramics (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the correct V oxidation state and avoiding competing binary vanadium iodides or AgI-rich phases; related analogues show repeated annealing, impurity formation, twinning, or poor phase control (CS-5, SS-2, TS-2, TS-3). Overall, the material is reasonably synthesizable, but single-phase bulk material will likely require strict dry handling and careful iodine chemical potential control.
