### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0210 eV/atom places it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. [Target] Thermodynamic analogues cluster around the same metastability range, including TlCdI3 at 0.0167 eV/atom, AgTl2I3 at 0.0272 eV/atom, TlPbI3 at 0.0114 eV/atom, and TlMnI3 at 0.0170 eV/atom. [TS-1 / TS-2 / TS-3 / TS-4] Chemical analogues also include stable TlGaI4/TlAlI4 and near-stable TlPbI3, which supports the general stability of ternary iodides in this chemical space. [CS-2 / CS-5 / CS-3] Remaining uncertainty is that the exact CuPbI3 phase is slightly metastable and may compete with binary CuI + PbI2. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the most relevant TlPbI3 analogue uses evacuated quartz ampoules, Bridgman growth, slow cooling, and long annealing, which are controlled and multi-step conditions. [CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-3] Additional iodide analogues require dry glovebox/Ar handling and repeated annealing to achieve phase purity. [CS-1 / TS-4] The temperature is moderate rather than extreme, so no >1000 °C penalty applies. [CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-3] The main uncertainty is whether CuPbI3 melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically, which is not provided by CS/SS/TS. [Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary iodide melts are kinetically favorable relative to refractory solid-state oxide synthesis, and TlPbI3 was synthesized by direct melting of iodides. [CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-3] The target shares the PbI6 sheet motif with TlPbI3/PbI2-like structures, lowering the structural-reconstruction burden. [Target / CS-3 / SS-1 / SS-2] However, Cu must form trigonal-planar CuI3 coordination rather than Tl L-shaped coordination, so complete structural transfer is not guaranteed. [Target / CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-3]

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest thermal profile because CuTl2I3 and AgTl2I3 analogues form at 160–175 °C in HI-containing autoclaves. [SS-3 / TS-2] The route avoids vacuum ampoule melting and high temperature, but HI is volatile/corrosive and requires sealed pressure equipment. [SS-3 / TS-2] Toxic PbI2 and acid handling create a safety penalty. [Reasonable Inference] There is insufficient evidence from CS for hydrothermal Pb–Cu iodide synthesis specifically, so the CS signal for this route is neutral with respect to hydrothermal transferability. [Insufficient evidence from CS]

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution/HI transport should give high precursor activity and fast ion mobility, as shown by CuI and AgI reacting in concentrated HI to form ternary iodides. [SS-3 / TS-2] CuTl2I3 reportedly gives good-quality single crystals, supporting crystallization feasibility for Cu iodides. [SS-3] The limiting kinetic uncertainty is phase selectivity: AgTl2I3 exhibited twinning and low yield of untwinned crystals, and no source directly proves CuPbI3 formation. [TS-2; Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for CuPbI3]

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses low temperatures similar to TlGaI4/TlAlI4 sealed-ampoule syntheses, so the thermal penalty is low. [CS-2 / CS-5 / SS-5] It still requires sealed vacuum or inert conditions and glovebox-style handling, which were used in the halide analogues. Low-temperature sealed-tube iodide chemistry can give crystalline products in 48–96 h, indicating useful mobility in soft halide lattices. [CS-2 / CS-5 / SS-5] However, the closest low-temperature analogues are 0D TlGaI4/TlAlI4 clusters rather than a 2D Pb–Cu iodide sheet, so structural reconstruction may be more difficult.
