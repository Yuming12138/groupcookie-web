### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. Thermodynamically similar borate analogues are also frequently on the hull, including TS-1 through TS-5, which supports the idea that borate oxide networks can be thermodynamically stable. Insufficient evidence from CS for B₂O₃-specific thermodynamic stability because CS mostly contains SiO₂ and Al–O analogues rather than pure B₂O₃; CS is therefore neutral except for showing oxide polymorph accessibility. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: a 0 K hull result does not guarantee easy kinetic crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses ambient pressure and ordinary furnace temperatures, consistent with SS-1/TS-1, where H₃BO₃-containing mixtures are heated in air at 300, 500, and 810 °C. The target route is inferred to need lower temperatures than many multication borates because pure B₂O₃ forms directly by dehydration **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main deduction is for a multistep dehydration/annealing/cooling protocol, supported by multistep heating in SS-1/TS-1 and slow thermal control in CS-1. Insufficient evidence from TS/SS for exact pure B₂O₃ crystallization temperatures, so the score keeps some caution.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

H₃BO₃ is an active precursor because structurally similar and thermodynamically similar borates use it successfully (SS-1; TS-1; SS-3). However, converting a hydrated molecular precursor into an ordered 2D orthorhombic B₂O₃ network requires network reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**. Long heating in oxide/borate analogues, such as 48 h in SS-1/TS-1 and 7 days in CS-1, suggests ordering kinetics may be slow. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Pnc2-B₂O₃ formation, so the kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is based on flux and high-temperature solution growth analogues that use standard furnaces and Pt crucibles rather than extreme pressure equipment (SS-3, SS-4; TS-2). Although some analogues use 750–1000 °C (SS-3, SS-4; TS-2), a pure B₂O₃ self-flux route is inferred to operate lower because B₂O₃ is itself a low-melting oxide **[Reasonable Inference]**. Deductions are assigned for multistep slow cooling and for controlling volatile/hygroscopic B₂O₃ in a covered crucible **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS for B₂O₃ flux behavior specifically, so CS is mainly supportive only by analogy to oxide polymorph control.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The kinetic advantage of Route 2 is that a liquid/self-flux medium should bypass long-range solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with successful borate crystal growth from high-temperature solutions in SS-3 and SS-4/TS-2. B₂O₃ is already compositionally matched to the target, increasing precursor activity **[Reasonable Inference]**. The limiting kinetic issue is nucleation of a crystalline orthorhombic network instead of glass, and SS-3 shows that borate flux syntheses can yield impurities or fail by direct solid-state synthesis. Therefore Route 2 has the best F3 score but not an excellent one.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Hydrothermal analogues use 460 °C and ~200 atm for an Al–O material (CS-5) or 565–574 °C and 20–21 kpsi in sealed ampoules for hydrated borates (SS-5; TS-3; TS-5). These conditions are below the rubric’s >5 GPa high-pressure penalty but still experimentally demanding, requiring sealed pressure equipment. The route is multistep because hydrothermal crystallization must be followed by drying/dehydration to reach anhydrous B₂O₃ **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct hydrothermal anhydrous B₂O₃ synthesis makes this a backup route.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution transport in hydrothermal media should improve mass transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, and SS-5/TS-3/TS-5 demonstrate crystallization of borate frameworks at high hydrothermal temperature. However, those closest analogues are hydroxylated borates, not anhydrous B₂O₃, so the required post-dehydration step may reconstruct or collapse the framework **[Reasonable Inference]**. Phase control is also difficult in SS-5/TS-3/TS-5, where specific mineralizers and feedstocks determine product formation. This gives Route 3 only moderate kinetics despite good precursor reactivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

By the specified checks, the target triggers none: it has only B as a cation, no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell, and no described mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral connectivity. This formal F4 score is high. However, independent physicochemical knowledge indicates B₂O₃ is a strong glass former **[Reasonable Inference]**, and the need for slow cooling in borate/oxide analogues supports caution (CS-1; SS-3; SS-4; TS-2). Thus F4 is high by rubric but the expert adjustment penalizes glass-forming risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its E above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom in the provided target data, and several thermodynamically similar borates are also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The analogues support synthesis of BO₃-containing crystalline networks using ordinary solid-state, flux, and hydrothermal methods (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5), but none directly demonstrates the exact pure orthorhombic Pnc2 B₂O₃ phase, so there is residual uncertainty. The most critical enabling factor is the chemical simplicity of the target—only B and O are required—and the availability of H₃BO₃/B₂O₃ precursors used in related borates (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic crystallization versus glass formation, inferred from B₂O₃ chemistry and supported indirectly by the slow cooling/long annealing requirements in oxide and borate analogues (CS-1; SS-3; SS-4; TS-2). Overall, the material appears feasible, but careful dehydration, nucleation, and slow-cooling control will likely determine success.
