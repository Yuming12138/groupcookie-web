### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high-synthesizability threshold in the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with nearly identical hull energies are experimentally reported, including NaHgCl3 at 0.055 eV/atom (TS-1) and KFeBr3 at 0.052 eV/atom (TS-4), supporting that this metastability level is accessible. Chemical and structural analogues are even closer to the hull or stable, including CsAuBr3 at 0 eV/atom (CS-2), Cs3Bi2Br9 at 0 eV/atom (CS-3), and K2PdBr4 at 0 eV/atom (SS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly proves CsPtBr3 is the lowest-energy phase in the Cs–Pt–Br diagram; therefore the score is high but not perfect.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because chemically similar CsPbBr3 forms at 35 °C after aqueous dissolution at 80 °C (CS-1), Cs3Bi2Br9 forms by slow evaporation in HBr solution (CS-3), and K2PdBr4 grows from aqueous evaporation (SS-2). No high pressure or special atmosphere is required in the analogues (CS-1, CS-3, TS-1, SS-2). The main severity penalty is chemical hazard from Pt bromides/HBr, inferred from the need to handle heavy-metal halides and hydrohalic acids [Reasonable Inference], with analogous Hg systems demonstrating toxic-halide solution chemistry (TS-1, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is PtBr2 solubility and Pt(II) speciation in aqueous HBr.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization gives excellent ion transport, as shown by aqueous crystallization of CsPbBr3 (CS-1), Cs3Bi2Br9 (CS-3), NaHgCl3 (TS-1), and K2PdBr4 (SS-2). Precursor activity is high because CsBr and metal bromide complexes can dissolve or partially dissolve in bromide-rich acid [Reasonable Inference], analogous to HBr-mediated Pd halide synthesis (SS-1, SS-5). The kinetic risk is structural selectivity: d8 Pd/Pt bromides can form discrete square-planar salts like K2PdBr4 (SS-2) and Cs2Pd halide phases (SS-4, SS-5), so the polymeric CsPtBr3 motif may require careful composition control. This uncertainty keeps F3 below 90.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The solvothermal route is moderate because structurally similar Pd halides are made at only 423–450 K in sealed quartz ampoules (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5). It avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but sealed ampoules with hydrohalic acid/halogen components introduce pressure and handling constraints (SS-1, SS-4). Toxic/corrosive reagents are expected for PtBr2/HBr/possible Br2 [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the halogen/acid handling described in SS-1 and CS-2. Insufficient evidence from CS for Pt-specific solvothermal behavior is treated as neutral relative to the strong SS signal.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

SS-1, SS-4, and SS-5 show that Pd halide frameworks crystallize readily from hydrohalic acid sealed-ampoule conditions, so a Pt analogue should benefit from similar liquid-phase transport [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is low enough to avoid bulk Pt diffusion limits, and controlled cooling at 1 K/h is directly supported by SS-1. Phase selectivity is still uncertain because the structurally similar sources form A2PdX4/interhalogen-containing phases rather than ABX3 exactly (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). This route is kinetically strong but slightly less attractive than ambient evaporation because sealed acid pressure increases complexity.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The solid-state route is more severe than solution methods because it uses sealed evacuated or inert ampoules, as required for KFeBr3 at 873 K (TS-4) and CsAuBr3 at 630 °C (CS-2). The temperature remains below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies under the rubric. Vacuum/Ar sealing and possible volatile bromine control introduce deductions, supported by evacuated ampoules in TS-4 and sealed Br2-containing tubes in CS-2. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct solid-state CsPtBr3 formation is neutral but does not remove the condition penalties.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Binary bromides are more reactive than oxides, and KFeBr3 forms from KBr + FeBr2 in sealed ampoules (TS-4). However, solid-state Pt transport is expected to be slow because Pt is a refractory noble metal when not fully converted into mobile halide species [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is only partially favorable: KFeBr3 is ABBr3/Pnma (TS-4), but the target Pt environment is rectangular see-saw-like rather than FeBr6 octahedral (target structure versus TS-4). Therefore kinetics are moderate rather than easy.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

The flux route is supported by CsAuBr3 self-flux growth at 630 °C in sealed quartz tubes under Ar with Br2 handling (CS-2). Temperature remains moderate by the rubric, but volatile Br2 in a sealed tube and multi-operation flux handling cause deductions (CS-2). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsPtBr3 is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than an unequivocal deep ground-state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom from the target data. The CS analogues strongly support the general crystallizability of cesium bromide frameworks, especially CsPbBr3 by slow evaporation (CS-1) and CsAuBr3 by flux growth (CS-2), while the SS analogues support d8 metal bromide crystallization but also warn of competing A2MBr4-type products (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is high bromide activity in solution or flux, which promotes metal-bromide complex transport and crystallization, as shown in HBr-based Bi/Pd systems (CS-3, SS-1, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is Pt(II) phase selectivity: Pt may prefer discrete square-planar [PtBr4]2− salts rather than the ABBr3 network [Reasonable Inference based on SS-2/SS-4/SS-5]. Overall, the material appears reasonably synthesizable, with Route 1 or Route 2 preferred for screening and Route 4 preferred for larger single crystals.
