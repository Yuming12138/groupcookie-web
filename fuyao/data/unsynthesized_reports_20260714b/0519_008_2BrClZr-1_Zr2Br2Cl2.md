### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.024 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window [Target]. TS analogues include experimentally treated reduced halides with comparable E_hull values, such as ZrBr₂ at 0.0319 eV/atom and ScCl at 0.0214 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-5). SS also supports stability through ZrCl₂ at E_hull = 0 and ZrI₂ nearly on-hull (SS-1, SS-2). The uncertainty is that mixed Cl/Br ordering may be metastable relative to separated ZrCl₂/ZrBr₂ even if the total hull distance is small [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/SS-3].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and avoids high pressure, consistent with ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ syntheses at 600–850 °C (SS-1, SS-2). The main severity penalties come from vacuum/He sealed Ta handling, volatile Zr tetrahalides, and air/moisture sensitivity, all inferred from the sealed-Ta/drybox CVT conditions in SS-1 and SS-2. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact ZrBrCl CVT chemistry, so CS is neutral for Route 1 condition specificity.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic score is favorable because vapor transport is directly demonstrated for ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂, reducing dependence on slow bulk Zr diffusion (SS-1, SS-2). Structural reconstruction is limited because the target is molybdenite-derived and ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ are molybdenite-like 2D Zr dihalides (SS-1, SS-2). The uncertainty is Cl/Br ordering, because no CS/SS/TS source explicitly reports ZrBrCl.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Direct annealing uses no high pressure and likely remains below 1000 °C, consistent with reduced rare-earth and scandium halide annealing in sealed Ta at 870–1070 K or 960 °C (TS-3, TS-5). It is penalized for sealed vacuum/inert conditions, possible volatile ZrX₄ transport species, multiple annealing/regrinding steps, and air-sensitive halides [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/SS-2/TS-5]. Phase equilibration may require weeks to months, as seen for ZrCl₂ and ScCl analogues (SS-1, TS-5).

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are only moderate because solid-state interdiffusion of Cl/Br between ZrX₂ layers may be slow, and reduced halide analogues often require extended anneals (SS-1, TS-5). The structural framework is favorable if preformed ZrCl₂/ZrBr₂ are used, but ordering into monoclinic Pm ZrBrCl is not guaranteed [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/SS-3]. TS suggests reduced halide phase formation can be slow and low-yield under very reducing conditions (TS-2).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The high-pressure flux route inherits severe requirements from ZrNCl/ZrNBr syntheses: 3–5 GPa, 900–1100 °C, capsules, controlled cooling, and flux workup (CS-1, CS-2). The rubric does not penalize pressure unless >5 GPa, but practically the equipment burden is high (CS-1, CS-2). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence that pressure is needed for ZrBrCl, so SS/TS are neutral regarding pressure necessity.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux conditions can accelerate crystallization, as CS ZrNCl/ZrNBr analogues form within ~1 h at pressure (CS-1, CS-2). However, those structures include N and differ in Zr coordination from the target’s ZrBr₃Cl₃ octahedral sheets, lowering structural-transfer confidence (CS-1, CS-2). There is also risk of N/H incorporation from NH₄X fluxes [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-2].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, a small site count, and edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers apply [Target structure]. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for ZrBrCl; insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for this factor, so they are neutral source signals. The main uncertainty is not amorphization but phase separation or stacking/polytype disorder, as seen in ZrCl₂-related phases (SS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrBrCl is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a strongly unstable hypothetical compound, because its E_hull is only 0.024 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar molybdenite-like ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂, both made by sealed-tube vapor transport or related transport reactions (SS-1, SS-2), with thermodynamic support from ZrBr₂ and other low-E_hull reduced halides (TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is halide vapor transport, which can overcome the slow diffusion typical of reduced zirconium halides (SS-1, SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is control of mixed Cl/Br ordering versus formation of separate ZrCl₂- and ZrBr₂-rich phases [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/SS-3]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable but likely requires sealed Ta chemistry, very dry handling, and long equilibration.
