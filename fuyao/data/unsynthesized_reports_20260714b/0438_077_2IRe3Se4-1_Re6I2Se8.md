### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is predicted on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom [Target], which strongly supports ground-state accessibility. ReSe₂ is also on the hull [SS2/TS1], and the closest Re–Se–Cl structural analogue is nearly on hull at 0.0016 eV/atom [SS1/TS2], so the Re–Se–halide chemical space is not obviously metastable. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee a wide synthesis window, particularly when volatile iodine is involved [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 CVT condition severity.**  
This route is penalized because ReSe₂ selenide CVT uses high temperatures around 1080–1000 °C in evacuated quartz-glass tubes with halogen carriers [SS2/TS1]. Iodine and selenium volatility require sealed ampoules and careful loading [SS2/TS1, Reasonable Inference]. The method is well-established for Re–Se crystals, but operational severity is high because of halogen vapor pressure and temperature gradients [SS2/TS1].

**F3 — Route 1 CVT kinetics.**  
CVT improves mass transport through volatile species, supported by ReSe₂ transport growth using iodine or bromine carriers [SS2/TS1]. However, Re metal is refractory and the target requires insertion of stoichiometric iodine into a Re–Se sheet rather than merely transport of ReSe₂ [Target, Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are moderate-to-poor despite the favorable vapor-transport mechanism.

**F2 — Route 2 direct sealed-ampoule severity.**  
The closest structural analogue uses 900 °C, vacuum-sealed fused silica, pure dinitrogen handling, slow cooling, and quenching [SS1/TS2]. This avoids >1000 °C conditions but still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed-tube handling, and reactive halide chemistry [SS1/TS2, Reasonable Inference]. The severity is moderate because no high pressure or exotic equipment is required [SS1/TS2].

**F3 — Route 2 direct sealed-ampoule kinetics.**  
The structural match is excellent because Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂ has very similar P-1 2D Re–Se–halide square-pyramidal sheets [SS1/TS2]. Kinetics are limited by Re diffusion and by the long 7000 min treatment required in the analogue [SS1/TS2]. Using Re iodide rather than only Re metal may improve reactivity, extrapolated from ReCl₅ use in the chloride analogue [SS1/TS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 flux severity.**  
Flux growth can operate in a lower-temperature window, with MnBi₂Se₄ crystals grown by LiCl–RbCl flux from 800 to 400 °C over 1–2 weeks [CS2]. It still requires sealed evacuated tubes and volatile iodine/selenium management [CS2, Reasonable Inference]. The lack of direct Re–Se–I flux precedent increases uncertainty but not the nominal condition severity.

**F3 — Route 3 flux kinetics.**  
A molten halide flux should improve transport and crystal growth compared with dry solid-state reaction, consistent with successful selenide flux growth in CS2 and SS4 [CS2, SS4]. The Re-containing target still suffers from refractory Re chemistry, and no direct Re–Se–I flux example is provided; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for iodide flux specifically, treated as neutral. The strong structural precedent from Re–Se–Cl sheets supports the reconstruction component [SS1/TS2].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, Re, so no cation-radius mismatch trigger is present [Target]. The structure is crystalline P-1 and 2D with edge/corner-sharing Re square pyramids, but no face-sharing is reported [Target]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization in this exact composition, so the score relies primarily on the target structure description and is therefore high but not perfect.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re₃Se₄I is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis outlook is supported most strongly by the very close Re–Se–Cl structural analogue Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂, which forms 2D P-1 Re₃ chalcogenide-halide sheets [SS1/TS2], and by ReSe₂ CVT chemistry in the same Re–Se system [SS2/TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-mediated transport or fluxing, which can overcome sluggish Re diffusion and assist growth of layered crystals [SS2/TS1, CS2]. The most critical limiting factor is iodine chemical-potential control, because iodine is volatile and must be incorporated stoichiometrically rather than only used as a transport agent [SS2/TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires sealed-tube optimization and careful suppression of ReSe₂ or other Re–Se competing phases [SS2/TS1, Reasonable Inference].
