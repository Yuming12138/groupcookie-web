### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest stability category in the required rubric. TS analogues with E_hull ≈0.058–0.079 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by vacuum, flux, or controlled-atmosphere methods (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5), supporting metastable accessibility. CS includes stable Sr–Ta perovskite-derived analogues (CS-4, CS-5), but insufficient evidence from CS proves that the exact Sr₂TaNbO₆ R3 phase is a ground state. Remaining uncertainty is mainly redox: if Nb³⁺ is required, the phase may be metastable against oxidized Sr–Ta–Nb oxides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because it likely requires >1000 °C and controlled low-pO₂ atmosphere, consistent with reduced perovskite precedents in TS-2/TS-5 and high-T ceramic Sr–Ta precedents in CS-4/CS-5. The kinetic score is improved by the proposed sol–gel/molecular mixing step **[Reasonable Inference]**, but Ta/Nb diffusion remains difficult because CS-4/CS-5 use high-temperature ceramic cycles. SS gives insufficient evidence for this exact Sr–Ta–Nb R3 topology, so structural reconstruction is only partially supported.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is experimentally simple because CS-4/CS-5 show Sr–Ta oxides can be made in air using standard ceramic equipment. However, the target’s likely need for reduced Nb makes air synthesis chemically questionable **[Reasonable Inference]**, and TS-2/TS-5 indicate reduced perovskites generally require vacuum or reducing atmospheres. Kinetics are poor because oxide/carbonate solid-state reactions involving refractory Ta/Nb require high temperatures and repeated cycles, as shown in CS-4/CS-5 and TS-2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild thermal severity because CS-1/CS-2 grow niobates at only 500–600 °C in molten KOH, but sealed caustic flux handling adds complexity. Kinetically, flux can overcome refractory oxide diffusion, strongly supported by CS-1/CS-2; however, CS gives insufficient evidence for Ta incorporation and insufficient evidence for reduced Nb stabilization in KOH flux. The main uncertainty is whether the basic flux will favor an unwanted K/Sr/Nb/Ta phase or oxidized niobate/tantalate products.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is severe because SS-1/TS-1 require 1450 °C, vacuum, sealed Ta tubes, and controlled cooling. It is redox-compatible with reduced or oxygen-sensitive phases, as supported by SS-1/TS-1 and TS-2, but it is less kinetically attractive than flux because the reaction remains largely solid-state. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that sealed Ta-tube conditions will selectively form Sr₂TaNbO₆ rather than reduced tantalate/niobate impurities.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large Sr versus Ta/Nb size mismatch triggers one amorphization risk criterion **[Reasonable Inference]**, although such mismatch is common and tolerable in perovskite-derived oxides as seen in CS-4/CS-5. The target description includes edge-sharing and corner/edge-sharing motifs, but not face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the target’s full crystallographic unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂TaNbO₆ is likely a marginally metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS analogues strongly support the feasibility of Sr–Ta oxide ceramic synthesis, while TS analogues support the accessibility of similarly metastable reduced oxides under controlled oxygen potential. The most critical enabling factor is control of oxygen chemical potential, because reduced perovskite-like oxides in TS-2/TS-5 require vacuum or Ar/H₂ conditions. The most critical limiting factor is slow Ta/Nb cation diffusion and possible formation of competing oxidized Sr–Ta–Nb phases. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful low-pO₂ processing and repeated optimization rather than a simple air firing.
