### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0010 eV/atom, which is essentially thermodynamically stable and falls in the highest scoring range. The thermodynamic analogue set contains several stable or near-stable iodides/chalcoiodides, including NbSeI₃, Nb₃TeI₇, Nb₃I₈, and TiI₃ with E_hull ≈ 0 (SS-1–5; TS-1–5; CS-1, CS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the desired monoclinic C2/m polymorph will be the experimentally accessible low-temperature phase.

#### F2: Route 1 — CVT / Sealed Halogen Transport

The temperature severity is moderate because the closest CVT/iodide analogues use 500–800 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS-1; SS-4; TS-4). The deductions come from the need for vacuum, volatile iodine, sealed ampoules, and multi-step transport/purification, all of which are directly supported by Nb₃I₈, HfI₄, ThI₄, and TiI₃ preparations (CS-1, CS-3–5; SS-4, SS-5; TS-4, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for any need for high pressure, so no high-pressure deduction is applied.

#### F3: Route 1 — CVT / Sealed Halogen Transport

Kinetically, CVT is favored over simple bulk reaction because iodine can form volatile/intermediate metal iodide species, reducing reliance on W lattice diffusion; this inference is supported by successful Nb₃I₈ chemical transport and HfI₄ sublimation (CS-1, CS-3; SS-4; TS-4). However, W metal is highly refractory and may iodinate slowly, so precursor activity is not high [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is plausible because related layered iodides form by transport, but the exact WI₃ C2/m phase has no direct analogue synthesis in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2: Route 2 — Direct Sealed-Tube Solid-State

Direct sealed-tube synthesis has fewer process steps than CVT and is supported by NbSeI₃ and Nb₃TeI₇ direct element reactions (SS-1–3; TS-1–3). The main severity penalties are vacuum, volatile iodine in a sealed tube, and iodine toxicity, all evident across iodide analogues (CS-3–5; SS-1–5; TS-1–5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure or >1000 °C operation for WI₃, so those deductions are not applied.

#### F3: Route 2 — Direct Sealed-Tube Solid-State

The main kinetic limitation is W metal’s refractory nature and slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. NbSeI₃ required two weeks plus quench/reheat/slow cooling and still had phase-purity difficulty, indicating that structurally related iodides can have slow ordering and competing phases (SS-1; TS-1). Direct synthesis is feasible, but likely less reliable than CVT for crystalline phase-pure WI₃.

#### F2: Route 3 — Sublimation / Vapor Growth from WIₓ

The sublimation route uses moderate temperatures and no high pressure, consistent with HfI₄ sublimation and ThI₄ distillation/transport (CS-3, CS-5). It receives deductions for vacuum, volatile iodide species, sealed-tube operation, multi-step processing, and toxic/moisture-sensitive iodides (CS-3, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for >1000 °C or external pressure.

#### F3: Route 3 — Sublimation / Vapor Growth from WIₓ

Preformed tungsten iodide species should be more mobile/reactive than W metal [Reasonable Inference], and HfI₄ demonstrates that sublimation can yield well-crystallized metal iodides (CS-3). The weakness is compositional control: WI₃ requires W in a lower iodide state than many volatile tungsten iodides, so the vapor may over-iodinate unless iodine activity is buffered [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-purity difficulty in NbSeI₃ and impurity formation in TiI₃ support caution for phase control (SS-1; TS-1; CS-4).

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target is a binary single-cation iodide, so there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell or explicit mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence to trigger amorphization penalties for the target, so the amorphization risk is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

WI₃ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong for making layered or low-dimensional metal iodides in sealed evacuated ampoules using iodine transport, especially from Nb₃I₈, TiI₃, HfI₄, ThI₄, NbSeI₃, and Nb₃TeI₇ examples (CS-1, CS-3–5; SS-1–5; TS-1–5). The most important enabling factor is iodine-assisted vapor transport, which can overcome the poor bulk diffusion expected for refractory W [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1 and CS-3]. The main limiting factor is phase and iodine chemical-potential control, because related iodides show impurity formation, polytype competition, or difficult phase-pure synthesis (CS-4; SS-1–3; TS-1–3). Overall, WI₃ should be experimentally accessible, but high-purity single-phase material will probably require careful sealed-ampoule gradient optimization.

| 1 | All | Volatile iodine loss or excessive iodine vapor pressure | High | Use evacuated sealed ampoules and staged heating; sealed-tube iodine routes are used for Nb₃I₈, TiI₃, HfI₄, ThI₄, NbSeI₃, and Nb₃TeI₇ (CS-1, CS-3–5; SS-1–5; TS-1–5). |
| 2 | All | Competing tungsten iodide phases, e.g., lower/higher iodides | High | Control iodine chemical potential with stoichiometric or slight-excess I₂ and a temperature gradient. This is [Reasonable Inference]; impurity/phase competition is supported by TiI₃ mixtures and NbSeI₃ phase-purity difficulty (CS-4; SS-1; TS-1). |
| 3 | Route 1 | Transport gradient optimization for crystal growth | Medium | Use 500–800 °C hot/cold-zone gradients similar to Nb₃I₈ CVT and TiI₃ sealed-gradient synthesis (CS-1, CS-4; SS-4, SS-5; TS-4, TS-5). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Slow reaction of refractory W metal | High | Use fine/activated W powder, longer dwell times, and possibly regrinding/resealing. Long high-temperature dwell and repeated thermal treatment are supported by NbSeI₃ synthesis (SS-1; TS-1); W-specific activation is [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 5 | Route 3 | Over-iodination or wrong WIₓ vapor species | High | Buffer iodine activity with W metal and avoid large I₂ excess. This is [Reasonable Inference] based on the need for phase control in transport/sublimation analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). |
| 6 | All | Moisture/oxygen sensitivity of iodide products or precursors | Medium | Dry ampoules, handle iodine/iodides under dry inert gas where possible. Moisture exclusion is explicit for HfI₄ and drybox handling appears for ThI₄ (CS-3, CS-5). |
| 7 | All | Phase-pure crystallinity may require slow cooling or post-growth purification | Medium | Use slow cooling, transport purification, or sublimation; NbSeI₃ used quench/reheat/slow cooling and HfI₄ used slow sublimation for pure crystals (SS-1; TS-1; CS-3). |
| 8 | Route 2 | Polytype or structural ordering uncertainty | Medium | Characterize by powder/single-crystal XRD and vary synthesis temperature; Nb₃TeI₇ analogues show temperature-dependent polytypes (SS-2, SS-3; TS-2, TS-3). |
