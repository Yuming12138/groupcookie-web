### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0340 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic window of ≤0.05 eV/atom. This is strongly supported by TS analogues with very similar E_hull values: TS-1 = 0.0309, TS-2 = 0.0238, TS-3 = 0.0210, TS-4 = 0.0281, and TS-5 = 0.0328 eV/atom. CS evidence is mixed because CS-1 has E_hull = 0.1269 eV/atom and CS-2 has a very high E_hull = 2.1869 eV/atom, so CS gives insufficient consistent thermodynamic support beyond demonstrating that related halide phases can be reported. SS analogues are often ground-state or near-ground-state iodides, but several are compositionally different, so SS is supportive but less decisive than TS.

#### Route 1 F2/F3: Sealed-ampoule solid-state halogenation

The route is moderately severe because vacuum/sealed ampoules and volatile halogens are required, as seen in CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2, and TS-3. Temperatures are mild relative to oxide solid-state chemistry, since TS-3 makes CuHgSI at ~280 °C and SS-1/SS-2 show iodide-rich ampoule growth at 150–350 °C. Kinetically, binary Cu halides and I₂/ICl should be reactive [Reasonable Inference], but formation of the specific 1D CuI₂Cl ribbon from binary salts requires structural reconstruction. Long heating in TS-2 and TS-3 indicates ordering may require days to weeks.

#### Route 2 F2/F3: Acidic hydrothermal / solvothermal growth

The route avoids high temperature and external high pressure, but it uses corrosive volatile acids in sealed vessels, which is supported by TS-1 and CS-5. Kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal media transport halide species efficiently: TS-1 obtains CuHgSX crystals in concentrated HX after 24 h, while CS-5 obtains iodide-rich products in HI after 5 days. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, because CS-5 reports multiphase/intergrown products and the target has no chalcogenide or additional metal framework. SS-5 supports hydrothermal iodide crystal growth, but it is not chemically identical to Cu–I–Cl.

#### Route 3 F2/F3: Chemical vapor transport

CVT is more condition-sensitive because it requires an evacuated sealed ampoule, volatile transport species, and a temperature gradient, consistent with SS-1, SS-2, and TS-4. Kinetically it is attractive because halogen vapor transport can crystallize iodide-rich phases at 150–350 °C, and SS-1 reports quantitative crystal deposition under a 150→120 °C gradient. The uncertainty is that none of the CVT analogues directly show ternary Cu–I–Cl ribbon formation, so vapor transport may instead yield CuI, CuCl, or interhalogen-rich molecular phases [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides general sealed-halide support but insufficient direct CVT evidence for Cu mixed halides.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Cu, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered. The described CuI₂Cl structure is a 1D ribbon with low-coordinate Cu sites and no explicit large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network, so C2 and C3 are not triggered from the target description. SS and TS analogues include many low-dimensional crystalline halides, supporting crystallization rather than amorphization, but they do not directly quantify amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty comes from possible disorder among I/Cl/interhalogen positions [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuI₂Cl is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a deeply metastable one, because its E_hull is only 0.0340 eV/atom and TS analogues cluster around similar small hull energies. The strongest support comes from TS, where Cu-containing halide analogues are synthesized in evacuated tubes or hydrothermal HX media, and from SS, where iodide-rich low-dimensional phases crystallize by sealed-ampoule transport. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical activity of halide/interhalogen species, which allows crystallization at low to moderate temperatures. The main limiting factor is stoichiometry control of I:Cl and the iodine/chlorine chemical potential, because the target contains a specific mixed-halide/interhalogen-like motif. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with hydrothermal mixed-HX growth and sealed halogen-transport routes being the most promising.
