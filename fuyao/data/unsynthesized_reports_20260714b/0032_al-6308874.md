### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the high-score metastability boundary, so it is best described as near-ground-state or mildly metastable. (Target data) CS and SS analogues provide strong positive evidence because many similar hydrated alkali oxyanion or arsenate phases are at or near the hull. (CS-1–5, SS-1–5) TS analogues show experimentally accessible compounds even at ~0.052–0.103 eV/atom, supporting synthesis despite finite hull energy. (TS-1–5)

**F2 — Route 1:** Hydrothermal conditions are moderate rather than severe because the supporting analogues use ~200–220 °C, one-pot aqueous chemistry, and PTFE-lined autoclaves rather than >1000 °C or high-pressure synthesis. (CS-4, CS-2, TS-2) The only formal deduction is arsenic precursor toxicity, directly tied to arsenic acid hydrate in the arsenate analogue. (CS-4) **Insufficient evidence from SS** for this hydrothermal factor is scored neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1:** Kinetics are favorable because the reaction proceeds through dissolved or hydrothermal species, avoiding slow solid-state cation diffusion. (CS-4, CS-2, TS-2) Structural reconstruction is also favorable because a direct K hydrogen arsenate framework with AsO₄ tetrahedra is hydrothermally accessible. (CS-4) Remaining uncertainty is whether Cs and K order into the exact target stoichiometry rather than forming simpler Cs/K arsenate salts. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2:** Slow evaporation has very mild temperature, atmosphere, and pressure requirements, as shown by alkali oxyanion salts crystallized from aqueous solution at room temperature or 65 °C. (CS-1, SS-2) The route still receives a toxicity deduction because arsenic acid is the logical As(V) source. (CS-4, [Reasonable Inference]) **Insufficient evidence from TS** for exact slow evaporation is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2:** Precursor activity is high because alkali carbonates and oxyacids react in water, but structural assembly is less certain. (CS-1, SS-2) CS-1 explicitly notes failed compositions and mixture formation for some related systems, so phase-window control lowers the kinetics/structure score. (CS-1) The uncertainty is strongest for mixed Cs/K incorporation. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3:** Cooling crystallization is mild because the closest structural analogue crystallizes from a saturated acidic solution near 313 K, and TS cooling examples use hot saturated solutions without high pressure. (SS-3, TS-4) The only formal deduction is toxic arsenic acid. (CS-4, [Reasonable Inference]) **Insufficient evidence from CS** for true cooling crystallization is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3:** The liquid pathway gives high mobility and rapid ion equilibration. (SS-3, TS-4) Structural reconstruction is better than simple evaporation because saturated-solution cooling can favor well-formed single crystals, but K/Cs ordering remains uncertain. (SS-3, [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 4:** Gel or antisolvent diffusion occurs at room temperature and ambient pressure, so severity is low. (CS-5, SS-4, TS-1) Deductions are assigned for multistep diffusion setup and arsenic precursor toxicity. (CS-5, SS-4, TS-1, CS-4) The chemical match is weaker for CS/SS gel examples, so their source signal is lower than TS-1. (CS-5, SS-4, TS-1)

**F3 — Route 4:** Diffusion growth is kinetically gentle and can improve crystal quality by lowering supersaturation. (CS-5, SS-4, TS-1) The penalty is long growth time and less direct chemical transferability, since the gel examples are not Cs/K arsenates. (CS-5, SS-4, [Reasonable Inference]) This makes the route useful as a crystal-growth fallback rather than the primary synthesis route. ([Reasonable Inference])

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃KAs₄(H₂O₇)₂ is likely a **near-ground-state / mildly metastable** phase rather than a deeply metastable one because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and many CS/SS analogues are on or near the hull. (Target data, CS-1–5, SS-1–5) The synthesis case is well supported by CS because a direct hydrothermal hydrogen arsenate analogue exists, and by SS because Cs hydrogen oxyanion frameworks crystallize readily from aqueous media. (CS-4, SS-2, SS-3) TS supports the idea that phases in this hull-energy range can still be synthesized experimentally. (TS-1–5) The most critical enabling factor is **aqueous/hydrothermal mobility of Cs⁺, K⁺, and protonated AsO₄ species**, which should allow ordered crystallization at low temperature. (CS-4, CS-2, SS-3) The most critical limiting factor is **phase selectivity in a mixed Cs/K acidic arsenate solution**, where competing alkali arsenates or different hydration/protonation states may form. (CS-1, [Reasonable Inference])
