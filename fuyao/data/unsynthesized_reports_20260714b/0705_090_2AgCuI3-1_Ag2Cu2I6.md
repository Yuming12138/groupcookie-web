### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0360 eV/atom, placing it within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore near the stable/metastable boundary. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally synthesized: CuHgSI has E_hull ≈0.021 eV/atom [TS1], CuHgSBr ≈0.031 eV/atom [TS2], AgTl₂I₃ ≈0.027 eV/atom [TS3], and TlCdI₃ ≈0.017 eV/atom [TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy does not guarantee the specific P-31m layered polymorph rather than decomposition to AgI/CuI/I₂-containing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures like 473–573 K sealed-tube analogues [SS2/SS3/TS1/TS4], so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The penalties arise from evacuated sealed ampoules [SS2/SS3/TS1/TS4], volatile iodine required for AgCuI₃ stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**, and corrosive/volatile iodine handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether iodine pressure can be controlled without generating separate I₂/polyiodide phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
AgI and CuI are reactive soft iodides, and analogous Ag/Cu halide compounds form in sealed tubes [SS2/SS3/TS1]. However, solid-state routes in analogues require long anneals of 1 week to 4 months [SS1/SS2/SS3], implying ordering kinetics can be slow. Structural reconstruction from binary iodides to the 2D P-31m sheet is likely partial-to-substantial **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The melt/slow-cooling route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, matching CuGaI₄ growth from 573 K to ambient in sealed ampoule [CS2] and Bridgman growth for TlCdI₃ [CS1/TS5]. Deductions come from sealed/dry handling [CS2], volatile I₂ **[Reasonable Inference]**, and moisture/iodine sensitivity **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is the safe and useful melt window for AgI–CuI–I₂ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Melt growth gives high ion mobility and good crystallization kinetics, supported by CuGaI₄ single crystals from an iodide melt [CS2]. Slow cooling at 1 K/h in CS2 suggests the target may require similarly controlled cooling to obtain ordered sheets rather than quenched disorder. The limiting kinetic issue is not diffusion but selecting the correct layered polymorph **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Hydrothermal HI synthesis uses only moderate temperatures, 155–220 °C, in AgTl₂I₃ and AgISe₃ analogues [CS3/CS4/TS3]. Penalties are assigned for sealed corrosive HI/I₂ media and multiple procedural steps, consistent with quartz tube/autoclave handling in CS3 and pressure-vessel synthesis in CS4/TS3. There is insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal Ag–Cu–I specifically, so SS is treated as neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Hydrothermal iodide media can transport Ag/I species, as AgTl₂I₃ forms in aqueous HI [CS4/TS3] and AgISe₃ forms in concentrated HI [CS3]. Solution transport should reduce diffusion barriers relative to solid-state reaction **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS3 reports hydrothermal products with multiple phases/intergrowth, and CS4/TS3 reports twinning/low yield of untwinned AgTl₂I₃ crystals.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperatures but requires evacuated sealed tubes and slow thermal programs, as in CS5. Additional penalties come from volatile iodine, multistep flux processing, and possible washing/flux removal [CS5] plus iodine handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. There is insufficient direct TS evidence for iodide flux growth of AgCuI₃, so TS support is indirect/neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Flux growth should provide high mobility and can yield crystals of complex Ag-containing phases [CS5]. However, CS5 also reports low yields and difficult phase isolation, so the structural reconstruction/phase-selectivity term is not scored as high. The flux route is attractive for crystals but less reliable for bulk phase-pure powder **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains dissimilar Ag and Cu cation environments and mixed edge/corner connectivity in the described AgI₆/CuCuI₃ sheet, which increases local ordering requirements. The cell appears small enough that the >40 atom criterion is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether the layered P-31m framework forms readily or competes with disordered Ag/Cu iodide phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCuI₃ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.036 eV/atom. The analogue data support synthesis reasonably well: Ag/Cu/Tl/Cd iodide and Cu/Ag halide-chalcogenide materials are made by sealed-tube, melt, and hydrothermal routes at modest temperatures [CS2/CS4/SS2/SS3/TS1/TS3]. The most important enabling factor is the softness and high mobility of iodide lattices and iodide-rich solution/melt media, which allows crystallization below harsh solid-state temperatures [CS2/CS3/CS4/TS3]. The most important limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control, because the target is iodine-rich and may compete with AgI, CuI, I₂/polyiodide-containing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful iodine activity control and slow crystallization.
