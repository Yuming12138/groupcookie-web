### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull = 0.042 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so it is near the convex hull and plausibly isolable as a metastable or marginally stable phase. The closest Co–Se analogue Co3Se4 has a similar E_hull of ~0.0458 eV/atom and is experimentally synthesized by solid-state reaction (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). TS gives the strongest thermodynamic support because Co3Se4 is the top thermodynamic analogue with high similarity (TS-1). Remaining uncertainty is that a small positive E_hull still implies competition with other Co–Se phases, especially Co3Se4, Co9Se8, and CoSe2 **[Reasonable Inference from CS-1, CS-3]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives a low-to-moderate severity score because the closest Co–Se analogues require sealed evacuated silica/quartz tubes, high temperatures around 1050–1100 °C, quenching, and long anneals (CS-1, CS-3; SS-1; TS-1). The use of elemental Se in sealed tubes indicates volatility/containment concerns (CS-1, CS-3). Equipment is not exotic, but the combined penalties for vacuum, volatile Se, high temperature, and multistep annealing are substantial. CS, SS, and TS all support this route strongly via Co3Se4, while no source suggests it is a simple one-step low-temperature reaction.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Co + Se are simple but not highly reactive compared with salts or molecular precursors; this is directly the precursor set used for Co3Se4 and Co9Se8 (CS-1, CS-3). Kinetics are helped by high temperature and likely Se vapor/liquid transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the long anneals and repeated treatments in Co–Se analogues indicate slow homogenization and ordering (CS-1, CS-3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because Co3Se4 has nearly identical local CoSe6 octahedral connectivity and tilt angles (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The uncertainty is whether the hexagonal 2D P-6m2 polymorph nucleates instead of more common bulk Co–Se phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is severe because high-pressure analogues require ~50–65 kbar, specialized belt or anvil apparatus, high temperatures, quenching, and sealed capsules (CS-2, CS-4). CS provides clear evidence that high-pressure selenide synthesis is feasible, but it also shows the method is technically demanding (CS-2, CS-4). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis of the target-like Co2Se3 structure, so their route-specific signal is neutral. The large pressure penalty dominates this score.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High pressure and high temperature can accelerate phase formation, and CS analogues report reaction times of 0.5–6 h (CS-2). However, the structurally relevant target is a 2D hexagonal Co–Se sheet, whereas the high-pressure CS examples are Fe/Ni pyrite/marcasite-type selenides (CS-2, CS-4). This mismatch lowers the structural reconstruction score because pressure may stabilize denser phases rather than layered ones **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS do not provide useful high-pressure evidence, so uncertainty is high and treated neutrally.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest conditions because the hydrothermal NiSe2 analogue forms at 120–210 °C without external high pressure or evacuated high-temperature ampoules (CS-5). The route is still multistep because CS-5 uses precursor nanosheet formation, selenization, and Se solution preparation. Toxicity remains relevant because Se species, Co salts, and possible reducing agents are hazardous **[Reasonable Inference from CS-5 chemistry]**. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for hydrothermal synthesis of the target, so the low severity is based primarily on CS-5 and reasonable extrapolation.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution precursors and hydroxide nanosheets are much more reactive than elemental Co metal **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS-5 demonstrates hydrothermal conversion to 2D NiSe2 nanosheets at low temperature. Diffusion distances are short in nanosheets, improving kinetic accessibility **[Reasonable Inference from CS-5 morphology]**. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: converting Co hydroxide/oxyhydroxide to a specific P-6m2 Co2Se3 sheet is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS. Therefore, Route 3 scores well in precursor activity and diffusion but only moderately in structure selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Co, so cation-size mismatch does not trigger the amorphization criterion **[Reasonable Inference from target formula]**. The formula unit is small, and no provided target information suggests a >40-atom unit cell **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target does have mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing CoSe6 octahedra, which triggers one amorphization risk check; this same mixed connectivity is seen in the closest Co3Se4 analogue (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Co2Se3 phase is likely metastable or near-metastable rather than a robust ground-state phase because its E_hull is positive but small at 0.042 eV/atom; this is similar to experimentally accessible Co3Se4 at ~0.0458 eV/atom (CS-1; TS-1). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the feasibility of making related Co–Se octahedral frameworks, especially Co3Se4 with very similar CoSe6 connectivity and tilt geometry (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The most important enabling factor is the close local structural match to Co3Se4 and the low E_hull, which together imply the target is not far from known Co–Se chemistry (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: Co–Se has multiple nearby competing compositions, and analogues require quenching/annealing/tempering to manage phase purity (CS-1, CS-3). A hydrothermal nanosheet route may best exploit the target’s 2D nature, but this is less directly proven than sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis and therefore carries higher structure-identification risk (CS-5; insufficient evidence from SS/TS).
