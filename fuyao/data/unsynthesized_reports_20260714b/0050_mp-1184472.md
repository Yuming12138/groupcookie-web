### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target lies at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is low enough to be experimentally plausible but still positive, so it is best treated as borderline metastable. [Target] TS analogues with similar hull values are high-pressure metastable germanides, so I scored high but not near-perfect. [TS] CS and SS both contain accessible analogues at comparable or higher E_hull values, which supports feasibility. [CS; SS]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc melting is chemically direct because FeGe2 is arc-melted, but the severe local temperature and vacuum anneal produce significant condition deductions. [CS] TS gives insufficient evidence from TS for this ambient-pressure arc route, so TS is neutral source score = 50 for this factor. [TS]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are better than ordinary solid-state synthesis because melting homogenizes Fe and Ge. [CS; Reasonable Inference] The remaining kinetic penalty comes from converting a FeGe2-like binary chemistry into beta Cu3Ti-like FeGe3 rather than simply reproducing the FeGe2 structure. [CS; SS; Target]

**F2 — Route 2**  
The HP/HT route is condition-severe because SS/TS analogues require multianvil-type pressures, inert handling, and quenching. [SS; TS] The temperature can likely remain below 1000°C for the initial screen because several HP germanides form around 820–1200 K, although some analogues use higher temperatures. [SS; TS; Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are favored relative to ambient solid-state synthesis because Ge-rich HP analogues form under pressure at temperatures well below Fe melting, suggesting pressure-assisted reaction and possible Ge-rich flux behavior. [SS; TS; Reasonable Inference] Using FeGe2 + Ge reduces the reconstruction burden compared with elemental Fe + Ge. [CS; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Ambient sealed-tube synthesis has milder external conditions than HP/HT, but vacuum sealing and repeated annealing still impose moderate severity. [CS] SS and TS give insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for ambient-pressure formation of this target-like phase, so both are neutral source score = 50 for this factor. [SS; TS]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Solid-state kinetics are weak because dense Fe and Ge require long diffusion paths below Fe melting. [Reasonable Inference] CS sealed-tube analogues show that long annealing/regrinding can be needed, and SS/TS analogues imply that target-like Ge-rich phases may not form at ambient pressure. [CS; SS; TS]

**F2 — Route 4**  
CVT avoids high pressure and extreme furnace temperatures, but iodine volatility/toxicity and evacuated ampoule handling add condition penalties. [CS] SS and TS provide insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for CVT, so both are neutral source score = 50 for this factor. [SS; TS]

**F3 — Route 4**  
CVT kinetics can be favorable for crystal growth, but no source confirms FeGe3 transport or direct crystallization. [CS; insufficient evidence from SS; insufficient evidence from TS] Composition control is likely difficult because a 1:3 Fe:Ge phase must compete with FeGe2 and elemental Ge. [CS; Target; Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the mixed corner/edge/face-sharing cuboctahedral network triggers the amorphization checks, while the target appears to be a compact intermetallic rather than a large-cell framework. [Target] CS/SS/TS are insufficient for direct C1/C2 evaluation, so those source-specific checks are neutral source score = 50. [CS; SS; TS] The final amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because SS/TS show crystallization of dense Ge-rich frameworks under controlled HP/HT conditions. [SS; TS]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeGe3 is most likely a **metastable or borderline-stable phase**, because its E_hull is positive but only 0.0500 eV/atom. [Target] CS supports Fe–Ge chemical accessibility through arc-melted FeGe2, but it does not directly prove the FeGe3 beta Cu3Ti-like phase. [CS; Target] SS and TS provide stronger route guidance because many Ge-rich structural/thermodynamic analogues are synthesized only under HP/HT conditions and are described as metastable or narrow-window phases. [SS; TS] The critical enabling factor is high pressure combined with a Ge-rich, partially reacted precursor such as FeGe2 + Ge. [CS; SS; TS; Reasonable Inference] The critical limiting factor is phase competition against FeGe2 and residual Ge, especially if pressure/temperature/quench conditions are not tightly controlled. [CS; SS; TS; Target; Reasonable Inference]
