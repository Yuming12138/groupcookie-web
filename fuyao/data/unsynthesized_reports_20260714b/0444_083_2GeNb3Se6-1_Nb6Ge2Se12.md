### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the predicted ground state, so the assigned F1 score is very high. This is reinforced by thermodynamically similar phases with E_hull = 0.0, including NbSe₂ (SS-4; TS-1), Nb₃VSe₆ (CS-4; TS-4), and Co₂Nb₄PdSe₁₂ (TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of polymorph competition; such polytype issues are reported for NbSe₂ vapor-grown forms (SS-3/4; TS-1/2).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses common sealed-tube solid-state conditions rather than pressure or exotic atmospheres, which is supported by CS-2/4/5 and SS-5. The main deductions are for vacuum, volatile Se requiring a sealed ampoule, and Se toxicity; all are consistent with the evacuated sealed-tube approaches in CS/SS/TS. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or >1400 °C conditions are needed, so those deductions are not applied and are effectively neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Nb is the kinetic bottleneck because it is refractory and diffuses slowly [Reasonable Inference], even though elemental Nb is used successfully in CS-2/3/4/5 and SS-5. The target’s layered character is supported by structurally similar layered NbSe₂ phases (SS-1/2/3/4/5), but incorporation of Ge into a distinct Nb₃GeSe₆ framework requires more rearrangement than forming binary NbSe₂ [Reasonable Inference]. Impurity traces and ordering difficulty in related Nb–M–Se systems lower the kinetics score (CS-2/4/5; TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is harsher because it uses multistage high-temperature annealing near or above 1000 °C, following the precedent of staged Nb₅SnSe₈-related synthesis (CS-3) and long 1050 °C annealing in layered Nb–Se chemistry (SS-2; TS-5). Vacuum and sealed tubes are again required, and Se remains volatile/toxic. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure requirements, so high-pressure deductions are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The longer and hotter program should help overcome Nb diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], consistent with high-temperature/long-duration analogues in CS-3 and SS-2/TS-5. However, repeated or extended heating can also indicate slow ordering and competing phase formation; related Nb–M–Se phases report minor impurities and ordering difficulty (CS-2/4/5; TS-4). Thus the kinetic score remains moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids very high temperature in the recommended 900–1000 °C window, which is consistent with NbSe₂ vapor transport at 750–950 °C (SS-3/4; TS-1/2). It is penalized for vacuum, volatile Se/halogen transport agents, toxicity/corrosivity of Br₂ or I₂, and the multistep nature of preparing feed plus CVT growth; Br₂ use in CVT is directly supported by TS-3. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed, so no pressure penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport has the best kinetic score because layered NbSe₂ single crystals are explicitly grown by vapor transport (SS-3/4; TS-1/2), and a multinary layered selenide is grown by CVT using Br₂ (TS-3). The main uncertainty is composition control for Ge incorporation, because the provided vapor-transport analogues report polytypes, twinning, and composition deviations (SS-3/4; TS-1/2). Pre-reacted feed is recommended as a [Reasonable Inference] to reduce stoichiometric drift.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with different size and bonding preferences, Nb and Ge, which triggers one amorphization/complexity factor [Reasonable Inference supported by Nb high-coordinate chalcogen environments in CS/SS/TS and p-block chalcogen bonding in CS-1/3]. The unit cell atom count is not specified; therefore there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and the target description to trigger the >40 atom check, scored neutrally. Mixed polyhedral sharing is common in analogues (CS-1/2/3/4; TS-4), but the target description does not explicitly establish simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃GeSe₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis strongly at the family level: Nb–Se layered phases and ternary Nb–M–Se chalcogenides are repeatedly made by sealed-tube solid-state reactions (CS-1/2/3/4/5; SS-5; TS-4/5), and layered Nb selenides are also accessible by vapor transport (SS-3/4; TS-1/2). The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for forming layered Nb–Se frameworks under sealed evacuated conditions. The most critical limiting factor is phase/composition control: Ge may form competing Ge–Se binaries, while NbSe₂ and related Nb-rich selenides are strong competing phases [Reasonable Inference supported by impurity/ordering issues in CS-2/4/5 and polytype issues in SS-3/4]. Overall, the material appears moderately synthesizable, with CVT or vapor-assisted growth most attractive for crystalline material and direct sealed-tube reaction best for initial phase screening.
