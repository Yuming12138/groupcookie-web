### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high-synthesizability boundary in the provided scoring rubric. Chemically similar double perovskites are often near the hull, including Sr2CaMoO6 at 0.0 eV/atom, Sr2ZnMoO6 at 0.0109 eV/atom, and Sr2CoMoO6 at 0.0331 eV/atom [CS-2, CS-3, CS-5]. Thermodynamic Cr analogues around 0.034–0.084 eV/atom have reported syntheses, sometimes requiring high pressure or sensitive atmospheres [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether the computed Cr(VI) double perovskite is stable against SrCrO4/MgO-type mixtures under real oxygen chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Solid-state ceramic route

The condition severity is moderate because analogous Sr double perovskites are made with standard furnaces, air, grinding, pelletizing, and firing up to 1200 °C [CS-2, CS-3, CS-5]. The kinetic score is limited by oxide/carbonate diffusion and likely slow B-site ordering, as analogues needed repeated firings and regrinding [CS-2, CS-3]. Structurally, the route is credible because Sr2ZnMoO6 and Sr2CoMoO6 share the same A2BB′O6 octahedral network motif [CS-3, CS-5]. The uncertainty is Cr(VI): Mo(VI) analogues in air are encouraging [CS-2, CS-3], but Cr(VI) may form chromate side phases instead [TS-5; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Pechini route

The Pechini route has lower severity because it can plausibly crystallize at ≤1000 °C after molecular-level cation mixing, following the Ca2FeMoO6 polymeric complex route [CS-1]. Its kinetic advantage is the random cation distribution in the resin, explicitly described for Ca2FeMoO6 [CS-1], which should help overcome MgO diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. The main risk is that organic burnout can locally reduce Cr(VI), so oxidizing decomposition and possibly an intermediate low-temperature oxygen anneal are important [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS directly supports Pechini processing, so those sources are treated as neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: High-pressure route

High-pressure synthesis is severe because Cr perovskite analogues require 65–70 kbar or 9 GPa and specialized presses/capsules [SS-4, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3]. Kinetically, HP routes can rapidly form dense Cr perovskites, as single-phase 6H-BaCrO3 was obtained after 30 min at 9 GPa and 900 °C [SS-5, TS-3]. However, B-site ordering in Sr2MgCrO6 may need longer annealing than a short high-pressure dwell permits [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best reserved for cases where ambient-pressure routes yield chromate/oxide mixtures or decomposition products [SS-4, TS-2, TS-5].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination contrast between SrO12 and MgO6/CrO6 units, which triggers the ionic-radius-difference check [Target description; Reasonable Inference]. The structure is nevertheless a recognizable perovskite-derived framework with corner-sharing MgO6/CrO6 octahedra and SrO12 cavities, similar to successful Sr double perovskites [CS-3, CS-5]. The unit cell does not appear excessively large from the provided inequivalent-site count, and the strict vertex+edge+face coexistence criterion is not met because edge sharing is not reported [Target description]. Remaining uncertainty comes from the triclinic P-1 distortion, which may indicate a narrow ordering/tilt stability window [Target description; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2MgCrO6 is likely a low-energy metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the upper edge of the high-stability category [Target data]. The chemical analogues strongly support synthesis of Sr-based A2BB′O6 double perovskites by ceramic and wet-chemical routes [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5], while structural/thermodynamic Cr analogues warn that chromium perovskites can require careful redox or pressure control [SS-4, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known Sr double perovskites with SrO12 cages and alternating BO6/B′O6 octahedra [CS-3, CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Cr in the +6 oxidation state without forming SrCrO4, Cr2O3, MgCr2O4, or oxygen-deficient phases [TS-5; Reasonable Inference]. The best first attempt is an oxidizing Pechini route because it maximizes cation homogeneity while minimizing the high temperatures that could destabilize Cr(VI) [CS-1; Reasonable Inference].
