### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0010 eV/atom, which is essentially on the convex hull and strongly supports synthesizability. TS is especially supportive because TlCuCl₃, Cd₂AsCl₂, CuHgSCl, Cu₆PS₅Br, and Hg sulfide halides are all near-hull thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values from ~0.0014 to 0.0238 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). SS also supports stability because several structurally related halides are at or near hull, including AuCl and Tl/Hg/Pd chlorides (SS3–SS5), while CS gives mixed polytype evidence because one ZnS analogue is high above hull but several are near hull (CS2–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that a near-zero hull energy does not guarantee the specific orthorhombic Pmm2 polymorph will nucleate instead of the common CuCl structure [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperature comparable to sealed-tube CuHgSCl at 573 K and CuS at 620–670 K, so no high-temperature deduction is needed (SS1/TS3; CS1). It requires sealed vacuum or inert handling, supported by evacuated sealed tubes in SS1/TS3 and CS1. Deductions are mainly for vacuum, sealed volatile halide handling, multiple drying/loading/sealing/heating steps, and CuCl air/moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The kinetic benefit is that CuCl already has the target stoichiometry, so no long-range cation mixing is needed [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS3 show that CuX-containing solids can react at only 573 K, and CS1 shows Cu-containing binary solids can form at 620–670 K, indicating manageable kinetics. The uncertainty is polymorph reconstruction: converting common CuCl into the Pmm2 2D sheet may require slow ordering or may revert on cooling [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis avoids >1000 °C and high pressure; SS2 uses 670 K in sealed ampoules/autoclaves and SS3 uses 150 °C in sealed quartz ampoules. The penalties arise from sealed-vessel pressure, corrosive/toxic concentrated HCl, multi-step loading/sealing/cooling, and Cu(I) sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Relative to CVT, the route avoids Cl₂ gas and high-temperature gradients, so it receives the highest F2.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
SS2 is the strongest kinetic analogue because CuCl participates directly in a concentrated HX hydrothermal reaction and yields crystals after only 24 h. Solution-mediated transport should minimize solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling in SS2 supports crystal growth. The main uncertainty is selectivity for the target Pmm2 CuCl polymorph rather than another CuCl phase or dissolved/reprecipitated common CuCl [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT of halides occurs at moderate temperature in SS4, TS2, and TS5, so thermal severity is moderate rather than extreme. However, Route 3 needs sealed ampoules, vacuum or Cl₂/halogen control, volatile halide transport, and multi-step gradient growth, all supported by SS4 and TS2/TS5. Toxic Cl₂ or transport additives increase practical difficulty [SS4; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport is kinetically favorable for crystal growth because similar halides form crystals by CVT or gas-phase growth (SS4; TS2; TS5). Composition control in binary CuCl is simpler than multicomponent Hg/S/Br/Cl phases in TS5 [Reasonable Inference], but Cl chemical potential must be tightly controlled because AuCl CVT requires a chlorine atmosphere and AuCl has stability issues (SS4). The route is plausible but less direct than hydrothermal growth because no source directly reports CuCl Pmm2 formation by CVT.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The described structure has two inequivalent Cu and two inequivalent Cl sites, not a >40-atom complex cell, and the target description indicates corner-sharing pyramids without mixed edge/face/vertex coexistence. SS and TS analogues are generally crystalline products, including single crystals and phase-pure solids (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5), so amorphization risk is low; the larger uncertainty is polymorphism rather than amorphization, as suggested by ZnS polytypes in CS2–CS5.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CuCl phase is very likely a near-ground-state or extremely low-energy metastable polymorph because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom. The SS and TS analogues strongly support synthesis of related chloride and Cu-halide/chalcogenide-halide crystals under moderate sealed-tube, hydrothermal, and CVT conditions (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5), while CS provides weaker chemical transferability because most CS examples are sulfides rather than chlorides (CS1–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary CuCl stoichiometry combined with high thermodynamic stability and availability of CuCl as a direct precursor [Reasonable Inference; CuCl precursor use supported by SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the desired orthorhombic 2D Pmm2 sheet may compete with common CuCl polymorphs, analogous to the polytype-control issues seen for ZnS (CS2–CS5). Overall, hydrothermal recrystallization in chloride-rich medium is the best first attempt because it offers high mobility at low temperature while maintaining chloride chemical potential (SS2; SS3).
