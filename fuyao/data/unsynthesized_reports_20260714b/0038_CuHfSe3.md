### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

#### Route 1: Direct Elemental Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State Reaction

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Direct reaction of elements in evacuated silica/quartz tubes is repeatedly successful for transition-metal selenides/chalcogenides in CS, SS, and TS datasets; especially Ta/Ni/Se at 650 °C for 7 days [CS1], Nb/Cr/Se at 600 °C for 15 days [SS3], Mn/Se in evacuated silica tubes with multiple firings [SS5/TS4], and Fe/Cr/Se at 550–800 °C for several weeks [TS2]. |
| Precursors | Hf powder, Cu powder, Se pellets/powder, all ≥99.9%; slight Se excess of ~1–3 mol% is recommended to offset Se volatility [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS2, TS2]. |
| Temperature program | Grind under Ar → staged prereaction at ~300–400 °C for 10–24 h to moderate Se vapor pressure → heat at 750–850 °C for 5–15 days → regrind/re-press → anneal again at ~800 °C for 5–14 days → slow cool at ~1–3 °C/h to 500–600 °C, then furnace cool [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS1, TS2]. |
| Atmosphere | Evacuated sealed silica/quartz ampoule; Ar glovebox loading preferred to avoid Hf powder oxidation and Se contamination [SS2, TS1, TS3, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. |
| Pressure | Ambient pressure inside sealed evacuated ampoule [CS1, SS2, TS2]. |
| Additional conditions | Intermediate grinding/reannealing is recommended because SS1 required prolonged annealing and reannealing for single-phase material, and TS2 reports several-week solid-state reactions for related selenides [SS1, TS2]. |
| Transferability to target | Good elemental transferability because structurally similar Hf–Cu–Se/Tl compounds were synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state routes near 800 °C [SS1, SS2]; however, the target has CuSe6 octahedra rather than CuSe4 tetrahedra in the closest SS analogues, so phase selectivity is uncertain [Target, SS1, SS2]. |
| Estimated difficulty | **Challenging** because Hf is refractory and Se is volatile, while analogous sealed-tube methods are feasible [SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 high-score cutoff, so F1 is scored at the conservative lower end of the 90–100 range [Target]. TS analogues at ~0.054–0.069 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, which supports feasibility for slightly metastable selenides [TS1–TS5]. CS and SS also contain synthesized near-hull or on-hull chalcogenides, including Hf–Cu–Se structural analogues at E_hull = 0 [CS1–CS4, SS1, SS2]. Remaining uncertainty is that the calculated hull does not guarantee the Pnma CuSe6 ordering will be the experimentally preferred polymorph [Target, SS1, SS2].

**F2 — Route Severity**  
Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, Se volatility, and repeated annealing/regrinding, but not for high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures [CS1, SS2, TS2]. Route 2 has the same sealed-tube and multistep penalties, but its binary precursor strategy is operationally standard in SS/TS chalcogenide synthesis [SS2, TS1, TS5]. Route 3 is more severe because it adds toxic/corrosive halogen transport agents, as used in CS1/CS3/SS3; insufficient evidence from TS for CVT gives TS a neutral 50 route signal [CS1, CS3, SS3]. Route 4 has moderate severity for self-flux but weaker direct evidence, while Route 5 is most severe due to high-pressure equipment and possible >1000 °C conditions [CS4, CS5, SS4].

**F3 — Reaction Kinetics**  
Route 1 has the weakest kinetics because elemental Hf is refractory and must fully react/reconstruct into a ternary phase [SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 improves kinetics because HfSe2/CuSe binaries pre-form key Hf–Se and Cu–Se bonding and reduce the elemental diffusion burden [SS2, TS1, TS5]. Route 3 can improve crystal growth through transport, but Hf halide transport and Pnma phase selection are uncertain [CS1, CS3, SS3]. Route 4 may improve mass transport through flux, but insufficient evidence from SS/TS for HfCuSe3-like flux growth limits confidence; Route 5 can force dense coordination but has uncertain quench retention [CS4, CS5, SS4].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
No F4 trigger is clearly met because the target is not described as >40 atoms/cell, does not show face-sharing coexistence, and likely does not exceed the 15% cation-radius mismatch threshold [Target, Reasonable Inference]. SS analogues show related Hf–Cu–Se polyhedral networks crystallize, supporting a low amorphization tendency [SS1, SS2]. CS has insufficient evidence for target-specific radius/cell-size checks and is neutral 50 for F4, while TS provides only indirect evidence from complex crystalline selenides [TS1, TS3, TS5]. The main uncertainty is that Pnma CuSe6 ordering may compete with lower-dimensional Hf–Cu–Se phases [Target, SS1, SS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfCuSe3 is best viewed as a **near-ground-state, marginally metastable** target because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS analogues containing Hf–Cu–Se frameworks synthesized in sealed ampoules near 800 °C, plus TS analogues showing that 0.05–0.07 eV/atom selenides can be experimentally accessible [SS1, SS2, TS1–TS5]. The key enabling factor is use of sealed-tube selenide chemistry with controlled Se chemical potential and slow cooling [SS2, CS1, TS5]. The key limiting factor is structural selectivity: closest Hf–Cu–Se analogues contain CuSe4 tetrahedra, whereas the target requires CuSe6 octahedra in a Pnma perovskite-like framework [Target, SS1, SS2]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require binary precursors, careful Se control, and iterative annealing rather than a single simple firing [SS2, TS2, TS5].
