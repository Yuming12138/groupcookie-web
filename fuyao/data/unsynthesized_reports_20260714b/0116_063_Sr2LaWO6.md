### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the requested ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability boundary. CS strongly supports stability because Ba₂CaWO₆ polymorphs are at 0–0.003 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-3). SS also supports feasibility because several structurally similar perovskites are at the hull (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). TS shows that compounds around 0.04–0.076 eV/atom are experimentally accessible (TS-3 to TS-5), so the remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic existence but phase competition and redox control.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The route is penalized because CS-3 and SS-4/SS-5 require high-temperature ceramic treatment above 1000 °C, and SS-4/SS-5 require H₂/N₂ reducing atmospheres. Multiple grinding/pelletizing/firing cycles are common in CS-1/CS-3 and SS-5. No high-pressure equipment is indicated by CS/SS/TS, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a mandatory toxic precursor.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by oxide/carbonate diffusion and by the likely need to order La and W on the octahedral sublattice, consistent with repeated heating and regrinding in CS-1/CS-3 and SS-4/SS-5. The perovskite topology is favorable because it matches CS and SS analogues. The main uncertainty is whether W metal/WO₂ reacts completely at the chosen temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed/inert route inherits the high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere penalties from CS-1/CS-2 and TS-2. It is more severe than Route 1 because ampoule sealing or Ar/vacuum redox control adds risk, especially if carbonate decarbonation is incomplete [Reasonable Inference]. High pressure is not involved; CS-1/CS-2 ampoules are containment/atmosphere-control methods, not GPa synthesis.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are slightly worse than Route 1 because ampoule-compatible temperatures may be lower than the 1300–1400 °C ceramic treatments used in CS-3 and SS-4/SS-5. TS-2 supports redox solid-state synthesis of an M⁵⁺ perovskite, but it still uses staged firing and careful stoichiometry. The uncertainty is whether sealed oxygen fugacity can be tuned narrowly enough to favor Sr₂LaWO₆ over SrWO₄/La₂O₃/W oxide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The sol–gel route still requires a controlled reducing anneal, justified by SS-4/SS-5, and a final temperature likely above 1000 °C, inferred from CS-3 and SS-4/SS-5. It avoids sealed tubes and may avoid >1400 °C [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sol–gel synthesis of this exact family.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The kinetics score is highest because nitrate/citrate or Pechini precursors shorten diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. However, crystallizing a monoclinic ordered double perovskite from an amorphous precursor still requires high-temperature reconstruction, and analogues in CS-3 and SS-5 show that repeated heat treatment may be needed. The main uncertainty is maintaining W⁵⁺ during organic burnout and final reduction.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation size/charge contrast among Sr²⁺, La³⁺, and W⁵⁺, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii]. The structure is an ordered tilted double perovskite, not an obviously glass-forming mixed-connectivity network; the target description gives corner-sharing LaO₆/WO₆ octahedra. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a >40 atom unit cell trigger, so that check is treated as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LaWO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS strongly supports the double-perovskite tungstate framework, while SS strongly supports tilted Sr/Ln/MO₆ ordered perovskites made under reducing conditions. TS supports the idea that M⁵⁺ perovskites near this metastability range can be synthesized with careful redox control. The most critical enabling factor is a reducing oxygen chemical potential sufficient to stabilize W⁵⁺, analogous to Mo⁵⁺ stabilization in SS-4/SS-5 and Re⁵⁺ synthesis in TS-2. The most critical limiting factor is competing phase formation from W⁶⁺ tungstates or over-reduced W-containing impurities if oxygen fugacity and stoichiometry are not controlled.
