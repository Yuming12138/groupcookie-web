### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic criterion [Target]. CS supports this because the closest bromide analogue Ta3SBr7 is nearly on hull at ~0.0049 eV/atom [CS1]. SS and TS also support high stability through layered Nb3Br8 and Ta3TeI7/related Ta halide phases with E_hull near zero [SS2/SS4; TS2/TS3/TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee a wide experimental stability window; Ta3TeI7 analogues show side products despite low hull energy [SS4].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, because Ta3SBr7 was synthesized at 550 °C and Ta3QI7 at 450 °C [CS1; SS4]. The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, Br2 volatility/toxicity, and purification or repeated processing described for Ta3SBr7 [CS1]. Insufficient evidence from TS indicates no additional unique severity beyond what TS1 repeats from Ta3SBr7 [TS1].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Direct elemental reaction has favorable structural transfer because Ta3SBr7 is a near-identical Ta3QBr7 layered material [CS1/SS1/TS1]. However, Ta metal is refractory and long anneals/crystal-growth periods were needed for the analogue, indicating slow kinetics [CS1]. The volatile bromine environment likely helps transport but also complicates stoichiometry [CS1; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
CVT avoids >1000 °C operation but requires high vacuum, sealed ampoules, volatile halogens, and a temperature gradient [SS2/TS2]. Ta3SBr7 also used a prolonged gradient for crystal growth, supporting the need for controlled transport conditions [CS1]. The main uncertainty is whether I2 is required; if used, it may increase chemical complexity and risk of iodide contamination [SS2/TS2; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport improves kinetic access because Nb3Br8 and Ta3SBr7 analogues crystallize or purify under halogen gradients [SS2/TS2; CS1]. Diffusion through a vapor-mediated pathway partially offsets the refractory nature of Ta [Reasonable Inference based on SS2/TS2]. The uncertainty is nucleation selectivity: transport may preferentially deposit TaBr5 or other Ta–Br phases, as side products are noted in Ta3SBr7 chemistry [CS1].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Ta pentahalide routes in TS use sealed tubes, inert handling, and 600–800 °C temperatures, without high pressure [TS3/TS4]. The route is penalized for air-sensitive/volatile halide precursors and controlled atmosphere [TS3/TS4]. It is less penalized for multistep complexity because the basic operation is mixing, sealing, and heating, as reported for TS3/TS4.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
TaBr5 is expected to be more reactive than metallic Ta alone, and TaCl5/Ta mixtures form related reduced Ta halide phases [TS3/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. This improves precursor activity relative to Route 1 but still requires reduction/assembly of a layered Ta3TeBr7 framework [TS3/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Direct evidence for Ta3TeBr7 from TaBr5/Ta/Te is absent, so structural reconstruction uncertainty remains [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a small, ordered layered halide with one metal cation and an 11-atom formula unit, and close analogues are crystalline layered phases [Target; CS1; SS4]. No CS/SS/TS source indicates glass formation or amorphous products for the closest Ta3QX7 family; instead, the challenge is crystalline phase purity [CS1; SS4]. TS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so it is treated as neutral-supportive rather than decisive [insufficient direct evidence from TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta3TeBr7 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from Ta3SBr7, which is chemically and structurally very close and was synthesized from Ta, S, and Br2 in sealed ampoules [CS1/SS1/TS1], and from Ta3TeI7, which confirms Te compatibility in the Ta3QX7 layered motif [SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-mediated transport/reactivity, either from Br2 vapor or TaBr5, because related bromide/iodide phases form at only 450–760 °C despite refractory Ta [CS1; SS2/TS2; SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: Ta3SBr7 and Ta3TeI7 analogues report side products such as TaBr5, TaI5, Ta6I14, or residual Ta [CS1; SS4]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful bromine chemical-potential control and sealed-tube optimization [CS1; SS2/TS2; TS3/TS4].
