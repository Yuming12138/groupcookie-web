### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.013 eV/atom, so it is very close to the convex hull and falls in the 90–100 score range by the supplied rubric. TS strongly supports this assessment because the nearest thermodynamic analogues are also stable or near-stable: BaAl₄Se₇ at 0.0186 eV/atom (TS-1), AlPNCl₅ at 0.0121 eV/atom (TS-2), and AlBi₂Se₂BrCl₄ at 0.0074 eV/atom (TS-4). CS provides mixed but useful evidence: the closest direct analogue GaTeCl is on the hull (CS-5), while some chemically similar but structurally different compounds have high hull energies (CS-1, CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed AlSeCl phase is entropically or kinetically accessible at 350–450 °C rather than decomposing to Al₂Se₃ + AlCl₃-related phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is not temperature-severe because GaTeCl forms at 400 °C for 7 days (CS-5), far below the >1000 °C threshold. It does require evacuated ampoules/inert handling, consistent with CS-5 and with sealed Al–Se syntheses such as BaAl₄Se₇ (SS-1/TS-1). Deductions are mainly due to vacuum/sealed operation, volatile AlCl₃, and air-sensitive chloride/chalcogenide handling, all supported by AlCl₃/NaCl melt procedures and hydrolysis sensitivity in SS-2/SS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact decomposition pressure of AlSeCl, so volatile-loss risk is inferred from AlCl₃ behavior.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 has favorable kinetics because the closest analogue GaTeCl forms from a metal chloride plus metal chalcogenide at only 400 °C (CS-5). Al₂Se₃ already contains Al–Se bonding, and AlCl₃ supplies Al–Cl bonding, so the reaction needs partial reconstruction rather than complete element-to-framework formation [Reasonable Inference from CS-5 and SS-1]. Long annealing of 7 days in CS-5 suggests ordering/crystal growth is not instantaneous, so the structural reconstruction score is high but not maximal. The main uncertainty is whether smaller Al–Se–Cl sheets prefer the computed P3m1 polymorph or a competing AlCl₄/selenide salt phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses low temperatures, 130 °C in AlCl₃/NaCl melts (SS-2/SS-5) or 165 °C ionothermal conditions (TS-4), so the thermal severity is low. However, the route requires glovebox loading, sealed vessels, corrosive AlCl₃-rich media, and post-synthesis flux removal, as explicitly reported for SS-2, SS-5, and TS-4. Volatile AlCl₃ and hydrolysis-sensitive products create handling risk (SS-2, SS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists that AlSeCl specifically crystallizes from a flux, so the score is limited by phase-selectivity uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux/ionothermal media improve ion mobility, and crystalline chalcogenide halides are formed at low temperature in AlCl₃-rich media (SS-2, SS-5, TS-4). This supports high precursor activity and low diffusion barriers. The penalty is structural: the demonstrated products are Bi-containing chalcogenide-halide frameworks with AlCl₄ units, not the exact AlSeCl tetrahedral sheet (SS-2, SS-5, TS-4). Therefore, the route may be kinetically facile but compositionally less selective [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is low-temperature to moderate-temperature and uses no external high pressure, consistent with Cu(AlCl₄)₂ growth at 155–195 °C (TS-5) and GaTeCl formation at 400 °C (CS-5). It still requires sealed tubes, volatile AlCl₃/Al₂Cl₆, and moisture-free handling, which justifies the same major deductions. TS-5 also notes chloride crystals form in a temperature gradient and are sensitive near synthesis temperature, supporting a narrow operating window. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Al–Se–Cl vapor species, making this a less certain primary synthesis route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Halide vapor transport can grow crystals when volatile chloride complexes are available, as shown by Cu(AlCl₄)₂ from CuCl₂ + Al₂Cl₆ in a sealed gradient (TS-5). However, no provided source confirms that Al₂Se₃ or AlSeCl has a useful volatile transport species, so precursor vapor availability is only moderate. Composition control is likely difficult because AlCl₃ is volatile and the target requires a precise 1:1:1 ratio [Reasonable Inference], while TS-5 suggests chloride phases can have narrow thermal stability. This route is best treated as a crystal-growth optimization after Route 1 confirms phase formation.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Direct elemental sealed-tube synthesis is well precedented for chalcogenides: CoPSe was made from elements at 800–850 °C for 2–4 weeks (CS-3), and Mg₂Al₂Se₅ was made from elements at 1000 °C (SS-3). For AlSeCl, the proposed route can be staged at lower temperature by including AlCl₃, but it still involves sealed ampoules, volatile Se/AlCl₃, and inert handling [Reasonable Inference]. Multiple steps or intermediate grinding may be needed, as in CS-3. The severity score is therefore similar to Route 1, but the kinetic score is worse.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Elemental routes often require complete structural reconstruction and can be slow, as demonstrated by CoPSe requiring multiple heat treatments and intermediate grindings over weeks (CS-3). Elemental Al has low reactivity due to oxide passivation [Reasonable Inference], and Se/AlCl₃ volatility complicates uniform reaction. SS-3 shows direct elemental Al–Se frameworks can form, but at 1000 °C rather than the milder conditions preferred for AlSeCl. Thus, this route is plausible but not optimal.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple 1:1:1 composition with only one cation, so cation-size disorder is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. Its described connectivity is corner-sharing AlSe₃Cl tetrahedra, matching the corner-sharing GaTe₃Cl tetrahedral motif in GaTeCl (CS-5), with no mixed edge/face-sharing network indicated. SS-1 and SS-3 also show that Al–Se tetrahedral frameworks crystallize well in ordered selenides. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for glass formation in AlSeCl specifically, but the simple layered tetrahedral network supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlSeCl is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.013 eV/atom, and several TS analogues lie at similarly low hull energies (TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest support comes from CS-5: GaTeCl has the same MChCl stoichiometry, two-dimensional sheet character, and corner-sharing MCh₃Cl tetrahedral motif, and it was synthesized at 400 °C in sealed ampoules. SS and TS data further support the stability of Al–Se tetrahedra and Al–Cl/chalcogenide-halide chemistry in sealed or flux environments (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of highly reactive AlCl₃ + Al₂Se₃ binary precursors that map directly onto the target stoichiometry, analogous to GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ for GaTeCl (CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining chloride/selenide stoichiometry under sealed-tube conditions because AlCl₃ is volatile and moisture-sensitive, and related AlCl₃-containing chalcogenide halides require careful dry handling (SS-2, SS-5, TS-5).

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Moisture sensitivity of AlCl₃/chalcogenide chloride products | High | Use Ar glovebox loading and sealed ampoules, as used for AlCl₃/NaCl chalcogenide halide syntheses (SS-2, SS-5) and BaAl₄Se₇ sealed-tube synthesis (SS-1/TS-1). |
| 2 | All | Volatile chloride loss / AlCl₃ sublimation | High | Use evacuated sealed ampoules and slight AlCl₃ excess; AlCl₃ sublimation/temperature-gradient control is explicitly used in SS-2/SS-5 and halide transport in TS-5. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Competing Al₂Se₃-rich or AlCl₄⁻-rich phases | Medium | Start with exact AlCl₃:Al₂Se₃ = 1:1 stoichiometry, based on GaCl₃:Ga₂Te₃ = 1:1 for GaTeCl (CS-5); screen small AlCl₃ excess only after phase identification [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow layered ordering / small crystal size | Medium | Anneal for 5–10 days and slow cool; GaTeCl required 7 days at 400 °C to obtain single crystals (CS-5). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Flux traps product or stabilizes AlCl₄-containing byproducts instead of AlSeCl | Medium–High | Use Al₂Se₃-rich compositions and reduce excess AlCl₃ after initial trials; AlCl₃/NaCl fluxes are known to form AlCl₄-containing frameworks (SS-2, SS-5), so composition tuning is essential. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Product hydrolysis during washing/removal of flux | Medium | Remove flux by dry sublimation where possible, following excess AlCl₃ sublimation practice in SS-2/SS-5; avoid aqueous washing. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Unknown volatility of Al–Se–Cl transport species | High | First prepare AlSeCl by Route 1, then use small AlCl₃/Al₂Cl₆ as transport agent; TS-5 supports halide-gradient crystal growth but not Al–Se transport specifically. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Narrow thermal stability window | Medium | Use shallow gradients and keep below decomposition-prone temperatures; TS-5 reports chloride crystals forming only in a specific gradient and decomposing slightly above synthesis temperature. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Precursor reactivity mismatch: Al metal vs volatile Se/AlCl₃ | High | Prefer preformed Al₂Se₃; direct elemental chalcogenide syntheses often need high temperature and repeated grinding, as shown for CoPSe (CS-3) and Mg₂Al₂Se₅ (SS-3). |
| 10 | All | Phase verification and polymorph control | Medium | Use powder XRD after each anneal and adjust temperature; SS-1 reports minor impurity in BaAl₄Se₇, indicating that even close Al–Se systems can need phase optimization. |
