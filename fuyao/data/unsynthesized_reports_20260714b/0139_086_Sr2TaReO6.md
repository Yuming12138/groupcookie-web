### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has Eₕᵤₗₗ = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-score threshold, so it is likely metastable but not prohibitively unstable. CS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable perovskite-derived oxides, including Ba₃MgTa₂O₉ and Ba₂InTaO₆ at Eₕᵤₗₗ = 0 (CS-1, CS-4). SS/TS include perovskites with comparable metastability, such as EuNbO₃ at 0.0236 eV/atom and CaNbO₃ at ~0.059 eV/atom (SS-2/SS-3; TS-1/TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific Re³⁺/Ta⁵⁺ ordering is experimentally stable under accessible oxygen fugacity.

#### F2 — Route 1

The ceramic sealed-tube route inherits high-temperature and repeated-processing burdens from Ta/Nb perovskite analogues, where 1000–1575 °C and multiple regrindings are common (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-4; TS-5). Low-pO₂ sealed synthesis is justified by reduced perovskite analogues requiring vacuum or Ar-sealed tubes (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The major uncertainty is Re-specific behavior, because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Re volatility or Re³⁺ stabilization.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are poor because SrCO₃/Ta₂O₅/Re oxide-metal mixtures are refractory and require long diffusion distances, consistent with long anneals in CS-1, CS-4, CS-5 and SS-4/TS-5. The target also requires B-site ordering between Ta and Re, analogous to the difficult In/Ta ordering reported for Ba₂InTaO₆ (CS-4). The route is plausible but likely slow and impurity-prone.

#### F2 — Route 2

The solution-derived route still needs sealed low-pO₂ annealing, supported by SS-1/SS-2/SS-4 and TS-2/TS-5, but it may avoid the very high temperatures used in CS-1/CS-4/CS-5. Its multistep nature lowers the condition score, but the final temperature can plausibly remain near 950–1100 °C [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for a Pechini Sr-Ta-Re oxide, so this route carries extrapolation risk.

#### F3 — Route 2

The main kinetic advantage is molecular-scale cation mixing, which should improve Ta/Re distribution and lower diffusion requirements [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 supports use of nitrate chemistry in complex oxide perovskite synthesis, while CS-4 shows that B-site ordering can still require prolonged heating even when thermodynamics are favorable. Thus the route has the best kinetic outlook but still needs careful annealing.

#### F2 — Route 3

High pressure is strongly penalized because SS-5 used ~80 kbar for BaNbO₃-type perovskites. While the high-pressure route may stabilize a dense perovskite framework, it requires specialized equipment and capsule containment, making it more severe than the ambient-pressure routes. CS data mostly show ambient-pressure Ta perovskites, so high pressure is not the first choice.

#### F3 — Route 3

Short high-pressure dwell times can form perovskites, as in SS-5, but precursor reactivity remains limited if using oxide/metal mixtures. The dense perovskite framework may be favored under pressure, but B-site ordering of Ta/Re may not fully equilibrate during short high-pressure holds. Therefore, kinetics are moderate-to-poor despite the stabilizing pressure.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large A-site Sr cation and smaller octahedral Ta/Re cations, so cation size contrast triggers one amorphization-risk point. However, the target’s connectivity is conventional for perovskite-derived structures, with corner-sharing B-site octahedra and A-site cuboctahedra similar to CS-2/CS-4 and SS/TS perovskites. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has a >40-atom unit cell, so that check is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂TaReO₆ is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state compound because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the practical metastability boundary. The CS data strongly support accessibility of Ta-containing perovskite-derived oxides by ceramic routes, while SS/TS data support reduced perovskite synthesis under sealed vacuum or Ar conditions. The most important enabling factor is the perovskite framework compatibility: Sr/Ba A-site cuboctahedra and Ta/Nb/Re-like B-site octahedral networks are well represented across CS, SS, and TS. The most important limiting factor is oxygen fugacity control for Re, because Re must likely remain near +3 while avoiding Re metal, ReO₂ impurity, or volatile high-valent Re oxides. A reactive precursor route followed by sealed low-pO₂ crystallization is therefore the best compromise between phase stability, kinetic accessibility, and valence control.
