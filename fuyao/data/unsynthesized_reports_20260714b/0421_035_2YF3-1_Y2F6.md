### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and earns a high F1 score of 95/100 from the target data. CS #1 shows another YF₃ polymorph is much higher in energy at about 0.2005 eV/atom, which reinforces that YF₃ polymorph selection matters but does not reduce the target’s thermodynamic score. SS #2/#4 and TS #1/#3 contain near-hull ScF₃ analogues, supporting the general stability of MF₃ fluoride frameworks, but they do not prove the exact YF₃ P-31m phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 precipitation

Route 1 uses mild ambient-to-low-temperature aqueous conditions, directly supported by YF₃ precipitation from Y-salt solution with NaF in CS #1. No high temperature, high pressure, or controlled atmosphere is required in CS #1, so deductions are minimal. A toxicity deduction is applied because NaF/NH₄F fluoride salts are hazardous [Reasonable Inference], though this is not a severe synthesis-condition penalty.

#### F3 — Route 1 precipitation

The kinetics are favorable because soluble Y salts and F⁻ can react immediately in solution, consistent with the direct YF₃ precipitation reported in CS #1. The uncertainty is structural: CS #1 produced cubic YF₃, not the target trigonal P-31m layered phase. Therefore, the route is kinetically easy but only moderately reliable for polymorph-specific synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 2 solvothermal / hydrothermal

The solvothermal ScF₃ analogues use 150–200 °C, water/ethylene glycol, NH₄F, and a Teflon-lined autoclave, which are moderate and common conditions (CS #2/#3; SS #2/#3; TS #1/#2). The route avoids high temperature, high pressure above 5 GPa, and reactive gas atmospheres. The main severity penalty is fluoride toxicity from NH₄F.

#### F3 — Route 2 solvothermal / hydrothermal

Solvothermal conditions provide high precursor activity and strong ionic mobility, as shown by ScF₃ formation from Sc(NO₃)₃ and NH₄F at only 150–200 °C (CS #2/#3; SS #2/#3; TS #1/#2). Excess fluoride is reported to improve productivity for ScF₃, suggesting a useful control parameter transferable to YF₃ (CS #2/#3; SS #2/#3; TS #1/#2). The remaining uncertainty is that ScF₃ analogues are mostly 3D corner-sharing frameworks, while the target YF₃ is a 2D edge-sharing sheet, so structural reconstruction/selectivity is not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 3 solid-state polymorph conversion

Solid-state fluoride synthesis can be done at moderate-to-high temperatures, with Sc-related examples at 550–600 °C for days and sealed ampoules or fluorine stream conditions (CS #5; SS #1; TS #4). Sealed metal tube fluoride reactions at 800–1000 °C are also reported for SmZrF₇ (SS #5), showing that sealed fluoride processing is feasible. Deductions are assigned for controlled/sealed atmosphere, possible repeated annealing, and fluoride toxicity.

#### F3 — Route 3 solid-state polymorph conversion

Starting from YF₃ avoids compositional diffusion between different cations, which helps relative to multicomponent solid-state reactions [Reasonable Inference]. However, dense fluoride powders require solid-state rearrangement, and related fluoride syntheses can require multi-day sintering (CS #5; SS #1; TS #4). The target’s edge-sharing 2D topology differs from cubic/3D YF₃ and ScF₃ analogues, so structural conversion may be sluggish or path-dependent (CS #1; target).

#### F2 — Route 4 direct fluorination

Direct fluorination is supported indirectly by fluorine-stream synthesis of Sc-containing fluorides at 550–600 °C for days (CS #5; SS #1; TS #4). It requires hazardous fluorinating conditions such as HF/F₂/NH₄HF₂ [Reasonable Inference], so it receives deductions for reactive atmosphere, volatile fluorinating species, multi-step processing, and toxic precursors. The method is chemically plausible but operationally more severe than precipitation or solvothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4 direct fluorination

Y₂O₃ is a stable refractory oxide, so conversion to YF₃ may be limited by product-layer diffusion and incomplete fluorination [Reasonable Inference]. Related fluorination/sintering examples require 2–4 days at 550–600 °C, supporting slower kinetics (CS #5; SS #1; TS #4). This route is useful for making anhydrous YF₃, but there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that it selectively yields the target P-31m layered polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary single-cation fluoride, so the cation-radius-difference criterion is not triggered by definition of the target composition. The target structure is described as YF₆ octahedra in a 2D edge-sharing sheet, not a mixed-connectivity network, so the mixed-polyhedra criterion is not triggered. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of P-31m YF₃, but the target’s structural simplicity supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target YF₃ P-31m phase is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue evidence supports MF₃ fluoride synthesis strongly: exact YF₃ precipitation is reported in CS #1, and multiple ScF₃ analogues form under solvothermal or solid-state conditions in CS/SS/TS. The most critical enabling factor is the simple charge-balanced Y³⁺/F⁻ chemistry, which allows direct precipitation or solvothermal crystallization from soluble salts (CS #1; CS #2/#3; SS #2/#3; TS #1/#2). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control, because CS #1 reports a different cubic YF₃ phase and the target has a distinct layered edge-sharing topology. Overall, solvothermal synthesis is preferred because it combines mild conditions, high precursor activity, and better opportunity for low-temperature polymorph selection.
