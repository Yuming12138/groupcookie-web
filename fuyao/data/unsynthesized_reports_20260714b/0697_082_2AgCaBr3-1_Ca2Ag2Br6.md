### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.0010 eV/atom, so it is effectively on the convex hull by the provided scoring rubric [target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low hull energies have reported syntheses, including SrBr₂ at 0.0012 eV/atom (TS1) and BaZnCl₄ at 0.0019 eV/atom (TS2), and chemically similar bromides are also stable or near-stable (CS1, CS2, CS4). This strongly supports synthesizability. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that the exact trigonal P-31m CaAgBr₃ sheet is the experimentally preferred polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because CS bromides form at 250–450 °C or 873 K in ampoules (CS1, CS2, CS5). The main severity penalties are vacuum/dry atmosphere, sealed-system handling, multi-step ampoule processing, and air/moisture sensitivity, all supported by CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1, and SS5. Insufficient evidence from TS changes this route severity directly; TS mainly confirms that similar low-hull halides can be made, not the exact CaAgBr₃ processing window, so TS is neutral for Route 1 F2 where not directly applicable.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary bromide precursors should be reasonably active because ternary bromides are repeatedly synthesized from binary halides (CS2, CS5, SS3). Diffusion is aided by moderate temperatures and possible partial melting, consistent with melt-assisted CS1 and CS2. The structural-reconstruction score is limited because no exact CaAgBr₃ structural analogue is reported in CS/SS/TS; SS analogues show mixed polyhedral bromide frameworks but not the same Ag–Ag trigonal-pyramidal layer.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperature and no pressure, consistent with SS1 and SS5, but it requires oxygen-/water-free Ar and sealed ampoule handling. Several-week tempering in SS1 and SS5 makes the process longer and operationally more demanding. Insufficient evidence from CS for a need for several-week annealing in CaAgBr₃ specifically, so the severity is based mainly on SS analogues and treated as moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 2

Long annealing below the melt improves equilibrium ordering but indicates slower kinetics, as shown by SS1 and SS5. Precursor activity remains acceptable because anhydrous binary halides are used successfully for related ternary bromides (SS1, SS3, SS5). Structural reconstruction remains uncertain because the target sheet topology is not reproduced exactly in SS; therefore the kinetics score is lower than Route 3.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is operationally similar to CS1, CS2, CS4, and TS2 slow-cooled melt methods, requiring only moderate temperature and no high pressure. It still requires controlled dry Ar or vacuum ampoules, sealed systems for volatile halides, and careful multi-step melt/cooling procedures (CS1, CS2, CS4, TS2). The route is therefore moderate in severity despite favorable temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 3

Melt or self-flux growth gives the best kinetic pathway because CS1, CS2, and CS4 demonstrate that ternary bromides can crystallize directly from slow-cooled melts. Ion diffusion is highly favorable in a liquid/flux environment, and slow cooling supports ordering. The principal uncertainty is whether Ag–Ag dimerized trigonal-pyramidal units form reproducibly; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms that motif.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines larger CaBr₆ octahedra with lower-coordinate AgBr₃/Ag units, giving a cation-size and coordination mismatch [target; Reasonable Inference]. The target also has mixed edge- and corner-sharing connectivity [target], and similar mixed connectivity is common among SS analogues (SS1, SS5). The unit-cell atom count trigger is not activated because the provided target description does not establish >40 atoms per cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on this check is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaAgBr₃ is likely very close to a ground-state phase because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is only 0.0010 eV/atom [target], though the exact P-31m polymorph could still be metastable because CS/SS/TS do not report this exact Ca–Ag bromide structure. The analogue set supports synthesis well at the chemistry/process level: ternary bromides are repeatedly made from binary bromides in sealed ampoules, dry Ar, vacuum, and slow-cooled melts (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5; SS1, SS3, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halide melt or flux pathways, which bypass slow solid diffusion and are directly supported by CS1, CS2, and CS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving AgBr volatility/photosensitivity and possible competing CaBr₂/AgBr or Ag⁰ impurities [Reasonable Inference; sealed/dry handling need supported by CS1, CS2, CS5, SS5]. Overall, the best route is slow-cooling melt/self-flux growth, followed by post-annealing if powder XRD shows incomplete ordering.
