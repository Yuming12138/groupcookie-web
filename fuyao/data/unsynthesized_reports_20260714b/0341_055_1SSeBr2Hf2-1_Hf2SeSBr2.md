### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0040 eV/atom, which is well within the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom window [Target]. Chemically and thermodynamically similar analogues such as MnBiS2Br, Ta3SBr7, and Ce4NS3Cl3 are also near the hull [CS3, SS1/TS1, CS2/TS5]. The main uncertainty is that near-hull energy does not guarantee the specific mixed-anion ordering, especially because some structurally similar examples have much higher hull energies [SS3, SS5].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, similar to 550 °C for Ta3SBr7 and 600 °C for MnBiS2Br [SS1, CS3]. The score is reduced because evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile Br/S/Se species, and hazardous halide handling are required [SS1, SS2]. There is no evidence for high pressure from CS/SS/TS, so high-pressure penalties are not applied.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Direct reaction with Hf metal is kinetically difficult because Hf is refractory and likely has a passivating oxide surface [Reasonable Inference], while analogues using dense metals require long anneals or careful heating [SS1, SS2]. Phase formation may be slow because the target requires ordering of S, Se, and Br in a layered octahedral framework [Target]. CS3 explicitly indicates difficulty obtaining phase-pure related sulfide bromide samples, supporting a low-to-moderate kinetic score.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Flux synthesis avoids >1000 °C conditions and is supported by low-temperature aluminum halide melts for reduced Zr halides [TS2]. The penalties arise from sealed/vacuum handling, volatile/air-sensitive HfBr4 or AlBr3, and multi-step flux processing [TS2, CS4]. Insufficient evidence from SS for exact Hf bromide flux chemistry is treated neutrally for route-specific severity, but SS4 still supports sealed flux growth generally.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Route 2 receives the best kinetic score because molten halide media can dissolve and transport early-transition-metal halide species, as shown for ZrCl3-type chemistry [TS2]. This should reduce the diffusion limitation associated with refractory Hf metal [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether the flux can simultaneously maintain the correct Hf redox state and mixed S/Se/Br stoichiometry without forming HfS2, HfSe2, or HfBr4-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT uses no external high pressure and moderate-to-high temperatures similar to Zr–S–Se transport and bromide vapor transport [SS3, TS3]. It still requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile transport agents, and hazardous halogen/chalcogen handling [SS3, TS3, SS1]. CS provides insufficient direct CVT evidence for this target class, so CS is neutral for this route’s severity.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport improves mass transfer compared with pure solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated for Zr–S–Se systems [SS3]. However, the target’s low-symmetry mixed-anion Hf sheet likely has a narrow nucleation window [Target; Reasonable Inference]. SS1 shows that related Ta–S–Br crystal growth can yield small crystals and phase mixtures, which limits the score.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one cation type, no reported >40-atom unit cell, and edge/corner but not face-sharing connectivity [Target]. These features give low amorphization tendency under the specified criteria. CS and TS do not provide direct amorphization data, so those source categories are neutral for this factor, while SS supports crystallizability of related layered networks [SS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf2SeSBr2 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0040 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is reasonably strong: SS/TS provide a close 2D Ta–S–Br bromide/chalcogenide example [SS1/TS1], CS provides sulfide bromide and selenide chloride solid-state precedents [CS3, CS1], and TS provides reduced early-transition-metal halide chemistry [TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is use of a sealed halide-rich environment or flux to transport Hf and buffer Br chemical potential [TS2, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: related bromide/chalcogenide solids can form mixtures or resist phase-pure synthesis [CS3, SS1]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful redox, halogen fugacity, and S/Se stoichiometry control.
