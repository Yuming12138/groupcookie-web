### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target RbMgAs has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target data. The closest analogues KMgAs and KMgSb are also E_hull = 0.0 and experimentally synthesized, which supports the idea that the AMgPn Matlockite family is thermodynamically accessible [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a broad experimental phase field, and CS/SS/TS data do not directly report RbMgAs phase width.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits the proven KMgAs/KMgSb high-temperature program of 1150 °C followed by 800 °C annealing [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. This earns deductions for T > 1000 °C, sealed volatile components, multiple thermal steps, and toxic/air-sensitive reactants. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any need for high pressure, H₂, NH₃, or vacuum, so those are scored as not applying.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is structural identity: KMgAs has the same P4/nmm Matlockite-type MgAs₄ tetrahedral framework as the target except K is replaced by Rb [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The long several-day anneal used for KMgAs/KMgSb indicates ordering and diffusion are not instantaneous [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. Rb melting early in the heating schedule may improve transport relative to a fully solid mixture [Reasonable Inference], but Rb/As volatility complicates kinetics and mass balance.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions by using preformed binary arsenides, analogous to KLi₂As synthesis from Li₃As/K₃As at 623 K and from elements at 823 K [SS3, TS3]. It still requires sealed ampoules and inert handling because alkali arsenides are air-sensitive and arsenic-containing [SS3, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for this specific binary route in Mg arsenides is treated as neutral rather than negative.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary arsenides should reduce the need for direct As transport and can shorten diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], consistent with KLi₂As being accessible from binary arsenides [SS3, TS3]. However, KLi₂As products were reported as not entirely free from starting compounds despite optimization, implying that binary routes can suffer from incomplete conversion and narrow reaction windows [SS3, TS3]. Thus the route is kinetically plausible but not guaranteed phase-pure.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses lower temperatures than the direct KMgAs route, supported indirectly by KSnAs at 590 °C and K₄ZnAs₂ at 923 K [CS3, SS2, TS2]. The deductions arise from volatile sealed alkali/As components, multi-step flux handling, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors [CS3, SS2, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for RbMgAs flux synthesis, so the favorable temperature is an inference rather than a demonstrated condition.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A Rb-rich melt should enhance ion transport and may promote crystal growth, analogous to plate-like KSnAs and K₄ZnAs₂ products from alkali-rich/intermediate routes [CS3, SS2, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. The low melting point of Rb is kinetically favorable [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling can aid ordering into the layered P4/nmm phase. The limiting uncertainty is composition control, because excess alkali can stabilize Rb-rich competing arsenides, as multiphase behavior was noted for K₄ZnAs₂ before optimization [SS2, TS2].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered P4/nmm structure closely related to crystalline KMgAs, which argues against strong amorphization tendency [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The main amorphization/disorder risk is the large cation size mismatch between Rb and Mg [Reasonable Inference], but the target does not have a very large unit cell and does not show vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence in the supplied structure. The remaining uncertainty is that amorphous or poorly crystalline products could occur if Rb/As volatilization shifts stoichiometry during heating.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbMgAs is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple close alkali arsenide analogues are also on the hull and experimentally synthesized [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The strongest support comes from KMgAs, which is the direct K analogue with the same tetragonal P4/nmm Matlockite-type MgAs₄ tetrahedral framework [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a proven AMgAs-type synthesis route using elemental alkali metal, Mg, and As in sealed Ta/Fe containment [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The main limiting factor is Rb volatility and air sensitivity, which likely make stoichiometry control harder than for K analogues [Reasonable Inference]. A lower-temperature Rb-rich flux or binary arsenide route may reduce thermal severity, but it has less direct evidence than the high-temperature KMgAs route [CS3, SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3].
