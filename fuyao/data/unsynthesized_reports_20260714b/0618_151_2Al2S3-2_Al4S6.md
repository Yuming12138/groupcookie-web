### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.024 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and therefore strongly within the commonly synthesizable metastability window [target data]. TS analogues include several synthesized chalcogenides with similar low positive E_hull values, such as Li₂Ga₂GeS₆ at 0.035 eV/atom, InGaS₃ at 0.017 eV/atom, BaAl₄Se₇ at 0.019 eV/atom, and SiS₂ at 0.020 eV/atom [TS1–TS4]. CS1/CS2 show that even much higher-energy Al₂S₃ polymorphs were experimentally accessible, supporting kinetic accessibility but also warning that polymorph competition is significant [CS1, CS2]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the Pmc2₁ target is the experimentally selected polymorph under the 750 °C/quench protocol or whether another Al₂S₃ polymorph nucleates first [CS1, CS2].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, no external high pressure, and standard sealed-ampoule equipment, which is favorable [CS1, CS2]. Deductions arise from the required vacuum ampoule, volatile sulfur, staged heating, long anneal, and quenching [CS1, CS2]. SS2 supports the need for slow heating in elemental metal–sulfur reactions to avoid violent sulfur pressure buildup [SS2]. There is insufficient evidence from TS that the exact target requires more severe conditions, so TS is neutral for Route 1 severity at 50 [insufficient evidence from TS].

#### F3 — Route 1

The direct reaction kinetics are credible because exact Al₂S₃ analogues form from Al and S at 750 °C [CS1, CS2]. The Al melting point is below 750 °C, so a liquid-assisted Al–S reaction path is plausible and should improve diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction/polymorph selection because CS1/CS2 required 7 days and quenching for metastable Al₂S₃ [CS1, CS2]. SS2 also indicates that metal sulfide solid-state reactions may require carefully staged thermal programs [SS2].

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT avoids extreme pressure and stays below 1000 °C in most analogues, but it requires evacuated ampoules, volatile iodine, a temperature gradient, and post-growth handling [SS1, SS5, CS3]. Toxic/corrosive iodine adds a practical safety burden [CS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. TS2 supports iodine CVT for a thermodynamically similar sulfide polymorph system but gives insufficient temperature/time details, so TS2 is useful for method plausibility but not quantitative severity [TS2; insufficient evidence from TS2 for exact severity].

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT kinetics are favorable for crystal growth because GaS, CdAl₂S₄, ZnAl₂S₄, and InGaS₃ analogues crystallize by halogen transport [CS3, SS1, SS5, TS2]. However, no source directly demonstrates CVT of pure Al₂S₃, so volatile Al–S–I transport chemistry remains uncertain [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Composition control is likely the limiting kinetic factor because sulfur and iodine transport can shift local stoichiometry, and CS1/CS2 show Al₂S₃ polymorph control is sensitive [CS1, CS2; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses sealed-tube annealing similar to TS1/TS3 and SS3/SS4, with temperatures in the 650–850 °C range inferred from the exact Al₂S₃ synthesis temperature [CS1, CS2, TS1, TS3]. It is penalized for vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multi-step precursor preparation, and dry handling [TS1, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. It is less hazardous than direct elemental sulfur/aluminum ignition once Al₂S₃ is preformed, but prior preparation of phase-pure Al₂S₃ is itself a step [CS1, CS2]. SS gives useful support for sealed-tube Al sulfide annealing; TS gives useful support for binary sulfide precursor routes [SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3].

#### F3 — Route 3

Using preformed Al₂S₃ improves chemical matching and avoids long-range Al/S interdiffusion from separate elements [Reasonable Inference]. TS1/TS3 show binary chalcogenide precursors are effective in sealed-tube solid-state synthesis [TS1, TS3]. The kinetic drawback is that polymorph conversion may require breaking and reforming Al–S connectivity, including formation of both AlS₄ and AlS₅ units in the target [target structure]. CS1/CS2 indicate Al₂S₃ polymorphs need long annealing and quenching, so reconstruction is not trivial [CS1, CS2].

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux/halide-assisted growth can operate at moderate 700–850 °C temperatures, as supported by TS5 and SS5 [TS5, SS5]. The severity score is reduced by sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile halides, multi-step flux handling, and likely moisture sensitivity [TS5, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct CS evidence for flux synthesis of pure Al₂S₃, so CS is neutral at 50 for this route’s condition severity [insufficient evidence from CS]. The route is experimentally plausible but less established than direct reaction [TS5, SS5].

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux growth should improve kinetics by dissolving and recrystallizing Al–S species, as generally demonstrated for sulfide flux synthesis at 800 °C [TS5]. Halide-assisted growth of Al-containing sulfides also supports the idea that halides can mobilize sulfide frameworks [SS1, SS5]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: no source confirms that the Pmc2₁ Al₂S₃ polymorph crystallizes from flux rather than a competing Al₂S₃ phase [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Therefore the route receives strong diffusion/activity scores but only moderate structural-reconstruction confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Al, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [target structure]. The described cell appears small, with two inequivalent Al and three inequivalent S sites, and no evidence is given for a >40-atom cell [target structure]. The structure contains both tetrahedral AlS₄ and square-pyramidal AlS₅ units sharing corners and edges, but no face-sharing is reported, so the specified mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered [target structure]. CS/SS analogues crystallize as ordered sulfide frameworks rather than amorphous products, but TS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence for pure Al₂S₃ and is treated as neutral [CS1, CS2, SS1–SS5; insufficient evidence from TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable Al₂S₃ polymorph because its E_hull is only 0.024 eV/atom [target data]. The chemical-similarity evidence is strong: exact-composition Al₂S₃ polymorphs were synthesized from Al and S in sealed evacuated ampoules, even though those reference phases are much higher in energy than the target [CS1, CS2]. Structural and thermodynamic analogues further support that Al–S and related chalcogenide frameworks crystallize by sealed-tube solid-state reaction or CVT under accessible temperatures [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the simple binary chemistry and demonstrated Al₂S₃ formation from elements at 750 °C [CS1, CS2]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the target Pmc2₁ layered structure must compete with other Al₂S₃ polymorphs, so temperature history, sulfur chemical potential, and quenching are likely decisive [CS1, CS2; target structure].
