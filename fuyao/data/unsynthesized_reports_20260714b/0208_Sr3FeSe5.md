### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket [Target]. Several thermodynamically similar selenides are also hull-stable, including KTa(AgSe2)2, KNb(AgSe2)2, CsYb3Se4, NaYbSe2, and Sr2GeSe4 [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee easy kinetic access, especially for a complex Sr-rich Fe selenide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 solid-state severity.**  
Solid-state analogues use high temperatures, sealed ampoules, vacuum or Ar, and sometimes refiring or quenching [CS1, CS2, SS1]. The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, volatile Se, multistep processing, and air/moisture-sensitive SrSe or Sr-containing precursors [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS alone to define exact solid-state conditions for Sr3FeSe5, so TS is neutral for route-specific severity except for Sr2GeSe4 precedent [TS5].

**F3 — Route 1 solid-state kinetics.**  
The key kinetic penalty is the use of dense Fe metal and stable SrSe, which require substantial diffusion and reconstruction [SS1, CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Analogues show long reaction times, excess Se, regrinding, and phase-control issues such as impurities or polyphasic products [CS1, CS2, SS1]. This supports a moderate-low kinetic score.

**F2 — Route 2 flux severity.**  
Flux routes avoid very high temperatures but require vacuum-sealed ampoules, slow thermal profiles, volatile Se/polyselenides, and washing or flux separation [SS2, SS3, TS4]. Chloride and chalcogenide flux analogues use 450–500 °C to 1123 K for up to a week [SS2–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The severity is moderate rather than easy because air-sensitive polyselenides/SrSe and sealed tubes are still required [TS4; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 flux kinetics.**  
Flux growth improves mass transport, and structurally/thermodynamically similar selenides are reported as single crystals from fluxes [SS2, SS3, TS3, TS4]. The score is not higher because reported flux systems sometimes have low yields or require precise temperature programming [SS2, SS3, TS1, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS for a directly analogous Sr–Fe–Se flux route, so the route remains an inference rather than a confirmed transfer.

**F2 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman severity.**  
RbFe2Se3 can be grown by slow cooling stoichiometric melts and modified Bridgman growth [CS5]. However, sealed ampoules, high temperatures near 900–1050 °C, volatile Se, and careful cooling are required [CS5, SS1]. The route is more severe than flux because high Se vapor pressure and uncertain melting behavior increase risk [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman kinetics.**  
Liquid or partially molten conditions can enhance crystallization, as shown for RbFe2Se3 [CS5]. The uncertainty is whether Sr3FeSe5 melts congruently or decomposes into SrSe and Fe–Se phases, for which no direct CS/SS/TS evidence is available. Therefore the kinetic score is moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Sr/Fe cation size mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk check [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target polyhedra show corner and edge sharing, but no face sharing, and the likely unit cell is not obviously above 40 atoms [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of Sr3FeSe5, so the risk is judged low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr3FeSe5 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Sr selenides made by sealed solid-state reactions [CS1, CS2, TS5] and structurally related Fe–selenide frameworks formed under sealed high-temperature conditions [SS1, CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining selenium chemical potential in a sealed system, since many successful analogues use evacuated or sealed ampoules with Se-containing precursors [CS2, SS1, SS2, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and slow Fe/Sr diffusion, since related systems report impurities, amorphous competing phases, repeated heating, or low-yield crystal growth [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2]. Overall, synthesis is plausible and moderately favorable, with flux growth offering the best balance of transport, crystallinity, and manageable temperature.
