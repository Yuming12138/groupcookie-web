| Field | Details |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-stability cutoff used in the rubric [Target]. The strongest CS/SS entry is essentially the same CaCo₂O₄ phase and is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which supports experimental accessibility [CS1, SS1]. TS analogues with E_hull values from ~0.0316 to 0.0773 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, so TS supports that this metastability range is not prohibitive [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target’s 0.0500 eV/atom polymorph is identical to the zero-hull CS/SS analogue or a closely related metastable variant [Target, CS1, SS1].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is severe because it requires 1500 °C and 6 GPa, both directly stated for CaCo₂O₄ synthesis [CS1, SS1]. The route avoids H₂/vacuum chemistry and volatile sealed precursors, but the high-pressure apparatus and multi-step precursor/capsule workflow impose major penalties [CS1, SS1]. TS3 gives general support that high-pressure oxide synthesis can stabilize metastable oxide structures, but CS/SS are weighted more heavily because they are exact to CaCo₂O₄ [TS3, CS1, SS1].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to other solid-state routes because the reported precursor is already a Ca–Co–O cobaltate rather than only binary oxides [CS1, SS1]. The 1500 °C hold should overcome cation diffusion limitations, and the exact structure is already demonstrated by CS/SS [CS1, SS1]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is reproducibility and phase purity, since high-pressure phases often have narrow stability windows [CS1, SS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 keeps the high-pressure requirement from the direct CaCo₂O₄ route but may reduce the final temperature by using a chemically homogeneous precursor [CS1, SS1, CS4]. It adds wet-chemistry, calcination, and toxic cobalt nitrate/oxalate handling, so its condition severity remains high [CS4, Reasonable Inference]. TS3 supports using chemically mixed nitrate-derived precursors before high-pressure oxide synthesis [TS3].

**F3 — Route 2**  
Nitrate/oxalate or polymer-complex precursors have high activity and should reduce diffusion length scales [CS4, TS3]. Structural reconstruction remains nontrivial because the precursor must crystallize into Pnma CaCo₂O₄ under pressure [CS1, SS1, Reasonable Inference]. This route is a reasonable optimization, not a directly reported synthesis of CaCo₂O₄ [CS4, CS1, SS1].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux analogues operate below the direct 1500 °C high-pressure route, so the thermal and pressure severity is lower [SS2, SS5, TS2]. However, chloride/nitrate fluxes introduce containment, volatility/corrosion, washing, and possible impurity risks [SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS for flux stabilization of CaCo₂O₄, so CS is neutral 50 for this route [insufficient evidence from CS].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux transport improves kinetics and enables cobalt oxide crystal growth in several SS/TS analogues [SS2, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS2]. The key kinetic weakness is structural reconstruction: none of the flux examples directly form CaCo₂O₄, and the strongest exact analogue uses high pressure [CS1, SS1]. Thus, flux is plausible for screening or crystal growth but uncertain as a primary route [SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Ambient-pressure controlled-pO₂ solid-state annealing avoids 6 GPa equipment, but it still requires high temperatures and repeated heat/grind cycles [CS2, CS3, CS5]. Ferrite analogues use H₂–H₂O/vacuum control, but CaCo₂O₄ should prefer oxidizing conditions because charge balance implies Co³⁺ [CS2, CS5, Target, Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for ambient-pressure CaCo₂O₄ formation, so they are neutral 50 for this route [insufficient evidence from SS/TS].

**F3 — Route 4**  
Carbonate/oxide precursors have moderate-to-low activity, and long anneals in calcium ferrite analogues indicate slow ordering and narrow phase fields [CS2, CS3, CS5]. The route requires complete reconstruction into the Pnma CoO₆ framework without the high-pressure stabilization used in the direct analogue [CS1, SS1]. The main uncertainty is whether ambient pressure can stabilize the target rather than Ca₃Co₄O₉ or other Ca–Co–O phases [CS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 5**  
Topochemical/molten-salt exchange has mild temperatures and ambient pressure, supported by sodium/Ag cobaltate analogues [SS4, TS4]. It is penalized for multi-step precursor synthesis, washing, volatile/toxic redox media, and molten-salt handling [SS4, TS4]. CS provides insufficient evidence for Ca²⁺ topochemical exchange into CaCo₂O₄, so CS is neutral 50 for this route [insufficient evidence from CS].

**F3 — Route 5**  
Molten salts can enable ion transport, but Ca²⁺ exchange and charge compensation are harder than monovalent Na⁺/Ag⁺ exchange [SS4, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction into Pnma CaCo₂O₄ is likely substantial, and TS4 explicitly notes residual precursor issues in related ion-exchange chemistry [TS4]. This route is best treated as exploratory rather than a primary synthesis path [SS4, TS4].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a large cation-size contrast between 8-coordinate Ca and octahedral Co, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Target, CS1, SS1, Reasonable Inference]. The reported Ca₄Co₈O₁₆ cell has 28 atoms, so the unit-cell-size criterion is not triggered [CS1, SS1]. The structure contains corner- and edge-sharing CoO₆ octahedra but no reported face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered [Target, CS1, SS1]. TS does not provide exact target connectivity, so TS is neutral 50 for F4 [insufficient evidence from TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCo₂O₄ is best viewed as a borderline-stable or weakly metastable phase because the target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, while the closest CS/SS analogue is reported at zero hull [Target, CS1, SS1]. The analogue support is unusually strong because the top CS and SS entries are essentially the same CaCo₂O₄/Pnma material and give a direct high-pressure recipe [CS1, SS1]. The most critical enabling factor is high-pressure stabilization at ~6 GPa combined with high temperature, which directly produced the CaCo₂O₄ phase [CS1, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is the severe equipment/condition requirement and likely competition from other Ca–Co–O phases such as Ca₃Co₄O₉ under milder synthesis routes [CS1, SS1, CS4]. Overall, the material is synthesizable, but the most reliable route is specialized high-pressure synthesis rather than conventional ambient-pressure ceramic processing [CS1, SS1].
