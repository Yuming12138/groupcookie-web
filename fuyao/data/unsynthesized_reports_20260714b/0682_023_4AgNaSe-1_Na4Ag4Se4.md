### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

NaAgSe has E_hull = 0.0180 eV/atom, which is within the high-probability metastable/near-ground-state window. KAgSe, the closest P4/nmm analogue, is on the hull (CS-1; SS-5), and many Ag–alkali chalcogenide analogues are also on the hull or within a few meV/atom (SS-1 to SS-4). TS further supports that materials with comparable small positive hull energies can be synthesized, including KAg₃S₂ at 0.009 eV/atom and Na₃As at 0.043 eV/atom (TS-1; TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether NaAgSe is a true equilibrium low-temperature phase or only accessible under controlled cooling.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct elemental route

The severity is moderate because the route uses 750–800 °C, Ar, and sealed containment rather than high pressure or >1000 °C synthesis (CS-1; SS-5; TS-2). The main penalties are Na/Se volatility and air sensitivity, inferred from Na ampoule loading in TS-2 and excess K/inert handling for KAgSe in CS-1/SS-5. Kinetics are favorable because KAgSe forms directly in the same P4/nmm structure, but Ag metal diffusion and 24 h annealing justify not assigning a perfect kinetics score (CS-1; SS-5). TS gives useful support for sealed Na handling but insufficient evidence from TS for the exact Na–Ag–Se P4/nmm phase, so TS is neutral for exact structure transferability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Carbonate/flux-fusion route

The route is more severe than direct synthesis because carbonate-fusion syntheses often require 850–1000 °C, slow cooling, washing, and management of volatile Se (SS-1 to SS-3; CS-5). Kinetics are good because flux/fusion reactions form Ag–alkali chalcogenides within hours, but structural selectivity is weaker because the cited selenide products are mostly Ag-rich C2/m phases rather than 1:1:1 P4/nmm NaAgSe (SS-1 to SS-3). CS supports analogous sulfide carbonate fusion (CS-5), while CS-1/SS-5 suggest that the P4/nmm target may instead prefer direct stoichiometric elemental reaction. TS provides insufficient evidence for carbonate flux specifically, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Binary selenide sintering route

This route has lower temperature but greater handling severity because Na₂Se/Ag₂Se-type precursors are air-sensitive and may require prior synthesis or inert transfer (CS-3; Reasonable Inference). Kinetics are acceptable because alkali selenide sintering successfully forms ternary alkali selenides at 673–873 K, but CS-3 requires 2–7 days, indicating slower solid-state equilibration. Structural reconstruction is moderate because binary selenides must rearrange into the layered P4/nmm target rather than an already related precursor topology [Reasonable Inference]. SS gives only indirect support via Ag–alkali–Se chemistry, and TS is neutral for this specific binary-selenide route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Supercritical ethylenediamine route

The low synthesis temperature is attractive, but the sealed-tube/autoclave protocol, 2400 psi Ar counterpressure, supercritical ethylenediamine, and drybox handling make the route experimentally challenging (SS-4; TS-1). Kinetics are strong because supercritical ethylenediamine enables crystalline Ag chalcogenides at ~300–340 °C and avoids bulk diffusion, with TS-1 showing subcritical reactions gave amorphous products. The largest uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS-4 produces Ag-rich RbAg₅Se₃ and reports mixed products during optimization, not the 1:1:1 P4/nmm target. CS gives insufficient evidence for this supercritical route, so CS is neutral for Route 4 transferability.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small, ordered P4/nmm cell analogous to KAgSe (Target; CS-1; SS-5), so large-cell amorphization risk is low. The cation size contrast between Na⁺ and Ag⁺ is not expected to exceed the >15% trigger in high coordination [Reasonable Inference], and the target description reports corner and face sharing but not the full vertex+edge+face coexistence criterion. SS and CS analogues show crystalline Ag–alkali–Se compounds are readily obtained under high-temperature or flux conditions (CS-1; SS-1 to SS-5). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for amorphization in NaAgSe, but TS-1 warns that inappropriate solvothermal conditions can yield amorphous products.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaAgSe is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.018 eV/atom. The strongest evidence for synthesis is the direct same-structure analogue KAgSe, which crystallizes in tetragonal P4/nmm and is made from elements at 800 °C under Ar (CS-1; SS-5). Broader SS data show Ag–alkali–selenide chemistry is robust, although many flux products are Ag-rich rather than 1:1:1 (SS-1 to SS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural and chemical match to KAgSe, making Na substitution highly plausible (CS-1; SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Na and Se stoichiometry during high-temperature synthesis because both can be volatile/reactive and because competing Na₂Se/Ag₂Se or Ag-rich phases may form [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, SS-1 to SS-3, TS-2].
