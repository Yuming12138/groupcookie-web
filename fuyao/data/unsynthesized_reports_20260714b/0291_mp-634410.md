### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom is exactly at the favorable threshold and receives 90/100 under the provided rubric (Target). TS analogues with similar low E_hull values are experimentally accessible by mild solution routes, including Tl2CN2 at 0.0536 eV/atom, PbCN2 at 0.0376 eV/atom, and BaN3Cl at 0.0870 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). CS gives contradictory computed thermodynamics because experimentally grown NaN3 polymorph entries have much higher calculated E_hull values, yet they are synthesized by slow evaporation, so CS suggests computation may over-penalize some NaN3 polymorphs (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). SS gives insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence for NaN3 stability and is scored neutral 50 for this sub-factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is very mild because the exact NaN3 chemical analogue is grown by slow evaporation from ethanol under ambient or implied room-temperature conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). There is no evidence for high pressure, high temperature, vacuum, H2/NH3, or sealed ampoule requirements in the direct NaN3 references (CS-1 to CS-3). The only deduction is for sodium azide toxicity/energetic hazard, consistent with broader azide safety concerns in structurally similar Ag/Au azides (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because the precursor is already NaN3 and the process is recrystallization, not a solid-state reaction requiring long-range Na/N diffusion (CS-1 to CS-3). The reported production of single crystals suitable for diffraction indicates that nucleation and growth are manageable under slow evaporation (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The residual uncertainty is polymorph selectivity: the target C2 phase is related to but not identical with the C2/m NaN3 structures in CS (CS-1 to CS-3; Target).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Aqueous evaporation routes are also mild because related azides and azide salts are crystallized at room temperature in water or by desiccator evaporation (SS-4, SS-5, TS-3). There is no high-temperature, high-pressure, or controlled-atmosphere requirement in these analogues (SS-4, SS-5, TS-3). As with Route 1, sodium azide toxicity and the possibility of forming sensitive heavy-metal azides justify a safety deduction (SS-4, SS-5).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Solution transport makes cation/anion diffusion easy, and structurally similar azides produce crystals suitable for X-ray work from aqueous solution (SS-4, SS-5). TS-3 shows that an azide-containing salt, BaN3Cl, can be obtained by slow aqueous crystallization in a desiccator over CaCl2, supporting slow evaporation as a general azide crystallization strategy. The uncertainty is that direct aqueous recrystallization of pure NaN3 is not explicitly shown in CS/SS/TS, so ethanol remains the better-supported solvent for the target (CS-1 to CS-3).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Cooling crystallization uses mild temperatures and ambient pressure, as structurally similar complex azides crystallize from solution by cooling or aging (SS-1). However, compared with simple slow evaporation, it may require more procedural control: heating/dissolution, filtration, controlled cooling, and aging, so a multi-step deduction is applied ([Reasonable Inference] from SS-1). The same azide toxicity deduction applies because NaN3 is the essential precursor (CS-1 to CS-3; SS-5).

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Precursor activity is high because the solute is already NaN3, and solution crystallization avoids bulk diffusion barriers (CS-1 to CS-3). Cooling crystallization is supported by SS-1 for complex azides, but SS-1 also reports long crystallization times for K2Cd(N3)4, implying nucleation/growth may be slow or sensitive. The key uncertainty is whether cooling selects the desired C2 polymorph rather than the C2/m variants observed in CS (CS-1 to CS-3; Target).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple single-cation ionic azide, so cation-size disorder is absent (Target). Direct CS data shows NaN3 can crystallize as single crystals rather than amorphous material by mild evaporation, which strongly lowers amorphization concern (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The target contains edge- and corner-sharing motifs but no reported face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met (Target). There is insufficient evidence from SS and TS to quantify NaN3 amorphization directly, so those sources are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable NaN3 polymorph because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold (Target). The chemical-similarity evidence is exceptionally strong because the same composition, NaN3, is repeatedly crystallized from ethanol by slow evaporation, although the reported structures are mainly C2/m rather than the target C2 polymorph (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Structurally similar azides further support mild solution crystallization as a viable route, including precipitation, diffusion, slow evaporation, and cooling crystallization (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is that NaN3 can be recrystallized directly from solution without high-temperature synthesis or precursor conversion (CS-1 to CS-3). The main limiting factor is polymorph control and safe azide handling, because energetic azide analogues can be explosive and sensitive to impurities or handling (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5).
