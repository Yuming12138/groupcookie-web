### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target Eₕᵤₗₗ of 0.041 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable near-hull range, giving 92/100 by the provided rubric. CS-1/SS-1 report the closest PtI₂ analogue at Eₕᵤₗₗ = 0.0 eV/atom, strongly supporting thermodynamic plausibility. TS analogues show several near-hull halides with successful syntheses around 0.028–0.077 eV/atom, but they are not Pt–I compounds, so their support is indirect (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is a slightly different PtI₂ polymorph/orientation than the exact stable analogue, so it may be metastable rather than the ground-state form.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses 420 °C hydrothermal conditions for 2–3 weeks in closed quartz ampoules with CO₂ counter-pressure, which is demanding but not ultra-high-temperature or GPa pressure (CS-1, SS-1). Deductions are mainly for volatile I₂ in a sealed system, multistep ampoule/autoclave processing, and hazardous iodine/Pt iodide handling (CS-1, SS-1; [Reasonable Inference]). TS is insufficiently specific for this hydrothermal PtI₂ route and is treated as neutral for severity transferability. The route remains practical for a specialized solid-state/materials chemistry laboratory.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal solution chemistry should provide high precursor activity and bypass slow Pt solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/SS-1 aqueous synthesis]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because CS-1/SS-1 are essentially the same layered PtI₂/P2₁/c family as the target. The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the desired polymorph and crystal perfection, because CS-1/SS-1 note mixtures, defects, twinning, and poor crystal quality.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is milder in temperature at ~290 °C and uses closed glass ampoules under low-pressure hydrothermal conditions (CS-2, SS-2). The same deductions apply for sealed volatile iodine, multistep ampoule handling, and iodine/Pt iodide hazards (CS-2, SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). CS/SS support the condition envelope, while TS gives insufficient direct signal for this PtI₂/PtI₃ aqueous boundary and is neutral. The condition severity is therefore similar to Route 1 despite lower temperature, because pressure/phase-control constraints remain significant.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The aqueous PtI₄/KI/I₂ medium should be reactive, and hydrothermal transport should enable crystallization without solid-state diffusion limitations (CS-2, SS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). However, the demonstrated phase is PtI₃, not PtI₂, and CS-2/SS-2 explicitly describe a narrow existence region with adjacent PtI₂/PtI₄ impurities. The route is kinetically plausible but compositionally sensitive, so it scores lower than the direct PtI₂ route.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT/sealed vapor transport uses moderate temperatures in the 150–450 °C range in analogues, but requires evacuated or Ar-filled sealed ampoules and volatile halogen transport agents (SS-3, SS-4, TS-1, TS-5). Deductions are therefore applied for vacuum/controlled atmosphere, volatile I₂, multistep ampoule preparation, and toxic/air-sensitive halide handling. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for PtI₂ CVT and is neutral. The route is experimentally common but less direct than hydrothermal synthesis.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
I₂ vapor transport is well supported in analogous halide systems, and sealed gradients can grow crystalline iodides/halides (SS-4, TS-1, TS-5). The weak point is Pt volatility and the need to form transportable Pt–I species without overshooting into higher-valent Pt iodides [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/SS-2 phase sensitivity]. Because no direct PtI₂ CVT synthesis is reported in CS/SS/TS, composition control is scored only moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is chemically simple with one cation and a relatively small PtI₂ layered unit, so C1 and C2 are not triggered [Target structure; CS-1/SS-1]. The target has square-planar Pt bonded to iodide in a 2D sheet rather than a mixed polyhedral network with multiple sharing modes, so C3 is not triggered [Target structure]. CS-1/SS-1 show crystalline PtI₂ analogues, supporting low amorphization risk. TS gives insufficient PtI₂-specific amorphization evidence and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PtI₂ phase is likely **slightly metastable** because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.041 eV/atom, although this is close enough to the hull to be highly accessible. The CS and SS evidence is very strong because the top chemical and structural analogue is PtI₂ in monoclinic P2₁/c made by hydrothermal synthesis from PtI₄/KI/I₂ (CS-1, SS-1). TS evidence is supportive but indirect, showing that near-hull halide/iodide compounds can be grown in sealed ampoules or transport conditions (TS-1, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated iodide chemistry, which can crystallize Pt–I phases at 290–420 °C without requiring refractory Pt solid-state diffusion (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase control among PtI₂, PtI₃, and PtI₄, because the Pt–I hydrothermal system has narrow stability regions and reported twinning/defects (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2).
