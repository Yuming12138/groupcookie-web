### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff, so it receives 90/100. CS and SS contain many Na/alkali transition-metal fluorides that are stable or nearly stable, while TS includes related metastable materials around similar hull energies. The remaining uncertainty is whether the P2_1/c Na3FeF6 polymorph is the experimentally accessible low-temperature phase or a metastable ordered variant.

**F2/F3 Route 1 — Solid-state.**  
CS and SS strongly support binary-fluoride solid-state synthesis, but CS also shows strict O₂/H₂O exclusion and long heating at 750 °C for 8 d. Kinetics are moderate because binary fluoride diffusion and Na/Fe ordering may be slow, as implied by multi-day CS/SS anneals and 1–2 week TS anneals. The route is robust but likely less efficient than a flux route.

**F2/F3 Route 2 — Flux.**  
SS provides the strongest transferability: FeF3 reacts in fused KHF2/NaHF2 to form K2NaFeF6, a structurally similar Fe(III) double fluoride. The molten bifluoride medium lowers diffusion barriers and promotes crystallization, justifying high F3. The main penalties are HF/bifluoride toxicity, volatility, and post-reaction flux removal.

**F2/F3 Route 3 — Direct fluorination.**  
SS demonstrates normal-pressure fluorination at 550 °C can produce Fe(III) perovskite-derived fluorides, so kinetics can be favorable. However, F2 handling is severe, specialized, and hazardous, which strongly lowers F2. Phase control for Na-only Na3FeF6 is less certain than for large-cation elpasolites.

**F2/F3 Route 4 — Hydrothermal.**  
SS and TS show HF hydrothermal media can crystallize Fe/alkali fluoride or oxyfluoride phases at 150–250 °C, so temperature severity and diffusion barriers are low. The uncertainty is chemical selectivity: TS hydrothermal examples often form oxyfluorides, while the target is anhydrous fluoride. Strong fluoride activity and low water activity would be essential.

**F2/F3 Route 5 — High pressure.**  
CS/SS/TS show high pressure can access dense or metastable fluoride polymorphs, especially for NaFeF3 and elpasolite-like systems. The severe equipment penalty dominates F2, and HP may produce an unintended polymorph. This is best reserved as a rescue route if ambient-pressure routes fail.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a substantial Na⁺/Fe³⁺ size and charge contrast, which can promote disorder or glassy/poorly ordered products if quenched too fast. However, the target is mostly corner-sharing octahedral/coordination network rather than a highly mixed edge/face-sharing structure, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that amorphization is a dominant issue for these fluorides. Thus the amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na3FeF6 is likely a borderline stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest synthetic support comes from SS, where Fe(III) Na-containing fluoride frameworks are made by bifluoride flux, and from CS, where Na–Fe fluorides form under anhydrous high-temperature conditions. The most important enabling factor is maintaining a high fluoride chemical potential with good ion mobility, best achieved by NaHF2/NaF flux. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing NaF, FeF3, NaFeF3, or hydrated/oxyfluoride products could form if stoichiometry, moisture, or redox conditions are not controlled. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with flux growth the best first attempt.
