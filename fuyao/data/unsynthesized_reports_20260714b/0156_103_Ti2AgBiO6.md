### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold specified in the scoring rubric, so it receives **90/100**. CS analogues are synthesized at low hull energies, including CS1 at ~0.021 eV/atom, CS2 at ~0.007 eV/atom, CS3 at 0 eV/atom, CS4 at ~0.008 eV/atom, and CS5 at ~0.024 eV/atom. TS analogues include experimentally accessible phases at comparable or higher metastability, such as TS3/TS4 near 0.054–0.066 eV/atom and TS5 at ~0.088 eV/atom, supporting the idea that 0.050 eV/atom is synthetically reachable. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact Ti₂AgBiO₆ composition is not represented in CS/SS/TS, and Ag/Bi redox volatility may narrow the kinetic window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses air, ambient pressure, and ≤850–900 °C, which is consistent with CS1/CS2 and does not trigger high-temperature or controlled-atmosphere penalties. It does require repeated grinding/firing, as seen in CS1, CS2, CS3, and CS5, so a multi-step deduction is applied. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct oxide solid-state synthesis of Ti–Ag–Bi oxide specifically, so SS is neutral for this route-specific transferability. The overall condition severity is modest, hence **90/100**.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The oxide solid-state route has limited precursor activity because TiO₂ is refractory [Reasonable Inference], although Bi₂O₃ can assist reaction by softening/fluxing [Reasonable Inference] and is widely used in CS1–CS4. CS1/CS2/CS3/CS5 repeatedly show long firing, regrinding, or refiring, indicating slow solid-state kinetics and phase-purity challenges. The target’s P1 structure with multiple distorted Ti, Ag, and Bi coordination environments likely requires substantial reconstruction from binary oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore Route 1 receives a moderate-low kinetic score of **48/100**.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 remains below or near 1000 °C and uses ambient pressure, consistent with CS4 flux growth and CS5 crucible heating/cooling. It requires staged heating, slow cooling, and flux separation, so the multi-step penalty is applied. CS4 uses a Pt crucible and no special pressure; CS5 uses alumina crucible and controlled cooling. Because no sealed vacuum/reactive atmosphere is required, the severity remains low, giving **90/100**.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux synthesis improves kinetic access by enhancing mass transport; this is directly supported by CS4, where BiMg₂VO₆ single crystals are grown from Bi₂O₃ flux. CS5 also suggests controlled cooling can stabilize complex low-symmetry Bi-transition-metal oxides. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether TiO₂ dissolves sufficiently and whether Ag remains as Ag⁺ rather than reducing or segregating [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, Route 2 receives **65/100** for kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses ambient pressure and moderate crystallization temperatures, so it does not trigger high-temperature, high-pressure, or controlled-atmosphere penalties. It is multi-step because gelation, drying, organic burnout, calcination, and crystallization are all required [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sol-gel synthesis of the analogues, so the severity assessment is based on general chemical processing inference rather than direct source evidence. The severity score is **90/100**.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The sol-gel route offers the best precursor-scale homogeneity because Ti, Ag, and Bi can be mixed at molecular or near-molecular length scales [Reasonable Inference]. This addresses the repeated grinding/refiring issue observed in CS1/CS2/CS3/CS5, but it introduces a crystallization challenge because a low-symmetry ordered P1 phase must nucleate from an amorphous precursor [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 warns that phase control in Bi-transition-metal oxides can be nontrivial because an attempted synthesis produced a different compound. Route 3 receives **65/100**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines cations with very different sizes and coordination preferences: Ti is short-bonded to oxygen, Ag is three-coordinate with ~2.31–2.38 Å Ag–O bonds, and Bi is three-coordinate with ~2.14–2.18 Å Bi–O bonds in the target structure. This radius/coordination mismatch triggers C1 and increases risk of glassy or poorly crystalline intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom count appears small from the P1 formula description, and the target description does not explicitly demonstrate simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. F4 is therefore **78/100**, indicating low-to-medium amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂AgBiO₆ is likely a marginally metastable but accessible phase rather than a strongly stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and TS analogues strongly support that Bi-containing complex oxides and Ti/Ag oxide frameworks can be synthesized by conventional heating or flux methods, while SS analogues provide more general structural/kinetic support rather than exact chemical transferability. The most critical enabling factor is the use of Bi₂O₃-rich flux or highly homogeneous precursors to overcome slow Ti–O solid-state diffusion, supported most directly by CS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving Ag⁺/Ag⁰ and Bi volatility, which is [Reasonable Inference] based on Ag/Bi oxide chemistry and the phase-control issues seen in CS2 and CS5. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with reactive flux or sol-gel-assisted crystallization preferred over a purely dry oxide route.
