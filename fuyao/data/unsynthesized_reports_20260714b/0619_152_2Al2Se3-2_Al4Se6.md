### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.024 eV/atom falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin, so it receives a high F1 score of 95. CS evidence is encouraging because Al₂S₃ polymorphs with much larger E_hull values near 0.50 eV/atom are still experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube reaction and quenching (CS1, CS2). SS/TS evidence is also favorable because low-hull Al–Se and selenide analogues are synthesized by conventional sealed-tube solid-state routes, including BaAl₄Se₇ and Mg₂Al₂Se₅ (SS1, SS3, TS1). The remaining uncertainty is that the exact Pmmn Al₂Se₃ polymorph is not directly reported in the reference set.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because analogues use 750–1000 °C sealed-ampoule conditions, not >1000–1400 °C high-pressure synthesis (CS1, CS2, SS3). The main penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Se/chalcogen management, and Se toxicity, all consistent with sealed ampoule use in Al₂S₃ and selenide syntheses (CS1, CS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). The route is not scored as a ≥3-step chemical process because direct elemental loading and heating is conceptually one reaction sequence. Uncertainty remains in whether quenching or slow cooling gives the target Pmmn polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Al is less reactive than salts or molecular precursors, but Se melting/vapor transport should improve contact **[Reasonable Inference]**, and elemental routes are experimentally successful for Al₂S₃ and Mg₂Al₂Se₅ (CS1, CS2, SS3). Diffusion is favorable because Al has a low melting point relative to refractory cations **[Reasonable Inference]**. The low structural-reconstruction score reflects the need to build the AlSe₄ sheet network from elements, and Al₂S₃ analogues require long holds and quenching for polymorph control (CS1, CS2). The largest uncertainty is whether the binary Al–Se reaction forms the desired layered phase or a competing Al₂Se₃ polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses similar moderate temperatures but adds penalties for multistep handling if Al₂Se₃ must be pre-synthesized, ground, resealed, annealed, and quenched, consistent with multicycle homogenization used in TS2/TS3. Vacuum sealing and inert handling are justified by Al₂Se₃ use in sealed-tube BaAl₄Se₇ synthesis and glovebox loading (SS1, TS1). Toxic/air-sensitive handling is a practical concern for aluminum selenides **[Reasonable Inference]**, and related selenide syntheses use gloveboxes or sealed ampoules (SS1, SS5, TS1). The route is conditionally best if a suitable Al₂Se₃ precursor is available.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Pre-reacted Al₂Se₃ should lower kinetic barriers because the Al:Se stoichiometry and local bonding motifs already exist **[Reasonable Inference]**; Al₂Se₃ is explicitly used as a solid-state precursor in SS1/TS1. Ion diffusion remains favorable because only Al cations must rearrange and the relevant temperatures are 700–850 °C **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with CS1/CS2/SS1. Structural reconstruction is partial rather than complete, but phase selection may require quenching by analogy to metastable Al₂S₃ (CS1, CS2). The main uncertainty is whether annealing converts to the desired Pmmn sheet structure or to a lower-energy Al₂Se₃ polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT uses moderate high temperatures but requires controlled vacuum ampoules, volatile/corrosive transport agents, and a temperature gradient, as shown for GaS/GaSe and MnIn₂Se₄ (CS3, CS5, TS4). The multistep penalty is justified because CVT generally requires charge preparation, ampoule loading, sealing, and gradient growth; this is directly reflected in CS3/CS5/TS4. Toxic/corrosive risk arises from I₂ or AlCl₃ transport agents **[Reasonable Inference]**, with AlCl₃ handling complexity noted in TS4. The absence of direct Al₂Se₃ CVT evidence keeps this route challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor availability is plausible because I₂ transports layered Ga chalcogenides and AlCl₃ transports MnIn₂Se₄ analogues (CS3, CS5, TS4). Crystallization of layered chalcogenides from vapor is supported by CS3/CS5/TS4, but the exact Pmmn Al₂Se₃ polymorph is not confirmed. Composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because Se volatility and halide transport can create non-stoichiometric deposition **[Reasonable Inference]**, and analogous CVT systems require optimized gradients and transport-agent concentrations (CS5, TS4). This route may be most useful for single crystals after bulk synthesis is established.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Al, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The provided target description does not indicate a large >40 atom unit cell, and the formula Al₂Se₃ is simple; however, CS/SS/TS do not directly prove the target cell size, so their signal is neutral for that check. The target has corner- and edge-sharing AlSe₄ tetrahedra, but the stated criterion requires vertex + edge + face sharing coexistence, and face sharing is not reported. Therefore the amorphization tendency is low, with the main uncertainty being polymorph selection rather than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Al₂Se₃ Pmmn phase is likely a mildly metastable phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.024 eV/atom. The CS analogues are especially encouraging because metastable Al₂S₃ polymorphs with much higher hull energies are experimentally accessible by sealed-tube synthesis and quenching (CS1, CS2). The SS/TS analogues support the broader feasibility of Al–Se tetrahedral networks and sealed-tube selenide synthesis at 800–900 °C (SS1, SS3, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty combined with simple binary stoichiometry. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the synthesis must avoid more stable Al₂Se₃ polymorphs and retain the layered orthorhombic Pmmn arrangement.
