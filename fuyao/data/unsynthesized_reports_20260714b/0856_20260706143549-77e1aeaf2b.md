### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin. Several analogues also have zero or near-zero hull energies, including LaAlH6, PrAlH6, NdAlH6, Zr(BH4)4, ZrH2, TiH2, CrH2, and TaH2 (CS-2–5; SS-1–3,5; TS-1–5). The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not specify whether YH10 is stable only under pressure or recoverable at ambient pressure, so pressure-dependent stability is inferred rather than proven [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
DAC synthesis is penalized heavily because the route requires very high pressure, hydrogen containment, laser heating, and specialized handling [Reasonable Inference]. High-pressure need is supported by Mg6VH16 and Mg7TiH16 anvil-cell syntheses (CS-1; SS-4). SS/TS electrochemical hydride routes are insufficient evidence for H10 synthesis and are therefore neutral for this severity estimate (SS-1–3; TS-1–3,5).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are partly helped by micron-scale DAC geometry and laser heating [Reasonable Inference]. However, elemental Y is a dense metallic precursor and the YH10 clathrate-like H network requires major hydrogen incorporation and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Fm-3m hydride analogues support structural feasibility but not direct YH10 formation (CS-1; SS-4).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Large-volume high-pressure synthesis remains severe because it still requires >5 GPa, a sealed H-rich environment, and controlled quenching [Reasonable Inference]. The high-pressure anvil-cell precedent is directly supported by Mg6VH16 and Mg7TiH16 (CS-1; SS-4). The lack of exact temperature/time data in CS-1/SS-4 adds uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Bulk diffusion is more difficult than in DAC because sample dimensions are larger [Reasonable Inference]. Elemental metals and hydrogen can produce high-pressure hydrides, as shown by Mg6VH16/Mg7TiH16, but those sources do not provide a detailed kinetic window (CS-1; SS-4). Binary MH2 electrochemical/arc routes demonstrate hydride accessibility but not H10 stoichiometry, so their transferability is limited (SS-1–3; TS-1–3,5).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The hybrid mechanochemical/HP route is severe because it combines air-sensitive hydride handling with high pressure and a multistep workflow. Ball milling under Ar/H2 is supported by REAlH6 analogues, which use NaAlH4 and rare-earth chlorides for 4–10 h under 1–15 bar H2 (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5; SS-5; TS-4). High-pressure need still comes from H-rich anvil-cell hydrides Mg6VH16/Mg7TiH16 (CS-1; SS-4).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Mechanochemical activation improves precursor reactivity and reduces diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], consistent with successful ball-milled REAlH6 and Zr(BH4)4 syntheses (CS-2–5; SS-5; TS-4). Phase control is still difficult because REAlH6 analogues show low yields, impurities, and partial decomposition during milling (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5; SS-5; TS-4). The high-pressure anneal is expected to supply the structural driving force toward the dense Fm-3m hydride [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference]. The conventional Fm-3m YH10 cell is likely large enough to trigger the atom-count check [Reasonable Inference], but the target description reports edge/corner sharing rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of YH10, so this factor remains partly inferential.
