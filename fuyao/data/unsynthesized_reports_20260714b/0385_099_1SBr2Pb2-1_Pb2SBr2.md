### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which lies in the high-synthesizability range and suggests it is either a ground-state-adjacent or very weakly metastable phase. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally accessible, including Pb₇Bi₄Se₁₃ at 0.0115 eV/atom (TS-1), RbPb₂Br₅ at 0.0080 eV/atom (TS-2), and PbI₂ polytype at 0.0019 eV/atom (TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms Pb₂SBr₂, but the thermodynamic signal is strongly favorable.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses sealed tubes and moderate temperatures, similar to Pb/Bi chalcogenide sealed reactions at 773 K (CS-2 / SS-4 / TS-1). The deductions come from vacuum/sealed handling, Pb toxicity, and volatile halide management; sealed halide reactions are common in CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, and SS-3. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no direct Pb₂SBr₂ reaction temperature, so 450–550 °C is a **[Reasonable Inference]** based on PbBr₂ participation.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

PbS + PbBr₂ should be kinetically more favorable than refractory oxide routes because PbBr₂ can melt and act as a reactive halide flux **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structurally, PbS octahedral motifs in SS-5 are close to the target Pb–S sublattice, lowering reconstruction burden. However, CS-2 / SS-4 / TS-1 note long anneals or phase-control issues in related Pb chalcogenides, so the structural score is not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT is well supported by chalcogen-halide and layered Pb chalcogenide analogues (CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, SS-3, TS-3), but it requires sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile halogen transport agents. The route is more complex than simple annealing because it usually involves pre-reaction plus gradient growth, as in CS-1 and SS-3. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly defines the optimal transport agent for Pb₂SBr₂, so Br₂/PbBr₂/I₂ selection is a **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT offers good kinetics because growth proceeds through mobile vapor species rather than bulk diffusion, as demonstrated for Bi₃Se₄Br (CS-1), BiSeI (CS-3 / TS-3), and layered PbS/PbSe analogues (SS-1 / SS-3). It is especially attractive for single crystals of layered compounds. The uncertainty is that transport chemistry could over-halogenate the sample or produce PbBr₂/PbI₂ impurities **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Solution/gel/hydrothermal conditions are mild: TS-5 grows PbI₂ at room temperature and TS-4 uses 200–230 °C hydrothermal conditions. The score is reduced mainly for Pb toxicity and practical multi-step control of gel, sulfide release, and crystallization **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates a Pb–S–Br sulfide-halide made this way.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and avoids bulk diffusion, supported by PbI₂ gel growth in TS-5 and hydrothermal Pb oxyhalide growth in TS-4. The limiting factor is structural reconstruction and selectivity: Pb²⁺ + S²⁻ tends to precipitate PbS rapidly **[Reasonable Inference]**, while TS-2 already reports composition/impurity problems in aqueous bromoplumbate crystallization. Thus the route is kinetically fast but phase-selectivity uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Pb, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target description reports a simple 2D P-3m1 sheet and only edge/corner-sharing octahedra, with no face sharing and no large cell; therefore all F4 triggers are absent. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no amorphization tendency for Pb₂SBr₂ specifically, but structurally similar crystalline layered PbS/PbSe/PbI₂ analogues are reported in SS-1, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-5.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pb₂SBr₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS/SS/TS analogues showing that Pb/Bi chalcogenides and chalcogen-halides can be made in sealed ampoules, often by moderate-temperature solid-state reaction or CVT (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, TS-1, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the chemical compatibility of Pb²⁺ with both sulfide and bromide in octahedral/low-dimensional coordination, supported by PbS octahedral analogues in SS-5 and Pb–Br coordination in TS-2. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining bromide chemical potential without forming separate PbBr₂, PbS, or bromoplumbate impurities, a concern inferred from TS-2 composition problems and SS-1/SS-3 phase-control requirements. Overall, synthesis is feasible, with sealed-tube annealing followed by CVT optimization being the most defensible experimental strategy.
