### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high-score boundary for synthesizability. TS1–TS5 include experimentally synthesized analogues with E_hull values around 0.054–0.069 eV/atom, so thermodynamic metastability at this level is not prohibitive. CS1 and CS5 also show chemically similar selenides with low positive E_hull values that are synthesized. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.050 eV/atom may still permit competing ZrSe2/Cu2Se/other ternary phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** Route 1 uses the most common synthesis pattern in CS1, CS5, SS4, and TS1: elements sealed in evacuated quartz/silica and heated at 700–1050 °C. Its severity score is lowered by vacuum sealing, volatile Se, multiple firings, and hazardous/air-sensitive components. Kinetically, direct elemental Zr reaction is slow because Zr is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and SS1/SS5/TS1 show that related chalcogenides often need long anneals or reanneals. The route is feasible but likely slower and less phase-selective than binary or CVT routes.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Route 2 is supported most directly by SS3, where binary selenides/sulfides were used for CuCrSe2-type ternary chalcogenides, and by TS5 where binary Ga2Se3-type precursors are part of multinary chalcogenide synthesis. Its condition severity is similar to Route 1 because it still needs vacuum sealing and multistep annealing. Its kinetic score is higher because Zr–Se and Cu–Se bonding is pre-established, reducing diffusion/reaction mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that binary precursors can stabilize competing ZrSe2 or Cu2Se impurities if the ternary phase has a narrow stability window.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** Route 3 is strongly motivated by CS3, where iodine CVT crystallized a Pnma ternary selenide, and by SS2/TS2, where iodine improved crystal growth or reaction kinetics. Its severity remains moderate because iodine and Se require sealed evacuated ampoules and long thermal gradients. Kinetically it scores best because vapor transport can overcome slow solid-state diffusion and improve crystallinity. The key uncertainty is whether Zr, Cu, and Se transport at compatible rates without fractionation [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target is a crystalline Pnma perovskite-like selenide with corner-sharing ZrSe6 octahedra, and the description does not indicate simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing networks. Its likely Pnma cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference], and the Zr/Cu cation size mismatch is not severe enough to trigger the radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS analogues show some mixed-connectivity structures, but that is not reported for the target; TS gives insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrCuSe3 is likely a marginally stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common practical stability threshold. The CS/SS/TS analogues collectively support synthesis by sealed-tube chalcogenide chemistry: CS1/CS5/TS1 support direct elemental solid-state reaction, SS3 supports binary selenide precursor use, and CS3/SS2/TS2 support iodine-assisted transport. The most critical enabling factor is the broad precedent for ternary/multinary selenides forming in evacuated quartz ampoules at 600–1050 °C. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Se volatility and possible competition from stable binary Zr–Se and Cu–Se phases [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical strategy is to prepare a homogeneous bulk precursor using Route 2 and then improve crystallinity or isolate crystals using Route 3.
