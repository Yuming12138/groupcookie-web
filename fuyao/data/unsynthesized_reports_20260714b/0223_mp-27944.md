### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has **E above hull = 0.0500 eV/atom**, placing it exactly at the high-score threshold in the requested rubric [Target]. Chemical analogues include stable CuTlSe₂ and stable Tl–Hg–Se phases, indicating that Tl–Se ternaries can be true thermodynamic phases [CS-3, CS-4]. Thermodynamic analogues occupy similar metastability ranges, including 0.037–0.086 eV/atom, so a 0.050 eV/atom target is not unusually high-risk [TS-1 to TS-4]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be metastable with respect to Ag₂Se/Tl₂Se/other Ag–Tl–Se binaries, and CS/SS/TS do not directly report this exact AgTlSe₂ phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses evacuated sealed ampoules, high but conventional chalcogenide temperatures, and long annealing, consistent with CuTlSe₂ and related multication selenides [CS-3, SS-3, TS-1]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is required in the proposed adaptation, but vacuum sealing, volatile Se/Tl control, and toxic precursors impose major deductions [CS-3, SS-3, TS-1; Reasonable Inference]. The route is operationally standard but chemically hazardous. SS/TS support the need for long anneals, while CS gives the closest chemistry [CS-3, SS-3, TS-1].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary Ag₂Se/Tl₂Se precursors should react more readily than refractory oxides, but solid-state cation ordering can still be slow [Reasonable Inference; SS-3 and TS-1 long anneals]. The low melting points of Tl and Se and the sub-1000 °C Ag melting point favor diffusion relative to refractory systems [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is the main kinetic penalty because no cited analogue has the exact target P6₃/mcm AgTlSe₂ framework [CS/SS/TS collectively]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact reconstruction kinetics of AgTlSe₂, so this subfactor remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by sealed-ampoule melt growth of structurally and thermodynamically similar Tl chalcogenides at 600–700 °C [SS-1, TS-2]. It avoids extreme pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but it still requires vacuum/dry Ar sealing, controlled cooling, and handling of toxic volatile Tl/Se species [SS-1, TS-2; CS-4]. The severity is therefore moderate rather than easy. CS provides indirect support through sealed evacuated chalcogenide reactions, but not specifically stoichiometric AgTlSe₂ melt growth [CS-3, CS-4].

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth gives the best kinetic score because molten Tl/Se-rich mixtures should homogenize faster than dry solid-state pellets [SS-1, TS-2; Reasonable Inference]. Similar Tl chalcogenides crystallize from molten stoichiometric mixtures with controlled heating/cooling rates [SS-1, TS-2]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the target’s hexagonal P6₃/mcm framework differs from I4/mcm-type Tl chalcogenide analogues [Target, SS-1, TS-2]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on whether AgTlSe₂ melts congruently, so slow cooling plus annealing is recommended.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth is based on Tl₂Q flux synthesis of chemically related Tl–Hg–Se phases in evacuated carbon-coated fused-silica tubes [CS-4]. It operates at lower temperature than the melt route, but it requires additional steps: flux preparation, loading/sealing, long slow cooling, and flux separation [CS-4, TS-5]. Toxic Tl₂Se/Se handling and sealed-tube volatility hazards dominate the condition severity [CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. SS gives little direct signal for Tl₂Se self-flux, so SS is neutral for this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth should provide high precursor activity and liquid-mediated diffusion at relatively low temperature [CS-4]. The successful growth of incongruently melting Tl₂Hg₃Q₄ crystals from Tl₂Q flux supports this strategy for phases that may not crystallize cleanly from stoichiometric melts [CS-4]. The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the desired AgTlSe₂ phase instead of binary Ag₂Se, Tl₂Se, or other ternaries [Reasonable Inference]. TS-5 supports flux methods in selenides generally, but it is chemically less close than CS-4.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains two very different cation environments: Ag in AgTlSe₄ square pyramids and Tl in both TlSe₆ polyhedra and trigonal-planar Tl–Ag coordination [Target]. Mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra are explicitly present, which increases structural frustration and amorphization risk during rapid quenching [Target]. Unit-cell size is not provided, and CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for this exact target’s cell-size-driven amorphization tendency, so that check is not triggered. Slow cooling or post-annealing is therefore preferred [SS-1, TS-1, CS-4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgTlSe₂ is likely a **borderline metastable or near-ground-state phase**, because its E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from chemically related CuTlSe₂ made by sealed solid-state synthesis and Tl-rich selenides grown by melt/flux routes [CS-3, CS-4], while structurally and thermodynamically similar Tl chalcogenides support sealed melt crystallization at 600–700 °C [SS-1, TS-2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of liquid-assisted Tl/Se-rich reaction media, which should improve diffusion and crystallization kinetics [SS-1, TS-2, CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity under volatile/toxic Tl–Se conditions, because small stoichiometry deviations could favor Ag₂Se, Tl₂Se, or other Ag–Tl–Se phases [Reasonable Inference from sealed/flux phase-control issues in CS-4 and long anneals in SS-3/TS-1]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires careful sealed-tube stoichiometry and cooling control.
