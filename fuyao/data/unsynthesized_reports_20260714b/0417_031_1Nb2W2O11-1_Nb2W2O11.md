### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0270 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Chemically similar materials with comparable E_hull values were experimentally synthesized, including CS1 at 0.0297 eV/atom and CS2 at 0.0271 eV/atom. Structurally similar phases include stable or near-stable analogues, especially BaNb₂V₂O₁₁ at 0 eV/atom [SS1], TaVO₅ at 0 eV/atom [SS2], and ZrMo₂O₈ at ~0.0002 eV/atom [SS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is slightly metastable rather than exactly on the hull, so competing Nb–W oxides may form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 solid-state

The solid-state route uses moderate temperatures comparable to SS1 at 700–850 °C, SS2 at 800 °C, and SS3 at 975 K, so no severe temperature penalty is applied. Multi-step processing is required because SS2 uses ball milling/pelletizing/calcination and SS3 uses repeated regrinding/repressing. CS supports ceramic oxide processing [CS1, CS3], while TS gives insufficient direct signal for a binary Nb–W oxide ceramic route and is treated as neutral 50. The main uncertainty is WO₃ volatility and competing phase formation at high temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 solid-state

Kinetics are limited by refractory Nb₂O₅ and W-containing oxides, and SS3 shows that even a closely related P-3m1 layered oxide required 71 h and multiple regrindings. The structural reconstruction penalty is moderate rather than severe because the target shares corner-linked octahedral/tetrahedral motifs with SS1–SS3. CS3 supports that similar octahedral–tetrahedral oxides can form by solid-state reaction, but CS does not specifically resolve Nb–W diffusion barriers. Remaining uncertainty is whether Nb/W ordering in the target sheet equilibrates before denser niobium tungstate phases form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 flux

Flux growth is more conditionally severe than solid-state because SS5 uses 1156–1200 °C and slow cooling, triggering the >1000 °C deduction. The route is multi-step due to melting, slow cooling, and leaching, as shown in SS5 and TS2–TS5. CS provides insufficient direct flux evidence and is neutral 50 for this factor. The uncertainty is whether an oxide flux can dissolve enough Nb₂O₅ without incorporating Na/K/Li into the product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 flux

Flux strongly improves reaction kinetics through liquid-mediated mass transport, consistent with SS5 single-crystal tungstate growth and TS2–TS5 flux-assisted oxidotungstate formation. Ion diffusion is still not ideal because Nb and W are refractory cations [Reasonable Inference], but the liquid flux partially compensates. Structural reconstruction is favorable for WO₄-containing frameworks [SS5, TS2–TS5], though alkali-bearing structural analogues indicate possible competing alkali-stabilized phases [SS1, SS5]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity between alkali-containing and alkali-free layered products.

#### F2 — Route 3 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis has mild temperature relative to ceramic routes because CS2 used 573 K, but it requires high autogenous pressure around 500 bar. The provided F2 rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, so the formal score remains high. SS and TS provide insufficient direct hydrothermal evidence and are neutral 50. The key uncertainty is practical equipment limitation and whether anhydrous Nb₂W₂O₁₁ can crystallize rather than hydrated niobium/tungsten oxides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal

Solution-mediated reaction should improve precursor activity and bypass slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS2 demonstrates hydrothermal formation of Mo/W oxide crystals at 573 K. However, CS2 explicitly reports a multiphasic sample, so the structural reconstruction/phase-selectivity score is reduced. SS and TS analogues do not validate hydrothermal formation of the target sheet, so they are treated as insufficient evidence from SS/TS and neutral 50. The remaining uncertainty is high: pH, mineralizer, and Nb/W speciation may control whether the target phase nucleates.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed NbO₆ octahedra and WO₄ tetrahedra with substantially different cation radii/coordination environments, triggering one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula has only 15 atoms per formula unit, and the described connectivity is corner-sharing only, so the large-cell and mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity checks are not triggered. Structurally similar crystalline analogues with octahedral–tetrahedral networks exist [SS1–SS3], supporting a low-to-medium amorphization risk. The main uncertainty is whether the layered P-3m1 sheet remains crystalline during low-temperature hydrothermal or rapid flux crystallization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₂W₂O₁₁ is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase rather than a deep ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0270 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from structurally similar octahedral–tetrahedral oxides, especially BaNb₂V₂O₁₁ [SS1], TaVO₅ [SS2, CS3], and trigonal P-3m1 ZrMo₂O₈ [SS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known corner-sharing MO₆–MO₄ frameworks, which suggests the target topology is chemically plausible [SS1–SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: hydrothermal analogues can be multiphasic [CS2], and solid-state analogues sometimes require long anneals and repeated regrinding [SS3]. Overall, the target is feasible, with hydrothermal screening and oxide flux growth being the most promising discovery routes, followed by conventional ceramic synthesis for phase-purity optimization.
