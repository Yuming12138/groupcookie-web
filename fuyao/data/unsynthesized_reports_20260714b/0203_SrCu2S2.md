### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

#### Route 1: Evacuated-ampoule solid-state reaction from SrS/Cu/S

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Structurally closest analogue BaCu₂S₂ is Pnma and is reported by solid-state ampoule methods in the Ba–Cu–S family [SS1]. Related BaCu₄S₃ was made from BaS + Cu + S at 800 °C for six days in evacuated Vycor ampoules [SS3]. Thermodynamic analogues with Cu–chalcogen frameworks are often made by sealed-ampoule solid-state reactions at 500–1000 °C [TS4, TS5]. Chemical-similarity data also supports sealed-tube chalcogenide solid-state synthesis at 850 °C under vacuum [CS1, CS3]. |
| Precursors | SrS, Cu powder, S powder; ≥99.9% purity recommended. SrS substitutes for BaS by alkaline-earth homology [Reasonable Inference from SS1/SS3/TS2]. |
| Temperature program | Grind stoichiometric SrS:Cu:S = 1:2:1; pelletize; seal; ramp 50–100 °C/h to 750–850 °C; hold 4–7 days; slow cool to 650–700 °C, then either furnace cool or quench depending on phase purity. Long holds and slow/controlled cooling are supported by Ba–Cu–S ampoule syntheses [SS1, SS3] and sealed chalcogenide analogues [TS4, TS5]. |
| Atmosphere | Evacuated sealed quartz/Vycor ampoule. Vacuum sealing is directly supported for Ba–Cu–S and related chalcogenides [SS1, SS3, TS4, TS5]. |
| Pressure | Ambient external pressure; sealed under vacuum, typically <10⁻³–10⁻⁴ Torr/mbar by analogy [CS1, CS3, TS1, TS5]. |
| Additional conditions | Regrinding/repelletizing after first firing may improve homogeneity; slow cooling may help order the Pnma phase [CS1, CS3, SS1]. Sulfur excess of 1–5 mol% can compensate sulfur volatility [Reasonable Inference from sealed sulfur-containing ampoule syntheses in SS3/TS5]. |
| Transferability to target | Strong: SrCu₂S₂ is chemically homologous to BaCu₂S₂ and has the same Pnma space group as BaCu₂S₂ [SS1]. Sr²⁺ is smaller than Ba²⁺, so a slightly lower or comparable reaction temperature may be adequate, but phase competition may differ [Reasonable Inference from alkaline-earth size trends]. |
| Estimated difficulty | Moderate. Ampoule work and SrS air sensitivity add complexity, but no high pressure or exotic equipment is required [SS3, TS5]. |

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|

| Estimated difficulty | Moderate, possibly easiest for crystals. Main risks are flux removal, moisture sensitivity, and possible mixed polymorphs [TS2]. |

#### Route 3: Alkali/polysulfide flux at 650–750 °C

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | NaBa₂Cu₃S₅ was synthesized from Cu, Na₂S, BaS, and S in molten alkali/polysulfide flux at 700 °C under vacuum with slow cooling [SS2, TS1]. K₂Ni₃S₄ was grown in K₂S₃ flux at 750 °C for 6 days under vacuum [CS5]. |
| Precursors | SrS, Cu powder, S powder, optional Na₂S/K₂S plus excess S to generate Na₂Sₓ/K₂Sₓ flux; ≥99.9% recommended. |
| Temperature program | Load in graphite crucible or carbon-coated ampoule; ramp to 650–750 °C over 10–20 h; hold 24 h to 6 days; slow cool at 5–10 °C/h to room temperature; remove flux by dry/inert washing where possible. Program follows TS1/SS2 and CS5. |
| Atmosphere | Vacuum-sealed fused silica tube, with glovebox loading recommended for sulfides [SS2, TS1, CS5]. |
| Pressure | Ambient external pressure; sealed tube under vacuum, typically <10⁻³–10⁻⁴ mbar/Torr [SS2, TS1, CS5]. |
| Additional conditions | Alkali flux may introduce Na/K competing phases, so charge composition and flux removal must be controlled [SS2, CS5]. Graphite crucible helps isolate melt from silica and is used in Ba–Na–Cu–S flux synthesis [SS2, TS1]. |
| Transferability to target | Good for enhancing Cu/S mobility and crystal growth, but less direct than Route 2 because the closest product is NaBa₂Cu₃S₅ rather than AECu₂S₂ [SS2, TS1]. |
| Estimated difficulty | Moderate to challenging due to air-sensitive sulfides and possible alkali incorporation [CS5, SS2]. |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0270 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.03–0.045 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable, including NaBa₂Cu₃S₅ at 0.0307 eV/atom [TS1], BaCu₂Se₂ at 0.0369 eV/atom [TS2], Ba₂V(CuS₂)₃ at 0.0385 eV/atom despite phase-purity issues [TS3], and Cu₈GeS₆ at 0.0451 eV/atom [TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee a unique polymorph or easy phase purity, as TS2 reports α/β competition for BaCu₂S₂/BaCu₂Se₂.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard sealed-ampoule solid-state conditions, supported by BaCu₄S₃ synthesis at 800 °C for six days in evacuated Vycor ampoules [SS3] and related vacuum ampoule chalcogenide syntheses [CS1, CS3, TS5]. The severity score is reduced mainly by vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multistep grinding/pelletizing, and SrS air sensitivity [SS3, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or >1000 °C is required, so those deductions are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state kinetics are moderate because Cu metal and SrS are not highly reactive molecular precursors, and BaCu₄S₃ required six days at 800 °C [SS3]. Structural reconstruction is helped substantially by the same Pnma BaCu₂S₂ analogue [SS1]. The main uncertainty is whether SrCu₂S₂ forms directly or competes with SrS, Cu₂S, SrCuS₂, or Cu-rich sulfides; phase-purity problems in Ba–Cu–S analogues indicate this is plausible [SS1, SS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest temperature, since TS2 reports BaCu₂S₂/BaCu₂Se₂ formation in potassium thio-/selenocyanate flux at 480 °C for 8 days under Ar. Deductions remain for sealed volatile sulfur/flux chemistry, multistep flux growth/washing, and sulfide precursor air sensitivity [TS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS specifically supports thiocyanate flux for SrCu₂S₂, so CS/SS are neutral for Sr-specific flux transferability, but TS2 is highly relevant.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth strongly improves kinetics by dissolving/transporting Cu–S species at temperatures far below the Cu melting point, and the direct BaCu₂S₂ flux precedent is strong [TS2]. The structural-reconstruction score is high because BaCu₂S₂ is structurally closest and Pnma [SS1], while the flux route directly produces BaCu₂S₂ polymorphs [TS2]. The uncertainty is polymorph control and crystal survival during washing, since TS2 notes α/β coexistence and washing sensitivity for related BaCu₂Se₂.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses 650–750 °C molten alkali polysulfide flux, supported by NaBa₂Cu₃S₅ at 700 °C under vacuum [SS2/TS1] and K₂Ni₃S₄ at 750 °C in K₂S₃ flux [CS5]. It is more severe than Route 2 because it uses vacuum sealing and air-sensitive alkali sulfides, while still requiring multistep flux removal [SS2/TS1, CS5]. There is insufficient direct evidence from TS/SS/CS that alkali flux yields SrCu₂S₂ specifically, so the route is scored below the direct KSCN-type flux.

#### F3 — Route 3

Polysulfide flux improves reaction kinetics relative to dry solid state, as demonstrated by Cu-containing sulfide crystal growth at 700–750 °C [SS2/TS1, CS5]. However, structural transferability is less direct because the closest polysulfide-flux product is NaBa₂Cu₃S₅ rather than AECu₂S₂ [SS2/TS1]. The principal kinetic uncertainty is alkali incorporation or formation of Na/K-containing Cu sulfides, which is suggested by the alkali-rich products in SS2 and CS5.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains a large Sr/Cu cation size contrast, and analogous Ba–Cu–S structures also combine large alkaline-earth coordination with CuS₄ units [SS1, SS3]. The target Pnma description contains corner- and edge-sharing CuS₄ tetrahedra, but not the full corner/edge/face-sharing coexistence criterion, so only one amorphization trigger is applied. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives a precise target unit-cell atom count, but the formula and common Pnma Z values suggest it is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCu₂S₂ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues around this hull energy have reported syntheses [TS1–TS4]. The strongest support comes from BaCu₂S₂, which is both structurally very close and Pnma [SS1], and from the direct report that α-/β-BaCu₂S₂ can be obtained in thiocyanate flux at 480 °C [TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is flux-mediated Cu–S transport, which reduces diffusion limitations and may allow the layered/orthorhombic phase to crystallize at moderate temperature [TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among competing alkaline-earth copper sulfides and polymorphs, since Ba–Cu–S analogues show mixed phases or difficult purification in several cases [SS1, SS3, TS2]. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, with the best strategy being a low-temperature sealed flux route followed by careful phase screening.
