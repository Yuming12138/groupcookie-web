### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category. This is reinforced by thermodynamically similar ground-state alkali manganese tellurides such as Cs₂Mn₃Te₄, Cs₂MnTe₂, and K₆MnTe₄ with E_hull = 0.0 (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The uncertainty is that the target stoichiometry/structure may compete with nearby K–Mn–Te phases, because chemically similar KMnTe₂/RbMnTe₂ are reported at modest positive hull energies but are experimentally synthesized (CS-1, CS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state / cation-exchange

Condition severity is high because related AMnTe₂ syntheses require sealed Nb tubes, evacuated silica jackets, inert handling, long 750 °C holds, and slow cooling (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Kinetics are moderate: cation exchange can preserve Mn–Te connectivity, but 200 h anneals and reported phase mixtures imply slow diffusion and narrow phase fields (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports this exact K(MnTe)₂ cation-exchange route, so TS is neutral for this route-specific kinetic claim.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Reactive alkali telluride flux

Flux synthesis benefits kinetics because Cs₂Te₃ flux produced Cs₂Mn₃Te₄ crystals after high-temperature dissolution and slow cooling (CS-4; SS-1; TS-1). The method is still conditionally severe because it uses vacuum-sealed tubes, air-sensitive alkali telluride flux, and manual extraction from a reactive melt (CS-4; SS-1; TS-1). The main uncertainty is K/Cs transferability and whether the K–Te flux will stabilize the target rather than K-rich telluromanganates [Reasonable Inference from TS-3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Carbonate / H₂–Te route

This route scores best on F2 because it uses ambient-pressure gas-flow synthesis, short 7–12 h times, and common carbonate/MnTe/MnCO₃ precursors in corundum vessels (SS-2; TS-2; TS-3; TS-5). It still loses severity points for the H₂/Te controlled atmosphere and air-sensitive products (TS-3, TS-5). Kinetically, the route is favorable because many alkali manganese tellurides form rapidly under chalcogen-charged H₂, but structural selectivity is uncertain because reported phases include K₂MnTe₂ and K₆MnTe₄ rather than K(MnTe)₂ (SS-2; TS-2; TS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Direct elemental sealed synthesis

Direct reaction is strongly supported structurally by KMnAs/RbMnAs 122-type analogues made from elemental mixtures at 700–1100 °C under Ar (SS-3, SS-4). However, telluride chemistry requires sealed Nb/Ta containment and more stringent volatility/air-sensitivity control, as shown by AMnTe₂ analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Kinetics are less favorable than flux or carbonate routes because dense Mn and elemental diffusion dominate [Reasonable Inference], though structural reconstruction into a 122-like framework is plausible from SS-3/SS-4.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch, with K in 8-coordinate Te cages and Mn in tetrahedral MnTe₄ units, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination-disparity risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS-1]. The target also explicitly contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing MnTe₄ tetrahedra, giving a second amorphization/disorder trigger. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a large unit cell for the target, so the unit-cell-size check is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(MnTe)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several similar alkali manganese tellurides are also computed ground states (target data; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports the existence and synthesizability of related K/Rb/Cs–Mn–Te frameworks with K/Rb/Cs–Te high coordination and MnTe₄ tetrahedra (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4; SS-1, SS-5; TS-1–TS-3). The most important enabling factor is that K–Mn–Te and related alkali manganese telluride chemistries are experimentally accessible by several bulk methods, including sealed-tube, flux, and H₂/Te gas-flow routes (CS-1, CS-4; TS-2, TS-3). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity, because nearby K–Mn–Te compositions such as KMnTe₂, K₂MnTe₂, and K₆MnTe₄ are known or inferred competitors (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2; TS-3). A practical synthesis campaign should therefore emphasize Te activity, K:Mn ratio, and post-reaction PXRD screening.
