### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and is scored at the top of the required rubric. Similar stable or near-stable analogues include MnBi2Se4 with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1/SS-3/TS-2), SbCrSe3 with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-2/TS-1), and Pb7Bi4Se13 close to hull at 0.0115 eV/atom (CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic, not thermodynamic: CS-1 and SS-2/TS-1 both warn that phase purity and stoichiometry can still be difficult even for stable phases.

#### F2 — Route 1, elemental solid-state

The solid-state route uses moderate temperatures, similar to 700–800 °C CuSbSe2 (SS-1), 500–750 °C SbCrSe3 (SS-2/TS-1), and 773 K Pb7Bi4Se13 (CS-5). The penalties come from evacuated sealed tubes, volatile Se, and multi-step grinding/annealing, all reported in SS-1, SS-2, and CS-5. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that extreme pressure or >1000 °C is required, so those penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1, elemental solid-state

Kinetics are moderate because elemental Fe is refractory and likely slow to react **[Reasonable Inference]**, although elemental routes do work for Cr/Sb/Se and Pb/Bi/Se analogues (SS-2/TS-1; CS-5). Structural reconstruction is helped by the close Pnma edge-sharing CrSe6 analogue SbCrSe3 (SS-2/TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether FeBiSe3 has a narrow homogeneity range like SbCrSe3, where binary-precursor attempts yielded two-phase mixtures (SS-2/TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2, flux growth

Flux growth avoids extreme temperature and pressure; MnBi2Se4 used 800→400 °C LiCl–RbCl flux in evacuated tubes (CS-1/SS-3/TS-2). Penalties remain for vacuum sealing, volatile Se, multiple operations, and toxic Se/Bi handling. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the LiCl–RbCl flux is air-sensitive in this exact use, but dry handling is a reasonable precaution.

#### F3 — Route 2, flux growth

Flux improves kinetics by dissolving/reacting species and enabling slow crystallization, as seen when MnBi2Se4 crystals were obtained via lower-temperature flux after difficulties with stoichiometric preparation (CS-1/SS-3/TS-2). This route is chemically transferable because it directly involves a Bi–transition-metal selenide analogue. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: MnBi2Se4 had defect/stoichiometry challenges, suggesting FeBiSe3 may also require careful flux ratio and cooling optimization (CS-1/SS-3/TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3, CVT / halide-assisted

CVT conditions are moderate in temperature, with Bi3Se4Br using 623 K prereaction and 823/783 K gradient growth in evacuated ampoules (CS-3/SS-4). Severity arises from sealed vacuum ampoules, volatile halide/Se species, and multi-step growth. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that halide-assisted transport will preserve halide-free FeBiSe3, so the method is scored as challenging despite favorable kinetics.

#### F3 — Route 3, CVT / halide-assisted

CVT can give high precursor mobility and good crystallization, supported by Bi3Se4Br single-crystal growth in a gradient (CS-3/SS-4). The 1D ribbon motif in Bi3Se4Br supports structural transferability to the 1D target, but Fe incorporation and avoiding halide substitution are uncertain **[Reasonable Inference]**. This route is therefore kinetically attractive but chemically less certain than flux.

#### F2 — Route 4, high-pressure

TS-5 shows high-pressure selenides can be synthesized at 65–89 kbar and 400–1200 °C, requiring a tetrahedral anvil press and quenching. The high-pressure penalty is large, and the method is experimentally severe. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS that target FeBiSe3 requires pressure; in fact, closer analogues are made at ambient pressure in sealed tubes or flux (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2).

#### F3 — Route 4, high-pressure

High pressure may shorten reaction times, as TS-5 reports 0.5–6 h runs, but the structural match is poor because TS-5 concerns pyrite CdSe2 rather than Pnma Fe/Bi ribbon selenides. Pre-reacted binaries may help kinetics **[Reasonable Inference]**, but sample scale and phase control are limiting. This is best treated as a fallback route if ambient routes fail.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The cation size/coordination mismatch between Fe and Bi likely triggers one amorphization-risk point **[Reasonable Inference]**, although analogous mixed-cation selenides crystallize successfully in CS-1, CS-5, and SS-2. The target’s Pnma cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms **[Reasonable Inference]**, and the described target connectivity emphasizes edge-sharing FeSe6 rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity. Remaining uncertainty is that CS-1 and SS-2 show defect and homogeneity issues, which may manifest as disorder rather than full amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeBiSe3 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, consistent with stable analogues such as MnBi2Se4 and SbCrSe3 (CS-1/SS-3/TS-2; SS-2/TS-1). The strongest support comes from the structural/thermodynamic analogue SbCrSe3, which shares Pnma symmetry and edge-sharing MSe6 octahedra (SS-2/TS-1), and from Bi-containing selenide syntheses by flux or sealed-tube methods (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is using sealed-tube chalcogenide chemistry to maintain Se chemical potential, as done across SS-1, SS-2, CS-1, and CS-5. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: close analogues show narrow homogeneity ranges, defect structures, or impurity formation (CS-1, SS-2/TS-1, TS-3). Overall, FeBiSe3 should be synthesizable, but likely requires slow annealing or flux/CVT crystal growth rather than a single short solid-state firing.
