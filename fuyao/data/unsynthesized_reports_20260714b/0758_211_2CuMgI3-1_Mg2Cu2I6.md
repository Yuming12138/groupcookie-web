### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull = 0.021 eV/atom places it in the high synthesizability range of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamically similar iodides cluster close to this value: TlCdI₃ at 0.0167 eV/atom, TlMnI₃ at 0.0170 eV/atom, AgTl₂I₃ at 0.0272 eV/atom, and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (TS1–TS4). Chemically similar iodides include several hull-stable compounds, such as LiInI₄, NaAlI₄, and CuGaI₄ (CS1, CS4, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that SS sources mostly describe binary CdI₂ polytypes, so SS gives structural but not direct Mg–Cu–I thermodynamic evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature and no high pressure, consistent with CS iodide melt growth at 300 °C to 573 K and TS/CS annealing at 250–350 °C for related iodides (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, TS2). The main severity penalties are sealed-system operation and air/moisture-sensitive iodides, both supported by strict moisture exclusion and dry glovebox use in CS2, CS4, CS5, and TS2. There is no evidence for >1000 °C or high-pressure requirements in CS/SS/TS. The uncertainty is that MgI₂/CuI may require a higher homogenization temperature than the In/Ga/Al iodide melts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary iodides are reactive precursors, and many analogues form from binary halides by melt or slow cooling (CS1, CS4, CS5). The layered octahedral iodide motif is strongly supported by CdI₂ structural analogues with CdI₆ sheets (SS1–SS5), while Cu–I coordination is supported by CuGaI₄ and CuHgSI (CS5, TS4). However, Mg/Cu ordering and the target Cu–Cu contact are not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction is scored only moderate. Repeated annealing required for TlFeI₃ indicates possible kinetic barriers to phase purity (CS2, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 2

The low-temperature annealing route benefits from moderate temperatures similar to TlFeI₃/TlMnI₃ and CuHgSI (CS2, TS2, TS4). It is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule use, volatile iodides, likely multi-step grinding/annealing, and air-sensitive precursors, all of which are reflected in CS2, TS2, and TS4. No source indicates a need for high pressure or ultra-high temperature. Uncertainty remains because low-temperature solid-state diffusion may be slower for Mg/Cu ordering than for the heavier Tl/Mn/Fe/Cd analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The route has lower kinetic favorability than Route 1 because it may not form a full liquid phase. Long annealing times are explicitly reported for TlMnI₃, TlFeI₃, and CuHgSI (CS2, TS2, TS4), supporting a moderate kinetic penalty. The target structure is layered and low-dimensional like several SS analogues, but cation ordering into MgI₆ and CuCuI₃ units is nontrivial. The major uncertainty is whether MgI₂ and CuI interdiffuse sufficiently at 250–350 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydroiodic-acid solvothermal synthesis occurs at mild temperature, 155–175 °C, in TS3, so thermal severity is low. The deductions arise from sealed autoclave operation with volatile/corrosive HI and toxic/air-sensitive iodide handling. No high-pressure >5 GPa condition is involved, only autogenous pressure as in TS3. The uncertainty is whether aqueous HI is compatible with obtaining an anhydrous MgCuI₃ phase rather than solvated or hydrated products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution conditions provide excellent transport and low diffusion barriers, supported by AgTl₂I₃ formation in aqueous HI at 155–175 °C (TS3). CuI may dissolve in iodide-rich acid as iodocuprate complexes, and Mg²⁺ should remain soluble in acid [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic/phase risk is that solvothermal iodides can twin or form low-yield crystals, as TS3 reports twinned crystals and low yield of untwinned crystals. Because no CS or SS source confirms Mg–Cu–I hydrothermal crystallization, structural selectivity is uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4

Vapor-phase transport is more severe than melt growth because it requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and a controlled thermal gradient, as implied by SS5 vapor-phase CdI₂ growth. Volatile iodides and possible iodine transport-agent use add pressure and corrosion risks [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 and TS5 show sealed-tube halide reactions with volatile halogen components, but those are bromide examples rather than direct MgCuI₃ evidence. Insufficient direct evidence from CS and TS for MgCuI₃ vapor growth leads to a conservative score.

#### F3 — Route 4

SS5 supports layered CdI₂ crystal growth from vapor, which is structurally relevant to the 2D target sheet. However, SS evidence is binary CdI₂, and no source demonstrates ternary cation ordering in vapor transport. Different volatilities of MgI₂, CuI, and possible I₂ make composition control difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the route is plausible but kinetically/compositionally challenging.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization-risk trigger: different cation coordination environments, MgI₆ octahedra versus CuCuI₃ trigonal pyramids, imply a significant cation size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference based on target structure]. The target description reports corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion requiring vertex + edge + face coexistence is not triggered. The formula/unit description does not imply a >40-atom unit cell, so the cell-size trigger is not applied. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for MgCuI₃; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, treated as neutral source-specific signal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgCuI₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.021 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar iodides exist near the same hull energy (TS1–TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar iodides that are grown from binary iodide melts under dry, sealed, slow-cooling conditions (CS1, CS4, CS5) and from Tl-based ternary iodides formed by annealing/Bridgman methods (CS2, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the softness and mobility of iodide chemistry, which allows crystallization at moderate temperature rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion, as shown across CS and TS examples. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: Mg/Cu ordering in a 2D sheet with both MgI₆ octahedra and CuCuI₃ pyramids is not directly demonstrated by any provided analogue. Moisture sensitivity and iodide volatility further require sealed, dry handling, as indicated by CS2, CS4, CS5, and TS2.
