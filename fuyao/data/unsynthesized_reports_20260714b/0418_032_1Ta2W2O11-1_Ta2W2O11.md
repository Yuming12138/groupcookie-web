### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket by the provided rubric. Several reference materials with similar octahedral–tetrahedral oxide chemistry are also on-hull or near-on-hull, including TaVO5, BaNb2V2O11, ZrMo2O8, TaOPO4, and Pb3Nb4O13 (CS5, SS1–SS3, SS5, TS1–TS4). This strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee rapid formation or absence of kinetic competing Ta–W oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate ceramic temperatures, because close analogues form at 700–850 °C or near 975 K (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). No high pressure or special atmosphere is required in the closest structural analogues (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The main deduction is for multistep milling/firing/regrinding, which is supported by TaVO5 and especially ZrMo2O8 procedures (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for toxic or air-sensitive precursors, so that factor is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The reaction uses refractory oxide Ta2O5 and oxide WO3, so precursor activity is limited compared with nitrate/sol-gel chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. Very high Ta/W cation melting points imply slow cation diffusion by the rubric **[Reasonable Inference]**, although analogues demonstrate that oxide networks can form far below those melting points (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because TaVO5 shares TaO6–tetrahedral corner-sharing and ZrMo2O8 shares the P-3m1 layered motif (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The uncertainty is whether Ta2W2O11 requires a narrower Ta:W:O chemical potential window than the V/Mo analogues **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits moderate temperatures from CS1 and SS3/TS3 but adds sealed-tube/vacuum handling. CS1 explicitly used evacuated silica tubes for U–W oxide synthesis, which justifies the controlled-atmosphere deduction. WO3 volatility management is a **[Reasonable Inference]** from W oxide chemistry and the sealed-tube precedent in CS1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any air-sensitive precursor penalty, so that factor remains neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Sealed conditions may improve W oxide chemical potential and transport relative to open air **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the underlying precursors are still refractory/simple oxides. CS1 also warns that U–W oxide sealed-tube products included multiphase specimens, residual UO2/WO3, and cubic UxWO3, indicating phase-control challenges. Structural support still comes more strongly from SS1/SS3/TS1/TS3 than from CS1. The main uncertainty is whether vacuum would partially reduce W6+ or shift oxygen stoichiometry unfavorably **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe than Route 1 because it may require a WO3-rich melt/flux, slow cooling, and post-growth flux removal **[Reasonable Inference]**. Slow-cooled oxide crystal growth is supported by TS5, and Pt crucible high-temperature oxide processing is supported by SS5/TS4/TS5. The volatile/excess WO3 penalty is applied because the route intentionally uses WO3 as a flux **[Reasonable Inference]**. No high pressure is required.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux assistance should substantially improve precursor activity and mass transport compared with purely solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**. This is attractive because ZrMo2O8 required long annealing and repeated regrinding, indicating kinetic limitations in related layered octahedral–tetrahedral oxides (SS3, TS3). Structural reconstruction remains favorable due to SS1 and SS3/TS1 and TS3. The uncertainty is stoichiometry: excess WO3 may stabilize W-rich impurity phases or leave residual flux **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is clearly more severe because CS2 used 50 kbar and 1570 K for Pr(WO3)5. The proposed route may require ~4–6 GPa and >1000 °C **[Reasonable Inference based on CS2]**, so the high-pressure and high-temperature deductions apply. However, it avoids sealed volatile handling as a separate issue because the sample is encapsulated under pressure. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports air-sensitive precursor penalties.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure and high temperature can overcome some kinetic barriers **[Reasonable Inference]**, but short dwell times and refractory Ta/W oxides keep diffusion challenging. CS2 shows that high pressure can form W-rich oxide structures, but that specific analogue is less structurally matched than SS1 and SS3. The graphite reducing environment in CS2 is potentially unsuitable for W6+ Ta2W2O11 **[Reasonable Inference]**, so an oxidizing/neutral capsule would be needed. Thus, this route is a backup rather than the preferred synthesis path.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant cation radius/coordination mismatch between octahedral Ta5+ and tetrahedral W6+ **[Reasonable Inference using ionic radii]**, giving one amorphization-risk trigger. However, the target cell is small by formula atom count and contains ordered corner-sharing TaO6/WO4 connectivity without mixed edge/face-sharing coexistence in the target description. Structurally similar crystalline analogues with comparable octahedral–tetrahedral networks are experimentally reported (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3), reducing concern about glass formation. Remaining uncertainty is that W oxide-rich melts can vitrify or form poorly crystalline products if quenched too rapidly **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta2W2O11 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple similar oxides are also on or near the hull (target, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS4). The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar TaVO5 and ZrMo2O8: TaVO5 validates TaO6–tetrahedral corner-sharing chemistry, while ZrMo2O8 validates the trigonal P-3m1 layered sheet motif (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match to known ceramic-synthesized octahedral–tetrahedral oxides (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is sluggish diffusion and phase competition in refractory Ta/W oxide solid-state chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with repeated regrinding needs in ZrMo2O8 and multiphase U–W oxide products in CS1/SS3/TS3. Overall, ambient-pressure ceramic synthesis should be attempted first, with WO3-rich flux or sealed-tube control as secondary strategies.
