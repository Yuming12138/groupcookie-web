### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.026 eV/atom, which is within the commonly accessible metastability range and falls in the highest prescribed scoring bin. TS analogues are especially relevant because they include low-hull halides at 0.017–0.028 eV/atom, including TlMnI3 at 0.017 eV/atom and Bi(TeBr2)2 at 0.028 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-5). CS analogues also show many halide phases near the hull or on the hull (CS-1 to CS-5). The main uncertainty is that thermodynamic hull stability does not guarantee redox compatibility of Mn(IV) with iodide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to HfI4 synthesis at 250 °C and Ga/In iodide synthesis at 300–350 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (CS-2, SS-2). The penalties arise from vacuum/inert sealed ampoule handling, volatile iodine, and a multistep reaction-plus-sublimation workflow, all supported by CS-2 and SS-2. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct elemental Mn + I2 → MnI4 synthesis is treated as neutral regarding direct transferability.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic advantage is that I2 vapor can transport iodine and form volatile/low-dimensional iodides, as shown for HfI4 (CS-2). The kinetic disadvantage is the use of dense Mn metal, which may need surface activation before complete conversion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable because HfI4 is a 1D tetraiodide ribbon analogue, but the target has distinct 5-coordinate Mn rather than HfI6 octahedra (CS-2 and target structure).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is less severe than Route 1 because it can be treated as a direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction using MnI2 and I2, without obligatory sublimation purification [Reasonable Inference]. Similar sealed-tube halide reactions occur at 250–280 °C for W bromides, Hg3Se2I2, and CuHgSI (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, TS-2). The route still requires vacuum/inert handling and volatile iodine, so the condition score remains moderate.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
MnI2 is a chemically activated precursor because Mn–I bonds already exist, and MnI2 is explicitly used in TlMnI3 preparation (TS-1). Iodine vapor should reduce diffusion limitations relative to dense elemental Mn, consistent with low-temperature halide reactions in CS-1, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-2. The main kinetic uncertainty is the required oxidation/reconstruction from MnI2-like local coordination to the predicted MnI4 ribbon, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has favorable low temperatures because analogous iodide and bromide CVT/sublimation systems operate at ~100–350 °C (CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-5). However, it necessarily requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile iodine, multistep loading/transport/crystallization, and air-sensitive halide handling (CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS for MnI4-specific vapor transport is neutral, but the general halide CVT signal is strong.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT/sublimation provides the best kinetic pathway because crystallization occurs through vapor transport rather than long-range solid-state diffusion, as seen for HfI4 sublimation growth (CS-2). SS iodide analogues show that sealed ampoule temperature gradients can produce crystalline iodide materials over hours to days (SS-1, SS-2). The remaining uncertainty is whether MnI4 has sufficient volatility or stability in I2 vapor to transport without decomposing to MnI2 + I2 [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target is compositionally simple, with only one cation and a small MnI4 formula, so it does not meet the cation-size or large-unit-cell triggers [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described structure is a 1D ribbon with terminal and bridging iodides, not a complex network with simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization tendency for MnI4, so the source-level signal is neutral, but the target descriptors imply low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnI4 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a confidently established ground-state compound, because its E_hull is only 0.026 eV/atom but not zero. The CS evidence is strong for binary tetraiodide-like chemistry through HfI4, especially sealed-tube elemental iodination and sublimation growth (CS-2), while TS evidence supports Mn–I ribbon formation from MnI2-containing mixtures (TS-1). SS evidence supports sealed-ampoule iodide crystal growth, but mostly in ternary molecular iodides rather than binary MnI4, so it is supportive but indirect (SS-1, SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high iodine chemical potential in a sealed ampoule or vapor-transport geometry, as in CS-2 and SS-2. The most critical limiting factor is the redox instability expected for formal Mn(IV) iodide, which could drive decomposition to MnI2 and I2; this concern is a [Reasonable Inference] because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports MnI4.
