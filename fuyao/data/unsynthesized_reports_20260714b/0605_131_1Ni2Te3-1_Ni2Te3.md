### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Ni₂Te₃ has E_hull = 0.033 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range [Target]. Thermodynamically similar references include experimentally represented compounds near this range, such as CrRh₂Te₄ at ~0.034 eV/atom [TS1], CoTe₂ at ~0.019 eV/atom [TS3], and NbPdTe₅ at ~0.021 eV/atom [TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy supports accessibility but does not guarantee this exact P-6m2 polymorph rather than a competing Ni–Te phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

The sealed-tube route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, consistent with NiTe₂ at 800 °C [CS1, SS1] and CoTe₂ at 600 °C [SS3, TS3]. Deductions come from vacuum sealing, Te volatility requiring a sealed ampoule, multi-step processing, and Te handling risk [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not indicate a need for glovebox-level air sensitivity, so no additional penalty beyond toxic/volatile handling is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Ni + Te is directly supported by CoTe₂/Ni–Co–Te synthesis [SS3, TS3], and sealed-tube elemental routes are supported for layered NiTe₂ [CS1, SS1]. Kinetics are moderated by Ni metal diffusion but helped by molten/vapor Te and long annealing [Reasonable Inference; CS1/SS1/SS3/TS3 report long high-temperature holds]. Structural similarity to layered NiTe₂/CoTe₂ supports partial topotactic/structural transfer, but the stoichiometry change from NiTe₂ to Ni₂Te₃ creates uncertainty [Target, CS1, SS1, SS3, TS3].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

High-pressure analogues require 15–89 kbar or 60–89 kbar and temperatures up to 1200–1600 °C, with quenching [CS2, CS3, SS2, TS2, TS4]. The equipment is specialized, including tetrahedral anvil press, BN crucible, graphite heater, and pyrophyllite assembly [CS2, SS2, TS2]. Although high pressure may help trap metastable phases, the severity score is low because >5 GPa pressure and quench processing are major barriers [CS2, CS3, SS2, TS2, TS4].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-pressure reactions of related tellurides proceed in short 0.5–6 h windows [CS2, CS3, SS2, TS2, TS4], suggesting favorable reaction rates once compressed and heated. However, the closest high-pressure NiTe₂ analogue is pyrite-like rather than the target layered P-6m2 structure [CS2, SS2, TS2], so structural reconstruction/selection is uncertain. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure specifically for Ni₂Te₃ P-6m2, so the kinetic/structural score is kept moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT analogues use moderate temperatures of ~600–750 °C and standard evacuated silica tubes, avoiding high pressure [SS4, TS5]. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Te/I₂, toxic/corrosive iodine, and multiple processing steps [SS4, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The equipment burden is much lower than high-pressure synthesis but the chemical handling burden is higher than a simple ampoule anneal [SS4, TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Iodine transport can improve mass transport and crystal growth, as demonstrated for NbNiTe₅ and NbPdTe₅ layered tellurides [SS4, TS5]. This route likely reduces the bulk diffusion bottleneck of Ni metal [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is substantial because the references are ternary layered tellurides, not binary Ni₂Te₃, and iodine transport may favor different Ni–Te stoichiometries [SS4, TS5; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ni, so cation radius mismatch does not apply [Target]. The described target structure is a relatively simple 2D Ni–Te sheet, and the closest NiTe₂ analogue is also a simple layered structure [Target, CS1, SS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS provides exact amorphization behavior for Ni₂Te₃, but the small binary composition and ordered layered motif suggest low amorphization tendency [Target, CS1, SS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₂Te₃ is likely a **near-hull metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.033 eV/atom rather than zero [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: layered NiTe₂ and CoTe₂ are accessible by evacuated sealed-tube annealing [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS3], while thermodynamically similar tellurides near 0.02–0.04 eV/atom are experimentally represented [TS1, TS3, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the strong chemical and structural compatibility with layered Ni/Co tellurides that form from elemental powders in sealed tubes [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in the Ni–Te system, especially because the target composition lies between common NiTe/NiTe₂-type binaries [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues are noted for Ni–Co–Te in SS3/TS3]. High pressure and CVT are useful fallback routes, but they are less directly matched to the target structure [CS2, SS2, TS2, SS4, TS5].
