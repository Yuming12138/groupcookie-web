### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.010 eV/atom places it well inside the commonly synthesizable metastability window, giving a high F1 score. This is reinforced by experimentally reported low-hull analogues such as SnSb₄S₇ at 0.0075 eV/atom (CS3), SnPSe₃ at 0.0032 eV/atom (CS4/SS2/TS4), AgGa(PSe₃)₂ at 0.0161 eV/atom (TS3), and CuInP₂Se₆ at 0.0173 eV/atom (TS5). The main uncertainty is that CS1/CS5 include M–Si–Se phases with high hull energies but still experimentally accessible, showing that thermodynamic rank alone does not guarantee phase purity.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, since CS1/CS5 report 700–800 °C for M–Si–Se sealed-tube reactions and SS4 reports ~700 °C for SiPb₂Se₄. The severity penalty comes mainly from evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile Se, both recurring in CS1/CS5/SS4. Toxicity of Sb/Se is a [Reasonable Inference], and the remaining uncertainty is whether an intermediate grinding/reannealing cycle will be mandatory.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetic limitation is Si incorporation, because elemental Si is refractory and likely slow to react [Reasonable Inference], even though M–Si–Se analogues are accessible from elements (CS1, CS5). Long reaction times up to two weeks in CS1/CS5 and 3–4 weeks in CS2 support a moderate kinetic penalty. Use of Se-rich vapor/liquid transport in sealed ampoules may partially offset diffusion limitations, inferred from the success of sealed-tube selenide synthesis in CS1/CS5/SS4.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, consistent with SnPSe₃ CVT at 650–700 °C (CS4/SS2/TS4). However, it requires evacuated sealed quartz ampoules, volatile I₂/Se, and a multistage growth process, all directly supported by CS4/SS2/TS4. The toxicity/corrosivity penalty is a [Reasonable Inference] due to I₂, Se, and Sb.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT should improve mass transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, as SnPSe₃ single crystals were grown by iodine transport (CS4/SS2/TS4). The target has a related covalent dimer–selenide tetrahedral motif, but Si/Sb transport chemistry may differ from Sn/P transport chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether Si forms sufficiently volatile iodide species under the same conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses the 750 °C evacuated-tube Bridgman/melt conditions reported for layered selenophosphates (TS3, TS5), so it is not temperature-extreme. It still requires sealed evacuated ampoules, control of volatile Se, and controlled cooling/annealing, as in TS3/TS5. The toxicity penalty is a [Reasonable Inference] from Sb/Se handling.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

This route receives the highest F3 because melt or partial-melt growth improves mixing and cation ordering relative to purely solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. TS3 and TS5 are strong analogues because they are layered trigonal selenides containing octahedral metal–Se units and covalent P–P/PSe₃ tetrahedral units, closely paralleling the target’s SbSe₆ and Si–Si/SiSe₃ units. Remaining uncertainty comes from possible incongruent melting, which TS3/TS5 explicitly note for some related compounds.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized because SS1/TS2 and SS3/TS1 require 3–7 GPa or 5.5 GPa and temperatures up to 1050–1200 °C. Specialized multi-anvil/cubic-anvil equipment, BN capsules, and graphite heaters are required in SS1/SS3/TS1/TS2. This route is therefore a contingency rather than the preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

High pressure and high temperature can rapidly drive reconstructive crystallization, as SS3/TS1 report 10–30 min treatments and SS1/TS2 report 8 h treatments. However, SS1/SS3/TS1/TS2 also show narrow pressure–temperature requirements and metastability, reducing the structural-reconstruction score. Insufficient evidence from CS for high-pressure necessity in the Si–Sb–Se target is treated as neutral relative to this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very different cation coordination preferences: Si forms a Si–Si bonded tetrahedral unit, while Sb forms SbSe₆ octahedra, which triggers the cation-radius/coordination mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, a motif seen in related layered selenides such as TS3/TS5 and M–Si–Se analogues CS1/CS5. Unit-cell-size risk is not supported by the provided data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral for that check.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SiSbSe₃ is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.010 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally realized low-hull chalcogenides in CS3, CS4/SS2/TS4, TS3, and TS5. The strongest support comes from TS3/TS5, which show that layered trigonal selenides with octahedral metal sites and covalent X–X selenide tetrahedral units can be synthesized in sealed tubes by melt/Bridgman methods. CS1/CS5 support the chemical feasibility of Si–Se ternary sealed-ampoule synthesis, although those analogues are not structurally identical. The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed-ampoule Se-rich chemistry to promote mass transport and prevent Se loss, as consistently shown in CS1/CS5/SS4/TS3/TS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering between Si–Si tetrahedral units and SbSe₆ octahedral sheets, with phase-mixture risk supported by CS3 and composition-optimization concerns in CS1/CS5.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy from CS/SS/TS |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Se volatility and ampoule pressure | High | Use evacuated sealed silica/quartz/Vycor ampoules as in CS1, CS5, SS4, TS3, and TS5; use slow low-temperature prereaction hold [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | All | Competing Sb₂Se₃ / SiSe₂ / elemental Si secondary phases | High | Use long annealing and composition optimization; CS1/CS5 note trial-and-error composition control, and CS3 reports mixtures with related sulfide phases. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Slow reaction of elemental Si | Medium–High | Use fine Si powder, repeated grinding, or pre-reacted SiSe₂ [Reasonable Inference]; long sealed-tube reactions up to two weeks are supported by CS1/CS5. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Non-stoichiometric CVT due to unequal Si/Sb/I transport | Medium–High | Start with low I₂ loading and use pre-reacted feed [Reasonable Inference]; temperature-gradient control is essential in SnPSe₃ CVT (CS4/SS2/TS4). |
| 5 | Route 3 | Incongruent melting or segregation during Bridgman cooling | Medium | Use controlled cooling and post-annealing; TS3/TS5 explicitly use quenching/annealing or controlled cooling depending on melting behavior. |
| 6 | Route 4 | Need for specialized high-pressure equipment and narrow stability window | High | Use high-pressure route only if ambient routes fail; SS1/SS3/TS1/TS2 show pressure/temperature optimization and controlled cooling/quenching are required. |
| 7 | All | Layered phase crystal growth difficulty | Medium | Apply slow cooling/Bridgman or CVT; TS3/TS5 support controlled cooling from melt, while CS4/SS2/TS4 support iodine CVT for layered selenides. |
| 8 | All | Mixed coordination/polyhedral ordering may favor disorder or amorphous products | Medium | Favor slow cooling plus annealing to promote ordering; long annealing is used in CS2 and composition/phase optimization is noted in CS1/CS5. |
