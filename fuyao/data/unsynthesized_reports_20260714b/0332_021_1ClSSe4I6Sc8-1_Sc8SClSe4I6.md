### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic driving force is highly favorable under the given computational criterion. This is consistent with many stable reference compounds: CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, and TS-5 all report E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. Insufficient evidence from SS alone exists to prove the exact Sc₈Se₄SI₆Cl phase is experimentally stable, because SS references are mainly rare-earth iodides/oxyiodides rather than Sc mixed chalcogenide halides.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The route inherits demanding sealed-ampoule, drybox, and controlled-cooling requirements from rare-earth iodides and telluride iodides (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-4, TS-5). Chemically similar selenides also use sealed silica tubes and long high-temperature holds (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The main uncertainty is whether silica alone is compatible with Sc halides; Ta containment is a [Reasonable Inference] from SS/TS iodide routes.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Kinetics are limited by refractory Sc-containing solids and the need to order four anions into a layered octahedral sheet. Long heating times in CS selenides and SS/TS iodides support the expectation of slow equilibration (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-5). The score is not lower because halides may form transient melts or reactive intermediates, as suggested by iodide-rich synthesis routes (SS-2, TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 2: alkali-halide flux growth

Flux growth still requires sealed ampoules, volatile components, and air-sensitive halides, giving the same severity deductions as Route 1. However, the maximum temperature can likely remain near 800 °C because CS-1 and CS-5 demonstrate CsI-flux growth of chemically similar selenides at 800 °C. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for alkali-halide flux growth of the exact Sc chalcogenide-halide, so the temperature window remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2: alkali-halide flux growth

Flux improves kinetics by dissolving/reacting precursors and promoting crystal growth, directly supported by CsI-flux selenide syntheses in CS-1 and CS-5. Slow cooling protocols in SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, and TS-5 support the need for controlled crystallization of iodide-rich layered phases. The limiting uncertainty is whether Cl incorporates into the target sheet rather than remaining in flux or forming ScCl₃-rich byproducts.

#### F2 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

CVT severity is moderate-to-high because TS-2 requires a sealed quartz ampoule, I₂ transport agent, and high temperature near 950 °C. The method avoids high pressure, unlike SS-5/TS-1, but volatile iodine/chalcogen species require careful ampoule design. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS exists for CVT of the full Sc–Se–S–I–Cl composition.

#### F3 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

TS-2 is a strong signal that Sc sulfide species can be transported with I₂ at high temperature, which improves kinetic feasibility relative to dense solid-state diffusion. However, vapor-phase transport may fractionate Se, S, I, and Cl, so composition control is likely harder than in flux growth. This concern is a [Reasonable Inference] because TS-2 only treats ScS, not mixed-anion Sc halochalcogenides.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any additional cation-disorder risk. The formula unit contains 20 atoms, below the >40-atom trigger, and the described connectivity is corner/edge sharing without face-sharing coexistence. Layered iodide analogues with edge-sharing motifs are known in SS-1 and TS-5, supporting a relatively low amorphization risk despite mixed anions.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₈Se₄SI₆Cl is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, consistent with stable analogues in CS and TS. The strongest synthetic support comes from CsI-flux growth of chemically similar selenides at 800 °C (CS-1, CS-5) and sealed Ta/silica iodide synthesis of structurally similar layered halides (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is a halide-rich molten/flux environment that can lower diffusion barriers and buffer iodine/chlorine chemical potentials. The most critical limiting factor is multianion stoichiometry control, especially preventing loss or segregation of I, Cl, S, and Se. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but experimentally nontrivial, with flux growth being the most promising first attempt.
