### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is explicitly reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, giving a high F1 score. Similar Tl pnictides/chalcogenides such as AuTlSb, CdTlSb, Tl₂(CdSb)₃, TlSbTe₂, and TlBiTe₂ are also on the hull in the supplied data (CS1–CS3, SS4–SS5, TS1–TS4), supporting the idea that Tl-containing Zintl/intermetallic pnictides can be true equilibrium phases. SS1 and SS3 provide weaker thermodynamic support because their listed E_hull values are positive, so they are treated as neutral/less useful for this factor. Remaining uncertainty is experimental: a computed ground state can still be difficult to isolate if the phase has a narrow temperature or composition stability window.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule direct solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the temperatures are not extreme, and direct elemental sealed-tube synthesis is standard for CdTlSb and TlSbTe₂ (CS2/SS5/TS3; SS4/TS2). The main penalties come from vacuum/controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed-tube requirements, and toxic Tl/Pb/As handling [Reasonable Inference, supported by sealed ampoule use in CS1, CS2, SS4, TS2, TS3]. Kinetically, elemental precursors are less active than solution precursors, but low-melting Tl and Pb should improve contact and diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because the target’s 2D Pmn2₁ structure may require careful ordering, and long reaction times are typical in related systems (CS1, CS2/SS5/TS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: melt homogenization / slow cooling

Route 2 has harsher practical requirements than Route 1 because it involves a melt, slow cooling/Bridgman-like control, and post-annealing, giving a lower F2 score. However, F3 is best for this route because a Tl/Pb-rich melt should strongly improve mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling has directly enabled crystal growth in AuTlSb (CS1) and controlled crystallization in TlSbTe₂ (SS4/TS2). TS4 further supports controlled solidification/annealing for thermodynamically similar Tl-containing crystals, although it uses different halide chemistry. The remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS1 reports multiphase products for an unoptimized AuTlSb stoichiometry, and CS3/SS2/TS1 notes narrow temperature stability/disorder for TlBX-type compounds.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: thin-film vapor deposition

Thin-film deposition avoids bulk diffusion limits but introduces high-vacuum equipment and severe composition-control issues. SS3 provides the only useful support, showing Tl/Pb-containing materials can be made by vacuum evaporation followed by heating, but it also reports defect/disordered phases and narrow composition windows. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports direct thin-film growth of TlPbAs, so this route is scored as exploratory. The biggest kinetic limitation is maintaining the 1:1:1 Tl:Pb:As ratio during annealing because Tl and As are volatile [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple three-element, layered crystalline description with one Tl, one Pb, and one As site, so the unit-cell/structural-complexity risk appears low from the target data. No mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network is reported for TlPbAs, unlike some more complex analogues with octahedral sharing (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS4/TS2), so C3 is not triggered for the target. The Tl/Pb size mismatch is likely modest because both are heavy, soft post-transition elements [Reasonable Inference], but direct radii data are not provided by CS/SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is that layered materials can disorder or stack fault, but that is more a crystallinity/phase-purity issue than an amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlPbAs is likely a **ground-state phase**, because the target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: several Tl pnictides/chalcogenides are made directly from elements in sealed ampoules at 600–750 °C or around 1000 K (CS1, CS2/SS5/TS3, SS4/TS2), and many are also thermodynamic ground states (CS1–CS3, TS1–TS4). The most important enabling factor is the low-melting, chemically soft Tl/Pb/As system, which should allow melt-assisted reaction and improved diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is volatile/toxic Tl and As management in sealed tubes, especially maintaining exact stoichiometry. A secondary limiting factor is phase selectivity, because AuTlSb and TlBX-type analogues show multiphase behavior, narrow stability ranges, or disorder under some conditions (CS1, CS3/SS2/TS1).
