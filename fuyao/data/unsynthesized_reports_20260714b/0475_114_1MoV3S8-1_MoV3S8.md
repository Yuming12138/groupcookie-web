### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0200 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom range [Target]. Thermodynamically similar references cluster around E_hull ≈0.019–0.040 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS5], strongly supporting accessibility. Chemically and structurally similar sulfides also include stable or near-stable phases [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or cation ordering in the specific V₃MoS₈ phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses sealed evacuated silica tubes and high temperatures around 850–1000 °C, which are standard for sulfide solid-state reactions [CS1, CS3, TS4]. The main deductions come from vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile sulfur, and multi-step grind/refire procedures [CS1, TS4]. CS gives strong support for this route because Mo/V/S-containing mixtures were fired in evacuated tubes [CS1]; SS gives indirect support through related sulfides but not the exact V–Mo–S chemistry [SS3–SS5]; TS supports similar high-temperature evacuated-tube sulfide synthesis [TS4]. No high pressure or ultra-high temperature is indicated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental V and Mo are refractory, so direct diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with analogues requiring long heating and repeated grinding [CS1, CS3, TS4]. The route benefits from sulfur vapor in sealed tubes, which can form binary sulfides and increase reactivity [Reasonable Inference], but cation ordering between V and Mo is still likely the rate-limiting step. CS supports chemical transferability [CS1], while SS provides insufficient direct evidence for V/Mo ordering in this structure. The kinetic score is therefore relatively low despite favorable thermodynamics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires an evacuated sealed tube, iodine vapor, volatile sulfur, and a temperature gradient, all of which increase operational severity [SS4, TS2, CS4]. Some analogues operate at 900→800 °C [SS4, TS2], while another related CVT route uses 1050→900 °C [CS4], so the T >1000 °C deduction is conservatively applied. I₂ is hazardous and volatile [SS4, TS2], and optimization of transport-agent loading is likely required [SS2, TS1]. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS for high pressure.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT can overcome slow bulk diffusion by forming mobile vapor species, as shown by V₅S₈ crystal growth from V + S + I₂ [SS4, TS2]. Structurally similar layered VS₂-related phases also benefit from iodine-assisted crystallization [SS2, TS1]. However, the target contains both V and Mo, and CS/SS/TS do not directly prove congruent V/Mo transport or ordered V₃MoS₈ crystallization. Thus, the kinetics are better than simple elemental diffusion but still uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C because V–M–S analogues use 750 °C H₂S sulfidation and 850 °C sealed annealing [CS5, SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5]. The severity remains significant because dry H₂S is toxic/corrosive and sulfur annealing requires sealed tubes [CS5, SS3, TS5]. The process is inherently multi-step: sulfidation, grinding, sulfur adjustment, pelletization/sealing, and long anneal [CS5, SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that Mo oxide precursors behave cleanly in this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Oxide sulfidation with H₂S is chemically active and is proven for several V–M–S sulfides [CS5, SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5]. However, conversion from oxides to a layered ternary sulfide requires major structural reconstruction, and long annealing is needed for homogeneous crystalline products [CS5, SS3, TS5]. The presence of Mo increases uncertainty because Mo oxide sulfidation may form MoS₂ preferentially [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the kinetic score is moderate-low despite reactive sulfur chemistry.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no triggered amorphization checks: V–S and Mo–S bond lengths are close, the formula unit is small, and the described target network does not explicitly mix vertex, edge, and face sharing [Target]. Structurally similar materials are crystalline layered sulfides [SS1–SS5], which provides some confidence, but amorphization is not explicitly discussed in CS/SS/TS. Some analogues do show mixed corner/edge/face connectivity [SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5], but this is not stated for V₃MoS₈ and is therefore not applied as a trigger. The main uncertainty is whether nanoscale V/Mo disorder could produce poorly crystalline products before annealing [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃MoS₈ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0200 eV/atom [Target], and thermodynamically similar sulfides with comparable hull energies are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS5]. The analogue support is good for sulfide chemistry and layered/VS₆ frameworks, with direct sealed-tube elemental synthesis supported by CS and TS references [CS1, CS3, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube sulfur chemistry, which maintains sulfur activity and allows refractory metals to convert to sulfides [CS1, CS3, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is slow V/Mo cation diffusion and ordering, because Mo and V are refractory and the target requires a specific ternary layered arrangement [Reasonable Inference]. Competing binary phases such as VS₂/V₅S₈ and MoS₂ are plausible risks because structurally similar V sulfides are stable/synthesizable [SS1, SS4, TS2] and Mo sulfide competition is a common concern in Mo–S chemistry [Reasonable Inference].
