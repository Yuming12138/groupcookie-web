### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability band and is scored 100. This is reinforced by several chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically related bromides that are also on or very close to the convex hull: Zr(InBr₃)₂ and CuAlBr₄ [CS-2, CS-3], RuBr₃ and PdBr₂ [SS-2, SS-5], and the listed thermodynamic analogues [TS-1 to TS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or redox compatibility during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT / Bromine-Assisted Transport

The severity score is reduced because sealed evacuated ampoules and controlled temperature gradients are standard in the analogues but still require careful handling [CS-1, SS-1, SS-3]. Br₂ or AlBr₃/Al₂Br₆ transport agents are volatile and moisture-sensitive, as shown in Ti(AlBr₄)₂ and Ru/Pd bromide transport [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. Temperatures remain below 1000 °C in the recommended program, so no high-temperature penalty is applied; high-pressure >5 GPa is also not required based on the analogues [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5].

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT / Bromine-Assisted Transport

CVT improves kinetic access because vapor transport can bypass slow solid-state diffusion; this is supported by RuBr₃ and PdBr₂ single-crystal growth under Br₂-assisted gradients [SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3]. However, the target is a ternary ordered Re/Ni/Cu bromide sheet, while many analogues are binary or cluster/ribbon bromides [SS-2, SS-3, TS-1]. The major uncertainty is whether volatile Re, Ni, and Cu bromide species transport at compatible rates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Molten Bromide Flux / Self-Flux Growth

The flux route avoids very high temperature and GPa pressure, matching the moderate conditions of Zr(InBr₃)₂ flux growth at 475 °C and Cu-halide melt chemistry at 250 °C [CS-2, CS-3]. Deductions are still applied for vacuum/sealed operation, volatile halide fluxes, and moisture-sensitive metal bromides [CS-2, CS-3, CS-5]. CS provides strong support for flux and melt processing, while SS/TS provide less direct flux evidence for ternary Re/Ni/Cu bromides; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this exact flux chemistry is treated as neutral source signal = 50.

#### F3 — Route 2: Molten Bromide Flux / Self-Flux Growth

Flux growth receives the highest kinetics score because liquid bromide media enhance precursor activity and ion transport, as shown by Zr oxidation in InBr₃ melt and slow-cooling crystal growth [CS-2]. Cu-containing halide frameworks can form from molten halide precursors in sealed tubes [CS-3], supporting the use of a molten bromide medium for Cu/Re/Ni bromide assembly. The main uncertainty is redox control: Cu(II) can reduce to Cu(I), and Re(IV) must be maintained [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Direct Bromination / High-Pressure Halogenation

This route is penalized more strongly because high Br₂ pressure and autoclave containment are explicitly used in TlFeBr₄ and RuBr₃ analogues [CS-4, TS-1]. Although the pressure is far below 5 GPa, it introduces safety and equipment complexity [CS-4]. A second CVT or annealing step is likely needed to improve crystallinity, analogous to RuBr₃ powder synthesis followed by crystal growth [TS-1].

#### F3 — Route 3: Direct Bromination / High-Pressure Halogenation

Direct bromination is kinetically less favorable because Re metal is refractory and ternary mixing must occur after bromination [Reasonable Inference]. RuBr₃ required 6–8 days of high-pressure halogenation before crystal growth [TS-1], indicating slow kinetics even for a binary transition-metal bromide. The route is plausible but carries high risk of binary bromide impurities such as ReBr₄/ReBr₅, NiBr₂, CuBr/CuBr₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: the Re/Ni/Cu octahedral cation size mismatch is near or slightly above 15% [Reasonable Inference]. The provided target structure contains edge-sharing octahedra only, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered; related materials show face-sharing or corner-sharing motifs separately, but not target mixed vertex/edge/face coexistence [SS-2, SS-3, SS-4]. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given; based on the described formula and P-1 sheet model there is insufficient evidence for >40 atoms, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target amorphization, neutral source signal = 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ReNiCuBr₈ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase** because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of crystalline metal bromides with MBr₆ octahedra, sealed-ampoule processing, CVT, and flux/melt growth [CS-1 to CS-3, SS-2 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a **molten bromide flux or bromine-buffered sealed environment**, which provides high bromide activity and cation mobility at moderate temperature [CS-2, CS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is **redox and stoichiometry control** among Re, Ni, and Cu, especially maintaining Re(IV) and Cu(II) without forming binary bromides or Cu(I)-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require dry, sealed-tube halide chemistry and careful slow-cooling optimization.
