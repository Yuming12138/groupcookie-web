### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0090 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom range by the supplied rubric. Chemically similar sulfides in CS-1 to CS-5 are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and structurally similar examples in SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, and SS-5 are also on the hull, supporting that this chemical/structural family contains stable phases. Thermodynamic analogues in TS-1 to TS-5 are near-hull, ~0.002–0.016 eV/atom, consistent with accessible layered sulfide polymorphs. Remaining uncertainty is that a 0.009 eV/atom phase may still be metastable against very close competing V–W sulfides or WS₂/VS₂ mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1, sealed solid-state

Route 1 uses standard sealed-ampoule conditions similar to CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-5, so no high-pressure or >1400 °C penalty is justified. The main penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube handling, sulfur volatility, and likely repeated grinding/annealing, all supported by evacuated-tube and long-duration practices in CS-1 and TS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a need for toxic reagents in this route, so that source contribution is neutral for toxicity. The route is moderately severe but experimentally conventional.

#### F3 — Route 1, sealed solid-state

The kinetic score is limited by refractory W/V diffusion [Reasonable Inference], despite the fact that analogous sulfides form at 700–1000 °C in CS-2, CS-3, SS-5, and TS-5. Using WS₂/VS₂-like precursors should improve structural matching to the molybdenite-derived target [Reasonable Inference supported by layered TS analogues], but ordering V on a minority metal sublattice may require long annealing. CS-1, CS-2, and TS-5 report phase-purity or homogenization issues, supporting a lower kinetics score. Remaining uncertainty is whether VW₃S₈ forms congruently from binaries or requires vapor/flux transport.

#### F2 — Route 2, iodine CVT

Route 2 is supported by many close analogues grown by iodine CVT: CS-4, CS-5, SS-3, SS-4, TS-1, and TS-4. It avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but it requires evacuated ampoules, a volatile transport agent, multiple processing steps, and toxic/corrosive I₂ [toxicity is Reasonable Inference; I₂ use is from CS/SS/TS]. The severity score is therefore lower than direct annealing despite better crystal-growth precedent. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether V/W iodide volatility can be perfectly balanced, so this remains a key uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2, iodine CVT

CVT improves kinetics by transporting material through volatile iodine complexes, as shown for Ta₄FeS₈, FeTa₃S₆, FeNb₃S₆, TaS₂, and MoS₂-related materials in CS-4, CS-5, SS-3, TS-1, and TS-4. The layered molybdenite-derived target matches the broad structural class of CVT-grown TS analogues, increasing the structural reconstruction score. However, V and W may form iodides with different volatilities, creating composition gradients or off-stoichiometric crystals [Reasonable Inference]. This is the best route because it balances near-equilibrium crystallization with enhanced mass transport.

#### F2 — Route 3, flux

Flux synthesis is directly supported only by TS-3, which reports KCl melt at 900 °C under N₂ or H₂S for TaS₂/NbS₂-derived phases. The route does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, but it may require controlled atmosphere, flux washing, and possibly toxic H₂S, giving a moderate severity penalty. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of close ternary V/W sulfides, so their source contribution for this route is neutral where not applicable. The route is plausible but less evidenced than CVT or sealed annealing.

#### F3 — Route 3, flux

Molten salt can improve contact and mobility relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and TS-3 demonstrates KCl-assisted synthesis at 900 °C. However, TS-3 also reports difficulty with crystal quality, stacking disorder, and phase control, which lowers the structural/phase kinetics score. For VW₃S₈, flux may help overcome W/V diffusion but may also stabilize competing layered sulfides or chloride-contaminated products [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is high because no CS/SS analogue directly demonstrates KCl flux for this specific ternary V–W sulfide family.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ordered crystalline 2D sulfide with a relatively small formula unit, and no explicit target evidence indicates a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity. CS/SS/TS layered sulfides frequently form crystalline sheets or single crystals, especially TS-1, TS-4, CS-4, and SS-3, which argues against strong amorphization tendency. The cation size mismatch between V and W is unlikely to exceed 15% in similar coordination [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius evidence, so those checks are treated neutrally at the source level. The main uncertainty is not amorphization but cation ordering and competing phase formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VW₃S₈ is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0090 eV/atom, and TS analogues are also near-hull layered sulfides. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis well: direct sealed reactions, long anneals, and CVT are all repeatedly successful for related transition-metal sulfides, especially in CS-4, CS-5, SS-3, TS-1, and TS-4. The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for 2D molybdenite-like sulfide formation and CVT crystal growth under 800–1000 °C sealed-ampoule conditions. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control of V/W ordering in a W-rich sulfide lattice, especially because W is refractory and V/W iodide transport may be unequal [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable with moderate effort, with CVT after a prereaction being the preferred route.
