### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.046 eV/atom, which is inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues near this energy, including BaBrCl at 0.0484 eV/atom and SrThBr₆ at 0.0451 eV/atom, have reported solid-state syntheses [TS1, TS4]. The phase is probably metastable rather than a strict ground state, but its energy is low enough that kinetic trapping or entropic stabilization is plausible [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is the exact competing Sc–Se–Cl phase field, which is not provided by CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed solid-state.**  
Severity is moderate because similar reactions require evacuated ampoules, high temperature near 900 °C, volatile chalcogen/halide species, and moisture-sensitive chloride handling [CS2, SS2, SS5]. Kinetics are only moderate because binary Sc₂Se₃ and ScCl₃ must reconstruct into a layered ScSeCl sheet, and analogues often require 7–20 d holds [CS2, SS2, SS5]. The strong enabling evidence is that phase-pure HoSI and single-phase DySI form by direct ampoule reaction [SS2, SS5]. The limiting uncertainty is whether small Sc³⁺ favors the same layered chalcogenide chloride structure without forming Sc₂Se₃ or ScCl₃-rich byproducts.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, halide flux.**  
Severity is similar to Route 1 because vacuum/inert handling and volatile halide/chalcogen chemistry remain necessary [CS4, SS1, TS1]. Kinetics improve because halide fluxes promote ion transport and crystal growth, as CsCl does for YCuS₂ [CS4] and NaCl/halide media are relevant to Er–Se–halide chemistry [SS1]. TS analogues support slow-cooled halide melts or sealed-tube halide reactions as viable near-hull synthesis environments [TS1, TS2]. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, because SS1 reports difficulty obtaining some chloride/selenide-halide products.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, CVT.**  
CVT has higher severity because it adds a separate crystal-growth step and uses volatile/corrosive I₂ or metal-halide transport agents [CS1, SS1]. Kinetically, vapor transport can produce good single crystals, as shown by GdPS and Er–Se–halide related work [CS1, SS1], but stoichiometry control in a ternary Sc–Se–Cl vapor system is likely narrow [Reasonable Inference from CS2/SS1 phase-control issues]. TS gives insufficient evidence for CVT specifically and is treated as neutral for this route. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth optimization after Route 1/2 establishes phase formation.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, high pressure.**  
High-pressure synthesis is severe because CS3/CS5 require 3–5 GPa, specialized multianvil equipment, sealed noble-metal capsules, controlled heating/cooling, and reactive halide fluxes. Kinetically, it can be favorable because layered ZrNCl/ZrNBr phases form in ~1 h under high pressure [CS3, CS5]. However, the chemical transfer from nitride chloride/bromide to selenide chloride is weaker, and TS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure ScSeCl formation. This route is a fallback if the omega-derived trigonal polymorph is inaccessible at ambient pressure.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered. The formula and P3m1 layered structure imply a small unit cell rather than a >40 atom complex cell, and the described structure contains edge-sharing ScSe₃Cl₃ octahedra rather than mixed edge/vertex/face networks. Similar layered chalcogenide halides are crystalline under ampoule conditions [SS2, SS5], supporting low amorphization risk. The remaining uncertainty is that flux quenching or off-stoichiometry could produce poorly crystalline side phases, but the intrinsic structural amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScSeCl is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unambiguous ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.046 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is favorable: structurally similar layered lanthanide chalcogenide halides form by sealed-ampoule reaction [SS2, SS5], chemically similar LaSeCl forms from La/LaCl₃/Se at 800–900 °C [CS2], and thermodynamic analogues around the same hull energy are experimentally accessible [TS1, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of chloride-rich sealed or flux conditions that maintain Cl chemical potential while allowing slow crystallization [CS4, TS1, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry drift from volatile Se/chloride species, as seen in LaSeCl byproduct behavior and Er–Se–halide phase-control challenges [CS2, SS1]. Overall, halide-flux growth in an evacuated ampoule is the best first attempt.
