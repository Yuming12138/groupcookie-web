### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0110 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin; this directly supports a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low hull energies are experimentally synthesized, including CuHgSI at 0.02099 eV/atom (SS1/TS1), TlCdI3 at 0.01669 eV/atom (CS3/TS2), Hg3Se2I2 at 0.00531 eV/atom (SS5/TS3), and HgBrI at 0.01050 eV/atom (TS5). CS also contains stable or near-stable iodides such as CuGaI4 with E_hull = 0 (CS1) and TlCdI3 at 0.01669 eV/atom (CS3), supporting that low-energy ternary iodides are accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility of the specific P-31m layered polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The route requires vacuum or Ar sealed ampoule handling, which is directly supported by SS1/TS1, SS4, SS5/TS3, and CS4/TS4, so deductions were applied for controlled atmosphere and sealed volatile iodide conditions. The route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because analogues form at 250–350 °C or ~553 K (CS4/TS4, SS1/TS1, SS4). Air/moisture sensitivity is supported by strict moisture exclusion in CS1 and dry glovebox handling in CS4/TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact NiCuI3 decomposition pressure, so the volatile precursor deduction is conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Binary iodides should be more reactive than refractory oxides, and iodide analogues react at modest temperatures in sealed ampoules (SS1/TS1, SS4, CS4/TS4). However, CS4/TS4 reports repeated powdering and annealing for TlFeI3 purity, indicating ordering and phase-purity challenges for transition-metal iodides. The target requires arrangement of NiI6 octahedra and CuCuI3 trigonal pyramids, not a directly demonstrated analogue structure, so structural reconstruction was scored moderate. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for a direct P-31m Ni–Cu iodide analogue.

#### F2 — Route 2: melt growth / self-flux slow cooling

This route is supported by CuGaI4 grown from a CuI/GaI3 melt under strict moisture exclusion and sealed ampoule slow cooling (CS1). TlFeI3/TlMnI3 synthesis from binary iodides by melting/annealing under Ar/dry glovebox conditions also supports the route (CS4/TS4), while Bridgman growth of TlCdI3 supports melt-based crystal growth for ternary iodides (CS3/TS2). Deductions arise from vacuum/Ar control, volatile iodide components, and moisture-sensitive precursors, but not from high temperature or high pressure. Insufficient evidence from SS for exact NiI2–CuI melt behavior remains.

#### F3 — Route 2: melt growth / self-flux slow cooling

Melt or flux conditions strongly improve kinetics because diffusion occurs through a liquid or partially molten iodide medium; CS1 directly demonstrates slow-cooling crystallization from a CuI-containing melt. CS4/TS4 shows transition-metal iodides can be processed by melting/annealing, although repeated annealing may be required for purity. The score is not maximal because the target’s mixed octahedral/pyramidal 2D network is not directly represented by CS1’s tetrahedral CuGaI4 or CS4/TS4’s ribbon iodides. The main uncertainty is whether a CuI-rich flux stabilizes NiCuI3 rather than segregating CuI/NiI2.

#### F2 — Route 3: iodine-assisted CVT

SS2 and SS3 support sealed-ampoule iodine-rich crystal growth under vacuum/Ar and temperature gradients, with careful temperature programming. Deductions were applied for controlled atmosphere, volatile iodine, multi-step ampoule preparation, and air/moisture-sensitive iodide chemistry. Temperatures remain modest, so no high-temperature or high-pressure deductions were applied. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS exists for direct Ni–Cu–I CVT chemistry, making this route more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3: iodine-assisted CVT

Vapor availability is moderate because I2 transport is well supported by SS2/SS3, but NiI2 volatility and co-transport with CuI are uncertain. SS2 shows direct crystal growth can be achieved if the temperature ramp is carefully controlled, but the target phase may require post-annealing or precise iodine chemical potential. Composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because CuI and iodine vapor pressures can shift the Ni:Cu:I ratio. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a broad NiCuI3 stability window.

#### F2 — Route 4: hydrothermal / solvothermal HI

CS5 supports hydrothermal iodide synthesis at 220 °C for 5 days in concentrated HI using quartz tube/autoclave containment. This route avoids high temperature and high pressure by the scoring definition, but it involves a sealed volatile/corrosive medium and multiple handling steps. Toxic/air-sensitive deductions are justified by concentrated HI and iodide sensitivity described in CS5. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports hydrothermal synthesis of transition-metal copper iodides, so the route is exploratory.

#### F3 — Route 4: hydrothermal / solvothermal HI

Solution-mediated transport in concentrated HI can give high precursor activity and low-temperature reaction, as shown by CS5. However, CS5 explicitly reports multiphase products, contaminants, and intergrowths, so structural selectivity is a major concern. For NiCuI3, Cu(I)/Cu(II) redox balance and Ni iodide complexation could prevent nucleation of the desired layered phase. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for hydrothermal formation of the specific NiI6/CuI3 2D network.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small formula unit and only corner- and edge-sharing connectivity, with no face-sharing described, giving a low amorphization risk by the prescribed checks. The cation size mismatch between Ni(II) and Cu(I) is estimated to be below or near the 15% threshold; this is [Reasonable Inference] because CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii. Structurally similar iodides in SS/TS are crystalline rather than amorphous under sealed-ampoule conditions, supporting low amorphization tendency (SS1/TS1, SS4, SS5/TS3). Remaining uncertainty is possible local disorder between Cu/Ni or iodine vacancies.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NiCuI3 is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, within the range of experimentally synthesized analogues in CS/SS/TS. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Cu- and transition-metal iodides made in sealed ampoules by melt growth, Bridgman growth, or annealing (CS1, CS3, CS4/TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, mobile iodide chemistry: several analogues form at only 150–350 °C or ~553 K in sealed tubes rather than requiring refractory solid-state conditions (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially maintaining Cu(I), Ni(II), and iodine chemical potential without forming CuI, NiI2, or iodine-rich competing phases; repeated annealing was needed for related TlFeI3 purity (CS4/TS4). Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with melt/flux slow cooling as the best first route.
