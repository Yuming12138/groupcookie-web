### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable cutoff in the supplied rubric, so a score of 90 is justified. TS-1 is highly relevant because NaHgCl3 has E_hull ≈ 0.055 eV/atom and is experimentally crystallized from aqueous HgCl2/NaCl, indicating that slightly metastable alkali mercury chlorides can be accessible. SS-1 and SS-3 are structurally similar and on-hull, supporting the general stability of Hg chloride frameworks, while CS is mixed because it includes both stable chloride salts and a high-energy Hg/N/Cl analogue; therefore CS alone is not decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses room-temperature aqueous crystallization, supported by SS-2/TS-1 and CS-1, so it avoids high temperature, pressure, vacuum, or specialized atmosphere. The only major F2 penalty is HgCl2 toxicity, which is unavoidable for direct Hg–Cl synthesis. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for any need for inert atmosphere in the target’s aqueous route, so no atmosphere penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because SS-2/TS-1 show direct aqueous crystallization of NaHgCl3 from HgCl2 and NaCl at room temperature. The target’s HgCl2 clusters are chemically compatible with molecular HgCl2-rich crystallization, supported by CS-1 and SS-4. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS-2 and TS-5 report that related HgX2 evaporation systems can yield mixtures, so structural assembly of the exact CsHg5Cl11 phase may require careful stoichiometry and evaporation rate.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is low-temperature but more severe than Route 1 because SS-1 and SS-3 use evacuated quartz ampoules, long heating, and HgCl2-containing sealed systems. The penalties for vacuum, volatile HgCl2, multistep preparation, and HgCl2 toxicity are all applicable. There is insufficient evidence from TS for this exact sealed-ampoule Cs–Hg–Cl target, so TS contributes only general thermodynamic support, not condition simplification.

#### F3 — Route 2

SS-1 and SS-3 demonstrate that structurally similar Hg chloride phases can form phase-pure at ~280 °C, implying good precursor activity and low diffusion barriers due to HgCl2 flux/transport. However, those analogues require 4–8 weeks for crystal growth in some cases, showing slow ordering kinetics. The target’s one-dimensional ribbon plus molecular HgCl2 architecture may benefit from vapor/flux transport, but exact Cs/Hg ordering remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids high external pressure and very high temperature, and TS-4 shows hydrothermal Hg halide crystal growth at 670 K in sealed vessels. It still receives deductions for sealed volatile/corrosive media, multistep autoclave/ampoule handling, and HgCl2/HCl hazards. There is insufficient evidence from CS and SS for a direct Cs–Hg–Cl hydrothermal route, so this route is less strongly supported than Routes 1–2.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal solution transport should give favorable kinetics, and TS-4 reports Hg-containing halide crystals after 24 h with controlled cooling. High chloride activity should stabilize chloro-mercurate species and reduce hydrolysis risk. The uncertainty is structural transferability, because TS-4 involves Cu/Hg/S/Br chemistry rather than a pure Cs–Hg–Cl phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Cs+ and Hg2+, triggering the ionic-radius criterion by reasonable physicochemical inference. The unit cell size appears moderate from the formula and target description, and the reported connectivity is mainly corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. Thus the amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a borderline metastable phase rather than a robust deep ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold between highly stable and moderately metastable. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well: SS-2/TS-1 directly support room-temperature alkali mercury chloride crystallization, while SS-1/SS-3 support sealed-ampoule HgCl2-rich routes for structurally similar compounds. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and strong complexation of HgCl2/chloro-mercurate species, which allows crystallization under mild solution or low-temperature flux conditions. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity, because related HgX2 evaporation systems can form mixtures and the target requires a specific Cs:Hg:Cl ratio with both HgCl2 clusters and CsHg4Cl9 ribbons. Overall, the best path is careful aqueous crystallization with slow supersaturation control, followed by sealed-ampoule optimization if the solution route yields competing phases.
