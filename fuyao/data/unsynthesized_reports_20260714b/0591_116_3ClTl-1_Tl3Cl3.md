### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0270 eV/atom lies in the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom category. TS-2 provides a close thermodynamic analogue with E_hull ≈ 0.0272 eV/atom that was synthesized hydrothermally, and TS-4/TS-5 show other halide phases with E_hull ≈ 0.02–0.026 eV/atom prepared by ampoule solid-state routes. CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 show exact TlCl is very near the hull in a cubic polymorph, supporting chemical stability but not proving the target polymorph. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph competition rather than decomposition.

#### F2 — Route 1: Low-temperature vacuum evaporation

The severity score is lowered mainly because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 require vacuum, cryogenic substrate control, slow evaporation, and controlled heating, while TlCl is a toxic thallium precursor [Reasonable Inference from precursor identity]. The route avoids high temperature and high pressure, which strongly helps the F2 score. CS-2/SS-2 support that closely related TlBr can be handled under similar conditions. The main uncertainty is whether the tetragonal P4/mmm phase can be stabilized within the same temperature window.

#### F3 — Route 1: Low-temperature vacuum evaporation

Kinetics are favorable because TlCl is directly evaporable in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1, giving high precursor-vapor availability. Crystallization is only moderately scored because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 report metastable polymorph sensitivity, meaning the target may require a narrow temperature/deposition-rate window. Composition control is easier for single-source TlCl than multi-source films [Reasonable Inference], but volatile loss and substrate effects remain possible. This route is still the best because it most directly accesses kinetic trapping.

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule melt/quench/anneal

SS-3, SS-4, TS-4, and TS-5 show that sealed ampoules and halide melts/anneals are common for related thallium/halide materials. The route avoids extreme temperature and pressure but requires evacuated sealing, volatile/toxic TlCl containment, and multiple handling steps. Insufficient evidence from CS directly supports bulk melt formation of the target tetragonal TlCl polymorph, so CS is neutral for the phase-specific part. The route is synthetically practical but less selective.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule melt/quench/anneal

Melt processing gives good atomic mobility, supported by SS-3 Bridgman/melt growth and TS-4/TS-5 halide melting/annealing. However, structural reconstruction is difficult because the target layered P4/mmm polymorph differs from the exact cubic TlCl analogue in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. Long annealing, as used in SS-3 and TS-4/TS-5, may actually allow relaxation to the stable polymorph rather than preserve the metastable one [Reasonable Inference]. Quenching is therefore critical but unproven for this phase.

#### F2 — Route 3: Hydrothermal/halide-acid recrystallization

TS-2, TS-3, and SS-5 show thallium halide-containing phases can be synthesized at only 120–200 °C, so thermal severity is low. The deductions come from sealed evacuated or autoclave operation, multiple steps, and thallium toxicity. Insufficient evidence from CS supports hydrothermal synthesis of binary TlCl polymorphs, so CS is neutral for this route. The method is operationally moderate but phase-selectivity uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal/halide-acid recrystallization

Solution-mediated transport gives high effective precursor activity, consistent with TS-2/TS-3/SS-5 hydrothermal thallium halide chemistry. Ion diffusion is not limiting because crystallization occurs from solution at moderate temperature. The structural score is low because no source demonstrates hydrothermal formation of the target binary P4/mmm TlCl, and TS-3 notes that some targeted halide compositions can yield different phases. Hydrothermal growth may be useful for crystals but is not the most evidence-backed route.

#### F2 — Route 4: CVT/sublimation recrystallization

CS-3 and CS-4 show monohalide vapor transport at moderate temperatures using sealed tubes and gradients. The severity score is penalized for sealed volatile transport, possible Cl2/HCl atmosphere, multiple steps, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from SS for TlCl CVT specifically, so SS is neutral for this method. The route is chemically plausible but not directly validated for the target.

#### F3 — Route 4: CVT/sublimation recrystallization

Vapor transport kinetics are plausible because CS-3 and CS-4 grow monohalide crystals by transport. However, the target is a metastable TlCl polymorph, and CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 imply that TlCl polymorphs are sensitive to temperature and may transform on warming. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target TlCl P4/mmm phase is likely a **low-energy metastable polymorph**, not the dominant ground-state form, because its E_hull is 0.0270 eV/atom while exact TlCl is known in a low-energy cubic Fm-3m form in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support that TlCl and closely related thallium halides can be synthesized, especially by low-temperature vacuum evaporation in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 and CS-2/SS-2. The most critical enabling factor is the very low energy above hull combined with direct availability of volatile TlCl for kinetic thin-film trapping, supported by CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control, because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 explicitly state that metastable halide phases depend sensitively on deposition temperature, rate, and heating history. Overall, the material is synthesizable but most likely as a low-temperature thin film or transient/metastable phase rather than as an equilibrium bulk powder.
