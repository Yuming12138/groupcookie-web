### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0240 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range associated with high synthesizability. [Target] Thermodynamic analogues AgCuS, TlCu₃Se₂, and Tl₅Cu₁₄Se₁₀ are experimentally synthesized at similar E_hull values of ~0.019–0.046 eV/atom, supporting that this energy range is accessible. [TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5] CS analogues are mostly near-hull Tl sulfides, supporting Tl–sulfide stability, although they differ in transition metal and stoichiometry. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5] Remaining uncertainty is that CuTlS may be a low-temperature or narrow-composition phase rather than a robust ground-state compound. [Reasonable Inference from TS-3/TS-5 phase-mixture behavior]

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable and consistent with AgCuS synthesis at 300 °C in evacuated ampoules. [SS-2, TS-1] The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Tl/S components, and Tl toxicity. [CS-1, CS-3, TS-3; Reasonable Inference] CS high-temperature data are not directly required for this route but support sealed-tube handling of Tl sulfides. [CS-1, CS-3]

#### F3: Route 1

Binary sulfides should provide better chemical activity than elemental Cu/Tl/S, as demonstrated by AgCuS formation from Ag₂S/Cu₂S. [SS-2, TS-1] Long reaction times in AgCuS and Tl–Cu–Se analogues indicate that cation ordering and diffusion may still be slow. [SS-2, TS-1, TS-3, TS-5] The structure requires reconstruction into a 2D CuTlS sheet, so F3 is moderate rather than high. [Target; Reasonable Inference]

#### F2: Route 2

Route 2 is supported by CS high-temperature sealed-tube syntheses of Tl sulfides at 800–1000 °C and SS/TS Ag₃CuS₂ synthesis at 1273 K. [CS-1, CS-3, SS-3, TS-2] It is penalized because high-temperature Tl/S chemistry requires sealed evacuated ampoules and careful cooling/quenching. [CS-1, CS-3] High pressure is not selected because only the Pb analogue explicitly required 40 kbar, and that route is not directly necessary for CuTlS. [CS-2]

#### F3: Route 2

High temperature improves diffusion, and CS analogues demonstrate formation of Tl sulfides under 800–1000 °C conditions. [CS-1, CS-3] However, direct synthesis from elements requires complete structural reconstruction and may promote competing binaries or off-stoichiometric ternaries. [CS-1 phase-control note; TS-3/TS-5 phase-mixture behavior] Therefore, F3 is lower than Route 1 despite higher T.

#### F2: Route 3

Mechanochemical activation avoids very high temperature but introduces additional steps and inert/sealed handling. [SS-5, TS-4] The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, multistep processing, and Tl toxicity. [SS-5, TS-4; Reasonable Inference] It may be safer thermally than Route 2 because lower annealing temperatures reduce Tl/S vapor pressure. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3: Route 3

Mechanical alloying produced high-purity Ag₃CuS₂ in the analogue literature, which supports improved kinetics through intimate mixing and defect formation. [SS-5, TS-4] Long annealing was still required in the analogue, so structural ordering remains a moderate barrier. [SS-5, TS-4] This route scores slightly better kinetically than Route 1 but worse in condition severity due to processing complexity. [SS-5, TS-4]

#### F2/F3: Route 4

Bridgman growth is only weakly supported because SS-1 confirms Bridgman preparation of a structurally similar sulfide but gives no detailed conditions. [SS-1] Insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports Bridgman growth of CuTlS, so those source contributions are neutral at 50. [CS/TS] The route is mainly useful for single crystals after a powder synthesis route is established. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Cu/Tl cation size mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk check. [Reasonable Inference] The target description does not indicate a very large unit cell or mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing polyhedral framework, so only one of three checks is triggered. [Target] The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with multiple crystalline Cu/Tl chalcogenide analogues being reported. [TS-3, TS-5, SS-2]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuTlS is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0240 eV/atom. [Target] The CS data support sealed-tube Tl sulfide synthesis, while SS/TS data strongly support Cu-containing sulfide/chalcogenide formation in evacuated ampoules at low to moderate temperature. [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-1, TS-3] The most critical enabling factor is the use of binary sulfide precursors in a sealed ampoule, which minimizes volatile-element loss and reduces diffusion distance. [SS-2, TS-1; Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control in Tl–Cu–chalcogenide chemistry, since TS analogues show multiple ternary phases, homogeneity ranges, and phase mixtures. [TS-3, TS-5] Overall, the material is synthetically plausible but will likely require careful low-temperature annealing and repeated phase analysis. [SS-2, TS-1, TS-3]
