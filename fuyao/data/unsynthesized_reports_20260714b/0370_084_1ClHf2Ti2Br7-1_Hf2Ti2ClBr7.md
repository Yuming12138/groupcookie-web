### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0090 eV/atom, which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom range generally associated with isolable ground-state or near-ground-state materials [Target]. Thermodynamically similar halides have low hull energies and documented syntheses, including Gd₂Br₃ at 0.00798 eV/atom [TS1], LaBr₂/LaBr₃-related bromide at 0.00293 eV/atom [TS2], RbTiBr₃ at 0.00430 eV/atom [TS3], and Ta₃SBr₇ at 0.00489 eV/atom [TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or ordering of Hf/Ti and Br/Cl [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Reductive sealed-tube solid-state synthesis

The condition-severity score is limited mainly by sealed inert/vacuum operation, volatile halide precursors, and air-sensitive/corrosive reagents, all of which appear in ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ and Ti bromide analogue syntheses [SS2, SS3, TS3, TS5]. The kinetic score is moderate-low because Hf and Ti metals are refractory and require long anneals for homogenization, similar to the 4–10 week or 20–30 day times reported for related halides [SS2, SS5, TS1]. The main advantage is that the product topology is close to known layered MX₂ halides [SS1–SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether a homogeneous Hf/Ti mixed layer forms rather than segregating into Hf-halide and Ti-halide phases [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Chemical vapor transport / vapor-assisted equilibration

Route 2 receives the best kinetic score because vapor transport can increase mass transport and help equilibrate layered halides, as demonstrated for ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ [SS2, SS3]. Its severity remains high because sealed Ta tubes, drybox loading, volatile halide sources, and temperature gradients are required [SS2, SS3, TS3]. The route is structurally well supported by molybdenite-like ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ and TiBr₂ analogues [SS1–SS3]. The key uncertainty is mixed-anion Br/Cl ordering in triclinic P1, for which no direct analogue is supplied [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Direct elemental halogenation

Route 3 is plausible because TiBr₂ is directly obtained from Ti and Br₂ vapor, and Ta₃SBr₇ is obtained from elements including Br₂ in evacuated tubes [SS1, TS4]. Its severity is comparable to other routes because Br₂/Cl₂ are volatile, toxic, and require sealed or controlled-flow apparatus [SS1, TS4]. Kinetically, elemental halogenation gives high anion activity but less control over cation distribution and mixed Br/Cl stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference from SS1, TS4]. The largest uncertainty is overhalogenation to volatile tetrahalides or separation into binary Hf/Ti halides [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization/strain trigger: significant Ti–Hf size mismatch in octahedral halide coordination [Reasonable Inference]. The formula unit is small and the target description does not indicate a complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, so C2 and C3 are not triggered [Target]. Similar layered halides are crystalline when synthesized under long anneals or transport conditions [SS1–SS3, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is that P1 mixed-cation/mixed-anion ordering could broaden diffraction or form stacking disorder rather than a clean crystalline phase [Reasonable Inference from polytype/intergrowth issues in SS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂Ti₂Br₇Cl is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0090 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar molybdenite-like TiBr₂, ZrCl₂, and ZrI₂ layered halides, all of which are experimentally synthesized under vapor/ampoule conditions [SS1–SS3]. Thermodynamic analogues also support isolability of low-hull bromide/chloride-rich reduced halides [TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is volatile halide transport, which can overcome slow Hf/Ti diffusion and help form layered sheets [SS2, SS3]. The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding binary TiBr₂/HfClₓ/HfBrₓ segregation and maintaining the 7:1 Br:Cl ratio [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS5, TS1, TS4].
