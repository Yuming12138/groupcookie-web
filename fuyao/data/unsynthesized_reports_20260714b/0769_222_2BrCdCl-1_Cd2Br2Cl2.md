### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull = 0.022 eV/atom places it in the high-synthesizability stability bin, giving F1 = 94. **TS-1** is especially relevant because it has E_hull = 0.0217 eV/atom, nearly identical to the target, and was experimentally synthesized by direct fusion in an evacuated ampoule. **CS-1**, **CS-2**, and **SS-1–5** also show that near-hull halide or Cd-containing analogues are experimentally accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is metastable rather than exactly on the hull, so kinetic trapping or careful cooling may be needed.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule melt / solid-state route

The condition severity is moderate because the route uses vacuum sealing and toxic Cd halides, which correspond to deductions; vacuum ampoule approaches are directly supported by **SS-5**, **TS-1**, and **TS-4**. The route avoids >1000 °C temperatures, high pressure, and complex apparatus, consistent with moderate-temperature halide syntheses in **SS-5** and **TS-4**. Kinetically, molten CdCl2/CdBr2 should be highly reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, but converting binary halide salts into ordered CdBrCl sheets requires structural rearrangement and halide ordering. The main uncertainty is whether the product crystallizes as the predicted Pmc2_1 mixed-halide phase or as CdCl2/CdBr2 solid solutions.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT / self-transport route

CVT has greater procedural severity because it requires an evacuated sealed ampoule, a controlled thermal gradient, volatile halide transport, and toxic Cd salts; these features are seen in **CS-1**, **SS-1–3**, and **TS-2**. Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion, as demonstrated by CVT-grown halide analogues in **CS-1** and **SS-1–3**. The structural pathway is plausible because several structurally similar low-dimensional halides crystallize from vapor in evacuated ampoules **SS-1–3**. The uncertainty is compositional fractionation: CdCl2 and CdBr2 may have different vapor pressures, so Br:Cl stoichiometry could drift **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: solution crystallization

Solution crystallization is mild in temperature and pressure, and cadmium halide solution growth is supported by **CS-4**, while room-temperature halide crystallization is supported by **SS-4**. However, because the target is anhydrous CdBrCl and **CS-4** produced Cd(NBr)2 from ammoniacal cadmium halide solution, the structural-reconstruction/phase-selectivity score is low. Solution-phase Cd2+ can form hydrates, solvates, and complexes **[Reasonable Inference]**, making this route less reliable despite favorable kinetics. Insufficient evidence from **TS** exists for solution growth of directly comparable CdBrCl-like phases, so TS is neutral, score 50, for this route-specific factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: halide flux route

Flux growth is supported by **CS-2**, where a chloride-containing oxyhalide was grown from TlCl flux, and by **TS-4**, where bromide salts were reacted in sealed ampoules with slow cooling. The route has moderate-to-high condition penalties because it requires sealed/dry handling, slow cooling, flux separation, and toxic Cd salts. Kinetically, a CdCl2/CdBr2-rich melt should provide excellent ion mobility **[Reasonable Inference]**, giving a high F3. The uncertainty is that flux components may stabilize competing halide complexes or inclusions, a general phase-control risk supported by impurity issues in **CS-2** and **TS-4**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Cd2+, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structure is described as a simple corner-sharing CdBr2Cl2 tetrahedral sheet, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not triggered by the target description. Insufficient evidence from **CS/SS/TS** is available for the exact unit-cell atom count, but the simple formula and described sheet motif suggest a low amorphization risk. Therefore F4 = 95.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CdBrCl is likely a mildly metastable but readily accessible phase, because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom and several thermodynamically similar phases near this energy are experimentally synthesized **TS-1–5**. The CS and SS analogues strongly support sealed ampoule and CVT halide chemistry, especially **CS-1**, **SS-1–3**, and **SS-5**. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of cadmium halides, which can enable melt, flux, or vapor equilibration **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with halide transport examples in **CS-1** and **SS-1–3**. The most critical limiting factor is Br/Cl stoichiometry control, because volatile halide systems and flux-grown halides often show phase-purity sensitivity in **CS-2**, **SS-1–3**, and **TS-4**. The recommended first attempt is Route 1 because it uses the simplest chemically matched precursors while maintaining anhydrous, sealed conditions.
