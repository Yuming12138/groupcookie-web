### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the top of the 90–100 scoring bin. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable or higher E_hull values are experimentally synthesized: Sr2UZnO6 at 0.054 eV/atom (TS-1), RbPrNb2O7 at 0.0559 eV/atom (TS-2), CaTiO3 at 0.0645 eV/atom (TS-3), and SrSm2Nb2O9 at 0.0663 eV/atom (TS-5). CS and SS analogues also include stable or near-stable perovskite-derived oxides, such as Sr2CrOsO6 at E_hull = 0 (CS-1), Sr2MgWO6 at E_hull = 0 (CS-5), Sr3Ta2ZnO9 at 0.0098 eV/atom (SS-1), and Sr2GdRuO6 at E_hull = 0 (SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom may indicate a metastable polymorph rather than the true ground-state form.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Conventional air ceramic route

The high-temperature air ceramic route is strongly supported by SS-1 for Sr–Ta perovskite-derived oxides and by CS-5 for Sr double perovskites, but those examples require temperatures near or above 1200–1450 °C. The severity score is reduced because SS-1 reaches 1723 K and CS-5 reaches 1473 K, while repeated grinding/pelletizing is common in CS-5, SS-1, and SS-5. Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta2O5 and Cr2O3 [Reasonable Inference], and the need for repeated heat treatments in CS-2 to CS-5 and SS-1 indicates slow diffusion and ordering. Uncertainty remains around whether air will stabilize Cr3+ cleanly or generate chromate-like secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Controlled-atmosphere ceramic route

Controlled atmospheres are well supported for chemically similar double perovskites where B-site oxidation state is important: Sr2CrOsO6 uses sealed oxygen control (CS-1), while Sr2LnMoO6 analogues use H2/N2 to stabilize Mo5+ (CS-2 to CS-4). This route receives a condition penalty for controlled atmosphere and possible toxic/air-sensitive reagents, but it may allow lower maximum temperature than the most aggressive air ceramic route [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically it remains an oxide/carbonate solid-state process, so refractory Ta diffusion is still the main limitation, although atmosphere control may reduce competing redox products. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports Sr–Cr–Ta redox control, so TS is treated as neutral except for general atmosphere tunability in TS-1.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Sol-gel assisted route

No CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates sol-gel synthesis of this target family; therefore this route is explicitly a [Reasonable Inference]. The route is motivated by the repeated need for high-temperature, repeated grinding in CS-2 to CS-5, SS-1, and SS-5, which suggests that improved precursor-scale mixing could improve kinetics. Its F2 score is better because crystallization may occur below the highest ceramic temperatures, but the process is still multi-step and may involve moisture-sensitive Ta alkoxide and toxic Cr salts [Reasonable Inference]. Its F3 score is highest because nitrate/acetate/alkoxide/citrate precursors are highly active by the rubric, although final B-site ordering will likely still need a high-temperature anneal.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size contrast between Sr in 12-fold cuboctahedral coordination and Cr/Ta in octahedral coordination, satisfying C1 by physicochemical reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description does not indicate a unit cell above 40 atoms, and CS/SS/TS do not provide evidence that this specific P-1 cell is large; this check is therefore not triggered. The target shows corner-sharing octahedra and face-sharing SrO12–BO6 contacts, but edge-sharing is not stated, so the strict “vertex + edge + face” criterion is not fully met. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2CrTaO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the feasibility of making Sr-based double perovskite or perovskite-derived oxides by ceramic methods, especially Sr2CrOsO6 (CS-1), Sr2MgWO6 (CS-5), Sr3Ta2ZnO9 (SS-1), and Sr2GdRuO6 (SS-5). The TS set supports that oxides with similar metastability, including E_hull ≈0.054–0.066 eV/atom, can still be synthesized by standard solid-state methods (TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is charge-compatible Cr3+/Ta5+ B-site chemistry in a known Sr double-perovskite framework [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is slow cation diffusion and B-site ordering involving refractory Ta oxide, plus possible Cr oxidation-state/secondary-phase sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].
