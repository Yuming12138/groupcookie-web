### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0130 eV/atom [Target], placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS analogues include on-hull or near-hull layered halide/chalcogenide-halides such as CrSBr and NbSe₂Cl₂ [CS1, CS5], while SS includes on-hull molybdenite-like ZrCl₂ and nearly on-hull ZrI₂ [SS3, SS5]. TS analogues around 0.014–0.023 eV/atom are reported as synthetically accessible reduced halides [TS1, TS3, TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that TiBrCl is a mixed-halide ordered phase, so a low hull does not guarantee ordering against TiCl₂/TiBr₂ intergrowths. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids external high pressure and extreme >1000 °C temperatures, consistent with ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ transport at 600–850 °C [SS3, SS5]. It still requires vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile halides, drybox practice, and multi-step gradient control, as seen in CrSBr, Nb₃Br₈, ZrI₂, and ZrCl₂ syntheses [CS1, SS2, SS3, SS5]. The penalty is therefore dominated by atmosphere, sealed volatile reagents, and air-sensitive/toxic halides rather than thermal severity. Uncertainty is mainly whether quartz is compatible or Ta containment is mandatory. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are helped by vapor transport, which overcomes the sluggish diffusion expected from Ti metal [SS1/TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Close structural analogues, especially TiBr₂ edge-sharing octahedral sheets and molybdenite-like ZrCl₂/ZrI₂, support reconstructive accessibility [SS1/TS5, SS3, SS5]. However, the long durations reported for ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ transport indicate slow equilibration and ordering [SS3, SS5]. The mixed Cl/Br ordering adds a kinetic and thermodynamic selectivity issue absent from binary TiBr₂. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The vapor-phase route uses temperatures similar to TiBr₂ at 900 °C and thus avoids >1000 °C penalties [SS1/TS5]. It requires dry Ar flow, halogen vapors, purification, selective deposition, and rapid cooling, all of which are explicitly part of the TiBr₂ procedure [SS1/TS5]. Toxic and corrosive halogen precursors create significant operational severity. The uncertainty is whether continuous-flow conditions can maintain exact 1:1 Cl/Br stoichiometry in the deposited solid. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor availability is favorable because Br₂ vapor directly reacts with Ti to form TiBr₂ [SS1/TS5], and volatile titanium halides are common in Ti halide chemistry [TS1]. Crystallization by deposition is plausible because TiBr₂ is obtained on a cooler zone/finger cooler [SS1/TS5]. The limiting kinetic/compositional issue is mixed-halide control: different Ti–Cl and Ti–Br volatilities may cause gradients, phase separation, or binary TiCl₂/TiBr₂ domains. [Reasonable Inference] Evidence from CS is supportive only generally for vapor transport in layered halides [CS1, CS5], not specifically Ti mixed halides.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The sealed Ta flux route avoids high pressure and likely stays within 600–800 °C, consistent with KTiBr₃ and ZrCl₂ analogues [TS1, SS5]. It requires drybox handling, Ta welding, evacuated silica jackets, volatile halides, and slow cooling, which are all specialized but standard for reduced halide chemistry [TS1, SS5, TS3]. Toxicity and moisture sensitivity remain significant. The source evidence is strong from TS/SS, while CS offers only general support for sealed halide reactions and CVT [CS1, CS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux can improve kinetics relative to direct solid-state diffusion, and KTiBr₃ synthesis demonstrates Ti halide reactions in sealed Ta containers with slow cooling [TS1]. However, Ti metal remains a relatively low-activity precursor, and ZrCl₂/ScCl analogues show long equilibration periods and difficult phase control [SS5, TS3]. Structural similarity is moderate because the desired motif is a reduced layered halide sheet [Target, SS1/SS5], but alkali flux introduces risks of competing alkali titanate halides [TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are less favorable than CVT because mass transport may be less clean. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

High-pressure nitride-halide analogues require 3–5 GPa, 900–1100 °C, controlled heating/cooling, and noble-metal capsules [CS2, CS3]. Even though the scoring table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, the equipment and encapsulation burden are high [CS2, CS3]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports high pressure for TiBrCl, so SS and TS are neutral 50 for route relevance. This route is included only as exploratory.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

NH₄X fluxes can rapidly form or transform layered nitride halides under high pressure [CS2, CS3], which suggests potentially good local mobility. However, the target lacks nitrogen and hydrogen [Target], so NH₄X chemistry may introduce contamination rather than help [Reasonable Inference]. High-pressure analogues are chemically less transferable than TiBr₂/ZrCl₂-type reduced halides [SS1/TS5, SS5]. Therefore, kinetics are not the main obstacle; chemical relevance is.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a single cation species, Ti, so cation-size disorder is absent [Target]. The described framework consists of edge-sharing TiBr₃Cl₃ octahedra rather than mixed edge/corner/face-sharing polyhedral networks [Target], and close analogues TiBr₂ and ZrCl₂ are crystalline layered compounds [SS1/TS5, SS5]. CS gives weaker mixed evidence because some chemically similar compounds have more complex polyhedral connectivity [CS1, CS4, CS5], while TS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is treated neutral 50 for this factor. The main residual amorphization risk is not topology but halide disorder or off-stoichiometry. [Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiBrCl is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.013 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS TiBr₂, which has closely related 2D edge-sharing TiBr₆ sheets and is experimentally prepared by vapor-phase reaction [SS1/TS5], plus molybdenite-like ZrCl₂/ZrI₂ reduced halides made by sealed-tube transport [SS3, SS5]. CS supports the broader feasibility of layered halide/chalcogenide-halide CVT chemistry, but some CS analogues involve different anions or high-pressure nitride-halide chemistry, so their transferability is mixed [CS1–CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is halide vapor transport, which can overcome slow diffusion and promote layered crystal growth [CS1, SS3, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is precise Cl/Br chemical-potential and ordering control to avoid TiCl₂, TiBr₂, TiCl₃/TiBr₃, or volatile TiX₄ by-products. [Reasonable Inference]
