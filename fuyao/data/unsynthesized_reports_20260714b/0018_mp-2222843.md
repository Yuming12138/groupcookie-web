### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target sits at **0.0500 eV/atom**, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score bin but close enough to the boundary to justify **90/100** rather than a near-perfect score [Target data]. TS analogues with E_hull near this range were synthesized, including TS1 at 0.0556 eV/atom, TS3 at 0.0405 eV/atom, TS4 at 0.0801 eV/atom, and TS5 at 0.0561 eV/atom [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5]. CS and SS give additional support because many chemically or structurally related IO6/oxide phases are experimentally accessible at or near the hull [CS1, CS3, CS5, SS1–SS5].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is mild in temperature and pressure because SS2/TS3 precipitation analogues use aqueous conditions from room temperature to ~90 °C, with optional low-temperature annealing by inference [SS2, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. The main deductions are multi-step processing and toxic Ni salts [CS2, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact Mg/Ni/K periodate precipitation is treated as neutral 50 for that route-specific transferability.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Aqueous nitrates/acetates give strong precursor activity, supported by SS2, TS3, and CS2 solution routes [SS2, TS3, CS2]. The kinetic weakness is structural ordering because the target requires Mg/Ni/I octahedral ordering, while the closest precipitation analogues are simpler Sn/Pb periodates [SS2, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. This gives good reaction kinetics for nucleation but only moderate confidence in the final ordered P312 phase.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids extreme temperature because SS3/SS4 IO6 ceramics form at 650 °C in air, and SS5 forms related oxides at 600 °C [SS3, SS4, SS5]. Deductions are for repeated grinding/heating and toxic Ni precursors, while sealed/high-pressure equipment is not required [SS3, SS4, Reasonable Inference]. The route is experimentally conservative but not the most kinetically favorable.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state kinetics are slower because MgO/NiO-type precursors require cation diffusion and reconstruction into an ordered edge-sharing framework [TS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. SS3/SS4 show that repeated heating cycles can form IO6 phases, but the target adds Mg/Ni cation ordering not directly shown in the structural analogues [SS3, SS4, Reasonable Inference]. **F2 — Route 3**

Deductions are for multi-step flux processing and toxic Ni precursors [CS5, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for a specific KNO3/KIO4/Mg/Ni flux recipe is treated as neutral 50, but SS still supports the IO6 temperature window [SS3, SS4].

**F3 — Route 3**  
The molten flux route scores highest kinetically because TS1 demonstrates low-temperature molten nitrate access to otherwise difficult ordered oxides, while CS5 demonstrates flux crystallization with slow cooling [TS1, CS5]. The flux should reduce diffusion limitations and improve cation ordering compared with dry solid-state firing [TS1, CS5, Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports KMgNiIO6 or a Mg/Ni periodate flux product.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Ion exchange is mild in temperature and pressure because TS1 uses molten LiNO3 at 300 °C without high pressure [TS1]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KMgNiIO6I is best interpreted as a **near-hull, likely metastable-accessible phase** rather than an unequivocal deep ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and TS analogues around 0.04–0.08 eV/atom were experimentally synthesized [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5]. The SS data strongly supports IO6 periodate framework formation, especially RbSnIO6 with matching I–O ≈1.92 Å and similar alkali coordination [SS2]. The CS data supports Mg-containing oxide synthesis and flux/solid-state processing but is less direct for iodine(VII), so CS is supportive but not decisive [CS3, CS4, CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is **low-temperature oxidizing flux chemistry**, which can preserve I(VII) while improving Mg/Ni ordering [TS1, CS5, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is **simultaneous K retention and Mg/Ni/I cation ordering** in the ilmenite-derived P312 framework, which no exact analogue demonstrates [SS2, TS1, Reasonable Inference].
