### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic score bin. This is reinforced by stable fluoride analogues with E_hull = 0.0, including CsOIF4 [CS-1], CsSbClF3 [CS-2], CsRuF6 [SS-1, TS-1], and Cs2IrF6 [SS-4, TS-3]. The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly validate the Os–N–S chemical motif; they mainly validate Cs–metal–fluoride stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from mild solution crystallization, supported by acetonitrile recrystallization of CsOIF4 [CS-1] and room-temperature slow evaporation of Cs2IrF6 [SS-4, TS-3]. The severity penalty comes from the likely need for dry inert handling, volatile/toxic Os–N–S fluoride precursors, and prior precursor synthesis **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for the stability or commercial availability of an OsNSF5-type precursor.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because solution-state ion transport and slow crystallization avoid refractory solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated by CS-1, CS-2, SS-4, and TS-3. If the Os–N–S fluoride unit is preassembled, the main kinetic requirement is Cs+ salt crystallization and sheet assembly **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether the target sheet forms rather than a molecular Cs salt or simple hexafluoroosmate phase; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct fluorination is supported by CsRuF6 synthesis at 350 °C in F2 [SS-1, TS-1] and by high-pressure F2 syntheses of complex fluorides [TS-4, TS-5]. The route is hazardous because F2 is reactive and toxic, and Os compounds may form volatile toxic fluorides/oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**. Although temperatures are not extreme, the atmosphere and safety burden make the severity high.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Gas-phase fluorination can activate metal chlorides, as shown by CsRuF6 formation from RuCl3 and alkali chloride [SS-1, TS-1]. However, incorporating and preserving a linear Os–N–S ligand during oxidative fluorination likely requires major structural reconstruction and careful redox control **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that direct F2 fluorination can retain N–S bonding, so the kinetics score is low.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate solvothermal temperatures comparable to CsVOF3/RbVOF3 synthesis at 100–160 °C [SS-2, SS-3, TS-2] and BaClBF4 at 200 °C [CS-4]. The main penalties are sealed HF/fluoride media, toxic Os compounds, and multi-step handling. This is less severe than F2 fluorination because it avoids high-pressure fluorine gas, but HF remains a major hazard.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

HF/fluoride-rich media support dissolution, transport, and crystallization of Cs transition-metal fluorides/oxyfluorides, as seen for CsMn(OF2)2 [CS-3] and CsVOF3/RbVOF3 [SS-2, SS-3, TS-2]. Low-temperature solution transport greatly reduces cation diffusion barriers relative to solid-state routes. The uncertainty is chemical selectivity: water or oxidizing fluoride media may produce oxyfluorides or hexafluoroosmates rather than the N–S-containing target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for N–S retention.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Cs+ with much smaller octahedral Os, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch criterion; similar size mismatch is tolerated in CsRuF6 and Cs2IrF6 analogues [SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-3]. The target formula is small, and the provided description does not prove a >40 atom cell or coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing. Therefore the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high, with uncertainty because the 2D sheet topology may require ordered F/Cs packing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsOsNSF5 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many Cs–transition-metal fluoride analogues in CS/SS/TS are also thermodynamically stable [CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1, SS-4, TS-3]. The analogues strongly support formation of Cs–F–metal octahedral frameworks and solution or fluorination access to alkali metal fluorides, but they do not directly support the Os–N–S ligand chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride activity combined with low-temperature crystallization, as shown by HF/solvothermal and slow-evaporation analogues [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2, SS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the linear Os–N–S unit without hydrolysis, oxidation, or conversion to simple OsF6/hexafluoroosmate species; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible but requires specialized Os/N–S fluoride precursor control.
