### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0150 eV/atom, which is well within the normally accessible metastability range and maps to a 90–100 score by the requested rubric [target data]. Experimentally synthesized analogues at similar hull energies include CrSe₂ at 0.0154 eV/atom (TS1), VTe₂ at 0.0185 eV/atom (CS3; TS2), and MoTe₂ at 0.0139 eV/atom (CS5). CS and TS therefore provide strong positive evidence; SS provides indirect structural support but insufficient direct hull-based evidence for binary CrTe₂, so SS is treated as neutral-to-positive rather than decisive.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

F2 is moderate because VTe₂ forms at 1000 K for 1 week in evacuated quartz ampoules (CS3/CS4; TS2), and MnTe₂ forms at 550 °C in evacuated tubes (CS2), so no extreme temperature or high pressure is needed. The deductions arise from vacuum/sealed-tube handling and Te volatility/toxicity [CS2/CS3/CS4/TS2; Reasonable Inference]. F3 is limited by Cr metal’s slow diffusion and by phase-control issues, since VTe₂ analogues mention Te impurity and unsuccessful crystal growth attempts (CS3/CS4; TS2), though structural similarity to layered MTe₂ is favorable.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Bridgman / directional melt growth

F2 is somewhat harsher because Bridgman growth may require approaching or exceeding ~1000 °C depending on the Cr–Te liquidus [Reasonable Inference], although Cr₂Te₃ single crystals are directly reported by Bridgman (CS1; SS3). F3 is favorable because melt growth bypasses solid-state diffusion limitations and is proven for a binary Cr–Te phase (CS1; SS3). The main uncertainty is phase selection: Cr₂Te₃ is stable and known (CS1; SS3), while the target CrTe₂ is slightly metastable [target data], so Te-rich conditions are essential.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Halide/salt flux growth

F2 is penalized by vacuum sealed-tube operation, flux handling, and multi-step processing, exactly as the CrSe₂ flux synthesis used binary precursors, LiCl/KCl, sealed carbon-coated fused-silica tubes, 780 °C, 165 h, and slow cooling (TS1). F3 is good because the flux provides liquid-phase transport and because CrSe₂ is a close thermodynamic/structural analogue of the target CrTe₂ (TS1). Remaining uncertainty comes from substituting Te for Se and possible incorporation of Bi or residual flux components [Reasonable Inference from TS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Chemical vapor transport

F2 is moderate because NbPdTe₅ CVT uses 700/750 °C, I₂, an evacuated silica tube, and 8 days (TS4), while MoTe₂ uses bromine vapor transport (CS5); these are controlled but not extreme conditions. F3 is the highest because vapor transport reduces diffusion limitations and is established for layered tellurides (CS5; TS4). The uncertainty is that no source directly demonstrates CVT of CrTe₂, and chromium halide volatility could either enable transport or destabilize stoichiometry [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Cr, so cation-size disorder does not apply [target structure]. The structure is a simple layered CrTe₂ network of edge-sharing CrTe₆ octahedra with two Te sites, not a large multi-cation cell with mixed edge/corner/face connectivity [target structure]. CS/SS/TS do not report amorphization issues for similar crystalline tellurides; however, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on amorphous byproducts is treated as neutral, and the target structural simplicity drives the high F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CrTe₂ with E_hull = 0.015 eV/atom is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because it is slightly above hull while Cr₂Te₃ is a stable Cr–Te compound in the references (target data; CS1; SS3). The CS and TS analogues support synthesis strongly: VTe₂ has the same layered MTe₂ motif and is made by direct sealed-ampoule reaction (CS3/CS4; TS2), while CrSe₂ is a close CrX₂ analogue made by flux growth at similar metastability (TS1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high Te chemical potential in a sealed or transport environment, supported by Te-containing evacuated-tube syntheses and Te impurity observations in VTe₂ (CS2/CS3/CS4; TS2). The most critical limiting factor is competition with other Cr–Te phases, especially Cr₂Te₃, which is experimentally established and stable (CS1; SS3). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with CVT or Te-rich flux/direct sealed-tube routes most likely to access crystalline CrTe₂.
