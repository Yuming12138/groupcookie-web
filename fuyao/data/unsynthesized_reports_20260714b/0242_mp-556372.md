### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high end of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” 90–100 range given in the prompt. Exact CaSO₄ analogues have E_hull = 0.0135 eV/atom in CS-1 and 0.0563 eV/atom in CS-2/TS-1, so the target’s energy is consistent with a low-energy metastable polymorph. SS-1 to SS-3 show several sulfate frameworks on or near the hull, supporting the general stability of sulfate oxides, though insufficient evidence from SS alone identifies the exact P6₄22 CaSO₄ phase. Remaining uncertainty is whether the DFT low-energy phase is experimentally quenchable.

#### F2 — Route 1 high pressure

Route 1 is heavily penalized because CS-1/CS-2/SS-5/TS-1 require up to 21.4 GPa and 1450 K laser heating. These conditions are feasible in a DAC or multi-anvil facility but are not routine bulk synthesis, as explicitly indicated by the specialized equipment in CS-1/CS-2/SS-5/TS-1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any special gas atmosphere or air-sensitive precursor, so those factors are neutral. The major uncertainty is whether the P6₄22 zircon-like polymorph is the phase stabilized in the same P–T region as the reported CaSO₄ high-pressure phases.

#### F3 — Route 1 high pressure

The kinetic advantage is that CaSO₄ is already the correct stoichiometry, as in CS-1/CS-2/TS-1, so no cation interdiffusion between separate precursors is needed. However, CS-1/CS-2/TS-1 describe pressure-induced polymorphic transitions, implying reconstructive rearrangement and sensitive phase control. Laser heating should overcome barriers, but quenching and recovering the exact target phase remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal

Route 2 scores highly because CS-4/CS-5 synthesize zircon-like CaSeO₄ at only 70–220 °C, and TS-2/TS-3 show sulfate-related hydrothermal crystal growth at 155–230 °C. No high pressure beyond autogenous water pressure, no inert atmosphere, and no >1000 °C step are required in those sources. The route uses corrosive acid by [Reasonable Inference], but the scoring rubric penalizes toxic/air-sensitive precursors, not routine acid corrosion. The main uncertainty is not condition severity but polymorph selectivity.

#### F3 — Route 2 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal solution transport gives high precursor activity and bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, supported by crystal growth in CS-4/CS-5 and TS-2/TS-3. The structural match to CaSeO₄ zircon-like phases in CS-4/CS-5 strongly supports forming a CaO₈–XO₄ framework. The uncertainty is that sulfate SO₄ is smaller than selenate SeO₄, and exact CaSO₄ high-pressure data in CS-1/CS-2/TS-1 suggests the target polymorph may not be favored at low pressure.

#### F2 — Route 3 solid-state

Solid-state sintering conditions are moderate: SS-1/SS-2 use 600 °C for 100 h, and TS-4 uses 823 K for 48 h, with no high pressure or special atmosphere. The deduction is for multi-step processing, because SS-1/SS-2 explicitly require dehydration, grinding, annealing, and repeated grinding. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS indicates air-sensitive precursors, so that factor is neutral. The method is operationally simple but slow.

#### F3 — Route 3 solid-state

SS-1/SS-2 demonstrate that sulfate frameworks can form by solid-state reaction, but the 100 h anneal and repeated grinding indicate kinetic limitations. Exact CaSO₄ polymorph evidence in CS-1/CS-2/TS-1 suggests that ambient pressure annealing may relax to common anhydrite rather than the target P6₄22 phase. Thus the kinetics of making CaSO₄ composition are acceptable, while the kinetics and thermodynamics of trapping the target polymorph are unfavorable.

#### F2 — Route 4 flux

Flux growth avoids extreme pressure and temperature, with TS-5 operating at 623–773 K and SS-3 using 80–95 °C evaporation from acidic sulfate media. The route is penalized for multiple steps because TS-5 includes heating, temperature cycling/holding, and washing. No special atmosphere is reported in SS-3/TS-5, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS requires no air-sensitive handling. Possible acidic flux volatility is a [Reasonable Inference] but not directly established for this target.

#### F3 — Route 4 flux

Flux growth gives high ion mobility and can yield single crystals, as shown by TS-5, while acidic sulfate media crystallize related sulfates in SS-3. This should help overcome diffusion barriers compared with Route 3. However, no CS/SS/TS source directly reports flux-grown zircon-like CaSO₄, so structural selection is uncertain. The likely challenge is avoiding crystallization of stable anhydrite or hydrated CaSO₄.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency
