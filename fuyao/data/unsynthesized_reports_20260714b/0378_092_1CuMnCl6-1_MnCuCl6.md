### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score. CS analogues include multiple hull-stable or near-hull chloride frameworks: VAl₂Cl₈ is at 0 eV/atom (**CS-1**), Mg(AlCl₄)₂ is at 0 eV/atom (**CS-3**), CdAl₂Cl₈ is at 0 eV/atom (**CS-4**), and Cu(AlCl₄)₂ is only ~0.010 eV/atom above hull (**CS-2**). TS analogues also include hull-stable or near-hull chlorides such as MoCl₅, ReTeSeCl₄, and Zr(SeCl₆)₂ (**TS-1**, **TS-2**, **TS-5**). Remaining uncertainty is that computational hull stability does not guarantee a broad temperature stability window; Cu(AlCl₄)₂ is reported to decompose slightly above synthesis temperature (**CS-2**).

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, because close CS and SS analogues crystallize between ~140 and 250 °C (**CS-1**, **CS-2**, **CS-5**, **SS-2**). The severity penalty comes from vacuum/sealed-tube requirements and volatile/moisture-sensitive chlorides, as explicitly used in VAl₂Cl₈, Cu(AlCl₄)₂, and Ti(AlBr₄)₂ syntheses (**CS-1**, **CS-2**, **CS-5**). The remaining uncertainty is the Mn/Cu oxidation-state control, which is not directly addressed by CS/SS/TS and is therefore a **[Reasonable Inference]** risk.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics should be favorable because vapor-assisted transport bypasses slow bulk diffusion; analogous chlorides form at low temperature over days (**CS-2**, **CS-5**, **SS-2**). The target contains octahedral CuCl₆/MnCl₆ motifs, and CS includes CuCl₆ and transition-metal Cl₆ octahedra in low-dimensional frameworks (**CS-1**, **CS-2**). The uncertainty is that the target has a pure Mn–Cu edge-sharing sheet, whereas CS analogues often include AlCl₄ tetrahedra, so structural reconstruction is only partially supported (**CS-1**, **CS-2**).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 also avoids high temperatures and high pressure, as chloride melt/fractional crystallization analogues operate at 190–250 °C (**CS-3**, **CS-4**). Deductions are assigned for controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoules, volatile AlCl₃, and multi-step growth/zone refinement, all explicitly present in **CS-3** and **CS-4**. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically supports AlCl₃-flux melt growth, so SS/TS are neutral for that sub-factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has the best kinetic score because a chloride melt/flux offers high precursor activity and fast mass transport; CS-3 explicitly uses melting, quenching, and zone refinement, while CS-4 uses slow cooling from a 250 °C chloride melt. Low synthesis temperature is not a kinetic problem because the flux provides a liquid pathway, as shown by Mg(AlCl₄)₂ and CdAl₂Cl₈ formation (**CS-3**, **CS-4**). The main uncertainty is phase competition with Al-containing tetrachloroaluminate products, since the closest flux precedents all contain AlCl₄ units (**CS-2**, **CS-3**, **CS-4**).

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses modest temperatures comparable to SS/TS solvothermal chloride reactions, such as 150 °C in SOCl₂ and 473–573 K for MoCl₅ polymorphs (**SS-3**, **SS-1**, **TS-1**). It is penalized because volatile chlorinating solvents/media require sealed evacuated ampoules, glovebox handling, and sometimes safety autoclaves/counterpressure (**SS-1**, **SS-3**, **TS-1**). The evidence is less chemically matched to Mn–Cu chlorides, so severity may be underestimated if side reactions demand additional purification **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Reactive chlorinating media should provide good kinetic activation; SS-3 reports nearly quantitative conversion in SOCl₂ at 150 °C, and SS-1/TS-1 demonstrate chloride crystallization under solvothermal ampoule conditions. However, structural transfer to MnCuCl₆ is weaker because the SS/TS examples are mostly molecular or cluster chlorides rather than extended Mn–Cu sheets (**SS-1**, **SS-3**, **TS-1**, **TS-5**). Therefore the kinetics score is lower than Routes 1–2 despite favorable low-temperature reaction media.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization risk: Mn and Cu octahedral sites have different preferred bonding/ionic sizes, reflected by the target’s unequal Mn–Cl and Cu–Cl bond lengths, especially the elongated Cu–Cl contact at 2.76 Å **[Reasonable Inference from target structure]**. The unit formula is small and the target description reports only edge-sharing octahedra, so the large-cell and mixed-connectivity checks are not triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues demonstrate that low-dimensional chlorides can crystallize well, but polymorphism and moisture sensitivity in MoCl₅ and related chlorides indicate some remaining risk (**SS-1**, **TS-1**).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCuCl₆ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS data provide the most relevant support, especially CuCl₂/Al₂Cl₆ sealed-tube crystallization and chloride melt growth of low-dimensional metal chloride frameworks (**CS-2**, **CS-3**, **CS-4**). SS and TS data reinforce that low-dimensional chlorides frequently crystallize in sealed evacuated ampoules at low temperature, but they are less chemically matched because many are Mo/W/Zr/Re molecular or cluster chlorides rather than Mn–Cu sheets (**SS-1**, **SS-2**, **TS-1**, **TS-5**). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of anhydrous chloride media, which allow crystallization below 300 °C (**CS-2**, **CS-3**, **CS-4**). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Mn/Cu oxidation state and stoichiometry while avoiding competing AlCl₄-containing phases or simple binary chlorides **[Reasonable Inference]**.
