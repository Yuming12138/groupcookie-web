### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.014 eV/atom, which falls in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so F1 is 95 [Target]. TS gives strong support because thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.0136–0.0186 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by melt or flux routes [TS1, TS2]. SS also supports this because several structurally similar Tl chalcogenides are on or near the hull and synthesized [SS2–SS5]. CS shows that even higher-energy Tl–Te compositions are associated with reported synthesis/growth, so the target’s lower hull energy is favorable [CS1, CS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct Melt / Bridgman–Stockbarger

The severity score is reduced mainly because Tl/Te synthesis requires evacuated sealed tubes, controlled Ar handling, and toxic Tl handling [CS2]. The route does not require high pressure or >1000 °C conditions, consistent with analogous melt routes at 550–700 °C [SS2, TS2, TS3]. Kinetically, molten Tl–Te should have good diffusion because Tl and Te are low-melting compared with refractory oxides/metals [Reasonable Inference], and the exact Tl2Te composition has been grown by Bridgman–Stockbarger [CS2]. The main uncertainty is whether the grown Tl2Te corresponds to the target orthorhombic Pmmn molybdenite-like polymorph rather than another Tl2Te modification [Target; insufficient polymorph evidence from CS2].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Tl–Te Self-Flux

The severity is similar to Route 1 because it still uses sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile/toxic Tl, and multiple steps including flux preparation and separation [TS1, SS5]. Kinetics are favorable because flux growth creates a liquid medium and analogues crystallize by slow cooling over days at 723–773 K or around 550 °C [TS1, SS2]. Structural reconstruction is moderately favorable because Tl-rich telluride melts are chemically compatible, but phase competition from Tl5Te3 and other Tl–Te phases is a concern [CS1, SS2]. Uncertainty remains because no source explicitly reports Tl2Te self-flux growth into the Pmmn layered polymorph [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Thin-Film Evaporation

The condition severity is moderate-to-challenging because vacuum deposition and post-annealing are required, though no high pressure is involved [CS1, CS5]. Kinetically, thin-film deposition can access layered or metastable structures, but CS/SS/TS only support telluride evaporation generally, not this exact Tl2Te polymorph [CS1, CS5; insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS]. Composition control is the limiting factor because Tl and Te volatility can cause off-stoichiometry, and the Tl–Te system contains multiple nearby phases [CS1, CS2; Reasonable Inference]. This route may be useful if substrate stabilization is needed for the 2D Pmmn sheet structure [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: I2-CVT

CVT severity is reduced by sealed evacuated ampoule operation, volatile iodine, and toxic Tl handling [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetic prospects are moderate because I2-CVT yields high-quality layered Bi–Te crystals at 770–820 K [CS3], and layered tellurides often transport through volatile halide species [Reasonable Inference]. However, direct evidence for Tl2Te CVT is absent from CS/SS/TS, and TlI formation may shift the transport stoichiometry [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, CVT is plausible but less preferred than melt/flux routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The described structure is a simple Tl2Te sheet with edge-sharing TeTl6 units, not a complex mixed-connectivity framework [Target]. CS/SS analogues include more complex mixed polyhedra in Tl5Te3 and related phases, but that complexity is not present in the target description [CS1, SS1]. The remaining uncertainty is the exact conventional unit-cell size, but no evidence indicates >40 atoms [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl2Te with E_hull = 0.014 eV/atom is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase rather than a strongly metastable compound [Target]. The analogue support is strong chemically because the exact Tl–Te system and even Tl2Te composition have been grown by Bridgman–Stockbarger [CS2], while structurally and thermodynamically similar Tl chalcogenides are routinely made by sealed-tube melt, flux, or slow-cooling routes [SS2, SS5, TS1–TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature liquid/melt chemistry of Tl–Te, which should provide fast diffusion and good crystallization kinetics [CS2, SS2; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, because Tl is toxic/volatile and the Tl–Te binary contains competing phases such as Tl5Te3 and TlTe [CS1, CS2]. The best practical route is sealed-ampoule Bridgman/slow-cooling with careful stoichiometry compensation and post-growth annealing [CS2, SS2, TS1].
