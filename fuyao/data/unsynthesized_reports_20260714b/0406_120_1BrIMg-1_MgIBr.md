### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0190 eV/atom is within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom range. Thermodynamic analogues with nearly identical hull values are experimentally reported: Li₂MgBr₄ at 0.0146 eV/atom [SS4, TS2], Li₂ZnBr₄ at 0.0172 eV/atom [SS5, TS1], and Na₃GdBr₆ at 0.0156 eV/atom [TS5]. CS is supportive but less uniform: LaSeCl, LaCuS₂, and HoSI are stable [CS1, CS3, CS5], while Mg(NI)₂ has high database hull despite reported synthesis [CS2], so CS is treated as partially supportive rather than decisive.

Source signal scores for F1: CS = 70, SS = 90, TS = 95.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires sealed ampoules, vacuum/Ar handling, and anhydrous halide precursors, all supported by Li₂MgBr₄ and Li₂ZnBr₄ syntheses [SS4, SS5, TS1, TS2]. The temperature is moderate for solid-state synthesis and well below >1000 °C, unlike some chalcogenide examples reaching 900 °C or higher [CS1, CS5]. The main severity penalties are controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile halides, and air/moisture-sensitive anhydrous salts; CS gives insufficient direct evidence for MgBr₂/MgI₂ melt behavior specifically, so CS is neutral-to-supportive at 50–60.

Source signal scores for Route 1 F2: CS = 50, SS = 75, TS = 75.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halide precursors are kinetically favorable compared with refractory oxides, and the Li₂MgBr₄ analogue is made by melting stoichiometric anhydrous halides and slow cooling [SS4, TS2]. Li₂ZnBr₄/Na₂ZnBr₄ require multweek tempering below melting [SS5, TS1], showing that ordering kinetics may still be slow. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target MgX₆ octahedral motif is consistent with MgBr₆ analogues [SS4, TS2], but the exact 2D MgIBr omega-derived layer is not directly shown in CS/SS/TS.

Source signal scores for Route 1 F3: CS = 55, SS = 85, TS = 85.

#### F2 — Route 2

Direct Mg + halogen synthesis uses volatile I₂/Br₂/IBr in sealed ampoules, analogous to elemental iodine ampoule routes for HoSI [CS5] and AlI₃ [SS3]. Although the proposed temperature is moderate, halogen vapor pressure, corrosion, and toxicity make the route more hazardous than using preformed Mg halides [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for elemental Mg + halogen reactions, so TS is neutral at 50 for this route.

Source signal scores for Route 2 F2: CS = 65, SS = 60, TS = 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental halogen reactions can be highly reactive, as shown by direct metal + iodine syntheses [CS5, SS3]. However, forming an ordered MgIBr layer directly from elements requires greater structural reconstruction than Route 1, and Mg surface oxide or halogen overpressure may cause inhomogeneity [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS support feasibility of elemental halide formation, but neither directly demonstrates mixed MgBrI ordering.

Source signal scores for Route 2 F3: CS = 70, SS = 65, TS = 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

The CS2 autoclave route operates at only 350 °C, which is mild thermally, but it requires sealed steel autoclaves and an ammonium-halide/NH₃-rich environment [CS2]. The added deammoniation and annealing steps are inferred because CS2 products are Mg halide ammine/nitrogen-containing phases, not anhydrous MgIBr [CS2, Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for ammonium-halide synthesis of MgIBr, so they are neutral at 50 for Route 3 severity.

Source signal scores for Route 3 F2: CS = 70, SS = 50, TS = 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

CS2 provides strong kinetic evidence that Mg and ammonium halides react at 350 °C to form crystalline Mg halide materials. The weakness is structural: CS2 reports Mg(NH₃)₂X₂/Mg(NI)₂-type products rather than anhydrous MgIBr, so removal of NH₃/NH₄-derived species and conversion to the target layer may be difficult. SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for this low-temperature conversion, so they are neutral at 50.

Source signal scores for Route 3 F3: CS = 75, SS = 50, TS = 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Mg, so there is no cation radius mismatch trigger. The described P3m1 MgIBr sheet with one Mg, one I, and one Br site is small and not a >40 atom complex unit cell; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the exact target cell size and are neutral at 50 for C2. The target uses edge-sharing MgI₃Br₃ octahedra, and edge-sharing MgBr₆ octahedra are already observed in Li₂MgBr₄ [SS4, TS2], so the amorphization risk is low.

Source signal scores for F4: CS = 50, SS = 80, TS = 80.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgIBr is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0190 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized low-hull halides such as Li₂MgBr₄ [SS4, TS2] and Li₂ZnBr₄ [SS5, TS1]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS halide melt analogues using anhydrous binary halides in sealed ampoules, especially Li₂MgBr₄, which contains MgBr₆ octahedra [SS4, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-melting/reactive Mg halide precursors, allowing diffusion through a molten or soft halide medium rather than refractory solid-state diffusion [SS4, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is Br/I ordering and suppression of phase separation into MgBr₂/MgI₂ or other mixed-halide products, since mixed-halide formation problems are reported for related halogenozincates [SS5, TS1]. Overall, the target is synthetically plausible, with Route 1 being the most defensible first attempt.
