### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂LuSbO₆ is most likely a **low-energy metastable phase**, not a clearly proven ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS analogues strongly support Lu/Sb oxide chemistry and rare-earth antimonate formation, while SS-3 strongly supports K-containing peroxide-like oxo chemistry; together they support synthesis but do not prove the exact target structure [CS-1, CS-5, SS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is **low-temperature alkaline peroxide chemistry**, because it best matches the target’s K environment and O–O contacts [SS-3, CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is **simultaneous phase control of K, Lu, Sb redox/speciation, and O–O bond retention**, which no single reference demonstrates directly [CS-5, SS-3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with the highest-probability route being hydrothermal KOH/H₂O₂ synthesis followed by careful phase screening [CS-5, SS-3].

|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | Competing K–Sb, Lu–Sb, or simple oxide phases may form because no single source demonstrates exact K₂LuSbO₆ [CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3]. | High | Use iterative PXRD screening and adjust KOH/H₂O₂ or firing conditions, as CS ceramic routes use repeated grinding/checking and CS-5 uses optimized hydrothermal conditions [CS-1, CS-5]. |
| 2 | Route 2 / Route 3 | Peroxide/O–O bond retention is sensitive to temperature and redox conditions [SS-3; Reasonable Inference]. | High | Use low-temperature KOH/H₂O₂ conditions, avoid high-temperature calcination after crystallization, and dry gently [SS-3, CS-5]. |
| 3 | Route 1 / Route 4 | K volatility or alkali loss can shift stoichiometry during high-temperature treatment [SS-4, SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. | Medium–High | Use K excess, covered or sealed crucibles, and lower-temperature flux/hydrothermal routes where possible [SS-4, SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow Lu₂O₃ reaction kinetics can prevent complete conversion [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Pre-fire/grind Lu₂O₃ finely, use repeated regrinding, or switch to hydrothermal reactive Lu precursor [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5]. |
| 5 | Route 2 / Route 3 | Lu solubility in strong alkaline peroxide solution may be low [CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Use freshly precipitated Lu hydroxide or Lu nitrate-derived gels instead of dense Lu₂O₃ [CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. |

| 7 | Route 4 | Anhydrous alkali flux handling is moisture-sensitive and hardware-dependent [SS-4, SS-5]. | Medium | Use dry Ar loading, sealed Au/Ag/Pt-compatible containers, and slow cooling as in alkali oxide analogues [SS-4, SS-5, TS-1]. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Single-crystal growth may be slow or may precipitate simple salts instead of target crystals [TS-2; Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Use slow cooling or methanol diffusion only after hydrothermal/solution speciation is optimized [TS-2, SS-3]. |
