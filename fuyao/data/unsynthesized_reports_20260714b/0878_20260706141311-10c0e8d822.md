### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and likely thermodynamically stable. The closest Eu₂Ga₄Te₈ analogue is also E_hull = 0.0 and appears in chemical, structural, and thermodynamic similarity lists (CS1, SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is polymorphic: the closest reference is Cccm while the target is Imma, so the stability of this exact symmetry setting may depend on synthesis temperature or ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the exact analogue is synthesized at 1100–1150 K and homogenized at 950 K in a sealed ampoule (CS1/SS1/TS1). The main severity penalties are sealed-tube handling for volatile Te and air-sensitive Eu handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore moderate rather than easy, mainly because of the long sealed-ampoule anneal.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because Ga and Te are low-melting constituents and the highest cation melting point is not refractory [Reasonable Inference]. The exact analogue’s successful sealed-ampoule synthesis strongly supports structural accessibility (CS1/SS1/TS1). The long 350–360 h homogenization in the reference indicates slow ordering or diffusion, so the kinetics are good but not trivial (CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Direct melt is supported by TlGaTe₂ single-crystal growth from stoichiometric elements with slow cooling (SS3, TS2). However, the route requires vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions like InGaTe₂ synthesis under high vacuum (SS5), and Te volatility makes containment important [Reasonable Inference]. Eu handling adds an air-sensitivity penalty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth should provide faster kinetics than purely solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and related Ga–Te tetrahedral frameworks crystallize from melts or ampoule reactions (SS3, TS2, SS5). The uncertainty is phase selection: the target Imma phase may not be the first crystallizing polymorph, and the exact composition reference is Cccm rather than Imma (CS1/SS1/TS1). Slow cooling may improve crystals but could also favor the thermodynamically preferred polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT is more severe because it requires high vacuum, sealed tubes, transport agents, and a multi-step thermal gradient process, as shown by TeBr₄-assisted telluride CVT (CS3). AlCl₃-assisted CVT was used when single-phase chalcogenide synthesis was difficult (CS5), but halide chemistry increases handling and contamination risk [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS that Eu(GaTe₂)₂ itself is CVT-grown.

#### F3 — Route 3

Transport agents are available for telluride CVT, with TeBr₄ explicitly demonstrated in CS3 and AlCl₃ in CS5. However, Eu transport may be inefficient or may form stable Eu halides [Reasonable Inference], and no direct Eu–Ga–Te CVT example is provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact transfer. Composition control is likely the limiting kinetic factor because multicomponent transport can fractionate species [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is supported by MnIn₂Te₄-II and MnGa₂Te₄-II analogues made at ~1.5 GPa / 15 kbar and ~800 °C (CS4, SS2). The rubric does not penalize pressure below 5 GPa, but practically the modified belt-type apparatus is specialized and challenging (CS4, SS2). This route is therefore scored moderately by rubric but judged experimentally difficult.

#### F3 — Route 4

The route could access a polymorph if Imma is not reachable by ambient annealing [Reasonable Inference], and high-pressure telluride polymorphs are demonstrated in CS4 and SS2. However, transformation from a pre-synthesized parent requires structural reconstruction, and the product may need quenching like MnGa₂Te₄-II (SS2). Because exact Eu(GaTe₂)₂ already has an ambient sealed-ampoule analogue, high pressure is a contingency rather than the best kinetic path (CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination contrast: Eu is 8-coordinate with Eu–Te ≈3.41–3.46 Å, while Ga is tetrahedral with Ga–Te ≈2.62–2.66 Å in the target. This triggers one amorphization-risk criterion by ionic-radius mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target and closest analogue show edge/corner sharing but not face-sharing, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom unit cell, so the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Eu(GaTe₂)₂ in the Imma setting is likely synthesizable and probably close to a ground-state or low-energy ordered polymorph because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the exact composition analogue is also zero-hull (target, CS1/SS1/TS1). The strongest support comes from the exact Eu₂Ga₄Te₈ / Eu(GaTe₂)₂ sealed-ampoule synthesis, which shares the same Eu–Te coordination and GaTe₄ tetrahedral framework motifs (CS1, SS1, TS1). The key enabling factor is the chemically matched, thermodynamically stable Eu–Ga–Te analogue that already forms by conventional sealed-ampoule synthesis. The main limiting factor is polymorph/ordering control between the known Cccm analogue and the target Imma structure [Reasonable Inference from target versus CS1/SS1/TS1]. Long homogenization and sealed-tube control of Te chemical potential are likely essential for phase purity (CS1/SS1/TS1).
