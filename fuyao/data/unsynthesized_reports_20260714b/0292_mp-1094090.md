### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

#### Route 1: High-pressure direct nitridation of Ni metal

| Field | Details |
|:--|:--|
| Reference basis | Directly inferred from Ni₃N synthesis from solid 3d transition metals, including Ni, plus N₂ at ~10 GPa and 1800 K using DAC/YAG laser heating [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. This is the strongest chemical and thermodynamic analogue because it is the same binary Ni–N system [CS-1, TS-1]. |
| Precursors | Ni metal foil/powder, ≥99.9%; high-purity N₂ gas, preferably loaded cryogenically or as compressed N₂ [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. |
| Temperature program | Compress to target pressure → laser heat to ~1500–1600 °C, corresponding to 1800 K, for ~30 min → quench at pressure → decompress [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. |

| Pressure | High pressure, ~10 GPa [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. |

| Transferability to target | Ni₃N was synthesized under these conditions from Ni + N₂ [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1], so Ni₄N should be accessible if nitrogen chemical potential and quench path stabilize the less N-rich cubic Pm-3m phase [Reasonable Inference]. |
| Estimated difficulty | Challenging, because the method requires ~10 GPa, laser heating, and phase discrimination among Ni–N phases [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the high-score boundary in the required rubric, so F1 = 90. The closest binary analogue Ni₃N has E_hull = 0.0147 eV/atom and was experimentally synthesized under high-pressure nitrogen [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The structurally close cubic CdNi₃N is on hull and synthesized by NH₃ treatment [SS-2]. The uncertainty is that Ni₄N is likely metastable or borderline stable rather than a deep ground state because the target hull value is not zero.

#### F2 — Route 1 high-pressure direct nitridation

Route 1 is severe because the Ni₃N analogue requires ~10 GPa and 1800 K, which corresponds to >1400 °C [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. It also requires a supercritical nitrogen fluid and DAC/YAG laser heating [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The source evidence is strong for feasibility of Ni–N formation but also strongly indicates harsh conditions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for using milder high-pressure conditions specifically for Ni₄N, so the severity score remains low.

#### F3 — Route 1 high-pressure direct nitridation

The kinetics are helped by high temperature and high nitrogen activity, as Ni₃N was directly synthesized from Ni and N₂ in 30 min [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. However, Ni metal is a relatively low-activity precursor compared with salts or molecular precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Structural change is likely partial N insertion/reordering rather than a fully reconstructive multication reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the high nitrogen chemical potential drives the composition to Ni₃N rather than Ni₄N [CS-1/TS-1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 ammonolysis

The SS analogue CdNi₃N uses only 743 K, a tube furnace, alumina boats, and NH₃, which makes the thermal and equipment burden moderate [SS-2]. The penalties come from NH₃ as a reactive/toxic controlled atmosphere and the repeated heat–grind–pellet–sinter sequence [SS-2]. There is insufficient evidence from CS and TS for this exact ammonolysis route to binary Ni₄N, so those sources are neutral for route severity. The route is still plausible because SS-2 directly demonstrates moderate-temperature formation of a Pm-3m Ni–N framework.

#### F3 — Route 2 ammonolysis

Kinetics are better than conventional sealed solid-state synthesis because NH₃ provides active nitrogen and SS-2 achieved Pm-3m CdNi₃N at 743 K after two 8 h cycles [SS-2]. Ni powder is less reactive than nitrates or sol-gel precursors, so precursor activity is not scored high [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because N must enter and order in a Ni lattice, but the target topology closely matches the SS-2 Pm-3m analogue. The main uncertainty is whether binary Ni₄N has a sufficiently wide stability window under NH₃ before converting to Ni₃N or decomposing to Ni [CS-1/TS-1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 reactive sputtering

The thin-film route avoids high temperature and high pressure, so the condition-severity score is high [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires a reactive plasma atmosphere containing N₂ or NH₃, which receives the controlled-atmosphere penalty [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sputtering Ni₄N, so the source-specific thin-film signal is neutral. The route is favored because it can kinetically stabilize near-hull metastable nitrides at low temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reactive sputtering

Ni metal targets and N₂/Ar gases are readily available, but reactive nitrogen incorporation lowers the vapor-availability score [Reasonable Inference]. Crystallization is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct thin-film crystallization of Ni₄N. Composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because Ni₃N is a known competing Ni–N phase [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The score is therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ni, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization criterion is not triggered [target structure]. The Pm-3m cell is small, with no large multi-site complexity, and the analogous CdNi₃N Pm-3m framework is crystalline [SS-2]. The described connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered [target structure]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for cubic Ni₄N, so they are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₄N is best regarded as a borderline thermodynamic/metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the low-energy metastability threshold. The CS and TS data strongly support formation of binary Ni nitrides but under harsh high-pressure N₂ conditions, especially for Ni₃N [CS-1/TS-1]. The SS data are especially encouraging structurally because CdNi₃N has the same cubic Pm-3m Ni–N octahedral framework and is made under moderate NH₃ conditions [SS-2]. The most important enabling factor is the simple small-cell Pm-3m structure with NNi₆ octahedra, which should have low amorphization risk [target structure; SS-2]. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity against Ni metal and Ni₃N, because the closest chemical analogue is Ni₃N rather than Ni₄N [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1].
