### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring band specified in the prompt. CS analogues are mostly on the hull and experimentally synthesized as Sr2LnB′O6 double perovskites [CS1, CS2, CS5], while TS shows Cr oxide phases with similar metastability can be synthesized but sometimes need high pressure [TS1, TS2]. SS provides structural plausibility but no direct target thermodynamic confirmation, so SS is only moderately informative [SS1–SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed triclinic ordered phase is the experimentally selected polymorph.

**F2/F3 Route 1 — Ceramic solid-state.** CS strongly supports ceramic synthesis of Sr2LnB′O6 materials using SrCO3/Ln2O3/B-site oxides at 1200–1400 °C with repeated grinding [CS1, CS2, CS5]. SS/TS confirms Sr–Cr oxides are accessible by solid-state routes, but phase selection is sensitive to atmosphere, heating rate, and impurities [SS1, TS5]. The severity score is lowered by high temperature, controlled atmosphere, and multi-step processing, while kinetics are limited by refractory Sm2O3/Cr2O3 and long diffusion lengths [CS5, SS3]. The uncertainty is the correct oxygen potential for Cr5+.

**F2/F3 Route 2 — High pressure.** TS gives strong evidence that Cr perovskites such as BaCrO3 and CaCrO3 can require 6–9 GPa-class synthesis [TS1, TS2], and SS repeats this signal for BaCrO3 [SS2]. The condition severity is poor because specialized belt/Walker apparatus and capsules are required [TS1, TS2]. Kinetics may be acceptable because high pressure stabilizes dense perovskite connectivity, but ordered Sr2SmCrO6 is not directly shown by TS. CS actually suggests high pressure may not be necessary for chemically similar double perovskites [CS1, CS2, CS5], so Route 2 is best viewed as a fallback.

**F2/F3 Route 3 — Sol–gel/Pechini.** No CS/SS/TS entry directly reports sol–gel synthesis, so this route is a [Reasonable Inference]. Severity remains moderate because final high-temperature annealing and controlled atmosphere are still likely needed [CS1, CS2, SS1]. The major uncertainty is chromium precursor chemistry and avoiding unwanted Cr3+/Cr6+ side products.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has a large cation size contrast between Sr, Sm, and Cr, so C1 is triggered based on the target bond lengths and standard ionic-size reasoning [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2SmCrO6 is likely a **borderline metastable but accessible** phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability/synthesizability threshold. CS gives the strongest positive signal: multiple Sr2LnMoO6 double perovskites with analogous Sr/Ln/B′/O chemistry are made by standard ceramic methods [CS1, CS2, CS5].
