### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.047 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score range. Structurally and thermodynamically similar materials with E_hull around 0.03–0.06 eV/atom have reported syntheses, including MnIn₂Se₄ and BiBr₃ [TS1, TS5]. Chemically similar references include many hull-stable Ga/Cu/In/Ag selenides [CS2–CS4], but CuSe₂Br demonstrates that halide selenide phase selection can be metastable and cooling-rate sensitive [CS1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether GaSeBr is a true isolable low-temperature phase or a kinetically accessible metastable sheet compound.

Source signal notes: CS useful; SS useful; TS useful. No neutral source scoring needed for F1.

---

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses sealed ampoules and moderate temperatures, supported by CuSe₂Br at 550 °C in evacuated quartz [CS1] and Ga₂TeSe₂ in evacuated sealed quartz [CS3/SS5]. The major deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Se/GaBr₃, multistep handling, and air-sensitive/corrosive bromide precursor use **[Reasonable Inference]**. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, unlike some chalcopyrite syntheses requiring 1050–1150 °C [CS2, CS4]. Uncertainty remains about whether GaBr₃ reacts cleanly or over-transports/decomposes the target.

Source signal notes: CS strong; SS moderate; TS insufficient evidence for this exact binary Ga₂Se₃/GaBr₃ route, neutral TS = 50.

---

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary precursors should reduce reconstruction relative to elemental reaction **[Reasonable Inference]**, analogous to Ga₂TeSe₂ preparation from Ga₂Se₃/Ga₂Te₃ [CS3/SS5]. The moderate proposed temperature is supported by CuSe₂Br formation at 550 °C [CS1]. However, very long annealing for Ga₂TeSe₂ [CS3/SS5] and the cooling-rate sensitivity of CuSe₂Br [CS1] indicate possible slow ordering or a narrow kinetic window. Thus kinetics are favorable but not easy.

Source signal notes: CS strong; SS useful; TS insufficient evidence for kinetics of Ga–Se–Br binaries specifically, neutral TS = 50.

---

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Elemental melt/quench is based on successful elemental sealed-ampoule syntheses of Ga-containing selenides [CS2, CS4, SS1, SS3] and direct Bi + Br₂ bromide synthesis [TS5]. It is penalized for Br₂ toxicity/volatility, sealed-tube pressure, staged heating, quench, and annealing **[Reasonable Inference]**. It avoids high pressure and likely avoids >1000 °C if adapted to the more volatile bromide target, unlike CuInSe₂/related high-temperature Bridgman methods [CS4]. The largest uncertainty is ampoule safety and stoichiometry retention.

Source signal notes: CS strong; SS moderate; TS useful for Br₂ direct bromide chemistry.

---

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt routes improve diffusion, as seen for MnGa₂Se₄ and AgGaSe₂-type materials [SS1, SS3]. Elemental Ga and Se are reactive enough for many selenides [CS2, CS4], but Br₂ introduces gas–liquid/solid transport complications **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction from elements into a specific 2D Pca2₁ sheet is more demanding than forming common chalcopyrite networks, and CuSe₂Br shows cooling-rate-dependent competing products [CS1]. Therefore Route 2 is kinetically plausible but less controlled than Route 1.

Source signal notes: CS strong; SS useful; TS useful only for bromide volatility/direct reaction, not target kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT requires high-vacuum sealed tubes and a temperature gradient, as reported for MnIn₂Se₄ using AlCl₃ at 840–820 °C and 10⁻⁶ mbar [SS2, TS1]. Similar thermodynamic selenides used iodine in evacuated ampoules at 600–950 °C [TS2, TS3]. The route is penalized for multistep feed preparation, transport-agent handling, volatile species, and air-sensitive/hygroscopic AlCl₃ [SS2, TS1]. It avoids high pressure and can likely be run below 1000 °C **[Reasonable Inference]**.

Source signal notes: TS strong; SS strong; CS insufficient evidence for CVT of GaSeBr, neutral CS = 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CVT can bypass bulk diffusion limitations because related layered/tetrahedral selenides grow as single crystals by vapor transport [SS2, TS1]. I₂-assisted ampoule synthesis of similar selenides and BiBr₃ sublimation support availability of transport chemistry [TS2, TS3, TS5]. The limiting factor is composition control: Ga, Se, and Br may have different vapor transport rates, and MnIn₂Se₄ required optimization to avoid multiphase condensation [SS2, TS1]. Hence the route is useful for crystal growth but not necessarily the easiest discovery synthesis.

Source signal notes: TS strong; SS strong; CS insufficient evidence for CVT, neutral CS = 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaSeBr is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a robust deep ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.047 eV/atom, just below the high-stability cutoff. The analogue set supports synthesis moderately well: chemically similar CuSe₂Br proves that layered selenide bromides can be made in evacuated ampoules [CS1], while Ga–Se tetrahedral networks are common in chalcopyrite and Ga₂TeSe₂ analogues [CS2, CS3]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed-ampoule chemistry, which can retain volatile Se/Br and stabilize low-dimensional halide/chalcogenide phases [CS1, TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity under volatile halogen/chalcogen chemical potentials, since CuSe₂Br required a critical cooling rate and CVT analogues required careful transport optimization [CS1, SS2/TS1]. Overall, the target is experimentally plausible but will likely require careful stoichiometry, sealed-tube practice, and rapid screening of cooling/annealing conditions.
