### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0010 eV/atom, so the rubric assigns it a very high stability score [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues are also near-hull layered halides, including LaBr₂, ZrCl₂, Gd₂Br₃, ZrI₂, and RbTiBr₃ [TS1–TS5]. Structurally similar materials show that 2D metal halide sheets can be stable or near-stable, with ZrCl₂ at E_hull = 0 and ZrI₂ essentially on hull [SS2, SS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee cation ordering or suppression of binary/ternary bromide competitors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because analogous ZrCl₂ and ZrI₂ are synthesized at 600–850 °C in sealed Ta systems [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The penalty comes from vacuum/inert sealed-tube handling, volatile halide precursors, multi-step ampoule preparation, and air-sensitive halides [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The method is experimentally mature for layered dihalides but not simple because ZrCl₂ analogues require 4–10 weeks and careful gradients [SS2, TS2]. CS provides limited additional signal except Ta₃SBr₇, which confirms sealed bromide chemistry is feasible but complex [CS4].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Hf/Zr/Ti metals, but volatile halides can mediate transport, as shown for ZrI₂ and ZrCl₂ [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the product motif is MBr₂-like and close to TiBr₂/ZrCl₂/ZrI₂/LaBr₂ [Target, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The main kinetic uncertainty is the need to distribute three cations over one 2D layer without forming segregated TiBr₂/ZrBr₂/HfBr₂-like domains [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Elemental bromination is supported by TiBr₂ synthesis from Ti and Br₂ at 900 °C and by Ta₃SBr₇ synthesis from elements including Br₂ at 550 °C [SS1, CS4]. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C, but Br₂ is volatile, toxic, and requires controlled Ar-flow or sealed evacuated ampoules [SS1, CS4]. The route receives the same severity score as Route 1 because the handling hazard is high even though the apparatus can be simpler. There is insufficient evidence from TS for direct elemental synthesis of the target quaternary phase, so TS is neutral for route-specific feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 2

TiBr₂ provides strong evidence that bromine vapor can generate a layered Ti–Br network [SS1]. However, Hf and Zr incorporation into the same phase by direct bromination is not demonstrated in the provided sources [insufficient evidence from SS/TS]. The high reactivity of Br₂ may form higher bromides before the desired average +2 bromide equilibrates [Reasonable Inference from use of tetrahalide transport in SS2/SS4]. Therefore, kinetics and phase selectivity are scored lower than flux/CVT routes.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the sealed Ta container strategy shown for RbTiBr₃ and Gd₂Br₃, with temperatures below 1000 °C and long annealing under inert conditions [TS5, SS5, TS3]. It is penalized for drybox work, sealed volatile halides, multi-step preparation, and air-sensitive/corrosive halide precursors [TS5, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high-pressure equipment and extreme temperature, which helps its severity score [TS5]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for halide flux synthesis of the target, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

A halide flux/metathesis environment should improve precursor activity compared with only dense metals, consistent with RbTiBr₃ formation from Ti halides and alkali halides in sealed Ta at 525–800 °C [TS5]. Slow cooling at 1 K/h can promote crystallinity and cation ordering [TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The structural pathway is still uncertain because TS5 gives ternary alkali titanium bromides rather than Hf/Zr/Ti MBr₂ sheets [TS5]. Overall this route has the best kinetic score because flux/transport partially offsets the refractory cation diffusion problem.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target includes Hf, Zr, and Ti in one low-symmetry P1 cation layer, and Ti–Br bonds are generally shorter than Hf/Zr–Br bonds in the target description, suggesting a meaningful cation size mismatch [Target]. The formula has only 12 atoms and the description does not indicate a >40-atom cell, so the cell-size amorphization trigger is not applied [Target]. The connectivity appears to be a layered octahedral/three-coordinate-Br network without mixed vertex/edge/face sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is whether local Hf/Zr/Ti disorder may occur even if the material is crystalline [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂ZrTiBr₈ is likely very near a ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS analogues: TiBr₂, ZrCl₂, ZrI₂, and LaBr₂ all show that 2D Molybdenite-like metal dihalide sheets are synthetically accessible under sealed halide conditions [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is halide vapor/flux transport, which allows layered halides to form at 525–850 °C despite refractory metal cations [SS2, SS4, TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is quaternary cation ordering and phase competition among binary or ternary bromides, because no provided analogue demonstrates a mixed Hf–Zr–Ti MBr₂ sheet [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require stringent moisture exclusion, sealed Ta hardware, and careful bromine chemical potential control [SS2, SS4, TS5].
