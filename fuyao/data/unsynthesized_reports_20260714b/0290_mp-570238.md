### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable threshold of the rubric, giving **90/100**. SS1 directly reports the target Au7P10I by chemical transport, which strongly supports experimental accessibility despite being slightly above the convex hull. TS gives mixed evidence: TS1 and TS2 show similar-energy phosphides may require high pressure, while TS3–TS5 show similar-energy compounds can be made in sealed ampoules. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Au–P–I stability and is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, as SS1 uses 970–920 K. The main severity penalties are evacuated sealed-tube handling, volatile P/I2, iodine toxicity/corrosivity, and multi-step CVT practice, all consistent with SS1. CS and TS do not directly alter this route severity because they do not report Au–P–I CVT; they are insufficient for route-specific severity beyond generic sealed-tube parallels.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because SS1 directly reports well-developed Au7P10I crystals after only two days, implying efficient vapor transport. The Au–P/P–P motifs in SS1 Au2P3 match the target bond lengths and local connectivity, reducing structural reconstruction uncertainty. The remaining uncertainty is control of exact iodine stoichiometry and suppression of Au2P3 or other Au–P phases.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures supported by SS2 and CS sealed-tube analogues, so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty applies. It still requires vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile red P/I2, and likely staged heating/regrinding, supported by SS2 and CS1/CS2. TS gives insufficient specific support for this iodine-mineralized Au–P–I route and is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Bulk solid-state kinetics are less favorable than CVT because dense Au and red P must interdiffuse, and SS2 reports long 7–35 day anneals for Ag polyphosphides. Iodine can improve transport, as shown in SS2, but the route is not directly demonstrated for Au7P10I. Structural reconstruction is therefore only moderate, since the target may require ordered iodine incorporation that simple annealing may not provide.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses standard sealed ampoule/furnace equipment according to SS3 and SS5, and the preferred Pb-flux temperature near 800 °C avoids high-temperature penalties. However, it has penalties for controlled atmosphere/vacuum, volatile P/I, multi-step flux removal, and toxicity of Pb/I. CS and TS do not provide direct flux evidence for Au–P–I and are treated as insufficient for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth improves kinetics by dissolving Au and P, with SS5 showing Au–P material growth in Pb flux and SS3 showing polyphosphide growth in Sn flux. However, iodine incorporation into Au7P10I in a flux is not directly demonstrated and is only a [Reasonable Inference] from SS1/SS2. The major uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the desired Au7P10I rather than Au2PbP2-like, Au2P3-like, or flux-contaminated phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a relatively ordered polyphosphide/iodoaurate network with corner-sharing motifs only in the provided target description, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not triggered. There is only one metallic cation, Au, so cation-size-disorder amorphization is unlikely. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, but the exact SS1 crystal-growth report indicates crystallization is feasible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Au7P10I is likely a slightly metastable but readily accessible phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and SS1 explicitly reports well-developed crystals by chemical transport. The strongest support comes from SS1, which is both structurally close and directly includes the target compound; CS is mostly generic sealed-tube chalcogenide evidence, and TS is mixed because some similar-energy phosphides require high pressure while others do not. The key enabling factor is iodine-mediated chemical transport, which bypasses slow Au–P solid-state diffusion and supplies iodine directly to the target structure. The key limiting factor is volatile-component and stoichiometry control, especially maintaining the correct Au:P:I balance without forming Au2P3, Au iodides, or P-rich polyphosphides. Overall, Route 1 should be prioritized for crystals, while Route 2 may be useful for powder attempts and Route 3 is exploratory.
