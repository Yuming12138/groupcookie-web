### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom range. This is consistent with many relevant analogues also being on or near the hull, including Hg₃Se₂I₂ (CS-2), Hg₂TeBr₃ (CS-5), AuBr (SS-1/TS-1), AuCl (SS-5/TS-2), and HgCdAsBr (TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is kinetic and chemical rather than thermodynamic, because Au(I) halides can exhibit sluggish transformations or disproportionation behavior (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses modest temperatures similar to low-temperature sealed halide syntheses, e.g., Hg₃Se₂I₂ at 260 °C (CS-2), Hg₂TeBr₃ at 200–280 °C (CS-5), and Ag₂HgSI₂ at 120 °C for 4 months (TS-4). The severity comes from vacuum sealing, volatile/sensitive bromide precursors, and possible repeated annealing/regrinding, all of which are supported by sealed-ampoule analogues (CS-5, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct AuAgBr₂ solid-state formation, so the score is moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary halides should be more reactive than refractory oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**, and many analogues form from binary halides/chalcogenides in sealed ampoules (CS-2, SS-4, TS-4). However, the target requires formation of Au–Ag metal-metal connectivity and a 2D sheet, not just preservation of AuBr chains; this is only partially supported by AuBr local bonding similarity (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). Long anneals in analogues, including 400 h for Hg–Se–I (CS-1) and 4 months for Ag₂HgSI₂ (TS-4), indicate possible slow ordering kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The hydrothermal route is mild in temperature because analogous Ag/Tl and Cu/Tl iodides crystallize at 160–175 °C in concentrated HI (CS-3, CS-4). It avoids vacuum ampoule handling and high-temperature sintering, but uses a sealed autoclave with volatile/corrosive HBr by analogy to HI systems (CS-3, CS-4) and requires sensitive Au halide redox control **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for hydrothermal AuAgBr₂ specifically, so the route is not scored as easy despite mild conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution-mediated transport in concentrated hydrohalic acid should greatly improve ion mobility relative to solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, and this is consistent with CS-3/CS-4 hydrothermal crystallization at only 160–175 °C. The presence of strong Au–Br local structural compatibility from AuBr analogues supports plausible nucleation of Au–Br units (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether Au(I) and Ag(I) remain simultaneously soluble and crystallize into the ternary 2D phase rather than separate AuBr/AgBr phases; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for that specific chemical selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT-like growth uses moderate temperatures, similar to AuCl CVT at 247–280 °C (SS-5, TS-2) and AuBr decomposition at 40–55 °C under dry N₂/vacuum (SS-1, TS-1). The deductions arise from reactive Br₂/Au₂Br₆ chemistry, sealed-tube requirements, and multi-step precursor preparation, all supported by the Au halide analogue procedures (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). The route is chemically plausible but operationally more hazardous than hydrothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Au halide vapor chemistry is well supported by AuBr/AuCl analogues (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2), but Ag transport is much less certain; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for efficient AgBr co-transport into a ternary phase. Crystallization may be complicated by Au(I) disproportionation or metastability, as AuCl requires careful low-temperature handling (SS-5, TS-2). Composition control is likely narrow **[Reasonable Inference]**, because Au and Ag bromide species probably have different vapor pressures and transport rates.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple composition and does not appear to require a large complex unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any large-cell amorphization tendency, so that check is neutral/low risk. The main structural risk comes from mixed edge- and corner-sharing AuAg₄Br₂ octahedra in the target, which may require ordering of Au/Ag and Br sublattices. Similar mixed-connectivity motifs exist in structurally related Ag₃SI and other halide frameworks (SS-3), but the target’s thermodynamic ground-state status offsets part of the amorphization concern.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuAgBr₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close Au/Ag/halide analogues are also on or near the hull (CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1, TS-3). The analogues support synthesis most strongly at the level of local chemistry: AuBr provides nearly identical Au–Br distances (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1), and Ag halide frameworks crystallize by both sealed solid-state and hydrothermal methods (SS-3, SS-4, CS-3, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is soft halide chemistry, which allows low-temperature ion mobility through hydrohalic acid solution or sealed halide reactions (CS-3, CS-4, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is Au(I) stability and phase selectivity, because Au halides can undergo sluggish transformations or disproportionation/metastability (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with hydrothermal HBr crystallization the most attractive first attempt.
