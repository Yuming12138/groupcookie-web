### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high end of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” scoring bracket and suggests near-synthesizable metastability. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, including CsFe2F6, CsCuF4, Cs2NaMnF6, and Cs2KMnF6, are reported as synthesized with controlled sealed, fluorinating, or pressure-assisted methods (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not identify whether CsTlF4 competes with CsTlF3, Cs2TlF6-type phases, CsF, or TlF3 decomposition products [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids F2 gas and high external pressure, which is favorable compared with fluorination/autoclave routes; structurally similar fluorides are made at 400–700 °C in Ni/Ag bombs or sealed Au/Pt tubes (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). However, it still requires dry Ar/vacuum sealing and toxic Tl precursors, as CsTlF3-related syntheses require glovebox handling and sealed tubes (CS-2/SS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS indicates whether ambient-pressure binary fluoride reaction is enough to stabilize this exact 0.05 eV/atom Pbcm phase.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluoride precursors are chemically active relative to refractory oxides and are directly used in many Cs fluoride analogues (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). Diffusion should be manageable because analogous fluoride frameworks form at moderate temperatures, but structural reconstruction into tilted Pbcm TlF6 octahedra and possible cation/anion ordering may require slow annealing. SS-3 notes that pure samples were not fully realized for a related Cs2SnF6-type system, so phase purity remains uncertain.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has moderate temperatures but uses F2, which is the dominant condition-severity penalty; F2 fluorination is explicitly used for Cs2PtF6, RbAuF4/KAuF4, and KHgF3/CsHgF3 analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). It also involves toxic Tl precursors and likely multiple processing stages, consistent with CsTlF3-related precursor preparation and dry handling (CS-2/SS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS rules out whether high pressure is required, but CS data support ambient-pressure fluorination as a first attempt.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Gas-phase fluorination of chloride/oxide precursors should provide high precursor activity, supported by rapid 3–4 h F2 syntheses of Au/Hg fluoride analogues (CS-3, CS-5). Preforming a Cs–Tl chloride precursor should improve cation mixing because CsTlF3-related synthesis uses Cs2TlCl5·H2O/CsCl/TlCl/TlCl3 chemistry (CS-2/SS-4). The main kinetic uncertainty is phase reconstruction into CsTlF4 rather than formation of simpler CsTlF3 or Cs2TlF6-type phases [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/SS-4].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 uses moderate temperature but high-pressure F2 autoclave conditions, directly analogous to CsCuF4 synthesis at pF2 ≈350–360 bar and 400 °C (TS-3). Although this is below the scoring-table 5 GPa threshold, it is operationally hazardous and requires specialized Monel equipment (TS-3). The method is justified by TS analogues near the same metastability range, but it is more severe than ambient fluorination.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
High pF2 should accelerate fluorination and stabilize high-fluorine/high-valent chemistry, as demonstrated for CsCuF4 from CsCuCl3 (TS-3). Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull require pressure, slow cooling, or quenching, showing that pressure can access phases not easily made at ambient pressure (TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the desired Pbcm CsTlF4 or instead drives competing dense fluoride polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Solution crystallization is mild in CS-4, where Cs2IrF6 forms by slow evaporation at room temperature from Cs2CO3 and H2[IrF6]. However, no CS/SS/TS source directly shows Cs–Tl condensed fluoride frameworks forming from solution, and Tl(III)/HF chemistry adds safety and stability concerns [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the nominal condition score is high, but confidence is low.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Solution chemistry eliminates bulk diffusion barriers, as shown by room-temperature crystal growth in CS-4. The weak point is structural: CS-4 supports isolated or molecular complex-fluoride salt crystallization, while the target is a corner-sharing TlF6 network. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports solution nucleation of the target topology, so structural reconstruction/pathway confidence is poor.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large A-site/B-site cation size contrast, with Cs in 9-coordinate geometry and Tl in octahedral coordination, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description reports corner-sharing TlF6 octahedra but not mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, so the connectivity risk is not triggered. The exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so C2 is treated as not triggered but uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsTlF4 is likely a **near-threshold metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues near 0.045–0.055 eV/atom are experimentally accessible but often require controlled fluorination, pressure, or slow cooling (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The CS and SS analogues strongly support the feasibility of Cs–metal fluoride synthesis, including Cs–Tl fluoride chemistry and Cs-based octahedral fluoride frameworks (CS-2/SS-4, SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high fluorine chemical potential and Tl(III) stability, best achieved by controlled F2 fluorination as used in CS-1, CS-3, and CS-5. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against CsTlF3/Cs2Tl2F6-like phases or Tl fluoride/oxyfluoride impurities, since CsTlF3-related analogues show impurity and polymorph issues (CS-2/SS-4). Overall, the target appears synthesizable but not routine, with the best initial strategy being ambient-pressure diluted-F2 fluorination followed by dry annealing.
