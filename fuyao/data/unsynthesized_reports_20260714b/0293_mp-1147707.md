### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s Eₕᵤₗₗ of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the high-synthesizability boundary in the provided rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable or higher metastability are reported as synthesizable, including KFeBr₃ at 0.0516 eV/atom [TS3], Cs₃Fe₂Cl₉ at 0.0755 eV/atom [TS1], and CsBrF at 0.1253 eV/atom [TS2]. CS and SS include several stable mixed-halide or layered copper analogues [CS2, CS4, SS1], supporting that the chemistry is not intrinsically inaccessible. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is metastable rather than clearly ground-state, so kinetic trapping and phase competition matter.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is strongly supported by the closest structural analogue Rb₂CuCl₂Br₂, which is made by sealed-tube melting at 800 °C and long annealing [SS1]. The severity score is reduced because SS1 requires vacuum sealing, glovebox manipulation for hygroscopic halides, and a long multi-step heat treatment. Kinetically, a halide melt should provide good diffusion [SS1], but F substitution introduces stronger Cu–F bonding and possible silica reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS on direct Rb₂CuCl₂F₂ melt formation was treated as neutral where direct transferability is not established.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has lower temperature severity because Rb/Cu halides can be annealed in evacuated ampoules at 373–583 K in SS3. The penalty comes from vacuum sealing, repeated grinding, and dry handling [SS3]. Kinetically, the absence of a full melt slows diffusion, and SS3 explicitly reports long anneals and intermediate grindings. The main uncertainty is whether low-temperature diffusion can order F and Cl into the tetragonal I4/mmm CuCl₄F₂ octahedral arrangement.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from high ionic mobility and mild temperatures: CS1 shows room-temperature slow crystallization of mixed chloride/fluoride salts, TS1 shows chloride double salts from concentrated HCl solution, and SS5 shows hydrothermal crystallization at 150 °C. The severity is moderate because sealed ampoules, acidic media, and possible HF/fluoride hazards are involved [SS5, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic score is high for ion transport but reduced for structural selection because CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate solution growth of Rb₂CuCl₂F₂. The largest uncertainty is competition from hydrated Cu²⁺, Cu chloride complexes, or separate RbF/RbCl-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Flux growth is supported by CS4, where excess flux/component suppresses a more stable competing phase, and by SS1, where mixed Rb/Cu halides are melted and annealed. The route has similar severity penalties to Route 1 because it still requires sealed dry high-temperature halide handling [SS1, CS4]. Kinetically, flux growth is the strongest bulk route because liquid halides enhance ion transport and crystal growth [SS1, CS4]. The uncertainty is whether a RbCl/RbF flux can stabilize the target rather than dissolving or converting it to binary/ternary halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Rb⁺ and Cu²⁺, triggering C1 by fundamental ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure also has mixed edge, corner, and face sharing in the anion-centered polyhedra, triggering C3 directly from the target description and matching complex mixed connectivity seen in SS1. The atom-count criterion is not triggered because the formula is small and the I4/mmm analogue family does not imply a large >40-atom cell [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. The resulting F4 score of 60 reflects moderate structural-disorder risk rather than severe glass-forming tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂Cu(ClF)₂ is likely a **near-threshold metastable phase**, not a deeply stable ground-state, because its Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.0500 eV/atom. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: the strongest structural support is Rb₂CuCl₂Br₂ made by sealed halide melting [SS1], while thermodynamic analogues show phases with similar or larger hull energies can be isolated [TS1–TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and tunable halide chemical potential available in solution, melt, or flux media [CS1, SS1, CS4, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of F/Cl ordering and Cu²⁺ coordination without forming competing Rb–Cu–Cl, Rb–Cu–F, or hydrated copper phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require careful sealed or acidic conditions and iterative phase-screening.
