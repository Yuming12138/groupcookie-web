### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

The closest Hg–P–S analogue Hg₂P₂S₇ is also E_hull = 0 eV/atom and appears as the top chemical, structural, and thermodynamic match (CS, SS, TS). Additional SS/TS analogues such as CdPS₃, CrAg(PS₃)₂, AgBi(PS₃)₂, and AgInP₂S₆ are also reported at E_hull = 0 eV/atom, supporting stability of thiophosphate frameworks (SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee an easily accessible kinetic pathway [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature because the closest analogue Hg₂P₂S₇ was synthesized at 240 °C (CS, SS, TS). The main penalties are evacuated sealed ampoule conditions, volatile S/P species, and Hg toxicity; the sealed ampoule and sulfur excess are directly supported by the Hg₂P₂S₇ synthesis (CS, SS, TS), while volatility/toxicity risks are [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high pressure; therefore high pressure is scored as not applicable. The route is moderate rather than easy because the anneal is long and sealed Hg–P–S ampoules require careful safety control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The closest analogue forming at 240 °C indicates favorable reaction kinetics or vapor-assisted mass transport in the Hg–P–S system (CS, SS, TS). The 4-week duration indicates slow crystallization/ordering despite low-temperature feasibility (CS, SS, TS). Structural reconstruction is modest because both target and Hg₂P₂S₇ contain PS₄ tetrahedra and Hg–S coordination networks (CS, SS, TS), but the target stoichiometry and Hg(2) site require rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are favorable but not rapid.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct elemental sealed-tube reactions are supported for several structurally and thermodynamically similar thiophosphates, often in evacuated tubes at 700–850 °C (SS, TS). For Hg₃(PS₄)₂, such high temperatures would likely be reduced or replaced with HgS-based feed because Hg/S/P volatility is a major risk [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct elemental Hg/P/S synthesis of the target-like phase, so Route 2 is penalized relative to Route 1. The condition severity remains moderate because vacuum sealing and toxic/volatile reagents are unavoidable.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental P and S are reactive, and direct element reactions produce multiple thiophosphate phases (SS, TS). However, forming Hg₃(PS₄)₂ from elements requires full network assembly and may encounter competing sulfides or phosphorus sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Long synthesis times and impurity issues are reported for some similar MPS₃ and AgBiP₂S₆ systems (SS, TS), lowering the structural-reconstruction score. The route is feasible but less kinetically targeted than using HgS + P + S under low-temperature conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

HgGa₂S₄ demonstrates that Hg-containing sulfide crystals can be grown by iodine-assisted CVT (CS). However, no SS or TS source directly confirms iodine-CVT for Hg–P–S thiophosphates, so evidence from SS and TS is insufficient for this specific route and is scored neutral where route-specific confirmation is needed. The route requires evacuated ampoules, volatile iodine, and toxic Hg compounds [Reasonable Inference], increasing severity. It may also require prereaction plus transport growth, making it more operationally complex than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can enhance mass transport through volatile halide or chalcogenide species [Reasonable Inference], and iodine-assisted growth is directly shown for HgGa₂S₄ (CS). The low-temperature gradient used for Hg₂P₂S₇ supports the idea that temperature-gradient transport/crystallization can work in Hg-containing sulfide chemistry (CS, SS, TS). The uncertainty is that Hg–P–S may transport incongruently, possibly forming HgI₂, P–S vapor species, or sulfur-rich byproducts [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics for crystal growth are attractive, but composition control is a major unknown.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very different cation environments: covalent PS₄ tetrahedra and softer Hg–S coordination, including HgS₄ and see-saw-like HgS₄ units. This large cation-size/coordination mismatch triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The likely crystallographic cell size is above 40 atoms if the orthorhombic Aea2 cell contains four formula units, triggering C2 [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, so C3 is not triggered.
