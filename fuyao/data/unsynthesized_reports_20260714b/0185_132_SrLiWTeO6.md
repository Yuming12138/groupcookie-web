### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff in the provided rubric, so F1 = 90. Chemically similar W oxides in CS1–CS4 are computed on the hull, and CS5 is nearly on-hull, which supports the general stability of multication W oxides. Thermodynamic analogues TS1 and TS5 have E_hull values near 0.05–0.06 eV/atom and are experimentally accessible, but TS3/TS4 show that some similar-energy phases need special conditions; therefore the target is likely accessible but not guaranteed as a bulk equilibrium phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Conventional Solid-State / Ceramic

CS1/CS2/CS5 support ceramic synthesis of W oxides using oxide/carbonate precursors at 800–1200 °C, including air or forming gas and alkali excess. The severity score is moderate because no high pressure is required, but Li/Te volatility, Te toxicity, and multiple grinding/annealing steps introduce deductions. Kinetically, this route is weaker because WO3/SrCO3/Li2CO3/TeO2 require long-range diffusion and extensive structural reconstruction; CS evidence supports feasibility but not fast kinetics.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Citrate / Soft Chemistry

CS3/CS4 strongly support citrate resin routes for W double oxides, using drying at 120 °C, decomposition at 600 °C, and final crystallization at 850–900 °C under reducing gas. This lowers diffusion barriers and improves cation homogeneity, giving a better F3 than conventional ceramic synthesis. The main penalties are the multistep procedure, possible need for H2-containing gas, and uncertainty about tellurium precursor solubility and redox behavior.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux / Self-Flux Growth

SS1 is the strongest structural support for this route because it uses TeO2–MoO3 self-flux to grow a Te-containing oxide at 750 °C, while TS5 supports Li-containing molybdate melt growth by slow cooling. The flux route reduces diffusion limitations and may stabilize a low-symmetry P1 framework through solution-mediated crystallization. The remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: the same TeO2/WO3/Li flux could crystallize simpler Li tungstates, Sr tungstates, or tellurites instead of LiSrWTeO6.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Sealed-Ampoule / Mild High-Pressure

SS3 supports sealed-ampoule synthesis for a structurally similar K/Sb/Mo oxide, and TS3 shows a W oxide phase can form under 1.5 GPa Ar at 900 °C. However, TS3 was a serendipitous byproduct with competing phases, so it is a weaker direct guide for controlled LiSrWTeO6 synthesis. This route is most useful as a fallback if volatility or metastability prevents open-crucible synthesis, but it has higher operational complexity and uncertain phase purity.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has very different cation sizes and coordination environments: LiO5, SrO8, WO5, and TeO2/L-shaped units, so the radius/coordination mismatch check is triggered. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell, and it reports corner sharing rather than coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing networks, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high, with the main concern being the need to order unlike cations into a low-symmetry P1 framework.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiSrWTeO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS analogues strongly support synthesis of multication W oxides by ceramic and soft-chemistry methods, while SS1 and TS5 specifically support flux/melt routes for Te- and Li-containing complex oxides. The most important enabling factor is the availability of TeO2/WO3 or Li2WO4-based flux chemistry, which can overcome slow W-containing solid-state diffusion. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity under volatile Li/Te conditions: competing Li tungstates, SrWO4, Sr tellurites/tellurates, or W/Te redox variants may form. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with flux growth or soft chemistry more promising than a direct oxide/carbonate solid-state route.
