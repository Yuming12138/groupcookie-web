### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifying a high thermodynamic score of 94 [Target]. Similar experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues include VS₂-like material at E_hull ≈0.019 eV/atom and V/Ni/Tl sulfides around 0.037 eV/atom [TS-1, TS-3, TS-4]. CS and SS analogues include multiple hull-stable or near-hull Nb chalcogenide halides [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee a unique phase because halogen and sulfur chemical potentials can stabilize competing V chlorides or sulfides [Reasonable Inference from phase-control issues in CS-1, CS-3].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures near 500 °C, consistent with Nb(SBr)₂ synthesis at 500 °C and Nb–Se–Cl reactions near 530 °C [CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-5]. The main severity penalties are from evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/chlorine species, and toxic or air-sensitive chlorine/V chloride reagents [CS-2, CS-4; Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide strong useful signal for this factor; TS is less specific to V–S–Cl sealed halide synthesis, so TS condition evidence is only indirect and scored neutral, 50, for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The exact structural analogue Nb(SBr)₂ forms in a single sealed-tube heating step, supporting feasible nucleation of the target motif [CS-2, SS-1]. However, elemental V is refractory and diffusion-limited, so the kinetic score is moderated despite reactive sulfur/halogen chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Simultaneous polymorph formation in the Nb(SBr)₂ analogue indicates ordering and phase selection may not be trivial [CS-2, SS-1]. TS provides useful V–S bond-length support through VS₂ but insufficient direct evidence for V–S–Cl kinetic pathways, so TS is neutral, 50, for the detailed route kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT uses moderate absolute temperatures but requires evacuated sealed quartz tubes, volatile transport agents, long dwell times, and a controlled thermal gradient [CS-1, SS-3]. CrSBr CVT also requires evacuated ampoules and a temperature gradient, supporting the severity assessment for halochalcogenides [CS-4]. The toxic/air-sensitive penalty comes from likely Cl₂/VCl₄/I₂ handling [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, CS-4]. TS supports iodine-assisted crystal growth in a V sulfide-related material but at much higher temperatures, so it is only partially transferable [TS-1].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by transporting volatile species rather than relying solely on bulk diffusion, which is supported by large-crystal growth of NbX₂Y₂ phases [CS-1, SS-3]. The target’s layered halochalcogenide structure is closely related to these transported phases, so crystallization is plausible [Target; CS-1, SS-3]. The score is not higher because the analogues require 3–4 weeks and show phase-window sensitivity and contamination issues [CS-1]. TS gives partial support for iodine-assisted crystallization of V sulfide layers but not for V(SCl)₂ specifically [TS-1].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth operates at 550–700 °C, below the >1000 °C penalty threshold, but still requires evacuated sealed tubes and slow cooling [SS-2, SS-4]. Use of V chlorides and sulfur introduces volatility and air/moisture-sensitivity concerns [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides the primary evidence; CS gives insufficient direct evidence for V(SCl)₂ flux synthesis and is neutral, 50, for this route; TS also provides insufficient direct flux-route evidence and is neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Halide flux should enhance mass transport and crystal growth, as structurally similar Nb–S phases are grown from alkali chloride fluxes with slow cooling [SS-2, SS-4]. This route may reduce the diffusion penalty of refractory V compared with dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, no directly analogous V–S–Cl flux synthesis is provided, so structural reconstruction and phase-selection uncertainty remain significant [insufficient direct evidence from CS/TS]. The route is plausible but less evidence-backed than sealed-tube or CVT methods.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply [Target]. The described structure is a simple 2D sheet with V, S, and Cl coordination, and there is no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [Target]. CS and SS analogues are crystalline low-dimensional compounds made by sealed-tube/CVT methods, which supports low amorphization tendency [CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-3]. TS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V(SCl)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0210 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from the exact structural analogue Nb(SBr)₂, which has the same monoclinic C2/m layered motif and is synthesized in a sealed tube at 500 °C [CS-2, SS-1]. Additional support comes from NbX₂Y₂ chalcogenide halides grown by CVT over 400–515 °C and from V–S bonding compatibility in VS₂-like phases [CS-1, SS-3, TS-1]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, which can overcome slow diffusion of refractory vanadium [CS-1, CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is control of chlorine and sulfur chemical potentials to avoid competing vanadium sulfides, chlorides, or off-stoichiometric halochalcogenides [CS-1, CS-3, TS-4; Reasonable Inference].
