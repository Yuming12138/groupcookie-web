### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.037 eV/atom, which is within the commonly synthesizable metastability range and maps to a high F1 score. CS analogues include low-hull chalcohalides such as CS2 at 0.0136 eV/atom and CS3 at 0.00745 eV/atom, while SS and TS analogues also cluster below or near 0.04 eV/atom (SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is not exactly on the hull, so it may require kinetic trapping or a narrow chemical potential window.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** The direct solid-state route is strongly supported by AgVP₂Se₆ synthesis from elements at 600 °C in evacuated silica (SS3) and by chalcohalide ampoule reactions at 400–600 °C (CS1–CS3). Severity is reduced by moderate temperature but penalized for vacuum sealing and volatile P/Se/Br chemistry. Kinetics are moderate because Ag/P/Se are not refractory, but long anneals in CS1 and TS3 suggest slow ordering and possible competing phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** The self-flux/melt route benefits from liquid-mediated transport, supported by flux growth of Ag-containing selenides (SS1) and CuSe₂Br formation from CuBr/Se at 550 °C (SS5). Severity is similar to Route 1 because it still requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile/toxic components. Kinetically it is better than direct solid-state reaction, but SS5 warns that cooling rate can determine which phase forms.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** Hydrothermal synthesis has milder temperature and no vacuum requirement, and Ag–Se–halide chemistry is directly supported by AgSe₃I in concentrated HI at 220 °C (SS4). TS5 further supports hydrothermal acidic halide media for chalcogen-halogen systems. The main uncertainty is P–Se framework formation under acidic hydrothermal conditions; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly proves that P–Se–Ag–Br sheets can be assembled this way.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.** Low-temperature solution diffusion is inspired by the closest thermodynamic/topological analogue Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂, which forms by diffusion of As₄S₃ organometallic adducts and copper halides at ambient temperature (TS1). This route scores well for mobility but poorly for structural certainty because soluble P–Se cluster chemistry is not provided in CS/SS/TS. It is therefore more exploratory than the ampoule or hydrothermal routes.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target combines soft Ag–Br/Ag–Se coordination with covalent P–Se units, creating a mixed ionic/covalent network; analogous mixed edge/corner sharing is present in CS2, CS3, and TS1. The target’s described AgSe₂Br₂ units share both edges and corners, which raises disorder/crystallization risk. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes whether the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is treated as non-triggered/neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag₂P₄Se₃Br₂ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.037 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS data collectively support synthesizability: Ag–P–Se sheets form by direct ampoule reaction (SS3), Ag–Se–halide motifs form hydrothermally or from halide melts (SS4, SS5), and a very similar Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂ topology exists in the thermodynamic set (TS1). The key enabling factor is the low-to-moderate temperature mobility of soft Ag/Se/Br chemistry, which is repeatedly exploited in ampoule, flux, and hydrothermal analogues (SS1, SS4, SS5). The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: several analogues report low yields, mixed products, impurities, or cooling-rate sensitivity (SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5). Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires screening of Br/Se chemical potential and cooling/annealing conditions.
