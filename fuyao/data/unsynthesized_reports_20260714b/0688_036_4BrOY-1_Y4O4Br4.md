### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target YOBr has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the computed ground-state hull and warrants a high F1 score. This is consistent with multiple REOBr analogues such as SmOBr, GdOBr, NdOBr, and LuOBr also being reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS evidence also supports oxyhalide stability through stable YOCl/LaOBr/LaOCl analogues, although one YOCl polymorph is slightly above hull at ~0.047 eV/atom, indicating polymorph sensitivity rather than chemical instability (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly experimental phase selection among possible Y–O–Br stoichiometries, not bulk thermodynamic instability (SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1: Thermal decomposition

The route is moderate in condition severity because it operates at only 450–500 °C for Y-containing oxybromides, avoiding high-temperature/high-pressure penalties (CS-4, SS-5, TS-4). Deductions are justified by corrosive 48% HBr precursor use, hydrate-preparation/decomposition steps, and the requirement for humidified N₂ rather than simple air firing (CS-4, SS-5, TS-4). The uncertainty is the exact hydrate stoichiometry and dehydration pathway for a modern YOBr batch, which would need TGA/XRD confirmation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Thermal decomposition

Kinetics are favorable because the reactive bromide hydrate precursor decomposes directly to oxybromide, rather than requiring long-range diffusion between dense oxide and halide solids (CS-4, SS-5, TS-4). The cited 40–60 min decomposition time supports relatively fast conversion (CS-4, SS-5, TS-4). Some reconstruction from hydrate to layered P4/nmm YOBr is still required, so the score is below excellent. The remaining uncertainty is whether nanoscale residual Y₂O₃ or YBr₃ could persist without optimized humidity [Reasonable Inference based on phase-control issues in CS-1/CS-2].

#### F2 — Route 2: NH₄Br solid-state oxyhalogenation

This route uses moderate temperatures: 230–360 °C for Y ammonium-halide chemistry and 400–900 °C for REOBr analogues, so it avoids >1000 °C penalties (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). Severity comes from volatile/decomposing NH₄Br, inert/moisture-excluded atmosphere, and multi-step intermediate formation noted for YOX chemistry (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). The source evidence also warns that phase control can be sensitive to molar ratio and temperature, supporting a moderate rather than easy rating (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2: NH₄Br solid-state oxyhalogenation

Kinetics are mixed: NH₄Br chemically activates bromination, but Y₂O₃ remains a refractory oxide (CS-1, CS-2). Formation of YOX through ammonium hexahalometallate intermediates suggests reactive transport, improving kinetics over direct oxide/halide sintering (CS-1, CS-2, TS-2). However, the route may form polymorphs or halide-rich impurities, reducing the structural-reconstruction and phase-control component (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3: Flux growth

The flux route avoids high pressure and usually remains at 850–1000 °C, but it requires dry-box handling, anhydrous REBr₃/YBr₃, sealed or dry inert operation, slow cooling, and post-growth flux removal (SS-1, TS-1, SS-4, TS-3). These requirements make condition severity worse than the hydrate route despite excellent crystallization potential. The evidence for SmOBr and GdOBr flux methods is strong, but specific YOBr flux growth is inferred rather than explicitly demonstrated (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-3).

#### F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

Flux-mediated diffusion is favorable; SmOBr and LaOBr single crystals were grown from REBr₃ flux by slow cooling, and GdOBr analogues used REBr₃/KBr recrystallization (SS-1, TS-1, SS-4, TS-3). The target structure closely matches the P4/nmm REOBr analogues, so structural reconstruction risk is low once the composition is in the correct oxybromide field (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-3; target structure). The main uncertainty is crystal size/yield, because analogue flux growth was described as not fully optimized and morphology-dependent (SS-1, TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 4: Sealed Y₂O₃/YBr₃ solid-state route

This route uses manageable temperatures around 750 °C in the closest Y–O–Br example, but the evacuated sealed ampoule and hygroscopic YBr₃ impose controlled-atmosphere and air-sensitivity penalties (SS-3). LuOBr evidence also uses sealed metal/container handling with halide chemistry, reinforcing the need for specialized containment (TS-5). The route is not high-pressure, but it is operationally less convenient than hydrate decomposition.

#### F3 — Route 4: Sealed Y₂O₃/YBr₃ solid-state route

Kinetics are the weakest among the four routes because the closest Y–O–Br sealed reaction required 12 days and produced Y₃O₄Br as a by-product rather than targeted YOBr (SS-3). This indicates slow ordering and competing stoichiometries in the Y–O–Br phase field (SS-3). While YBr₃ may provide some halide transport, the route still likely depends on solid-state reaction with refractory Y₂O₃ [Reasonable Inference from SS-3].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple single-cation layered oxyhalide with a small P4/nmm-type cell and no cation-size disorder, so amorphization tendency is low (target structure; SS-1, SS-5, TS-4). The OY₄ tetrahedra are corner- and edge-sharing, but face-sharing is not reported, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met (target structure; CS-4, SS-1, SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly processing-related: flux quenching or rapid dehydration could produce poorly crystalline material, but the equilibrium structure itself does not suggest intrinsic glass-forming tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YOBr is very likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple REOBr analogues are also hull-stable (target data; SS-1, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The CS/SS/TS analogue support is strong: NdOBr, SmOBr, GdOBr, LaOBr, and LuOBr all demonstrate the same rare-earth oxybromide chemistry and closely related P4/nmm layered structures (CS-3, CS-4; SS-1, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature reactive bromide routes, especially bromide hydrate decomposition explicitly applicable to Y (CS-4, SS-5, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control in the Y–O–Br system, because Y₃O₄Br can form under sealed Y₂O₃/YBr₃ conditions and YOX chemistry can show polymorph or halide impurity sensitivity (SS-3; CS-1, CS-2). Overall, YOBr should be experimentally accessible, with hydrate decomposition the most direct powder route and flux growth the best route for larger crystals.
