### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is on the favorable boundary of the 90–100 bin in the supplied rubric [Target]. Chemically close Sr₂HoTaO₆ and Ba₂LuNbO₆ analogues are calculated at E_hull = 0 eV/atom [CS1, CS5], and structurally close Sr₂YTaO₆ is only 0.017 eV/atom above hull [SS1]. TS analogues with E_hull near 0.05–0.067 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable [TS1–TS3], so the target is likely accessible, though possibly slightly metastable.

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
Route 1 uses air, ambient pressure, and standard furnace processing, which is favorable and directly supported by Sr₂HoTaO₆ synthesis [CS1, SS2]. The main deductions are high temperature above 1000 °C and multiple ceramic steps, both explicitly present in Ho tantalate and Sr₂YTaO₆ analogues [CS1, SS1]. TS gives insufficient route-specific evidence for Sr₂LuTaO₆ direct oxide synthesis and is treated as neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Direct reaction of SrCO₃, Lu₂O₃, and Ta₂O₅ is kinetically limited because rare-earth oxides and Ta₂O₅ are refractory, and analogous Y/Ta systems required high-temperature firing and regrinding [CS3, SS1]. Sr₂HoTaO₆ being obtained at 1280 °C/10 h shows the route is feasible [CS1], but complete B-site ordering and elimination of residual Ta-rich phases may be slow [CS3, CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is the smaller Lu³⁺ radius relative to Ho³⁺, which may alter the phase window [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
The LuTaO₄ precursor route is more step-intensive but does not require high pressure, inert atmosphere, or sealed ampoules, matching YTaO₄-derived Sr₂YTaO₆/Ba₂YTaO₆ syntheses [SS1, CS3, CS4]. It still requires >1000 °C and repeated firing/regrinding [SS1, CS3]. TS provides insufficient evidence for LuTaO₄ precursor synthesis specifically and is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Pre-forming LuTaO₄ should reduce Lu/Ta interdiffusion compared with separate Lu₂O₃ and Ta₂O₅ [Reasonable Inference], and the analogous YTaO₄ strategy successfully produced Sr₂YTaO₆/Ba₂YTaO₆ [SS1, CS3]. However, the route still relies on solid-state Sr diffusion and long high-temperature annealing, as shown by 1400 °C/3 days for Sr₂YTaO₆ [SS1]. Phase purity should be better than Route 1 but at the cost of longer processing.

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
The sol–gel route is inferred rather than directly reported, so CS/SS/TS evidence is insufficient for the exact method and is treated cautiously. The final firing can likely occur in air because all close ceramic analogues use air when specified [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS2]. Severity is penalized for multi-step solution processing and possible moisture-sensitive Ta alkoxide chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The main advantage is high precursor activity from molecular mixing, which should reduce diffusion length and improve cation homogeneity [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting factor remains crystallization of a tilted, ordered double perovskite containing refractory Ta–O and Lu–O octahedra, for which CS/SS analogues generally require high-temperature annealing [CS1, CS3, SS1]. Because no CS/SS/TS sol–gel example is supplied, the kinetic score is favorable but uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation size/coordination contrast, evident from Sr–O, Lu–O, and Ta–O bond lengths in the target description [Target], so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned. However, the network is a corner-sharing octahedral double perovskite without reported edge/face-sharing coexistence [Target], and no evidence indicates a >40-atom cell. Thus, amorphization tendency is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LuTaO₆ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target], while close Sr₂HoTaO₆ and Sr₂YTaO₆ analogues are stable or nearly stable [CS1, SS1, SS2]. The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis: Sr₂HoTaO₆ is almost a direct chemical analogue [CS1, SS2], and Sr₂YTaO₆ is a close structural analogue synthesized by a ceramic route [SS1]. The most important enabling factor is the robust A₂B′TaO₆ double-perovskite framework, which forms for Sr/rare-earth/Ta compositions under ordinary air sintering [CS1, SS1]. The main limiting factor is slow solid-state kinetics and B-site ordering involving refractory Lu₂O₃/Ta₂O₅ or LuTaO₄-derived intermediates [CS3, SS1]. A sol–gel or precursor-mediated route is likely to improve kinetics, but the most evidence-backed practical route remains high-temperature ceramic synthesis.
