### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold; by the provided rubric this supports a high F1 score. The closest chemical and structural reference is exact-composition RbIn₃F₁₀ with E_hull = 0.0066 eV/atom and experimental synthesis by melt growth (CS-1; SS-1), which strongly supports accessibility. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values near or above the target, such as Ba₂Ni₇F₁₈ at 0.0548 eV/atom and Ba₂Ni₃F₁₀ at 0.1097 eV/atom, are also experimentally synthesized (TS-2; TS-1), suggesting that modest metastability is not prohibitive. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target Pmma polymorph is the ground-state polymorph or a near-degenerate metastable variant.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses only 790 °C, no high pressure, and a simple melt/slow-cool sequence, all directly supported for RbIn₃F₁₀ by CS-1/SS-1. The main severity penalties are the sealed Pt tube requirement and the moisture/volatility sensitivity expected for high-temperature fluorides; sealed noble-metal tubes are also used in related fluoride syntheses (CS-3, CS-4, SS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the exact internal atmosphere in the RbIn₃F₁₀ Pt tube, so no additional atmosphere penalty was applied. Overall severity is moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 1

The melt route has favorable kinetics because the exact compound was crystallized from a molten RbF/InF₃ mixture at 790 °C with slow cooling (CS-1; SS-1), which bypasses slow solid-state diffusion. Precursor activity is good but not maximal because binary fluorides are less reactive than nitrate/sol-gel precursors. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the reference has the same composition and similar InF₆/InF₇ connectivity (CS-1; SS-1). The uncertainty is polymorph selection between P222₁ and target Pmma.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 requires controlled dry/inert or vacuum sealed-tube processing, supported by LiInF₄ synthesis using dry glovebox/outgassing/dry Ar in gold tubes (SS-2), BaAlF₅ synthesis in sealed Au/Pt tubes under Ar (CS-3, CS-4), and weberite fluoride synthesis in welded Pt ampoules under vacuum (SS-3). Temperatures are not extreme, generally below 1000 °C in these analogues (SS-2; CS-3; CS-4; SS-3). However, the need for controlled atmosphere, sealing, drying, and possibly multiple anneals increases severity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that sub-melting RbIn₃F₁₀ will form cleanly, so the score is lower than for melt growth.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state kinetics are moderate because binary fluoride precursors can react in sealed tubes, as shown for LiInF₄ from LiF + InF₃ (SS-2) and BaAlF₅ from BaF₂ + AlF₃ (CS-3, CS-4). Diffusion should be manageable at 600–850 °C based on these analogues, but not as favorable as in a melt. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor because the target contains mixed InF₆ octahedra and InF₇ pentagonal bipyramids, and analogous fluoride systems can show polymorphism and phase-control issues (CS-3, CS-4). The route is plausible for powders but less ideal for large single crystals.

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature, with TS-3 using 175–225 °C in a Teflon-lined Parr autoclave. No high pressure beyond autogenous pressure is required in TS-3, and no external controlled gas is specified. The main severity penalty is the likely use of HF/HPF₆ or related fluoride mineralizers, which are toxic/corrosive; HF-containing acidic solution chemistry is present in CS-5 and fluoride-rich hydrothermal media in TS-3. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence exists for RbIn₃F₁₀ hydrothermal synthesis, so this is an extrapolated route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution-mediated transport can be kinetically favorable, as fluoride-rich hydrothermal syntheses form crystalline products at low temperature in TS-3 and acidic fluoride solution crystallization occurs in CS-5. However, the structural-reconstruction score is low because TS-3 produces oxyfluoride/phosphate/hydrogen-containing phases, not anhydrous Rb–In fluorides. The target’s anhydrous framework may require dehydration and high fluoride activity that are difficult to maintain in water [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, hydrothermal kinetics are potentially good, but phase selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Rb⁺ coordination with smaller In³⁺ polyhedra, giving a substantial cation size mismatch as inferred from the much longer Rb–F distances and shorter In–F distances in the target description. The cell-size trigger is not established because the formula unit contains 14 atoms and the closest exact-composition reference is listed as Rb₂In₆F₂₀, i.e. 28 atoms (CS-1; SS-1). The connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but no face sharing in the target, and the closest analogue similarly emphasizes corner sharing plus limited edge sharing (CS-1; SS-1). Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbIn₃F₁₀ in the target Pmma structure is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state polymorph, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and an exact-composition RbIn₃F₁₀ polymorph has very low E_hull and experimental melt-growth evidence (CS-1; SS-1). The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis because the closest entry uses the identical RbF + 3 InF₃ chemistry and forms well-shaped crystals at 790 °C (CS-1; SS-1). The TS data are less chemically specific, but they show that fluoride phases with comparable metastability can be synthesized in sealed tubes or other controlled routes (TS-1; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a molten fluoride route that promotes diffusion and crystal growth (CS-1; SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control between closely related orthorhombic RbIn₃F₁₀ structures and avoidance of volatile/moisture-induced off-stoichiometry.
