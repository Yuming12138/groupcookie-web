### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom 90–100 range by the required rubric [Target]. This is consistent with many reference analogues being on or near the hull: ZrNCl, ZrNBr, Ti₅Al₂C₃, Ti₂PbC, and Ti₂TlC are reported with E_hull ≈ 0 [CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3, TS1, TS2, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low computed hull energy does not guarantee a broad synthetic phase field, as Nb₂S₂C and Ti₅Al₂C₃ analogues still show phase-control difficulty despite near-hull stability [CS4, CS5, SS1, TS1, TS3, TS4].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids the very high 1200–1500 °C conditions used for some MAX/carbide analogues [SS1, CS4, CS5, TS1, TS3, TS4] by using chloride topochemistry at a lower inferred temperature [Reasonable Inference]. However, it requires dry Ar/vacuum-like control because Nb₂S₂C synthesis specifically required avoiding oxidation of the layered framework [CS4, CS5, TS3, TS4]. The main severity penalties come from chloride volatility/corrosivity and multi-step handling, which are inferred from chloride chemistry and the repeated extraction/washing logic in Nb₂S₂C protocols [CS4, CS5, TS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable relative to direct solid-state synthesis because Ti₃AlC₂ supplies a preorganized Ti–C layered framework [SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Framework-preserving topochemical extraction is directly supported by Nb₂S₂C formation from Cu-containing layered precursors [CS4, CS5, TS3, TS4]. The uncertainty is whether Cl termination can be made fully ordered and stoichiometric rather than mixed with vacancies, O, OH, or residual Al-derived species [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses sealed-tube temperatures comparable to Ti₂PbC/Ti₂TlC syntheses at about 850 °C [SS2, SS3, TS2, TS5], so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty if kept below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires sealed containment because chloride precursors are volatile/corrosive [Reasonable Inference], and anhydrous Ti chlorides are moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides useful evidence that halide fluxes are viable [CS1, CS3], while SS/TS mainly support sealed carbide sintering rather than chloride handling [SS2, SS3, TS2, TS5].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction from TiC/Ti/TiClₓ has slow kinetics because TiC and Ti are refractory [Reasonable Inference], and structurally similar H-phase carbides often required very long sintering times of 600–900 h [SS2, SS3, TS2, TS5]. Chloride flux could improve transport, as NH₄X fluxes enabled Zr/Hf nitride halide formation [CS1, CS3]. The largest kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction: converting TiC/TiClₓ into a 2D Ti₃C₂Cl₂ sheet is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS, so evidence is incomplete and the score is conservative.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

High-pressure ZrNCl/ZrNBr analogues required 3–5 GPa, 900–1100 °C, controlled heating/cooling, quenching, and Au/Pt capsules [CS1, CS3]. These conditions are significantly more severe than standard tube-furnace synthesis but do not trigger the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty if the pressure is kept at 3–5 GPa [CS1, CS3]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct support for high-pressure chloride-flux synthesis of Ti carbides, so the score is primarily based on CS evidence with neutral structural/thermodynamic transfer beyond stability.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Reactive halide fluxes improve kinetics in the chemically similar Zr/Hf nitride halides [CS1, CS3]. High pressure may help stabilize a layered halide-terminated phase and enable crystallization at moderate temperature [CS1, CS3; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that the target’s Ti–C MXene-like sheet is structurally different from ZrNCl/ZrNBr, so the method may produce TiC, TiClₓ, or other chloride phases instead [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no mixed cation sublattice, so the cation radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The target’s reported P-6m2 2D sheet is a small-cell layered material rather than a >40 atom complex cell [Target]. The mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing CTi₆ octahedral connectivity triggers one amorphization risk factor [Target], but analogous layered carbides are experimentally accessible, suggesting the risk is not prohibitive [SS1, CS4, CS5, TS1, TS3, TS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₃(CCl)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar Ti–C layered/MAX phases [SS1, TS1] and topochemical layered carbide/chalcogenide syntheses that preserve a 2D framework [CS4, CS5, TS3, TS4]. Chemically similar halide analogues show that early-transition-metal halide layers can be synthesized using chloride/halide flux chemistry, including under high pressure when necessary [CS1, CS3]. The most critical enabling factor is use of a preorganized Ti–C layered precursor so that only extraction/termination chemistry is required [SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the exact Cl termination and avoiding competing TiC, TiClₓ, oxychloride, or mixed-termination products [CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5; Reasonable Inference].
