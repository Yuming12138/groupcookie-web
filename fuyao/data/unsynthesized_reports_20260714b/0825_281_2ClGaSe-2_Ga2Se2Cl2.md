### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0110 eV/atom is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so the base score is high. This is supported by TS analogues with similar E_hull values that have reported syntheses, such as MnGa₂Se₄ at 0.0132 eV/atom (TS-1), BaGa₄Se₇ at 0.0109 eV/atom (TS-2), and Li₂In₂GeSe₆ at 0.0171 eV/atom (TS-3). CS and SS give particularly strong support because GaTeCl, the Te analogue of the target halochalcogenide, is stable on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS-3, SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that a small positive hull energy can still indicate metastability relative to competing Ga–Se and Ga–Cl phases, but the value is low enough to be routinely accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild in temperature because the closest GaTeCl analogue forms at 400 °C for 7 days (CS-3, SS-5). The main penalties are controlled atmosphere/vacuum, volatile sealed-tube chloride chemistry, and air/moisture sensitivity, all indicated by inert-gas grinding and evacuated glass ampoules for GaTeCl (CS-3, SS-5). TS does not provide useful halide condition evidence, so TS is neutral 50 for the halide-specific severity component. The uncertainty is whether GaSeCl requires a slightly different temperature than GaTeCl because Se is smaller and may favor different Ga–Se binaries.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because GaTeCl forms from binary precursors under low-temperature annealing rather than high-temperature fusion (CS-3, SS-5). The use of GaCl₃ likely enables reactive liquid/vapor-assisted transport [Reasonable Inference], and the same GaQ₃Cl tetrahedral topology lowers the structural reconstruction penalty relative to unrelated chalcogenides (CS-3, SS-5). The caution is that related Ga₂TeSe₂ required very long annealing at 800 K to produce a new ordered phase (SS-2), so ordering of the Se analogue may not be instantaneous. Overall, Route 1 is kinetically the best-supported route.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Elemental sealed-ampoule syntheses are well represented for chalcogenides, including MnGa₂Se₄ by melt and anneal under vacuum (SS-4, TS-1), Cd₄GeSe₆ by direct fusion in evacuated quartz (TS-4), and Ga₂TeS₂ from elements in sealed silica under vacuum (CS-4). However, the target route includes GaCl₃ and Se, so sealed-tube volatility, inert handling, and possible repeated annealing add severity [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS support the general sealed-ampoule method but not direct elemental GaSeCl synthesis; therefore, direct evidence is weaker than for Route 1. The route is feasible but less preferred due to stoichiometry and pressure control.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Elemental Ga/Se reactions can be rapid if Ga becomes liquid and Se provides vapor transport [Reasonable Inference], and melt/anneal selenide routes are experimentally successful in SS-4, TS-1, and TS-4. However, forming GaSeCl from elements plus GaCl₃ requires nucleation of the layered halochalcogenide rather than simple substitution into an existing Ga–Se–Cl framework. Some analogues show long annealing or impurity formation, including CoPSe requiring weeks with intermediate grindings (CS-1) and Cd₄GeSe₆ giving a GeSe₂ impurity (TS-4). Thus, kinetics are acceptable but less controlled than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The vapor/film route avoids very high temperatures if operated near the GaTeCl annealing range of 400 °C (CS-3, SS-5). Nevertheless, it requires vacuum or controlled vapor conditions, volatile GaCl₃ and Se management, and probably post-deposition annealing [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS support vapor involvement indirectly through GaCl₃ ampoule chemistry and gas-phase reaction in Ga₂TeSe₂ formation (CS-3, SS-5, SS-2), but no direct thin-film source is available. Therefore, the severity score is limited by process complexity rather than temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Precursor vapor availability is plausible because GaCl₃ is used directly in GaTeCl ampoule synthesis (CS-3, SS-5), and gas-phase interaction is reported in Ga₂TeSe₂ synthesis (SS-2). However, crystallization into the correct layered Pmn2₁ GaSeCl phase is not confirmed by any CS/SS/TS thin-film analogue, so direct crystallization evidence is insufficient and treated cautiously. Composition control is the main kinetic/process challenge because both Se and Cl can be volatile [Reasonable Inference]. This route is plausible for exploratory films or crystals but not the most reliable bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple single-cation Ga framework and corner-sharing tetrahedral sheets, and the closest GaTeCl analogue is also a layered corner-sharing GaQ₃Cl tetrahedral material (CS-3, SS-5). No mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity is indicated for the target, while more complex mixed-polyhedra analogues in TS are less structurally relevant and provide insufficient amorphization signal. The small structural unit and lack of competing cation sizes make amorphization unlikely. Remaining uncertainty is that thin films in Route 3 may deposit amorphously before annealing, but that is route-specific rather than an intrinsic bulk amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaSeCl is most likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase, because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom and several TS analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally synthesized (TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest support comes from GaTeCl, which is both chemically and structurally very close and is synthesized from GaCl₃ + Ga₂Te₃ at 400 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS-3, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a direct metathesis-like binary precursor route, GaCl₃ + Ga₂Se₃ → GaSeCl, which should preserve the Ga–Cl and Ga–chalcogen tetrahedral chemistry [Reasonable Inference based on CS-3/SS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is control of volatile and moisture-sensitive chloride chemistry, especially avoiding GaCl₃ loss, hydrolysis, or competing Ga–Se binary formation. Overall, the material appears highly synthesizable by sealed-ampoule annealing, with moderate practical handling difficulty.
