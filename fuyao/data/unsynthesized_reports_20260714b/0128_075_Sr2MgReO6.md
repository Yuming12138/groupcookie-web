### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the upper boundary of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” high-stability category. The exact composition Sr2MgReO6 exists computationally as a nearly stable cubic phase with E_hull ≈ 0.0042 eV/atom and has reported synthesis conditions (CS1, SS1). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull near 0.06 eV/atom are still experimentally synthesized by solid-state or flux methods, supporting accessibility of phases in this energy range (TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether the triclinic P-1 target polymorph, rather than the cubic polymorph, is experimentally isolable under ambient conditions (CS1, SS1).

#### F2: Condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate-low F2 because it avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but requires dry Ar, vacuum sealing, ReO3 containment, and air-sensitive SrO handling (CS1, SS1). Route 2 scores better because it uses ambient pressure and no sealed final tube, although it requires multiple solution/thermal steps and a 1200 °C final anneal (CS3). Route 3 scores poorly because the closest high-pressure analogue uses 6 GPa and 1500 °C in a Pt capsule with oxidizer (CS2). Route 4 is similar in severity to Route 1, with added oxygen-potential control by getter/sealed tube based on Sr–Re oxide chemistry (SS2, SS3).

#### F3: Reaction kinetics

Route 1 has limited kinetics because MgO/SrO solid-state diffusion is slow, but exact Sr2MgReO6 forms after 24 h at 850 °C, proving kinetic feasibility (CS1, SS1). Route 2 has the best kinetics because citrate/nitrate chemistry gives molecular-scale cation mixing and analogous Sr2MReO6 phases crystallize after final inert annealing (CS3). Route 3 benefits from high temperature and pressure but still starts from relatively refractory oxides/metals, so its kinetic score is moderate rather than high (CS2). Route 4 is kinetically hindered by oxide diffusion and adds pO2 constraints; however, Sr/Re oxides do form at 800 °C in sealed tubes, so it remains plausible (SS2, SS3).

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large Sr A-site cation and smaller Mg/Re octahedral B-site cations, so the ionic-radius contrast check is triggered based on the target and double-perovskite analogues (Target; CS1, SS1). The target description does not establish a >40-atom cell, and the described connectivity lacks edge-sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. Double perovskites are generally crystallizable by solid-state routes in the references, which supports a low-to-medium amorphization risk rather than a high risk (CS1, CS3, SS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the triclinic distortion may indicate a narrower crystallization window than the cubic reference phase (CS1, SS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Sr2MgReO6 triclinic P-1 phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the dominant ground-state polymorph, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while the exact composition is reported as a nearly hull-stable cubic double perovskite (CS1, SS1). The analogue support is strong for the composition and double-perovskite framework, especially from exact Sr2MgReO6 and Ba2MgReO6 oxide syntheses (CS1, SS1, SS5). The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated stability of MgO6/ReO6 corner-sharing ordered perovskite frameworks at moderate temperatures under inert/sealed conditions (CS1, SS1). The main limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the known synthesis yields cubic Sr2MgReO6, whereas the target is lower-symmetry triclinic, so cooling rate, oxygen potential, strain, or precursor homogeneity may be critical [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS1, CS3]. Overall, the material is quite synthesizable, but isolating the specified triclinic polymorph may require optimization beyond simply reproducing the cubic reference synthesis.
