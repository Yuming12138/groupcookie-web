### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is listed with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin. CS supports low thermodynamic penalty because several chemically similar halides are at or near the hull, including CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, and CS-5. SS/TS provide additional support from SnBr₂ and bromostannate analogues near the hull, including SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-2. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not ensure that Ag/Sn ordering and the P-31m sheet topology will nucleate readily.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state / reactive melt

Route 1 has moderate severity because SS-3/SS-5 and CS-4 use evacuated ampoules, elevated temperatures, and multi-day anneals, while CS-4 specifically reports strong cooling-rate sensitivity. The kinetics are better than oxide solid-state synthesis because halide precursors are softer and more reactive, as reflected by bromide reactions at 250–550 °C in CS-2/CS-4/CS-5 and 400–470 °C in SS-3/SS-5. The limiting kinetic issue is structural reconstruction from AgBr + SnBr₂ into the 2D AgSnBr₃ sheet; no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates this exact topology.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: vapor-phase transport / CVT

Route 2 is conditionally feasible because SnBr₂ vapor-phase transport is strongly supported by SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-2, while CVT of bromide-containing compounds is supported by CS-3 and TS-5. Severity is lowered by moderate temperatures but penalized by vacuum sealing, volatile halides, and potential air sensitivity. Kinetics for crystallization are favorable once vapor transport occurs, but composition control is uncertain because AgBr transport is not demonstrated and TS-5 reports low yields/equilibrium mixtures in a complex halide CVT system. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for congruent Ag–Sn–Br vapor transport, so this is more of a crystal-growth follow-up than first-choice synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: hydrothermal / solvothermal HBr route

Route 3 has moderate severity because CS-1 uses concentrated hydrohalic acid in an autoclave at 220 °C for 5 d, which is chemically harsh but thermally mild. Kinetically, solution-mediated transport should be favorable, and CS-1 directly shows Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide solids can form under hydrothermal hydrohalic conditions. The main uncertainty is phase purity: CS-1 reports hydrothermal products as mixtures/intergrowths, and SS/TS provide insufficient hydrothermal evidence for Ag–Sn–Br, scored neutral for that specific factor. This route is preferred because it balances low temperature with direct Ag–hydrohalide precedent.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: solution precipitation / slow crystallization

Route 4 is mild and scores well on condition severity because TS-3 demonstrates room-temperature Sn(II) mixed-halide precipitation. However, the structural and phase-control score is low because TS-3 also reports stoichiometry-dependent mixtures, and no CS/SS/TS source confirms Ag–Sn–Br layered crystallization from solution. Physicochemically, AgBr’s low solubility and rapid precipitation may outcompete formation of ordered AgSnBr₃ [Reasonable Inference]. This route is useful for exploratory screening but is not the recommended primary route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization trigger is clearly present: mixed vertex and edge connectivity in the target structure, from the target description. The target appears to have a small formula cell and comparable Ag(I)/Sn(II) cation size, but these are [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly supported by CS/SS/TS. SS/TS analogues show crystalline low-dimensional Sn–Br frameworks are accessible, including SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-2, which lowers concern about unavoidable amorphization. Remaining uncertainty is that mixed Ag/Sn coordination may create competing disordered or glassy bromide phases during rapid precipitation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgSnBr₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of related soft halide frameworks by sealed ampoule, hydrothermal, and vapor transport routes, with the strongest direct Ag-containing hydrohalic precedent from CS-1 and the strongest Sn–Br framework precedent from SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-2. The most critical enabling factor is the soft, low-temperature bromide chemistry, which allows ion transport by melt, solution, or vapor without refractory diffusion barriers, as seen in CS-2 to CS-5 and SS-3/SS-5. The most critical limiting factor is phase control among AgBr, SnBr₂ polymorphs, and possible bromostannate side phases; cooling-rate and gradient sensitivity are documented in CS-4 and SS-1/SS-2. Overall, the material is synthetically plausible but will likely require careful bromide chemical-potential and stoichiometry control.
