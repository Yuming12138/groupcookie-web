### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives 100/100 under the required rubric. Several related indium sulfides in SS/TS are also on or very near the hull, including HgIn₂S₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (SS1/TS1), TlIn₃S₅ near 0 eV/atom (SS5/TS2), and TlIn₅S₆ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS3), supporting that indium sulfide frameworks can be thermodynamically robust. The uncertainty is that calculated thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic access to a hydrogen-containing sulfide; direct hydrosulfide synthesis evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for H₂S/sealed-atmosphere handling because SS1/TS1 explicitly uses H₂S or evacuated conditions for sulfide synthesis, and H₂S is toxic/volatile by **[Reasonable Inference]**. It avoids the very high temperatures used for many indium sulfide analogues in SS1/TS1 and TS3/TS5 because the target contains S–H bonds; the lower proposed temperature is a **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly sourced. The remaining uncertainty is whether low-temperature sulfiding gives sufficient crystallinity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, In₂S₃ is a chemically matched precursor supported by SS1/TS1 and TS5, so less cation redistribution is required than starting from elements. However, solid-state diffusion and rearrangement into a monoclinic 2D hydrosulfide sheet may be slow; related indium sulfides require long annealing in SS5/TS2, TS3, and TS5. The H₂S gas may enhance surface reactivity, but that benefit is a **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has mild temperature and no high-pressure penalty, consistent with solution-phase crystallization concepts in CS3/CS4, though those examples are halide complexes rather than indium sulfides. Penalties are applied for sealed solvothermal H₂S-generating chemistry, multiple operations, and toxic sulfide reagents; these are **[Reasonable Inference]** based on NaHS/thioacetamide chemistry. The uncertainty is that no provided source directly validates solvothermal InHS₂ crystallization.

#### F3 — Route 2

The reaction kinetics are favorable because soluble InCl₃ is a reactive indium source, supported by CS2, and sulfide release in solution avoids the long solid-state diffusion evident in TS3/TS5. Solution crystallization can, in principle, assemble low-dimensional protonated sulfide networks at lower temperature; this is **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether the product will crystallize as InHS₂ rather than amorphous indium sulfide or In₂S₃.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits the controlled sealed-ampoule and iodine volatility penalties from CVT methods in SS2/SS3. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, unlike high-pressure SS4, but still requires pre-reaction, transport, and controlled cooling. The use of H₂S or sulfur buffering to preserve H/S chemical potentials is **[Reasonable Inference]**, increasing practical severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Iodine CVT is effective for related layered indium sulfides, especially InGaS₃ and ZnIn₂S₄-type materials in SS2/SS3. However, the target contains an S–H bond, and no source shows that a protonated sulfide survives CVT; composition control is therefore difficult. Mixed phases and homogeneity concerns in SS3 and TS5 further justify a lower kinetic score.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The reported structure has corner- and edge-sharing InS₆ octahedra but no face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not triggered. Unit-cell size is not reported as >40 atoms, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to assume a large complex cell; therefore the amorphization score is high, though solution routes may still form amorphous In sulfide if sulfide is added too quickly.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InHS₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS analogues showing many indium sulfide frameworks can be synthesized in sealed ampoules, by CVT, or by high-temperature solid-state routes, while CS mainly supports general indium salt reactivity and solution crystallization rather than the target chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the close match between the target’s In–S octahedral distances and those in known indium sulfides such as HgIn₂S₄, InGaS₃, ZnIn₂S₄, and TlIn₃S₅ (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS3). The most critical limiting factor is preserving and ordering the S–H unit, because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports hydrogenated indium sulfide synthesis. The best practical route is therefore low-temperature solvothermal crystallization, with sealed H₂S/In₂S₃ sulfiding as a chemically direct backup.
