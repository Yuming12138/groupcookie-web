### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so by the required rubric it receives a high thermodynamic score. CS1/CS2/CS4/CS5 include Zr nitride halides/chalcogenide analogues with E_hull values at or near zero, supporting the plausibility of stable Zr–N–anion layered compounds. TS4 is especially relevant because it is an omega-derived nitride chloride with E_hull = 0. SS primarily contains binary nitrides such as ZrN/HfN and provides insufficient direct evidence for ternary chloride stability, so SS is neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is harsh because CS1/CS2 require multianvil high-pressure equipment and sealed capsules, even though the required-pressure deduction is not triggered because the rubric threshold is >5 GPa. CS1 uses 3 GPa, 900 °C, NH4Cl flux, controlled cooling, and quenching; CS2 uses 3–5 GPa and 1000–1100 °C for related Zr nitride halides. The main uncertainty is whether the target Zr2NCl2 requires the same high-pressure stabilization as β-ZrNCl or can form at lower pressure.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS1/CS2 show that NH4Cl/NH4X flux enables crystallization of Zr nitride halides at 900–1100 °C, which improves kinetics relative to direct refractory Zr nitridation. However, the target changes nitrogen coordination toward NZr6 octahedra, so conversion from ZrNCl is not purely topotactic. The kinetic score is therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

TS4 supports sealed niobium-tube synthesis at 850–950 °C, avoiding external high pressure but requiring welded Nb tubes, long heating, and strict exclusion of oxygen/moisture. The use of volatile/moisture-sensitive chlorides and possible NaN3 creates safety and handling penalties. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for ternary chloride sealed-tube synthesis and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

TS4 shows that omega-derived nitride chlorides can form from metal, metal chloride, and nitrogen sources in sealed tubes, but the reported durations are one to six weeks, indicating slow ordering and transport. Starting from elements or simple halides requires full framework construction, so structural reconstruction is more difficult than in a ZrNCl-derived route. The route is plausible but kinetically less favorable than Route 3.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CS3/CS4 provide a moderate-temperature metathesis precedent: 800 °C, 72 h, in vacuo, using ZrNCl. This avoids external high pressure but still requires sealed tubes, dry handling, and likely volatile/air-sensitive Zr chloride reagents by [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is therefore similar numerically to Route 2 but experimentally somewhat more accessible.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CS3/CS4 demonstrate that ZrNCl can react by solid-state metathesis at 800 °C, and CS5 shows NaCl flux accelerates formation of a Zr nitride chalcogenide at 900 °C. Starting with ZrNCl gives chemically matched local Zr–N–Cl motifs, improving precursor activity and structural continuity. The remaining uncertainty is whether a reductive chloride metathesis can cleanly produce Zr2NCl2 rather than ZrN, ZrNCl, or zirconium chlorides.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so no cation radius mismatch penalty applies. The described P-3m1 layered structure is simple and low-atom-count, unlike complex large-cell phases, and its polyhedral network contains edge and corner sharing but not face sharing. CS/TS analogues such as ZrNCl (CS1) and Sc4NCl6 (TS4) are crystalline nitride halides, supporting low intrinsic amorphization risk, although phase impurities remain possible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr2NCl2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although experimental accessibility may still depend on redox and chloride chemical potentials. The strongest support comes from CS: ZrNCl and related Zr nitride halides are experimentally synthesized, and ZrNCl undergoes metathesis reactions at 800 °C. TS4 adds important structural support because an omega-derived nitride chloride framework is known for Sc4NCl6. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of ZrNCl or volatile Zr chloride chemistry to deliver a preorganized Zr–N–Cl environment. The most critical limiting factor is phase control under reducing chloride-rich conditions, because competing ZrN, ZrNCl, and Zr chloride phases are likely [Reasonable Inference].
