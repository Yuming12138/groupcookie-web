### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high end of the rubric’s 90–100 range. This is supported by thermodynamic analogues Sr₂YSbO₆ and Ca₂LaSbO₆ with E_hull ≈0.052 and 0.050 eV/atom that are experimentally synthesized by solid-state routes (TS1, TS2). Chemically similar Ba₂BiSbO₆ polymorphs are also synthesized and lie on/near the hull (CS1–CS3), while Ba₂BiTaO₆ structural analogues are synthesized and low energy (SS1–SS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is not a simple Ba₂BB′O₆ perovskite but a Sr/La/Bi/Sb corundum-derived R3 phase, so low E_hull does not guarantee the exact ordered polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solid-state ceramic

Condition severity is moderate because analogous syntheses require 800–1100 °C, repeated regrinding, and long sintering but only standard air/O₂ furnaces and alumina crucibles/boats (CS1, CS2, TS1, TS2). Kinetics are moderate-to-slow because oxide/carbonate routes in CS2 and TS1 require 135–240 h or multiple regrinds, indicating diffusion and ordering limitations. The low-melting Bi/Sb oxides may assist reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS3 flux behavior], but La₂O₃/Sr carbonate homogenization remains a limitation. Insufficient evidence from SS for the exact Sr/La/Bi/Sb R3 phase formation by this route; SS is therefore neutral, 50, for exact target phase selectivity.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Soft-chemistry route

Condition severity is favorable because the final temperature can plausibly be kept below 1000 °C, while CS5 demonstrates multication oxide formation from citrate-derived homogeneous precursors at 850–900 °C. Kinetics are better than Route 1 because nitrate/citrate gels provide intimate cation mixing and reduce diffusion lengths, which is the explicit purpose of the CS5 soft-chemistry procedure. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS5 involves Fe/W perovskites under reducing atmospheres, not Bi/Sb/Sr/La corundum-derived R3 oxides. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for citrate synthesis of this exact R3 structure; this uncertainty is included in the structural-reconstruction subscore.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux-assisted synthesis

Condition severity is moderate because CS3 requires 1200 °C and slow cooling but uses standard alumina crucibles covered with Pt foil and no high pressure. Kinetics are favorable because flux growth bypasses long solid-state diffusion and directly produced Ba₂BiSbO₆ single crystals (CS3). However, flux composition and Sr/La incorporation are uncertain because CS3 used BaBiO₃ flux for Ba₂BiSbO₆ rather than Sr/La-containing flux. Insufficient evidence from TS for flux synthesis of Sr/La/Sb analogues; TS is neutral, 50, for flux transferability.

#### F4 — Amorphization / disorder tendency

The target has a large cation-size and bonding mismatch, as indicated by much shorter Sb–O bonds than Sr–O/La–O/Bi–O bonds in the target structure; this supports one amorphization/disorder trigger [Reasonable Inference from target bond lengths]. The target also explicitly mixes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, which increases topological complexity and triggers the mixed-connectivity check. Unit-cell atom count is not provided, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the >40 atom criterion, counted as not triggered but uncertain. This gives a medium disorder risk and F4 = 55.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrLaBiSbO₆ is likely a marginally stable or mildly metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and TS analogues strongly support bulk oxide synthesis in air/O₂ using standard ceramic methods, especially for Bi–Sb and Sr/La–Sb oxides (CS1–CS3, TS1–TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of chemically compatible oxide/carbonate/nitrate precursors and the demonstrated ability of related Bi/Sb and Sr/La/Sb oxides to form under conventional high-temperature conditions. The most critical limiting factor is achieving the specific Sr/La/Bi/Sb ordering and R3 corundum-derived connectivity without forming simpler perovskite, pyrochlore, or binary oxide impurity phases. A soft-chemistry route is therefore preferred because it improves cation homogeneity while avoiding excessive Bi/Sb volatilization.
