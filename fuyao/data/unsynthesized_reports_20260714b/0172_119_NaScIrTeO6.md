### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin. CS contains several stable or near-stable Na complex oxides, including CS1 at 0 eV/atom and CS4 at 0.0013 eV/atom, supporting chemical feasibility. TS contains experimentally synthesized oxides with E_hull values around 0.06–0.095 eV/atom, showing that phases slightly above the hull can be accessible (TS1–TS5). SS gives insufficient direct thermodynamic signal for NaScIrTeO₆ because the analogues are structurally similar but chemically different Os/Re oxides; neutral signal = 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
CS1 and CS4 show air-fired Na-containing oxide synthesis without high pressure or special gas handling, so condition severity is moderate rather than severe. The main deductions are multi-step firing/regrinding and the use of toxic/volatile Te/Ir-containing reagents, which are [Reasonable Inference] risks supported indirectly by CS4’s need for Na excess and SS2’s special handling of volatile OsO₄. Remaining uncertainty is whether Te or Na loss becomes serious enough to require sealing.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited because Sc₂O₃ and IrO₂ are relatively refractory [Reasonable Inference], whereas CS4 required 1050 °C and 16 h for an oxide/carbonate Na perovskite-derived phase. CS1 provides a favorable counterexample where Na–Te oxide chemistry proceeded at 973 K. The uncertainty is whether NaScIrTeO₆ has an accessible ordered phase field or instead forms binaries such as Sc₂O₃, Na–Te–O, and IrO₂.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
CS2/CS3 show that citrate processing uses ordinary solution equipment plus furnace annealing, not high pressure. It is multi-step and involves nitrate/chloride/Ir/Te handling, so it is not “easy.” SS provides no direct support for citrate synthesis and is scored neutral = 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
CS2/CS3 explicitly used aqueous citric acid and nitric acid to make homogeneous cation resins, which strongly improves precursor activity. The lower diffusion length should help overcome Sc/Ir sluggishness [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that the target’s Cm structure may require specific cation ordering and Te/Ir valence balance not demonstrated in CS2/CS3.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
TS1 and TS3 demonstrate hydroxide flux reactions at only 600–700 °C, so the temperature severity is low. However, sealed silver tubes, caustic flux, toxic Ir/Te reagents, and post-growth washing increase practical difficulty. CS provides only partial support through Te-containing oxide synthesis, while SS is insufficient for hydroxide flux and neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux synthesis benefits from a liquid transport medium, and TS1 directly demonstrates an Ir-containing double perovskite grown from KOH flux. TS3 further supports molten hydroxide flux as a route for Os oxides. The uncertainty is solubility and stoichiometry: Na and Te may partition into flux or wash out during product recovery [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
SS1–SS4 support high-pressure oxide synthesis as technically feasible but conditionally severe. The score is lowered by possible >5 GPa pressure, sealed oxidizing capsules, multi-step press operation, and toxic/oxidizing reagents. CS and TS do not indicate high pressure is needed for related Na/Ir/Te oxides, so this route is mainly a fallback.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
SS1 shows that high pressure plus an oxygen source can stabilize dense Os oxide frameworks at 900 °C. However, the reaction remains mostly solid-state with short diffusion lengths only if finely mixed, and the target chemistry differs from the SS analogues. The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes NaScIrTeO₆ or instead favors competing dense Ir/Sc/Te oxides.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large cation size contrast, inferred from the given Na–O distances being much longer than Ir–O and Sc–O distances, which triggers C1. The structure also contains multiple distorted coordination environments and edge-sharing motifs, triggering C3. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve it; this check is treated as insufficient evidence with neutral signal = 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaScIrTeO₆ is likely a marginally stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is near the usual experimental accessibility threshold. The CS analogues support ambient-pressure Na–Te and Na perovskite-derived oxide synthesis most strongly, while TS analogues support Ir-containing oxide formation and show that comparable metastable oxides can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is homogeneous multication mixing, best addressed by the citrate/soft-chemistry route supported by CS2/CS3. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Na/Te volatility and Ir/Te oxidation state, which is not directly proven in any single analogue. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance coming from a reactive precursor route followed by moderate-temperature air/O₂ annealing.
