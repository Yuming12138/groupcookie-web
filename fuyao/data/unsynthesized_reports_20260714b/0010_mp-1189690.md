### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the upper edge of the highest scoring stability bin. The exact CS/SS TaCoB₂ entry reports E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which is more favorable than the target value and suggests the discrepancy may reflect database/version differences. [CS1; SS1] TS analogues with E_hull values around 0.045–0.068 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, supporting accessibility of low-hull refractory borides. [TS1; TS2; TS3; TS4; TS5]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 loses points for >1000 °C synthesis, high vacuum, and multiple processing steps. [CS1; SS1] It does not require high pressure or exotic precursors, which keeps the severity moderate rather than extreme. [CS1; SS1] The main uncertainty is whether the optional 1400 °C homogenization step is always required for phase purity. [CS2; SS2]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta and solid-state diffusion, even though elemental powders are straightforward precursors. [CS1; SS1; Reasonable Inference] The exact target structure is already demonstrated, which strongly improves the structural-reconstruction component. [CS1; SS1] Remaining uncertainty is the number of grind/reanneal cycles needed to obtain a sharp, homogeneous XRD pattern. [CS2; SS2]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Arc melting requires controlled Ar/He atmosphere, very high effective temperatures, and repeated melting/annealing. [CS5; TS3; TS5] It avoids high pressure, but the high-temperature melt route increases severity relative to solid-state sintering. [TS3; TS5] Exact TaCoB₂-related sources caution that melting can produce heterogeneous samples, lowering confidence. [CS1; SS1]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Liquid formation improves mixing compared with dry solid-state diffusion, but Ta’s refractory nature and boron loss still constrain kinetics. [TS3; TS5; Reasonable Inference] Related arc-melted borides often contain side phases or majority-phase products rather than single-phase material. [CS5; TS3] The main uncertainty is whether post-annealing can fully transform melt-segregated products into Pnma TaCoB₂. [CS1; SS1; CS2; SS2]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth is penalized for 1600 °C operation, controlled Ar atmosphere, slow cooling, and post-growth separation. [TS2] It does not require high pressure, which prevents it from being extreme on the F2 scale. [TS2] Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for direct TaCoB₂ flux growth means their route-specific support is neutral at 50. [CS neutral; SS neutral]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux improves transport and can access single crystals not available by direct methods, but the evidence comes from a thermodynamic analogue rather than TaCoB₂. [TS2] Structural reconstruction is uncertain because no CS/SS source reports Pnma TaCoB₂ flux crystallization. [CS neutral; SS neutral] The largest uncertainty is whether Ta, Co, and B have a suitable solubility window in Cu/Al flux. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a relatively small 16-atom reported cell, which reduces amorphization risk. [CS1; SS1] A Ta/Co cation size mismatch plausibly triggers one amorphization check, but the exact source does not report complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity for TaCoB₂. [CS1; SS1; Reasonable Inference] Because only one check is triggered, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. [CS1; SS1]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaCoB₂ is likely at least near-ground-state and may be a borderline low-energy metastable phase if the target E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is used. [Target; CS1; SS1] The synthesis evidence is unusually strong because the exact material appears in both chemical and structural similarity sets with a high-confidence solid-state sintering route. [CS1; SS1] The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated Pnma TaCoB₂ formation from elemental Ta/Co/B under high vacuum with annealing. [CS1; SS1] The most critical limiting factor is slow homogenization/phase control in a refractory Ta–Co–B boride, especially because related notes indicate TaCoB₂ may require higher temperature for complete homogenization. [CS2; SS2] Arc-melting and flux routes are plausible backups, but they are less reliable because related melt routes report heterogeneity or side phases. [CS1; SS1; CS5; TS3]
