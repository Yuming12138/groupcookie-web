### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin [Target]. Stable analogues include AuBr with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS3, TS3), AuTeI with E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1), Au4Tl2S3 with E_hull = 0.0 (SS2, TS2), and Te3(PdBr)4 with E_hull = 0.0 (SS5, TS4). CS contains some less favorable BiSe analogues with E_hull ≈0.27 eV/atom (CS4, CS5), so the chemical-neighbor evidence is not uniformly positive, but the target’s own hull stability dominates. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed Pmma AuSe phase is the experimentally accessible low-temperature polymorph or requires kinetic stabilization.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The proposed conditions are moderate in temperature because Au-containing analogues form at 650 °C in sealed quartz (SS2, TS2), and selenides can be made in evacuated quartz containers (TS5). The main penalties are the vacuum/sealed environment and Se volatility/toxicity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the use of evacuated silica tubes in CS3 and TS5. No high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is required by analogy to SS2/TS2. The uncertainty is whether Au and Se react completely without flux because Au powder can be kinetically sluggish [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetics are moderate because Au powder is dense and noble [Reasonable Inference], though Au powder does react in sealed-tube Au4Tl2S3 synthesis (SS2, TS2). Diffusion is helped by operating near 500–650 °C and by possible transient Se vapor/liquid transport [Reasonable Inference], but the target layered ordering likely requires multi-day annealing as in SS2/TS2 and TS5. Structural reconstruction from elemental Au and Se is significant because no same-structure precursor is identified in CS/SS/TS. Regrinding may be needed, consistent with AuBr decomposition requiring frequent powdering (CS1, CS2).

#### F2 — Route 2: Halide/salt flux growth

Flux growth requires sealed tubes, dry salts, slow cooling, and flux removal, so the route is operationally more complex than direct solid-state reaction. The temperature range of 650–780 °C is supported by Au4Tl2S3 synthesis at 650 °C (SS2, TS2) and BiSe-related flux growth at 780 °C (CS4, CS5). Vacuum-sealed carbon-coated fused-silica tubes are directly supported by CS4/CS5. The main uncertainty is possible halide incorporation or reaction with Au, since Au–halide phases are stable in the AuBr analogues (CS1, CS2).

#### F3 — Route 2: Halide/salt flux growth

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten salt improves mass transport and supports crystallization of layered sheets, as shown for BiSe analogues in LiCl/KCl flux (CS4, CS5). Au-containing chalcogenide crystallization with controlled cooling also gave single-phase material in SS2/TS2. Structural reconstruction remains only partially supported because no CS/SS/TS entry gives binary AuSe in the same Pmma structure. Remaining uncertainty is whether Au dissolves sufficiently in chloride flux without forming Au chloride/halide complexes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Iodine-assisted CVT / mineralization

CVT is more severe than flux because it requires pre-reaction, controlled I2 loading, sealed evacuated tubes, and a thermal gradient. CS3 supports iodine-assisted CVT in evacuated silica tubes for layered selenide-related crystals, but at high temperatures of 800–850 °C. The proposed lower range is a [Reasonable Inference] to reduce Se loss and Au–I side-phase formation. The biggest uncertainty is chemical selectivity: AuTeI forms under iodide-rich conditions (SS1, TS1), indicating iodide may incorporate.

#### F3 — Route 3: Iodine-assisted CVT / mineralization

Kinetics are favorable for mass transport because I2 can generate volatile species, as demonstrated by CS3. Diffusion limitations are therefore lower than in bulk solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural and compositional control are less certain because Au–Te–I chemistry forms a stable layered AuTeI phase (SS1, TS1), and Au–Br phases are also stable (CS1, CS2). The route is therefore plausible for crystal growth but risky for phase purity.

#### F2 — Route 4: Low-temperature decomposition route

The decomposition route is inspired by AuBr formation from Au2Br6 at 40–55 °C under dry N2 or vacuum (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4, TS3). It avoids high temperature, but it is penalized for controlled atmosphere, likely volatile Se loss, multi-step precursor preparation, and hazardous Au/Se precursor chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no direct Au–Se decomposition precedent, so evidence for this route is weaker. The route is best treated as exploratory rather than primary.

#### F3 — Route 4: Low-temperature decomposition route

A reactive higher-valent Au–Se precursor could improve local reaction kinetics, analogous to Au2Br6 decomposition to AuBr (CS1, TS3). However, AuBr analogues show sluggish phase transitions, poor reproducibility, and phase-control difficulty (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4, TS3). Because the target Pmma AuSe sheet structure may require long-range reorganization, decomposition may yield poorly crystalline or multiphase products [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate but not high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary AuSe phase with no cation-size mismatch because Au is the only cation [Target]. The described structure has two Au sites and one Se site, with linear and 4-coordinate bonding but no explicit large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [Target]. Crystalline layered analogues are commonly obtained by hydrothermal, flux, CVT, and sealed-tube methods (SS1, SS2, CS3, CS4, CS5), which argues against strong amorphization tendency. Remaining uncertainty is whether rapid quenching or low-temperature decomposition could produce poorly crystalline Au–Se material, especially in Route 4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuSe is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of Au-containing low-dimensional chalcogenide/halide phases by sealed-tube, flux, hydrothermal, CVT, and decomposition methods, with several analogues also lying on the convex hull (CS1, SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube or flux environments that control volatile chalcogen activity and promote layered crystal growth, as demonstrated by BiSe flux growth and Au4Tl2S3 sealed-tube synthesis (CS4, CS5, SS2, TS2). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Au readily forms stable halides/iodides and multiple low-dimensional motifs, as shown by AuBr and AuTeI analogues (CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1). Overall, flux growth under carefully controlled Se chemical potential is the best first attempt.
