### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom directly places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues are mostly hull-stable Tl/Zn/Cd/Sb/As-related phases, supporting possible ground-state stability in chemically adjacent pnictide systems [TS1–TS5]. CS gives supportive but indirect evidence because CS phases are also hull-stable intermetallics, though most lack Tl/As/Be chemistry [CS1–CS4]. SS is mixed: SS1 is a slightly metastable high-pressure ZnAs polymorph, while SS3/SS4 are hull-stable, so SS is less decisive than the target hull value [SS1, SS3, SS4].

#### F2 — Route 1

The sealed direct route avoids >1000 °C conditions, unlike some higher-temperature TS1/TS3 analogues, but requires sealed inert/vacuum handling due to Tl and As volatility [TS1, TS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Toxic Be/Tl/As lowers the severity score even though the equipment is standard for pnictide chemistry [TS1, TS4]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for BeTlAs volatility management, so it is treated as neutral for this hazard beyond general inert synthesis precedent [CS1–CS4].

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental precursors are common in TS/SS/CS solid-state syntheses, but Be metal is kinetically sluggish due to high melting point and surface oxide [TS1, TS4, CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Liquid Tl at reaction temperature may improve transport, which is why the diffusion score is not extremely low [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing is expected because TS1/TS2/CS2/CS4 show 24 h to 10 day timescales for related compounds [TS1, TS2, CS2, CS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc/induction melting uses controlled Ar and high localized temperatures, matching CS arc-melting protocols [CS1, CS2, CS4]. The target’s Tl/As content adds a volatility penalty absent or less severe in the Th–Ni–Si CS analogues [Reasonable Inference; CS1–CS4]. Multi-step pre-reaction and annealing are needed to make the route credible for volatile pnictides [Reasonable Inference; TS3].

#### F3 — Route 2

Melting improves homogenization and is successful for several CS intermetallics after annealing [CS1–CS4]. However, Be–Tl–As is not represented directly in CS, and open arc melting risks off-stoichiometry from Tl/As loss [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are better than sealed powder reaction, but composition control is worse [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route operates at lower temperatures similar to TS2/SS4 low-temperature arsenide synthesis, which reduces thermal severity [TS2, SS4]. It still requires sealed containment, controlled atmosphere, and toxic Tl/As/Be handling [TS2, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Decanting or separating toxic Tl-rich flux adds process complexity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

A Tl-rich melt should provide superior mass transport and partially bypass Be solid-state diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. TS2/SS4 demonstrate long, low-temperature, slow-cooling sealed syntheses for related arsenides, supporting this approach [TS2, SS4]. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, because TS2/SS4 report side products and crystallinity problems in similar arsenide systems [TS2, SS4].

#### F2 — Route 4

High pressure imposes the largest severity penalty because SS1 requires 6 GPa multianvil synthesis for structurally similar ZnAs [SS1]. The route also retains sealed-capsule volatility and toxicity concerns for Tl/As/Be [SS1; Reasonable Inference]. Because the target is hull-stable, high pressure is a fallback rather than the preferred route [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

SS1 shows high-pressure synthesis can form a structurally related arsenide quickly at 1273 K, which supports reaction kinetics under pressure [SS1]. However, SS1 also reports difficult phase control, metastability, and optimization challenges, lowering the structural-reconstruction score [SS1]. The high-pressure route is therefore useful for screening but not ideal for scalable synthesis [SS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The strongest amorphization/defect risk is the large size mismatch between Be and Tl, which can create strain and ordering challenges [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s P4/nmm layered structure is likely a small-cell crystalline phase, so large-cell glass-forming complexity is not indicated [Target]. The target includes corner- and edge-sharing BeAs₄ tetrahedra but no face-sharing coexistence, so only one F4 trigger is counted [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeTlAs is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The thermodynamic analogues give the strongest support, since several Tl/Cd/Zn/Sb/As-related pnictides are hull-stable and synthesized from elements in sealed ampoules [TS1–TS5]. The chemically similar CS set supports general intermetallic melting/annealing practice but is less directly relevant because it mostly lacks Tl–As volatile pnictide chemistry [CS1–CS4]. The most important enabling factor is use of sealed Tl-rich or Tl–As flux conditions to retain volatile Tl/As while enhancing Be transport [Reasonable Inference; TS2/SS4]. The main limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control, because related arsenides can show side products, narrow phase windows, or disorder [TS1, TS2, SS4].
