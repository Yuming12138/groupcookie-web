### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.029 eV/atom, which falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. TS analogues with comparable hull energies, such as CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom and CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized (TS4, TS5). CS includes both very stable Cd₂AsCl₂ at 0.0027 eV/atom and more metastable but reported halide/oxyhalide phases (CS1, CS5), supporting that low-hull Cd halide chemistry is accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee the specific ordered Br/Cl Pmm2 layer rather than halide-disordered CdX₂-derived phases.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 sealed ampoule.**  
The severity score is reduced mainly because evacuated sealed ampoules are required, as in SS5 and TS5, and because Cd halides are toxic and volatile/subvolatile at the proposed temperature. Kinetically, the route is favorable because CdCl₂/CdBr₂ are reactive halides, not refractory oxides, and related halide compounds form at 320–450 °C in CS3, SS5, TS3, and TS5. The main uncertainty is whether the ordered 1:1 Br/Cl layer forms directly or requires long annealing/slow cooling for halide ordering.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 CVT/sublimation.**  
CVT is strongly supported by Cd₂AsCl₂ synthesis from CdCl₂-containing mixtures at 693/673 K (CS1) and by multiple structurally similar halide CVT syntheses (SS1–SS3). The method is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile halides, toxicity, and likely multi-step gradient optimization. Kinetically, vapor availability is good, but Br/Cl composition control is uncertain because SS1–SS3 report low yields and equilibrium mixtures in halide CVT systems.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 solution crystallization.**  
This route has mild conditions because CS4 and TS2 show halide crystallization by slow evaporation or aqueous room-temperature crystallization. The kinetic mobility of solvated Cd²⁺/halides should be excellent, but structural reconstruction into an anhydrous 2D CdBrCl sheet is the weak point. CS4 specifically involves ammoniacal solution and yields a different Cd–N–Br compound, so the evidence is useful for precursor solubility but not definitive for CdBrCl.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 self-flux.**  
Flux growth can improve kinetics, and CS2 demonstrates flux growth for a chemically similar halide/oxide at 450 °C. The route is penalized for molten toxic Cd halides, sealed/dry handling, and post-growth separation steps. Kinetically it is attractive because a molten halide medium should accelerate ordering, but CS2 also reports phase-control difficulty, so the route is best used for crystal growth after Route 1 establishes the phase.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, a small formula, and corner-sharing tetrahedral connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers is met from the target structure. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for CdBrCl, so their direct signal for F4 is insufficient and treated as neutral source evidence. Physicochemically, simple ionic halides usually crystallize readily if water/complexation is excluded, but halide disorder remains possible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CdBrCl is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.029 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from TS analogues with similar hull energies that are experimentally accessible by sealed-tube or hydrothermal routes (TS3–TS5), and from CS/SS examples showing Cd/halide and layered halide compounds can form under moderate sealed-ampoule or CVT conditions (CS1, CS4, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of anhydrous, reactive CdCl₂/CdBr₂ precursors in a sealed system, which should maintain halide stoichiometry and provide sufficient mobility. The most critical limiting factor is Br/Cl ordering versus formation of residual CdCl₂/CdBr₂, hydrated salts, or halide-disordered phases. Overall, the target appears reasonably synthesizable, with sealed-ampoule annealing as the best first attempt.
