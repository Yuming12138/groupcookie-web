### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin and receives 98/100. This is reinforced indirectly by many similar bromides being reported on or near the hull: **CS-1**, **CS-4**, **TS-1**, **TS-2**, **TS-3**, **TS-4**, and **TS-5** all have E_hull = 0.0, while **CS-3** and **CS-5** are only slightly above hull. Insufficient evidence from **SS** for thermodynamic stability as a class, because some structurally similar entries such as **SS-1** and **SS-3** have high E_hull despite being reported compounds.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state bromination / annealing

The severity score is penalized because evacuated sealed ampoules are required, as in **CS-3**, **CS-4**, **CS-5**, and **TS-3**, and Br₂ use introduces volatile/toxic sealed-system hazards, as in **CS-1**, **SS-4/TS-1**, and **TS-4**. Temperatures are moderate compared with oxide solid-state synthesis; **CS-3** uses 300 °C and **TS-3** uses 700 K, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Kinetically, binary halides are reactive, but Pt/Ag ordering into a 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet may be slower than simple precipitation; **TS-3** warns that stoichiometric deviations can produce by-products.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Chemical vapor transport / vapor-assisted growth

CVT severity is moderate because **SS-4/TS-1**, **TS-4**, **CS-5**, and **SS-2** show that sealed bromide vapor transport is practical but requires controlled gradients and volatile halogens. Kinetically, CVT can grow good crystals, directly supported by PdBr₂ CVT in **SS-4/TS-1** and Ti(AlBr₄)₂ transport in **CS-5**. The main uncertainty is whether Pt and Ag bromides transport congruently; **TS-3** indicates mixed-cation bromides may be sensitive to stoichiometry, so composition control is scored only moderate.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Oxidizing hydrobromic-acid hydrothermal / solvothermal synthesis

This route scores best in severity because it uses lower temperatures and avoids vacuum ampoules or high-temperature halogen transport; acid solution precipitation of bromides is supported by **SS-1**, and hydrohalic noble-metal crystal growth by **SS-5/TS-2**. Kinetically, solution/hydrothermal media eliminate most long-range solid diffusion barriers and can rapidly assemble halide frameworks, as in **SS-1**. The key uncertainty is chemical speciation: Pt may remain as discrete [PtBr₆]²⁻ complexes and Ag may precipitate as AgBr rather than forming the extended PtAgBr₆ sheet [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Low-temperature bromide flux growth

The severity score is reduced by sealed ampoule operation, possible Br₂ use, and flux handling, consistent with sealed/vacuum conditions in **CS-2**, **CS-3**, and **CS-4**. Kinetically, a molten bromide flux should improve ion mobility compared with direct solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], paralleling low-temperature flux growth in **CS-2**. However, **CS-2** explicitly reports mixed phases and unsuccessful pure-phase isolation for AuCuSe₄, so phase control is a significant uncertainty.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Pt–Br versus Ag–Br bond-length mismatch, suggesting a significant cation-size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference], so C1 is triggered. The target description indicates a relatively simple PtAgBr₆ sheet with edge-sharing octahedra, not a large-cell or mixed-connectivity framework; insufficient evidence from **CS/SS/TS** supports triggering C2 or C3. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high, because layered halide frameworks are repeatedly crystallized in **SS-1**, **SS-3**, **SS-5/TS-2**, and **TS-3**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PtAgBr₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The reference set supports synthesis broadly: **CS-3** supports sealed binary-halide annealing, **SS-4/TS-1** and **TS-4** support Br₂-assisted bromide CVT, and **SS-1** plus **SS-5/TS-2** support low-temperature hydrohalic crystallization. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high bromide chemical potential, which is repeatedly important in bromide syntheses using HBr, Br₂, or sealed halide ampoules in **SS-1**, **CS-1**, **SS-4/TS-1**, and **TS-4**. The most critical limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control of the Pt–Ag bromide network, because mixed-cation halides can form by-products under small stoichiometric deviations as shown in **TS-3**, and Ag may reduce or precipitate separately [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with hydrothermal HBr screening recommended first and sealed-ampoule/CVT routes as stronger options for anhydrous phase confirmation.
