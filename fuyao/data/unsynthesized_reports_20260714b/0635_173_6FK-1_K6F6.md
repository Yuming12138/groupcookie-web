### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0360 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 scoring bin and indicating it is only mildly metastable [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues include pressure-accessible RbF at E_hull ≈0.0516 eV/atom [TS1] and low-hull fluoride phases such as Rb₂KInF₆ and Cs₂KMnF₆ [TS2; TS4]. CS and SS evidence are also supportive because chemically and structurally related fluorides are reported at low hull energies or experimentally synthesized [CS1; SS1–SS4]. The uncertainty is that low E_hull does not identify the required pressure/temperature field for the specific tetragonal P4/mmm KF polymorph [Target; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses high pressure, but the closest analogue RbF transforms between 9–15 kbar, below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty threshold [CS1; SS3; TS1]. It still requires specialized high-pressure equipment and precise pressure control, which the analogue explicitly notes through high-pressure X-ray work [CS1; SS3; TS1]. Dry handling is necessary because related fluoride syntheses use glove boxes, dry Ar, and water contents below 5 ppm [SS2; SS4]. The remaining uncertainty is whether KF needs heating, quenching, or a different pressure than RbF to form the tetragonal layered phase [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because the route starts from the same chemical composition, KF, analogous to direct high-pressure conversion of anhydrous RbF [CS1; TS1]. Ion diffusion should be easier than in multication refractory oxides because only K and F rearrange [Target; Reasonable Inference]. However, the structural change from common alkali-halide coordination to the target’s mixed KF₅/KF₆ layered topology is likely reconstructive [Target vs CS1/TS1]. This reconstruction uncertainty lowers the kinetics score despite favorable chemical simplicity.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids bulk high pressure and uses temperature/atmosphere regimes directly supported by dry fluoride syntheses: 700 °C for Cs₂CaF₄ [SS2], 400–700 °C in Ni/Ag bombs [TS3], and dry Ar heating for K₃GdF₆ [SS4]. The penalties come from sealed-system handling, multiple operations, and moisture-sensitive fluoride processing [SS2; SS4; TS3]. The route is less severe than high-pressure synthesis in equipment demand but more vulnerable to volatilization or recrystallization into stable KF [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that no source reports ambient-pressure formation of tetragonal KF [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The precursor is kinetically active because it is already KF and because analogous fluoride solid-state reactions use simple binary fluorides [SS2; SS4; TS3]. Similar fluoride systems react or crystallize over days at 400–700 °C or 700 °C [SS2; TS3], suggesting ion mobility is adequate. The weakness is structural selectivity: heating a pure binary compound may simply equilibrate to the stable KF polymorph rather than the target layered P4/mmm phase [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control difficulties in related fluorides, such as controversy/decomposition for K₃GdF₆ and pure-phase issues in double fluorides, support this caution [SS4; TS3].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 requires vacuum deposition and likely volatile alkali/fluoride flux control, so it receives penalties for controlled atmosphere, volatility, multi-step processing, and sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no direct thin-film evidence, so source support is neutral at 50 for this route [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Dry handling requirements are still supported indirectly by fluoride solid-state analogues [SS2; SS4]. The uncertainty is large because KF thin-film growth chemistry is not established in the provided sources.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Epitaxy could help stabilize the target because the structure is two-dimensional and low in energy above hull [Target; Reasonable Inference]. However, precursor vapor availability and composition control are difficult for alkali fluorides because K/F volatility can produce off-stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct crystallization of similar thin films, so crystallization-pathway evidence is neutral and weak [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. This gives the lowest route kinetics score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **metastable but low-energy phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom rather than zero [Target]. The strongest support comes from the direct alkali-fluoride pressure analogue RbF, which forms a high-pressure polymorph from anhydrous RbF between 9–15 kbar [CS1; SS3; TS1]. Structurally and thermodynamically similar fluoride compounds demonstrate that dry fluoride handling, sealed capsules, and controlled atmospheres are standard and effective for crystallizing fluoride phases [SS2; SS4; TS3; TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of **pressure or epitaxial/kinetic trapping** to access a non-ambient KF topology [CS1/TS1; Target; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is **phase selectivity**, because no provided source directly confirms tetragonal P4/mmm KF and ambient annealing may revert to the stable KF polymorph [Target; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].
