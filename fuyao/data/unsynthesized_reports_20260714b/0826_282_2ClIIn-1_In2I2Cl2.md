### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E above hull is 0.0190 eV/atom, which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS analogues with similar small positive hull energies, such as TS1 at 0.0259 eV/atom, TS2 at 0.0170 eV/atom, TS3 at 0.0167 eV/atom, and TS4 at 0.0285 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized, supporting that a small positive hull energy does not preclude isolation. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves InICl is a true ground state, so it is best treated as near-stable/metastable.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
CS1 strongly supports mild mixed-halide precipitation for SnICl, and SS5 supports low-temperature crystallization of Ga halide ribbon phases from organic solvent under Ar. The condition score is reduced because dry atmosphere and corrosive/volatile HI or iodide reagents are likely required; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms aqueous InICl stability. Kinetics should be favorable in solution, but phase purity may be limited by indium redox/disproportionation.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
SS2 is the strongest indium-specific support because reduced indium bromides are prepared from InBr₃ + In shot in sealed ampoules; SS1 and SS3 further support group-13 halide ribbon formation at 250–450 °C. The route requires vacuum/dry Ar and volatile, moisture-sensitive halides, giving moderate condition severity. Kinetically, halide melts/vapors should accelerate transport, and the structural match to 1D ribbon halides is favorable.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
TS5 shows iodide-rich hydrothermal synthesis in HI at 155–175 °C, and CS1 shows HI can drive mixed-halide precipitation. However, TS5 also reports crystal twinning/low yield, and insufficient evidence from TS supports indium chloride–iodide hydrothermal stability. The kinetics are favorable because solvothermal transport is rapid, but hydrolysis and redox control are significant uncertainties.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
CS5 demonstrates melt/Bridgman growth of an indium mixed chalcohalide at 520 °C, and TS3 reports Bridgman growth for a thermodynamically similar iodide. The method is penalized for likely sealed-ampoule requirements, volatile halides, and multi-step crystal growth. The major uncertainty is whether InICl melts congruently or decomposes/sublimes before useful crystal growth.

**F2/F3 — Route 5.**  
CS3/CS4/TS4 support CVT or vapor-transport growth of halide-containing low-dimensional crystals, while SS2 directly shows vacuum sublimation can improve indium bromide crystal quality. The route is kinetically attractive because volatile halides provide vapor transport, but condition severity is higher due to sealed ampoules, gradients, and possible transport agents. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms congruent vapor transport of InICl.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a low-dimensional 1D ribbon compound, and CS1, SS1, SS3, and SS5 show that related low-dimensional halide ribbons crystallize rather than forming amorphous solids. One amorphization concern is possible mixed-valent In(I)/In(III) size mismatch, inferred from the target’s two inequivalent In sites and average In oxidation state +2. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has a >40-atom cell or highly mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral network, so those checks are not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InICl is likely a **near-stable metastable phase** rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E above hull is only 0.0190 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well: CS1 gives a directly related SnICl mixed-halide ribbon, SS2 provides indium reduced-halide ampoule chemistry, and SS1/SS3 show structurally similar group-13 halide ribbons. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of indium halides under sealed-ampoule conditions, which should permit equilibration at only 250–350 °C. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry/redox control, because InICl likely requires an average In oxidation state near +2 and may compete with InCl, InI, InCl₃, InI₃, or mixed-valent indium halides. Overall, the target appears realistically synthesizable, with sealed-ampoule comproportionation as the most robust first attempt.
