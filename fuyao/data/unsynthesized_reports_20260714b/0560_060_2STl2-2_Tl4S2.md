### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.034 eV/atom lies in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so the baseline thermodynamic score is high. TS1 reports a synthesized Tl chalcogenide at E_hull = 0.037 eV/atom, TS3 reports one at 0.0136 eV/atom, and TS5 reports one at 0.0235 eV/atom, showing that similar near-hull Tl chalcogenides can be experimentally realized. The remaining uncertainty is that the target may be a metastable polymorph of Tl₂S rather than the equilibrium binary phase **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to SS1 and SS3, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions, volatile sulfur, multiple staged steps, and Tl toxicity; these are consistent with sealed evacuated syntheses in SS1, SS3, SS4, and TS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any need for high pressure, so high pressure is scored neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because Tl is low-melting and sulfur is reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, and SS1/SS3 show Tl sulfides can react at 350–500 °C. However, structural reconstruction from elements to a specific layered Pmmn polymorph is not guaranteed; SS3 and SS4 require long annealing, implying ordering can be slow. The score is therefore good but not exceptional.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, sulfur volatility, multi-step melt growth, and Tl toxicity. These requirements are directly supported by CS1, CS4, SS1, SS5, and TS5, all of which use sealed ampoules and controlled cooling or Bridgman-type growth for related chalcogenides. No >1000 °C or >5 GPa penalty is applied because the relevant analogues operate below those extremes.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt growth offers the best kinetic pathway because liquid transport reduces diffusion limitations; this is supported by successful Tl/chalcogenide melt growth in CS1, SS5, and TS5. Slow cooling is a proven strategy in SS1 and TS5, and layered chalcogenides such as Bi₂Se₃ are grown by Bridgman methods in CS4. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the target’s E_hull = 0.034 eV/atom suggests it may require controlled crystallization rather than simple equilibrium annealing.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has milder temperatures than Routes 1–2 because TS2 demonstrates solvothermal Tl chalcogenide synthesis at 433 K. Penalties remain for sealed vessels, multiple processing steps, and Tl toxicity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS supports solvothermal Tl₂S specifically, so the lower severity does not automatically imply higher reliability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution chemistry gives highly active precursors and avoids long-range solid-state diffusion; TS2 supports this for a Tl chalcogenide. The main kinetic weakness is structural crystallization: a binary Tl₂S precipitate may form rapidly as amorphous or another polymorphic phase **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore, despite favorable precursor activity, the structural reconstruction score is only moderate-low.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, so ionic-radius mismatch between cations is not relevant. The formula and structural description do not indicate a large unit cell or complex mixed connectivity; the target is described as edge-sharing STl₆ units in a 2D sheet. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any glass-forming multication complexity for binary Tl₂S, so the amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Tl₂S phase is likely metastable or weakly metastable because its energy above hull is 0.034 eV/atom, not zero, but this value is within the experimentally accessible range demonstrated by thermodynamic analogues in TS1, TS3, and TS5. The CS/SS/TS data strongly support sealed-ampoule Tl chalcogenide synthesis, especially melt/Bridgman and slow-cooling approaches in CS1, SS1, SS5, and TS5. The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility of Tl–chalcogen systems, evidenced by Tl sulfide analogues forming at 350–500 °C in SS1 and SS3. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph/phase control, because the target is a specific 2D Pmmn Tl₂S structure rather than merely any Tl₂S composition. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best practical route being controlled melt or self-flux growth in an evacuated sealed ampoule.
