### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability band by the supplied rubric. Thermodynamic analogues K(As₂O₃)₂Br/I, ZnTlAsO₄, CuPbAsO₄, and SnPO₃F are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [TS1–TS5], which supports the possibility that related low-dimensional oxide frameworks are genuine ground states. CS evidence is mixed but still favorable: B₄SnO₇ is on hull [CS4], while BAsO₄ and CuAs₂O₄ are only slightly above hull [CS1, CS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee an accessible hydrate under laboratory water activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature compared with solid-state or high-pressure routes: related arsenic oxide phases form near 200–227 °C [CS3, SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. The main deductions arise from sealed-vessel handling of volatile/toxic arsenic species and arsenic precursor toxicity [CS3, CS5, SS2, TS1]. There is no evidence for >5 GPa pressure, reactive gas, or >1000 °C heating in this route [CS3, SS2, TS1]. Uncertainty is mainly whether As(III)/As(V) redox/speciation remains fixed during hydrothermal treatment [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated transport allows crystallization at low T in several arsenic oxide analogues [CS3, SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. The target’s As–O distances match known As(III) oxide units and As(V) tetrahedra in the references, supporting assembly from As₂O₃/H₃AsO₄ building blocks [CS3, SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. The penalty comes from structural ordering: related hydrothermal syntheses can yield byproducts such as claudetite-I As₂O₃ or multiphase mixtures [SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. Thus kinetics are good but phase selection is not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest severity because SS1 shows room-temperature crystallization of a structurally similar protonated 1D phosphate in aqueous solution/vacuum desiccator conditions. It avoids high temperature, high pressure, and reactive atmospheres [SS1]. The only major severity deduction is arsenic precursor toxicity, inferred from the arsenic reagents used in CS3/CS5 and the proposed As₂O₃/H₃AsO₄ chemistry [CS3, CS5, Reasonable Inference]. Direct CS and TS evidence for ambient crystallization of the arsenic-only target is insufficient, so that evidence is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution crystallization offers excellent mass transport, analogous to the SS1 protonated ribbon crystallization route. The target is low-dimensional and protonated, so the SS1 structural analogy is meaningful, but the chemical substitution P/Tl → mixed-valence As-only framework is substantial [SS1, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient CS/TS evidence exists for the exact room-temperature mixed As(III)/As(V) crystallization pathway, so phase selection uncertainty remains. This is why F3 is high but not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but it requires sealed handling because arsenic oxides and water can generate toxic vapor/pressure [CS5, Reasonable Inference]. It is also more procedurally complex than simple hydrothermal mixing because it involves grinding, drying, sealing, heating, and cooling [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 supports the need to manage arsenic volatility in heated arsenate syntheses. SS and TS give insufficient direct evidence for this sealed low-temperature dehydration path, so they are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state or semi-solid dehydration has weaker kinetics than solution routes because diffusion and homogenization are limited at 120–250 °C [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 demonstrates high-temperature arsenate formation but at 600–900 °C, which is not compatible with retaining OH in the target [CS5, Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction into ordered P2₁ ribbons is therefore uncertain. High-pressure synthesis is supported for several analogues but is severe: BAsO₄ uses 8.0 GPa and 1108 K [CS1], B₄SnO₇ uses 7.5 GPa and 1100 °C [CS4], and ZnTlAsO₄ uses 20 kbar and 1400 °C [SS3, TS2]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull [TS1–TS5]. The strongest synthesis support comes from hydrothermal arsenic oxide/arsenate analogues, where As₂O₃-containing systems crystallize at ~200–227 °C in acidic or acetate media [CS3, SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated transport, which can assemble low-dimensional As(III) oxide and HAsO₄ units at temperatures low enough to preserve OH [SS1, CS3, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with As₂O₃ polymorphs, other arsenic oxides, or overoxidized arsenates, as byproducts are reported in related hydrothermal systems [SS2, TS1, SS4, TS3]. Overall, the material appears highly synthesizable, especially by slow solution crystallization or mild hydrothermal methods, but arsenic speciation and water activity must be tightly controlled.
