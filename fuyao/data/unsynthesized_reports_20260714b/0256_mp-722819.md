### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom cutoff, so F1 = 90 rather than a higher 95–100 because it is boundary-stable. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally synthesized: Na₂PdC₂ at 0.045 eV/atom [TS1], CsMnAs at 0.0556 eV/atom [TS2], Na₂PtC₂ at 0.0574 eV/atom [TS3], and KMnAs at 0.0547 eV/atom [TS5]. CS supports feasibility of alkali/main-group binaries but mostly for phosphides at E_hull = 0 [CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5], not K–C specifically. SS is less decisive because some structurally similar entries have large E_hull or indirect synthesis assignments [SS1, SS5], so SS gives insufficient direct evidence for KC thermodynamic stability and is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate solid-state temperatures, below 1000 °C, consistent with K–P syntheses at 400 °C to 870 K [CS4, CS5], so no high-temperature penalty is applied. A sealed ampoule and volatile K impose a −10 penalty, supported by sealed Nb/Ta/glass ampoules in K–P reactions [CS4, CS5]. Air-sensitive K handling imposes another −10, consistent with glovebox and strict impurity control in K-rich phosphides and acetylides [CS4, SS2, SS3]. Insufficient evidence from TS indicates any need for high pressure, so high-pressure scoring is neutral/no deduction.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

K is reactive and molten at low temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**, but carbon/graphite is kinetically inert, so precursor activity is only moderate. Alkali pnictides form in sealed ampoules at moderate temperatures [CS4, CS5], suggesting K transport can be favorable, but there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that carbon will diffuse or react rapidly to rocksalt KC. Structural reconstruction is nontrivial because elemental carbon must become a monoatomic rocksalt sublattice; the existence of halite RbGe [SS5] and halite PRb [CS2] gives structural analogy but not direct K–C conversion evidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits vacuum/controlled-atmosphere penalties because CsNaC₂ is heated under vacuum [SS2] and Na₂PdC₂/Na₂PtC₂ analogues require Ar and air exclusion [SS3, TS1, TS3]. The use or prior preparation of K₂C₂ creates a multi-step penalty, because acetylide precursors are themselves specialized and air/moisture-sensitive [SS2, SS3]. The route avoids high temperatures and high pressures, consistent with 200–350 °C acetylide reactions [SS2, SS3, TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS for acetylide-to-rocksalt KC conversion keeps the severity assessment from being more favorable.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has better precursor activity because alkali acetylides are preactivated K–C compounds and have reacted at low temperature in several analogues [SS2, SS3, TS1, TS3]. Diffusion should be relatively favorable due to low reaction temperatures and intimate pelletized mixtures, supported by grinding/pelletizing procedures [SS2, SS3]. The main kinetic penalty is structural: acetylide C–C units persist in the reported analogues [SS2, SS3, TS1], whereas target KC would require monoatomic carbon sites **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore F3 is higher than Route 1 for activity but limited by reconstruction uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperature and high pressure, but requires UHV or vacuum deposition, giving a controlled-atmosphere penalty supported by K/N LT-ABD [SS4]. It is multi-step because deposition, warming, and possible annealing/transfer are required [SS4]. Air sensitivity is penalized because K/N metastable samples are oxygen/moisture sensitive [SS4], and related alkali acetylides/phosphides also require glovebox/inert handling [CS4, SS2, SS3]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/TS for vapor deposition of comparable KC, so the route remains challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

K vapor delivery is feasible by atomic-beam deposition, supported by the K/N LT-ABD source [SS4], but carbon incorporation and stoichiometric control are extrapolated **[Reasonable Inference]**. Crystallization is uncertain because SS4 reports metastability, decomposition below 263 K, and poor crystallinity for K/N phases, which suggests low-temperature routes can trap phases but not necessarily yield well-crystallized products. Composition control is likely narrow due to volatile K **[Reasonable Inference]**, and K-rich phosphide formation already shows sensitivity to K excess and side reactions [CS4]. Thus the vapor route is a plausible metastable-access strategy but not the best bulk synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple Fm-3m rocksalt structure with one cation and one anion site, so it does not trigger cation-radius mismatch or large-cell complexity. The target description contains edge- and corner-sharing octahedra but no face sharing, so the specified mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met. Structurally simple rocksalt-like analogues appear in CS/SS, including PRb [CS2] and RbGe [SS5], supporting low topological frustration, though those are not direct KC syntheses. Remaining uncertainty comes from unusual K–C bonding rather than amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KC in rocksalt Fm-3m is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold, and thermodynamic analogues around 0.045–0.057 eV/atom have been synthesized [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5]. The CS and TS data strongly support sealed, inert, alkali-rich solid-state chemistry as a feasible synthesis environment [CS4, CS5, TS2, TS4], while SS provides useful but indirect structural and kinetic analogies from alkali carbides/acetylides and halite RbGe [SS2, SS3, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high potassium chemical potential in a sealed, oxygen-free system, as K excess and strict impurity control are important in K-rich phosphides [CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is competing K–C chemistry, especially acetylide or graphite-containing products rather than a monoatomic rocksalt carbon sublattice **[Reasonable Inference]**, with no direct CS/SS/TS report of binary rocksalt KC. Overall, the target is synthetically plausible but should be treated as a challenging metastable/near-stable phase requiring careful phase control.
