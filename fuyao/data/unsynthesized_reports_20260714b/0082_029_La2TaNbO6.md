### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring range specified by the prompt. CS includes experimentally synthesized oxides with comparable or slightly higher E_hull, such as CS4 at 0.054 eV/atom and CS3 at 0.0985 eV/atom, so CS supports metastable synthesizability. SS/TS also include synthesizable Nb perovskites near this range, including SS2/TS5 at 0.0589 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not resolve the apparent redox/charge ambiguity of La₂TaNbO₆.

#### F2 — Route 1, air solid-state

CS1, CS2, and CS5 show that La/Ta-containing oxides can be prepared in air at high temperature, so the route does not require exotic atmosphere. However, CS2 requires 1400 °C for 8 days and SS/TS Nb perovskites require similarly harsh temperatures, so high thermal severity and multi-step processing are penalized. CS gives insufficient evidence for making a reduced La–Ta–Nb O₆ phase in air, which is the main uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 1, air solid-state

The kinetic score is low because refractory Ta₂O₅/Nb₂O₅ and La₂O₃ require long-range diffusion, consistent with long high-temperature treatments in CS2 and SS3/TS2. The target framework requires assembly of ordered TaO₆/NbO₆ octahedra from binary oxides, which is a complete structural reconstruction. Air may instead stabilize oxidized La–Ta–O and La–Nb–O impurity phases; CS gives insufficient evidence for avoiding this issue in La₂TaNbO₆.

#### F2 — Route 2, reducing/inert solid-state

SS1/SS4/TS1 and SS3/TS2 directly support vacuum or Ar sealed-tube methods for reduced Nb perovskites, but those conditions are more severe than air firing. The route requires high temperature, sealed containers, controlled oxygen fugacity, and likely metal powders, all of which lower F2. CS provides insufficient evidence for low-valent Ta/Nb La oxides, so the transferability relies mostly on SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2, reducing/inert solid-state

The route is structurally/redox relevant because SS1/SS4/TS1 form EuNbO₃ perovskites using Nb/NbO₂/Nb₂O₅ under vacuum, and SS3/TS2 form SrNbO₃ in Ar ampoules. Kinetics remain difficult because Nb/Ta oxides and metals are refractory, and SS3/TS2 report 20–130 h anneals. Ta reduction and B-site ordering introduce additional uncertainty not directly addressed by SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3, wet-chemical route

CS5 supports chemical co-precipitation using La nitrate and TaCl₅, pH ≈ 9 precipitation, and high-temperature heating. The route is multi-step and may use moisture-sensitive chlorides, so it is not easy, but it can avoid some severity of direct refractory oxide reaction. If the O₆ phase requires reduction, SS/TS evidence implies an added Ar/vacuum post-anneal.

#### F3 — Route 3, wet-chemical route

Wet-chemical mixing gives the best precursor activity because CS5 uses co-precipitated hydroxy-carbonates for complex tantalates. This should reduce diffusion length for La/Ta/Nb and improve cation homogeneity relative to Route 1. However, final crystallization still requires a major reconstruction into a perovskite-like oxide, and CS5 notes metastable/phase-control issues for complex tantalates.

#### F2 — Route 4, flux-assisted route

SS5 supports flux/container methods for Nb oxyframeworks, but it is an oxyfluoride and not a La–Ta–Nb oxide. Flux processing may reduce temperature, but sealed containers, halide/fluoride fluxes, and possible air-sensitive components make the route operationally demanding. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for direct La₂TaNbO₆ flux growth.

#### F3 — Route 4, flux-assisted route

Flux can improve mass transport, as implied by SS5 using Sn flux for a Nb compound. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that La₂O₃, Ta₂O₅, and Nb₂O₅ dissolve compatibly in a flux without incorporating flux ions. The structural mismatch between SS5 and the target lowers confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large La A-site and much smaller Ta/Nb octahedral B-sites, so the radius-difference check is triggered; analogous A/B size contrast is common in CS2 and SS3 perovskites. The target connectivity is mainly corner-sharing TaO₆/NbO₆ octahedra, so mixed edge/face octahedral connectivity is not triggered. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a marginally stable or low-energy metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS supports high-temperature La–Ta oxide synthesis, while SS/TS strongly support reduced Nb perovskite synthesis under vacuum or Ar, but no source directly demonstrates La–Ta–Nb O₆ formation. The most important enabling factor is the robust tendency of Nb/Ta to form octahedral oxide frameworks, supported by CS tantalates and SS/TS niobate perovskites. The most important limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control: the nominal La₂TaNbO₆ formula is difficult to reconcile with ordinary La³⁺/Ta⁵⁺/Nb⁵⁺ chemistry, so air synthesis may favor oxidized competing phases. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful precursor homogeneity and oxygen-fugacity control.
