### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted at **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, placing it on the convex hull and justifying a score of 100. CS2 and CS3 show chemically similar transition-metal fluorides can also be hull-stable, while SS/TS contain multiple E_hull = 0.0 fluoride analogues. However, SS/TS analogues are often Au-containing complex fluorides rather than binary CuF₂, so their thermodynamic relevance is supportive but chemically indirect. If CS/SS/TS are scored separately for this factor: CS = 90, SS = 85, TS = 85; none are neutral because all provide at least some stable fluoride examples.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures based on SS1’s 773 K fluoride reaction and CS3’s sealed-crucible metal fluoride heating. It avoids high pressure and F₂ gas, but dry sealed handling is required because SS1 reports air-sensitive fluoride reagents/products. The largest penalties are for air-sensitive fluoride handling and multi-step tube preparation. TS gives insufficient direct signal for this route, so TS is neutral at 50 for this condition assessment.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetic advantage is that CuF₂ is already the target composition, minimizing long-range cation interdiffusion. SS1 demonstrates CuF₂ can participate in solid-state fluoride chemistry at ~500 °C, and CS3 shows binary fluorides can crystallize from sealed thermal treatment. The uncertainty is polymorph selection: if commercial CuF₂ is structurally different, the Pmma sheet phase may require reconstructive rearrangement or pressure.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CS1 provides the main evidence: a chemically similar Ni fluoride was synthesized at **800 °C and 50 kbar**, implying severe equipment demands. Temperature is not extreme, but pressure near 5 GPa, capsule loading, and dry fluoride handling make the route challenging. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure binary CuF₂, so SS is neutral at 50; TS only indirectly supports controlled fluoride syntheses, not high-pressure CuF₂.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

High pressure can accelerate or enable polymorphic rearrangement, and CS1 shows a transition-metal fluoride reaction completed in 2 h at high pressure. Since CuF₂ is already compositionally correct, the kinetic challenge is mainly structural transformation rather than cation diffusion. Remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports high-pressure Pmma CuF₂, so the pressure window may require screening.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

TS5 supports mild hydrothermal temperatures of 100–230 °C in Teflon-lined vessels, so thermal severity is low. The route is penalized for HF toxicity, sealed autogenous pressure, and multi-step recovery/washing. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for hydrothermal anhydrous CuF₂ synthesis, so they are neutral at 50 for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

TS5 shows hydrothermal HF conditions can grow Cu–F crystals at low temperature, giving favorable ion-transport kinetics. The major kinetic/phase-selectivity concern is that TS5 produced Cu hydroxyfluorides and noted poor phase control/byproduct identification, meaning water activity can redirect crystallization away from anhydrous CuF₂. Thus, despite fast solution transport, structural selectivity is scored only moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

TS1/SS2 provide the basis for F₂/N₂ fluorination at 320 °C followed by sealed annealing at 580 °C for weeks. Although the temperature is moderate, F₂ gas, sealed Au tubes, dried Ar transfers, and long annealing make the route hazardous and operationally severe. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for CuO/Cu fluorination to CuF₂, so CS is neutral at 50 here.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

TS1/SS2 show that gas fluorination can form Cu fluoride phases, but the reported process required multiple steps and 4–5 weeks of annealing. Starting from CuO or Cu requires complete anion/cation coordination reconstruction, so kinetics are worse than direct CuF₂ annealing. The route is useful mainly for generating very high fluorine chemical potential or repairing partially hydrolyzed CuF₂.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so there is no cation size-mismatch trigger. The described target structure has corner- and edge-sharing CuF₅/CuF₆ units but no reported face-sharing, and there is no evidence of a very large unit cell. Therefore, amorphization risk is low; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so they are neutral at 50 for this subfactor, while the target structure itself drives the score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CuF₂ phase is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. CS data support that simple transition-metal fluorides can be stable and synthesizable, while SS1 is especially useful because it uses CuF₂ directly in air-sensitive sealed-tube fluoride synthesis. TS analogues show that Cu-containing fluoride phases can be made under fluorinating or hydrothermal conditions, but they also warn that phase control can become difficult when F₂/HF, water, or complex cations are involved. The key enabling factor is the availability of an anhydrous binary precursor, CuF₂, which eliminates cation-ratio complexity. The key limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: the Pmma two-dimensional CuF₂ structure may not be the default product of simple annealing and may require controlled cooling, high pressure, or seeding.
