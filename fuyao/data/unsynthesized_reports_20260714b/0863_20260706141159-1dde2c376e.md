### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. Thermodynamic analogues TS1–TS5 are all listed as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and structurally similar analogues SS1–SS5 are also ground-state entries. The only negative thermodynamic signal is CS2 with E_hull ≈0.299 eV/atom, but it is chemically less relevant because it is an oxy-sulfide/antimony-rich composition rather than an Rb–Hg–Ge sulfide. Remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic: the predicted ground state may still require a narrow sulfur/Rb/Hg chemical-potential window [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because comparable sulfide-flux syntheses occur at 400–500 °C or ~873–893 K in sealed silica tubes (CS1, CS5, SS4, TS4). Severity comes from vacuum/inert handling, sulfur-rich sealed tubes, air-sensitive Rb sulfide/polysulfide fluxes, and toxic Hg chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by CS5/SS4 glovebox flux practice]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Hg vapor pressure behavior in this target, so that component is treated as a conservative risk.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Sulfide/polysulfide fluxes provide favorable kinetics by dissolving/transporting chalcogenide species, as shown by the successful growth of RbGeBiS4 and K2PbGe2S6 analogues (CS5, SS4, TS4; CS1, SS1, TS1). The target contains common GeS4 tetrahedra and alkali–sulfide coordination, which transfer well from the analogues. Structural uncertainty remains because the target has HgS4 tetrahedra and a P2_12_12_1 orthorhombic framework, while the closest analogues are monoclinic or contain Pb/Bi instead of Hg (CS1, CS5, SS4).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Halide flux growth is moderate in severity because RbTaGeS5 was made at 750 °C for 72 h in vacuum-sealed silica tubes, not high-pressure or ultra-high-temperature conditions (CS3). Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, flux drying, volatile sulfur, and toxic Hg [Reasonable Inference from CS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS3 exists on whether RbCl alone can supply the Rb stoichiometry for Rb2HgGe3S8; this uncertainty lowers confidence.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
RbCl flux demonstrably promotes crystallization of an Rb–Ge–S compound with Rb–S and GeS4 motifs (CS3). However, the analogue’s TaS6 octahedra differ from the target’s HgS4/GeS4 tetrahedral framework, so structural transferability is only partial (CS3). The halide flux should improve diffusion, but composition control for Hg and Rb in a chloride flux is less directly evidenced than in sulfide flux [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Solid-state sealed ampoule synthesis uses accessible temperatures of 550–850 °C and standard tube furnaces in analogous Ge sulfides (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). The main penalties are controlled atmosphere, sealed-tube volatility, regrinding/annealing, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a direct solid-state Rb–Hg–Ge–S synthesis, so the severity score remains conservative.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solid-state analogues can form complex Ge sulfide frameworks, but they require long heating, controlled atmospheres, intermittent grinding, or careful cooling (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). Compared with flux routes, bulk diffusion and homogenization are less favorable, especially with Rb/Hg volatility and multiple cations [Reasonable Inference]. Phase purity is a concern because analogous solid-state syntheses report disorder or secondary phases in some systems (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3).

**F4 — Amorphization / complex-ordering tendency.**  
The target has very different cation sizes and coordination environments: large 7–9 coordinate Rb, tetrahedral Hg, and tetrahedral Ge, which supports one amorphization/ordering-risk trigger [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The likely large unit cell also triggers complexity risk, while the absence of face-sharing polyhedra prevents the strict mixed-connectivity trigger. The main uncertainty is whether flux growth bypasses amorphous intermediates; analogous flux syntheses yield single crystals, which mitigates this risk (CS1, CS5, SS4, TS4).
