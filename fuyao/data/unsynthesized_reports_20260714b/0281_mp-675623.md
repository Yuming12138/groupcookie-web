### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the boundary of the most favorable scoring bin. Experimental synthesis of analogues with similar positive E_hull supports feasibility: CsSeO₄-related material has E_hull = 0.041 eV/atom and is grown by aqueous evaporation (CS1), KBrO₄ has E_hull = 0.0327 eV/atom and is crystallized from solution (SS4/TS1), and TlBrO₄ has E_hull = 0.0520 eV/atom and is grown by slow evaporation (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is whether the listed tetragonal P4₂/nmc CsClO₄ phase is the room-temperature equilibrium polymorph or a low-lying metastable polymorph; the small hull energy suggests either is accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is very mild because close analogues are grown from saturated aqueous solution at room temperature or constant temperature: KClO₄ in SS1, KBrO₄ in SS4/TS1, and KNO₃ in TS3. Cs oxyanion salts are also grown from aqueous solution at 303 K or room temperature in CS1 and CS4. No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates a need for high temperature, high pressure, reactive atmosphere, or sealed apparatus, so this route receives the maximum F2 score.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because dissolution gives ionic-scale mixing and crystallization does not require solid-state diffusion; this is supported by saturated-solution crystallizations in SS1, SS4/TS1, and TS3. The target structure contains discrete ClO₄ tetrahedra and Cs–O coordination, analogous to KClO₄ and KBrO₄ in SS1 and SS4/TS1. The main uncertainty is polymorph selection for the tetragonal CsClO₄ target, but slow crystallization generally improves ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses mild solution chemistry similar to carbonate + oxyacid routes in CS1, CS2, and TS5. The severity penalty arises from the hazardous oxidizing nature of perchloric acid [Reasonable Inference] and the multi-operation sequence of neutralization, filtration/concentration, and evaporation, analogous to the recrystallization sequence in CS2. No high pressure, high temperature, or controlled atmosphere is supported by CS1, CS2, or TS5.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Neutralization of Cs₂CO₃ with HClO₄ should rapidly form solvated Cs⁺ and ClO₄⁻, analogous to carbonate neutralizations forming alkali oxyanion salts in CS1, CS2, and TS5. Since the product forms from solution, ion diffusion is not limiting, as indicated by room-temperature or 303 K crystallizations in CS1, CS2, and TS5. The remaining uncertainty is avoiding acid excess or inclusion of mother liquor in crystals [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Temperature-controlled crystallization remains mild because related materials crystallize at room temperature to 40–60 °C in CS3 and SS3. CS3 reports protective gas for barium hydroxide chemistry to prevent CO₂ contamination, but this is not directly required for CsClO₄ when using pre-formed salt, and SS1/SS4/TS1 show no controlled atmosphere for perhalate salts. The score is slightly lower than Route 1 due to added hot filtration and controlled cooling steps.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

This route benefits from solution-mediated transport, supported by aqueous crystallization of KClO₄, KBrO₄, and Sr chlorate analogues in SS1, SS4/TS1, and SS3. Controlled cooling can reduce nucleation density and improve crystal quality [Reasonable Inference], consistent with temperature-controlled crystallization in CS3. The uncertainty is whether the cooling path selectively yields the tetragonal P4₂/nmc polymorph rather than another CsClO₄ polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ionic oxyanion salt with isolated ClO₄ tetrahedra, comparable to alkali perchlorate/perhalate salts in SS1 and SS4/TS1, which argues against strong amorphization tendency. Only the large size/charge-center contrast between Cs⁺ and the Cl-centered tetrahedron triggers an amorphization-related check [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large unit cell or complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing framework in target CsClO₄, so the amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CsClO₄ phase is likely a low-energy, readily accessible phase, but because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, it may be either a near-ground-state or a mildly metastable polymorph. The analogue support is strong: KClO₄, KBrO₄, KNO₃, CsSeO₄-related material, and CsSO₃ all crystallize from aqueous solution under mild conditions according to SS1, SS4/TS1, TS3, CS1, and CS4. The most important enabling factor is the solution accessibility of simple alkali oxyanion salts, which removes solid-state diffusion barriers. The main limiting factor is polymorph control for the tetragonal P4₂/nmc target rather than merely obtaining crystalline CsClO₄. A secondary limitation is safe handling of perchlorate salts and especially perchloric acid if Route 2 is used [Reasonable Inference].
