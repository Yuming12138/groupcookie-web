### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic bracket of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS analogues with similar hull energies, including CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom and Ag₃CuS₂ at 0.0388–0.0462 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized (TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS gives mixed but useful support: AuCl/AuBr are stable in databases but show metastability and phase-control concerns experimentally (CS-1, CS-5), while layered PbS polymorphs near 0.058 eV/atom are accessible by CVT (CS-3, CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that the Au–S binary phase field may favor Au metal plus other sulfides under some conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, direct sealed-tube solid-state sulfuration

The condition severity is moderate because evacuated ampoules are required, as in AuPS₄ and Ag–Cu sulfide analogues (SS-3, TS-2, TS-5), and sulfur is volatile in sealed systems [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main limitation because Au metal is dense and relatively inert [Reasonable Inference], so forming an ordered 2D AuS sheet from elements likely requires long annealing or regrinding. SS-3 shows Au/S direct reaction is possible at 600 °C, but TS-3 and SS-5 show that sulfide ordering can require weeks. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for binary AuS solid-state synthesis, so CS is neutral for this specific route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, iodine-assisted CVT

CVT has good mechanistic support from CS: AuCl is transported by chlorine species at 247–280 °C (CS-1), and layered PbS-related sulfides use iodine or chlorine transport at high temperature gradients (CS-3, CS-4). The route has moderate-to-high condition severity because it uses vacuum sealing, volatile I₂/S, and corrosive halogen chemistry. Kinetically, vapor transport can bypass slow Au diffusion, but phase selectivity is uncertain because Au halides are themselves stable/metastable competitors, and CS-1/CS-5 explicitly show gold-halide phase-control difficulty. SS gives indirect support through 2D AuTeI and Au–S local motifs but insufficient evidence for AuS CVT specifically.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, hydrothermal / solvothermal iodide-mineralized synthesis

Hydrothermal synthesis is the best-scoring route because solution transport should overcome the poor solid-state reactivity of Au [Reasonable Inference], and SS-1 demonstrates hydrothermal growth of 2D AuTeI plates from Au/Te/I in HI. SS-4/TS-1 further shows hydrothermal hydrohalic-acid sulfide chemistry can yield high-quality crystals with no observed side products. Severity is moderate because corrosive HI and sulfide donors require sealed pressure equipment, but no high temperature, high vacuum, or GPa pressure is required. The largest uncertainty is whether iodide remains only a mineralizer rather than entering the final Au–S structure, since SS-1’s product is an iodide-containing phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, flux growth

Flux growth is supported by SS-2, where Au₄Tl₂S₃ with Au–S 2.32–2.34 Å and Au–Au 3.07 Å was grown from Au powder, Tl₂S, and S at 650 °C. This is locally very relevant because the target also has Au–S 2.31–2.40 Å and Au–Au 3.20 Å. The route is kinetically favorable relative to direct solid-state synthesis because liquid/flux transport can lower diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. However, the direct SS-2 flux contains Tl, and using Tl₂S would risk forming ternary Au–Tl–S phases rather than binary AuS; therefore an alkali halide/sulfide flux is recommended by extrapolation rather than direct evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Au, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The described Cmme structure has two inequivalent Au sites and one S site, with no indication of a large >40 atom unit cell or highly mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing polyhedral network. SS and TS analogues include crystalline 2D or low-dimensional sulfides and chalcogenides rather than amorphous products (SS-1 to SS-3, TS-1 to TS-5), supporting low amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is that metastable layered phases can crystallize only under narrow kinetic windows [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuS is likely a **near-ground-state metastable phase**, because its energy above hull is low at 0.0360 eV/atom but not zero. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: SS provides strong local Au–S bonding analogues, especially Au₄Tl₂S₃ and AuPS₄, while TS shows that sulfides with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible. The most critical enabling factor is **transport-mediated crystallization**, either by hydrothermal iodide mineralization, halogen CVT, or flux growth, because these routes bypass slow diffusion through metallic Au. The most critical limiting factor is **phase selectivity in the Au–S–halogen/redox space**, since CS gold halides show metastability and sluggish phase behavior, and no direct binary AuS synthesis is provided. Overall, the material appears synthesizable but likely requires careful control of sulfur activity, Au redox state, and cooling/crystallization conditions.
