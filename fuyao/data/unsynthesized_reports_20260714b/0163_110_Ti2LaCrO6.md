### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability window. TS gives strong evidence that phases at 0.050–0.075 eV/atom can be synthesized, including Nd₃ReO₇ [TS-3], Na₂Ca₂Ta₃O₁₀ [TS-1], Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ [TS-4], and Ca₂Ta₂O₆F [TS-5]. SS also supports this with Sm₃ReO₇ and Ho₃ReO₇ near 0.05 eV/atom [SS-3, SS-4]. CS is mixed: some chemically similar phases are stable and easily made [CS-1, CS-5], while others require high pressure despite higher hull energies [CS-2, CS-3].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because analogues often require high temperatures above 1000 °C and repeated grinding/refiring [CS-5, SS-4, TS-3]. Controlled atmosphere is likely needed because the target oxygen count suggests reduced Ti/Cr chemistry rather than a fully oxidized Ti⁴⁺/Cr⁶⁺ assemblage [Reasonable Inference], and reducing/inert atmospheres are used in SS-5 and sealed-tube syntheses in SS-3/SS-4/TS-3. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct La–Ti–Cr oxide synthesis, so the oxygen potential window is inferred rather than demonstrated.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are expected to be slow because Route 1 uses refractory binary oxides, and similar solid-state systems require multiple regrindings or long anneals [CS-5, SS-4, TS-3]. Complete structural reconstruction from La₂O₃/Ti₂O₃/Cr₂O₃ to triclinic P1 LaTi₂CrO₆ is required [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS support feasibility of such reactions, but they also indicate that phase purity may require repeated thermal cycling [SS-4, TS-3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth retains penalties for high temperature, sealed/controlled atmosphere, and volatile or corrosive halide flux handling [SS-2, TS-5]. It avoids high-pressure penalties and may be less severe than ceramic synthesis in terms of diffusion, but still requires careful containment. Evidence is strong for flux growth of structurally similar rare-earth oxides [SS-2], while direct chemical similarity to La–Ti–Cr remains insufficient from CS.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves reaction kinetics through liquid-mediated transport, and SS-2 directly shows single crystals of a structurally similar complex oxide obtained using NaCl flux. Slow cooling should help cation ordering and crystallization of the low-symmetry target [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that Ti/Cr redox stability in chloride flux is not validated by CS/SS/TS, so oxygen buffering may be necessary.

#### F2 — Route 3

High pressure causes the largest severity penalty because CS-2 and CS-3 require 10–15 GPa apparatus, gold capsules, graphite furnaces, MgO pressure media, and WC anvils. Although the temperature is only about 1000 °C in those examples [CS-2, CS-3], equipment complexity and capsule handling make this route challenging. SS/TS mostly show ambient-pressure access to related oxides, so high pressure is not the first-choice route [SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3].

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can stabilize dense or unusual frameworks and enabled chemically similar complex oxides in CS-2 and CS-3. However, short 30 min–2 h high-pressure runs may not allow complete La/Ti/Cr ordering, especially from refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic score is therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large La cation and much smaller Ti/Cr cations, triggering the radius-mismatch check; similar large A-site/small B-site frameworks occur throughout SS/TS analogues [SS-1, SS-3, TS-2]. The structure also contains multiple coordination environments, similar to mixed polyhedral networks reported in CS-1, SS-3, SS-4, and TS-3. The unit cell atom count trigger is not applied because the target description corresponds to a 10-atom formula description, and evidence for >40 atoms is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LaTi₂CrO₆ is likely a marginally metastable or near-ground-state phase because its energy above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS/SS analogues around this energy are experimentally accessible [TS-1, TS-3, SS-3, SS-4]. The CS/SS/TS data collectively support synthesis of complex oxides by ceramic, sealed-tube, and flux routes, but no source directly demonstrates the La–Ti–Cr composition, so redox control remains uncertain. The most critical enabling factor is oxygen-potential control, because matching LaTi₂CrO₆ stoichiometry likely requires reduced Ti/Cr relative to a simple TiO₂-rich air synthesis [Reasonable Inference; reducing/sealed precedents in SS-5, SS-3, SS-4, TS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is slow cation diffusion and competing phase formation during ordering of La, Ti, and Cr in a triclinic P1 framework, consistent with repeated firing requirements in related solid-state oxides [CS-5, SS-4, TS-3]. Overall, the best route is sealed flux growth because it balances thermodynamic accessibility with improved mass transport [SS-2].
