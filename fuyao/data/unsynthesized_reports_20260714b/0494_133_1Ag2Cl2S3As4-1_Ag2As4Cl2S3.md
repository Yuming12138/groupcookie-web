### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0370 eV/atom lies inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window, so it is likely metastable but close enough to the hull to be experimentally accessible. This is supported by thermodynamically similar synthesized compounds: Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂ at 0.0286 eV/atom (TS1), MnIn₂Se₄ at 0.0316 eV/atom (TS2), and Cu₃SbSe₃ at 0.0346 eV/atom (TS3). CS also supports this because the closest chemical analogue Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂ has E_hull = 0.0286 eV/atom and was synthesized (CS1). SS supports this through the same closest structural analogue and other low-E_hull Ag/chalcogenide analogues (SS1, SS3–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not encode volatility, kinetic trapping, or competing polymorphs.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild in temperature and pressure because the closest analogue was synthesized at ambient temperature and pressure by solvent diffusion (CS1/SS1/TS1). The penalty comes from N₂/Schlenk handling, non-common As₄S₃ organometallic adducts, and multistep precursor preparation, all reported for the Cu analogue (CS1/SS1/TS1). CS, SS, and TS all give a strong useful signal because the same Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂ entry appears in all three categories. Remaining uncertainty is whether AgCl or alternative Ag(I)/Cl⁻ sources give the same interdiffusion chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because the Cu analogue forms by solution diffusion at ambient temperature, avoiding bulk diffusion limitations (CS1/SS1/TS1). The structural reconstruction burden is low because the target and Cu analogue share the same 2D As₄S₃Cl₂ framework and M S₂Cl₂ tetrahedral connectivity (CS1/SS1/TS1). However, CS1/SS1/TS1 also report difficult phase control and a need for specific organometallic adducts, so the score is not perfect. Insufficient evidence from CS2–CS5, SS2–SS5, and TS2–TS5 for the exact Ag-substituted solution chemistry, so those sources are treated as neutral for this route.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures because chemically related chalcohalides were synthesized at 400–430 °C in evacuated quartz tubes (CS2/CS3), and Ag₅PS₄Cl₂ was synthesized at 725 K in sealed silica tubes (SS5). The deductions are for vacuum sealing, volatile S/As/Cl components, toxic arsenic chemistry, and multistep sealed-tube handling; these concerns are consistent with CS2/CS3/SS5 and with As₄S₃ precursor complexity in CS1. TS3 supports feasibility of vacuum ampoule synthesis at 350–450 °C but also shows long reaction times. Insufficient evidence from TS1 for a solid-state route to the exact Cu analogue, so TS1 is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Kinetics are moderate: AgCl and As₄S₃ are not refractory oxides, and related chalcogenides/chalcohalides form at 350–452 °C (CS2/CS3/SS5/TS3). However, the target’s As₄S₃-derived 2D sheet may require molecular cluster preservation or reconstruction, and the closest analogue used a solution-solubilized As₄S₃ adduct rather than direct sintering (CS1/SS1/TS1). Long annealing in related sealed-tube systems, such as two-week tempering in CS4 and weeks in TS3, suggests ordering may be slow. Remaining uncertainty is whether AgCl reacts cleanly with As₄S₃ or segregates as AgCl/Ag₂S/arsenic sulfide [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Hydrothermal/solvothermal temperatures are moderate: 220 °C for AgSe₃I (SS4), 285 °C for HgTlAsS₃ recrystallization (SS2), and 433 K for Tl₂TeS₃ solvothermal synthesis (TS5). There is no >5 GPa pressure or >1000 °C heating, but sealed acidic halide systems and arsenic sulfides introduce toxicity and handling penalties (SS2/SS4/TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Ag–As–S–Cl synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route. Remaining uncertainty is whether As₄S₃/As₂S₃ dissolves sufficiently under chloride hydrothermal conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable for crystal growth because dissolution–reprecipitation bypasses solid-state diffusion, as seen in recrystallization of HgTlAsS₃ at 285 °C (SS2) and repeated formation of AgSe₃I hydrothermal products at 220 °C (SS4). Structural transferability is only moderate because the hydrothermal analogues are not the exact Ag–As–S–Cl composition and often involve different anions or cations (SS2/SS4/TS5). SS4 reports multiphase products and intergrowths, and SS2 reports difficulty obtaining suitable crystals, lowering the phase-control portion of the kinetics score. CS1/SS1/TS1 still favor low-temperature crystallization of the As₄S₃ framework but do not specifically validate hydrothermal chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

CVT uses sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile transport agents, as shown by iodine transport at 800–750 °C (CS5) and AlCl₃ transport under 10⁻⁶ mbar at 840–820 °C (TS2). The target route is scored more harshly than hydrothermal because As/S/Cl volatility and transport-agent reactions require sealed high-temperature handling [Reasonable Inference]. SS3 supports evacuated-tube flux/transport-like growth at 450–500 °C but also involves slow ramps and washing. Insufficient evidence from CS1/SS1/TS1 that the exact As₄S₃Cl₂ sheet survives CVT temperatures, so that source is a caution rather than direct support.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Vapor transport can deliver material to a growth zone, but composition control is likely difficult because As, S, and Cl have volatile molecular species and AgCl/transport-agent equilibria may shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. TS2 explicitly notes that thermodynamic calculations were needed to optimize transport and avoid multiphase condensation, supporting moderate-to-difficult phase control. SS3 reports low yield and precise flux conditions for Ag chalcogenide crystal isolation, further lowering confidence. The closest Cu analogue crystallizes at ambient temperature rather than by high-T vapor transport (CS1/SS1/TS1), suggesting CVT is a secondary route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has strongly dissimilar cation environments: Ag is tetrahedral AgS₂Cl₂, while As has single-bond or trigonal non-coplanar As–S environments, which indicates a large size/coordination mismatch and triggers C1 (target description; [Reasonable Inference]). Mixed edge- and corner-sharing AgS₂Cl₂ tetrahedra trigger C3 directly from the target description, and the same mixed connectivity exists in the closest Cu analogue (CS1/SS1/TS1). C2 is not triggered because the given formula has 11 atoms and no evidence is provided that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target unit-cell atom count, so this check is treated neutrally. The resulting medium risk is consistent with phase-control concerns in CS1, SS4, and SS5.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag₂As₄S₃Cl₂ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom, which is close to but above the hull. The strongest support comes from the nearly isostructural and chemically analogous Cu₂As₄S₃Cl₂, which was synthesized by ambient-temperature solution diffusion and appears as the top CS, SS, and TS match (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is preservation and controlled assembly of the As₄S₃-derived unit under mild conditions, which the Cu analogue achieved using As₄S₃·M(CO)₅ adducts (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control in Ag–As–S–Cl chemistry, especially because AgCl has low solubility/light sensitivity and As/S/Cl species are toxic or volatile [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material should be synthesizable, but the best chance is a low-temperature diffusion/crystallization route rather than high-temperature equilibration.
