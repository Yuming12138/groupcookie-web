### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for high thermodynamic accessibility. CS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable, including NaCaVO4 at 0.0 eV/atom [CS-2] and Sr2CaMoO6 at 0.0 eV/atom [CS-3], supporting oxide-forming feasibility. SS and TS analogues include phases around 0.04–0.09 eV/atom [SS-3, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5], so metastable oxides in this range can be isolated but may need kinetic control. Uncertainty remains because the target is low-symmetry P1 and may contain peroxide-like O–O bonding, which is not directly covered by the closest CS examples.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity

Route 1 receives 80 because it uses standard ceramic equipment and air, as in CS-2, CS-3, and SS-1, but needs multistep grinding/annealing and may suffer Na/Mo volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 receives 80 because Na2MoO4/MoO3 flux-like synthesis can occur near 530 °C [SS-3/TS-5], but controlled cooling and possible volatile loss add penalties. Route 3 receives 80 because Pechini processing is moderate in temperature and equipment [CS-1], but uses corrosive HNO3 and multiple organic decomposition/calcination steps. Route 4 receives 85 because hydrothermal analogues use mild temperatures [TS-2, TS-3], but autoclave processing and oxidant/pH handling introduce moderate complexity.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics

Route 1 is kinetically limited because carbonate/oxide diffusion and structural reconstruction are slow, consistent with long anneals and repeated regrinding in CS-3 and SS-1. Route 2 improves kinetics through a Na–Mo-rich mobile medium, supported by Na2MoO4/MoO3 synthesis at 530 °C with slow cooling [SS-3/TS-5]. Route 3 improves cation homogeneity through the Pechini resin approach described in CS-1, but crystallization from an amorphous precursor to triclinic P1 still requires substantial rearrangement. Route 4 has the best intrinsic transport kinetics because solution-mediated hydrothermal synthesis avoids long-range solid diffusion [TS-2, TS-3], but the exact target phase is not demonstrated and peroxide-like bonding is uncertain.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant cation-size/field-strength contrast between Na/Ca and Mo, triggering one amorphization risk by ionic-radius mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly includes edge- and corner-sharing distorted NaO5 and O-centered polyhedra, triggering mixed-connectivity complexity from the target description; related mixed-polyhedral oxides are seen in CS-2, CS-3, and SS-1. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered. The resulting medium amorphization risk is most relevant to Pechini and hydrothermal routes, where amorphous or poorly crystalline intermediates may form.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2CaMoO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS analogues strongly support that Na–Ca oxides and Ca–Mo oxides can be synthesized by conventional routes [CS-2, CS-3], while SS/TS analogues support Na–Mo oxide formation at relatively low temperature using Na2MoO4/MoO3 media [SS-3/TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is use of a highly reactive or mobile precursor environment—Pechini resin, molybdate flux, or hydrothermal solution—to overcome slow multication ordering. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing CaMoO4, Na2MoO4, MoO3-rich, or peroxide/oxide variants may form depending on oxygen activity, pH, and temperature. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require systematic screening rather than a single straightforward ceramic firing.
