### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, so it falls at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. The strongest favorable evidence is that the same Sr2YIrO6 composition is reported as a stable monoclinic P2_1/c double perovskite with E_hull = 0 [CS1, SS1]. The uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target triclinic P-1 structure is slightly metastable relative to the hull, so synthesis may yield monoclinic Sr2YIrO6 unless kinetic or distortion-stabilizing conditions are used [Reasonable Inference]. TS analogues show that oxides around 0.05–0.075 eV/atom can be experimentally made [TS1–TS5], but TS provides insufficient evidence for the exact triclinic Sr2YIrO6 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because the directly reported synthesis uses 1473 K / 1200 °C for several days with repeated regrinding and repelletizing [CS1, SS1]. It does not require high pressure, inert/reducing atmosphere, or sealed tubes because the synthesis is conducted in air at ambient pressure [CS1, SS1]. The main condition penalties are high temperature above 1000 °C and multistep ceramic processing [CS1, SS1].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are the main weakness for Route 1 because SrCO3, Y2O3, and Ir metal are solid, relatively low-activity precursors, and Ir metal oxidation/diffusion can be slow [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference]. The need for several days and repeated regrinding in the direct Sr2YIrO6 synthesis supports a moderate kinetic penalty [CS1, SS1]. The structural reconstruction score is better because the desired double-perovskite framework is the same as the reported Sr2YIrO6 analogue [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less directly evidenced and chemically more complex because it introduces solution precursors and organic burnout not reported in CS/SS/TS; therefore direct CS/SS/TS evidence is insufficient and neutral for this specific route. The final oxide crystallization can still be performed in air and ambient pressure, consistent with Sr2YIrO6 ceramic routes [CS1, SS1]. The score is reduced for multistep processing and possible Ir chloride toxicity/corrosivity if chloride Ir precursors are used [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are likely improved because nitrate/citrate/Pechini processing gives better cation-scale mixing than oxide/carbonate/metal powder mixtures [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS entry directly proves this route for Sr2YIrO6, so this advantage is inferential rather than experimentally established. Final crystallization still must form the Sr–Y–Ir double perovskite, whose ceramic analogues require high-temperature annealing and repeated grinding [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the high-temperature staged ceramic philosophy reported for Ba–Y–Ir oxides in air [TS1]. The conditions are not exotic because they use air, ambient pressure, standard furnace equipment, and repeated grinding [TS1]. The main penalties are temperature above 1000 °C and multistep staged heating [TS1].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics remain limited because the precursor set is still solid-state SrCO3/Y2O3/Ir metal, which is directly supported but diffusion-limited [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference]. Staged heating and repeated grinding improve conversion, as used in thermodynamically similar iridates [TS1]. The uncertainty is that longer, hotter annealing may drive equilibration toward the monoclinic ground-state Sr2YIrO6 rather than preserving the triclinic polymorph [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation-size contrast among Sr, Y, and Ir, triggering one amorphization/structural-disorder risk factor [Reasonable Inference]. The octahedral network is corner-sharing YO6/IrO6 rather than mixed edge/face/corner-sharing, which lowers amorphization risk [target description, CS1, SS1]. The closest CS/SS analogues are crystalline double perovskites synthesized by ceramic methods [CS1, SS1], but polymorph control remains uncertain because the target is a triclinic metastable distortion.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2YIrO6 is highly likely to be synthesizable as a crystalline double perovskite, but the exact triclinic P-1 target is probably a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the ground state, because the same composition is reported as monoclinic P2_1/c with E_hull = 0 [CS1, SS1] while the target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS evidence is very strong for making Sr2YIrO6 chemistry by air-fired ceramic synthesis [CS1, SS1], whereas TS evidence mainly confirms that related iridate oxides tolerate high-temperature air synthesis [TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust ordered double-perovskite Sr–Y–Ir–O framework, which is directly represented in the closest analogues [CS1, SS1]. The main limiting factor is polymorph and phase control: standard long anneals may favor the monoclinic ground-state analogue rather than the triclinic target [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference]. A sol-gel/Pechini route may improve homogeneity and kinetic access, but this is inferential because no direct CS/SS/TS sol-gel precedent is provided.
