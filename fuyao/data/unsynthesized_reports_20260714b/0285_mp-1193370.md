**Routes not adopted:** The low-temperature slow evaporation entry in CS3 gives no credible transferable signal for synthesizing refractory Nb–Cu sulfide; insufficient evidence from CS for solution growth of Nb₂CuS₄, so this route is treated as neutral/irrelevant rather than plausible. Ion-exchange routes in TS2/TS3 are useful for alkali vanadium sulfides, but there is insufficient evidence from TS that they transfer to Cu/Nb sulfides without a suitable layered Tl/K/Rb precursor.

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff used in the scoring rubric. Chemically and structurally similar sulfides with E_hull values between 0 and ~0.08 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, including FeNb₃S₆ (CS1/SS2), CuV₂S₄ (CS2), Ti₃FeS₆ (SS1/TS1), and TlV₅S₈ (TS4/TS5). This supports the view that Nb₂CuS₄ is likely near-stable or mildly metastable rather than deeply unstable. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact Nb–Cu–S phase diagram may contain strong binary competitors such as Nb sulfides and Cu sulfides by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because analogous sulfides are made in evacuated tubes at 800–1000 °C without high pressure (SS1, SS3, SS5), but volatile sulfur requires sealed ampoules and repeated firings are often required (SS1, SS5). Kinetics are the main limitation because Nb is refractory by physicochemical reasoning, and SS1 explicitly reports repeated firings for complete reaction. The route is still plausible because structurally similar ternary sulfides form by direct reaction from elements or sulfide precursors (SS3, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Cu will remain stoichiometric or segregate as Cu₂S under long annealing by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Chemical vapor transport

Route 2 has higher procedural severity because it requires vacuum ampoules, volatile/corrosive transport agents, and a thermal gradient, all documented for Nb and Cu sulfide analogues (CS1, CS2, SS2). Its kinetic score is better than Route 1 because vapor transport bypasses some solid-state diffusion barriers and has produced single crystals of FeNb₃S₆ and CuV₂S₄ (CS1, CS2). This is the best-supported route for crystalline material because it combines Nb-sulfide and Cu-sulfide precedent. Remaining uncertainty is transport-agent selectivity: I₂ may transport Cu and Nb at different rates, potentially shifting stoichiometry by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Oxide-to-sulfide conversion

Route 3 is condition-severe because H₂S sulfidation is reactive/toxic and can require temperatures up to 1150 °C in the Nb₃VS₆ analogue (SS4), plus long sealed sulfur anneals in chemically similar sulfides (CS4, CS5). Kinetically, oxide conversion benefits from high sulfur chemical potential but requires complete reconstruction from oxide to sulfide, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score. This route is plausible because CS4/CS5/SS4 show successful sulfide formation from oxide precursors, but it is less attractive for Cu-containing phases due to redox and Cu₂S impurity risks by [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether oxysulfide residues can be fully eliminated.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target consists of crystalline Nb–S and Cu–S coordination motifs rather than a highly size-mismatched multication glass-forming composition, and analogous transition-metal sulfides crystallize readily under sealed-tube or CVT conditions (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS5). Structurally similar compounds can contain edge/corner/face-sharing octahedral networks and still crystallize (SS1, SS3, TS4), so mixed connectivity does not by itself imply high amorphization risk. The main uncertainty is the exact target unit-cell size, for which insufficient direct evidence is provided by CS/SS/TS; this was treated conservatively but not as a trigger.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₂CuS₄ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and similar sulfides with E_hull values up to ~0.08 eV/atom have experimental syntheses (CS2, SS1, TS1). The CS and SS data support synthesis particularly well: Nb sulfides are accessible by iodine CVT (CS1, SS2), Cu sulfides by halide CVT (CS2), and related ternary transition-metal sulfides by sealed-tube reaction (SS3, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed sulfur-rich/vapor-transport conditions to maintain sulfide stoichiometry and overcome slow cation diffusion, supported by CS1, CS2, CS4, and CS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control in the Nb–Cu–S system, especially possible Cu sulfide segregation or unequal vapor transport of Cu and Nb by [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible, with CVT recommended for crystals and sealed-tube solid-state synthesis recommended for initial phase screening.
