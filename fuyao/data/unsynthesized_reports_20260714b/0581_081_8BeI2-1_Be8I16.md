### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is **0.0320 eV/atom**, placing it in the high-scoring ≤0.05 eV/atom regime. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are reported as synthesizable, including ThPbI6 at 0.0334 eV/atom (TS1), Cd4GeSe6 at 0.0217 eV/atom (TS2), TlMnI3 at 0.0170 eV/atom (TS3), and K6Cd4Sn3Se13 at 0.0306 eV/atom (TS5). However, TS1 and TS5 also show that near-hull phases can still require careful quenching/tempering or special routes, so high thermodynamic score does not eliminate phase-control risk.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses sealed-tube metal + iodine chemistry, which is directly analogous to Al/Ga/Ti iodide syntheses involving I2 and evacuated ampoules (CS1, CS3, SS4). The temperature is not expected to exceed 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], so no high-temperature penalty is applied, but vacuum/sealed handling and volatile iodine penalties are justified by CS1, CS3, and SS4. The largest non-analogue penalty is Be toxicity, which is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly present in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental metal + iodine reactions are kinetically favorable in related iodides, as AlI3/GaI3/TiI3 analogues use metals and iodine in sealed systems (CS1, CS3, SS4). Be metal may be less reactive because of surface passivation and handling constraints [Reasonable Inference], so precursor activity is not scored as excellent. Formation of a layered BeI4 network from elements requires structural reconstruction, but similar tetrahedral iodide motifs in AlI3, NaAlI4, and LiInI4 support feasibility (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, SS3).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

The sublimation/vapor-transport route is well supported by AlI3/GaI3 analogues grown by slow sublimation in evacuated ampoules with small gradients (CS1, SS1, SS4). Its severity score is reduced because it likely requires at least synthesis, purification, and growth steps [Reasonable Inference], and volatile iodide handling is intrinsic to the route (CS1, SS4). Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for BeI2-specific sublimation temperature, so the route remains more uncertain than for AlI3/GaI3.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion, which is favorable and directly reflected in successful sublimation growth of AlI3/GaI3 (CS1, SS1, SS4). However, AlI3/GaI3 analogues also report twinning and difficulty finding suitable crystals, especially for AlI3 (CS1, SS4), reducing the crystallization-pathway score. Composition control is moderate because iodine volatility can alter stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference], although binary stoichiometry is simpler than multication iodides.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Melt growth under dry Ar/sealed ampoule conditions is directly supported by NaAlI4 and LiInI4 iodide analogues (CS2, CS4, SS3). Temperatures are moderate, around 300 °C in those analogues (CS2, CS4), and no high pressure is required. Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile iodide handling, and Be/iodide toxicity or air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference; air-sensitive handling supported by CS2, CS4, TS3].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The melt route receives the highest kinetic score because liquid-phase crystallization minimizes cation diffusion limitations, as shown by NaAlI4 and LiInI4 crystallization from iodide melts with slow cooling (CS2, CS4, SS3). The target is a single binary compound, so no multication ordering is required [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection into the orthorhombic Pnc2 sheet structure, since CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate BeI2 polymorph control.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Low-temperature annealing is supported by TlAlI4/TlGaI4 sealed halide reactions at 150–200 °C (SS2, SS5) and by Th/Pb iodide tempering at 330 °C (TS1). The route avoids high temperature and high pressure, but it still requires vacuum or inert sealed conditions and likely multiple processing steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that annealing alone can generate the target BeI2 polymorph, so the route is scored below the melt route.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Annealing a preformed BeI2 phase avoids long-range multicomponent diffusion [Reasonable Inference], which helps kinetics. However, TS1 and TS3 show that iodide phase purification or crystallinity improvement can require long tempering or repeated annealing, so the kinetics are not automatically easy. The score is moderate-high because halides are generally more labile than oxides [Reasonable Inference], but direct BeI2 evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any multication radius mismatch, so they are neutral. The target is a simple binary layered BeI2 structure, so the >40 atom count check is not triggered based on the target description; CS/SS/TS give no contrary signal. Mixed edge- and corner-sharing BeI4 tetrahedra are explicitly present in the target, and related iodides with edge/corner tetrahedral motifs exist in CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, and SS3, giving one amorphization trigger and a low–medium risk score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0320 eV/atom, which is comparable to thermodynamic analogues that have been synthesized (TS1, TS2, TS5). The CS/SS data strongly support the chemistry of tetrahedral metal iodide frameworks and sealed-ampoule processing, especially through AlI3/GaI3 sublimation and NaAlI4/LiInI4 melt growth (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, SS3, SS4). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/volatility of iodide systems, which allows crystallization from melt or vapor at moderate temperature rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS3). The most critical limiting factor is practical handling: Be toxicity is severe [Reasonable Inference], while iodine/metal iodide volatility and moisture sensitivity require sealed, dry conditions as seen across iodide analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS4, TS3). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best route being sealed-ampoule melt growth followed by slow cooling.

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Be-containing precursors/products are highly toxic, especially powders and dust [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Use sealed ampoules, glovebox loading, secondary containment, HEPA-controlled Be protocols; CS/SS/TS analogues support sealed/glovebox handling for sensitive iodides (CS2, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS3). |
| 2 | All | Iodine and metal iodides are volatile, causing pressure and stoichiometry-control risks. | High | Use evacuated or Ar-filled sealed ampoules with cold zone/temperature gradient; analogous sealed ampoules and gradients are used in CS1, CS3, SS1, SS4. |
| 3 | All | Moisture/air sensitivity and hydrolysis of anhydrous iodides [Reasonable Inference, supported by dry Ar/glovebox requirements in analogues]. | High | Load and handle under dry Ar/N2 glovebox; use dried ampoules and reagents, as in CS2, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS3. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Incomplete reaction of Be metal due to passivation or limited contact with I2 [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Use finely divided but safely contained Be, slow heating, slight iodine excess, and long hold; metal + iodine sealed reactions are supported by CS1, CS3, SS4. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Crystal twinning or small/poor-quality crystals during sublimation. | Medium–High | Use very small temperature gradients, slow growth, and crystal selection; AlI3/GaI3 sublimation uses 5–10 °C gradients and reports twinning difficulty (CS1, SS4). |
