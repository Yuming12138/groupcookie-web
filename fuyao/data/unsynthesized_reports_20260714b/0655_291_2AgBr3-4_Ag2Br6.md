### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.038 eV/atom, so it is within the commonly synthesizable metastability window and receives 94/100 by the required rubric. CS supports this because chemically similar bromides CuBr2, InBr3, and GaBr3 are at or near the hull and have reported syntheses (CS-1 to CS-5). TS strongly supports accessibility because the thermodynamic neighbors lie in a comparable E_hull range and are synthesized by CVT, hydrothermal, or solvothermal routes (TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that AgBr3 contains unusual Br–Br bonding and likely high bromine chemical potential requirements; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly proves the AgBr3 phase itself.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum/Ar sealed ampoules, volatile Br2, and toxic/corrosive bromine, matching the controlled ampoule requirements in CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1, TS-4, and TS-5. Kinetically, it is favorable because halide vapor transport/sublimation crystallizes related bromides at only ~140–200 °C in CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1, and TS-5. The main uncertainty is structural selectivity: TS-1 reports byproducts and TS-4 reports multiphase mixtures, so Br-rich vapor chemistry may not selectively yield AgBr3 without careful Br2 activity and gradient control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the best F2 because it avoids high temperature, high pressure, and specialized furnaces; this is consistent with room-temperature evaporation for CuBr2 in CS-1/CS-2 and mild acid precipitation/recrystallization in SS-3. Its F3 is high because solution crystallization eliminates diffusion limitations, as shown by immediate precipitation in SS-3 and slow evaporation in CS-1/CS-2. The uncertainty is high for Ag specifically: AgBr is poorly soluble and AgBr3 likely requires oxidizing Br2/polybromide conditions, which are [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because it uses sealed ampoules/autoclaves, volatile Br2/HBr, and potentially toxic solvents such as CCl4, directly paralleling TS-3 and TS-4. Kinetically it remains plausible because TS-3 gives quantitative bromide crystals under hydrothermal HBr conditions, and TS-4 forms bromide crystals from Br2-containing solvothermal media at 180–200 °C. The limitation is phase purity: TS-4 explicitly reports multiphase products requiring manual crystal separation, which lowers confidence for selective AgBr3 formation.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply; this gives no C1 trigger. The described structure is a 2D sheet of corner-sharing AgBr4 tetrahedra with Br–Br units, and the provided description does not indicate a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues include many low-dimensional halides that crystallize rather than amorphize under mild conditions (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5), although insufficient evidence from all sources gives exact target unit-cell size.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgBr3 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a robust ambient ground-state phase, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.038 eV/atom. The CS data support that simple metal bromides can crystallize under very mild conditions, especially CuBr2 by slow evaporation and In/Ga bromides by sublimation (CS-1 to CS-5). The SS and TS data support low-temperature sealed-ampoule, CVT, hydrothermal, and solvothermal growth of halogen-rich low-dimensional bromides, but they also show byproduct and phase-control risks in some systems (SS-1, TS-1, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high bromine chemical potential with Br2/HBr while allowing slow crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is the competition with ordinary AgBr plus free Br2 and the uncertain stability of Ag in the required bromine-rich coordination environment.
