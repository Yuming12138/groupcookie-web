### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.068 eV/atom, which places it in the moderately metastable but often synthesizable range. Chemically similar Ba sulfides include stable or nearly stable examples such as Ba2TiS4 at 0 eV/atom (CS1), Ba3Zr2S7 at 0.0097 eV/atom (CS2), and Ba2ZrS4 at 0.0016 eV/atom (CS5). Thermodynamic analogues include experimentally reported phases with E_hull values near or above the target, such as Ba2S3 at 0.0639 eV/atom (TS1), FeSc2S4 at 0.113 eV/atom (TS2), and EuS-related material at 0.160 eV/atom (TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that no direct Ba–Sc–S experimental analogue is provided.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard sealed-tube sulfide conditions, which are well supported by CS1, CS5, and SS3. The main severity penalties come from vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multistep prereaction/refiring, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors, all consistent with CS1/CS5 practices. The absence of high pressure makes it feasible, but phase-purity control may be difficult as CS5 reports impurities and repeated firings.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfides such as BaS and Sc2S3 should be more reactive than oxides, but still require solid-state diffusion, as reflected by long Ba2ZrS4 firings in CS5 and Sc sulfide synthesis at 1200 °C in TS2. The target has ScS4 tetrahedra, and tetrahedral MS4 Ba sulfides exist in CS1/SS3, supporting partial structural transfer. The uncertainty is whether Sc3+ prefers the target tetrahedral coordination over competing Sc2S3-type phases.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux synthesis requires sealed ampoules, controlled cooling, and air-free handling, as shown in CS2, SS1, SS2, and TS5. It avoids high pressure and may avoid very high temperatures, but volatile sulfur and flux washing add operational complexity. The severity is moderate rather than easy because BaCl2/CsCl fluxes must be dry and the product may be moisture sensitive.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because liquid-mediated transport is demonstrated for Ba3Zr2S7 in BaCl2 flux (CS2) and for structurally similar Ba-containing tetrahedral sulfides in polysulfide fluxes (SS1/SS2). Slow cooling improves crystallization and may help isolate metastable Ba2Sc2S5 before conversion to lower-energy competing phases. The key uncertainty is whether chloride or alkali fluxes introduce unwanted secondary phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

H2S synthesis is supported by TS2 for FeSc2S4, but H2S is toxic and requires a controlled reactive atmosphere. Compared with sealed-tube sulfur routes, it can maintain sulfur activity without relying on elemental sulfur vapor pressure. The route is less chemically matched to Ba2Sc2S5 than Routes 1–2, because TS2 is a spinel Sc sulfide rather than a Ba–Sc sulfide.

#### F3 — Route 3

H2S may accelerate sulfidation and suppress oxide contamination, which is relevant because CS5 reports oxide impurities in Ba2MS4 synthesis. However, the structural reconstruction from sulfide precursors to Pbca Ba2Sc2S5 is not directly supported. The kinetics score is therefore moderate.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is the most severe route because CS3 and TS1 use ~5 GPa-class equipment. It may be helpful for metastable or pressure-stabilized sulfides, but the equipment burden is high. Sulfur containment and air-sensitive precursors add further difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 4

HPHT synthesis can form phases inaccessible at ambient pressure, as shown for Ba–Cr sulfides in CS3 and Ba2S3 in TS1. Kinetically, high temperature and pressure can enhance reaction completion, but no direct structural match to Ba2Sc2S5 is available. This is best viewed as a backup route if ambient-pressure routes fail.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, Ba2+ and Sc3+, producing a large size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii and target structure]. The Pbca structure with multiple inequivalent Ba, Sc, and S sites suggests a relatively large unit cell, increasing ordering demands [Target structure]. However, the Sc network is described as corner-sharing ScS4 tetrahedra rather than mixed edge/face-sharing polyhedra, so the amorphization risk is medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba2Sc2S5 is likely a moderately metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.068 eV/atom. The CS and SS data support synthesis well: Ba sulfides with tetrahedral transition-metal sulfide units are accessible by sealed solid-state and flux methods (CS1/CS2/CS5/SS1/SS2/SS3). The TS data also supports feasibility because experimentally reported thermodynamic analogues exist at comparable or higher hull energies (TS1/TS2/TS3), although some require high pressure or reactive H2S. The most important enabling factor is the simple charge-balanced precursor reaction 2 BaS + Sc2S3 → Ba2Sc2S5 [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase competition and slow ordering in a large, low-symmetry Ba–Sc sulfide structure, especially because CS5 shows impurity and repeated-firing issues for related Ba sulfides.
