### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring bin. Structurally similar LiKSO₄ analogues are experimentally synthesized and lie very near the hull [SS1, SS2], while thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.038–0.058 eV/atom are also synthesized [TS1, TS3]. CS analogues are stable oxysalts with E_hull = 0 [CS1–CS5], but they are phosphates rather than sulfates, so their thermodynamic signal is supportive but less direct. Remaining uncertainty is whether LiCsSO₄ is a true low-temperature equilibrium phase or a metastable polymorph accessible only under kinetic crystallization control.

#### F2: Route 1 aqueous slow evaporation

Route 1 receives F2 = 100 because SS analogues use very mild aqueous crystallization at 313–333 K with no special atmosphere, pressure, or toxic/air-sensitive precursors [SS1, SS5]. Cs sulfate crystals are also grown from aqueous solution by evaporation or slow cooling [SS4, SS5]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for this aqueous sulfate route, so CS is neutral for route-specific severity. The main uncertainty is not condition severity but selective crystallization of the mixed Li/Cs phase.

#### F3: Route 1 aqueous slow evaporation

Route 1 receives F3 = 95 because soluble sulfate salts in water provide high precursor activity and eliminate long-range solid-state diffusion [SS1, SS2, SS5]. The structural pathway is favorable because LiKSO₄ is directly obtained from Li₂SO₄ and K sulfate in water [SS1, SS2], and Cs sulfate salts crystallize well from solution [SS4, SS5]. The score is not 100 because Cs⁺ is much larger than K⁺, so LiCsSO₄ may have different solubility and nucleation behavior. This uncertainty can be mitigated by slow evaporation, seeding, and PXRD screening.

#### F2: Route 2 solid-state reaction

Route 2 receives F2 = 90 because the proposed 500–650 °C temperature range is moderate and consistent with solid-state oxysalt syntheses [CS1, CS5] and sulfate crystallization at 660 °C [TS1]. No high pressure, inert atmosphere, or volatile sealed precursor is required by the proposed sulfate-salt route [CS1, CS5; SS1]. A −10 deduction is applied for multi-step drying, grinding, calcination, and possible regrinding, which is standard solid-state practice and consistent with staged thermal processing in CS1. SS gives insufficient direct evidence for solid-state LiCsSO₄, so SS is neutral for this route’s direct transferability.

#### F3: Route 2 solid-state reaction

Route 2 receives F3 = 82 because sulfate salts are more reactive than refractory oxides but still rely on solid-state ion redistribution. Ion diffusion is expected to be manageable because analogous oxysalts form at 500–660 °C [CS1, CS5, TS1]. The main kinetic limitation is cation ordering between Li⁺ and Cs⁺ and avoidance of residual Li₂SO₄/Cs₂SO₄, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from the lack of direct SS solid-state evidence. This route is likely useful for powders but less attractive than aqueous growth.

#### F2: Route 3 flux growth

Route 3 receives F2 = 90 because flux growth generally requires multiple thermal steps and controlled cooling, as in CS flux examples [CS2–CS4]. The proposed sulfate self-flux avoids the >1000 °C conditions used for some phosphate fluxes [CS3, CS4], so no high-temperature deduction is applied under the proposed adaptation. No special atmosphere or high pressure is required in the flux analogues [CS2–CS4]. The remaining uncertainty is sulfate melt stability and product recovery without dissolution.

#### F3: Route 3 flux growth

Route 3 receives F3 = 90 because molten flux provides high ionic mobility, and alkali oxysalts crystallize successfully under slow cooling in CS examples [CS2–CS4]. Structural reconstruction should be moderate because sulfate tetrahedra can remain intact while Li/Cs ordering occurs during crystallization [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1 and SS5]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for molten sulfate flux growth, so SS is neutral for flux-specific proof. The main risk is flux inclusions or preferential crystallization of simple sulfates.

#### F2: Route 4 hydrothermal

Route 4 receives F2 = 90 because TS sulfate hydrothermal synthesis occurs at only 155 °C for 48 h in an autoclave [TS3]. This is not high pressure in the >5 GPa sense, and no inert or reactive atmosphere is required [TS3]. A −10 deduction is applied for multi-step autoclave processing. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal sulfate synthesis of this type, so CS is neutral.

#### F3: Route 4 hydrothermal

Route 4 receives F3 = 88 because aqueous/hydrothermal media provide strong precursor activity and rapid transport [TS3]. Hydrothermal sulfate crystals suitable for XRD are reported [TS3], and solution-grown Li/alkali sulfates are directly supported by SS [SS1, SS2, SS5]. The score is below Route 1 because hydrothermal conditions are less directly demonstrated for LiCsSO₄-like mixed alkali sulfates. The uncertainty is whether heating in sealed water changes speciation toward acid sulfate or hydrated phases.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

F4 receives 78 because only one amorphization trigger is clearly present: the large Li⁺/Cs⁺ size mismatch, consistent with very different Li–O and Cs–O coordination environments in the target and Cs sulfate analogues [SS4, SS5]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered. The target description does not show coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral networks, and CS/SS/TS do not provide sufficient evidence to trigger C3. The remaining uncertainty is whether rapid evaporation produces glassy or poorly ordered salt mixtures rather than crystals.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiCsSO₄ is likely near the boundary between ground-state and metastable behavior because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from structurally similar LiKSO₄ phases that crystallize readily from aqueous sulfate solutions [SS1, SS2] and Cs sulfate phases that also grow from aqueous solutions [SS4, SS5]. Chemical-similarity data support general oxysalt crystallization by solid-state and flux methods [CS1–CS5], but those analogues are phosphates, so they are less direct than SS. The most critical enabling factor is high sulfate-salt solubility, which allows low-temperature solution crystallization and avoids sluggish solid-state diffusion [SS1, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against Li₂SO₄, Cs₂SO₄, hydrated sulfates, or acid sulfates, especially because the target is only marginally above the thermodynamic hull.
