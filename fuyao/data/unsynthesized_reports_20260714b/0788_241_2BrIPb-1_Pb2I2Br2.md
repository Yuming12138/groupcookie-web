### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the required ≤0.05 eV/atom range. SS/TS strongly support this because the closest PbI₂ layered analogues are also essentially on-hull, with E_hull ≈ 0.0008–0.0019 eV/atom (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). CS gives supportive but more chemically diverse evidence: SnICl and AuTeI are on-hull, HgBrI is near-hull, while TlInSe₂ is high above hull and less relevant to Pb–halide chemistry (CS2, CS5, CS3, CS4). Remaining uncertainty is mainly configurational: the calculation assumes an ordered PbIBr phase, while real samples may form mixed PbI₂/PbBr₂ or disordered halide phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, gel diffusion.**  
F2 is high because SS/TS PbI₂ gel growth occurs at room temperature or 273–323 K, ambient pressure, and ordinary room conditions with only simple test-tube/U-tube equipment (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). The only significant severity penalty is toxic Pb salt handling [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is very high because soluble lead acetate and KI are proven active precursors in SS/TS gel growth, and the target structure is a direct mixed-halide derivative of the PbI₂ sheet. The uncertainty is that SS/TS do not directly show Br/I ordering, so halide ratio and diffusion rate must be optimized [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, direct precipitation.**  
F2 is also high because CS2 demonstrates room-temperature aqueous mixed-halide formation without vacuum or high pressure, and SS/TS show PbI₂ forms from aqueous lead acetate/KI under mild conditions. F3 is slightly lower than gel diffusion because fast precipitation may trap disorder or produce separate PbI₂/PbBr₂, and CS2 explicitly notes that HI quantity affects formation of SnClI versus mixed phases. This route is kinetically facile but phase-selectivity-limited. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for direct non-gel precipitation of PbIBr specifically, so a neutral-to-moderate uncertainty remains.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, sealed ampoule melt/solid-state.**  
F2 is moderate because evacuated sealed ampoules and controlled heating/cooling are required, as seen in CS1, CS4, SS5, and TS5, and volatile halides plus toxic Pb compounds add hazards. F3 is favorable because Pb halides are not refractory and SS5 demonstrates direct melting of Pb-containing iodides in evacuated ampoules, while TS5 demonstrates crystalline iodides from sealed-ampoule heating at only 150–200 °C. Structural reconstruction should be limited if starting from PbI₂/PbBr₂, but Br/I ordering and avoiding phase separation remain limiting [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct CS evidence exists for PbIBr melt congruency.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, hydrothermal/solvothermal.**  
F2 is moderate because hydrothermal acid synthesis uses sealed autogenous pressure and volatile/corrosive HI/HBr, though not extreme temperature or >5 GPa pressure; CS5 supports hydroiodic-acid hydrothermal synthesis of AuTeI. F3 is good because solution transport reduces diffusion barriers, and SS/TS PbI₂ gel data show low-temperature solution crystallization of layered Pb–halide sheets. The main uncertainty is that CS5 lacks exact temperature/time details, and SS/TS do not directly demonstrate hydrothermal PbIBr synthesis. Thus, the route is plausible but less directly evidenced than gel diffusion.

**F2/F3 — Route 5, CVT.**  
F2 is lower because CS1-type CVT requires evacuated quartz ampoules, high-temperature gradients, long duration, and volatile halide species. F3 is moderate because CS1 proves chemical vapor transport can yield single crystals of a chemically similar bismuth iodochalcogenide, but mixed Pb–I/Br transport may fractionate due to different vapor pressures [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is the limiting kinetic/practical factor. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for vapor transport of mixed PbIBr specifically, so this route is best viewed as a crystal-growth follow-up rather than first synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Pb, so the cation-radius-difference criterion is not triggered; this is based on the target formula, and CS/SS/TS provide no contradictory signal. The structure is simple and layered, with edge-sharing PbI₃Br₃ octahedra only, matching the low-complexity edge-sharing PbI₂ analogues in SS/TS. No mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity is present in the target, unlike more complex frameworks. Remaining uncertainty is minor and relates to possible halide disorder, which is not the same as amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PbIBr is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest SS/TS PbI₂-type analogues are also essentially on-hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/TS: multiple PbI₂ polytypes with the same layered edge-sharing Pb–halide framework grow easily from silica gel under ambient conditions. CS provides additional support that mixed main-group halides can form by mild aqueous, solid-state, hydrothermal, and CVT routes, but CS2 also warns that mixed-halide stoichiometry can be phase-sensitive. The most critical enabling factor is the excellent structural match to PbI₂ layered sheets, which should allow Br substitution without major framework reconstruction. The most critical limiting factor is I/Br ordering and stoichiometry control, since competing PbI₂/PbBr₂ or disordered mixed-halide precipitates are plausible.
