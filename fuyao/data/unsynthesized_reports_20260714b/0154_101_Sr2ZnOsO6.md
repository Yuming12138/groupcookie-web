### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the most favorable cutoff in the scoring rubric, so a high but borderline score of 90/100 is assigned. Chemically similar Sr₂CuOsO₆ and Sr₂InOsO₆ are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting viability of Sr–Os double perovskites (CS-1, CS-4). However, Sr₂NiOsO₆ has E_hull = 0.123 eV/atom and Sr₂NiReO₆ has E_hull = 0.171 eV/atom, showing that closely related phases can be metastable yet experimentally accessible (CS-2, CS-3). Insufficient evidence from TS directly addresses Sr–Zn–Os thermodynamics, so TS is treated as neutral for target-specific F1 beyond general metastable oxide precedent.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized for high/dynamic vacuum, sealed ampoule handling, multi-step cycling, and toxic/volatile Os handling, all directly reflected in Sr₂CuOsO₆ and Sr₂NiOsO₆ procedures (CS-1, CS-3). Its advantage is relatively modest temperature, 800–850 °C, below the >1000 °C penalty threshold (CS-1, CS-3). Insufficient evidence from TS supports this exact Sr–Os–Zn sealed-vacuum route, so TS contributes neutral signal here.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are relatively poor because the route uses oxide/metal precursors, including dense Os metal, and analogues require repeated heating/grinding cycles (CS-1, CS-3). Structural reconstruction is helped by very close double-perovskite analogues Sr₂CuOsO₆ and Sr₂NiOsO₆ (CS-1, CS-3), but phase control is reported as challenging due Os loss and secondary phases (CS-1, CS-3). Insufficient evidence from TS for Os-metal sealed synthesis is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high pressure and >1000 °C treatment, because Sr₂InOsO₆ is made at 950–960 °C under Ar (CS-4; SS-2). It is still penalized for sealed-tube handling, multi-step long sintering with regrinding, and toxic OsO₂/SrO₂ handling (CS-4; SS-2). TS provides insufficient direct evidence for this SrO₂/OsO₂/ZnO route, so TS is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Oxide/peroxide precursors are somewhat more reactive than metal Os mixtures, but the 100 h synthesis of Sr₂InOsO₆ indicates slow diffusion and ordering (CS-4; SS-2). Structural transferability is good because Sr₂InOsO₆ is orthorhombic/perovskite-derived and contains corner-sharing OsO₆/InO₆ octahedra similar to the target framework (CS-4; SS-2). The Zn substitution introduces uncertainty in charge balance and ordering kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The soft-chemistry route requires multiple processing steps and high-temperature annealing at 1200 °C in N₂, as in Sr₂MReO₆ (CS-2). It avoids high pressure and sealed ampoules in the cited Re route, but Os toxicity/volatility still creates a safety penalty by analogy with Os double-perovskite sources (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). SS gives no direct soft-chemistry Os/Zn analogue, so SS is neutral for route-specific condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The citrate/nitrate resin provides the best precursor homogeneity and highest precursor activity among the bulk routes, directly supported by the Sr₂MReO₆ soft-chemistry procedure (CS-2). This should reduce Zn/Os diffusion lengths and improve B-site mixing compared with oxide grinding [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is that CS-2 is Re-based, not Os-based, and Os oxidation/volatility may complicate resin burnout and final annealing relative to Re (CS-2 vs CS-1/CS-4).

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 is heavily penalized because Sr₂MgOsO₆ high-pressure synthesis uses 6 GPa and 1500 °C in a Pt capsule (SS-1). It also requires sealed capsule preparation, oxidizer handling, and specialized high-pressure equipment (SS-1). TS-4 independently supports that some related perovskites require multi-GPa conditions, but this increases severity rather than ease.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

The high-pressure route has strong structural precedent because Sr₂MgOsO₆ is the top structural analogue and forms the same Sr₂B′OsO₆ framework (SS-1). High temperature and pressure accelerate formation, but the oxide/metal precursor set is not especially reactive and Zn volatility at 1500 °C is a concern [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient CS evidence supports high-pressure Zn–Os synthesis specifically, so CS is neutral for this route’s kinetics beyond general Os handling.

#### F2 — Route 5 condition severity

Flux growth benefits from low temperatures of 600–700 °C and no high pressure, as shown for Os/Ir oxide flux syntheses in sealed Ag tubes (SS-4; TS-1, TS-2). It is penalized for sealed-tube flux handling, caustic hydroxide medium, and Os toxicity/volatility risk (SS-4; TS-1/TS-2). CS does not provide a hydroxide-flux Sr₂ZnOsO₆ analogue, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 5 kinetics

Molten hydroxide flux enhances mass transport and can grow single crystals of perovskite-derived Os/Ir oxides at much lower temperatures than ceramic routes (SS-4; TS-1, TS-2). However, phase selection in flux is uncertain, and TS-1 notes that conventional solid-state methods failed for related Ir double perovskites before flux was used, indicating sensitivity. No direct Sr–Zn–Os double-perovskite flux synthesis is given, so this route remains plausible but less certain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ordered double perovskite with large Sr²⁺ and much smaller octahedral Zn/Os cations, triggering one ionic-size-difference risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports corner-sharing OsO₆/ZnO₆ octahedra rather than mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. The provided target and analogue descriptions do not demonstrate a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates otherwise. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂OsZnO₆ is likely near the boundary between ground-state and mildly metastable behavior because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the existence of Sr–Os double perovskites with divalent or trivalent B-site cations, especially Sr₂CuOsO₆, Sr₂NiOsO₆, Sr₂MgOsO₆, and Sr₂InOsO₆ (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4; SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). The most important enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing double-perovskite framework and the availability of several successful Sr–Os oxide synthesis strategies (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4; SS-1). The main limiting factor is control of Os oxidation state and volatility, especially avoiding Os loss or OsO₄ formation while maintaining the Os valence needed to charge-balance Zn²⁺ [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1/CS-3/CS-4]. The best practical route is a homogeneous citrate/nitrate precursor followed by inert annealing, but an oxide sealed-tube route is a strong backup if soft-chemistry Os handling proves problematic.
