### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it exactly at the high-score threshold, so it is likely near-stable rather than deeply unstable. CS analogues include stable or near-stable synthesized fluorides such as LiV2F6 at 0.0076 eV/atom and Li2VF6 at 0 eV/atom (CS1, CS4), while TS analogues show experimentally reported materials even at ~0.054–0.091 eV/atom (TS1, TS4, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target may still be metastable with respect to nearby Li–V–F competing phases, as phase impurities are reported for Li2VF6 (CS4).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, which keeps condition severity moderate (CS1, SS1). The penalties come from vacuum/drybox handling, sealed refractory containers, and air-sensitive VF2 chemistry (CS1, SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether sealing is needed only for redox/moisture control or also to suppress fluoride volatility; CS/SS/TS do not provide direct volatility evidence, so that subfactor is neutral/50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The closest analogues require very long anneals at 800 °C, indicating slow solid-state kinetics (CS1, SS1). The VF6 octahedral chemistry is transferable, but the target requires ordered Li/V/F reconstruction from binary fluorides [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS4/SS1]. This makes Route 1 kinetically challenging but still the best-supported route.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 uses milder temperatures supported by CS4, SS5, and TS3, so thermal severity is lower than Route 1. However, it still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed vessels, and air-sensitive fluoride handling (CS4, SS5, TS1). The lower temperature reduces equipment severity but increases uncertainty in phase completion, as CS4 reports a large secondary phase.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Lower-temperature annealing likely slows diffusion and increases the probability of incomplete reaction [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS4 impurity formation and SS5’s need for intermediate grinding. The route is plausible as an optimization route but less robust than Route 1 for phase-pure Li2V3F8. The main uncertainty is whether repeated grinding can overcome LiF/VF2 diffusion limits without oxidation during handling.

**F2 — Route 3**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized because TS2 uses 1.5–7.0 GPa and specialized belt-type equipment. It avoids long 30-day anneals and may stabilize metastable fluorides, but the pressure hardware makes it operationally difficult (TS2). No CS/SS source directly supports high-pressure Li–V–F synthesis, so transferability is partial.

**F3 — Route 3**  
TS2 demonstrates that Li-containing transition-metal fluorides can crystallize under high pressure in 1–2 h, which improves kinetic feasibility. However, the target R-3m Li–V–F phase is not directly shown in TS2, so structural transfer is uncertain. This route is best viewed as a backup if ambient-pressure synthesis yields competing phases.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 uses accessible temperatures and ambient sealed-tube conditions similar to CS1 and SS4. The severity comes from controlled atmosphere, redox-sensitive fluorides, and multi-step in situ reduction (CS1, SS4, TS1). It is not penalized for high pressure or >1000 °C operation.

**F3 — Route 4**  
In situ redox formation introduces V metal diffusion and redox equilibration, making kinetics weaker than direct VF2 use [Reasonable Inference from SS4]. SS4 supports that metal/fluoride redox routes can work, but they are more complex than direct binary fluoride reaction. The uncertainty is whether V metal fully converts before competing Li–V–F phases nucleate.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target appears to be a compact crystalline fluoride framework with edge-sharing VF6 octahedra, and CS/SS analogues show that related fluoride frameworks crystallize under sealed solid-state conditions (CS1, CS4, SS1). Explicit ionic radii and target unit-cell atom count are not provided by CS/SS/TS, so those source-specific checks are neutral/50 and interpreted using [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low; phase separation is a larger concern than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li2V3F8 is likely a **near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and TS includes synthesized analogues at similar or higher hull energies (TS1, TS4, TS5). The CS and SS data strongly support fluoride solid-state synthesis using LiF/alkali fluorides plus VF2-family precursors, especially at ~800 °C in sealed refractory containers (CS1, SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining reduced V(II)-rich chemistry through anhydrous sealed handling and VF2-based stoichiometry (CS1, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because related vanadium fluorides show disproportionation, oxidation, or secondary-phase formation (CS4, SS1, TS1). Overall, the target is **plausibly synthesizable but experimentally challenging**, with Route 1 providing the clearest path.
