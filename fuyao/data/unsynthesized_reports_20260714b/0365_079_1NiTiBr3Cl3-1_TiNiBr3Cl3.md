### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.047 eV/atom, placing it just inside the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. TS gives the strongest support because TS-5 includes AlCl₃-type MoCl₃ at 0.034 eV/atom and TS-1/TS-3 show experimentally accessible halides at ~0.06–0.073 eV/atom. CS also shows that chemically related halide/chalcogenide-halide compounds can be synthesized even at much higher hull energies, but those are less structurally direct than TS. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed Ti/Ni and mixed Br/Cl, so the calculated low hull does not guarantee cation/anion ordering is experimentally accessible.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT / Sealed-Ampoule Growth

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because CVT requires vacuum/sealed ampoules and volatile halogens, as seen in CS-3, TS-1, TS-5, SS-2, and SS-5. The synthesis temperature itself is not severe: analogous halides form between ~150 and 515 °C in SS-2, SS-4, CS-3, and TS-5. Kinetically, CVT is favorable because transport through volatile halide species reduces the need for long-range solid diffusion; this is directly supported by CS-3 and TS-5. The main uncertainty is whether Ti and Ni transport at compatible rates and whether mixed Br/Cl chemical potentials can stabilize the target rather than separate Ti and Ni halides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Sealed-Ampoule Reaction

The condition severity is similar to Route 1 because sealed halogen vapor and air-sensitive halides are still required, as in CS-5, TS-3, SS-4, and TS-4. Kinetics are less favorable than CVT because Ti/Ni ordering must occur through solid-state or molten-halide-assisted diffusion, and analogues such as CS-5 and SS-4 report simultaneous polymorphs or side products. The route is still plausible because direct sealed-tube bromination/chlorination is a standard path for metal halides (CS-5, TS-3). The uncertainty is phase purity: separate TiCl₃/TiBr₃ and NiCl₂/NiBr₂ phases may be more easily formed than the ordered TiNiBr₃Cl₃ sheet.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Nonaqueous Solution / Solvothermal Crystallization

This route has lower diffusion barriers and high precursor mobility, supported by CS-2, CS-4, and SS-3, but it has more operational complexity because it requires anhydrous solvents, inert handling, controlled oxidants, and slow crystallization. F2 is lower because CS-2 and SS-3 use vacuum lines/gloveboxes, sealed ampoules, and multistep purification or crystallization. Kinetically, solution growth can help Ti/Ni mixing and crystallization at lower temperatures, but the structural match is weaker because CS-2/CS-4/SS-3 mostly involve molecular, cluster, or polymorphic halide products rather than a 2D AlCl₃-derived sheet. The main uncertainty is whether the extended 2D network can nucleate from solution instead of forming solvated molecular complexes.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple edge-sharing octahedral network, and the description does not indicate mixed edge/corner/face-sharing connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. The unit cell appears small from the P1 one-sheet description, and no CS/SS/TS source provides evidence for a >40 atom cell, so C2 is not triggered. The Ti/Ni radius mismatch is borderline depending on Ni spin/oxidation state, but with high-spin octahedral Ni³⁺ the mismatch is below ~15%, so C1 is not counted. Remaining uncertainty is the formal oxidation state: if Ni is low-spin Ni³⁺ or if charge compensation induces defects, local disorder may increase.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiNi(BrCl)₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.047 eV/atom, and TS analogues show experimentally realized halides at similar or higher hull energies. The strongest support comes from TS-5, which is an AlCl₃-type layered MoCl₃ analogue synthesized by temperature-gradient/CVT methods, and from TS-1/CS-3, which demonstrate layered halide or halide-containing sheets grown by vapor transport. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical activity of volatile halide species in sealed-ampoule/CVT conditions, which can overcome slow Ti/Ni solid-state diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: mixed Ti/Ni cation ordering and mixed Br/Cl anion stoichiometry must be achieved while avoiding binary Ti and Ni halides. Therefore, the material is synthesizable in principle, but likely requires careful halogen chemical-potential control and single-crystal or microcrystalline CVT optimization.
