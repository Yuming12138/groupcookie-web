### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold where the rubric assigns 90–100, so a conservative 90 is used. Same-composition Sr₂CaWO₆ polymorphs are reported at 0.0208 and 0.0 eV/atom, showing that this chemistry has stable or near-stable phases (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). TS analogues with E_hull around 0.054–0.069 eV/atom are still experimentally synthesizable, indicating that small positive hull energies do not preclude oxide synthesis (TS1–TS5). The uncertainty is that the target is the triclinic P1 polymorph, not the lower-energy monoclinic/tetragonal polymorphs reported in CS/SS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires temperatures above 1000 °C and multiple firing/grinding steps, both directly supported by same-composition Sr₂CaWO₆ syntheses (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). It avoids high pressure and reactive atmosphere, since air/ambient-pressure ceramic synthesis is reported for Sr₂CaWO₆ and related tungstates (CS1, CS2; SS1–SS4; TS1). The main severity is thermal and procedural rather than equipment-based, because standard mortars, crucibles, and furnaces are sufficient (CS1, CS2; SS1–SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for any requirement for sealed ampoules or inert/reducing atmosphere for W⁶⁺ Sr₂CaWO₆.

#### F3 — Route 1

Carbonates plus WO₃ are proven but kinetically sluggish, as shown by repeated grinding/heating and long dwell times in Sr₂CaWO₆ analogues (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). The diffusion penalty is significant because W-containing oxide reactions often require high temperature; the analogue data partly offsets this because Sr₂CaWO₆ forms at ~1097 °C rather than requiring extreme temperatures (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). Structural reconstruction is favorable relative to unrelated materials because same-composition double-perovskite-related phases already exist, but the target P1 edge-sharing distortion is not the same as the corner-sharing octahedral frameworks in CS/SS references (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so direct evidence for its conditions is insufficient from CS, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS. It is nevertheless a reasonable extrapolation because the final oxide can be crystallized in air within the temperature range already proven for Sr₂CaWO₆ and related tungstates (CS1, CS2; SS1–SS4). It has similar thermal severity to Route 1 but more wet-chemistry steps; no high pressure or reactive atmosphere is inferred. The uncertainty is solution stability of W species and whether the route selects the triclinic polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 2

The precursor-activity score is higher because nitrate/citrate/metatungstate chemistry should improve cation mixing and reduce diffusion length, but this is **[Reasonable Inference]**, not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. The same refractory W-related diffusion issue remains during crystallization, so the diffusion score is not high. Structural reconstruction is less favorable than Route 1 because the material must crystallize from an amorphous or nanocrystalline precursor, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that sol–gel processing stabilizes triclinic P1 Sr₂CaWO₆.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different sizes and bonding preferences, reflected in the large spread of Sr–O, Ca–O, and W–O bond lengths in the target and analogues (target description; CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). Only one formal amorphization trigger is assigned because the described P1 formula unit is not >40 atoms and no face-sharing coexistence is specified. Mixed edge/corner connectivity in the target suggests local strain, but the absence of face sharing keeps the risk at low–medium rather than high. The remaining uncertainty is whether the reported P1 cell is a small primitive cell or part of a larger experimentally realized superstructure; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a larger cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Sr₂CaWO₆ triclinic P1 phase is likely a near-hull metastable polymorph rather than the unique ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while same-composition monoclinic Sr₂CaWO₆ is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS2; SS2). The CS and SS evidence strongly supports synthesis of Sr₂CaWO₆ chemistry by ceramic methods, but it more directly supports tetragonal/monoclinic double-perovskite forms than the exact triclinic edge-sharing P1 structure (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). TS evidence supports the broader idea that slightly metastable perovskite-like oxides and tungstates can be prepared by standard high-temperature ceramic routes, but it is less chemically specific than CS/SS (TS1–TS5). The key enabling factor is the robust air-stable W⁶⁺ oxide chemistry and direct same-composition precedent (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). The key limiting factor is polymorph and impurity control, especially avoiding SrWO₄/Sr₂WO₅ and selecting the lower-symmetry P1 arrangement rather than the known monoclinic/tetragonal polymorphs (CS1, CS2; SS4).
