### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.034 eV/atom, so it falls in the most favorable rubric bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. This is consistent with experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues AgCuS and Ag₃CuS₂ that have E_hull ≈0.0306–0.0462 eV/atom (SS-3, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3). CS analogues Tl₂Ni₃S₂ and Tl₂Rh₃S₂ are stable or nearly stable, E_hull ≈0–0.002 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-3), supporting that Tl sulfides can be thermodynamically accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is metastable relative to the hull, not a confirmed ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed-ampoule solid-state conditions, which are strongly supported by CS-1, CS-3, CS-4 and by SS/TS evacuated-tube sulfide syntheses (SS-3–SS-5; TS-1–TS-3). The severity is increased by vacuum/sealed handling, Tl toxicity, and volatile sulfur/Tl species, with the volatility/toxicity interpretation labeled [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Tl-specific toxicity/volatility, so SS is neutral on that claim; CS provides the useful signal because it actually uses sealed Tl sulfide reactions.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state reaction from metals/binary sulfides has moderate kinetics: binary sulfides are used successfully in SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, and TS-3, while Tl-containing sulfides are made by high-temperature sealed reactions in CS-1 and CS-3. However, long holds, slow cooling, and side products are reported in CS-1 and long annealing/mechanical alloying in SS-3/TS-1, indicating nontrivial ordering and phase-control kinetics. Insufficient evidence from TS for Tl-specific diffusion, so Tl-diffusion assessment is partly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 lowers the maximum temperature substantially, based on AgCuS at 300 °C for 50 h (SS-5/TS-3) and Ag₃CuS₂ at ca. 550 K with long annealing (SS-3/TS-1). It still requires evacuated sealed ampoules and toxic Tl sulfide handling, so the severity score remains limited. Insufficient evidence from CS for a low-temperature Cu–Tl–S binary-sulfide route specifically, so CS is neutral for the low-temperature condition claim.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary sulfide precursors should improve chemical compatibility because AgCuS/Ag₃CuS₂ form from binary sulfides in SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, and TS-3. The main kinetic limitation is long-range ordering into the target monoclinic 2D CuTlS structure, because no source directly shows this phase and Ag₃CuS₂ required long annealing/mechanical alloying for high phase purity (SS-3/TS-1). The use of mechanical alloying as an accelerator is a [Reasonable Inference] drawn from SS-3/TS-1.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 benefits from not requiring >1000 °C or high pressure; CsCu₃S₂ flux/melt synthesis occurs at 700–800 °C under Ar (SS-1), and Tl sulfides are compatible with sealed ampoule heating at 800–1000 °C (CS-1, CS-3). The score is reduced by sealed volatile Tl/S species, multi-step flux separation, and Tl toxicity, with Tl-specific risk inferred from the need for sealed Tl syntheses in CS-1/CS-3. Insufficient evidence from TS for flux conditions, so TS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux/reactive melt growth should have the best kinetics because SS-1 shows direct crystal isolation of Cu sulfides from a melt with excess sulfur, and liquid flux improves mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s Cu–S bond length, 2.17 Å, closely matches the linear Cu–S units in CsCu₃S₂ and Ag₃CuS₂, 2.16–2.19 Å (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4), supporting structural transferability. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS-1 indicates different Cu sulfides depend on time/temperature, and CS-1 reports side products in a Tl sulfide system.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is chemically simple and apparently small-cell, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40 atom unit cell or highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network. The large Cu⁺/Tl⁺ size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii], but soft sulfide chemistry and low-dimensional layered structures often crystallize readily under flux or slow-cooling conditions [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-1 and CS-1/CS-3 slow-cooling protocols]. Thus amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuTlS is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.034 eV/atom. The CS analogues strongly support sealed-ampoule Tl sulfide chemistry, while SS/TS analogues strongly support Cu–S linear coordination and low-energy Cu/Ag sulfide formation in evacuated tubes or fluxes. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a sealed sulfide-rich environment or flux to retain volatile Tl/S components and enhance mass transport, supported by CS-1/CS-3 and SS-1. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition/stoichiometry control, because CS-1 reports Tl sulfide side products and SS-1 shows phase selectivity depends on heating time/temperature. Overall, synthesis appears feasible in a well-equipped solid-state/flux laboratory with appropriate Tl safety protocols.
