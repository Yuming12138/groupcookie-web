### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the favorable threshold, so the thermodynamic score is high but not perfect. On-hull or near-on-hull hydrides are common in CS, including BaAlH5, K2PtH6, Rb2PtH6, and Sr2RuH6 (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Structurally similar borohydrides are also experimentally plausible at 0–0.016 eV/atom (SS-1 to SS-4), while TS shows experimentally accessible boron hydride salts even at ~0.08 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull near 0.05 eV/atom may indicate slight metastability relative to CsH/B/H2 or other boron hydrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 mechanochemical metathesis

Route 1 receives a mild-condition F2 score because SS-2, SS-3, and SS-4 show borohydride products formed by ball milling under Ar at room or low temperature, with no high-pressure or high-temperature furnace required. The only major severity penalty is air/moisture sensitivity, which is repeatedly implied by Ar glovebox handling and low-temperature storage in SS-1 to SS-5. Kinetically, salt metathesis is favorable because LiBH4/NaBH4 and CsCl are reactive ionic solids, and analogous MBH4 + metal chloride reactions proceed mechanochemically in SS-2 to SS-5. The uncertainty is phase purity: SS-2 explicitly notes that mechanochemical products often contain several compounds, and TS-1 reports decomposition/impurity sensitivity during milling.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 direct hydrogenation

Route 2 is more severe because hydride analogues often require H2 pressure and elevated temperature, ranging from 7 MPa at 473–513 K in CS-1 to >1500 bar at 500 °C in CS-3 and 50–90 bar at 400–510 °C in TS-3. It is kinetically weaker because direct conversion of CsH/B/H2 into isolated BH4− units requires B–H bond formation from refractory boron, which is not directly demonstrated in SS and is only a [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS support hydride stabilization by H2 pressure, but not specifically alkali tetrahydroborate formation from elemental boron. The remaining uncertainty is whether a lower-pressure pathway exists via activated boron or borane intermediates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 solution metathesis/recrystallization

Route 3 scores well in severity because TS-2, TS-4, and TS-5 show heavy alkali borate salts crystallized at room temperature with simple equipment, and solution ion exchange should be kinetically rapid [Reasonable Inference]. However, the TS compounds are closo-dodecaborates, not BH4−, so hydrolytic robustness is not transferable without caution. SS data strongly indicate that BH4− compounds are usually handled under Ar, not air, especially in SS-1 to SS-5. The main uncertainty is solvent compatibility and whether CsBH4 can be crystallized without hydrolysis or solvate formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple cubic F-43m structure with Cs coordinated by H and B in isolated BH4 tetrahedra, so it lacks the complex mixed polyhedral networks that typically increase amorphization risk. Structurally similar SS borohydrides contain BH4 tetrahedra with comparable B–H distances, but many are more complex frameworks than CsBH4 (SS-1 to SS-5), implying the target should be easier to crystallize. No unit-cell atom-count trigger or mixed-connectivity trigger is evident from the target description. The residual uncertainty is that ball milling can still generate amorphous salt/byproduct mixtures, as noted for mechanochemical products in SS-2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsBH4 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high end of the “highly synthesizable” thermodynamic window. The strongest support comes from SS borohydride metathesis analogues, where metal borohydrides are made from MBH4 and halides by ball milling under Ar (SS-2 to SS-5). CS hydride references support the broader stability of alkali/alkaline hydrides under H2 but are less directly transferable to BH4− salt synthesis. The key enabling factor is the simple ionic Cs+[BH4]− structure with standard BH4 tetrahedra, matching B–H distances in SS borohydrides. The key limiting factor is moisture/air sensitivity and byproduct/phase separation during metathesis, a recurring issue for borohydride mechanochemistry in SS-2 and TS-1.
