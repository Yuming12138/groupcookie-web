### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E above hull is 0.0110 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. CS analogues have similarly low hull energies, including MnSbSe₂Br at 0.0077 eV/atom (CS1), MnSbS₂Br at 0.0138 eV/atom (CS2), and MnBiSe₂I at 0.0058 eV/atom (CS4). SS/TS also support stability of related Cr chalcogenide/chalcogen-halide phases, especially CrSBr at 0.0 eV/atom (SS1/TS4) and NaCr₅Se₈ at 0.0143 eV/atom (TS1). The remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee accessible mixed-anion ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT / Vapor Transport

The CVT route requires vacuum/sealed ampoule handling and volatile halogen/chalcogen species, directly supported by CrSBr synthesis in evacuated quartz with a temperature gradient (SS1/TS4) and NbX₂Y₂ chemical transport (CS5). It avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, so condition severity is moderate rather than extreme. Toxicity/volatility deductions are justified by the iodine/selenium chemistry and sealed iodide selenide precedent in CS4. The uncertainty is that iodine vapor pressure may require lower temperatures than CrSBr, which is an extrapolation rather than directly shown.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT / Vapor Transport

Kinetically, vapor transport is favorable because volatile Cr–I/S/Se species can bypass slow solid-state Cr diffusion; CrSBr formation in 36 h under a gradient supports this (SS1/TS4). The structural reconstruction penalty is small because CrSBr has a very close 2D Cr chalcogen-halide octahedral framework with L-shaped halide coordination (SS1/TS4). However, target ordering of S and Se on distinct anion environments is not demonstrated by SS/TS, so the F3 score is not higher. CS5 supports transport synthesis of 2D chalcogen-halides but also warns of narrow phase windows.

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Ampoule

Direct solid-state synthesis uses moderate temperatures of 500–650 °C based on CS analogues prepared at 500–600 °C for 10 days (CS1–CS3) and MnBiSe₂I at 600 °C for 7 days (CS4). The main severity penalties come from the required evacuated ampoule and volatile/toxic iodine/selenium species. High pressure and ultrahigh temperatures are not needed according to CS1–CS4. SS gives insufficient direct evidence for direct solid-state synthesis of CrSBr-like layers, so SS is used mainly for structural plausibility, not condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Ampoule

Kinetics are less favorable than CVT because Cr metal is refractory and diffusion through solid binary phases can be slow; TS2 confirms direct Cr–Se reactions but gives insufficient detailed conditions. CS1–CS4 show that sealed-tube chalcogen-halides can form over 7–10 days, supporting feasibility but implying slow kinetics. Structural transfer from CS is imperfect because those compounds include Mn–Sb/Bi–chalcogen frameworks rather than a Cr-only 2D layer. Phase impurity in MnBiSe₂I (CS4) and by-products in CrSBr (SS1/TS4) further reduce the kinetic/phase-control score.

#### F2 — Route 3: Molten-Salt Flux

Flux synthesis requires vacuum-sealed tubes, long dwell, slow cooling, and flux handling, as shown for CrSe₂ flux growth at 780 °C for 165 h under vacuum (TS3). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C processing, but it is multi-step and involves toxic/volatile Se/I chemistry. CS and SS provide insufficient direct flux evidence for target-like materials, so they are neutral for this route’s condition severity. The proposed iodide flux is a [Reasonable Inference] to maintain iodine activity and avoid chloride incorporation.

#### F3 — Route 3: Molten-Salt Flux

The flux route has good kinetic potential because molten salts increase mass transport and TS3 demonstrates phase-pure CrSe₂ single crystals via flux. Diffusion limitations from refractory Cr are mitigated by the liquid medium, which is why the ion diffusion score is relatively high. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because CrSe₂ is a 2D Cr–Se octahedral material (TS3), but the target additionally requires S and I incorporation. CS/SS do not directly validate mixed S/Se/I flux ordering, which is the main uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only Cr as the cation, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent based on the target structure. The described formula and sheet do not imply a >40-atom unit cell, and no CS/SS/TS evidence indicates such a large complex cell. The target has edge and corner sharing but no face-sharing octahedra, which is similar to CrSBr’s edge/corner-sharing 2D network (SS1/TS4). Therefore amorphization tendency is low, although stacking disorder in 2D sheets remains a possible synthesis issue not captured by the three checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cr₂SeSI₂ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E above hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom, comparable to successfully synthesized low-hull CS and TS analogues. The strongest support comes from CrSBr, which has a very similar 2D Cr chalcogen-halide octahedral framework and is made by CVT (SS1/TS4). Chemically similar sealed-tube chalcogen-halides show that mixed chalcogen/halogen compositions can be accessed at moderate temperatures (CS1–CS4), but the exact Cr–S–Se–I composition has no direct precedent in the provided data. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport or iodine-buffered sealed-tube chemistry, which can overcome slow Cr diffusion and maintain iodide activity. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: Cr chalcogenides, Cr iodides, and S/Se-disordered or off-stoichiometric products are likely competitors, as suggested by by-products in CrSBr CVT (SS1/TS4), non-phase-pure MnBiSe₂I (CS4), and narrow transport windows in NbX₂Y₂ (CS5).
