### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin specified by the prompt; I assign 90 because it is not clearly below the threshold. Direct Sr2MgWO6 polymorphs in CS/SS have E_hull values of 0.0 and 0.0092 eV/atom [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2], supporting that this chemistry is intrinsically stable or nearly stable. TS contains analogues around 0.054–0.068 eV/atom that are still synthetically accessible [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5], supporting feasibility of slightly metastable oxides. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific triclinic P-1 polymorph is the equilibrium low-temperature phase or a metastable distortion.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses >1000 °C firing and multiple grinding/pelletizing/heating steps, so it receives deductions for high temperature and multi-step processing [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. It does not require high pressure, sealed ampoules, inert/reducing atmospheres, or toxic/air-sensitive precursors according to the reported same-composition syntheses [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. TS gives insufficient target-specific evidence for this exact route but is consistent with standard high-temperature oxide ceramic processing [TS1, TS3, TS5]. The main severity is thermal rather than chemical or atmospheric.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, SrCO3/MgO/WO3 are robust oxide/carbonate precursors, not highly reactive molecular precursors [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. The need for repeated firing and pelletization indicates diffusion and ordering limitations [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. Competing SrWO4/Sr2WO5 impurities reported in the detailed phase reasoning make complete reaction and phase control moderately challenging [CS1; SS1]. However, the direct same-composition evidence strongly reduces uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 still requires high-temperature ceramic firing near or above 1000 °C and multiple steps including MgWO4 preparation and refiring [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids special atmosphere, high pressure, and sealed volatile handling, consistent with CoWO4-derived double perovskite synthesis and Sr2ZnWO6/Sr2CoWO6 air-fired analogues [CS5; SS3, SS4]. The principal added burden is prior precursor synthesis. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence exists for MgWO4 specifically in Sr2MgWO6, so the route is somewhat less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Preformed MgWO4 may improve Mg/W homogeneity and reduce the amount of long-range W redistribution needed [Reasonable Inference from CoWO4 use in CS5]. Nevertheless, conversion to Sr2MgWO6 still requires Sr incorporation, carbonate decomposition, and B-site ordering, so diffusion remains nontrivial [CS5; SS3, SS4]. The structural reconstruction is partial rather than complete, which justifies a better score than Route 1. Uncertainty remains because CS5 is Co-based rather than Mg-based.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 likely requires calcination and final crystallization above 1000 °C, based on the temperatures used for direct Sr2MgWO6 and related Sr2MWO6 phases [CS1, CS2; SS1–SS4]. It is multi-step because it involves solution preparation, gelation, burnout, calcination, and final annealing [Reasonable Inference]. It does not require high pressure or controlled reducing/inert atmosphere; air is compatible with the oxide products in CS/SS [CS2; SS2, SS3, SS4]. Direct sol–gel evidence is absent, so the severity score is based on extrapolation rather than reported synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3

The sol–gel/Pechini route uses highly mixed molecular precursors, which should increase precursor activity and shorten cation diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. This directly addresses the repeated grinding/high-temperature diffusion limitations seen in ceramic routes [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. However, the final ordered perovskite still requires crystallization and B-site ordering, so a high-temperature anneal remains likely [CS2; SS3, SS4]. The main uncertainty is the absence of CS/SS/TS proof for sol–gel Sr2MgWO6.

#### F2 — Route 4

Using the strict rubric, Route 4 is penalized only for multi-step processing because TS4’s ca. 4 GPa is below the >5 GPa deduction threshold [TS4]. In practical terms, the cubic-anvil apparatus and capsule loading make it challenging despite the formal F2 score [TS4]. Direct CS/SS evidence argues high pressure is unnecessary for Sr2MgWO6 because same-composition materials are synthesized at ambient pressure [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. Thus, this is a contingency rather than preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 4

If pre-reacted Sr2MgWO6 is used, high pressure may help convert or retain a denser low-symmetry polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. However, TS4 reports impurity issues in related high-pressure Sr2CuMoO6 samples, indicating phase control is not automatically improved by pressure [TS4]. Short HP dwell times may limit cation ordering unless the precursor is already well reacted [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS source supports HP as necessary for Sr2MgWO6.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has very different cation sizes: large 12-coordinate Sr and much smaller octahedral Mg/W, so the ionic-radius-difference check is triggered [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The B-site framework is a regular corner-sharing MgO6/WO6 double-perovskite network, which lowers amorphization risk compared with mixed-connectivity frameworks [target structure; CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has a >40 atom unit cell. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2MgWO6 is very likely synthesizable, but the specific triclinic P-1 target polymorph may be slightly metastable because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while reported Sr2MgWO6 polymorphs include lower-energy tetragonal/cubic forms [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. The CS and SS evidence is especially strong because same-composition Sr2MgWO6 has already been made by conventional ceramic routes using SrCO3, MgO, and WO3 [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Sr2MgWO6 double-perovskite chemistry, with MgO6 and WO6 octahedra forming a stable corner-sharing framework [CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2]. The main limiting factor is kinetic: Mg/W ordering and suppression of SrWO4/Sr2WO5 impurities require repeated grinding, pelletizing, and high-temperature annealing [CS1; SS1]. A sol–gel-assisted route may improve homogeneity, but that route is inferred rather than directly demonstrated in the provided data.
