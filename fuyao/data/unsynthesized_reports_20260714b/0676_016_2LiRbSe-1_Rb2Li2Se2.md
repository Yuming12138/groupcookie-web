### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom, placing it inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window by the required rubric. CS1/SS1 show an exact-composition RbLiSe phase at E_hull = 0.0, which strongly supports chemical stability of the Rb–Li–Se composition. The uncertainty is polymorph selectivity: the target is metastable relative to the reported ground-state RbLiSe analogue, so thermodynamic feasibility is high but not guaranteed for this exact structure.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild thermal requirements because CS1/SS1 report successful RbLiSe synthesis at 415 °C, far below 1000 °C. The required sealed Ag crucible/quartz ampoule and Ar atmosphere are manageable but indicate volatile/air-sensitive chemistry (CS1/SS1). Related alkali chalcogenides are hygroscopic or air-sensitive, supporting the −10 precursor sensitivity penalty (CS2–CS5, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether sealed low-temperature annealing gives the target metastable polymorph or the known ground-state polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 1

CS1/SS1 directly demonstrate that Rb₂Se and Li₂Se react at 415 °C, indicating favorable kinetics for forming RbLiSe. The 14-day anneal shows that cation ordering or crystal growth is slow, so the structural-reconstruction score is not maximal (CS1/SS1). The target’s different described 2D coordination and nonzero E_hull introduce kinetic/polymorph uncertainty, even though the composition and space group family are strongly supported.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because it adds preparation of Rb₂Se and Li₂Se from elemental Rb/Li/Se, which CS1/SS1 state was done but do not detail. TS5 supports sealed-ampoule synthesis of a rubidium chalcogenide from elements, but elemental alkali metals and selenium require tighter safety and stoichiometric control. This route earns additional deductions for vacuum/sealed operations, multistep processing, and air-sensitive/reactive precursors.

#### F3 — Route 2

The elemental route has good chemical driving force because alkali metals readily form chalcogenides [Reasonable Inference], and TS5 shows Rb₂S can be formed at 623 K in 48 h. However, the pathway must pass through binary formation before ternary ordering, making it kinetically less direct than Route 1. The final ternary formation is supported by CS1/SS1, but target-polymorph control remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses higher annealing temperatures based on related alkali selenides formed at 673–873 K or 870 K (SS2, CS5/SS5), still far below the >1000 °C penalty threshold. It remains a sealed, air-sensitive chalcogenide synthesis and may involve multiple thermal steps such as high-T hold, slow cooling, and lower-T annealing [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly confirms that this higher-temperature protocol stabilizes the target RbLiSe polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 3

Higher temperature should improve diffusion, and SS2/CS5/SS5 show that related alkali selenides can form in days at 673–873 K. However, increased temperature and slow cooling may also favor the thermodynamic ground-state RbLiSe phase rather than the 0.042 eV/atom target polymorph (CS1/SS1; target data). Thus, kinetics for crystallization are favorable, but kinetics for selective metastable-phase trapping are uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Li⁺/Rb⁺ size mismatch triggers C1 and can increase local strain or ordering requirements [Reasonable Inference], although alkali-size mismatch is clearly tolerated in Matlockite-type RbLiSe and related alkali chalcogenides (CS1/SS1; CS2–CS5). The structure is not a large-cell complex material, so C2 is not triggered (target data; CS1/SS1). The target reports face- and edge-sharing tetrahedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face coexistence, so C3 is not triggered by the stated criterion.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target RbLiSe phase is likely metastable rather than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.042 eV/atom, while a closely related exact-composition RbLiSe reference is reported at E_hull = 0.0 (target data; CS1/SS1). The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis of the Rb–Li–Se composition by binary selenide solid-state annealing, especially because CS1/SS1 directly report RbLiSe from Rb₂Se and Li₂Se. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a proven low-temperature, sealed-ampoule route for the same composition (CS1/SS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph competition: long annealing at equilibrium may produce the known ground-state Matlockite RbLiSe rather than the target 2D metastable structure (CS1/SS1; target data). Overall, the material is synthetically plausible, but verification must distinguish the target polymorph from the lower-energy RbLiSe analogue.
