### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score under the required rubric (Target). Chemically similar quaternary Rb–Ln–Cu–S phases are also on or very near the convex hull, which supports the broader stability of complex Cu sulfides with large electropositive cations (CS). SS and TS include several stable compounds, but many are oxides, halides, fluorides, or nitrides rather than Ba–Fe–Cu sulfides, so they provide insufficient transferable chemical signal and are neutral for this factor (SS: 50; TS: 50). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of polymorphic/defect complications [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate high temperatures comparable to 973 K flux syntheses in CS, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied (CS). It still requires sealed ampoules, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, volatile sulfur control, and air-sensitive sulfide reagents, all consistent with CS sealed-tube sulfide practice and BaS handling inference (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient useful evidence for this exact sulfide flux route and are neutral (SS: 50; TS: 50). The main uncertainty is whether a Ba-containing sulfide flux will dissolve Fe/Cu sufficiently without forming competing binary sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Flux growth receives a relatively favorable kinetic score because CS demonstrates single-crystal formation of complex Cu sulfides from reactive A₂Q₃ flux at 973 K with slow cooling (CS). The target is not isostructural to the Rb–Ln–Cu–S analogues, so structural reconstruction is only partial rather than near-template-like (CS; Target). The liquid/flux pathway should mitigate the high melting point of Fe relative to direct solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], but no CS example contains Ba–Fe–Cu simultaneously, which limits confidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The direct sealed-tube route is based on the CS solid-state reaction for RbGd₂CuS₄ at 1173 K for 3 d in sealed fused-silica tubes under Ar/vacuum (CS). This avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but it requires volatile sulfur containment, controlled atmosphere, and air-sensitive sulfide handling (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS are chemically mismatched and therefore neutral for this route (SS: 50; TS: 50). The condition severity is moderate, but ampoule integrity and sulfur pressure are important uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Direct solid-state reaction has poorer kinetics because BaS, FeS, and Cu₂S must interdiffuse and reconstruct into a complex monoclinic phase with many inequivalent sites [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS confirms that multication Cu sulfides can form by solid-state reaction, but the CS route uses slow cooling and high temperature, indicating nontrivial ordering kinetics (CS). The target’s large cell and mixed polyhedral network likely require long annealing or regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS offer insufficient chemical kinetic guidance for this sulfide target and are neutral (SS: 50; TS: 50).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The halide-flux route uses 900 °C and 14 d in evacuated silica tubes, directly supported by CsCl-flux synthesis of complex Cu–Ln sulfides in CS (CS). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, so the severity score is slightly better than the sulfide-flux route. It still incurs penalties for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, sulfur volatility, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient practical support for halide flux in Ba–Fe–Cu–S and are neutral (SS: 50; TS: 50).

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Halide flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth, and CS shows CsCl can support crystallization of complex Cu sulfides at 900 °C (CS). However, the CS halide-flux example was obtained as a by-product, so phase selection may be difficult and structural reconstruction is uncertain (CS). Compared with direct solid-state reaction, flux-mediated diffusion justifies a higher kinetic score [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Ba–Fe–Cu–S has sufficient solubility and correct sulfur activity in chloride flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target triggers all three amorphization/structural-complexity checks: very different cation sizes and coordinations, a likely >40 atom unit cell, and simultaneous corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). This gives a high amorphization/poor crystallization risk score of 35 by the required rubric. CS analogues show that complex sulfide frameworks can crystallize under slow-cooling flux conditions, partly mitigating this risk (CS). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of Ba–Fe–Cu sulfides and are neutral (SS: 50; TS: 50).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₄Fe(CuS₃)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The strongest support comes from CS: multiple chemically similar complex Cu sulfides are synthesized by sealed-tube flux or solid-state methods and have low hull energies (CS). The most critical enabling factor is flux-assisted mass transport and slow cooling, because CS repeatedly shows that complex multication sulfides crystallize under such conditions (CS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target has a low-symmetry, large-cell, mixed-connectivity structure, and the closest halide-flux CS example was obtained as a by-product rather than as a clean primary target (CS; Target). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sulfur activity, dry inert handling, and systematic flux/stoichiometry screening [Reasonable Inference].
