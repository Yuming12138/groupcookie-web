### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0040 eV/atom, placing it within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Chemically similar Ta–M–Te compounds are mostly reported with E_hull = 0 or near zero, including TaOsTe₄, TaIrTe₄, NbIrTe₄, TaRhTe₄, and Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆ (CS-1–CS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar TaTe₂, Ta₂Te₃, and TaCoTe₂ are also stable or near-stable layered tellurides (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5; TS-1–TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee a wide phase field, especially in multicomponent tellurides where impurity phases were reported (CS-5, SS-3/TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The direct solid-state route uses standard sealed-tube telluride conditions: vacuum, Te-containing sealed ampoules, and 900–1000 °C heating, all supported by CS-3, CS-5, and SS-5/TS-2. The severity score is reduced because vacuum and volatile Te require a sealed system, and long multistep heating/regrinding is plausible based on NbIrTe₄ synthesis (CS-3). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure, reactive gas, or extreme >1400 °C temperatures are needed. The main uncertainty is whether Ta₃GeTe₆ forms congruently from elements or whether binary Ta–Te/Ge–Te phases dominate.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetically, elemental Ta is a refractory metal and likely reacts slowly [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long anneals in NbIrTe₄ and Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆ syntheses (CS-3, CS-5). The target’s layered structure is related to Ta tellurides, but starting from elements requires substantial reconstruction, and related materials often require days to weeks (CS-3, CS-5, SS-5/TS-2). Te may act as a transient liquid/vapor transport medium [Reasonable Inference], but there is no direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that this specifically solves Ge incorporation. Thus the kinetics score is relatively low.

#### F2 — Route 2: Chemical vapor transport

CVT avoids >1000 °C reaction temperatures, since TaTe₂ was transported at 873→773 K and related Ta–M–Te compounds at 820→700 °C (SS-1/TS-1, CS-1/CS-2/CS-4). The score is penalized for vacuum ampoule operation, volatile Te/halogen transport agents, multistep setup, and toxic/corrosive I₂ or TeBr₄ handling (SS-1/TS-1, CS-1/CS-2/CS-4). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed. The main uncertainty is the transport chemistry of Ge in the Ta–Ge–Te system, which is inferred rather than directly demonstrated.

#### F3 — Route 2: Chemical vapor transport

CVT has the best kinetic outlook because successful transport and crystallization of layered TaTe₂ and orthorhombic Ta–M–Te sheets are directly supported (SS-1/TS-1, CS-1/CS-2/CS-4, SS-4/TS-5). Vapor transport reduces the need for long-range solid-state diffusion of refractory Ta [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is also less severe when a pre-reacted feed is used, which is standard CVT logic and supported by TaTe₂ transport from source to deposition zone (SS-1/TS-1). The uncertainty is Ge: no direct CS/SS/TS example proves that Ge transports without compositional fractionation.

#### F2 — Route 3: Precursor-assisted solid-state route

This route inherits vacuum and sealed-tube penalties from the Ta₂Te₃ precursor-assisted synthesis, which used TaTe₂ + Ta under vacuum in sealed crucibles/ampoules (SS-2/TS-3). It may require temperatures approaching or slightly exceeding 1000 °C, because Ta₂Te₃ was made at 1150–1410 K (SS-2/TS-3). It is also multistep because TaTe₂ must be prepared or obtained first, and TaTe₂ CVT itself is a 28-day process in the reference (SS-1/TS-1). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or reactive gas is needed.

#### F3 — Route 3: Precursor-assisted solid-state route

Using TaTe₂ improves structural similarity because TaTe₂ is the top structural and thermodynamic analogue and has layered Ta–Te sheets (SS-1/TS-1). The precedent TaTe₂ + Ta → Ta₂Te₃ shows that Ta telluride frameworks can be chemically converted by solid-state annealing (SS-2/TS-3). However, incorporation and ordering of Ge into a ternary orthorhombic Ta₃GeTe₆ phase still requires reconstruction and diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore intermediate: better than elements-only solid-state, but less favorable than CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically distinct cations, Ta and Ge, with significantly different likely radii and bonding preferences [Reasonable Inference], triggering one amorphization-risk check. The structure is crystalline, layered, and composed of well-defined Ta–Te, Ta–Ge, and Ge–Te coordination environments; layered crystalline analogues are abundant in CS/SS/TS (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). However, the unit-cell atom count and explicit polyhedral sharing topology of the target are not provided, so C2 and C3 are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those checks. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃GeTe₆ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0040 eV/atom, within the range normally considered experimentally accessible. The synthesis outlook is well supported by many layered Ta–Te and Ta–M–Te analogues made by sealed-tube solid-state reaction or CVT (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5), although no direct Ta–Ge–Te analogue is provided. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural and thermodynamic compatibility with layered Ta tellurides, especially TaTe₂ and Ta₂Te₃ (SS-1/TS-1, SS-2/TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase and composition control in a volatile Te-rich multicomponent system, since impurities and narrow phase windows are reported in related tellurides (CS-5, SS-3/TS-4). Overall, CVT is favored for obtaining crystalline material, while direct solid-state reaction is a practical first attempt for phase screening.
