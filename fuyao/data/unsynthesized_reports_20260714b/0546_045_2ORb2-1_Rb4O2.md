### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket. Several close analogues are also on the hull, including Cs₃O (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), Rb₃CuO₂ (CS3 / SS5 / TS3), Rb₂HgO₂ (TS4), and Cs/Rb suboxometallates (SS2 / TS2), supporting the plausibility of stable alkali oxide phases. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: CS/SS/TS support stable Rb/Cs oxides generally, but do not explicitly confirm the trigonal P-3m1 Rb₂O polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct controlled oxidation

The condition severity is moderate because the closest direct analogue, Cs₃O, forms from alkali metal and oxygen at only 170 °C under Ar (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The main deductions are for volatile/air-sensitive alkali metal handling and the sealed system, which are supported by the Cs volatilization warning in CS1 / SS1 / TS1 and by the widespread use of sealed Ar systems in Rb oxide syntheses (SS3, TS4, TS5). There is insufficient evidence from CS2 for direct Rb₂O synthesis specifically, so CS2 is treated mainly as evidence for competing Rb₂O₂ rather than as a direct condition template.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct controlled oxidation

Kinetics should be favorable because Rb metal and O₂ are highly reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, and Cs₃O forms at low temperature by direct combination (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The limiting kinetic/phase factor is not diffusion but oxygen chemical potential control: Cs₃O synthesis required heating to decompose higher oxides (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), and Rb₂O₂ is a known Rb–O phase (CS2). Thus Route 1 scores high but not perfect.

#### F2 — Route 2: Solid-state annealing/recrystallization

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because Rb₂O-containing or Rb-rich oxide solid-state analogues use sealed Ag, Cu, Fe, or quartz/glass containers under Ar at moderate temperatures rather than extreme pressure or >1000 °C temperatures (SS3, CS3 / SS5 / TS3, TS4, TS5). The penalties come from sealed volatile/air-sensitive Rb oxide handling, which is consistently reflected by sealed bombs/ampoules and Ar atmospheres in the analogues (SS3, TS4, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS2 for direct annealing of Rb₂O₂ to Rb₂O means CS2 is not used to improve this route’s condition score.

#### F3 — Route 2: Solid-state annealing/recrystallization

Kinetics are acceptable but slower than direct oxidation. RbLiO single crystals required 30 days at 773 K from Li₂O and Rb₂O in sealed Ag bombs (SS3), and Rb₃CuO₂ required 10–14 days at 560–600 °C in sealed Cu cylinders (CS3 / SS5 / TS3), indicating that crystallization/order in Rb oxide solids can be slow. However, if the starting material is already Rb₂O, the structural reconstruction burden is relatively low **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3: Reduction/decomposition of higher oxides

Route 3 is more severe because it is multi-step and combines air-sensitive Rb metal with oxygen-rich Rb oxide. The concept is supported indirectly by the Cs₃O need to decompose higher oxides at elevated temperature (CS1 / SS1 / TS1) and by the existence of Rb₂O₂ (CS2), but no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Rb₂O₂ + Rb → Rb₂O. Therefore, the route is plausible but assigned a lower F2 score.

#### F3 — Route 3: Reduction/decomposition of higher oxides

The reactive Rb/Rb₂O₂ mixture should have good chemical activity **[Reasonable Inference]**, and Rb oxide analogues do not require refractory temperatures (CS3 / SS5 / TS3, TS4). The kinetic risk is structural and chemical: peroxide-to-oxide conversion may be incomplete, and Rb₂O₂ is a real competing phase (CS2). The Cs₃O precedent for decomposing higher oxides supports feasibility but also highlights the narrow oxygen-potential window (CS1 / SS1 / TS1).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary Rb₂O with a single cation species and edge-sharing ORb₆ octahedra in the target description, so none of the three amorphization checks are triggered. This is consistent with several crystalline alkali oxide analogues being synthesized by sealed solid-state routes rather than remaining amorphous (SS3, CS3 / SS5 / TS3, TS4). The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not explicitly report amorphization behavior for trigonal P-3m1 Rb₂O, so the low-risk assignment relies partly on structural simplicity **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂O in the specified trigonal P-3m1 structure is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general synthesis of alkali oxides and Rb-containing oxides under sealed Ar conditions, especially direct alkali metal oxidation for Cs₃O (CS1 / SS1 / TS1) and Rb₂O use in sealed solid-state synthesis (SS3, TS5). The most important enabling factor is the high reactivity and low-temperature mobility of alkali metal/oxide chemistry, as shown by low- to moderate-temperature analogue syntheses (CS1 / SS1 / TS1, SS3, TS4). The most important limiting factor is oxygen stoichiometry control, because higher oxides required decomposition control in the Cs analogue (CS1 / SS1 / TS1) and Rb₂O₂ is a known competing Rb–O phase (CS2). Overall, synthesis is likely feasible, but isolation of the exact trigonal sheet polymorph may require careful annealing and phase verification.
