### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a thermodynamic ground-state phase by the provided target data. SS/TS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable fluorides: CsSnF₃ and RbSnF₃ are on-hull in SS1/TS1 and SS2/TS2, Sn₂OF₂ is on-hull in SS5/TS3, SnF₂ is on-hull in TS5, and Sn₂F₃I is near-hull in SS4/TS4. CS is less directly useful because most CS entries are oxides rather than fluorides; CS2 and CS5 are on-hull, CS1 is near-hull, but CS4 is highly metastable, so CS gives mixed thermodynamic analogy.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with low-temperature sealed fluoride synthesis in SS5/TS3 and closed fluoride synthesis in SS3. The main severity deductions are for sealed vacuum/Ar conditions and likely volatile reduced fluoride control; SS3 explicitly uses closed Ag ampoules and notes impurity formation if ampoules leak. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for InF sealed-tube chemistry, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The route uses a dense metal plus an anhydrous fluoride, so precursor activity is only moderate; SS3’s 10 d fluoride anneals show binary fluoride reactions can be slow. However, indium’s low melting point and the low temperatures of SS5/TS3 and SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2 suggest ion transport should not be severely refractory. Structural reconstruction is the main uncertainty because the target layered PbO-like structure is not directly inherited from In metal or InF₃.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures but requires a sealed, dry, corrosion-resistant ampoule, consistent with SS3’s Ag ampoules for fluoride chemistry. It also requires flux handling and post-reaction flux removal, analogous in spirit to CS5 flux growth, so it is more operationally complex than Route 1. The risk of competing alkali indium fluorides is inferred from SS1/SS2, where alkali fluorides participate in ternary fluoride formation.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives a high kinetics score because molten fluxes improve mass transport and allow dissolution–reprecipitation rather than slow solid diffusion; CS5 demonstrates flux synthesis can suppress metallic impurities in PdO. SS3 supports closed fluoride reactions, while SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2 show fluoride crystallization can occur at relatively low temperatures. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether binary InF rather than ternary alkali indium fluoride crystallizes from the flux.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Solvothermal synthesis uses mild temperatures similar to SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2, but it requires sealed autoclaves, solvent pressure, inert loading, and moisture control. Optional HF or NH₄HF₂ would increase toxicity and corrosion risk. CS gives no useful solvothermal InF signal, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2 and TS5 strongly support rapid fluoride crystallization from solution or solvothermal media, giving high precursor activity and low diffusion difficulty. SS4/TS4 supports the possibility of 2D fluoride sheet crystallization. The limiting uncertainty is chemical rather than kinetic: In(I) may disproportionate or hydrolyze in solution, and CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate stabilization of InF in a solvothermal medium.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is chemically simple, with only one cation and a small reported structural basis, so C1 and C2 are not triggered. The target contains corner- and edge-sharing FIn₄ tetrahedra, but the criterion requires coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing; no face sharing is reported. SS/TS layered fluoride analogues such as SS4/TS4 crystallize rather than amorphize, supporting a low amorphization risk, although direct InF evidence is absent.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target InF is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The SS/TS analogues provide strong support that low-dimensional and low-temperature fluorides can be crystallized by sealed-tube, flux, or solvothermal methods, while CS provides only indirect support because most chemically similar examples are oxides rather than In–F compounds. The most critical enabling factor is the simple 1:1 binary composition combined with an obvious redox-balanced comproportionation route from In and InF₃. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining monovalent indium without disproportionation to In metal + InF₃ or oxidation/hydrolysis during handling. Overall, sealed-tube comproportionation is the best first attempt, with flux growth as a useful second route if crystallinity or phase purity is poor.
