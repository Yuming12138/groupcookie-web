### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, which falls exactly at the favorable threshold in the scoring rubric, so it receives **90/100** rather than a higher value. Thermodynamically similar phases include metastable or near-hull Ba chalcogenides with E_hull values around 0.03–0.10 eV/atom, some of which are synthesizable by sealed-tube or high-pressure methods (TS1–TS5). CS and SS analogues include many near-ground-state Ba chalcogenides, including CS1 at E_hull = 0 and SS1/SS3 near 0, supporting feasibility. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ba₂MnTeSe₂ lies on a narrow tie-line against BaSe/BaTe/MnSe/MnTe secondary phases.

#### F2: Route 1

Direct elemental synthesis is supported by CS1 and SS3, but both require evacuated sealed tubes and slow cooling; TS4 shows repeated annealing may be needed for Ba–transition-metal selenides. The main severity penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile Se/Te, air-sensitive Ba, and multistep sealed-tube handling. CS gives strong evidence for Ba–Te–Se elemental synthesis, SS gives strong evidence for Ba₂MCh₃ structural analogues, and TS gives evidence that Ba–Fe–Se powder synthesis may require iterative phase-purity optimization. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Mn–Te–Se volatility behavior in this specific target.

#### F3: Route 1

Elemental precursors are not maximally reactive, especially Mn metal, so kinetics are moderate. SS3 shows that Ba₂ZnSe₃/Ba₂ZnTe₃ can form from elements, but at 1173 K with long ramp/cooling schedules, while CS1 shows mixed Te/Se Ba₂Ge phases at 800 °C over 5 d. TS4 demonstrates that Ba–transition-metal–selenide formation can require repeated heating and regrinding. The uncertainty is whether Mn enters the tetrahedral site cleanly or forms MnSe/MnTe binaries first.

#### F2: Route 2

Binary chalcogenide sintering reduces elemental volatility but still requires evacuated sealed tubes and inert handling, as shown by SS1, SS2, SS4, and SS5. The route is not high pressure and should remain below 1000 °C if modeled after SS1 and CS1. It receives penalties for vacuum, possible volatile chalcogen adjustment, multistep precursor preparation, and air-sensitive Ba chalcogenides. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports binary Ba–Mn–Te–Se sintering, so TS is neutral for this route except for general Ba–transition-metal chalcogenide phase-purity concerns.

#### F3: Route 2

This route has the best structural transferability from SS1 and SS3 because Ba₂CdSe₃ and Ba₂ZnSe₃ share the Ba₂MCh₃ framework motif with tetrahedral MCh₄ units. Preformed BaSe/BaTe/MnSe should reduce diffusion distances and avoid the slow initial reaction of Ba metal with chalcogens. SS1 reports single-phase Ba₂CdSe₃ at 700–900 °C, supporting favorable kinetics for this framework. The main uncertainty is mixed Se/Te ordering, which is supported chemically by CS1 but not directly demonstrated for Mn.

#### F2: Route 3

Flux growth has the same sealed-tube, inert-handling, and volatile-chalcogen constraints as Routes 1–2, with added flux handling and post-growth separation. CS3 and CS4 support flux routes but also show more elaborate equipment or reactive flux handling, including carbon-coated tubes, glovebox loading, and washing. The temperature can remain below 1000 °C in the proposed adaptation, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Insufficient evidence from SS directly supports flux growth of the exact Pnma Ba₂MCh₃ structure, so SS is neutral for flux-specific severity.

#### F3: Route 3

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because liquid or reactive flux improves mass transport and can stabilize kinetic chalcogenide products; CS4 explicitly states reactive fluxes act as both reagent and reaction medium. CS3 and CS4 show that flux methods can produce single crystals of complex Ba/transition-metal or Mn chalcogenides. However, CS3 also reports that U assistance was essential for single-crystal formation in that system, and CS4 notes variable yields/contamination for some compositions. Thus flux is kinetically attractive but carries phase-selectivity uncertainty.

#### F2: Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is supported by TS1 and TS3 but is operationally severe because it needs multi-anvil or tetrahedral press equipment at ~5 GPa and high temperature. The rubric penalizes high pressure and >1000 °C operation, plus air-sensitive/toxic precursor handling. CS and SS do not require high pressure for close analogues, so they provide insufficient evidence that pressure is necessary for the target. High pressure is therefore treated as a fallback route, not the preferred route.

#### F3: Route 4

High pressure can accelerate reaction and stabilize metastable Ba chalcogenides, as shown by TS1 and TS3. However, short high-pressure holds may not allow optimal Se/Te ordering, and pressure may favor a different dense topology rather than the desired Pnma structure. SS1/SS3 provide stronger structural evidence for ambient-pressure formation of Ba₂MCh₃-type compounds. The main uncertainty is whether the target’s 0.050 eV/atom metastability is best overcome by pressure or simply by careful anion chemical potential control.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Ba/Mn size mismatch, consistent with Ba being high-coordinate and smaller metals forming tetrahedra in SS1/SS3 and CS4. The target also explicitly contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, a feature also present in SS1 for the closest structural analogue. The likely Pnma Ba₂MCh₃-type unit cell is probably below 40 atoms, based on SS1/SS3 structural family inference, but the exact target cell size is not explicitly supplied. The resulting medium risk mainly reflects disorder/defect formation rather than true glass formation under slow-cooled bulk synthesis.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₂MnTeSe₂ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase**, not a strongly unstable compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is favorable because CS1 directly supports Ba–Te–Se solid-state chemistry, SS1/SS3 strongly support the Ba₂MCh₃ structural motif, and CS4 supports Mn–selenide tetrahedral chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of closely related Pnma Ba₂CdSe₃/Ba₂ZnSe₃ analogues that form by conventional sealed-tube methods. The most critical limiting factor is mixed Se/Te and Mn²⁺ ordering without forming BaSe/BaTe/MnSe/MnTe secondary phases. Overall, the best practical strategy is sealed-tube binary-chalcogenide sintering for powders and flux-assisted growth for crystals.
