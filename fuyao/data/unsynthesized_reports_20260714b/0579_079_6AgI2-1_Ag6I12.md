### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0230 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom range assigned 90–100. Thermodynamically similar synthesized materials include TS-1 at E_hull ≈0.021 eV/atom and TS-5 at ≈0.031 eV/atom, showing that similar low-positive-hull soft halides can be isolable. SS analogues are mostly at 0 eV/atom and synthesized in sealed ampoules, supporting feasibility for related Ag/Hg iodide frameworks. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for AgI₂ stability because CdI₂ polytypes are chemically different, so CS is treated as neutral-to-supportive only.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-ampoule iodination.**  
F2 is reduced mainly because vacuum sealing and volatile iodine are required, consistent with sealed evacuated ampoule methods in SS-1, SS-5, and TS-1. The low temperature keeps severity moderate, since analogous Ag–I and Cu–I soft halides are made at 120 °C to 553 K rather than refractory temperatures. F3 is favorable for diffusion because Ag/I halides are soft and iodine can transport through vapor, but structural reconstruction into a P1 1D ribbon may require long annealing; this is consistent with the 4-month SS-1 reaction and 2-week TS-1 reaction.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, vapor-phase crystallization.**  
F2 is lower than Route 1 because a vapor-transport gradient adds operational complexity, and volatile iodine must be contained. CS-1 directly supports vapor growth for CdI₂, and TS-3 supports gas-phase growth of complex halides in evacuated quartz ampoules. F3 is limited by composition control: the target is iodine-rich, and CS/TS do not directly quantify the AgI₂ vapor-phase stability window, so this uncertainty is scored conservatively.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, solution crystallization.**  
F2 is favorable because CS-2 to CS-5 demonstrate mild slow-evaporation or solution growth of iodide crystals. F3 is kinetically favorable because solution growth avoids solid diffusion, but the target is not a simple CdI₂ polytype and AgI has poor solubility without iodide complexation **[Reasonable Inference]**. Phase purity is the main uncertainty because CS-2 to CS-5 note polytype/disorder difficulties even for CdI₂.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, hydrothermal/solvothermal growth.**  
TS-5 shows that concentrated hydrohalic acid hydrothermal growth can yield high-quality halide crystals in sealed vessels. F2 is moderate because sealed corrosive HI/I₂ media and autogenous pressure are required, although no high pressure above 5 GPa is involved. F3 benefits from liquid-phase transport, but direct evidence for binary AgI₂ hydrothermal stability is absent from CS/SS/TS, so the structural-reconstruction and phase-selectivity score is conservative.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, Ag, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered. The described cell contains six Ag and twelve I inequivalent sites, below the >40 atom threshold. Although the target has multiple Ag coordination environments, the provided structure description does not prove coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, and SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for amorphization in this exact binary Ag–I system.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a slightly metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.023 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as TS-1 and TS-5. The strongest support comes from SS analogues using AgI in evacuated ampoules and from TS analogues showing that low-positive-hull soft halides can be synthesized under sealed conditions. CS analogues strongly support mild iodide crystallization and vapor/solution growth, but they are CdI₂ polytypes and do not directly prove AgI₂ redox stability. The most critical enabling factor is the softness and mobility of Ag–I/I₂ chemistry, which should permit low-temperature equilibration. The most critical limiting factor is control of iodine chemical potential: too little iodine gives AgI, while too much or too high a temperature may yield iodine-rich molecular/polyiodide byproducts or decomposition.
