### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0020 eV/atom, giving a score of 98 because it is essentially on the convex hull [Target]. The direct Nb(SBr)₂ analogue is also near-hull at 0.0008 eV/atom, which strongly supports the stability of the same layered M(SBr)₂ topology [CS1, SS1, TS2]. Thermodynamic analogues in TS are also close to the hull, including Pd(Se₃Br)₂ at 0.0035 eV/atom and AgVP₂S₆ at 0.0069 eV/atom [TS1, TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic, because low E_hull does not guarantee clean phase selection [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderate in severity because the direct analogue forms at only 500 °C for 2 days in a sealed glass tube [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The main deductions are for Br₂ volatility and hazardous Br₂/Cd handling [CS1, SS1, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide the same useful signal here because the same Nb(SBr)₂ entry appears in all three categories [CS1, SS1, TS2]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that an inert or vacuum atmosphere is strictly required for Route 1, so no reactive-atmosphere deduction was applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is the direct structural analogue Nb(SBr)₂, which forms in a short 2-day hold despite using elemental Nb [CS1, SS1, TS2]. V metal is refractory and may react slowly, but Br₂/S/Cd likely provide a reactive halogen/chalcogen transport medium [CS1, Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is high because the target and Nb analogue share the same C2/m layered motif [Target, CS1/SS1/TS2]. The main uncertainty is polymorph or competing-phase control, since the Nb synthesis produced both polymorphs simultaneously [CS1, SS1, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because CVT analogues require evacuated sealed ampoules, temperature gradients, and often long durations [CS2, SS4, CS4]. The NbX₂Y₂ family requires 400–515 °C for 3–4 weeks with a controlled 5–20 °C gradient [CS2], and NbSeBr₃ requires 15 days at 500–460 °C [SS4]. Br₂ or S₂Br₂ volatility and toxicity produce additional deductions [SS4, CS4]. Insufficient evidence from TS supports a direct V(SBr)₂ CVT route, so TS is treated as neutral, 50, for this route-specific condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves transport kinetics by moving material through volatile halide/chalcogenide species, as shown for NbX₂Y₂ and NbSeBr₃ analogues [CS2, SS4]. However, the target phase is not directly reported by CVT, and the NbX₂Y₂ family shows phase-control sensitivity and polymorphism [CS2]. Precursor activity is intermediate because elemental V is refractory, while Br₂/S₂Br₂ are chemically aggressive and can activate transport [CS4, Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether transport favors the desired 2D V(SBr)₂ phase or simpler V bromides/sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has mild temperature requirements because TS1 used ~180 °C hydrothermal conditions for a bromoselenide [TS1]. It still requires sealed quartz ampoules and a steel autoclave in concentrated acid, which adds handling complexity [TS1]. The route is penalized for HBr volatility/corrosivity and multi-step autoclave handling [TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS supports hydrothermal formation of the target structure, so CS and SS are neutral, 50, for this route-specific condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis can provide high precursor mobility at low temperature, as shown by formation of Pd(Se₃Br)₂ crystals at 453 K in HBr/HCl media [TS1]. The kinetic score is reduced because TS1 is a Pd–Se–Br 1D ribbon, not a V–S–Br 2D C2/m sheet [TS1, Target]. Vanadium redox/hydrolysis chemistry may divert the reaction toward vanadium oxides, sulfides, or bromides rather than V(SBr)₂ [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for this hydrothermal pathway and are treated as neutral, 50, for this route-specific kinetic factor.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a single cation, so the mixed-cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The structure is a small layered M(SBr)₂ type rather than a large multicomponent cell, as supported by the direct Nb₂S₄Br₄ analogue [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The target description does not report coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not applied [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is low because the direct analogue crystallizes as red crystals rather than amorphous material [CS1, SS1, TS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V(SBr)₂ is very likely a near-ground-state phase rather than a highly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0020 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis evidence is unusually strong because the direct Nb(SBr)₂ congener has the same C2/m layered structure and was synthesized in a simple sealed tube at 500 °C [CS1, SS1, TS2]. The most important enabling factor is halogen-assisted reactivity: Br₂ can provide both the bromide ligand and a reactive transport/melt environment that allows formation at moderate temperature [CS1, CS2, SS4]. The main limiting factor is phase control, especially avoiding competing vanadium sulfides, vanadium bromides, and possible polymorph mixtures [CS1, CS2, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, Route 1 should be attempted first, with CVT as a follow-up if larger single crystals are required [CS1, SS1, TS2, CS2, SS4].
