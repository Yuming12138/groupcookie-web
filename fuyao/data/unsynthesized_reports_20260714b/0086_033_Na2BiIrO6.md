### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the favorable boundary for likely synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull around 0.055–0.096 eV/atom were experimentally obtained, but often by ion exchange, hydrothermal synthesis, or careful phase control rather than simple equilibrium synthesis (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). This supports a high but not perfect F1 score. Remaining uncertainty is whether the peroxide-like O–O unit makes the target metastable with respect to oxide + peroxide decomposition **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: NaOH Flux

NaOH flux synthesis is strongly supported for chemically similar Na–Ln–Ir oxides at 700 °C in sealed silver tubes (CS-2, CS-3). The condition severity is moderate because no external high pressure is required, but sealed corrosive hydroxide melts and multi-step isolation are necessary (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetics are improved by molten flux transport, yet Ir metal remains refractory and phase control in related flux-grown iridates can be narrow (CS-1, CS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS for hydroxide-flux behavior of Bi-containing target; TS is scored neutral as indirect support only.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Oxidizing Solid-State

Oxidizing solid-state chemistry is supported by Na₃OsO₅ synthesis from Na₂O₂/Os under O₂ at 400 °C and by NaIrO₃ synthesis using Na₂O₂/NaClO₃ (SS-1, SS-2). However, chemically similar CS materials are not made by direct solid-state reaction, and CS instead favors flux growth; therefore CS provides negative/weak support for this route. Kinetics are likely poor because Ir-containing oxide formation from refractory powders requires long diffusion paths **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS solid-state analogues show that sodium oxide volatility, repeated firings, and quenching can be needed for phase control (TS-3, TS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Alkaline Peroxide Hydrothermal

The target contains a short O–O distance of 1.44 Å, making peroxide-like chemistry plausible **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structurally similar K₃TaO₈ was crystallized from KOH/H₂O₂ solution, directly supporting alkaline peroxide chemistry for O–O-containing oxides (SS-5). A thermodynamic analogue was obtained hydrothermally at 200 °C for 7 days with slow cooling, supporting low-temperature autogenous-pressure crystallization (TS-2). The main uncertainty is that CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Na–Ir–Bi synthesis, so CS is treated as neutral/50 for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-Pressure Oxidizing

High-pressure oxidizing synthesis is directly supported for structurally similar NaIrO₃, which required 4.5 GPa, 800 °C, Na₂O₂/NaClO₃, and an Au capsule (SS-1). TS also supports high-pressure stabilization for some perovskite-related oxides, such as InRhO₃ at 6 GPa and 1500 K (TS-4). The severity score is low because specialized high-pressure apparatus and oxidizing sealed capsules are required. CS provides insufficient evidence that the target itself requires high pressure, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines Na, Ir, and Bi cations with large ionic size and bonding differences, triggering the cation-radius mismatch check **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target structure is low-symmetry P1 and contains several different Na, Ir, Bi, and O coordination environments, which increases configurational complexity, but the described primitive atom count is not >40. The target has corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with F4 = 80.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂IrBiO₆ is likely near the boundary between ground-state and metastable behavior because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The chemically similar Na–Ir oxides strongly support hydroxide flux synthesis of Na–Ir oxide frameworks, while structurally similar peroxide/oxo compounds support low-temperature alkaline peroxide chemistry for O–O-containing structures (CS-2, CS-3, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining an oxidizing, strongly alkaline medium that can stabilize both high-valent Ir/Bi chemistry and the short O–O unit **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing Na–Ir oxides, bismuth oxides/bismuthates, and decomposition of peroxide-like oxygen units are all plausible **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires careful low-temperature crystallization or flux optimization rather than routine ceramic firing.
