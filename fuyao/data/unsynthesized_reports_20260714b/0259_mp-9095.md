### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest stability bin in the required rubric, so F1 = 90. CS and SS support this favorable assessment because Dy10Si17 and Ho10Si17 are on the hull and TbSi2 is only 0.0256 eV/atom above hull (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-1–3). TS provides a more metastable but still experimentally accessible comparison, with YSi2/ErSi2/HoSi2-type analogues around 0.06–0.11 eV/atom being synthesized (TS-1–5). Remaining uncertainty is that Tb2Si3 may compete with nearby TbSi2/Tb5Si3-type phases not explicitly listed in the sources [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 Flux Growth

The route avoids extreme temperature and pressure because Dy/Ho flux analogues use 800–1000 °C and ambient-pressure sealed quartz ampoules (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3). Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, multistep processing, and rare-earth air sensitivity, all consistent with the reported vacuum ampoules and post-growth electrochemical dissolution (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that volatile precursor loss is the key limitation for Sn/In flux at these temperatures, so no volatility deduction was applied. The resulting F2 score is 65.

#### F3 — Route 1 Flux Growth

Flux growth improves transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, as shown by Dy/Ho RESi1.7 formation from molten Sn/In flux at 800–1000 °C (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3). However, the same sources state that the orthorhombic phase was rarely obtained and other methods failed, lowering the structural-reconstruction/phase-selection score (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3). The target is compositionally close but not identical to RESi1.7, so transfer is plausible but not guaranteed [Reasonable Inference]. The route receives F3 = 62.

#### F2 — Route 2 Direct Solid-State

Direct Ta-ampoule synthesis is experimentally simple and near 1000 °C, based on TbSi2/ErSi2/HoSi2 analogues at 1270 K (CS-2, CS-4; SS-1; TS-2, TS-3). The main severity penalties are sealed/controlled atmosphere and air-sensitive Tb handling [Reasonable Inference; sealed ampoule supported by CS-4/SS-1]. There is a conflict in the sources because some entries list “Lithium, Silicon” as precursors while the evidence says synthesis from elements; therefore, only the ampoule/temperature method is weighted, not the listed Li precursor (CS-2, CS-4; SS-1; TS-2, TS-3). F2 = 75.

#### F3 — Route 2 Direct Solid-State

Elemental Tb and Si are dense/high-melting precursors, so precursor activity is low to moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion is feasible near 1000 °C because RE disilicide analogues are prepared at 1270 K in Ta ampoules (CS-2, CS-4; SS-1; TS-2, TS-3). The structural match is weaker than flux RESi1.7 analogues because TbSi2 is tetragonal I41/amd while target Tb2Si3 is orthorhombic Cmcm (CS-4; SS-1). F3 = 50.

#### F2 — Route 3 Thin Film

Thin-film evaporation has low thermal severity because YSi2 analogues form by evaporation/annealing at 423–773 K (SS-4; TS-1). It still requires high vacuum and multiple controlled steps such as deposition and annealing (SS-4; TS-1). Air sensitivity of Tb adds a handling penalty [Reasonable Inference]. F2 = 65.

#### F3 — Route 3 Thin Film

Vapor availability is favorable because rare-earth metal and silicon evaporation is directly demonstrated for YSi2 films (SS-4; TS-1). Crystallization receives a moderate score because annealing is required rather than direct proof of target Tb2Si3 crystallization (SS-4; TS-1). Composition control is likely the biggest kinetic/processing challenge because CS/SS/TS disilicide references emphasize narrow phase widths and phase sensitivity (CS-4; SS-1; TS-2, TS-3). F3 = 60.

#### F2 — Route 4 High Pressure

The route is severe because TS-5 uses 12–15 GPa and temperatures up to 1400 K for rare-earth silicon-rich phases. It also requires glovebox loading, h-BN capsules, graphite heaters, and multi-anvil/DAC-type equipment (TS-5). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS for high-pressure Tb2Si3 specifically, so this route is treated as fallback rather than preferred. F2 = 45.

#### F3 — Route 4 High Pressure

Binary silicide precursors may reduce diffusion distances, as TS-5 uses CaSi2/YSi2/LuSi2-type precursors for high-pressure silicides. High pressure and high temperature can enable metastable framework formation, but the exact target composition and structure are not directly demonstrated in TS-5. Because the structural transfer from YSi3 to Tb2Si3 is partial, the reconstruction score is moderate. F3 = 58.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tb2Si3 is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support that rare-earth silicide networks with similar Tb/RE–Si and Si–Si bonding can be made, especially by flux growth and sealed ampoule methods (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-1–3). The most critical enabling factor is the use of a molten Sn/In flux to improve mass transport and access orthorhombic rare-earth silicide crystals at 800–1000 °C (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the closest orthorhombic Dy/Ho phases were rarely obtained, and competing defect AlB2-type or disilicide phases are likely (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require compositional screening and careful flux/cooling optimization.
