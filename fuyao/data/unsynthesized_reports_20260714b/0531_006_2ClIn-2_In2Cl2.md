### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.036 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket. The exact-composition InCl analogue is even closer to the hull at ≈0.003 eV/atom (CS1/SS1), strongly supporting chemical feasibility. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable or slightly higher E_hull values were synthesized by ampoule, solid-state, or solvothermal methods (TS2–TS5), so modest metastability does not preclude synthesis. The uncertainty is that the target Pmmn polymorph may be metastable relative to another InCl polymorph, as implied by the different Cmcm InCl analogue (CS1/SS1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is conditionally severe because the direct InCl analogue required UHV, an effusion cell, cryogenic to room-temperature substrates, and controlled vapor pressure (CS1/SS1). These factors trigger deductions for controlled atmosphere/vacuum, volatile precursor handling, and multi-step thermal control. No high-temperature or high-pressure penalty applies because the substrate was kept between −228 °C and room temperature (CS1/SS1). TS provides insufficient direct evidence for atomic-beam deposition of InCl, so TS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from excellent vapor availability because InCl was directly evaporated from α-InCl powder (CS1/SS1). The limiting factor is crystallization pathway: the analogue describes metastable β-InCl formation and phase transitions under narrow thermal windows, implying that the target Pmmn ribbon phase may require kinetic trapping (CS1/SS1). Composition control is relatively favorable because a single-source InCl precursor is used, but polymorph rather than stoichiometry is the major uncertainty (CS1/SS1). Insufficient evidence from TS for thin-film crystallization behavior of InCl; TS is neutral here.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids UHV equipment but requires sealed ampoules, dry/inert atmosphere, volatile chloride management, and possibly InCl₃ purification by sublimation in dry HCl (CS3). The temperature around 350–460 °C is moderate, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS3). The long sealed-ampoule workflow and air/moisture-sensitive halides justify multi-step and handling penalties (CS3; SS4/TS1/TS2). SS gives indirect support through evacuated halide ampoule methods, but insufficient evidence from SS for direct InCl comproportionation, so SS is only partially supportive.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic score is moderate because In/InCl₃ chloride chemistry should be much more reactive than refractory oxide solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by CS3]. Ion diffusion is favorable due to low-melting In and halide melt/vapor transport, while CS3 confirms sealed melt-tempering of In–Cl compositions. The weak point is structural reconstruction and phase selection: CS1/SS1 show InCl polymorphism, and CS3 required long tempering for indium chloride phases. Thus Route 2 may make InCl readily, but not necessarily the target Pmmn polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the same general sealed-ampoule/halide-vapor environment as structurally and thermodynamically similar halides, so vacuum/controlled atmosphere and volatile precursor penalties apply (SS2/SS4/TS1). The temperature remains moderate, around 250–475 °C by extrapolation from related InBr₃ flux systems and InCl volatility (SS2/SS4/TS1; CS1/SS1). Toxic/air-sensitive handling is expected because related halides require evacuated ampoules and protective atmospheres (SS4/TS1/TS2). The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source explicitly reports Pmmn InCl from vapor transport.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux/vapor transport receives the best kinetic score because molten/vapor halide media provide high precursor activity and overcome diffusion barriers, as demonstrated for InBr₃-based ribbon halides at 450 °C for 5 days and related flux growth at 475 °C (SS2/SS4/TS1). InCl volatility is directly supported by atomic-beam deposition (CS1/SS1), making transport plausible. The structural reconstruction score remains limited because the target is a specific 1D Pmmn polymorph and direct phase selectivity evidence is absent. This route is therefore kinetically attractive but experimentally uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a simple binary InCl with only one cation species, so cation-radius disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description gives L-shaped two-coordinate bonding in 1D ribbons, not a complex network of mixed vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. No evidence indicates a large >40 atom unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any large-cell amorphization risk, so that source signal is neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low, although thin-film deposition may still initially produce amorphous InCl as seen in the analogue route (CS1/SS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target InCl Pmmn phase is likely metastable rather than the dominant ground-state polymorph, because its E_hull is 0.036 eV/atom and a different near-ground-state InCl polymorph is reported at much lower E_hull in CS1/SS1. The CS/SS evidence is strong for making InCl chemically, especially through direct InCl evaporation, but weaker for selecting the exact Pmmn 1D ribbon structure. TS analogues support the broader idea that halide materials with similar modest metastability can be synthesized in sealed ampoules or flux media. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/volatility of indium chlorides, which allows vapor, melt, and flux pathways (CS1/SS1; CS3; SS2/SS4/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: avoiding the known Cmcm/α-InCl form and trapping the Pmmn ribbon arrangement.
