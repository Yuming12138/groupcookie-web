### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom, within the high-probability ≤0.05 eV/atom window. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally reported, including VTe₂ at 0.0185 eV/atom and UTe₅ at 0.0462 eV/atom (TS1, TS5), although TS5 has only medium-confidence synthesis evidence. The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee the specific ordered V₃WTe₈ polymorph over mixtures of VTe₂, WTe₂, or other tellurides; phase competition is noted in CS1, CS3, SS1, and SS2.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
This route is supported by direct elemental synthesis of VTe₂/V₁₊ₓTe₂ in evacuated ampoules at ~1000 K for 1 week (SS1, SS2; TS1) and by related layered tellurides made at 1000 °C in sealed evacuated tubes (CS1–CS3). Its condition severity is moderate because vacuum sealing, volatile Te, and long multistep heating are needed, but no high pressure or >1000 °C condition is strictly required. Kinetics are the weakness: elemental W is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and related syntheses require long anneals/reheating or show impurity phases (CS1–CS3, SS1, SS2, SS5).

**F2/F3 — Route 2 CVT.**  
CVT has direct support from structurally and thermodynamically related tellurides grown with I₂ at 675–750 °C (SS4, TS3) and W–Te transport using TeBr₄ at 820/700 °C (CS4, CS5). The severity remains moderate because sealed vacuum tubes and corrosive halogen transport agents are required. Kinetics are better than direct solid-state because vapor transport reduces reliance on cation diffusion, but the major uncertainty is whether V and W transport at compatible rates and preserve V₃WTe₈ stoichiometry; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for congruent V₃WTe₈ CVT.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Te-rich flux.**  
Te-rich synthesis is inferred from NbIrTe₄ and TaPtTe₅ examples where excess Te or Te-rich ratios improve formation (CS2, CS3), and it is chemically consistent with VTe₂ formation in evacuated Te-containing ampoules (SS1, SS2; TS1). Severity is similar to Route 2 because Te volatility and sealed-tube handling dominate. Kinetics should be improved over dry solid-state by molten/vapor Te [Reasonable Inference], but Te-rich conditions may leave elemental Te or favor binaries, consistent with Te impurity issues in VTe₂ syntheses (SS1, SS2).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a crystalline layered telluride with simple six-coordinate V/W–Te local environments, and close structural analogues VTe₂/V₁₊ₓTe₂ form crystalline layered sheets rather than amorphous products (SS1, SS2; TS1). The formula unit has only 12 atoms, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence is explicitly described for the target; however, CS/SS/TS do not directly measure amorphization tendency. The main uncertainty is cation ordering disorder between V and W sites rather than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃WTe₈ is likely a mildly metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its energy above hull is only 0.038 eV/atom, within the commonly synthesizable range. The strongest support comes from structurally similar VTe₂/V₁₊ₓTe₂ layered phases with nearly identical V–Te bond lengths around 2.72 Å (SS1, SS2; TS1), combined with W–Te CVT chemistry in related systems (CS4, CS5). The most critical enabling factor is sealed-tube telluride chemistry, because all relevant analogues rely on evacuated ampoules/tubes to control Te volatility and oxidation (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: related tellurides frequently show impurity phases or need excess Te, transport agents, or repeated heating (CS1–CS3, SS1, SS2, SS5). Overall, CVT or vapor-assisted Te-rich synthesis is the best route because it improves crystallization kinetics while staying within demonstrated telluride synthesis conditions.
