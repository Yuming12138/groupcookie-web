### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. TS analogues include multiple hull-stable borates, including SnB4O7, Sr/Ba pentaborate hydroxides, Pr(BO2)3, and Pb borate hydrate (TS1–TS5), supporting that related borate frameworks can be thermodynamic ground states. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee easy kinetic access or tolerance to fluoride loss [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis is supported by TS2/TS4 high-temperature sealed silver ampoule reactions and TS5 low-temperature Teflon autoclave synthesis. The route avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa, but fluoride precursors are toxic/corrosive and require sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for hydrothermal Ag oxyfluoroborates; insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal fluoride incorporation, so those signals are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics.**  
TS5 demonstrates that complex borate crystals can form from reactive solution/gel chemistry at 170 °C over 6 days, and TS2/TS4 show pentaborates under mineralized hydrothermal conditions. Solution transport should reduce solid diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is competition between F− and OH−/H2O, which may yield hydroxoborates rather than Ag2B5O8F [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 flux growth.**  
Flux growth is strongly supported by CS2–CS5 and TS3, with temperatures mostly 850–1150 °C and controlled cooling. For the Ag/F target, the proposed flux route is kept below or near 950 °C to reduce F/Ag volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Penalties arise from volatile fluoride, multi-step flux processing, and toxic/corrosive fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 flux kinetics.**  
CS2–CS5 show that borate frameworks crystallize well from Bi–B, Mo, sulfate/molybdate, and potassium molybdate fluxes; TS3 shows NaCl flux can favor single-crystal borate formation. A liquid flux should promote rearrangement of BO3/BO4 units and relieve diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because flux components may form AgCl, Ag metal, or contaminant-containing phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 sealed solid-state.**  
CS1 and TS3 support sealed-tube borate synthesis, but their temperatures are 850–950 °C, whereas Ag/F chemistry likely requires lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The route incurs penalties for vacuum/sealed handling, volatile fluoride, and fluoride toxicity. Insufficient evidence from SS for ambient-pressure sealed-tube formation of the target topology; SS analogues instead emphasize high-pressure access, so SS is neutral-to-negative for this route.

**F3 — Route 3 sealed solid-state kinetics.**  
Solid-state diffusion is less favorable than hydrothermal or flux transport, and CS1 required 12–36 h at 900–950 °C for a chemically similar borate. The target’s five inequivalent B sites and ordered F site imply significant reconstructive ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The likely risk is amorphous borate glass or incomplete crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 high pressure.**  
SS1/SS2/SS4 and TS1 show highly similar dense borates synthesized at 7.5–10.5 GPa and around 1000–1100 °C. The major severity penalty is the >5 GPa pressure and specialized multianvil workflow. The proposed Ag/F adaptation lowers temperature, but fluoride containment and toxicity remain penalties [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4 high-pressure kinetics.**  
High pressure can rapidly crystallize dense borate frameworks within minutes, as shown by SS1/SS2/SS4/TS1. Structural similarity is a positive signal for framework formation. However, high pressure may be unnecessary for a hull-stable Ag oxyfluoroborate and may worsen capsule/fluoride reactivity [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a complex low-symmetry framework with many inequivalent sites, including five inequivalent B sites and two inequivalent Ag sites from the target structure, which increases configurational/crystallization complexity [Reasonable Inference]. The structure appears to use corner-sharing borate units rather than mixed edge/face sharing, which reduces topological frustration. SS2 reports amorphous phases or impurities at lower pressure for a structurally similar borate, suggesting borate frameworks can vitrify if conditions are not well tuned.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2B5O8F is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports borate crystallization by flux and solid-state methods, while TS evidence strongly supports hydrothermal growth of complex pentaborates and hull-stable borate frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is solution or flux transport, because it can assemble mixed BO3/BO4/BO3F units without relying on slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS2–CS5 and TS2/TS4/TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ag(I), fluoride activity, and borate polymerization, because excessive water may form hydroxoborates and excessive halide may form AgF/AgCl-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is favorable but condition-sensitive, with hydrothermal crystallization the best first attempt.
