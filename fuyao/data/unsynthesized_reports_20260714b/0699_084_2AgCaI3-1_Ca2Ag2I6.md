### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0160 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom band and close to the thermodynamic analogues TlMnI3 and TlCdI3, which have E_hull values near 0.017 eV/atom and are reported as synthesized (TS). CS and SS include many iodide analogues at E_hull = 0 or near zero, showing that ternary iodides in this energy range are experimentally accessible (CS, SS). The remaining uncertainty is that CaAgI3 is slightly metastable, so polymorph or decomposition competition cannot be excluded [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because similar iodides form at 573 K to 300–400 °C, and some iodide solid-state routes extend to 650 °C (CS, SS, TS). The deductions arise from sealed/vacuum or Ar handling, volatile iodide risk, and air-sensitive CaI2-like halide handling [Reasonable Inference based on iodide handling in CS/SS]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is indicated by any analogue (CS, SS, TS).

#### F3 — Route 1

Melt growth gives high kinetic accessibility because NaInI4, KGaI4, and NaAlI4 crystallize from molten binary iodide mixtures under slow cooling (CS, SS). Ion transport in a molten halide phase should be much faster than in dense solids [Reasonable Inference], and this is consistent with analogue syntheses at modest temperatures (CS, SS). The main uncertainty is whether CaAgI3 requires a narrow Ca:Ag:I chemical-potential window to avoid CaI2 or AgI secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it likely requires multiple grind/anneal cycles; Ag/Cs iodide solid-state syntheses explicitly used pellets with intermediate grindings until single-phase material was obtained (SS). Evacuated ampoules are supported by both Ag/Cs iodides and Sr iodide analogues (SS, TS). The route still avoids high pressure and very high temperature, consistent with the halide analogue data (SS, TS).

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary iodides are reasonably reactive, but solid-state transformation from CaI2 + AgI into a 2D ternary framework requires reconstruction and cation ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Similar Ag/Cs halides required repeated annealing/grinding for phase purity (SS), and TlFeI3 required repeated powdering/annealing to obtain pure sample (TS). Therefore, kinetics are acceptable but inferior to melt growth.

#### F2 — Route 3

The hydrothermal route has mild temperature, since AgTl2I3 was grown in HI at 155–175 °C (TS). The main severity penalties are toxic/corrosive HI and multi-step autoclave handling [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence from TS for high pressure beyond autogenous pressure, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS for hydrothermal Ca–Ag iodide formation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution transport should reduce diffusion limitations, and AgI can react in HI to form an Ag ternary iodide as shown by AgTl2I3 (TS). However, target CaAgI3 is anhydrous, while aqueous HI may stabilize hydrated or solvated calcium iodide phases [Reasonable Inference]. The AgTl2I3 analogue also reported twinning/low untwinned yield, indicating crystal-quality or phase-control difficulty (TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines CaI6 octahedra and AgAgI3 trigonal pyramids, so there is substantial cation coordination mismatch and mixed corner/edge connectivity [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Mixed polyhedral networks are also common in CS/SS iodide analogues, but those analogues crystallize successfully, suggesting amorphization risk is not prohibitive (CS, SS). Insufficient evidence from TS directly addresses amorphization tendency in Ca–Ag iodides, so the F4 score remains medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaAgI3 is likely a slightly metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.016 eV/atom, close to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as TlMnI3 and TlCdI3 (TS). The strongest support comes from CS and SS, where several ternary iodides crystallize from sealed melts or ampoules under moderate temperatures and slow cooling (CS, SS). The most critical enabling factor is the use of an anhydrous sealed iodide melt, which provides high ion mobility while suppressing iodine/moisture loss (CS, SS). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with binary CaI2 and AgI or alternative Ca–Ag–I polymorphs during cooling [Reasonable Inference]. Hydrothermal HI chemistry is plausible for Ag iodides but less reliable for an anhydrous Ca phase because hydrated calcium iodides may compete [Reasonable Inference; TS].
