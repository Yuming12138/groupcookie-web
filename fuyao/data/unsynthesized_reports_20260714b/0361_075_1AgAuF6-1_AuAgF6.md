### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high F1 score. Similar Au fluorides in CS, SS, and TS also report E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Ag(AuF₄)₂, Cd(AuF₄)₂, Hg(AuF₄)₂, and LiAuF₄ (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4; CS-5 / SS-2 / TS-2; SS-1 / TS-1; SS-3 / TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic but chemical: the target requires stabilizing both Au and Ag in highly fluorinated octahedral environments.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for high-pressure F₂, controlled dry handling, and hazardous air-sensitive fluorides, all directly supported by Ag(AuF₄)₂ synthesis at ~200 bar F₂, 400 °C, and 14 d in a Monel autoclave (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4). It avoids very high temperature and avoids >5 GPa pressure, so those deductions do not apply. The main uncertainty is whether 200 bar F₂ is sufficient to form AuAgF₆ rather than the known Ag(AuF₄)₂ phase.

#### F3 — Route 1

Ag₂CO₃ + Au has direct precedent for Ag–Au fluoride formation (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4), but Au metal is kinetically sluggish until fluorinated. High-pF₂ improves chemical transport, and analogous phases form at 350–400 °C rather than requiring near-melting diffusion (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4; SS-3 / TS-3). Structural reconstruction remains moderate because the target is an octahedral 2D sheet, not the Ag(AuF₄)₂ structure.

#### F2 — Route 2

Gold-tube reactions at ~400 °C for 30 d are well supported for Cd/Hg Au fluorides (CS-5; SS-1 / TS-1; SS-2 / TS-2). The route is strongly penalized for multi-step precursor synthesis, sealed gold tubes, AuF₃ handling, and moisture-sensitive fluorides, all supported by CS-5 and SS-1 / TS-1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that AgF₂ + AuF₃ specifically yields AuAgF₆, so the adaptation is inferential.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary fluorides are more reactive than metals, and AuF₃ is a plausible Au source based on Hg/Cd analogues (SS-1 / TS-1; CS-5). However, long anneals of 30 d indicate slow ordering and reconstruction in related materials (CS-5; SS-1 / TS-1). The major kinetic uncertainty is cation ordering into alternating AuF₆/AgF₆ octahedra.

#### F2 — Route 3

The AHF route operates at only 300 °C for 1 d, which is favorable (CS-3). However, it requires anhydrous HF, a sealed Monel autoclave, glovebox handling, and controlled cooling, all explicitly reported for Ni(AuF₄)₂ (CS-3). These hazards justify a challenging severity score despite the low temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

AHF can dissolve or mobilize fluoride/fluorometallate species, enabling rapid crystal growth at 300 °C in CS-3. This gives the best kinetic score among the routes. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS-3 states the Ni(AuF₄)₂ formation was accidental, so the route may be kinetically facile but difficult to control.

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct F₂ fluorination at 300–400 °C for several hours is supported for RbAuF₄ and related alkali Au fluorides (SS-5 / TS-5), and F₂/N₂ fluorination is supported for CuFAuF₄ (CS-1). The route avoids sealed autoclave pressure if done as flow fluorination, but still requires toxic F₂ and controlled staged heating. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows direct fluorination alone can stabilize the target AuAgF₆ sheet.

#### F3 — Route 4

Gas fluorination gives high anion activity and can rapidly form Au fluoride frameworks (SS-5 / TS-5). However, the structural reconstruction score is limited because direct fluorination may terminate at square-planar AuF₄-containing phases such as Ag(AuF₄)₂ (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4) rather than octahedral AuAgF₆. Composition and oxidation-state control are therefore the kinetic bottlenecks.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only two cations and a simple AuAgF₆ formula, and its reported connectivity is corner- plus edge-sharing octahedra without face-sharing. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient quantitative evidence for ionic-radius mismatch or unit-cell atom count, so those subclaims rely mainly on the target structure and [Reasonable Inference]. The amorphization risk is low relative to the much larger chemical risks of fluorine handling and competing crystalline phases.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuAgF₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many similar Au fluorides in CS/SS/TS are also reported on the hull. The analogue support is strong chemically because Ag(AuF₄)₂ contains the same Ag–Au–F elements and is experimentally accessible under high-pF₂ conditions (CS-4 / SS-4 / TS-4), but structural support is incomplete because the target requires AuF₆/AgF₆ octahedral sheets rather than AuF₄ square-planar units. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high fluorine chemical potential while excluding moisture, as shown across CS-1, CS-4, SS-3, SS-4, and TS-4. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against known AuF₄ fluorometallates and possible Ag fluoride side phases. Overall, the material is synthesizable but requires specialized fluorine or AHF chemistry and careful slow-cooling/annealing control.
