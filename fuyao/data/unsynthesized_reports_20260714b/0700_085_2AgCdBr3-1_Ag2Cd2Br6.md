### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase under the computational conditions [Target]. Several chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar references also have E_hull = 0 and were synthesized experimentally, which supports the general reliability of low-hull halide/chalcogenide predictions [CS2, CS5, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3, TS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for the exact AgBr–CdBr₂ binary/ternary phase relations, so the score remains high but not 100 [CS, SS, TS].

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed solid-state

The sealed solid-state route uses modest temperatures, comparable to 120 °C/4 months, 260 °C/2–3 days, and 473–573 K/1 week analogues [SS1, SS5, SS4, TS1, TS4, TS3]. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile halide containment, multiple handling steps, and toxic CdBr₂ [SS1, SS4, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact Ag–Cd–Br solid-state reaction, so SS/TS analogues carry more weight for this route [CS, SS, TS].

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed solid-state

Binary bromide precursors should be more reactive than oxides, and similar halide compounds form by direct sealed-tube reactions [SS4, SS5, TS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. However, solid-state diffusion and cation ordering into a 2D P-31m sheet may be slow, as suggested by long anneals in analogous solid-state systems [SS1, TS1]. The remaining uncertainty is whether AgBr and CdBr₂ react cleanly before forming competing binary-rich or polymorphic products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 melt growth

The melt route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressures, consistent with chemically related melt/quench or melt/anneal routes [CS2, CS5]. It still requires a sealed ampoule because bromides are volatile/corrosive at elevated temperature, and CdBr₂ introduces toxicity and moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 also shows cooling-rate sensitivity between metastable and stable phases, which increases operational severity [CS2].

#### F3 — Route 2 melt growth

A molten halide medium should provide excellent cation mobility and fast equilibration compared with dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 and CS5 support formation of mixed compounds from melt or melt-and-anneal treatments [CS2, CS5]. The main uncertainty is whether AgCdBr₃ melts congruently or requires slow crystallization from a CdBr₂-rich self-flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis has the mildest thermal profile because CS1 demonstrates Ag halide/chalcogenide crystallization at 220 °C rather than high-temperature fusion [CS1]. It avoids vacuum sealing but requires a sealed autoclave and corrosive concentrated hydrohalic acid, and Cd precursors are toxic [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for hydrothermal AgCdBr₃ specifically, so the route is inferred primarily from CS1 [SS, TS, CS1].

#### F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal bromide mineralization should give high precursor activity because Ag and Cd can be transported through solution or halide complexes [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports slow-cooled hydrothermal crystal formation in concentrated hydrohalic acid, but it also reports hydrothermal products containing multiple phases and intergrowths [CS1]. Thus, kinetics are favorable, while phase selectivity remains the main uncertainty [CS1].

#### F2 — Route 4 CVT

CVT uses moderate temperatures and standard evacuated ampoules, consistent with 360 °C/5 day halide transport analogues [SS3, TS2]. The route receives deductions for vacuum, volatile halide transport, multistep gradient control, and toxic CdBr₂ [SS3, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for CVT of Ag–Cd bromides specifically, so this route is less strongly supported than hydrothermal or melt routes [CS].

#### F3 — Route 4 CVT

Vapor transport can reduce diffusion limitations and improve crystal growth, as shown for structurally/thermodynamically related halide-rich compounds [SS3, TS2]. However, SS3/TS2 report low yields, equilibrium mixtures, unreacted precursors, and air/moisture sensitivity, all of which lower the expected kinetic/phase-control score [SS3, TS2]. The key uncertainty is whether AgBr and CdBr₂ have compatible transport volatilities in the required temperature gradient [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s small formula, ordered P-31m layered description, and lack of described face sharing suggest low amorphization risk [Target]. The Ag/Cd cation size mismatch is not expected to exceed the >15% trigger for strong glass-forming frustration [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on amorphization or glass formation for AgCdBr₃, so F4 is based mainly on the target structural descriptors and the provided rule set [CS, SS, TS, Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCdBr₃ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the broader feasibility of sealed-tube, hydrothermal, melt, and vapor-transport synthesis for soft metal halide/chalcogenide systems, but none is an exact Ag–Cd–Br analogue [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the soft, polarizable bromide chemistry, which should allow low-temperature diffusion, solution complexation, or melt-assisted crystallization [CS1, CS2, SS4, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is phase purity, because several analogues report metastability, multiphase products, low yields, long anneals, or sensitivity to thermal history [CS1, CS2, CS3, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2]. Overall, the best first attempt is hydrothermal bromide mineralization, followed by melt/self-flux growth if hydrothermal products are multiphase [CS1, CS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference].
