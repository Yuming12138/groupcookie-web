### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the border of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” high-synthesizability range. SS analogues are mostly on or very near the hull [SS1–SS5], while TS analogues include phases around 0.056–0.098 eV/atom that have reported syntheses [TS1–TS5]. CS analogues include both stable and moderately metastable phases, including CS4 at 0.127 eV/atom with hydrothermal synthesis, so 0.050 eV/atom is plausible [CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that target metastability may couple to Te redox and volatility, which E_hull alone does not capture.

**F2 — Route 1 ceramic severity.**  
Ceramic synthesis is well supported by SS1–SS5 and TS3, but the target likely requires sealed or controlled atmosphere because of Te volatility and Te redox control [Reasonable Inference], with sealed-tube precedent from CS1 and TS4. The route also likely needs multiple grind/fire cycles, as seen in SS5 and TS3. Therefore, the severity is moderate-to-high despite avoiding >1000 °C in the proposed Te-compatible version.

**F3 — Route 1 ceramic kinetics.**  
Dry solid-state kinetics are penalized because TiO₂ is refractory and Bi₄Ti₃O₁₂ required 1173 K for 50 h with regrinding [TS3]. SS analogues show Bi–Nb/Ta oxides can be made by ceramic methods [SS1–SS5], but the target has lower symmetry and mixed coordination, so complete reconstruction from oxides is likely [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether transient Bi₂O₃/TeO₂ liquid phases accelerate the reaction enough to offset Ti diffusion limitations.

**F2 — Route 2 flux severity.**  
Flux synthesis avoids >1000 °C, which reduces severity relative to SS5 and TS3. However, sealed or controlled atmosphere is still recommended for Te species [Reasonable Inference], and toxic Bi/Te oxides remain. The route is therefore less thermally severe but still chemically controlled.

**F3 — Route 2 flux kinetics.**  
A Bi₂O₃–TeO₂-rich flux should improve ion transport relative to dry oxide reaction [Reasonable Inference], and CS3 shows melt formation can occur in related oxide syntheses. Te-containing oxides can be made by high-temperature solid-state treatments [CS2], supporting Te oxide compatibility. The uncertainty is that flux may stabilize competing Bi–Ti–O or tellurite phases rather than LaTiBiTeO₆.

**F2 — Route 3 hydrothermal severity.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis has mild temperatures, as CS4 uses 453–503 K and TS4 uses 453 K. It avoids high-temperature Te volatilization, which is a major enabling factor [Reasonable Inference]. The deductions come from multistep pH/precursor preparation and toxic/moisture-sensitive reagents, especially Bi/Te compounds and Ti alkoxides.

**F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal kinetics.**  
Solution-mediated growth provides high precursor activity, supported by hydrothermal Bi oxide synthesis [CS4] and La hydrothermal chemistry [TS4]. However, TS4 reports extreme sensitivity to pH, concentration, and temperature, and CS4 notes byproducts in a Bi-containing hydrothermal synthesis. The largest uncertainty is whether Ti can be incorporated cleanly at low temperature, since insufficient evidence from SS and TS directly supports hydrothermal La–Ti–Bi–Te oxide formation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains cations of substantially different sizes and coordination preferences, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is low-symmetry P1 with mixed La, Ti, Bi, and Te coordination, increasing disorder risk, but the provided target description does not prove a >40-atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity. Thus the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LaTiBiTeO₆ is likely a mildly metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and synthesized TS analogues exist in the nearby 0.056–0.098 eV/atom range [TS1–TS5]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support the general feasibility of Bi-containing ceramic oxides [SS1–SS5], Ti–Bi oxide formation [TS3], Te-containing oxides [CS2], and hydrothermal Bi/La chemistry [CS4, TS4]. The most important enabling factor is low-temperature solution or flux transport, which can reduce Ti diffusion limitations and suppress Te loss [CS4, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of Te volatility/redox and phase selectivity in a quaternary low-symmetry oxide, which is not directly demonstrated by any single CS/SS/TS analogue. Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require empirical screening of pH or flux composition.
