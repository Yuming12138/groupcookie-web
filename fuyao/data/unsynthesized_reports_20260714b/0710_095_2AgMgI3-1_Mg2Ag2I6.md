### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.020 eV/atom lies well within the high-synthesizability bracket of ≤0.05 eV/atom. This is consistent with TS analogues near the same energy range: TlCdI3 at 0.0167 eV/atom, AgTl2I3 at 0.0272 eV/atom, and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). CS also contains several stable iodides with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, indicating that complex iodides can be true ground-state compounds (CS-1–CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is slightly metastable and may compete with MgI2, AgI, or other Mg–Ag–I phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses low to moderate temperatures, because chemically similar iodides are synthesized around 300–400 °C or 573 K (CS-1–CS-3), and thermodynamic iodide analogues are processed around 250–350 °C (CS-4/TS-2). The main deductions are for sealed volatile iodide chemistry and air/moisture-sensitive precursors, both supported by the need for sealed ampoules, Ar atmosphere, gloveboxes, and hygroscopic handling in CS-1–CS-4 and TS-2. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that >1000 °C, high pressure, or reactive H2/NH3 atmospheres are needed; these are therefore not deducted. SS is structurally supportive but provides insufficient evidence for MgAgI3 melt-condition severity, so SS is neutral, 50, for that specific severity inference.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Melt growth gives favorable kinetics because the reactants are binary iodides and analogous compounds crystallize from low-temperature melts by slow cooling (CS-1–CS-3). TS-1/TS-2 also show Bridgman or melt/anneal routes for thermodynamically similar ternary iodides. The structural reconstruction score is not maximal because the target’s MgI6 octahedra plus AgAgI3 trigonal pyramids are not exactly represented by CS analogues, although SS layered CdI2 supports formation of 2D iodide sheets. Remaining uncertainty is cation ordering between Mg2+ and Ag+ in the layered lattice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is mild in temperature because TS-3 used 155–175 °C hydrothermal conditions for AgTl2I3. It requires an autoclave but not high pressure above 5 GPa, so no high-pressure deduction is applied. Deductions are assigned for a sealed volatile/corrosive HI medium and moisture-sensitive MgI2 handling; TS-3 supports HI autoclave use, while CS-1–CS-4 support dry handling needs for iodide materials. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Mg–Ag iodide synthesis, so CS is neutral, 50, for transferability of this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Solution transport should be fast, and TS-3 confirms that AgI can participate in hydrothermal iodide crystallization in HI. SS-1–SS-4 demonstrate that layered iodides can be grown from solution/evaporation, but these are CdI2 polytypes and not Mg–Ag ternaries. The reconstruction score is reduced because Mg2+ hydration and AgI solubility may redirect crystallization into hydrated salts, binary AgI, or solution complexes [Reasonable Inference]. TS-3 also reports twinned crystals and low untwinned yield, indicating phase/crystal-quality challenges.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is low-temperature but operationally more severe due to evacuated sealed tubes, volatile iodides, likely repeated grinding/annealing, and air-sensitive MgI2. These deductions are supported by CS-4/TS-2, where ternary iodide phase purity required powdering and repeated annealing, and by TS-5, where a sealed evacuated Pyrex tube was used for two weeks. There is no evidence that high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is needed. SS provides insufficient evidence for solid-state annealing of MgAgI3 specifically, so SS is neutral, 50, for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Binary iodides are more reactive than refractory oxides, giving acceptable precursor activity, and low-temperature iodide reactions are supported by CS-4/TS-2/TS-5. However, solid-state diffusion is slower than melt or solution transport, and the target requires ordered Mg and Ag coordination environments, reducing the structural reconstruction score [Reasonable Inference]. The repeated processing reported for TlFeI3/TlMnI3 analogues in CS-4/TS-2 indicates that phase-pure ternary iodides may need multiple annealing cycles. Remaining uncertainty is whether MgAgI3 forms directly or requires partial melting/fluxing to overcome cation-ordering barriers.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization risk: a substantial Mg2+/Ag+ size and coordination mismatch, reflected by MgI6 octahedra and AgAgI3 trigonal pyramids in the target description [Reasonable Inference from standard ionic radii]. However, the structure is a small-cell crystalline layered iodide and lacks face-sharing/edge/corner coexistence all at once, so the total number of triggers is only one. SS strongly supports crystallinity in layered iodide sheets, especially CdI2 polytypes grown as crystals (SS-1–SS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS specifically addresses amorphization in mixed Mg/Ag iodides, so TS is neutral, 50, for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgAgI3 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.020 eV/atom rather than exactly zero. The CS and TS datasets give strong support for synthesis: many ternary iodides form by low-temperature sealed melt growth or Bridgman methods, and several thermodynamic analogues lie in the same low-energy-above-hull range. The SS dataset supports the plausibility of 2D iodide sheets, though it is chemically less specific because the closest structures are CdI2 polytypes rather than mixed Mg–Ag iodides. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and low synthesis temperature of iodide melts, which should allow crystallization without harsh conditions. The most critical limiting factor is controlling moisture, iodide volatility, and Mg/Ag stoichiometry during sealed-ampoule growth.
