### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0230 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore receives a high stability score. CS supports that a chemically close metastable LiI polymorph with E_hull ≈0.0289 eV/atom can be obtained by cryogenic deposition, while SS/TS show Li–Br-containing analogues with E_hull ≈0.0146–0.0199 eV/atom are synthesizable. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that the exact P4/nmm LiBr polymorph is a ground state, so the remaining uncertainty is phase persistence rather than thermodynamic proximity.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is condition-severe because CS requires high vacuum, cryogenic substrates, precursor drying, and controlled transfer for alkali-halide metastable phases. The absolute evaporation temperature is moderate, around 430–600 °C from CS, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for cryogenic pure LiBr deposition and are neutral for this route-specific severity, but they reinforce the need for dry/inert halide handling.

#### F3: Route 1 kinetics

CS gives strong evidence that LiBr is vapor-accessible and can condense under cryogenic high-vacuum conditions, giving a high precursor-vapor score. The crystallization score is lower because CS explicitly indicates metastable alkali-halide phases can transform to cubic phases on warming, and the target P4/nmm sheet structure is not the usual ambient LiBr structure. Composition control is reasonably favorable because LiBr can be evaporated as a binary salt [Reasonable Inference], but hydration and sublimation-rate control remain uncertainties supported by CS/TS moisture-control requirements.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

SS/TS show LiBr-containing bromide analogues can be processed in evacuated quartz ampoules with controlled cooling, so high pressure and extreme temperature are not required. Deductions are applied for vacuum/controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile halide processing, multi-step drying/sealing/melting/quenching, and moisture-sensitive LiBr handling. CS provides indirect support because LiBr is evaporated at 430 °C and must be kept dry, but CS does not demonstrate melt-quenching of pure P4/nmm LiBr.

#### F3: Route 2 kinetics

SS/TS melt-growth analogues imply rapid ion transport in halide melts and manageable kinetics for Li–Br-containing materials. However, the target requires a structural arrangement different from the expected stable rocksalt LiBr [Reasonable Inference], so the structural-reconstruction score is low. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that melt-quenching can trap P4/nmm LiBr specifically, making phase selection the major uncertainty.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits the high-vacuum, dry-transfer, and volatile-source challenges demonstrated in CS for LiBr evaporation. The epitaxial component is [Reasonable Inference], so SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for substrate-stabilized P4/nmm LiBr and are neutral for that aspect. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalties are needed because CS shows alkali-halide vapor deposition at much lower source temperatures.

#### F3: Route 3 kinetics

Precursor vapor availability is strongly supported by CS because LiBr was directly evaporated. Epitaxial crystallization is plausible [Reasonable Inference] for a 2D tetragonal polymorph, but no CS/SS/TS source directly reports epitaxial P4/nmm LiBr, so the crystallization score remains moderate. Composition control is easier than multicomponent films because the target is binary LiBr [Reasonable Inference], but moisture and volatile flux sensitivity remain.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The described P4/nmm LiBr cell appears simple and does not indicate >40 atoms, and the polyhedral network includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing. CS/SS/TS do not report amorphization as the dominant issue for LiBr-containing halides; the more important risk is polymorph transformation, not glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a metastable LiBr polymorph rather than the ambient ground-state rocksalt phase, because it is 0.023 eV/atom above hull and has an unusual 2D P4/nmm square-pyramidal network. CS provides the strongest support: chemically close Li–halide metastable phases are accessed by cryogenic high-vacuum deposition from LiBr/LiI sources. SS/TS support general Li–Br halide processability in dry, sealed, molten or solid-state environments, but they mostly stabilize ternary bromides rather than pure metastable LiBr. The most critical enabling factor is the low energy above hull combined with direct LiBr vapor accessibility. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: the P4/nmm phase may convert to stable cubic/rocksalt LiBr during warming, handling, or bulk crystallization.
