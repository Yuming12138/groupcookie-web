### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score boundary in the supplied rubric. CS and SS analogues are mostly on-hull, supporting the plausibility of related ordered oxide frameworks [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4]. TS analogues near 0.055–0.078 eV/atom are still reported as synthesized, showing that small positive hull energies do not preclude synthesis [TS1–TS5]. The uncertainty is that 0.050 eV/atom may correspond to a metastable phase requiring kinetic trapping rather than a broad equilibrium stability field.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

The ceramic air route is mild because CS1 forms a Te-containing monoclinic perovskite-related oxide at 973 K in air and CS2 uses 700–950 °C solid-state synthesis without special atmosphere. Its main kinetic weakness is the use of refractory Sc₂O₃/IrO₂-like oxides, consistent with SS4 requiring about 100 h for Sr–Sc–Os analogues. This route is experimentally simple but may need multiple firing/regrinding cycles, as seen in CS2 and SS4. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Te–Ir ceramic air synthesis; TS is neutral for this route-specific chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

The sealed-tube route is supported structurally by SS4, where Sr₂ScOsO₆-type double perovskites are made in sealed quartz tubes under Ar near 950–960 °C. Its condition severity is higher because sealed/vacuum or inert atmospheres are used in SS1–SS4 and TS5 for volatile or redox-sensitive 5d oxides. Kinetically, the route remains slow because it relies on solid-state diffusion through refractory oxide/metal particles. The uncertainty is whether Ir and Te require reducing, oxidizing, or buffered oxygen fugacity; CS gives insufficient direct evidence for sealed Ir–Te conditions and is neutral on that point.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

The soft-chemistry route scores best because CS3/CS4 explicitly use citric/nitric acid resin methods to create homogeneous reactive precursors for ordered monoclinic double perovskites. This directly addresses diffusion limitations from Sc and Ir by reducing cation diffusion distances before crystallization. Condition severity remains moderate because the process is multi-step and soluble Ir/Te precursors require careful decomposition. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for citrate processing of Sr–Sc–Ir–Te specifically, but SS4 supports the final perovskite-type target framework.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Flux growth is supported by SS1 and SS3, where Os/Re-like oxide crystals are grown or formed in KCl/KClO₃/KClO₄ sealed flux environments. Flux should improve kinetics by liquid-phase transport, but SS1 and SS3 also report multiple products or impurity phases, reducing confidence in phase selectivity. CS1 gives additional support for nitrate-containing Te oxide reactions at relatively low temperature. The uncertainty is high because no source directly demonstrates a Sr–Sc–Ir–Te flux phase field.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size mismatch between Sr and the smaller B-site/Te cations, triggering the radius-mismatch check. However, the target structure is largely corner-sharing rather than an edge/face-sharing mixed network, so the connectivity check is not triggered by the provided structure description. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to determine target unit-cell atom count or amorphization behavior; these sources are neutral for F4. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrScIrTeO₆ is likely a **low-energy metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible oxide**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data strongly support low-temperature Te-containing oxide synthesis, and SS4 strongly supports Sr–Sc–5d-metal perovskite-like frameworks near 950 °C, but no analogue contains the exact Sr–Sc–Ir–Te combination. The most critical enabling factor is **homogeneous cation mixing**, best addressed by the citrate–nitrate route demonstrated for ordered double perovskites in CS3/CS4. The most critical limiting factor is **simultaneous control of Te volatility/speciation and Ir oxidation state**, which is only indirectly supported by sealed/controlled-atmosphere 5d oxide syntheses in SS1–SS4 and TS1/TS5. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires XRD-guided optimization rather than a single firing.
