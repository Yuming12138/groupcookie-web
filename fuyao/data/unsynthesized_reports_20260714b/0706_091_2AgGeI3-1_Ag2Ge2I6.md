### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0210 eV/atom, placing it in the high synthesizability bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. CS supports this stability range because chemically related iodides such as AgSe₃I, TlGeI₃, and CuGaI₄ are reported at 0–0.017 eV/atom. TS gives especially relevant support because thermodynamically similar AgTl₂I₃ is 0.0272 eV/atom and CuHgSI is 0.0210 eV/atom, both comparable to the target. Remaining uncertainty is that near-hull phases may still be metastable with respect to kinetic products, especially in volatile iodide systems.

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure; CS and TS hydrothermal analogues operate at only 155–220 °C. Severity comes mainly from corrosive HI and the need for a sealed pressure vessel, as shown in CS AgSe₃I and TS AgTl₂I₃. GeI₂ air sensitivity and Ge(II) oxidation-state control are inferred from iodogermanium chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal Ag–Ge–I specifically, so SS contributes neutral support for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal HI should give high precursor activity because CS AgSe₃I and TS AgTl₂I₃ crystallize directly from iodide-rich solution. Kinetic transport is favorable compared with dry solid-state diffusion, since the reaction medium is fluid and strongly iodide-rich. The main kinetic penalty is phase selection: CS reports AgSe₃I as part of hydrothermal reaction products with phase-control complications, and TS AgTl₂I₃ reports twinned/low-yield single crystals. Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal kinetics, scored neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed ampoule

Route 2 has moderate severity because CS CuGaI₄, SS Ga iodides, and TS TlMnI₃ all require sealed ampoules, vacuum or Ar, and moisture exclusion. Temperature is moderate, generally 250–450 °C based on SS and TS, so no high-temperature deduction applies. The main deductions arise from volatile iodine/iodides, vacuum sealing, and likely multi-step annealing, consistent with TS TlMnI₃ repeated powdering/annealing. CS provides a milder counterexample, CuGaI₄ from a simple sealed melt, but the target’s Ge(II) sensitivity makes the conservative score appropriate **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed ampoule

Binary iodide precursors are relatively reactive compared with oxides, and CS/SS/TS all show ternary or binary iodides can form in sealed ampoules at moderate temperatures. Diffusion should be aided by partial melting or vapor transport of iodide species, as suggested by CS CuGaI₄ melt growth and SS Ga + I₂ reactions. Structural reconstruction is not trivial because the target is a 2D trigonal sheet, whereas SS/TS TlGeI₃ and TlMnI₃ are 1D Pnma ribbon structures. The need for annealing in TS TlMnI₃ lowers confidence in rapid phase formation.

#### F2 — Route 3 CVT/vapor-assisted growth

SS provides the main support for Route 3, with AlTeI₇ and GaTeI₇ grown in sealed ampoules using iodine-containing vapor/gradient methods at 150–350 °C. The route is not thermally severe, but it requires sealed evacuated/Ar ampoules, volatile I₂, and careful temperature gradients. Toxic/air-sensitive iodide handling is a significant penalty, consistent with SS descriptions of moisture- or air-sensitive crystals. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for AgGeI₃-specific CVT, so they are neutral for this route’s feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 3 CVT/vapor-assisted growth

Vapor availability is plausible for I₂ and Ge iodides, but Ag transport is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Ag–Ge iodide CVT. SS GaTeI₇ shows that controlled ramps between 300 and 350 °C are essential for high-quality crystals, implying a sensitive crystallization pathway. Composition control is likely narrower than in hydrothermal synthesis because iodine activity simultaneously controls transport and oxidation state **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore, Route 3 is plausible for small crystals but less robust for bulk phase-pure synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains different cation environments, with Ag in trigonal pyramidal AgAgI₃ units and Ge in GeI₆ octahedra; this supports a cation-size/coordination mismatch trigger **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target also has mixed connectivity: Ag–Ge corner sharing and Ge–Ge edge sharing, while SS/TS TlGeI₃-like analogues confirm edge-sharing GeI₆ or MI₆ motifs are common in related iodides. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered. The result is a medium disorder/amorphization risk rather than a high one.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgGeI₃ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0210 eV/atom, and TS analogues with similar hull energies have reported syntheses. CS and TS strongly support low-temperature iodide synthesis, especially through HI hydrothermal chemistry and sealed iodide reactions. The most critical enabling factor is the softness and mobility of iodide chemistry: CS and TS show that Ag–I-containing ternaries can crystallize at 155–220 °C under hydrothermal conditions. The most critical limiting factor is oxidation-state and stoichiometry control for Ge(II), since too much iodine or oxidative conditions may favor Ge(IV) iodides **[Reasonable Inference]**. Phase purity may also be difficult because CS hydrothermal AgSe₃I and TS AgTl₂I₃ report multiphase products, twinning, or low-yield high-quality crystals.
