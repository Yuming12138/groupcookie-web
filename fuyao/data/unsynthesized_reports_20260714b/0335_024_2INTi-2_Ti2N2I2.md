### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.013 eV/atom, so it lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Chemically close ZrNBr, ZrNCl, and HfNBr analogues are on or very near the hull (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5), and thermodynamically similar Hf₂N₂S is also close to the hull at 0.021 eV/atom (SS-2/TS-1). This supports the view that TiNI is a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase. Remaining uncertainty is that the Ti–N–I phase diagram may contain competing TiN/TiIₓ/TiI₃ phases not represented directly in the provided sources.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-pressure NH₄I reactive-flux synthesis

Condition severity is high in practice because CS-1/CS-3/CS-5 require multi-anvil equipment, noble-metal capsules, and 3–5 GPa, although the formal F2 rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa. The 900–1100 °C and 1–1.5 h window from CS-1/CS-3/CS-5 suggests the reactive flux strongly accelerates formation relative to ordinary solid-state diffusion. Kinetics are moderate because TiN is refractory, but NH₄I flux and high pressure should promote halogenation and layered phase formation. The main uncertainty is whether Ti prefers the same nitride-halide framework as Zr/Hf or instead forms binary TiN plus volatile Ti iodides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Ambient-pressure sealed-tube solid-state route

SS-1 gives a direct precedent for making α-HfNBr from Hf + NH₄Br in a fused silica tube at 760 °C for 12 h, so an analogous Ti + NH₄I experiment is plausible. The route is less severe than high pressure but still requires a sealed system because NH₄I and iodine-containing species are volatile. Kinetics may be acceptable through gas/transport assistance, but Ti metal surface oxide and competing TiN formation may slow reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS confirms that TiNI specifically is stable at ambient pressure, so this route is less certain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Topochemical halide exchange

SS-2/TS-1 show α-HfNBr can be transformed topochemically at 750 °C under vacuum, and SS-5/TS-3 support topochemical nitride chemistry at moderate temperatures. If a layered TiNCl or TiNBr parent can be prepared, halide exchange to TiNI should minimize Ti–N framework reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the best kinetic score, but F2 is reduced because the route is multi-step and depends on a precursor that itself may be difficult. The largest uncertainty is that no provided CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates halide exchange to an iodide nitride.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film reactive deposition / CVD

SS-3/TS-4 demonstrate early-transition-metal nitride formation by CVD using metal chloride, NH₃, and H₂, and TS-5 demonstrates early-transition-metal halide transport in sealed tubes. A thin-film route could stabilize a low-energy metastable P4/nmm TiNI phase through kinetic trapping [Reasonable Inference]. However, composition control is likely difficult because iodine is volatile, and no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Ti–N–I thin-film growth. Thus the route is plausible but less supported than high-pressure or topochemical routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple one-cation Ti–N–I composition and a small layered P4/nmm description, which does not indicate large-unit-cell complexity or severe cation-size mismatch. The described connectivity is a Ti–N sheet with terminal/single Ti–I bonding rather than highly mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral networks. CS/SS/TS analogues such as ZrNBr, ZrNCl, HfNBr, and Hf₂N₂S are crystalline layered compounds (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2/TS-1), supporting low amorphization tendency. Remaining uncertainty is that thin-film deposition could still produce amorphous Ti–N–I if iodine activity or substrate temperature is poorly controlled.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiNI is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.013 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar Zr/Hf nitride halides synthesized with NH₄X flux under high pressure, especially layered ZrNBr, ZrNCl, and HfNBr (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Structurally similar α-HfNBr and Hf₂N₂S show that layered metal nitride halide frameworks can form at ambient pressure and undergo topochemical transformations (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is use of a reactive iodide flux or parent layered precursor to overcome slow Ti diffusion and stabilize the Ti–N–I layered motif. The most critical limiting factor is iodine volatility and competing TiN/TiIₓ phase formation, especially outside a sealed or high-pressure environment.
