### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.025 eV/atom, placing it well within the commonly synthesizable near-hull regime. This is consistent with thermodynamic analogues: Cs2Hg3H2OI8 has E_hull ≈ 0.0115 eV/atom (SS1, TS1), TlCdI3 has E_hull ≈ 0.0167 eV/atom (TS2), and AgTl2I3 has E_hull ≈ 0.0272 eV/atom (TS4). CS analogues NaInI4/LiInI4 are reported on the hull, supporting that alkali iodometallates can be stable phases (CS1, CS2). Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be metastable relative to NaI + HgI2 at finite temperature even if near hull.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Melt growth

The condition severity is moderate because the temperatures inferred from CS1/CS2 are only 300–400 °C, far below high-temperature solid-state thresholds. The main deductions are HgI2 volatility/toxicity and moisture-sensitive iodide handling, supported by HgI2-based analogues (SS1/SS2) and moisture-sensitive alkali iodometallates (CS1/CS2). Kinetics should be favorable because slow-cooling melts provide high ion mobility, as directly shown for alkali iodometallates (CS1, CS2). The uncertainty is whether NaHgI3 melts congruently or forms via peritectic/solid-state crystallization.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Solution evaporation

This route has mild conditions because SS1/TS1 and SS3/SS5 show room-temperature evaporation crystallization of related Hg/Cd iodides. Kinetics are excellent in solution, but the structural-reconstruction score is reduced because the closest Cs–Hg–I solution analogue is hydrated, Cs2Hg3H2OI8 (SS1, TS1), while the target is anhydrous. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Na–Hg–I solution crystallization means CS is neutral for this route-specific phase selectivity. The key uncertainty is whether Na+ favors soluble iodomer­curate salts rather than the desired layered NaHgI3.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Hydrothermal/solvothermal

Hydrothermal iodide synthesis is supported by CS5 and TS4, where concentrated iodide/HI media at 160–175 °C produce iodometallate crystals. Severity is moderate because the route uses corrosive HI and sealed vessels, though not high pressure in the >5 GPa sense. Kinetics are solution-mediated and therefore favorable, but phase control is reduced because TS4 reports twinning/low yield, and aqueous HI may stabilize other iodomer­curate complexes. Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal NaHgI3 specifically is treated as neutral for exact phase selectivity.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Sealed-tube solid-state annealing

The route is chemically plausible because TS5 forms a Hg-containing iodide/chalcogenide from binaries in an evacuated Pyrex tube at ~280 °C, and TS3 uses binary iodides under Ar for ternary iodides. Severity is higher than the melt/solution routes because vacuum/sealed-tube handling, HgI2 volatility, toxicity, and potential regrinding cycles all apply. Kinetics are acceptable due to soft halide lattices, but long anneals or repeated grinding may be necessary, as TS5 uses 2 weeks and TS3 reports repeated processing for phase purity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for direct solid-state NaHgI3 kinetics is treated as neutral beyond the general iodide analogies.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered iodide structure with small formula complexity and no large reported unit-cell atom count, which lowers amorphization risk. The target connectivity is not a highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing framework; it contains edge-sharing NaI6 octahedra and trigonal-planar HgI3 units. SS3/SS5 show layered iodides crystallize readily by evaporation, indirectly supporting crystallinity in related low-dimensional iodides. Direct amorphization data are absent from CS/SS/TS, so residual uncertainty remains.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaHgI3 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.025 eV/atom. The synthesis is well supported by chemically similar alkali iodometallates grown from binary iodide melts (CS1, CS2) and by structurally similar layered Hg/Cd iodides that crystallize readily at low temperature (SS2–SS5). The most important enabling factor is the soft, low-melting iodide chemistry, which allows crystallization at 250–400 °C rather than requiring refractory diffusion. The major limiting factor is HgI2 volatility/toxicity and the risk of competing hydrated or iodomer­curate phases, especially in solution routes (SS1, TS1). Overall, sealed-ampoule slow cooling from NaI/HgI2 is the most defensible route for anhydrous NaHgI3.
