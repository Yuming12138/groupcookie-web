### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0470 eV/atom, which falls in the highest required scoring bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS-1 is a very close thermodynamic analogue with E_hull = 0.0466 eV/atom and is reported as synthesized, supporting feasibility near this metastability level. CS and SS contain many E_hull = 0.0 sulfide analogues synthesized by conventional ampoule/solid-state routes (CS-1/2/3/4/5, SS-1/2/3). The remaining uncertainty is whether NaTlS is a true low-temperature equilibrium phase or a metastable polymorph stabilized by kinetics.

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to direct alkali–Au–chalcogenide syntheses at about 600 °C (CS-4) and Na3AuS2 synthesis at 600 °C (SS-2), so no high-temperature penalty applies. It does require controlled/vacuum or sealed ampoule conditions, as in CS-1/2 and SS-1/2, and volatile sulfur requires sealed containment, as shown by the repeated use of ampoules in CS-1/2/4 and SS-2. Toxicity from Tl is a [Reasonable Inference], while air sensitivity of alkali sulfides/chalcogenides is supported by CS-2/4/5 and SS-4/5.

#### F3: Route 1

The direct elemental route benefits from reactive Na/S chemistry and low-melting cations [Reasonable Inference], but metallic Tl is a dense precursor and complete structural reconstruction is required. CS-4 shows direct fusion at 600 °C is feasible for related alkali chalcogenides, but CS-4 also indicates slow heating/cooling and tempering can be important. SS-2 supports transparent crystals from slow cooling a Na–Au–S melt, which improves confidence in mass transport. Uncertainty remains because no CS/SS/TS example directly contains Tl in this structure type.

#### F2: Route 2

Route 2 avoids elemental Na but introduces multi-step binary sulfide preparation/drying and possible regrinding/annealing, consistent with binary sulfide approaches in SS-1 and K2S preparation in CS-2/3. It still needs vacuum or inert ampoule conditions, as SS-1 and SS-2 indicate. Na2S air sensitivity is supported by CS-2/5 and SS-1, and Tl2S toxicity is a [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is therefore moderate despite modest peak temperatures.

#### F3: Route 2

Using Na2S and Tl2S should reduce redox and bonding rearrangement barriers because both cation–sulfur sublattices are preformed [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 demonstrates that mixtures of binary sulfides can crystallize related alkali metal sulfides under vacuum with slow cooling. However, NaTlS requires ordered Na/Tl placement in a 2D Pmmn framework, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for exact topotactic transfer. The kinetics are favorable but not trivial.

#### F2: Route 3

The sulfur-rich flux route has no extreme temperature or pressure demand, consistent with CS-1 and CS-3 flux/melt syntheses at 500–600 °C. It does require an evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile sulfur-rich conditions, both directly supported by CS-1. Multi-step cooling and product isolation are also supported by CS-1, which reports controlled cooling and isolation under nitrogen. Tl toxicity and possible Tl transport add additional practical severity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 3

Flux conditions give the best kinetic outlook because liquid/polysulfide media improve diffusion and crystal growth, as shown by CS-1 and CS-3. SS-2 also reports transparent crystals from slowly cooled Na–Au–S melt, supporting the idea that Na-containing sulfides can crystallize from melt/flux. The target’s two-dimensional sheet structure may benefit from slow cooling [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether a sulfur-rich flux will stabilize NaTlS rather than Tl2S, Na2Sx, or other polysulfide phases.

#### F2: Route 4

The solvothermal route has mild temperature severity, supported only indirectly by TS-2 hydrothermal synthesis at 115 °C. It still requires sealed evacuated vessels and likely multi-step precursor preparation, as TS-2 uses a melt step before hydrothermal treatment. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for hydrothermal NaTlS synthesis, so this route is not the main recommendation. Toxic Tl chemistry remains a major risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 4

Solution-mediated sulfide transport can be kinetically favorable [Reasonable Inference], and TS-2 demonstrates hydrothermal crystallization of a thermodynamic chalcogenide analogue. However, the chemical gap from K–Cd–Sn–Se to Na–Tl–S is large, and CS/SS analogues overwhelmingly favor dry ampoule synthesis. Phase control may be poor because Tl2S is extremely insoluble and could precipitate before NaTlS ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, the route is exploratory despite good formal kinetics.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: large Na⁺/Tl⁺ size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure contains corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but no face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered by the target description. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell contains more than 40 atoms, so C2 is treated as not triggered/neutral. The target’s simple ternary composition and low E_hull reduce amorphization concern, but layered metastable phases can still exhibit stacking disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaTlS is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0470 eV/atom, placing it just inside the high-synthesizability stability bin. CS and SS analogues strongly support dry ampoule synthesis of alkali metal sulfides at 500–750 °C with slow cooling, especially CS-1/3/4 and SS-1/2. TS analogues support that phases at similar hull energies can be synthesized, but TS is chemically less specific and flags possible phase-control difficulty. The most critical enabling factor is enhanced mass transport by a sulfur/alkali sulfide flux or melt, supported by CS-1/3 and SS-2. The most critical limiting factor is controlling volatile/toxic Tl–S/Na–S chemistry in a sealed ampoule while avoiding competing binary sulfides and polysulfides.
