The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the boundary of the highest stability bin. Comparable thermodynamic analogues near this range are synthesized: Na3Cu2SbO6 has E_hull = 0.056 eV/atom and is made by solid-state reaction (TS1), AgBiO3 has E_hull = 0.0499 eV/atom and is made by ion exchange (TS2), and Li2Cu2TeO6 has E_hull = 0.0556 eV/atom and is made by ion exchange (TS3). SS gives weaker thermodynamic support because several structural analogues are much higher in hull energy, e.g., K2CuTeO7 around 0.204–0.206 eV/atom (SS1/SS2), so SS is only neutral-to-moderate for F1.

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin. TS is highly supportive because Na3Cu2SbO6, AgBiO3, and Li2Cu2TeO6 have similar E_hull values and are experimentally synthesized by solid-state or ion-exchange methods (TS1–TS3). CS also supports synthesizability because several chemically related oxides are at or near the hull, including Ca2ScSbO6 and Li2ZrTeO6 (CS1, CS3). SS is less favorable thermodynamically because several structural analogues have high hull energies, so SS is treated as neutral-to-moderate rather than decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard air/oxygen ceramic conditions, which are supported by CS1, CS3, and TS1. The severity is moderate because repeated grindings/calcinations are required, as explicitly reported in CS1, CS3, and TS1. The main deductions are the multi-step nature and toxicity of Sb reagents; insufficient evidence from SS for this specific solid-state Na–Li–Sb route, so SS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetically, Route 1 is limited by reconstructive solid-state formation from carbonates/oxides. CS1 and CS3 show that repeated grinding/heating can overcome this for related Sb/Te oxides, while TS1 shows Na–Sb–O phases can be made by ceramic reaction with quenching. However, the target has Na/Li/Sb ordering and mixed edge/corner sharing, so complete crystallization may require multiple refirings; insufficient evidence from SS for direct Route 1 kinetics, so SS is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has mild thermal conditions because TS3 demonstrates Li exchange in molten LiNO3 at 300 °C, and TS2 demonstrates low-temperature ion exchange at 70 °C. Its severity arises from needing a parent phase plus exchange and washing, making it multi-step. CS gives indirect support through alkali/high-valent oxide chemistry but insufficient evidence for actual ion exchange in Na–Li–Sb oxides, so CS is neutral for the ion-exchange-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has the best kinetics because molten nitrate provides high alkali mobility, directly supported by TS3. Topochemical exchange reduces the need for full structural reconstruction, which is why TS3 could access Li2Cu2TeO6 when direct high-temperature synthesis was not effective. The uncertainty is that no provided CS/SS/TS source identifies a specific Na–Sb–O parent with the same framework as Na2LiSbO6, so this route is strong but not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate hydrothermal temperatures, supported by 230 °C synthesis in SS1/SS2 and hydrothermal preparation in CS2. It avoids high furnace temperatures and high-pressure solid-state methods, but it still requires sealed autoclave handling and toxic Sb reagents. TS gives insufficient evidence for hydrothermal Na–Li–Sb synthesis specifically, so TS is neutral for Route 3 condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because dissolved alkali and antimonate species can nucleate crystals without long-range solid-state diffusion; this is supported by SS1/SS2 and CS2. The route may be especially useful for low-symmetry crystals, since CS2 gives a triclinic P-1 sodium arsenate under hydrothermal conditions. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: SS5 shows that hydrothermal oxide growth can require long durations and specialized conditions, and no direct Na–Li–Sb hydrothermal analogue is provided.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination mismatch among Na, Li, and Sb, which raises crystallization difficulty; this is evident from the target coordination environments. The target also has mixed corner/edge connectivity, which appears in related TS structures such as Na3Cu2SbO6 and Li2Cu2TeO6 (TS1, TS3). The unit-cell-size trigger is not supported by the provided structural description, so that check is not applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiSbO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The TS analogues are the strongest support: several compounds with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible, especially by ion exchange or controlled solid-state reaction (TS1–TS3). CS supports the chemical feasibility of Sb5+/alkali oxide formation, especially through solid-state air synthesis using Sb2O5 or antimonic acid (CS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining an oxidizing, alkali-controlled environment while allowing Na/Li ordering, best achieved by molten nitrate exchange or carefully controlled ceramic synthesis. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because alkali volatility, Na/Li site disorder, and the unusual O–O bond may promote competing antimonates or amorphous oxygen-rich products.
