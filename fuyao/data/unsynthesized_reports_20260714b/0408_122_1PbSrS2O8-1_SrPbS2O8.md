### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0250 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom category. CS-1–4 report chemically similar sulfate/selenate double salts at E_hull = 0, SS-2/SS-4 show structurally similar double sulfates near/on the hull, and TS-1–5 include thermodynamic analogues in roughly the same low-hull range. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not identify whether SrPb(SO4)2 is the equilibrium phase against separate SrSO4 + PbSO4 under experimental sulfur activity.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

The evaporation route is mild because CS-1–3 crystallize double sulfates at room temperature/293 K from concentrated H2SO4, and SS-2/SS-5 use only 80–95 °C or 313 K evaporation. Its main severity penalty is toxic Pb precursor handling; CS/SS do not imply high pressure, high temperature, or controlled atmosphere. Kinetically, solution growth is favorable because cations are mixed at molecular/ionic scale, but the key uncertainty is low PbSO4/SrSO4 solubility, which is not directly addressed by the alkali/Au or Li/K analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal synthesis has modest condition severity because TS-4 uses 155 °C/48 h autoclave conditions and TS-2 uses 230 °C/3 days, both far below high-temperature solid-state regimes. The autoclave is specialized but not high-pressure in the >5 GPa sense, and no controlled atmosphere is reported in TS-2 or TS-4. Kinetically, hydrothermal acid can improve dissolution/recrystallization of poorly soluble sulfates, but hydrated or basic sulfate phases may compete, as TS-4 involves a hydrated sulfate product.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

The solid-state route is more severe because SS-4 required 600 °C for 100 h with repeated grinding to prepare related double sulfates. It avoids autoclaves and strong acid solution handling during the high-temperature step, but PbSO4 toxicity and possible SOx loss create practical concerns. Kinetically, binary sulfates reduce anion reconstruction, yet cation ordering and diffusion are slower than in solution, consistent with the long annealing in SS-4.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains regular SO4 tetrahedra and high-coordinate Sr/Pb sites, which generally favors crystallization rather than glass formation; this is consistent with many crystalline sulfate analogues in CS, SS, and TS. The Sr/Pb radius mismatch is not expected to exceed the 15% trigger in comparable high coordination, and the described target formula/inequivalent sites do not suggest a very large complex cell. However, CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify amorphization for SrPb(SO4)2, so their direct signal for F4 is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPb(SO4)2 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.025 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support solution-based crystallization of sulfate double salts, while TS analogues support hydrothermal and moderate-temperature alternatives. The most critical enabling factor is the robustness of SO4 tetrahedral chemistry and the availability of mild sulfate-rich crystallization routes demonstrated in CS-1–3 and SS-2/SS-5. The most critical limiting factor is the poor solubility and precipitation tendency of SrSO4 and PbSO4, which may favor binary sulfate segregation rather than ordered SrPb(SO4)2. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with slow acidic crystallization or hydrothermal recrystallization preferred over dry solid-state reaction.
