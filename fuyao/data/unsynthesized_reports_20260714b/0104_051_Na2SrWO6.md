### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold in the scoring rubric [Target]. Several analogues near 0–0.07 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized, including Sr₂CaWO₆/Sr₂MgWO₆ and alkali tungstates [CS1–CS5, SS1, SS5, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target’s peroxide-like O–O unit may impose kinetic/chemical constraints not fully captured by the hull energy [Target, Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

The ceramic route is only moderately severe because CS analogues use ordinary air, alumina/Pt crucibles, and standard furnaces [CS1–CS4, TS5]. It is penalized for >1000 °C and repeated grinding/heating, which are common in Sr–W oxide ceramics [CS1–CS4]. Insufficient evidence from SS for this exact Sr-containing ceramic route, so SS is neutral for Sr transferability here.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are less favorable because carbonate/WO₃ reactions require long diffusion lengths and repeated high-temperature firings in CS analogues [CS1–CS4]. Phase formation in CS materials can involve SrWO₄/Sr₂WO₅ impurities, indicating moderate phase-control difficulty [CS1–CS3]. The target structure is not the same as the perovskite-derived WO₆ CS analogues, so a larger reconstruction is expected [Target, CS1–CS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth reduces temperature severity relative to ceramic synthesis because alkali tungstate analogues crystallize near 530–750 °C [SS1, SS5]. However, SS5 requires Ar, sealed Au/Ni containers, air-sensitive alkali oxides, and very long anneals, so the route is operationally challenging [SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS for sealed alkali flux conditions, so CS is neutral for this severity factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux improves kinetics because molten alkali tungstate/oxide media enhance cation transport [SS5, Reasonable Inference]. Structural support is relatively strong from SS5 because KNa₃WO₅ contains NaO₅ and WO₅ trigonal bipyramids with edge/corner sharing, resembling target Na/W polyhedra [Target, SS5]. The main uncertainty is Sr incorporation and stabilization of the O–O bond under flux conditions, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS [Target].

#### F2 — Route 3

The hydrothermal route has mild temperature and moderate equipment requirements based on TS3, which uses 150 °C in Teflon-lined Parr vessels [TS3]. It avoids >1000 °C, high pressure >5 GPa, and refractory solid-state diffusion [TS3]. It is penalized only for oxidizing/peroxide/alkaline chemistry hazards inferred for the target’s O–O bond [Target, Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and short transport distances, supported by TS3’s 24 h hydrothermal crystallization of a Na/K/W oxyfluoride [TS3]. The target’s O–O bond makes a low-temperature oxidative route chemically plausible because high-temperature ceramic conditions may destroy peroxo units [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is substantial because no CS/SS/TS example directly shows an anhydrous Na–Sr–W peroxide oxide crystallized hydrothermally [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has highly dissimilar cations, Na/Sr/W, which triggers the ionic-size mismatch check [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not support a >40 atom cell, and it reports corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, so only one amorphization trigger is counted [Target]. This gives low–medium amorphization risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂SrWO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff but not clearly below it [Target]. The CS analogues strongly support Sr–W oxide formation by ceramic synthesis, while SS analogues support Na–W low-dimensional WO₅/alkali tungstate chemistry, and TS3 supports low-temperature hydrothermal crystallization of Na/K/W oxy-compounds [CS1–CS4, SS5, TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive Na–W solution or flux chemistry that can form distorted Na–O/W–O polyhedra without requiring extreme conditions [SS5, TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is preservation and ordering of the target’s O–O/peroxide-like unit while avoiding SrWO₄, Na₂WO₄, and related normal tungstate impurities [Target, CS1–CS3, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but the best attempt should prioritize low-temperature oxidative crystallization rather than direct high-temperature ceramic firing [Target, TS3, Reasonable Inference].
