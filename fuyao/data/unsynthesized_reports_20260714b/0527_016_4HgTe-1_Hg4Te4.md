### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom, so it is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range assigned 90–100 by the rubric. TS analogues with comparable low E_hull values, including CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom and Hg3Tl2Te4 at 0.0186 eV/atom, have reported syntheses [TS1, TS2]. SS analogues also include multiple stable or near-stable Hg-containing layered/low-dimensional phases [SS1–SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull indicates thermodynamic accessibility but not whether this specific HgTe polymorph is selected over other binary HgTe polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Sealed ampoule solid-state synthesis is supported by Hg chalcogenide/halide analogues prepared in evacuated glass or silica ampoules at 260–687 °C [SS3, SS5] and by direct Hg-containing chalcogenide reactions in quartz ampoules [TS3]. The severity score is reduced by vacuum, Hg volatility, and Hg toxicity, all requiring sealed-tube handling [SS3, SS5, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, low-melting Hg/Te chemistry is favorable, but converting to a monoclinic 2D polymorph likely requires significant structural reconstruction and slow annealing [SS5, TS4, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. The route is credible but may yield the common HgTe phase unless temperature/cooling/annealing are optimized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best total score because Te-rich melts produce telluride crystals [CS1] and Hg–Tl–Te flux growth under evacuated slow-cooling conditions is directly demonstrated [TS2]. F2 is penalized by vacuum, toxic/volatile Hg chemistry, and multistep flux handling [TS2, Reasonable Inference]. F3 is high because a liquid flux reduces diffusion limits and can enable low-dimensional telluride crystal growth, as seen in Hg–Tl–Te flux and Bi–Te layered systems [TS2, CS4]. The main uncertainty is whether a Te self-flux stabilizes monoclinic HgTe or simply crystallizes a more stable/common HgTe polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition is supported chemically by PbTe thermal evaporation and general telluride evaporation [CS2, CS3], but HgTe-specific evidence is insufficient from SS and TS. F2 is limited by high-vacuum requirements and toxic volatile Hg control [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference]. F3 is lower because Hg/Te stoichiometry control in films is expected to be narrow and because the monoclinic target likely requires careful low-temperature crystallization or post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The route could be valuable for metastable stabilization by substrate effects, but that is an inference rather than demonstrated by the provided analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

CVT is supported for layered Bi–Te phases using I2 at 770–820 K [CS4], and sealed ampoule Hg chalcogenide synthesis is common [SS3, SS5]. F2 is penalized by vacuum/I2 transport chemistry, sealed volatile Hg chemistry, multistep setup, and toxicity [CS4, SS3, SS5]. F3 is moderately strong because vapor transport bypasses solid diffusion, but Hg–Te–I or Hg–chalcogenide–halide phases are plausible competitors, as similar Hg–Te/Se–halide structures form readily [SS1, SS3, SS5]. Thus CVT is a secondary route rather than the preferred first attempt.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hg, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [target structure]. The target structure description includes mixed edge- and corner-sharing trigonal pyramids, but not face-sharing, so the rubric’s strongest mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met [target structure]. SS and TS analogues show many Hg chalcogenide frameworks can crystallize as ordered phases rather than amorphous products [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The residual uncertainty is that thin-film deposition may still produce amorphous Hg–Te if deposited too cold, but that is route-specific and not a bulk structural inevitability [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a near-hull metastable phase rather than a confidently established ground state, because its E_hull is 0.044 eV/atom rather than 0 [target structure]. The CS/SS/TS analogues collectively support synthesis of Hg-containing chalcogenides and tellurides in sealed ampoules, fluxes, and vapor/film routes [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The strongest enabling factor is the soft, low-temperature Hg–Te chemistry and the availability of sealed-tube/flux methods that avoid refractory diffusion limitations [SS3, SS5, TS2]. The strongest limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: binary HgTe can plausibly crystallize into competing structures, while the target is a low-symmetry 2D monoclinic variant [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, flux growth or long sealed-ampoule annealing is likely feasible, but isolating the exact P2/m sheet polymorph may require careful control of chemical potential, cooling rate, and post-growth annealing.
