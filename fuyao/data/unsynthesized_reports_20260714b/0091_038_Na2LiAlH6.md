### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the required cutoff for a 90–100 thermodynamic score. CS-1/SS-1 gives a directly matching Na₂LiAlH₆ composition with E_hull ≈0.004 eV/atom and successful synthesis, which strongly supports the chemical composition. TS analogues have E_hull values around 0.034–0.087 eV/atom and are synthesized mainly by high-pressure methods, indicating that hydrides in this stability range can be accessible but may require controlled conditions. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: CS-1/SS-1 supports cubic Na₂LiAlH₆, not necessarily triclinic P1 Na₂LiAlH₆.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity

Route 1 scores best because CS-1/SS-1 uses only 180 °C and 30 bar D₂, with the main penalties being controlled atmosphere and air-sensitive alanate/deuteride precursors. Route 2 is more severe because CS-2/SS-2 requires 100–700 bar H₂, 320–330 °C, pelletizing, and high-pressure containment. Route 3 is moderate-to-challenging because CS-3/SS-3 supports 50 bar H₂ and 315 °C, but binary hydride/metal reactions require milling and careful handling. Route 4 is practically the most equipment-intensive because SS-5/TS-4 uses multi-anvil conditions, although the formal deduction rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa and the proposed route uses 1–4 GPa.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics

Route 1 has the best kinetics because CS-1/SS-1 shows exact-composition Na₂LiAlD₆ can form by ball milling plus a short low-temperature anneal, implying highly reactive precursors and low diffusion barriers. Route 2 remains kinetically favorable because CS-2/SS-2 forms a closely related K₂LiAlH₆ perovskite-derived hydride at 320–330 °C under H₂. Route 3 is slower because forming Al–H coordination from Al metal and binary hydrides is less direct; CS-3/SS-3 supports this general approach for Na₂Mg₂NiH₆ but not for alanate AlH₆ formation. Route 4 can aid polymorph trapping under pressure, as suggested by SS-5/TS-4, but phase coexistence and pressure gradients reduce confidence.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target combines small Li/Al centers with larger Na sites, and the target structure shows very different Na–H, Li–H, and Al–H bond distances; this supports a cation-size mismatch trigger. The primitive P1 target description appears to contain only 10 atoms, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not supported; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a larger cell means this check is not triggered. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is inferred from the perovskite-derived nature and from CS-1/SS-1, where the cubic analogue contains corner- and face-sharing NaH₁₂/LiH₆/AlH₆ motifs. The remaining uncertainty is that the target description itself does not explicitly list all edge/face-sharing relations.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂LiAlH₆ in the target P1 structure is likely a near-threshold metastable polymorph rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while the exact-composition cubic analogue in CS-1/SS-1 is much closer to the hull. The CS and SS evidence strongly support synthesis of the Na₂LiAlH₆ composition, especially through mechanochemistry and low-temperature hydride annealing, but they support the cubic perovskite-derived structure more directly than the triclinic P1 variant. TS evidence is less chemically specific and mainly indicates that hydride phases with similar metastability often require pressure or controlled hydrogen chemical potential. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of highly reactive alanate/hydride precursors under H₂/D₂ pressure, as shown in CS-1/SS-1 and CS-2/SS-2. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: the desired P1 distortion may compete with the known cubic Na₂LiAlH₆ phase and with impurity phases such as Al or Na₃AlH₆ noted in CS-1/SS-1.
