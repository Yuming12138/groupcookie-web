### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be thermodynamically stable on the convex hull [Target]. This is reinforced by multiple Zr–C structural/thermodynamic analogues at E_hull = 0, including Zr₂PbC and Zr₂TlC [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4], and by chemically similar layered Zr halide/nitride or chalcogenide phases that are also on or near hull [CS1–CS3]. The remaining uncertainty is kinetic: a ground-state prediction does not specify whether Zr₃C₂Cl₂ is reachable without decomposition, halide loss, or competing ZrC/ZrClₓ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is severe because it uses refractory ZrC/Zr powders and volatile chlorides in sealed high-temperature conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Similar Zr–C layered compounds require hot pressing at 1200 °C and/or sealed-tube annealing up to very long times [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the exact Zr₃C₂Cl₂ solid-state reaction, so the severity score is conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetic penalty is large because ZrC and Zr metal are refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues report long annealing and phase-control problems in Zr–C systems [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4], supporting slow ordering kinetics. The favorable point is that Zr–C local coordination is already present in ZrC-like precursors and structurally similar carbides [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C processing, giving it the least severe thermal profile [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires dry inert/sealed handling because molten chlorides and Zr chloride species are moisture-sensitive/corrosive [Reasonable Inference], while analogous Zr nitride sulfide and Zr–C systems use in vacuo or inert handling [CS4, CS5, SS1]. CS/SS/TS do not directly confirm this exact topochemical route, so the score is not raised into the easy range.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 scores best kinetically because a layered Zr₃C₂ precursor could preserve the carbide slab while adding/ordering Cl termination [Reasonable Inference]. Flux media are supported by CS3, where NaCl accelerates Zr₂N₂Se formation at 900 °C, and by CS1/CS2, where halide fluxes enable layered Zr nitride halides. The main uncertainty is whether the target P-6m2 ordering forms rather than a disordered Cl-terminated carbide or mixed termination phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is severe because high-pressure halide flux synthesis requires 3–5 GPa, sealed noble-metal capsules, and controlled cooling/quenching [CS1, CS2]. Although the formal deduction table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, the practical equipment burden is still high because multianvil apparatus is required [CS1, CS2]. The route is therefore rated challenging despite moderate synthesis temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The reactive flux improves kinetics relative to dry solid-state reaction, as shown for ZrNCl/ZrNBr high-pressure flux growth [CS1, CS2]. However, the CS analogues are nitride halides rather than carbide chlorides, so carbon incorporation from ZrC may remain sluggish [CS1, CS2; SS5/TS5]. Structural reconstruction is more extensive than in the topochemical route, so the score is intermediate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The formula unit is small, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom cell [Target]. The main amorphization/defect risk is mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which can increase the number of competing local arrangements [Target; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₃C₂Cl₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The CS data strongly support the existence of layered Zr–halide bonding motifs, especially ZrNCl and ZrNBr [CS1, CS2], while the SS/TS data support stable layered Zr–C frameworks such as Zr₂PbC and Zr₂TlC [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is use of a flux or topochemical pathway that avoids full diffusion-controlled reconstruction of refractory ZrC [CS3–CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is chloride stoichiometry control, because volatile/reactive chloride precursors can cause loss of Cl or formation of competing ZrClₓ/ZrC phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but likely requires dry, chloride-rich, kinetically assisted conditions rather than simple ceramic sintering.
