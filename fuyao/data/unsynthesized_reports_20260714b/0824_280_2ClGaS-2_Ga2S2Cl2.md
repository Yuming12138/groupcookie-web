### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0210 eV/atom, placing it in the high-likelihood metastable/near-ground-state window. Thermodynamic analogues with similar low E_hull values are experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state or CVT methods [TS1, TS2, TS3]. Chemical data are partially contradictory: some chemically similar entries are very high above hull [CS1, CS3], but Ga sulfide analogues such as CuGaS₂ and CoPSe are stable and experimentally accessible [CS4, CS5]. Overall, TS evidence is weighted most strongly because it directly matches the low-hull regime; insufficient evidence from CS for exact GaSCl stability, scored neutral-to-positive overall.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed-tube chemistry, which is common across Ga sulfides and thermodynamic sulfide analogues [CS4, SS4, TS1]. The severity deductions come from vacuum sealing [CS4, SS4, TS1], volatile GaCl₃ requiring a sealed system [SS1, SS2], multi-step heating/annealing similar to CS4 and TS1, and moisture-sensitive halide/sulfide handling inferred from glovebox/vacuum protocols [SS1, SS2, TS1]. There is no evidence for high pressure or >1000 °C need; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target decomposition temperature.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The Ga₂S₃ + GaCl₃ combination should be kinetically favorable because GaCl₃ is volatile/reactive [SS1, SS2] and Ga₂S₃ is already a Ga–S precursor [TS1]. The low melting point of Ga-containing species and possible vapor/melt mediation reduce diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction into a 2D mixed-anion sheet, since related solid-state routes often require long anneals or repeated heat treatments [CS4, CS5, TS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

CVT/sublimation growth is well supported for Ga halides and related sulfides [SS1–SS3, SS5, TS3]. The route still requires evacuated sealed ampoules [SS1–SS3], volatile halide transport [SS1, SS2], and multi-step preparation/growth/selection [SS1, SS2]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact GaSCl transport temperature, so the proposed 450–700 °C range is an extrapolation.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Vapor availability is favorable because GaCl₃ and GaBr₃ sublime and crystallize in ampoules [SS1, SS2], and sulfide analogues grow by halogen-assisted CVT [SS3, SS5, TS3]. Crystallization is less certain because no source demonstrates mixed Ga–S–Cl sheet growth by CVT; this uncertainty lowers the crystallization score. Composition control is the limiting kinetic/process factor because the relative S and Cl activities can vary along the gradient [Reasonable Inference], and halide analogues report twinning/crystal-growth issues [SS1, SS2].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Direct element routes are established for Ga sulfides [CS4, SS4] and Ga halides [SS1, SS2], but Cl₂ introduces a higher handling burden. The route has sealed-vapor pressure hazards from sulfur and chlorine [CS4, SS4; Reasonable Inference] and requires staged heating to avoid uncontrolled reaction [Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure apparatus or >1000 °C conditions are required based on analogous sealed-tube chemistry [CS4, SS4].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Elemental Ga, S, and Cl₂ are reactive, so precursor activity is high relative to refractory solid-state routes [CS4, SS4, SS1, SS2]. Diffusion should be aided by liquid Ga and volatile S/Cl species [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural reconstruction and phase competition are severe because binaries such as Ga₂S₃ and GaCl₃ may form preferentially before GaSCl nucleates [Reasonable Inference], and related direct solid-state systems require careful multistep annealing [CS4, CS5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation species, Ga, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger [target description]. The structure is a small P3m1 GaSCl layer with only corner-sharing GaS₃Cl tetrahedra, so the atom-count and mixed-connectivity triggers are not met [target description]. Some analogues have edge-sharing/twinning complications [SS1, SS2], but those are not intrinsic to the target topology; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for amorphous GaSCl formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaSCl is likely a near-ground-state metastable phase rather than a deeply unstable compound because its E_hull is only 0.0210 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized low-hull thermodynamic analogues [TS1–TS3]. The analogues strongly support the availability of Ga–S tetrahedral chemistry [CS4, SS3, SS4, TS1] and Ga–Cl tetrahedral/terminal bonding [SS2], but none directly reports GaSCl, so the main uncertainty is phase selectivity. The most important enabling factor is the clean binary-precursor stoichiometry Ga₂S₃ + GaCl₃ → 3 GaSCl, which supplies Ga, S, and Cl without extraneous cations or counterions [Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is control of volatile GaCl₃/S-containing species in a sealed tube, since Ga halide analogues require sublimation/vacuum handling and can show crystal-growth/twinning issues [SS1, SS2]. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with the best chance via carefully dried, sealed-ampoule Ga₂S₃/GaCl₃ reaction.
