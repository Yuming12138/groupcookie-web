### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Chemically similar PdZnF6, PdCdF6, and MgPdF6 also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS3, CS4), and PdCdF6 is also thermodynamically similar and stable (TS5). SS/TS Au-fluoride analogues are stable but chemically less direct, so they provide supportive rather than decisive evidence (SS1/SS2/SS4/SS5; TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic/phase-selection rather than thermodynamic.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which is favorable, but it requires diluted F2 gas and a staged fluorination/tempering protocol (CS1/CS3/CS4). The main severity penalties come from reactive F2 atmosphere, multistep heating/tempering/cooling, and toxic/reactive precursors (CS1/CS3/CS4). SS provides insufficient evidence for this exact PdNi direct route and is scored neutral where not relevant; TS5 supports the same PdCdF6 fluorination chemistry.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Carbonate/acetate/oxide/Pd chloride mixtures are relatively reactive under F2, as shown by direct PdMF6 formation in CS1/CS3/CS4. However, the target structure differs from the trigonal corner-sharing PdMF6 analogues because it is a monoclinic 2D sheet with both edge and corner sharing, so cation ordering and structural reconstruction may slow phase formation. The exact reaction temperature is unspecified in CS, leaving uncertainty in diffusion kinetics.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperature but requires sealed Au/Cu/Pt-compatible tubes, inert loading, and potentially F2 or volatile/high-valent fluoride species, as seen in sealed-tube complex fluoride syntheses (CS2; SS1/SS2/SS5; TS1/TS2/TS4). Long annealing and controlled cooling add operational complexity. CS evidence is only moderately transferable because CS2 concerns Cu/Sn or Cu/Mo-type complex fluorides, not PdNiF6 specifically.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Binary fluorides are less chemically active than acetate/carbonate precursor mixtures, and analogous sealed solid-state fluoride syntheses often require days to weeks (CS2; SS1/SS2/SS5; TS1/TS2/TS4). Existing M–F bonds help reduce anion reconstruction, but the target’s specific 2D Pd/Ni ordering is not demonstrated in SS/TS. Thus kinetic score is moderate-low.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Autoclave fluorination uses moderate temperature but pressurized F2/N2, specialized corrosion-resistant equipment, and careful sealing/loading, as documented for LiAuF4-type analogues (SS4; TS3). It does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty, but it is operationally hazardous. CS direct-flow routes suggest high pressure may not be necessary for PdMF6 analogues, so Route 3 is a fallback rather than first choice (CS1/CS3/CS4).

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
High fluorine chemical potential should accelerate conversion and stabilize high-valent fluoride environments, consistent with pure microcrystalline fluoride preparation in autoclave conditions (SS4; TS3). However, long holds of several days remain likely, and no source directly confirms formation of the monoclinic PdNiF6 sheet. The kinetics are better than sealed inert binary-fluoride annealing but less straightforward than direct reactive precursor fluorination.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has simple octahedral PdF6/NiF6 building blocks, no reported face sharing, and likely no >40-atom cell from the formula/structure description. The Pd–F and Ni–F distances are close to those seen in PdMF6 and other fluoride analogues, suggesting compatible local coordination (CS1/CS3/CS4). Remaining uncertainty is not amorphization but possible formation of alternate crystalline Pd/Ni fluoride polymorphs.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PdNiF6 is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically similar PdMF6 fluorides are also calculated stable and experimentally accessible by direct F2 fluorination (CS1, CS3, CS4; TS5). The strongest support comes from chemical similarity: PdZnF6, PdCdF6, and MgPdF6 share the same MPdF6 stoichiometry and PdF6 octahedral chemistry, even though their trigonal structures differ from the target monoclinic 2D sheet (CS1/CS3/CS4). The most critical enabling factor is access to high fluorine chemical potential using diluted F2, which is repeatedly successful for Pd-containing complex fluorides (CS1, CS3, CS4; TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Ni may form simple NiF2 or competing Pd/Ni fluorides unless oxidation, stoichiometry, and thermal profile are tightly controlled. Overall, synthesis is feasible but requires specialized fluorination capability.
