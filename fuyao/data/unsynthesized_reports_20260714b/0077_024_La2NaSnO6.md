### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the high-synthesizability boundary under the provided rubric. Stable or near-stable chemically similar double perovskites exist, including NaLa2RuO6 at 0.0 eV/atom [CS1], La2LiSbO6 at 0.003 eV/atom [CS2], and NaNd2RuO6 at 0.0376 eV/atom [CS3]. A thermodynamically similar double perovskite, Ca2LaSbO6, has E_hull ≈0.0504 eV/atom and was experimentally prepared [TS3]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target decomposes into competing Na–Sn, La–Sn, or La2Sn2O7-type phases, because the structural stannate analogue in SS is pyrochlore rather than NaLa2SnO6 [SS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, solid-state ceramic

The ceramic route is credible because multiple oxide analogues were made by solid-state or ceramic methods in air, including La2LiSbO6 [CS2], Sr2HoSbO6 [CS4], Ca2LaSbO6 [TS3], and Sm2Sn2O7 [SS3]. Its severity is moderate because temperatures likely exceed 1000 °C and repeated grinding/firing is expected, as in CS4, SS3, and TS3. Kinetics are the main weakness: SnO2 and La2O3 are refractory oxide precursors, and stannate pyrochlores required 1000 °C plus 1400–1500 °C multi-day treatment [SS3]. Na volatility is an additional uncertainty inferred from alkali-loss issues in Li-containing oxides [SS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, molten NaOH flux

The flux route scores best because the most chemically transferable analogues, NaLa2RuO6 and NaNd2RuO6, were synthesized by molten hydroxide fluxes [CS1, CS3]. Conditions are mild in temperature, with NaNd2RuO6 grown at 600 °C for 12 h followed by slow cooling [CS3]. Kinetically, molten NaOH provides a liquid transport medium, avoiding the long diffusion distances that limit solid-state oxide reactions [CS3]. The key uncertainty is that SnO2 solubility and Sn valence stabilization in NaOH flux are not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS; this is handled as a reasonable extrapolation from RuO2 flux chemistry and SnO2 stannate chemistry [CS3, SS3, Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, nitrate/citrate ceramic

This route benefits from high precursor homogeneity, supported by La nitrate/Li nitrate use in La2LiSbO6 synthesis [CS2] and co-precipitation routes for thermodynamically similar rare-earth cuprates [TS2]. Its condition severity is low to moderate because the intended temperature is below or near 1000 °C and no high pressure is needed [CS2, TS2]. Kinetically, nitrate/citrate precursors should improve cation mixing relative to binary oxides [Reasonable Inference], but the structure must still crystallize into an ordered monoclinic Na/La/Sn framework. Direct evidence for sol-gel or nitrate synthesis of NaLa2SnO6 is insufficient from SS and only indirect from CS/TS, so this route is slightly less certain than flux growth.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, high-pressure oxidizing

High-pressure synthesis is justified only as a contingency because CS5 shows that high pressure plus KClO4 oxidizer can stabilize unusual high-valent oxide chemistry in La2LiFeO6. The target’s formal charge balance suggests Sn5+ if interpreted ionically, which may require stronger oxidizing conditions than ordinary air firing [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is low due to the 6–8 GPa requirement and reactive oxidizers [CS5]. F3 is moderate-good because CS5 achieved reaction in 30 min at 900 °C, but transferability is uncertain because no SS/TS source gives direct high-pressure Na–La–Sn evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization concern is the large cation-size mismatch among Na, La, and Sn sites, which triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is ordered and contains tilted corner-sharing NaO6/SnO6 octahedra, which is not inherently amorphous-prone, and analogous ordered oxides were experimentally crystallized [CS1, CS2, CS3, TS3]. The target description includes corner and edge sharing, but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count above 40 is not established from the provided target data, so C2 is not triggered and evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaLa2SnO6 is likely a **near-metastable** rather than strongly ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability boundary. The strongest support comes from chemically similar NaLa2RuO6 and NaNd2RuO6, which share Na–lanthanide–B-site oxide double-perovskite chemistry and were synthesized by molten hydroxide fluxes [CS1, CS3]. Thermodynamic support is also favorable because Ca2LaSbO6, a near-hull double perovskite with E_hull ≈0.0504 eV/atom, was made by solid-state sintering [TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is use of a flux or highly mixed precursor route to bypass slow La/Sn oxide diffusion [CS3, CS2]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Na stoichiometry and possible stabilization of a high formal Sn oxidation state [Reasonable Inference], neither of which is directly proven for NaLa2SnO6 in the provided CS/SS/TS data.
