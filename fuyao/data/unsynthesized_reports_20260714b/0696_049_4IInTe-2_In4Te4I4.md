### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. TS analogues with very similar hull energies are experimentally reported, including MnIn₂Te₄-II at 0.0354 eV/atom (TS-1), Bi₂TeI at 0.0388 eV/atom (TS-3), and InTe at 0.0386 eV/atom (TS-4). CS and SS provide an even stronger chemical/structural signal because InTeBr is on the hull and experimentally grown by Bridgman (CS-1 / SS-1). The remaining uncertainty is whether the iodide target is a true equilibrium phase or a shallow metastable phase stabilized only under iodine-rich conditions.

#### F2 — Route 1 Bridgman severity

The closest analogue InTeBr was grown from melt at only 520 °C, so the thermal severity is moderate rather than harsh (CS-1 / SS-1). No CS/SS evidence indicates high pressure, and TS high-pressure examples are structurally/thermodynamically relevant but not required for the close InTeBr analogue. Deductions come from the need for dry sealed handling of volatile iodides, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from halide volatility and the hygroscopic handling issues noted for InTeBr precursors in CS-1. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for Bridgman severity in In–Te–I; neutral TS signal.

#### F3 — Route 1 Bridgman kinetics

Melt growth strongly reduces diffusion limitations, and InTeBr crystallized directly from melt at 520 °C (CS-1 / SS-1). The target’s local bonding is almost identical: InTe₃I tetrahedra in the target versus InTe₃Br tetrahedra in InTeBr (CS-1 / SS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether iodine volatility changes the melt composition during growth; this is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed-tube severity

Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis is widely supported for related chalcogenides and halochalcogenides, including Te₃(PdBr)₄ at 700 K in sealed ampoules (CS-2), AgIn₅Te₈ by direct fusion in evacuated quartz (SS-5), and InGaTe₂ in high vacuum at 550–650 °C (TS-5). Severity is increased by vacuum sealing, iodine volatility, and possible multi-step heating. High pressure is not indicated by CS or SS for the closest InTeBr-type chemistry, while TS high-pressure examples are less directly applicable. The uncertainty is phase purity, because TS-5 and TS-3 both show phase-control issues in related systems.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed-tube kinetics

Elemental and binary precursors are reasonably reactive, and low-melting In plus volatile iodide species may enhance transport; this is consistent with successful direct fusion routes in SS-5 and TS-5. However, solid-state analogues sometimes require long heating, such as several weeks for InGaTe₂ (TS-5) or long annealing for Ga₂TeSe₂ (CS-4), so a kinetic penalty is applied. The target benefits from structural similarity to InTeBr (CS-1 / SS-1), but competing In–Te and In–I phases remain a risk by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 CVT severity

CVT conditions require evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile transport agents, supported by MnIn₂Se₄ CVT under high vacuum with AlCl₃ (TS-2) and Bi₂TeI gas-phase growth in evacuated quartz tubes (TS-3). Temperatures are moderate, typically 550–650 °C for iodotelluride gas-phase synthesis (TS-3), and no high pressure is required. Toxic/corrosive iodine species increase severity. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for CVT of InTeI; neutral CS signal.

#### F3 — Route 3 CVT kinetics

Vapor transport can overcome bulk diffusion limitations, and TS-2 confirms large single-crystal growth of a layered In chalcogenide by CVT. TS-3 confirms iodotelluride crystals can grow from iodide-rich vapor, but it also reports very small amounts, multiple phases, and twinning. Thus, kinetics for crystal growth are plausible, but composition control is the limiting kinetic factor. SS-4 further supports CVT as a way to obtain single-phase crystals when direct synthesis fails.

#### F2 — Route 4 hydrothermal severity

Hydrothermal synthesis in HI is supported by AuTeI formation from elements in hydroiodic acid (CS-3). The method likely uses much lower temperatures than melt or CVT routes, but CS-3 does not report exact temperature/time, so the mild thermal estimate is [Reasonable Inference]. The penalties are mainly for sealed autoclave use and corrosive/toxic HI/I₂. SS and TS provide insufficient direct hydrothermal evidence for In–Te–I; neutral signals from SS and TS.

#### F3 — Route 4 hydrothermal kinetics

Hydrothermal growth can provide high precursor mobility through dissolved iodide/tellurium species, and AuTeI thin plates formed by this route (CS-3). However, transferring AuTeI chemistry to InTeI is uncertain because In may hydrolyze or form stable iodide complexes; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the route has good nominal kinetics but reduced confidence in structural selectivity. SS and TS do not provide direct hydrothermal kinetic support for InTeI.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size disorder does not trigger the radius-difference criterion. Its described structure is a simple layered InTeI sheet with corner-sharing tetrahedra, not a large-cell mixed-polyhedral network; this is supported by the target description and by the close InTeBr analogue being a crystalline 2D sheet compound (CS-1 / SS-1). TS gives insufficient direct evidence on amorphization, but several thermodynamic analogues are crystalline layered phases (TS-2 / TS-3), giving no strong amorphization warning. The main uncertainty is whether rapid quenching from an iodide melt could trap disorder, so slow cooling is preferred.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InTeI is likely a shallow metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.0360 eV/atom, within the range of experimentally accessible TS analogues such as MnIn₂Te₄-II and Bi₂TeI (TS-1 / TS-3). The strongest synthesis support comes from the near-isostructural and chemically closest InTeBr analogue, which was grown from melt by Bridgman at 520 °C (CS-1 / SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a highly transferable InTeX layered tetrahedral motif with terminal halogen coordination (CS-1 / SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is iodine volatility and iodine chemical potential control, inferred from the need for sealed iodide/halide vapor or ampoule conditions in related systems (TS-3, CS-2, SS-5). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with Bridgman growth the most evidence-backed practical route and hydrothermal HI synthesis an attractive but less certain exploratory route.
