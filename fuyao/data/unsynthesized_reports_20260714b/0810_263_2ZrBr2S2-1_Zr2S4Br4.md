### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly indicates a predicted ground-state phase [Target]. The closest chemical analogue Nb(SBr)₂ is also essentially stable, with E_hull ≈ 0.0008 eV/atom, and it has been synthesized experimentally [CS1]. SS/TS analogues include multiple on-hull chalcogenide phases, supporting the idea that layered metal–chalcogen frameworks can be stable, but SS/TS do not directly validate Zr–S–Br chemistry; therefore SS/TS are treated as secondary, partially useful evidence rather than primary proof [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderate because the closest Nb(SBr)₂ analogue forms at only 500 °C in 2 days [CS1]. The main severity deductions come from volatile Br₂/S in a sealed ampoule and hazardous Br₂/Cd handling [CS1, CS5]. No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required by the analogue data [CS1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Zr metal reacts as readily as Nb under the same conditions, since Zr oxide passivation is not addressed in CS/SS/TS and is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics benefit strongly from the exact isostructural Nb(SBr)₂ precedent [CS1]. The presence of Br₂ and sulfur vapor likely enhances transport compared with a purely refractory metal solid-state reaction [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. However, Zr metal’s refractory nature and surface oxide can slow conversion [Reasonable Inference], and CS1 reports simultaneous polymorphs, which lowers the kinetics/phase-selection confidence [CS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures, because NbX₂Y₂ CVT occurs at 400–515 °C rather than extreme furnace conditions [CS2]. Deductions arise from evacuated sealed tubes, volatile halogen chemistry, and toxic/corrosive bromine handling [CS2, CS4, CS5]. The long duration does not directly appear in the F2 deduction table, but it is still a practical burden supported by the 3–4 week duration in CS2 [CS2]. SS/TS ampoule-gradient syntheses support the equipment pattern but do not specifically address bromine volatility in the target [SS2, TS2, TS5].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT receives the best F3 because vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion and has already produced related layered chalcogenide halides [CS2, CS4]. NbX₂Y₂ analogues form under a small gradient, showing that halogen-mediated transport is suitable for layered M–chalcogen–halogen compounds [CS2]. The limiting uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS2 notes polymorphs, stoichiometry sensitivity, and contamination issues, while CS4 reports CrSBr by-products such as CrBr₃ and Cr₂S₃ [CS2, CS4].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has similar moderate temperature requirements to CS3, where NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ was reacted around 530 °C [CS3]. It is penalized more than Route 1/2 because ZrS₂ may require prior synthesis and ZrBr₄ is expected to be moisture-sensitive/volatile by analogy to metal bromide handling in NbBr₅ work [SS3, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. The route still avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature [CS3]. The main uncertainty is whether ZrBr₄ participates cleanly without sublimation or side reactions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Binary precursors should improve kinetics by starting with Zr–S and Zr–Br bonds instead of requiring direct Zr metal conversion [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. The analogous NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ route demonstrates that chalcogenide + halide binaries can form metal chalcogenide halides in sealed tubes [CS3]. However, structural reconstruction from ZrS₂ plus ZrBr₄ into the specific S–S-containing Zr(SBr)₂ sheet may be nontrivial [Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS3 also warns of poor reproducibility and narrow stability, which lowers the kinetic confidence [CS3].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk [Target]. The described C2/m layered structure is not a large complex cell, and no >40-atom unit cell is indicated in the target description [Target]. The connectivity is not described as a coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, unlike some cluster or octahedral networks in other references [Target vs CS4/CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that layered halide/chalcogenide materials may show stacking disorder, but that is not the same as amorphization and is not explicitly reported for the target [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(SBr)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis case is strongly supported by the chemically closest analogue Nb(SBr)₂, which is isostructural and experimentally obtained by sealed-tube reaction at 500 °C [CS1]. CVT is the recommended route because related NbX₂Y₂ layered chalcogenide halides are grown by evacuated ampoule transport at moderate temperature gradients [CS2], and CVT should improve crystal quality relative to direct sintering [CS2, CS4]. The most important enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, which allows refractory transition-metal chalcogenide halides to form at 400–550 °C rather than near metal melting temperatures [CS1, CS2]. The most important limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control under volatile Br₂/S conditions, since related systems show polymorph coexistence, contamination, and by-products [CS1, CS2, CS4].
