### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the threshold for high thermodynamic synthesizability. CS and SS are strongly favorable because the closest Na–Sr–Al–P and Na–Sr–P analogues are reported as E_hull = 0 [CS1, SS1, SS2]. TS also supports feasibility because experimentally known thermodynamic analogues occur at similar metastabilities, e.g., NaBeSb at 0.048 eV/atom and KMnAs at 0.055 eV/atom [TS3, TS5]. The uncertainty is that 0.050 eV/atom may still allow competing phases, particularly NaSrP or NaSr₂AlP₃-like compositions [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 1

Direct elemental synthesis is supported by elemental routes for K₂NaGaP₂, K₂NaAlP₂, NaBaP, and AMnX pnictides [CS4, CS5, SS4, TS1]. The severity score is reduced because Na/P require sealed containment, and inert/vacuum ampoule conditions are repeatedly used in analogues [SS1, SS4, TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure, so high pressure is not penalized. The main uncertainty is whether the target can form before Na/P loss or competing binary/ternary phosphides dominate.

#### F2 — Route 2

Binary phosphide synthesis is directly suggested by CS analogues using KP/InP or LiP/AlP and sealed ampoules [CS2, CS3], and the closest Na–Sr–Al–P analogue is synthesized in a sealed niobium ampoule [CS1/SS2]. The route still requires controlled atmosphere and sealed systems because Na/Sr phosphides are reactive and phosphide mixtures are air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1, TS3]. Insufficient evidence from TS for target-specific binary precursor choice is treated as neutral, but TS confirms that sealed/inert pnictide solid-state chemistry is standard [TS1, TS4]. The largest uncertainty is availability and purity of Sr–P/Na–P binary precursors.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth avoids >1000 °C conditions because NaSrP was grown at 600 °C and related Sr phosphides at 900 °C [SS1, SS3]. However, the score is reduced for Ar glovebox/vacuum sealing, volatile Na/P, and multi-step flux separation or Na sublimation [SS1, TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of the exact Na–Sr–Al–P quaternary is a limitation, but SS provides a strong structural basis [SS1]. The route is operationally more complex than direct sintering but less thermally severe.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental precursors are reactive, and related elemental pnictide mixtures form crystalline phases at 870–1100 °C [CS4, CS5, TS1]. Diffusion should be helped by low-melting Na/Sr/Al components [Reasonable Inference], but the target framework must be built from scratch, making structural reconstruction significant. Long reaction times in SS/TS analogues imply slow ordering kinetics [SS1, TS4, TS5]. Thus kinetics are moderate rather than excellent.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary phosphides provide preformed M–P bonds, which should lower reconstruction barriers relative to elemental mixtures [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by AlP/LiP/KP/InP precursor use in chemically similar compounds [CS2, CS3]. The downside is that binary phosphides may be dense solids and require diffusion across solid interfaces, so precursor activity is not as high as a molten flux [CS1–CS3]. Overall kinetics are moderately favorable.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because Na/Sn flux enabled single-crystal NaSrP formation with long high-temperature holds and slow cooling [SS1]. Liquid flux should improve mass transport and compositional equilibration [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1 and TS2]. The remaining kinetic challenge is ordering Al into the Na–Sr–P framework and avoiding crystallization of simpler NaSrP or NaSr₂AlP₃-like phases [CS1, SS1]. Long flux holds reduce the structural-reconstruction score [SS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly size-mismatched cations Na, Sr, and Al in distinct coordination environments, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Target, CS1]. The formula unit is small, and the provided target description does not establish a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for this exact target cell-size factor is treated as neutral. Mixed connectivity is present in some analogues [CS1, SS5], but explicit target polyhedral sharing is not specified, so C3 is not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₃Sr₃AlP₄ is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and experimentally known TS analogues exist near this metastability range [TS3, TS5]. The CS evidence is strong because NaSr₂AlP₃ contains the same Na–Sr–Al–P chemistry and similar Na–P, Sr–P, and Al–P bond lengths [CS1/SS2]. The SS evidence is also strong because NaSrP shares the Na–Sr–P hexagonal structural motif and is experimentally grown by flux methods [SS1]. The most important enabling factor is sealed-ampoule or flux chemistry that suppresses Na/P loss and provides high activity for phosphide formation [SS1, CS1]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition among nearby Na–Sr–P and Na–Sr–Al–P compounds, especially because related phases were sometimes obtained as by-products or with impurities [CS1, SS1, SS4].
