### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom places it at the high end of the “readily accessible metastable” range by the required rubric. CS analogues include both stable and metastable synthesized phases, including E_hull = 0.0 for CS3 and E_hull = 0.127 eV/atom for CS1, showing that related oxides need not be strict ground states. TS analogues with similar thermodynamic distances include synthesized phases around 0.055–0.092 eV/atom, but several require ion exchange or high pressure, indicating that a 0.05 eV/atom phase may be accessible but route-sensitive (TS1–TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee the correct Na/Tl/Sb/Te ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal severity

Hydrothermal conditions are moderate because CS2–CS5 use 230 °C for 3 days in PTFE-lined autoclaves, and CS1 uses 453–503 K hydrothermal conditions. No GPa pressure, reducing atmosphere, or >1000 °C treatment is required in these CS examples. The main severity penalty is toxicity from Tl/Sb/Te precursors; Tl nitrate toxicity is directly relevant because TlNO3 appears in SS2. Insufficient evidence from SS for hydrothermal synthesis of the exact target, so the condition score remains high but not perfect.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis gives high precursor mobility through dissolution/reprecipitation, supported by single-crystal growth in CS1–CS5 and TS4. This should reduce solid-state diffusion limitations relative to oxide sintering. The kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: no CS/SS/TS source gives an exact NaTlSbTeO6 parent or isostructural analogue, so nucleation selectivity may be moderate rather than easy.

#### F2 — Route 2 flux/solid-state severity

Flux growth is supported by SS1 at 750 °C for 2 days in air, and solid-state Sb oxide synthesis is supported by TS3 at 1023–1123 K with quenching. The route avoids high pressure but introduces high-temperature volatility and toxicity concerns for Tl and Te, which are [Reasonable Inference] risks. SS3 and TS3 also show multi-step grinding/heating/cooling operations, so the severity is higher than hydrothermal.

#### F3 — Route 2 flux/solid-state kinetics

Flux can improve mass transport, as SS1 demonstrates single-crystal Pb2TeO5 formation using PbF2 flux. However, oxide/carbonate starting materials generally have lower activity than nitrates or hydrothermal solutions, and TS3 shows repeated calcination/firing/quenching for Na–Cu–Sb oxide formation. The target’s low-symmetry mixed-cation structure likely requires complete crystallization from melt/flux rather than a simple topotactic step, lowering the structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3 ion-exchange severity

Molten nitrate ion exchange occurs at only ~300 °C in SS2 and TS2, so temperature severity is low. The route is penalized because it requires at least parent synthesis, exchange, and washing/drying, matching the multi-step workflows in SS2 and TS2. TlNO3 toxicity is directly relevant from SS2. Insufficient evidence from CS for ion exchange in the closest Na/Tl/Sb/Te composition makes this route plausible but not the best first attempt.

#### F3 — Route 3 ion-exchange kinetics

Molten nitrate media are kinetically favorable for mobile cation exchange, as shown by SS2 and by Li exchange into Na2Cu2TeO6 in TS2. If a suitable Na–Sb–Te–O parent exists, structural reconstruction could be limited and topotactic. The key uncertainty is that SS2 reports stacking faults/incomplete exchange for TlSbO3-type products, while TS2 reports metastable/polymorphic complications, so phase purity may be difficult.

#### F2 — Route 4 HPHT severity

HPHT is severe because TS1 requires 10.5 GPa/1150 °C and SS4 requires 7 GPa/950 °C; TS5 also requires 7 GPa and specialized multianvil equipment. Noble-metal capsules and quenching/decompression add process complexity, as reported in TS1 and TS5. Toxic Tl/Sb/Te handling and volatile component containment further reduce practicality by [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis because the chemically closest analogues are hydrothermal.

#### F3 — Route 4 HPHT kinetics

High pressure and high temperature can promote dense oxide formation, supported by TS1, SS4, and TS5. However, these examples mainly stabilize perovskite or dense polymorphs, whereas the target is triclinic P1 with no confirmed high-pressure structural analogue. Thus, the route may overcome thermodynamic barriers but has uncertain structural selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Mixed Na/Tl/Sb/Te cation chemistry creates size and coordination mismatch, and analogous structures show diverse coordination environments in CS1, SS1, SS2, and TS3. The target description includes corner and edge sharing, which can frustrate simple crystallization, but face sharing is not reported, so the full mixed-connectivity trigger is not applied. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40-atom cell, so the atom-count trigger is neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaTlSbTeO6 is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and TS analogues in this energy range often require route control such as ion exchange or high pressure (TS1–TS3). The CS data most strongly support hydrothermal synthesis, with multiple chemically similar Te-containing oxides prepared in PTFE-lined autoclaves under moderate conditions (CS2–CS5), while SS and TS provide backup evidence for flux, ion-exchange, and HPHT approaches. The most critical enabling factor is solution or flux-mediated mobility, which can assemble a low-symmetry multication oxide without relying on slow solid-state diffusion, as supported by CS1–CS5 and SS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase/oxidation-state control among Tl, Sb, and Te, because SS2 and TS2 show defect/incomplete-exchange problems and TS3 notes the need to avoid Sb redox/alkali volatility issues. Overall, the compound is plausibly synthesizable, with hydrothermal screening recommended first.
