### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase rather than a high-energy metastable material [Target data]. Chemically and thermodynamically similar materials such as NbSeI₃ and NbSeBr₃ are also on the hull, which supports the plausibility of a stable low-dimensional chalcogenide-halide phase [CS1/SS1/TS1; SS2/TS2]. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide a Zr-specific experimental analogue, so the stability prediction may not fully capture volatile-iodide phase equilibria [insufficient Zr-specific evidence from CS/SS/TS; neutral 50 for that sub-factor].

#### F2 — Route 1, direct sealed-tube reaction

Route 1 uses standard evacuated ampoule conditions similar to Nb–Se–I and related elemental syntheses, so it avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature [CS1; CS3; SS5/TS4]. The severity deductions come mainly from vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile I₂/Se, and hazardous/toxic precursors [CS1; CS3; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Zr reacts cleanly with Se and iodine before forming stable binary side phases [insufficient Zr-specific evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1, direct sealed-tube reaction

Kinetically, elemental Zr is less reactive than preformed halide or vapor-transport intermediates because it is a refractory metal [Reasonable Inference]. Related systems can be made from elements, but often require long durations, quenching/reheating, or slow cooling, indicating slow ordering and crystallization [CS1/SS1/TS1; CS3; SS5/TS4]. This gives a relatively low kinetics score despite good thermodynamic stability.

#### F2 — Route 2, CVT/self-transport

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and simple evacuated quartz ampoules, matching the successful CVT conditions in CS2 and SS2/TS2. It still requires a controlled vacuum/sealed environment and volatile iodine transport agent, which impose safety and handling penalties [CS2; SS2/TS2; SS5/TS4]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C conditions are required by analogy [CS2; SS2/TS2].

#### F3 — Route 2, CVT/self-transport

CVT offers the strongest kinetic pathway because volatile iodide species can transport Zr and iodine, reducing reliance on bulk diffusion [CS2; SS5/TS4; TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Closely related layered NbX₂Y₂ materials are explicitly prepared by chemical transport, making this the most transferable route to a 2D Zr(SeI)₂ sheet [CS2; Target data]. The limitation is that phase windows in NbX₂Y₂ can be narrow and iodine excess may be required or harmful depending on composition [CS2].

#### F2 — Route 3, binary-precursor annealing

The binary-precursor route is based on NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ synthesis at 530 °C for 2–3 weeks, so the temperature itself is moderate [CS5]. However, it requires moisture-sensitive or volatile halide precursors and likely multi-step precursor preparation, increasing severity [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. Sealed tubes remain necessary because halide and chalcogen vapor pressures are significant [CS5; Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3, binary-precursor annealing

Preformed ZrSe₂ and ZrI₄ should improve chemical activity relative to elemental Zr, giving better kinetics than Route 1 [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The route may preserve some Zr–Se connectivity, but converting to the target Zr(SeI)₂ layer still involves substantial anion rearrangement [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. CS5 also warns that related binary-precursor halide selenides may have poor reproducibility and narrow stability, lowering confidence [CS5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation and a relatively simple low-dimensional composition, so cation-size disorder and large-cell amorphization risks are low [Target data]. The structural description does not show highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, which reduces topological frustration [Target data]. CS/SS/TS contain low-dimensional analogues with phase-purity issues, but those issues are more related to stoichiometry/polymorphism than amorphization [CS1; CS2; CS4; CS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(SeI)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target data]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support sealed-ampoule and CVT approaches for low-dimensional transition-metal chalcogenide-halides, especially Nb–Se–I/Br/Cl systems [CS1; CS2; CS3; SS1/TS1; SS2/TS2]. The most important enabling factor is iodine/halide vapor transport, which can accelerate mass transport and crystal growth at moderate temperatures [CS2; SS5/TS4; TS5]. The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, because related materials show phase-purity difficulty, polymorph coexistence, or poor reproducibility [CS1; CS2; CS4; CS5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with CVT/self-transport the best first experimental route.
