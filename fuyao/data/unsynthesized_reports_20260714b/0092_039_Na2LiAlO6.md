### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability edge of the required 90–100 scoring bracket. TS analogues with E_hull ≈0.059–0.096 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable (TS1, TS2, TS5), and CS analogues include a Na oxide at 0.060 eV/atom and Na2LiAlF6 at 0.010 eV/atom (CS1, CS3). SS analogues show that peroxide-like O–O compounds can be reported even at higher hull energies, but often with lability or phase-control concerns (SS2, SS3, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed target corresponds to a true peroxide/superoxide charge-balanced phase or a metastable oxygen-redox variant.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The proposed solid-state route avoids >1000 °C, unlike CS1 at 1000 °C and CS3 melting at 1223 K, because peroxide O–O units are likely unstable at high temperature by [Reasonable Inference] from SS2/TS3. It does require controlled O2 or a sealed system to retain oxidizing oxygen, analogous to sealed-tube control in TS1/TS5. Regrinding/reheating is supported by CS1, and air-sensitive peroxide precursors add risk. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct solid-state synthesis of the exact Na–Li–Al oxyperoxide, so the severity score is conservative.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
CS1 shows that conventional oxide solid-state synthesis can work but requires regrinding and reheating, suggesting diffusion/ordering limitations. CS3 shows Na–Li–Al chemistry can be accessed through a melt/sinter route, but the target oxide/peroxide requires more extensive reconstruction than the fluoride analogue. Peroxide precursors improve oxygen activity by [Reasonable Inference] based on SS2/TS3, but Al2O3/AlOOH incorporation may still be kinetically slow. The main uncertainty is whether an intermediate carbonate/aluminate phase competes before the P1 phase forms.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses mild hydrothermal temperatures similar to TS2 at 200 °C and below CS5 at 350 °C, with no high-pressure apparatus like CS2. H2O2/Na2O2 introduces oxidizer handling risk, supported by peroxide chemistry in SS2/TS3, but equipment is standard Teflon-lined autoclave as in TS2. SS1 and SS5 show inert/controlled handling may be useful for sensitive solution-grown phases, though not necessarily mandatory. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact hydrothermal Na–Li–Al oxyperoxide formation, so the score is high but not perfect.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Solution transport and alkaline mineralization are strongly supported by CS5, TS2, SS3, and SS4, and peroxide formation from LiOH/H2O2 is directly supported by SS2/TS3. Hydrothermal crystallization avoids long-range solid-state diffusion and can produce single crystals, as in TS2 and CS5. The structural reconstruction score is limited because SS3 and SS5 report hydrate transformations, instability, and air sensitivity in related solution-grown oxyanion phases. The largest uncertainty is whether hydrothermal conditions favor the anhydrous Na2LiAlO6 phase over hydroxides, aluminates, or hydrated peroxides.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux growth has strong precedent in TS1 and melt/sinter Na2LiAlF6 synthesis in CS3, but a sealed oxidizing flux is operationally more demanding than Route 2. Controlled atmosphere/sealing is justified by TS1/TS5 and by [Reasonable Inference] that alkali peroxide fluxes release oxygen and absorb moisture/CO2. The route is below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct molten-flux oxyperoxide growth, so sealed-flux risk remains.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux/liquid transport should give excellent kinetics, supported by TS1 flux growth and CS3 molten/sintered Na–Li–Al fluoride formation. Slow cooling helps crystallization and ordering, supported by TS1 and TS2. Structural assembly is plausible but not certain because no exact Na–Li–Al oxyperoxide flux analogue appears in CS/SS/TS. The route may be better for crystals than bulk powder but may require extensive optimization of flux composition.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The main amorphization risk is the large cation size/coordination mismatch among Na, Li, and Al, a [Reasonable Inference] from ionic-radius chemistry; however, CS3 demonstrates that Na/Li/Al can order successfully in a crystalline framework. The target structural description contains only about 10 atoms per formula description, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not supported by the target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests a larger supercell. Mixed edge/face connectivity is not clearly described for the target, while many CS/TS analogues do show complex edge/face sharing; because the target does not explicitly do so, this check is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is local disorder in peroxide O–O orientations.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiAlO6 is likely a **near-threshold metastable or marginally stable oxyperoxide** because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and the target contains short O–O bonds consistent with peroxide/superoxide oxygen redox. The CS data strongly supports Na–Li–Al framework chemistry through Na2LiAlF6 (CS3), while SS data supports peroxide-like O–O structural motifs but also warns of lability and phase-control challenges (SS2, SS3, SS5). TS data shows that materials in this thermodynamic range are experimentally accessible by hydrothermal, sealed-tube, or flux methods (TS1, TS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high peroxide/oxygen chemical potential at modest temperature, as inferred from SS2/TS3 and sealed-system examples TS1/TS5. The most critical limiting factor is avoiding hydrated aluminates, simple Li2O2/Na2O2, or alkali aluminate competing phases during crystallization.
