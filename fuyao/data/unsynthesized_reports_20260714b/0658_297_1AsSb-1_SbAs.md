### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0390 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and justifying a high F1 score of 94. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies have reported syntheses, including Na(GeAs)6 at 0.0425 eV/atom [TS1], Cu2As4S3Cl2 at 0.0286 eV/atom [TS3], As2Se3 at 0.0273 eV/atom [TS4], and InGaTe2 at 0.0349 eV/atom [TS5]. Chemical and structural analogues also show that phases above hull can still be synthesized, e.g., GeAs at 0.105 eV/atom [CS1] and GeTe at 0.114 eV/atom [CS3/SS2]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be a metastable polymorph rather than a ground-state As–Sb phase; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact AsSb P3m1 polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule direct solid-state

The condition severity is moderate because the route uses evacuated sealed quartz, as in GeAs2 and Na–Ge–As syntheses [CS4, TS1], and must manage volatile/toxic As [CS4, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, unlike CS1 and TS4, which improves F2. Kinetically, elemental As/Sb should diffuse more readily than refractory oxide systems [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s ordered 2D P3m1 sheet requires full reconstruction from elements [target structure]. Long anneals reported for Na–Ge–As and layered chalcogenide analogues indicate ordering may be slow [TS1, SS1, SS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT/vapor-assisted growth

F2 is lower than Route 1 because CVT adds a halogen transport agent, gradient operation, and increased sealed-ampoule pressure/corrosion hazards [CS5]. F3 is the best among the routes because halogen transport can bypass bulk diffusion and produce crystals from vapor species [CS5]. The method is transferable by analogy because As/Sb can form volatile halide species [Reasonable Inference], but no source directly demonstrates AsSb CVT, so composition control remains the largest uncertainty [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure synthesis

High pressure imposes a large F2 penalty because CS1 uses 15–65 kbar and TS4 uses 3–7 GPa, with specialized presses and capsules. F3 is acceptable because CS1 shows pnictide products can form in 1–2 h at high pressure, and TS4 shows high pressure can access metastable arsenic chalcogenide phases. The main uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes or destabilizes the desired 2D P3m1 AsSb sheet; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS addresses pressure effects in AsSb.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: thin-film deposition

F2 is moderate-to-low because SS4 requires vacuum deposition and post-heating, and As volatility imposes additional handling constraints [SS4; Reasonable Inference]. F3 is limited by vapor-flux mismatch and possible amorphous deposition before annealing, although SS4 confirms deposition/annealing can form related layered Ge–Sb–Te phases. The uncertainty is high because no CS or TS source directly supports thin-film AsSb formation, and SS4 also notes disorder/reproducibility issues in related films.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has a small unit cell and simple 3-coordinate T-shaped bonding, so it does not trigger the large-cell or mixed-connectivity amorphization checks [target structure]. The As/Sb size mismatch is near or slightly above 15%, triggering one amorphization-risk point [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for radii]. Layered analogues can crystallize, including Sb2Te3 nanosheets [SS5] and layered Ge–As/Ge–Sb–Te compounds [SS1, SS3, SS4], supporting a moderate-low amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is whether AsSb prefers a disordered alloy or segregated elemental phases rather than the ordered P3m1 sheet [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].
