### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility. This is consistent with several analogues having E_hull = 0.0 or near 0.0, including NbSeBr3 [CS2], Nb3TeI7/Nb6Te2I14 [CS4, CS5, SS5, TS5], Nb3Br8 [SS2, TS2], and Nb3I8 [SS4, TS4]. The main uncertainty is that the references do not explicitly report Nb3TeBr7 experimental synthesis, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** remains for the exact Nb–Te–Br phase competition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because the exact iodide analogue Nb3TeI7 forms at 600–800 °C [CS4, SS5, TS5]. The severity comes mainly from vacuum sealing and Br2 volatility/corrosivity, supported by Nb–Br syntheses requiring sealed/high-vacuum ampoules [CS2, SS2, TS2]. No high pressure or >1000 °C heating is required according to the closest analogues [CS4, SS5, TS5].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by halogen chemistry because Nb–Br phases can form via sealed-tube/CVT processes at 500–760 °C [SS2, TS2]. The main kinetic penalty is refractory Nb metal, which can react slowly unless converted into mobile halide species [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2]. The strongest positive factor is that Nb3TeI7 has the same P3m1 layered structure, so little structural novelty is required beyond I → Br substitution [CS4, SS5, TS5].

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires high vacuum, sealed ampoules, thermal gradients, volatile halogens, and multiple operations [CS2, SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. NbSeBr3 requires 15 days in a 500–460 °C gradient [CS2], while Nb3Br8/Nb3I8 transport uses high vacuum and 500–760/800 °C gradients [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4]. These factors make the route more condition-sensitive than direct synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT should provide favorable kinetics because transport avoids bulk diffusion and has already worked for Nb–Se–Br and Nb–halide phases [CS2, SS2, TS2]. The uncertainty is whether Nb3TeBr7 transports congruently without losing Te or over-brominating Nb [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Thus, CVT is especially attractive for crystals but not necessarily for phase-pure bulk powder.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth avoids very high temperature and high pressure; the analogous K4Nb6Br18 flux synthesis is performed at 1063 K, about 790 °C [SS3, TS3]. The route is penalized for multiple processing steps, sealed-tube handling, and moisture-sensitive halide fluxes [SS3, TS3]. It also risks alkali incorporation because the demonstrated flux contains LiBr/KBr and produces a K-containing niobium bromide [SS3, TS3].

#### F3 — Route 3

Molten halide flux should improve mass transport and reduce diffusion barriers, as shown for K4Nb6Br18 growth from Nb3Br8/Nb in LiBr/KBr [SS3, TS3]. However, there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** that Te can be incorporated into the desired Nb3TeBr7 sheet under flux conditions. The flux route is therefore kinetically promising but chemically less direct than the Nb3TeI7-style sealed-tube synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small formula unit, only one metal cation, and a well-defined layered crystalline topology [target description]. Crystalline layered analogues Nb3TeI7 and Nb3Br8 are experimentally accessible [CS4, SS5, SS2, TS2], arguing against severe amorphization. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for amorphous Nb–Te–Br products, so the residual risk is low but not zero.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb3TeBr7 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close Nb–halide/chalcogenide analogues are also stable or nearly stable [CS2, CS4, CS5, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS5]. The synthesis support is strong because the exact iodide analogue Nb3TeI7 has the same P3m1 layered structure and is made by direct elemental reaction in evacuated tubes [CS4, SS5, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, which allows refractory Nb to form layered Nb–halide frameworks at 500–800 °C [SS2, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Br2 chemical potential in a sealed ampoule, because Br2 is volatile/corrosive and can cause pressure hazards or competing bromide formation [CS2, SS2, TS2]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with the best initial attempt being a cautious sealed-tube direct reaction followed by CVT optimization for single crystals.
