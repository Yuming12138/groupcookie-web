### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.038 eV/atom, which is within the generally synthesizable metastability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. Thermodynamically similar compounds include MnIn₂Te₄-II at E_hull ≈0.035 eV/atom made by high pressure [TS1] and CuTe₂ at E_hull ≈0.053 eV/atom made by high pressure [TS2], supporting that nearby metastable tellurides can be isolated. The uncertainty is that low E_hull does not specify which polymorph forms under ambient sealed-tube conditions.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state.**  
Condition severity is moderate because the exact MnTe₂ composition was synthesized at 550 °C for 48 h in evacuated tubes using standard furnace equipment [CS1, SS1]. Kinetics are only moderate because the known MnTe₂ product is cubic pyrite rather than the target layered Pmma structure [CS1, SS1], while layered VTe₂ analogues require longer heating and slow cooling [CS4, CS5]. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports direct ambient-pressure synthesis of orthorhombic MnTe₂, so this route may yield the wrong polymorph.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, Te-rich flux/self-flux.**  
The flux route has moderate severity because it uses sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile Te, but not external high pressure [CS4, CS5, SS3]. Kinetics are favorable relative to dry solid-state reaction because excess molten Te can enhance mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and excess Te/slow cooling were used in related telluride syntheses [SS3]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: Te excess can also leave elemental Te impurity, as reported for VTe₂-related syntheses [CS4, CS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, high pressure.**  
High pressure is strongly supported for metastable chalcogenide/telluride phases, including FeTe₂ and CuTe₂ pyrite-like phases at 60–89 kbar [CS3, SS5, TS2] and MnIn₂Te₄-II at 1.5 GPa [TS1]. The severity score is low because specialized high-pressure equipment and quenching are required [CS3, SS5, TS1]. The main uncertainty is that the high-pressure analogues more strongly support dense pyrite-like structures than a 2D orthorhombic MnTe₂ sheet.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, CVT.**  
CVT is plausible because layered telluride crystals such as NbPdTe₅ were grown under a 700/750 °C iodine-assisted gradient for 8 days [SS4, TS4]. Severity is increased by evacuated sealed tubes, volatile/corrosive I₂, and likely need for pre-synthesized MnTe₂ feed [SS4, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS supports MnTe₂-specific CVT, and TS3 indicates CVT can produce morphology variability in tellurides.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a major amorphization driver [Target]. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target cell contains >40 atoms, so that check is neutral. The mixed face/edge/corner-sharing octahedral connectivity in the target triggers one amorphization/structural-complexity penalty [Target], though similar mixed connectivity exists in crystalline analogues [CS2, TS1], limiting the risk to low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target MnTe₂ Pmma phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the dominant ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.038 eV/atom [Target] while cubic MnTe₂ is reported as stable at E_hull = 0 in the reference data [CS1, SS1]. The analogue support is good for forming Mn–Te binaries by sealed-tube reaction [CS1, SS1] and good for forming layered MTe₂ tellurides by slow-cooled evacuated-ampoule synthesis [CS4, CS5], but no source directly reports this orthorhombic MnTe₂ polymorph. The most critical enabling factor is control of Te chemical potential and slow crystallization, supported by Te-rich/slow-cooling telluride syntheses [CS4, CS5, SS3]. The main limiting factor is polymorph competition with cubic pyrite MnTe₂, which is directly documented for the same composition [CS1, SS1]. If ambient-pressure routes fail, high-pressure/quench synthesis is a credible fallback for accessing metastable tellurides [CS3, SS5, TS1, TS2].
