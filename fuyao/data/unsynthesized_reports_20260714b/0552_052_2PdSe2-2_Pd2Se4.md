### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0440 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring range and indicates near-ground-state or weakly metastable thermodynamic stability [target]. TS analogues with comparable E_hull values, such as CrRh₂Se₄ at 0.043 eV/atom and FeRh₂Se₄ at 0.045 eV/atom, have reported solid-state syntheses (TS1, TS2). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not guarantee the desired orthorhombic polymorph over other Pd–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because evacuated sealed tubes are standard in the TS selenide syntheses (TS1–TS5), Se is volatile and requires containment (TS1–TS5), and repeated heating/grinding is common (TS1/TS2/TS4). It avoids extreme pressure and can likely stay below 1000 °C, consistent with SS1/SS2 and TS4/TS5. The main uncertainty is whether PdSe₂ forms cleanly at 650–800 °C or whether lower-temperature annealing is needed to preserve Se stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Pd is relatively unreactive compared with salts or fluxes [Reasonable Inference], and TS1/TS2 show that elemental selenides often need multi-day firings with grinding. However, Se vapor/liquid transport in sealed tubes can help reaction kinetics, as suggested by sealed-tube selenide syntheses across TS1–TS5 and Se vapor buffering in SS1/SS2. Structural reconstruction from elements into layered PdSe₂ is significant, lowering the kinetics score [target, Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures similar to PdBr₂ CVT at 700 °C and SnBr₂ transport at 650–700 °C (CS1–CS3). It is penalized for sealed ampoule/vacuum operation, volatile halogen transport agents, multiple steps, and hazardous halogen/selenium species (CS1, SS5/TS3). The uncertainty is that too much halogen activity may stabilize Pd–Se–halide complexes instead of PdSe₂, as shown by Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ formation (SS5/TS3).

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport should reduce solid-state diffusion limitations and enable recrystallization, directly supported by CS1–CS3. Starting from preformed PdSe₂ would make the pathway mostly crystal growth rather than chemical reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertain factor is finding a transport agent activity high enough for transport but low enough to avoid Pd–halide or Pd–Se–halide products (CS1, SS5/TS3).

#### F2 — Route 3

The chalcogen-halide melt route has low temperature, with Pd–Se–halide synthesis at 180 °C in SS5/TS3. It is nevertheless penalized for vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile/corrosive SeX₄ reagents, long reaction time, and likely washing/post-annealing steps (SS5/TS3). Uncertainty is high because the demonstrated product is not PdSe₂ but a halide-rich Pd–Se complex (SS5/TS3).

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetics are favorable because Se/SeX₄ melts can attack Pd metal and form Pd–Se bonds at only 180 °C (SS5/TS3). The analogue contains square-planar Pd coordination and Se–Se distances close to the target, which supports local motif transferability (SS5/TS3, target). The limiting kinetic issue is converting a molecular/chain halide environment into halide-free layered PdSe₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 has the mildest conditions because SS3/SS4 used 35 °C ultrasonication followed by only 500 °C annealing. It is penalized for multiple processing steps and hazardous Pd/Se reagents [Reasonable Inference based on SS3/SS4 precursor set]. The atmosphere requirement is milder than sealed-tube routes, although an inert or Se-rich anneal is recommended to protect stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference from SS1/SS2].

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution/sonochemical routes have high precursor activity because soluble Pt salt plus Se/Na₂SO₃ produced PtSe₂ in SS3/SS4, and analogous Pd salts should be chemically reactive [Reasonable Inference]. Nanoscale intermediates reduce diffusion distances during annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is structural: PtSe₂ in SS3/SS4 is trigonal octahedral, whereas target PdSe₂ is orthorhombic square-planar with Se–Se bonds, so phase selectivity is not directly proven.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not applicable [target]. Its described structure is a simple 2D PdSe₂ sheet with square-planar PdSe₄ units and Se–Se bonds, not a large mixed-polyhedra framework [target]. The low amorphization risk could be higher for the sonochemical route if the initial precipitate is amorphous, but that is a processing issue rather than an intrinsic structural complexity issue [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PdSe₂ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E above hull is only 0.044 eV/atom [target]. The strongest synthetic support comes from TS analogues showing that selenides with similar thermodynamic metastability can be prepared by sealed-tube solid-state reaction (TS1, TS2) and from SS/CS analogues showing that Pd/Se and layered diselenide chemistries are accessible by several routes (SS3–SS5, CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the chemical compatibility of Pd with square-planar chalcogen/halogen coordination and Se–Se bonding motifs, directly observed in Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ and related Pd compounds (SS5/TS3, CS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Pd–Se–halide complexes, elemental Se residues, Pd metal, or other Pd–Se phases may compete, especially under halide-rich or Se-poor conditions (SS5/TS3, SS1/SS2). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best practical route depending on whether the goal is nanopowder/film-like material or single crystals.
