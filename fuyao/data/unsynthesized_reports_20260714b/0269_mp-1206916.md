### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable end of the provided 0.05–0.20 eV/atom metastability range and at the cutoff for the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score category. CS supports feasibility because Tl–Fe/Tl–transition-metal sulfides exist with comparable hulls, including TlCo₂S₂ at 0.055 eV/atom and TlFeS₂ at 0.077 eV/atom (CS-3/CS-5). TS also supports synthesizability of phases with similar thermodynamic distance, e.g., Tl₂CS₃ at 0.075–0.086 eV/atom (TS-1/TS-2), while SS is less chemically specific and therefore provides weaker thermodynamic signal. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.05 eV/atom may indicate a metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because the closest Tl–Fe–S/Tl–transition-metal sulfide solid-state analogues require evacuated quartz ampoules, stepwise heating to 1050 °C, slow cooling, and quenching (CS-3/CS-4). The high temperature, vacuum, sealed volatile Tl/S environment, and toxic Tl chemistry all reduce the F2 score. The route is nevertheless standard for chalcogenide solid-state synthesis and does not require high pressure (CS-3/CS-4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly improves the severity assessment for FeTl₂S₂ solid-state synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate-to-difficult because Fe metal has low precursor activity compared with sulfide or solution precursors [Reasonable Inference], although Fe metal was successfully used in the CS-4 Tl–Fe–S synthesis. Ion diffusion is helped by the high 1050 °C temperature and long cooling in CS-3/CS-4, but the target’s Fe coordination differs from CS-4, increasing reconstruction uncertainty. Structural similarity to I4/mmm Tl–Fe–S in CS-4 supports partial transferability, while the target’s linear Fe–S environment is not directly demonstrated in any CS/SS/TS source.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions because Tl₂Q/Tl₂S flux analogues operate at 350–425 °C or 723–773 K (CS-1/CS-2). Deductions remain for vacuum ampoules, volatile/toxic Tl–S flux, multi-step slow cooling, and flux removal (CS-1/CS-2). Compared with Route 1, this route is less thermally severe but still requires careful sealed-tube handling. Insufficient evidence from TS for flux chemistry in Fe–Tl–S specifically leaves uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves kinetics because CS-1 and CS-2 show that Tl chalcogenide fluxes can transport and crystallize thallium sulfide phases over long slow-cooling schedules. FeS is expected to be more reactive in a sulfide flux than Fe metal alone [Reasonable Inference], and Tl–S bond lengths in CS-1/CS-2 are close to the target’s Tl–S distance. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the target’s unusual linear Fe–S coordination rather than forming known TlFeS₂ or TlFe₂S₂ competing phases (CS-4/CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate condition severity because SS-2 reports vacuum evaporation and post-heating at 300–700 °C, avoiding high bulk reaction temperatures. However, vacuum operation, volatile Tl/S species, and toxic Tl sources penalize the score (SS-2; volatility/toxicity Reasonable Inference). There is insufficient direct evidence from CS or TS for vapor-deposited FeTl₂S₂, so this route is more speculative despite manageable equipment demands.

#### F3 — Route 3

Thin-film kinetics are limited by composition control rather than bulk diffusion. SS-2 supports vapor deposition for some Tl chalcogenide-related phases, but also notes defect structures, disorder, and narrow composition windows, which weakens confidence for a ternary Fe–Tl–S phase. Fe, Tl, and S have mismatched vapor pressures and incorporation behavior [Reasonable Inference], and no source confirms the target structure forms by deposition. Thus Route 3 is feasible only as an exploratory metastable-phase route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes, Tl and Fe, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description also reports mixed edge- and corner-sharing STl₄Fe square pyramids, which can frustrate straightforward crystallization and raises amorphization/disorder risk. The unit cell is not indicated to be large, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests a >40-atom cell. Overall, amorphization risk is medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeTl₂S₂ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a confidently established ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data provide the strongest support: Tl–Fe–S and Tl–transition-metal sulfides are known from sealed-tube solid-state synthesis, and Tl-rich sulfides are known from Tl₂S/Tl₂Q flux routes (CS-1 through CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of a Tl₂S-rich flux, which can lower reaction temperature, maintain Tl/S chemical activity, and promote crystal growth, as demonstrated for Tl chalcogenide analogues (CS-1/CS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with known TlFeS₂/TlFe₂S₂-type compounds and the need to stabilize the target’s unusual linear Fe–S coordination, which is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS. Therefore, the material is reasonably synthesizable, but likely requires careful flux composition and slow-cooling optimization.
