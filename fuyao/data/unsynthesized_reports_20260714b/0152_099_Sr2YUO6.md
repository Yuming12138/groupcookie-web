### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability cutoff, so F1 is scored 90. CS supports favorable Sr–U oxide chemistry because Sr₂CaUO₆ is on the hull and Sr₂CdUO₆/Sr₂MgUO₆ are near-hull or stable analogues (CS1/CS3/CS4). SS supports related Sr/Ba uranates near or on the hull, including Sr₃UO₆ and Sr₂CaUO₆ (SS2/SS3), while TS shows that materials with comparable E_hull ≈0.05–0.07 eV/atom have been synthesized (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS reports the exact Sr₂UYO₆ phase, and the target may be metastable rather than a robust ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C firing because Sr₂CaUO₆, Sr₂CdUO₆/Sr₂MgUO₆, and Sr₂UZnO₆ analogues require high-temperature ceramic treatment (CS1/CS3/CS4/TS5). It is also penalized for repeated grinding/refiring because CS1/CS3/CS4 explicitly use repeated regrinding or long calcination. Air operation is favorable and supported by CS1, SS2, and TS5, so no controlled-atmosphere penalty is applied. The main uncertainty is whether air stabilizes the required U oxidation state for Sr₂UYO₆ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Carbonate/oxide precursors are standard and accessible, but Y₂O₃ and U oxides are refractory, so precursor activity is only moderate [Reasonable Inference based on CS/TS oxide/carbonate routes]. CS1/CS3/CS4 and SS2 show that related phases often require long holds or repeated regrinding, indicating slow diffusion. Structural reconstruction is penalized because the closest CS analogues are mainly corner-sharing double perovskites, whereas the target has edge-sharing U/Y polyhedra. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct conversion into this exact spinel-derived structure.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 still requires high-temperature crystallization, as TS4 and TS5 use high-temperature ceramic firing for uranium oxides. It is multi-step because solution mixing, drying/burnout, calcination, and sintering are needed [Reasonable Inference from TS4/TS5]. It avoids sealed systems and high pressure, which is consistent with TS4/TS5 ambient ceramic processing. Uranium precursor toxicity remains unavoidable for all uranium-containing routes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Uranyl nitrate and acetate precursors are more reactive than dense oxides, and their use is directly supported by Ba₂SrUO₆ and Sr₂UZnO₆ syntheses (TS4/TS5). Better homogeneity should shorten diffusion lengths and improve Y/U ordering [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural reconstruction remains nontrivial because TS4/TS5 are perovskite-derived analogues rather than the target’s spinel-derived structure. The remaining uncertainty is whether nitrate/acetate processing can stabilize U⁵⁺ without producing competing U⁶⁺ or U⁴⁺ phases.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized for controlled atmosphere because SS1 uses purified oxygen-free Ar, CS3/CS4 allow evacuated ampoules, and TS1 uses evacuated sealed quartz. It is also penalized for multiple steps and uranium precursor toxicity, as supported by SS1/TS1 and the U-containing CS routes. Temperature is less extreme than TS4 but still likely exceeds 1000 °C for complete reaction. The benefit is redox control, but this benefit comes with significant handling complexity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Oxide/uranate precursors have modest activity, and SS1’s use of preformed BaUO₃/Ba₂UO₅ implies precursor preparation complexity. Diffusion remains a bulk solid-state process, although analogous uranium oxides can form at 900–1250 K or 900–1150 °C (SS1/CS3/CS4). Structural reconstruction and phase control are challenging because SS1 reports limited stability and difficulty obtaining pure Ba₂U₂O₇. This route is best viewed as a backup if air processing fails to control U oxidation state.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 benefits from low temperature because CS5, SS4, and SS5 use 500–600 °C hydroxide flux conditions. It is penalized for sealed wet hydroxide flux, multistep crystal isolation, and uranium toxicity. It does not require high pressure or high-temperature sintering. The uncertainty is high because no source demonstrates Sr–U–Y oxide growth from hydroxide flux.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Flux growth strongly improves ion transport through dissolution/reprecipitation, as shown by the successful growth of La₂NaTaO₆, KLa₂NbO₆, and KNd₂NbO₆ crystals at 500–600 °C (CS5/SS4/SS5). This gives favorable diffusion kinetics despite refractory cations. The structural-transfer score is low because the flux analogues incorporate Na/K and contain Nb/Ta rather than U/Y/Sr chemistry. Alkali incorporation and U redox instability remain major uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with substantially different sizes and valences, Sr²⁺/Y³⁺/U⁵⁺, which increases disorder and amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure includes mixed edge- and corner-sharing motifs, and structurally similar SS entries also contain mixed polyhedral connectivity (SS1/SS4/SS5). The unit-cell atom count is not provided, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell. Overall, two triggers justify a medium-risk F4 score of 60.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂UYO₆ is likely a borderline metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and TS data strongly support synthesis of Sr/Ba uranium double oxides by ceramic routes, especially Sr₂CaUO₆, Sr₂CdUO₆, Sr₂MgUO₆, and Sr₂UZnO₆ (CS1/CS3/CS4/TS5). The SS data support related mixed-connectivity uranium oxides but also warns that some structurally similar Ba–U phases have narrow stability fields and purity challenges (SS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of robust high-temperature Sr–U oxide ceramic chemistry with repeated regrinding and phase monitoring. The most critical limiting factor is controlling the Y³⁺/U⁵⁺ charge balance and cation ordering in a spinel-derived edge-sharing framework not directly demonstrated by the provided analogues.
