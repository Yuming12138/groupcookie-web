### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 stability bracket in the requested rubric. CS gives favorable support because Ca₂ScOsO₆ is on hull [CS1] and Sr₂LiReO₆ polymorphs are on or near hull [CS2, CS3]. TS gives moderate support because several thermodynamic analogues lie in the 0.050–0.086 eV/atom range and are experimentally reported [TS1–TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is high because SS includes both on-hull Na₃OsO₅ [SS2] and higher-energy structural analogues [SS1, SS3–SS5], so structural similarity alone is not a guarantee of stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is penalized by vacuum sealing and multistep processing because Ca₂ScOsO₆ required evacuated fused silica tubes and a second PbO₂ oxidation step [CS1]. Toxic/oxidizing precursor risk is supported by OsO₂/PbO₂ use [CS1] and by sodium osmate air-sensitivity/OsO₄ concern [SS2]. The temperature is not extreme because CS1 used 900–950 °C, below the >1000 °C threshold. Insufficient evidence from TS for Os-specific sealed-tube severity, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetic score is low because OsO₂ and carbonate/oxide precursors generally require high-temperature diffusion, and CS1 used repeated solid-state processing [CS1]. CS2/CS3 show that Li-containing 5d oxides can form at 600–800 °C, but they use Re and Sr rather than Na–Os [CS2, CS3]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for Li incorporation in this route and is neutral for Li–Os ordering. The target’s triclinic P1 ordering likely requires substantial cation/anion rearrangement by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 benefits from low temperature because Na₃OsO₅ formed at 400 °C under O₂ [SS2]. It is penalized for controlled oxygen atmosphere and toxic/air-sensitive reagents because SS2 explicitly reports oxygen flow, hygroscopic behavior, and volatile OsO₄ risk [SS2]. Multistep handling is inferred from grinding, crucible loading, heating, and possible reannealing by [Reasonable Inference], consistent with repeated ceramic treatment in CS2/CS3. TS gives insufficient evidence for peroxide sodium osmate conditions, so TS is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The peroxide route has better precursor activity because Na₂O₂ reacted with Os powder to form a Na–Os oxide [SS2]. Kinetics remain limited by Os transport and by the need to introduce Li into a Na–Os framework; insufficient evidence from SS for Na–Li–Os–O quaternary ordering. CS2/CS3 support Li-containing 5d oxide formation but not peroxide chemistry. The 5% Na₅OsO₆ impurity in SS2 indicates competing sodium osmate phases may nucleate readily, reducing the kinetic/phase-selectivity score.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is equipment-intensive, but the requested F2 rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa; CS5 uses 20 kbar and TS3 uses 1.5 GPa, both below that cutoff [CS5, TS3]. It is still penalized for sealed oxidizing capsule handling, multistep recovery, and toxic/air-sensitive oxidizing precursors by [Reasonable Inference] based on KO₂/Au capsule use in CS5. CS5 and TS3 both show high-pressure sealed capsule methods at 900–1000 °C [CS5, TS3]. SS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High-pressure oxidizing alkali flux should improve reactivity because K₃NaRe₂O₉ formed from Re metal and Na-containing KO₂ in only 60 min at 1000 °C [CS5]. TS3 also shows alkali tungstate-related crystallization under 1.5 GPa and 900 °C [TS3]. However, TS3 describes an unintended byproduct with multiple phases, and CS5 reports hygroscopic crystals in a mixed product matrix [CS5, TS3]. Thus, high-pressure flux helps kinetics but does not guarantee phase selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Route 4 uses routine ceramic equipment analogous to Sr₂LiReO₆ preparation [CS2, CS3] and Na titanate firing [CS4]. It is penalized for controlled O₂ atmosphere by [Reasonable Inference] from Na₃OsO₅ requiring oxygen flow [SS2], and for multistep regrinding because CS2/CS3 used sequential firings with intermediate regrinding. Toxic Os chemistry is a persistent penalty supported by Os-containing CS1 and SS2. TS gives insufficient evidence for carbonate ceramic Na–Li–Os synthesis and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

The carbonate/oxide route has the weakest kinetics because carbonates and OsO₂ require decomposition and solid-state diffusion [CS1–CS4]. Sr₂LiReO₆ forms cleanly by ceramic processing [CS2, CS3], but Re chemistry does not ensure Os oxidation-state compatibility. Na₂Eu₂Ti₃O₁₀ requires excess Na₂CO₃ and high temperatures up to 1000–1100 °C, showing alkali stoichiometry control is nontrivial [CS4]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports direct carbonate synthesis of the target.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization/structural-disorder trigger is the large cation size/coordination contrast between Na, Li, and Os, which is evident from the target’s NaO₆, LiO₄, and OsO₆ environments and by comparison with Li/Re and Na/Os analogues [CS2, CS3, SS2]. The target description shows corner-sharing LiO₄–OsO₆ connectivity rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, so C3 is not triggered. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target primitive cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral. Overall, the risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂LiOsO₆ is likely a **near-threshold metastable phase** rather than a clearly robust ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS provides good support for Os oxide formation under controlled oxidation [CS1] and Li-containing 5d oxides by ceramic synthesis [CS2, CS3], while SS provides the most direct Na–Os–O precedent through Na₃OsO₅ [SS2]. TS supports the idea that phases near this E_hull can be experimentally isolated, but many TS examples require hydrothermal or high-pressure routes and often show phase competition [TS1–TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is **high oxygen chemical potential in an alkali-rich medium**, supported by PbO₂ oxidation in CS1, Na₂O₂/O₂ in SS2, and KO₂ high-pressure flux in CS5. The most critical limiting factor is **phase/oxidation-state control for Os in the presence of volatile alkalis**, because SS2 reports persistent impurity and air sensitivity, and CS4 shows Na excess is needed for sodium oxide synthesis.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Os toxicity and possible OsO₄ formation under oxidizing conditions | High | Use sealed or well-contained systems, dry/inert transfer, and exhaust trapping; OsO₄ concern is supported by Na₃OsO₅ air-sensitivity [SS2]. |
| 2 | All | Alkali volatility and Na/Li stoichiometry drift | High | Add controlled Na excess; Na₂Eu₂Ti₃O₁₀ used 30 mol% excess Na₂CO₃ [CS4]. |
| 3 | All | Competing sodium osmate phases | High | Screen Na:Li:Os ratio and oxygen fugacity; Na₃OsO₅ synthesis retained ~5% Na₅OsO₆ impurity despite optimization [SS2]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Over- or under-oxidation during sealed-tube post-oxidation | High | Use two-step oxidation with PbO₂ as in Ca₂ScOsO₆ [CS1], and vary PbO₂ amount/temperature. |
| 5 | Route 1 | Silica ampoule compatibility with alkali oxides/peroxides | Medium | Place pellet in alumina or Au liner inside ampoule; alumina crucible was used inside evacuated silica tube for CS1. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Peroxide reactivity and moisture sensitivity | High | Handle Na₂O₂/Li₂O₂ in dry glovebox; Na₂O₂-based Na₃OsO₅ route used controlled conditions and gold crucibles [SS2]. |
| 7 | Route 2 | Low-temperature synthesis may not provide enough Li/Na ordering | Medium | Add a second anneal/regrind cycle; intermediate regrinding improved ceramic formation in Sr₂LiReO₆ [CS2, CS3]. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Specialized high-pressure apparatus availability | High | Use 1.5–2 GPa range rather than >5 GPa; similar alkali oxide phases were made at 20 kbar [CS5] and 1.5 GPa [TS3]. |
| 9 | Route 3 | Mixed product matrix and crystal recovery difficulty | Medium–High | Use Au capsule and adjust flux ratio; CS5 used Au capsule but still produced mixed black/colorless matrix, so flux screening is needed [CS5]. |
| 10 | Route 4 | Slow solid-state diffusion of refractory Os source | Medium–High | Use fine OsO₂, repeated grinding, and staged heating; repeated ceramic heating/regrinding was used for Sr₂LiReO₆ [CS2, CS3]. |
| 11 | Route 4 | Air or O₂ may not stabilize the required Os oxidation state | High | Add internal oxidizer such as Na₂O₂ or PbO₂; controlled PbO₂ oxidation is demonstrated for Os oxide CS1, and Na₂O₂/O₂ for sodium osmate SS2. |
| 12 | All | Crystal growth / phase identification difficulty in low-symmetry P1 material | Medium | Use slow cooling and single-crystal screening; structurally similar low-symmetry compounds were often isolated by crystal growth/evaporation/hydrothermal methods [SS1, SS4, SS5], but insufficient evidence from SS for direct target crystal growth. |
