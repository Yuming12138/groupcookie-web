### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has `E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom`, which lies at the favorable threshold in the required rubric, so F1 is high but not maximal. TS analogues with similar low hull values, including `0.0508–0.078 eV/atom`, are experimentally accessible by hydrothermal or solution routes (TS1, TS4, TS5). CS provides an exact-composition N2SiF6 analogue, proving chemical accessibility, but it is a different cubic polymorph and has a different listed energetic profile (CS1/SS4). Remaining uncertainty is whether the monoclinic C2/m polymorph is the experimentally preferred low-temperature phase or a metastable polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS1/SS4 report exact N2SiF6 crystallization by spontaneous room-temperature evaporation. There is no high temperature, pressure, or controlled atmosphere in CS1/SS4. The main severity penalty is HF toxicity/corrosivity, which follows directly from the HF-containing synthesis in CS1/SS4 and standard chemical handling knowledge [Reasonable Inference]. Polymorph control is not counted in F2 but remains the practical uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the process is solution-mediated and exact-composition N2SiF6 crystallizes without solid-state diffusion (CS1/SS4). Isolated SiF6 clusters are already the relevant structural motif in both the exact analogue and the target, reducing reconstruction barriers (CS1/SS4 vs target). The main kinetic uncertainty is rearrangement from the known cubic polymorph to the target monoclinic polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is also mild because CS2 shows room-temperature crystallization by acetone diffusion with only vial/desiccator/beaker equipment. It avoids heating, pressure, and reactive atmospheres. A toxicity deduction remains because ammonium fluorosilicate/fluoride chemistry involves toxic fluoride salts [Reasonable Inference], although CS2 itself uses preformed `(NH4)2GeF6` rather than HF. Remaining uncertainty is whether acetone diffusion favors the desired monoclinic Si phase or the cubic analogue.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reaction kinetics should be fast because CS2 produced nearly millimeter-sized N2GeF6 crystals overnight from saturated aqueous solution. The Si analogue should have similarly mobile hexafluorometallate/ammonium species in solution [Reasonable Inference from CS2 and CS1]. The uncertainty is chemical transfer from GeF6 to SiF6 and polymorph specificity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because SS5 uses a Teflon-lined autoclave with 48% HF and 35% NH3(aq) at 220 °C for three days. TS3 and TS4 show hydrothermal crystallization at 70–200 °C, so the temperature range is moderate, not extreme. Deductions arise from toxic volatile HF/NH3, sealed-vessel operation, and multi-step crystallization/cooling. There is insufficient evidence from CS that hydrothermal conditions are necessary for N2SiF6; CS instead supports simpler evaporation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics remain good because hydrothermal HF/NH3 media dissolve and transport fluorometallate species effectively, as shown by SS5. TS3/TS4 support that low-temperature hydrothermal crystal growth can yield ordered phases over days. However, hydrothermal conditions may shift speciation, hydrolysis, or polymorph selection relative to the exact CS1/SS4 evaporation route [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is strong but below the solution evaporation/diffusion routes.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is the most severe among the proposed routes because CS3/CS4 require flowing dry NH3, sodium drying, stepwise heating to 280–310 °C, and specialized Monel equipment. The temperature itself is modest, but reactive-gas handling and multi-step procedure lower the score. There is insufficient evidence from TS for dry ammonolysis of this Si target, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 4

CS3/CS4 show that ammonium germanium fluorides can react under NH3 at moderate temperature, so kinetics are not impossible. However, those same references form GeF5-type products rather than simply preserving GeF6 octahedra, suggesting structural/speciation uncertainty for retaining SiF6 in N2SiF6. Therefore the route is plausible for exploratory polymorph screening but not preferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is zero-dimensional and cluster-based, which generally reduces extended-framework disorder and is consistent with crystallization of similar isolated fluorometallates in CS1, CS2, and SS4. The atom count is below the >40 threshold from the target structural description, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network is described. One risk trigger is assigned for highly dissimilar Si and N coordination environments [Reasonable Inference from target structure], producing a low–medium amorphization risk. TS gives insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph or near-ground-state polymorph because its `E_hull` is exactly `0.0500 eV/atom`, while an exact-composition cubic N2SiF6 analogue is already known experimentally by evaporation (CS1/SS4). The CS evidence is very strong for chemical synthesis of N2SiF6, while SS supports similar zero-dimensional fluorometallate motifs and TS supports accessibility of low-positive-hull phases by solution/hydrothermal methods. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of solution-stable isolated `[SiF6]2−` cluster chemistry that crystallizes under room-temperature evaporation (CS1/SS4). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: the known exact-composition route gives cubic Fm-3m N2SiF6, not the target monoclinic C2/m form (CS1/SS4). Therefore, the best strategy is not high-temperature synthesis but careful low-temperature crystallization screening with HF concentration, supersaturation, antisolvent diffusion, and seeding control.
