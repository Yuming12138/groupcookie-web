### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull = 0.027 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS analogues include stable ACr₅S₈ phases with E_hull = 0 [CS1, CS2], and TS analogues near this stability range include TlV₃Cr₂S₈ at 0.0368 eV/atom [TS2] and MnS at 0.0119 eV/atom [TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially for complex Sr–Mn sulfide stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is penalized for evacuated sealed-tube handling because sealed ampoules are used in SS2, SS4, TS2, and CS4. It is also penalized for volatile sulfur and likely repeated regrinding/reheating, since CS4 and TS1 explicitly describe recycling until single phase. It avoids H₂S toxicity and >1000 °C if kept near 850–950 °C, but Sr/SrS handling remains air/moisture sensitive [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Solid-state diffusion from elements or binaries is moderate-to-slow; CS4 and TS1 report several-day firings and repeated cycling. The kinetics are helped by the fact that MnS has MnS₄ tetrahedra with Mn–S = 2.14 Å [SS1, TS4], very close to the target Mn–S distances. Remaining uncertainty is whether Sr incorporation into the Mn sulfide framework is fast enough without a flux or gas-phase sulfurizing agent [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is penalized for high temperature and dry H₂S because CS1/CS2 use ~1270–1370 K in a dry H₂S stream. It avoids sealed sulfur overpressure and can be a one-step gas-solid reaction, as CS1/CS2 describe 12 h direct synthesis. The main severity is H₂S toxicity/corrosivity and the need for dry, controlled gas handling [CS1, CS2].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
H₂S provides high sulfur activity and can convert carbonate/metal mixtures into complex sulfides in 12 h [CS1, CS2], which supports better kinetics than purely sealed diffusion. The route is not directly proven for Sr–Mn–S; insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically supports H₂S synthesis, so SS/TS are neutral for this route. The likely kinetic risk is incomplete conversion of Sr carbonate/oxide or formation of stable binaries such as SrS and MnS [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 avoids the highest temperatures because TS5 uses 700 °C CVT and SS5 uses iodine to improve crystallization. It is penalized for sealed-tube/vacuum conditions, volatile I₂/S, and multi-step pre-reaction plus crystal growth [TS5, SS5]. I₂ handling adds toxicity/corrosion concerns [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Iodine can improve transport and crystallization, as shown by TS5 and SS5. However, TS5 reports only a few single crystals and notes sample-quality/stoichiometry challenges, so the method may be better for crystal growth than bulk phase discovery. Insufficient evidence from CS for iodine transport of Sr–Mn sulfides, so CS is neutral here.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size contrast between 9-coordinate Sr and tetrahedral Mn, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The target Mn network is described as corner-sharing MnS₄ tetrahedra, so the mixed edge/corner/face connectivity check is not triggered, unlike many CS/SS/TS octahedral analogues [CS1, SS3, TS1]. Unit-cell atom count is not provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target Z, so that check is treated neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrMn₄S₇ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom. The CS data strongly support high-temperature sulfide formation under H₂S [CS1, CS2], while SS/TS data support sealed-tube synthesis of structurally and thermodynamically related chalcogenides [SS2, SS4, TS2, TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur chemical potential, best achieved with dry H₂S or sealed excess sulfur [CS1, CS2; SS2, TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and cation ordering in a complex Sr–Mn–S composition, since several analogues require repeated cycling or show phase-purity problems [CS4, TS1, TS5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful stoichiometry, sulfur activity control, and iterative annealing.
