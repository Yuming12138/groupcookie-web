### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.041 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 score range and suggesting it is near-hull rather than highly unstable. Chemically and structurally related iodides such as TlPbI3 and PbI2 are also very near the hull (CS-1, CS-5; SS-2, SS-4), while thermodynamic analogues cluster around 0.027–0.031 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5), supporting the general feasibility of low-energy heavy-metal iodides. However, none of CS/SS/TS directly confirms AuPbI3 as a ground-state compound, so direct phase identity evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutrally.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: melt/Bridgman

Condition severity is moderate-high because TlPbI3 requires evacuated quartz ampoules, controlled heating/cooling, and long annealing (CS-1; SS-2), and the target route also requires volatile iodide retention [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable in the molten iodide state, consistent with melt growth of TlPbI3 (CS-1/SS-2), but forming ordered AuAuI3 pyramids in a PbI6 sheet is more demanding than simply crystallizing PbI2. Remaining uncertainty is AuI stability and whether AuPbI3 melts congruently; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on AuI–PbI2 phase behavior.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: low-temperature sealed annealing

This route has milder temperatures, consistent with 200 °C TlGaI4 synthesis (CS-4), 250–350 °C TlFeI3/TlMnI3 annealing (CS-3), and 558 K BiI formation (TS-2/TS-3). Kinetics are acceptable because iodides are soft and reactive, but solid-state ordering may be slow, as shown by repeated annealing needed for TlFeI3 purity (CS-3). The major uncertainty is whether low-temperature diffusion is sufficient to form the target layered Au–Pb ordering rather than a mixture of AuI and PbI2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: hydrothermal HI growth

Hydrothermal severity is comparatively low because TS-1 forms AgTl2I3 in 25% HI at 155–175 °C, with only autogenous pressure. Kinetically, HI provides solution transport and high iodide activity, which should help overcome diffusion limitations; PbI2 solution growth is also well established in CS-5/SS-4. The uncertainty is Au(I) speciation/redox stability in acidic iodide solution; insufficient direct Au hydrothermal evidence from CS/SS/TS means this route receives an expert penalty despite the best numerical score.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: iodine-assisted CVT

CVT uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed-tube equipment, supported by SS-1 and SS-3 for structurally similar iodide crystals. Kinetics can be good because vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion, but composition control is likely narrow for a ternary Au–Pb iodide [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is chemical transferability, because SS-1/SS-3 involve Al/Ga/Te iodide clusters rather than PbI6 layered frameworks.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines Au-centered trigonal pyramids, Au–Au contacts, and PbI6 octahedra, with both corner- and edge-sharing connectivity in the same 2D sheet; this raises structural-disorder risk relative to simple PbI2 sheets. PbI2 analogues show multiple polytypes and phase-control issues (CS-5; SS-4), and BiI thermodynamic analogues show multiple modifications and metastability (TS-2, TS-3), supporting concern about competing ordered variants. The unit-cell size trigger is not supported by the target description, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the exact AuPbI3 cell size, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuPbI3 is likely a **near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase**, not a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.041 eV/atom. The CS and SS data support synthesis of related layered iodides, especially TlPbI3 and PbI2, while TS data supports low-temperature hydrothermal access to complex iodides near similar thermodynamic stability ranges. The most critical enabling factor is the softness and high mobility of iodide lattices, allowing synthesis at moderate temperatures or through solution/vapor transport rather than refractory solid-state conditions. The most critical limiting factor is control of Au(I) chemistry, especially avoiding Au metal formation, iodine loss, or separation into AuI + PbI2. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance under iodide-rich sealed hydrothermal or low-temperature sealed-ampoule conditions.
