### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0390 eV/atom, which is below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore receives a high score of 95. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, including CrRh₂Se₄, FeRh₂Se₄, and NiRh₂Se₄ near 0.042–0.045 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized by direct solid-state methods (TS1–TS3). Structurally similar PtSe₂ and Tl₂Pt₄Se₆ are calculated at E_hull = 0 and are experimentally accessible, supporting the layered selenide framework (SS1; SS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is quaternary and triclinic P1, so a low hull energy does not guarantee cation ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1, direct solid-state

The severity score is reduced because sealed evacuated ampoules are required for volatile Se, as in many selenide analogues (CS4; CS5; SS4; TS1–TS5). TS2/TS3 show that related transition-metal selenides often need multiple firings, grinding, pelletizing, and slow cooling, so the route is not a simple one-step reaction. No high pressure or >1400 °C condition is indicated by CS/SS/TS, which limits the severity penalty. Uncertainty remains over whether Ir requires temperatures above 1000 °C for full reaction.

#### F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state

Elemental Ir, Pd, and Ni powders are relatively low-activity precursors, and TS1–TS3 show that dense transition-metal selenides often require high temperatures plus intermediate grinding. The target’s edge-sharing octahedral topology is favorable because SS1 PtSe₂ and TS Rh selenides contain related layered/edge-sharing motifs. However, the quaternary Ir/Pd/Ni ordering in P1 likely has slow kinetics, so the route receives a relatively low kinetics score. CS provides only partial support here: CS4/CS5 show elemental selenide reactions, but not Ir–Pd–Ni ordering.

#### F2 — Route 2, CVT

CVT requires vacuum, sealed tubes, volatile/corrosive Br₂, and Se management, giving the same base severity penalties as direct sealed-tube synthesis. CS2 and SS5 explicitly use Br₂ in evacuated silica tubes for layered Pd selenides, so the route is experimentally grounded. Temperatures are moderate, around 600–750 °C or 900–1000 K in analogues, so no high-temperature penalty above 1000 °C is applied (CS2; SS5). The main safety uncertainty is controlling Br₂ pressure and avoiding ampoule corrosion.

#### F3 — Route 2, CVT

CVT receives the best kinetics score because Br₂-assisted transport can overcome slow solid-state diffusion, as shown by crystal growth of Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂ and Nb₂Pd₃Se₈ (CS2; SS5). The layered selenide motif is structurally compatible with the target’s two-dimensional octahedral sheets (SS1; SS5). The remaining uncertainty is that Ir halide/selenide transport is not directly demonstrated in the supplied sources, so Ir incorporation may lag Pd/Ni [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives indirect support because similar near-hull transition-metal selenides can be synthesized, but not specifically by CVT.

#### F2 — Route 3, flux

The flux route uses lower temperatures than the sealed solid-state and CVT routes, supported by CS3 at 310 °C for AuCuSe₄. The severity score is still reduced because K₂Se/K₂Seₓ requires inert handling, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and flux removal with solvent, all reported in CS3. CS3 also indicates specialized glovebox handling and post-synthesis washing, making this more complex than a simple anneal. SS and TS provide insufficient direct flux evidence for Ir/Pd/Ni selenides; neutral source signal = 50 for flux-specific feasibility from SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3, flux

Flux chemistry can improve precursor activity and cation mobility, and CS3 demonstrates that metal selenides can form from K₂Se/Se flux at relatively low temperature. However, CS3 also reports mixed products and difficulty obtaining pure AuCuSe₄, so phase control is a serious kinetic/thermodynamic concern. For Ir-containing material, higher temperature than CS3 is probably needed because Ir is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. TS supports that near-hull selenides are accessible, but does not directly validate alkali flux for this composition.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s local structure consists of edge-sharing octahedra and does not show mixed edge/vertex/face connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk; PtSe₂ provides a structurally similar edge-sharing octahedral sheet motif (SS1). The target formula contains only 12 atoms per formula unit, and the described M–Se bond lengths are not wildly mismatched, so the three specified amorphization triggers are not activated. CS and TS do not provide direct amorphization data, so they are treated as neutral evidence for F4. The remaining uncertainty is that disorder among Ir/Pd/Ni sites could broaden diffraction peaks or produce stacking faults rather than a fully amorphous product [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ir₂PdNiSe₈ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0390 eV/atom, but this is well inside the experimentally accessible range shown by TS1–TS3. The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: SS1 supports the omega-derived edge-sharing layered framework, CS2/SS5 support CVT growth of layered Pd selenides, and TS1–TS5 show that near-hull transition-metal selenides can be made by sealed-tube reactions. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural compatibility with layered edge-sharing metal–selenium octahedral networks, especially PtSe₂-like motifs (SS1). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control of four metal/chalcogen components, especially incorporation and ordering of refractory Ir. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with CVT or transport-assisted annealing the most credible first route.
