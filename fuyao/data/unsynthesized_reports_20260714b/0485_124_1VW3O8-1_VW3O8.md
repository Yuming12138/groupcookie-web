### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.045 eV/atom places VW3O8 just inside the high-synthesizability thermodynamic window, so F1 = 92. Chemically and thermodynamically similar oxides with comparable hull energies were experimentally synthesized, including U–W oxide at 0.033 eV/atom (CS1), Rb–W oxide at 0.017 eV/atom (CS3), and Mg2Mo3O8 at 0.046 eV/atom (TS4). The main uncertainty is that the target is a V–W ternary oxide not directly represented in CS/SS/TS, so competing binary V/W oxides may still dominate **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because vacuum sealed ampoules are required, as in CS1, CS3, SS1/TS2, and TS4. The route uses ordinary furnaces and ampoules, but W/WO3 redox mixtures and volatile oxides require careful sealing **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are the largest limitation because W metal is refractory and because the target layered structure must form from binary oxides/metals **[Reasonable Inference]**; long holds in CS3, SS5, and TS4 support this concern. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct V–W solid-state synthesis, so TS is supportive only by analogy to reduced V/Mo oxides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux/self-flux

Flux growth has lower kinetic severity than dry solid-state synthesis because molten tungstate/oxide flux can enhance mass transport **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS5 and TS4 support sealed-tube flux/oxide approaches and post-synthesis washing for reduced Mo oxides, but CS gives insufficient direct flux evidence for W–V oxides and is treated neutrally for this route. The main risk is alkali incorporation or oxidation-state drift if K2WO4/Na2WO4 or V2O5-rich fluxes are used **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high pressure

High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for unusual W/V oxide frameworks by CS2 and TS5, but it has severe equipment and condition penalties. F2 is low because 3–6 GPa pressure, controlled capsule chemistry, and rapid quenching are needed **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics may be acceptable for small pellets, as TS5 used 30 min treatments, but W-containing reactions remain diffusion-limited **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS for high-pressure preparation, so SS is neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT/vapor-assisted growth

CVT gets the best overall score because vapor transport can bypass slow diffusion in refractory W-containing solids **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS4 directly demonstrates single-crystal growth of a complex reduced oxide by halide transport. F2 is limited by the need for reactive halogens, sealed ampoules, and long temperature-gradient runs, all consistent with CS4. F3 is moderate-to-good because pre-reacted source material can reduce structural reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the lack of direct V–W–O CVT evidence creates uncertainty; SS and TS provide insufficient direct CVT signal and are neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s V–O and W–O distances are very similar, suggesting low cation-size mismatch and a relatively coherent mixed-cation oxide network **[Reasonable Inference from target structure]**. The formula unit is small, and the provided Pmm2 description does not indicate a large complex unit cell. Because mixed vertex/edge/face sharing is not explicitly established for the target, no amorphization trigger is assigned; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence and are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VW3O8 is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.045 eV/atom rather than zero. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of nearby reduced W/V/Mo oxides under vacuum, sealed-tube, flux, high-pressure, and CVT conditions, but no direct VW3O8 analogue is provided. The most critical enabling factor is tight oxygen chemical potential control using WO3/W/VO2 buffering in sealed conditions, supported by W/WO3 syntheses in CS1 and CS3. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase-selectivity competition from stable binary oxides such as VO2/WO2/WO3-type phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with CVT or flux-assisted sealed-tube synthesis preferred over simple dry sintering.
