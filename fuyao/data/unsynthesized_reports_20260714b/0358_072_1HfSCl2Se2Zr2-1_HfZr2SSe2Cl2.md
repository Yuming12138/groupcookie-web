### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0120 eV/atom, which is strongly favorable and in the 90–100 scoring range. TS-3 is nearly identical thermodynamically at E_hull = 0.0121 eV/atom and is reported as synthesized by direct reaction, while TS-1 at E_hull = 0.026 eV/atom is also synthesized, supporting near-hull metastable accessibility. CS includes several E_hull = 0 sulfide/chloride analogues, supporting chemical stability, but SS includes one structurally similar Zr3S4 at 0.2875 eV/atom, so the structural set is more mixed. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee correct Hf/Zr ordering or S/Se/Cl site ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5 use evacuated sealed silica vessels at about 850 °C for 7 d, which is not extreme but requires controlled atmosphere. Volatile S/Se/Cl species require sealing, consistent with SS-1 and SS-5 chalcogen ampoule synthesis. Toxic/moisture-sensitive halides add handling difficulty by [Reasonable Inference] from chloride precursor use in CS and TS-2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact HfCl4/ZrCl4 behavior in this quinary system, so some risk remains.

#### F3 — Route 1

Flux improves kinetics because CS-1 and CS-4 explicitly state that NaCl or excess metal chloride secures complete/fast reaction and single crystals. However, Hf and Zr are refractory cations, and SS-4/TS-1 shows high-temperature treatment is needed for Zr chalcogenide/telluride reactions, so cation diffusion remains limiting. Structural reconstruction is substantial because none of CS/SS/TS provides the exact HfZr2Se2SCl2 structure. The long 7 d anneals in CS-1/CS-4/CS-5 support a moderate kinetic penalty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions by following SS-5, where Zr–S–Se CVT uses an 870→770 °C gradient. It still requires an evacuated ampoule and volatile/corrosive transport agents, as shown by SS-5 iodine CVT and TS-2 gas transport. Multi-step pre-reaction plus transport is likely needed by [Reasonable Inference] to prevent composition drift. Insufficient evidence from CS for CVT specifically makes the chemical-similarity signal neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT receives the best kinetics score because transport through volatile species reduces the need for long-range solid diffusion, and SS-5 directly demonstrates Zr–S–Se mixed crystal transport. TS-2 supports crystallization of a layered metal chloride sheet by gas transport, which is structurally relevant to the target’s 2D chloride coordination. The main kinetic uncertainty is compositional fractionation: SS-5 reports composition changes for ZrSe2-rich materials, so maintaining Hf:Zr:S:Se:Cl stoichiometry may be difficult. Hf transport chemistry is inferred rather than demonstrated directly.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because SS-4/TS-1 uses 1570 K high-temperature sealed Ta-tube conditions for ZrTe, and TS-4 uses sealed Ta ampoules for halide-containing phases. Although not high-pressure, this route requires specialized containment, inert handling, and volatile component management. High temperature increases the risk of losing S/Se/Cl or forming competing binaries. CS-3 warns that some layered selenide compounds have restricted thermal stability, supporting caution for high-temperature treatment.

#### F3 — Route 3

High temperature improves reaction completion but does not eliminate slow Hf/Zr ordering because both are refractory cations. SS-4/TS-1 and TS-4 show multi-day anneals are needed for related high-temperature halide/chalcogenide chemistry. The target’s 2D mixed-anion structure likely requires substantial reconstruction from elemental/binary precursors. The major uncertainty is whether the phase remains stable at 1000–1200 °C; CS-3 demonstrates that layered selenide analogues can have narrow temperature windows.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target uses Hf and Zr, which are closely size-matched in octahedral coordination by [Reasonable Inference], so cation-size mismatch should not drive amorphization. The described polyhedral network contains corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so it does not meet the full mixed-connectivity trigger; TS-3 has face-sharing, but that is an analogue rather than the target. The formula is compact, although the exact conventional unit cell atom count is not specified; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact cell size is treated neutrally. Overall amorphization risk is low, but anion disorder between S/Se/Cl remains a possible crystallinity issue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfZr2Se2SCl2 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0120 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues in TS-1 and TS-3. The analogue support is reasonably strong for synthesis method selection: CS supports halide/chalcogen flux reactions, SS supports Zr–S–Se vapor transport, and TS supports layered metal chloride crystal growth. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or fluxing, because CS-1/CS-4 and SS-5 show that halides improve reaction completion and crystal growth. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining quinary stoichiometry, especially S/Se/Cl volatility and Hf/Zr cation ordering. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with CVT or halide-flux ampoule synthesis preferred over very high-temperature bulk reaction.
