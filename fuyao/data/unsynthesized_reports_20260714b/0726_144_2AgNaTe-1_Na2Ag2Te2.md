### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

NaAgTe is predicted at only 0.0010 eV/atom above hull, so it is effectively on the convex hull by the provided target criterion. Chemically similar KNaTe, NaLiTe, and NaAuTe are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting stable alkali telluride chemistry (CS-1 / CS-3 / CS-4). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Ag chalcogenides also report E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1–SS-5 / TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is polymorphism rather than decomposition, because the closest NaAuTe analogue is not P4/nmm (SS-2 / TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 1 elemental sealed ampoule

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 600 °C, directly supported by NaAuTe synthesis at 600 °C in sealed ampoules (CS-3 / SS-2 / TS-2). The main severity deductions are sealed volatile Na/Te handling, air/moisture sensitivity, and multi-step ampoule processing, all consistent with alkali chalcogenide protocols (CS-3 / SS-2 / TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether elemental NaAgTe specifically needs repeated annealing, so that detail is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 elemental sealed ampoule

Elemental Ag is less reactive than preformed tellurides, but Ag/chalcogen reactions are demonstrated in structurally similar systems (SS-1 / SS-3 / TS-1 / TS-3). NaAuTe forms by fusion at 600 °C, suggesting cation diffusion can be adequate despite Ag not melting (CS-3 / SS-2 / TS-2). The kinetic uncertainty is structural: NaAuTe is hexagonal P6₃/mmc, not target P4/nmm, so nucleating the target polymorph may need longer annealing or slow cooling (SS-2 / TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2 binary telluride reaction

Route 2 inherits relatively mild 673–773 K conditions from binary telluride syntheses of NaLiTe/KNaTe (CS-1 / CS-4). The severity is moderate because the route still requires sealed ampoules, Ar handling, and air-sensitive Na₂Te-type precursors (CS-1 / CS-4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for Na₂Te + Ag₂Te specifically, so the Ag₂Te precursor choice is a [Reasonable Inference] informed by Ag–Te analogue chemistry (SS-1 / TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 2 binary telluride reaction

Preformed Na–Te and Ag–Te bonds should reduce the kinetic burden versus reacting elemental Ag with Te [Reasonable Inference]. CS evidence shows binary tellurides can form ternary alkali tellurides at only 400–500 °C over 2–7 days (CS-1 / CS-4). The remaining uncertainty is whether the P4/nmm ordering is the accessible annealed phase or whether competing Na–Ag–Te polymorphs form; insufficient direct structural evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for this exact stoichiometry.

#### F2 — Route 3 flux/carbonate fusion

Route 3 is based on Ag selenide flux/fusion analogues at 1000 °C under dry oxygen-free Ar (SS-3–SS-5 / TS-3–TS-5). Its penalties arise from high-temperature Na/Te volatility in the telluride adaptation, multi-step flux cooling/washing, and air-sensitive products, which are reported for related Ag chalcogenides (SS-4 / SS-5; TS-4 / TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS exists for carbonate-flux telluride NaAgTe, so the telluride flux adaptation is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 flux/carbonate fusion

Flux chemistry improves ion transport and can grow crystals of Ag chalcogenides by slow cooling (SS-3–SS-5 / TS-3–TS-5). The kinetic score is high for diffusion but lower for structural selectivity because the reported flux products are Ag-rich selenides, not 1:1:1 NaAgTe (SS-3–SS-5). The main uncertainty is whether a Na–Te flux stabilizes the target stoichiometry or instead favors Ag-rich tellurides analogous to RbAg₃Te₂ (SS-1 / TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 4 supercritical ethylenediamine

Route 4 benefits from low temperature, 300 °C, in the RbAg₃Te₂ analogue (SS-1 / TS-1). Its severity is high because it requires sealed quartz tubes, evacuation, supercritical ethylenediamine, autoclave operation, and 2400 psi Ar counterpressure (SS-1 / TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS exists for solvothermal NaAgTe, so transferability is mainly from SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 4 supercritical ethylenediamine

Supercritical ethylenediamine provides high precursor activity and bypasses solid-state diffusion, as shown by RbAg₃Te₂ formation at 300 °C (SS-1 / TS-1). However, the reported RbAg₃Te₂ route had yield/side-product issues and produces an Ag-rich monoclinic phase, which lowers structural selectivity for NaAgTe P4/nmm (SS-1 / TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether Na polytelluride chemistry will direct a 1:1:1 compound rather than soluble or Ag-rich products.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaAgTe is likely synthesizable as a near-ground-state phase because its predicted E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically related alkali/coinage-metal chalcogenides are reported on the hull (CS-1 / CS-3 / CS-4; SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). The analogue support is strong for Na–Te and Ag–Te chemistry, but not exact for the P4/nmm NaAgTe structure because the closest NaAuTe analogue is hexagonal and many Ag chalcogenide analogues are monoclinic or Ag-rich (SS-1 / SS-2 / TS-1 / TS-2). The most enabling factor is the availability of low-to-moderate temperature sealed-ampoule telluride chemistry using either elements or binary tellurides (CS-1 / CS-3 / CS-4). The main limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding Ag₂Te, Na₂Te, NaAuTe-type hexagonal analogues, or Ag-rich tellurides [Reasonable Inference from SS-1 / SS-2 / TS-1 / TS-2]. The recommended first attempt is Route 2, because binary tellurides should provide the best balance of low temperature, correct stoichiometry, and preformed bonding (CS-1 / CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).
