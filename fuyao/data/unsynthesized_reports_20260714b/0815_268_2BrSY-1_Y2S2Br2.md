#### Routes considered but not recommended: high-pressure nitride-halide synthesis

High-pressure methods are reported for ZrNBr and ZrNCl using 3–5 GPa, 900–1200 °C, NH₄X fluxes, and multianvil equipment [CS2, CS5]. These references provide insufficient useful signal for YSBr because they concern nitride halides with different chemistry, different structure type, and pressure-stabilized β phases [CS2, CS5]. Therefore, CS2/CS5 are scored neutral, 50, for transferability to YSBr synthesis conditions.

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the most favorable stability bin. The closest DySBr analogue is also E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS4, SS1, TS1], and several structurally related MSI phases, including HoSI, DySI, and GdSI, are also thermodynamically stable in the reference data [SS2, SS3, SS4, TS2, TS3, TS4]. This strongly supports YSBr as a likely ground-state compound rather than a high-energy metastable phase. Remaining uncertainty comes from possible Y-specific competing phases such as Y₂S₃, YBr₃, or mixed Y–S–Br phases not explicitly listed in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Br₂, and hazardous/air-sensitive handling, all directly indicated by the DySBr synthesis using Br₂ in sealed quartz under vacuum with cryogenic bromine condensation [CS4, SS1, TS1]. It is not penalized for high temperature or high pressure because the analogous DySBr route uses only 750 °C and no applied pressure [CS4, SS1, TS1]. CS2/CS5 high-pressure nitride halides contradict this by requiring multianvil pressure, but those are nitride-halide polymorphs and provide insufficient useful signal for YSBr; they are treated neutral, 50, for this factor [CS2, CS5]. The main uncertainty is whether Y requires a slightly different temperature than Dy.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are moderately favorable because the exact structure type forms from a sulfide + metal + bromine route in 5 days for DySBr [CS4, SS1, TS1]. The dense Y metal and Y₂S₃ likely slow diffusion, but volatile bromine can enhance reaction via transport or reactive intermediate formation [CS4, SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is low-risk because the target is nearly identical to DySBr in coordination and bond lengths [CS4, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is the availability/reactivity of Y₂S₃ powder and the degree of surface oxidation on Y metal.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the sealed-ampoule and halogen hazards from elemental MSI syntheses and DySBr bromination chemistry [SS2, TS2; CS4, SS1, TS1]. The temperature is moderate for solid-state synthesis, since related MSI iodides are phase-pure at 900 °C and do not require high pressure [SS2, TS2]. The route is not multi-step, but elemental S and Br₂ introduce vapor-pressure management challenges [SS2, TS2; CS4, SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether direct reaction of Y + S + Br₂ produces YSBr cleanly or instead forms YBr₃/Y₂S₃ mixtures.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental precursors can be reactive, but Y metal is dense and passivated, which lowers the precursor-activity score [Reasonable Inference]. The elemental lanthanoid routes require long high-temperature anneals, e.g., 900 °C for 10 days, indicating that diffusion and ordering are not instantaneous [SS2, TS2]. Still, the success of phase-pure MSI formation across Gd–Lu supports reasonable kinetic accessibility for rare-earth sulfide halides [SS2, TS2]. The largest uncertainty is the stronger volatility/reactivity of Br₂ compared with I₂.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized more heavily because it is not a one-step preparation: it requires preformed YSBr plus a transport experiment [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Iodine transport around 1000 °C is supported for related sulfide materials [CS3], but there is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for YSBr CVT specifically. The route uses volatile halogen transport agents and sealed ampoules, which adds safety and stoichiometry-control challenges [CS3]. The uncertainty is whether the transport chemistry preserves bromide stoichiometry rather than exchanging Br/I.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT has favorable apparent kinetics because mass transport occurs through vapor species rather than slow bulk diffusion; iodine transport produced sizable lath-shaped sulfide crystals in the reference data [CS3]. Starting from preformed YSBr should minimize structural reconstruction, because the route mainly recrystallizes the target phase [Reasonable Inference]. However, the CS3 example is GdPS/YPS-type chemistry, not YSBr, and SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for bromide CVT. Thus the route scores high for kinetics but lower overall due to uncertainty and process complexity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size disorder does not trigger the amorphization criterion; analogous DySBr and HoSI phases are ordered single-cation structures [CS4, SS1, TS1; SS2, TS2]. The structure is a simple layered Pmmn ternary, and there is no indication of a >40-atom unit cell from the close MSX analogues [CS4, SS1, TS1]. Mixed corner/edge sharing is present, but face sharing is not reported for the target or DySBr analogue, so the specified C3 criterion is not triggered [CS4, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is limited because the structural analogue evidence is strong.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YSBr is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest DySBr analogue is also stable and experimentally synthesized as a single-phase crystalline material [CS4, SS1, TS1]. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis unusually well because DySBr is essentially the same Pmmn layered MSBr structure, with nearly identical octahedral connectivity and bond lengths [CS4, SS1, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the proven stability and phase-pure sealed-ampoule formation of the rare-earth sulfide bromide structure type [CS4, SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is bromine handling and stoichiometry control, since Br₂ is volatile/toxic and requires sealed ampoule loading with cryogenic condensation [CS4, SS1, TS1]. Overall, YSBr should be experimentally accessible in a standard solid-state synthesis lab equipped for evacuated halogen ampoule chemistry.
