### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0340 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom range. TS is especially supportive because thermodynamically similar synthesized materials lie near 0.028–0.039 eV/atom, including Li₂CrCl₄ at 0.0394 eV/atom (TS1), Li₆FeCl₈ at 0.0388 eV/atom (TS2), and Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.0287 eV/atom (TS5). SS is also supportive because structurally similar chlorides are often on or near the hull (SS1–SS4). CS is somewhat mixed because Li₂CoCl₄ is higher at 0.097 eV/atom (CS1), but Li₂ZnCl₄ and Li₂CrCl₄ are near the hull (CS2/CS3).

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperature, similar to Li₂CrCl₄ and Li₆CoCl₈ syntheses at 400 °C for 1 week (CS3/TS1/TS3), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main penalties are vacuum sealing and anhydrous handling, both repeatedly required for related chlorides (CS1/CS3/TS3/SS4). Volatile/moisture-sensitive chloride management is a real limitation by analogy to hygroscopic chlorides in SS1 and glovebox requirements in TS2/TS3.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binary chloride precursors are reactive compared with oxides, and multiple Li–M–Cl analogues form at 200–403 °C under sealed conditions (CS2/CS3/TS2/TS3). However, the target’s layered trigonal structure differs from many CS/TS analogues, which are spinel-like, rocksalt-derived, or octahedral networks (CS2/CS3/TS3), so structural reconstruction and cation ordering may be slow. CS2 reports 20 months for Li₂ZnCl₄ at 403 °C, showing that low-temperature chloride ordering can be kinetically sluggish.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 remains below 1000 °C, with SS4 using 650 °C for RbMgCl₃ melt growth and CS1 using molten LiCl/CoCl₂ mixtures in evacuated quartz. The penalties arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoule operation, multi-step crystal growth, and moisture-sensitive chloride precursors, all supported by CS1/SS4/SS5. Equipment is specialized but not exotic; Bridgman furnaces and sealed ampoules are standard in chloride crystal growth (SS4/SS5).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Melt growth gives the best kinetics because ionic diffusion occurs in the liquid, supported by Li₂CoCl₄ melt synthesis (CS1) and RbMgCl₃/KMgCl₃ crystal growth (SS4/SS5). Slow cooling allows ordering into crystalline phases, as seen in CS1 and SS2. The main uncertainty is polymorph competition: BaZnCl₄ has temperature-dependent modifications (SS2), and RbMgCl₃ has multiple structural phases depending on conditions and impurities (SS4).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The self-flux route uses similar sealed chloride-melt conditions to Route 2 but possibly at somewhat lower temperatures. It still requires vacuum/inert handling and sealed ampoules, supported by CS1/CS3/SS4. Direct flux evidence for LiMgCl₃ is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the route is less certain despite reasonable condition severity.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A LiCl–MgCl₂ self-flux should enhance precursor activity and diffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with successful chloride melt routes in CS1 and SS4/SS5. Flux growth may also improve crystallinity and layered ordering. The uncertainty is flux removal and stoichiometric control; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for LiMgCl₃ self-flux separation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only a simple LiMgCl₃ formula and no evidence of a large >40 atom cell, reducing amorphization risk. The main trigger is the difference in Li and Mg coordination preferences, with Li trigonal planar and Mg octahedral in the target structure. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization tendencies for LiMgCl₃; therefore the score is based mainly on the target topology plus analogy to crystallizable chloride salts in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiMgCl₃ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground state, because the target E_hull is 0.0340 eV/atom rather than zero. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of related ternary chlorides by sealed anhydrous solid-state reaction and melt growth, especially Li–M–Cl compounds in CS1–CS3/TS1–TS3 and Mg chloride perovskites in SS4/SS5. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of binary chloride salts, which allows crystallization at moderate temperatures or from melts. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: many related chlorides show polymorphism, slow ordering, or sensitivity to cooling and impurities (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4, TS3). Overall, the material appears reasonably synthesizable, with melt growth or sealed-ampoule annealing as the most defensible routes.
