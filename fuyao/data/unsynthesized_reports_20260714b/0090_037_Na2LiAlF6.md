### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E above hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-score threshold for metastable but accessible phases. CS1 reports chemically identical Na₂LiAlF₆ with E_hull ≈ 0.010 eV/atom, which supports the Na–Li–Al–F composition as thermodynamically plausible, although not necessarily the same triclinic P1 polymorph. TS analogues include fluorides/oxyfluorides with E_hull values around 0.032–0.096 eV/atom that still have reported syntheses, supporting accessibility of small-hull metastable fluorides (TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target P1 phase may compete with the perovskite-derived Na₂LiAlF₆ polymorph in CS1.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it reaches 950 °C and involves melt/cool/sinter processing, but it does not exceed 1000 °C and does not require high pressure according to CS1. CS1 gives no atmosphere requirement, but related fluoride syntheses often use sealed ampoules or inert conditions to avoid hydrolysis/side reactions (CS2, CS5, TS4). The main condition penalty is the likely need to manage AlF₃/fluoride volatility and corrosion [Reasonable Inference] plus the multi-step thermal profile in CS1. SS gives insufficient exact evidence for this Na–Li–Al composition but supports that fluoride frameworks are commonly formed under moderate-to-high-temperature conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because CS1 uses a melt at 1223 K, which should greatly improve ion transport compared with dry diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The same precursor set and final stoichiometry were demonstrated in CS1, so chemical reaction kinetics are credible. The limiting kinetic/structural issue is transformation from or competition with the perovskite-derived octahedral CS1 polymorph versus the target triclinic P1 tetrahedral/lower-coordinate framework. CS4–CS5 show K₂LiAlF₆ polymorphism and temperature-dependent transformations, supporting the need for controlled thermal history.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids full melting and stays in the 650–850 °C range inferred from sealed fluoride analogues (CS2, CS5, TS1). It is penalized for vacuum/Ar sealed ampoule conditions because CS2, CS5, TS1, and TS4 use sealed or inert systems. It is also penalized for multi-step drying/grinding/sealing/heating and for the need to contain volatile/corrosive fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides useful chemical support via CS1, but SS and TS are less specific to the Na–Li–Al–F target.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state diffusion through stable binary fluorides is slower than a melt route, so precursor activity is only moderate [Reasonable Inference from CS1 vs CS2/CS5]. Related fluorides do form by sealed-tube heating at 700–870 °C (CS2, CS5, SS3), so ion diffusion should be manageable. Polymorph control is the main kinetic risk because quenching/annealing affects K₂LiAlF₆ phases (CS5), and the target differs structurally from the exact-composition CS1 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth has no evidence of >1000 °C or high pressure from CS3/CS4, but it usually requires sealed/dry high-temperature fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference from CS2/CS5/TS1]. It is penalized for likely sealed conditions, volatile fluoride containment, and multiple steps including melt, slow cooling, and flux removal. CS3 shows flux can be useful for difficult mixed fluorides, while CS4 shows K₂LiAlF₆ crystals can grow from melt-like conditions. Remaining uncertainty is the appropriate NaF–LiF–AlF₃ flux composition for the target.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux improves kinetics by enabling liquid-phase transport, and CS3 demonstrates flux-grown crystals for a related Na mixed fluoride. CS4 reports crystal growth below 875 °C in a K–Li–Al fluoride melt, supporting the general crystal-growth mechanism. However, CS3 explicitly reports difficulty obtaining pure powder free of competing phases, so structural selection and phase purity are significant risks. For the target, this route is best suited for exploratory single-crystal isolation rather than bulk phase-pure powder [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Hydrothermal conditions are thermally mild, with TS2 and TS5 using 150–225 °C in Teflon-lined Parr vessels. There is no high-pressure penalty under the rubric because these are autogenous hydrothermal pressures, not >5 GPa. The route is penalized for HF toxicity because TS2 explicitly uses aqueous HF, and for multi-step autoclave processing. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence that anhydrous Na₂LiAlF₆ can be made hydrothermally.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution-mediated transport gives good apparent kinetics at low temperature, as TS2/TS5 reactions crystallize fluoride/oxyfluoride phases within 24–72 h. However, the structural pathway is poorly supported because TS2 and TS5 examples contain O/H and are not anhydrous all-fluoride salts. Hydrolysis of Al–F chemistry and formation of oxyfluoroaluminates are plausible risks [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the route is kinetically mild but chemically uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, Li⁺, Na⁺, and Al³⁺, with distinct preferred fluoride coordination environments, so the radius/coordination mismatch trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target P1 cell description implies a small 10-atom formula cell, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not assigned; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a larger target supercell. The target description states corner-sharing connectivity but does not state edge or face sharing, so mixed connectivity is not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target triclinic P1 Na₂LiAlF₆ is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than an unambiguous ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and CS1 reports a chemically identical but structurally different Na₂LiAlF₆ polymorph. The CS data strongly support synthesis of the Na–Li–Al–F composition, especially CS1 using NaF/LiF/AlF₃, while SS and TS mainly support the broader feasibility of mixed fluoride frameworks and sealed high-temperature fluoride chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a directly demonstrated binary-fluoride route to Na₂LiAlF₆ (CS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: the target P1 structure has lower-coordinate LiF₄/AlF₄ units, while the demonstrated CS1 phase is perovskite-derived with LiF₆/AlF₆ octahedra. Containment of fluoride volatility and moisture exclusion are secondary but important processing controls supported by sealed-ampoule analogues (CS2, CS5, TS4).
