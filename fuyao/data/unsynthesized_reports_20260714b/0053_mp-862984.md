### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target sits exactly at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is the boundary of the top scoring bin and supports possible synthesis but not unequivocal ground-state stability [Target]. CS and SS analogues are all on-hull, which strongly supports the general stability of rare-earth/actinide boride chemistry [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. TS includes both low-hull and moderately metastable synthesized borides, so the target is consistent with experimentally accessible metastability [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that none of the references is exactly PmB3 in P6_3/mmc [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

**F2 — Route 1.** Arc melting is supported by CS1 and SS2/SS3/SS5, but it involves high local temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, multiple remelts, long annealing, and quenching [CS1, SS2, SS3, SS5]. The Pm precursor adds toxicity/air-sensitivity beyond the reference chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates volatile elemental precursor loss as a required limitation, so that sub-factor is neutral source-wise [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

**F3 — Route 1.** Arc melting improves kinetic mixing relative to pure sintering, and repeated remelting/long anneals are explicitly used for analogous borides [CS1, SS2, TS2]. However, the reaction still starts from elements and must build a boron network, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether PmB3 nucleates instead of adjacent Pm borides [TS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Flux growth avoids high pressure and can operate near 1075–1200 °C using halide/self-flux by analogy to GdCl3 flux synthesis [CS4]. Al-flux variants are hotter but well precedented for rare-earth borides and hexaborides [CS2, TS3]. The main deductions are temperature above 1000 °C, sealed volatile halide flux, multistep processing, and Pm radiochemical handling [CS2, CS4, TS3, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** Flux strongly improves precursor activity and diffusion because molten Al, molten rare-earth metal, or chloride flux transports B/Pm species [CS2, CS4, CS5, TS3]. Structural selection remains uncertain because CS/TS flux analogues are mostly tetraborides/hexaborides rather than PmB3 [CS2, CS4, TS3]. This route scores best because it balances kinetic access with manageable condition severity [CS2, CS4, TS3].

**F2 — Route 3.** Direct sintering requires 1400–1500 °C or higher and controlled Ar/vacuum environments [CS3, SS1, TS1]. It is ambient-pressure but still multistep because grinding, pressing, long annealing, and possible regrinding are needed [CS3, TS1]. Pm handling again adds a practical penalty [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.** Solid-state sintering has the weakest kinetics because dense/refractory precursors and B diffusion are slow [CS3, TS1]. The 100 h anneal in LuB4-type sintering and high-vacuum rare-earth boride synthesis indicate substantial kinetic barriers [CS3, SS4, TS1]. Phase competition is a major uncertainty because TS1 explicitly reports difficulty obtaining pure rare-earth hexaborides/tetraborides [TS1].

**F2 — Route 4.** High pressure imposes the largest severity penalty because TS4/TS5 require ~65 kbar and >1400 °C [TS4, TS5]. The route is justified only as a fallback for metastable phase trapping because CS/SS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure PmB3 synthesis and are neutral 50 on that route need [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. Pm handling remains an additional practical penalty [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4.** HPHT can access metastable borides, but short reaction times and impurity reports in TS4/TS5 limit the kinetic score [TS4, TS5]. It may help if PmB3 is not stable at ambient pressure, but there is no direct evidence that PmB3 requires pressure [Target, TS4, TS5]. The uncertainty is high because TS high-pressure analogues are dodecaborides, not triborides [TS4, TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has only one cation and a small two-site P6_3/mmc description, so the ionic-radius mismatch and large-cell triggers are not activated [Target]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PmB3 is most likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom [Target]. CS and SS strongly support the broader rare-earth/actinide boride family because many analogues are on-hull and experimentally synthesized by arc, flux, or sintering routes [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. TS supports the idea that slightly metastable borides can still be synthesized, including examples near or above the target’s hull energy [TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is a liquid-mediated route, especially rare-earth halide/self-flux, because it improves B transport and phase selection relative to direct sintering [CS2, CS4, CS5, TS3]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition with neighboring borides plus Pm-specific containment and radiochemical handling [TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but would require careful small-scale screening rather than a single deterministic recipe [CS4, TS1, Reasonable Inference].
