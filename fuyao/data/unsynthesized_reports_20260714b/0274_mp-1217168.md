### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom**, which is exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold in the prompt, so I scored it 90/100. CS analogues are mostly stable or nearly stable: V3Pd is 0.0 eV/atom (CS2), Ti3Al is 0.0 eV/atom (CS4), and Ti3Sn is 0.0026 eV/atom (CS1). SS gives mixed but useful support: Ti3Al/Ti3Sn are stable or nearly stable (SS1, SS2), while Ti3Hg is 0.071 eV/atom (SS4), suggesting related Ti-rich intermetallics can be metastable. TS is especially relevant because several thermodynamic analogues lie around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom and are still experimentally handled, e.g., Ti3Hg at 0.071 eV/atom (TS1), Zr3V3C at 0.051 eV/atom (TS2), and Zr2Fe at 0.063 eV/atom (TS3).

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

This route has moderate severity because arc melting Ti/Pd requires very high local temperature, likely above 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/CS2 show that such high-temperature melting is standard for similar intermetallics. The Ar atmosphere is not penalized under the prompt’s reactive/vacuum category, but oxygen control is still practically important because CS2 specifically uses Zr-gettered Ar. Multi-step processing is penalized because CS2 uses repeated remelting and annealing to ensure homogeneity. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct arc melting of Ti4Pd specifically, so TS is neutral for exact condition severity beyond showing related metallic systems can require specialized processing.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

Kinetics are helped by liquid-state mixing, as seen in Ti3Sn and V3Pd arc-melting examples (CS1, CS2). However, dense elemental Ti and Pd are low-activity precursors compared with salts or reactive powders [Reasonable Inference], and ordering after melt solidification may still require annealing. SS analogues Ti3Al/Ti3Sn support formation of Ti-rich ordered intermetallic frameworks (SS1, SS2), but they also show that long anneals can be needed for phase equilibration (SS1). TS gives insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Ti–Pd arc melting, so it is treated as neutral for this route’s F3 except for general metastability context.

#### F2 — Route 2: Induction melting + annealing

Induction melting has high thermal severity because Ti/Pd must be fully molten [Reasonable Inference], but it avoids vacuum, high pressure, volatile precursors, and necessarily complex multi-step processing. CS5 directly supports induction melting of elemental intermetallic compounds under Ar followed by 900 °C annealing. CS2 supports adding repeated remelting if homogeneity is insufficient. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for induction melting of Ti4Pd specifically; those sources are neutral for exact severity.

#### F3 — Route 2: Induction melting + annealing

The kinetic score is slightly better than arc melting because induction melting can provide uniform heating and bulk alloying, consistent with CS5. The precursor activity remains low because elemental metals are dense and not chemically activated [Reasonable Inference]. Annealing at 800–950 °C should enable ordering, supported by CS5’s 900 °C anneal and CS2’s annealing of arc-melted intermetallics. The main uncertainty is whether Ti4Pd is an equilibrium low-temperature phase or a metastable phase requiring controlled cooling, since the target is 0.050 eV/atom above hull.

#### F2 — Route 3: Solid-state diffusion

Solid-state diffusion has lower maximum temperature than melting but requires vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, which is explicitly penalized. CS4/SS1 and SS4/TS1 support evacuated-tube annealing over days to weeks, with temperatures from hundreds of °C up to 1200 °C. The route is multi-step because powder mixing, pressing, sealing, annealing, and possible regrinding are needed. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Ti4Pd powder diffusion kinetics, so the exact severity remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3: Solid-state diffusion

Powder precursors are more reactive than bulk pieces, but Ti and Pd remain metallic solids with slow interdiffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS4/SS1 show that Ti-rich intermetallic synthesis can require 170–500 h anneals, supporting a relatively low diffusion score. SS4/TS1 also show sealed solid-state diffusion routes over 4–47 days for Ti/Zr-based alloys, again indicating slow kinetics. The main uncertainty is whether fine Ti/Pd powders can form Ti4Pd before competing Ti–Pd phases dominate.

#### F2 — Route 4: Ball milling + crystallization anneal

Ball milling under Ar has mild nominal temperature/pressure conditions, as shown by TS5. It avoids high static pressure, high-temperature melting, volatile precursors, and vacuum if the anneal is done in Ar. The severity score is therefore high. However, this does not mean it is the most reliable route, because F2 measures condition harshness, not phase selectivity.

#### F3 — Route 4: Ball milling + crystallization anneal

Mechanochemical activation improves precursor contact and shortens diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], and TS5 supports high-energy milling under Ar. However, TS5 and TS3 emphasize amorphization/non-equilibrium alloy formation, which is a risk for obtaining crystalline Ti4Pd. The structural reconstruction score is therefore low because the route may produce amorphous Ti–Pd or other metastable phases requiring careful crystallization. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for mechanochemical Ti–Pd crystallization is treated as neutral-to-negative uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target triggers one amorphization check because the structure explicitly contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing TiTi12 cuboctahedra. CS1/CS4 and SS1/SS2 show similar cuboctahedral connectivity in Ti-rich intermetallic analogues, so the structural motif itself is not prohibitive. Radius mismatch and unit-cell size do not appear large enough to trigger the other two checks [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not provide direct radius or atom-count evidence, so those source signals are insufficient and treated neutrally. The resulting risk is low–medium, with special caution for ball milling because TS3/TS5 show amorphization tendencies in related metallic systems.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti4Pd is likely a **borderline metastable or weakly metastable phase**, because its energy above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than clearly 0.0 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues support synthesis well: Ti-rich intermetallics such as Ti3Sn and Ti3Al form by arc melting or annealing (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2), and the Pd-containing V3Pd analogue is directly made by arc melting from elements (CS2). The most important enabling factor is that Ti–transition-metal intermetallics are accessible by conventional metallurgical methods using elemental precursors under Ar (CS2, CS5). The most important limiting factor is phase competition/ordering control near a metastability threshold, especially because TS analogues around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom can require non-equilibrium or carefully optimized processing (TS1–TS3). Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires careful homogeneity control, oxygen exclusion, and post-melt annealing.
