### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin, so F1 = 90. Thermodynamic analogues support this choice because NiSe2 at 0.0614 eV/atom (TS-1), CoRh2Se4 at 0.0535 eV/atom (TS-2), MnSe2 at 0.0692 eV/atom (TS-4), and FeCr2Se4 at 0.0595 eV/atom (TS-5) are in a similar metastability window, while NiRh2Se4 is slightly lower at 0.0424 eV/atom (TS-3). CS also contains Co3Se4 at 0.0458 eV/atom and NiSe2 phases at 0–0.0614 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5), supporting accessibility of low-energy selenide phases. SS gives similar NiSe2/NiRh2Se4 examples but insufficient evidence from SS alone for the exact Ni2Se3 hull stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state ampoule

F2 is reduced because evacuated ampoules and sealed systems are standard for related selenides (CS-3, CS-5, SS-3, TS-2–5), Se is volatile/toxic [Reasonable Inference], and multiple firings or anneals are common in analogous solid-state syntheses (SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-5). F3 is moderate-low because elemental Ni is a dense metal and long diffusion lengths are expected [Reasonable Inference], consistent with multi-day or multi-week anneals in related materials (SS-3, TS-5). Structural similarity is partially favorable because Co3Se4 and FeCr2Se4 show mixed octahedral sharing similar to the target (CS-5, TS-5), but no CS/SS/TS source reports the exact hexagonal P-6m2 Ni2Se3 phase. Remaining uncertainty is whether solid-state annealing will equilibrate to NiSe/NiSe2 rather than preserve the 2D target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal/solvothermal selenization

F2 is high because the closest Ni–Se hydrothermal analogue uses only 120–210 °C, common autoclaves, and no special atmosphere (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1). F3 is also high because soluble Ni salts and in situ selenization bypass dense-metal diffusion, and NiSe2 sheet-like nanoarchitectures are explicitly produced under these conditions (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1). The structural match is not exact: CS/SS/TS show NiSe2, not Ni2Se3 P-6m2, so nucleation of the target phase remains the main uncertainty. The target’s 2D nature makes this route especially attractive because 2D NiSe2 nanosheets are reported in the hydrothermal analogue (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure synthesis

F2 is low because high pressure around 50–65 kbar is reported for related pyrite selenides (CS-1, CS-4), and such equipment is specialized. F3 is moderate because high pressure can promote compact NiSe6 octahedral frameworks and suppress Se volatility [Reasonable Inference], and high-pressure analogues form in short holds of 0.5–1.5 h (CS-1, CS-4). However, CS/SS high-pressure NiSe2 analogues are pyrite/marcasite-like rather than layered hexagonal P-6m2 (CS-1/SS-1), so structural transferability is only partial. The remaining uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the desired 2D sheet or instead favors denser 3D polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Alkali-polyselenide flux

F2 is moderate because SS-4 uses modest temperature but requires vacuum sealing, glovebox handling, K2Se, slow cooling, and flux removal. F3 is favorable for kinetics because polyselenide flux provides a liquid/chemically active medium at low temperature, as in SS-4, reducing diffusion limitations relative to solid-state synthesis. The main penalty is phase control: SS-4 explicitly reports mixed phases and unsuccessful full purification of AuCuSe4, so an analogous Ni–Se flux route may also produce competing phases. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS directly supports flux growth of Ni2Se3, so this route is more exploratory.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

F4 is 80 because only the mixed polyhedral connectivity check is triggered: the target has corner-, edge-, and face-sharing NiSe6 octahedra, and comparable mixed octahedral networks appear in Co3Se4 (CS-5) and FeCr2Se4 (TS-5). The cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered because the target has only Ni as cation; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any multi-cation disorder issue. The atom-count criterion is not triggered because the target description implies a compact binary sheet structure; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40-atom cell. The remaining uncertainty is that 2D layered products made hydrothermally can be poorly crystalline or nanoscale even if not fully amorphous [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni2Se3 at 0.0500 eV/atom is best regarded as a borderline stable/metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, consistent with thermodynamic analogues clustered around 0.04–0.07 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5). The CS/SS/TS data strongly support that Ni–Se and related transition-metal selenides are synthetically accessible by hydrothermal, ampoule, flux, and high-pressure routes, but none directly confirms hexagonal P-6m2 Ni2Se3. The most enabling factor is the existence of mild hydrothermal NiSe2 nanosheet synthesis using soluble Ni precursors and Se chemistry (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1), which aligns with the target’s 2D character. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Ni–Se compositions and structures, especially NiSe2-like pyrite/marcasite phases and possible NiSe/Ni-rich phases [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control comments in CS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-5]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, especially as a nanosheet or metastable product, but obtaining bulk single-phase crystalline material may be substantially harder.
