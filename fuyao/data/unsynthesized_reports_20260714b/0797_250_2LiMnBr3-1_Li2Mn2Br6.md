### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0400 eV/atom, within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. Several supplied analogues with comparable or even slightly higher hull energies are experimentally synthesized, including Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.0287 eV/atom [CS2, TS1], Li₂MnBr₄ at 0.0148 eV/atom [CS1, SS1, TS4], and CrInBr₃ at ~0.050 eV/atom [TS5]. The main uncertainty is that LiMnBr₃ may be metastable relative to other Li–Mn–Br phases despite the small hull distance [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct fusion route uses moderate temperatures similar to 723 K for Li₂FeBr₄ [CS2, TS1] and bromide melting for Li₂MnBr₄ [CS1, TS4], so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Deductions arise from evacuated ampoule/vacuum handling [CS2, TS1], sealed halide processing [CS5], multistep drying/fusing/annealing/quenching [CS2, TS1], and air/moisture sensitivity typical of anhydrous halide syntheses [CS3, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The severity is therefore moderate rather than extreme.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Reaction kinetics are favorable because LiBr/MnBr₂ are reactive halides and can form a molten or partially molten medium, as shown for Li₂MnBr₄ and Li₂FeBr₄ [CS1, CS2, TS1, TS4]. The main kinetic limitation is not diffusion but reconstructive ordering into the specific 2D P-31m LiMnBr₃ sheet, which is not the same as the known Cmmm Li₂MnBr₄ structure [CS1, SS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Reported phase-control difficulty for Li₂MnBr₄ lowers confidence [CS1, SS1, TS4].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Melt growth/Bridgman uses moderate halide temperatures, as TlMnI₃ was handled around 350 °C [SS2] and Li₂MgBr₄ by melt cooling in evacuated ampoules [CS5]. The penalties are mainly for vacuum/controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile halide systems, multi-step growth/cooling, and moisture-sensitive handling [CS5, SS2, SS4]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is supported by the references [CS5, SS2].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are strongest for Route 2 because a homogeneous melt and slow cooling allow rapid ion transport and better crystallization, consistent with Li₂MgBr₄ slow-cooling synthesis [CS5] and Mn-halide Bridgman growth [SS2]. Structural selection remains uncertain because the target is a 2D LiMnBr₃ sheet rather than the known Li₂MnBr₄ Cmmm phase [CS1, SS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically attractive but phase-selectivity-limited.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux annealing has the same sealed-ampoule and controlled-atmosphere penalties as the other halide routes because InMnBr₃/InCrBr₃ analogues use evacuated glass ampoules and protective handling [SS4, TS5]. It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, since related bromides form around 450 °C [SS4, TS5]. Additional complexity comes from flux separation and stoichiometry control [CS3, TS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux media should provide excellent diffusion and precursor activity, as bromide frameworks form in molten bromide conditions [SS4, TS5]. However, no exact LiMnBr₃ flux precedent is provided, so the structural reconstruction/phase-selection score is lower than the transport score [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact LiMnBr₃ flux crystallization]. Competing Li₂MnBr₄ formation is a realistic risk because it is the closest known Li–Mn–Br phase [CS1, SS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: the Li⁺/Mn²⁺ size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target formula and described P-31m sheet do not indicate a large >40-atom cell, and the connectivity does not show simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence [target description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Thus amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiMnBr₃ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0400 eV/atom, not zero [target data]. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis from anhydrous binary halides: Li₂MnBr₄ uses LiBr + MnBr₂ directly [CS1, SS1, TS4], and Li₂FeBr₄/Li₂MgBr₄ validate sealed-ampoule fusion and melt-cooling approaches [CS2, CS5, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical compatibility of molten bromide media, which repeatedly enables ternary halide formation at moderate temperatures [CS1, CS5, SS2, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with Li₂MnBr₄ and possibly MnBr₂-rich products, because the target requires a different Li:Mn ratio and a distinct layered P-31m topology [CS1, SS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears plausibly synthesizable, especially by sealed melt/slow-cooling methods, but phase-pure isolation may require iterative stoichiometry and cooling-rate optimization.
