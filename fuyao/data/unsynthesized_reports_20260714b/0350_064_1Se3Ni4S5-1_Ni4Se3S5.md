### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0420 eV/atom places it just below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold, so it is likely experimentally accessible but may be metastable rather than a robust ground state. TS1–TS4 provide the strongest thermodynamic signal because their E_hull values cluster near 0.042–0.045 eV/atom and they are reported as synthesizable chalcogenide phases. CS and SS give mixed but supportive evidence: CS1/CS2/CS3/CS5 and SS1–SS4 include E_hull = 0 examples, while CS4 and SS5 show metastable analogues can still be relevant. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific mixed S/Se ordering lowers or raises the practical decomposition tendency relative to the computed hull.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Route 1 has significant severity because CS1, CS3/CS4, SS4, and TS1/TS2/TS4/TS5 consistently use evacuated sealed tubes for chalcogenide synthesis, and S/Se volatility makes sealed handling necessary. TS1/TS2 and CS1 show long anneals, intermediate grinding, and slow cooling, so the route is operationally more complex than a single firing. SS2 suggests high pressure can stabilize some NiSe₂-related structures, but other CS/TS sources show ambient-pressure sealed-tube reactions are sufficient for many related chalcogenides, so high pressure was not included as mandatory. The main uncertainty is whether Ni₄Se₃S₅ forms before decomposition or segregation into NiS/NiSe₂-type binaries.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Elemental Ni has relatively slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS/TS analogues often require long times and grinding, lowering the kinetics score. However, the chalcogen components can melt/vapor-transport at reaction temperatures, and CS1/SS4/TS1 show chalcogenides form well below the metal melting points. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor because the target has an ordered 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet, while many analogues are spinel, pyrite, chalcopyrite, or Rh selenide frameworks. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a direct precursor with the same omega-derived structure, so structural kinetics remain uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2: Alkali polysulfoselenide flux growth

Flux growth reduces temperature severity, as SS1 forms a layered monoclinic selenide at 310 °C and CS2 forms a 2D chalcogenide at 550 °C. The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se/S, air-sensitive K₂Se/K₂S-type fluxes, and multistep flux removal, all supported or implied by SS1. SS1 also requires glovebox handling and DMF washing, increasing practical complexity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Ni specifically dissolving and crystallizing as Ni₄Se₃S₅ in this flux, so route transferability is not guaranteed.

#### F3 — Route 2: Alkali polysulfoselenide flux growth

SS1 strongly supports fast mass transport in alkali polychalcogenide fluxes because a layered selenide crystallizes at only 310 °C. The flux route should reduce diffusion limitations for Ni compared with direct solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The structural score is limited because SS1 reported mixed products and difficulty obtaining pure AuCuSe₄, indicating narrow phase-selectivity windows. The uncertainty is whether the mixed S/Se target is stabilized by the flux or whether soluble competing nickel polysulfide/polyselenide phases dominate.

#### F2 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / solvothermal mixed chalcogenization

Route 3 has the mildest conditions because SS3 forms Ni₃S₄ at 120 °C and SS5/TS3 forms NiSe₂-related sheet architectures at 120–210 °C without external vacuum or high pressure. The main severity penalties are multistep processing and toxic chalcogenide/selenium reagents, both supported by SS3 and SS5/TS3. Compared with Route 1 and Route 2, it avoids sealed evacuated high-temperature ampoules during the primary reaction. The uncertainty is that hydrothermal conditions may produce nanoparticles or metastable binaries rather than the ordered bulk Ni₄Se₃S₅ phase.

#### F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / solvothermal mixed chalcogenization

Kinetics are favorable because soluble Ni salts and reactive sulfide/selenide species are used in SS3 and SS5/TS3. SS3 and SS5/TS3 show Ni chalcogenide formation at low temperature, meaning the route bypasses the slow diffusion of dense elemental Ni powders. The structural reconstruction score is much lower because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates the exact Ni₄Se₃S₅ mixed-anion omega-derived structure by hydrothermal synthesis. The largest uncertainty is whether S/Se ordering can occur at 160–220 °C or whether a mild post-anneal is required.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Ni, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered by the target structure. The target description gives edge-sharing Ni-centered octahedra and does not report simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. The formula unit has 12 atoms, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40-atom cell. Therefore the amorphization tendency is low, although nanoscale hydrothermal products may still be poorly crystalline if ordering is kinetically limited.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₄Se₃S₅ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase** rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom, just below the common metastability cutoff. The CS/SS/TS analogues broadly support synthesis of Ni chalcogenides and layered/monoclinic chalcogenides by sealed-tube, flux, and hydrothermal routes, with the strongest direct Ni–S/Ni–Se evidence coming from SS3 and SS5/TS3. The most important enabling factor is the chemical compatibility of Ni with octahedral S/Se coordination, supported by the Ni chalcogenide analogues in SS3, SS5/TS3, and TS3. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: mixed S/Se ordering in a specific monoclinic omega-derived 2D sheet is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with hydrothermal mixed chalcogenization followed by mild annealing being the best first route and sealed-tube solid-state synthesis serving as the most conventional backup.
