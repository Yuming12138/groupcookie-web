### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff, so it receives 90/100. Chemically similar SrP3 and BaP3 phases are on or near the hull (CS-2 to CS-4), and structurally similar BaP8/BaP10 are also near the hull (SS-2, SS-3), supporting the feasibility of P-rich phosphide networks. Thermodynamically similar ZnP4 and InP3 at ~0.055–0.063 eV/atom have been synthesized under high pressure (TS-1, TS-2), indicating that this metastability range is experimentally accessible. The uncertainty is that Rb3P11 may lie on a narrow binary Rb–P phase boundary not sampled directly by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because Rb and P require inert and sealed handling, similar to sealed ampoule BaP3/BaP10 methods (CS-3, CS-4; SS-3, SS-5). Kinetically, Rb is highly reactive and potentially molten at low temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**, which helps mixing, but the target’s complex P sheet requires substantial reconstruction from elemental precursors. The main uncertainty is that CS/SS analogues are Sr/Ba-rich or Ba–P systems, while direct Rb–P phase selectivity is not demonstrated; **insufficient evidence from TS** for direct Rb ampoule synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux growth

The flux route has similar atmosphere/volatility penalties but improved kinetics because structurally similar K4P8Te4 crystallizes from flux at 450 °C over 6 days (SS-1), and alkali selenophosphates are made by molten alkali flux methods at 450–500 °C (TS-5). The liquid/flux medium should improve diffusion and allow P-network rearrangement **[Reasonable Inference]**. The limitation is phase specificity: SS-1 reports failed attempts for other alkali analogues, so alkali size and composition windows may be narrow. CS gives little direct support for binary Rb–P flux chemistry, so that component remains inferred.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure direct synthesis

The high-pressure route scores best because closely related P-rich polyphosphides BaP8 and SrP3 were synthesized at 3 GPa and 950–1000 °C in BN crucibles with controlled cooling/quench (CS-1; SS-2). TS also supports high pressure as an enabling method for metastable phosphides near the target’s E_hull, such as ZnP4 and InP3 (TS-1, TS-2). Kinetics are favorable because high pressure suppresses phosphorus loss and promotes dense P-network formation **[Reasonable Inference]**, but structural selectivity remains uncertain. The largest practical limitation is specialized multianvil equipment, even though the scoring table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: transport-assisted ampoule

This route is supported by BaP10 formation from BaP2 + red P with iodine transport in sealed quartz (SS-3). Using a preformed Rb phosphide should reduce violent elemental Rb–P reaction and improve stoichiometric control **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are improved by transport/mineralizer action, but long anneals in SS-3 imply slow ordering and possible competing phases. TS provides insufficient direct support for iodine-transport binary phosphides and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The structure is complex, with 3 inequivalent Rb and 11 inequivalent P sites in monoclinic C2/m, so a large-cell/ordering penalty is triggered **[Reasonable Inference from target structure]**. Mixed edge-sharing motifs are explicitly present in the target, but simultaneous vertex + edge + face sharing is not established, so C3 is not triggered. The resulting low–medium amorphization risk is consistent with the existence of complex crystalline P-rich analogues in CS and SS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3P11 is best viewed as a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the chemical plausibility of extended P–P networks with alkali/alkaline-earth cations, while TS suggests that phases in this metastability range can be synthesized, often under high pressure. The most critical enabling factor is containment of volatile/reactive Rb and P while allowing P-network polymerization, best achieved by high-pressure BN-capsule synthesis or sealed flux methods. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among multiple possible Rb phosphides and P-rich networks. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful stoichiometry, inert handling, and either high pressure or flux-assisted crystallization.
