### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase by the supplied target data. CS analogues are also mostly stable or nearly stable: LiBeN, KMgP, NaLiS, and NaBeAs have E_hull = 0, while LiMgN is only ~0.003 eV/atom above hull (CS1–CS5). SS and TS analogues are all reported at E_hull = 0 in the provided list (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5), supporting a high thermodynamic score. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field, and CS5 notes moisture sensitivity/no phase width for NaBe pnictides.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions because the closest Na–Be–pnictide analogue is made at 660 °C for 24 h (CS5). The deductions come from sealed volatile Na/P chemistry and toxic/air-sensitive precursors, consistent with sealed ampoule use in CS5 and CS3. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically on NaBeP volatility is noted, so SS/TS are used only as general sealed-phosphide support.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The kinetic advantage is that Na is mobile/molten in the proposed temperature range and CS5 demonstrates direct reaction of Na/Be/pnictogen elements at moderate temperature. The kinetic limitation is Be metal, which is refractory and may react slowly [Reasonable Inference], so precursor activity is not scored as high as sol-gel or salt routes. SS/TS analogues show that repeated annealing may be needed for phosphide ordering (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4), adding uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures comparable to binary precursor reactions in CS1, CS4, and SS5/TS5, so no >1000 °C deduction is needed. It is penalized for sealed volatile/air-sensitive phosphide handling and likely precursor-preparation steps [Reasonable Inference], paralleling the precursor complexity noted for CS1. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence exists for Na₃P + Be₃P₂ specifically, so this route is less certain than Route 1.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Binary phosphides should give better chemical intimacy and preformed P bonding than elemental Be + P [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported indirectly by successful binary precursor routes in CS1, CS4, and SS5/TS5. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction: Na₃P and Be₃P₂ do not share the target P4/nmm layered framework, so conversion may require long annealing.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 is penalized because structurally/thermodynamically similar phosphides often require 900–1100 °C repeated annealing and grinding (SS2, SS4; TS2, TS4), and CS3 uses temperatures up to 1150 °C for KMgP. Those conditions are more severe for Na/P-containing samples because sealed-tube pressure and alkali volatility become more problematic [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient CS evidence indicates that NaBeP specifically requires such high-temperature treatment; Route 3 is mainly a contingency.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Repeated grinding and annealing improve diffusion and ordering, as shown in SS1–SS4 and TS1–TS4. However, the need for multiple cycles indicates slow ordering or phase homogenization in related phosphides, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score. The route may be effective for impurity removal but risks Na loss or ampoule failure [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Na/Be cation size mismatch, giving one amorphization-risk trigger [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is not a large complex cell based on its simple P4/nmm layered description and related Matlockite-type analogues in CS3/CS4, so the unit-cell-size trigger is not applied. The target reports edge/face sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is only partially supported and not counted.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaBeP is likely a ground-state phase because the supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically related phosphides/pnictides are also on the hull (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from the chemically closest Na–Be–pnictide analogue, prepared directly from Na, Be, and pnictogen elements in sealed Ta/quartz ampoules at 660 °C (CS5). The key enabling factor is the compatibility of alkali–Be–pnictogen chemistry with sealed moderate-temperature solid-state synthesis, plus the target’s predicted thermodynamic stability. The main limiting factor is handling and retaining volatile/air-sensitive Na and P while safely reacting toxic, refractory Be. Phase purity may require careful annealing because SS/TS phosphide analogues often need repeated grinding or long tempering to suppress side phases (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4).
