### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0320 eV/atom places it in the high synthesizability range of 90–100 by the provided rubric [Target]. TS strongly supports feasibility because thermodynamically similar halides with E_hull values of 0.0285 and 0.0364 eV/atom are reported as synthesized (TS1, TS2). SS also supports low-hull halide/oxychloride crystallization in the 0–0.03 eV/atom range (SS1–SS3), while CS is mixed and less directly relevant to PbCl₂ chemistry, so CS is supportive but lower confidence. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: a small positive hull implies metastability relative to another PbCl₂ phase or decomposition set [Target + Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 aqueous cooling

Route 1 has mild conditions because TS2 and SS3 use aqueous solution cooling rather than high pressure or high temperature. The only major deduction is toxicity from PbCl₂, since lead salts are hazardous [Reasonable Inference] and PbCl₂ is explicitly used in TS2/SS1. CS gives insufficient direct aqueous signal and is neutral for this route. Remaining uncertainty is not severity but whether the desired metastable Pmmn sheet polymorph crystallizes rather than the common stable form [Target + Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 aqueous cooling

Kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization avoids solid-state cation diffusion, as shown by TS2 and SS3 aqueous crystallizations. Precursor activity is high because PbCl₂ is already the target composition and can recrystallize without cation exchange [TS2/SS1 + Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is limited to nucleation/polymorph selection, but the target’s 0.032 eV/atom metastability lowers confidence [Target]. The key uncertainty is whether seeding or supersaturation control is needed.

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed-ampoule sublimation / CVT

Route 2 is more severe because it uses vacuum or chlorine-containing sealed systems, as in CS1 and SS2/TS1. The temperature is not extreme compared with high-pressure CS examples, but sealed volatile halide handling and optional Cl₂ add deductions [CS1, SS2/TS1]. TS5 supports sublimation purification in evacuated ampoules for binary halides, indicating feasibility but not simplicity. Remaining uncertainty is the exact PbCl₂ vapor pressure/transport window, which is inferred rather than given.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed-ampoule sublimation / CVT

Vapor-route kinetics are moderately favorable because CS1, SS2/TS1, and TS5 show halides can be transported or sublimed to form crystals. Crystallization from vapor can produce high-quality crystals, but the target’s metastability means higher-temperature equilibration may favor another polymorph [Target + Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is simpler than multinary analogues because PbCl₂ is binary, but chlorine loss or hydrolysis must be controlled [CS1, SS2/TS1]. CS is the strongest support for this route; SS/TS reinforce sealed-ampoule halide handling.

#### F2 — Route 3 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis uses moderate temperature and autogenous pressure, not high pressure >5 GPa, consistent with SS1. It receives deductions for multi-step autoclave operation and Pb toxicity. CS and TS provide insufficient hydrothermal-specific evidence and are treated as neutral for severity. The method is less severe than CVT because it avoids chlorine gas and high-temperature vapor transport.

#### F3 — Route 3 hydrothermal

SS1 gives strong kinetic support because PbCl₂-containing mixtures crystallize Pb–Cl frameworks at only 200–230 °C over 4 days. Solution-mediated transport should give high ion mobility and reduce diffusion limitations [SS1 + Reasonable Inference]. However, because SS1 is an oxychloride route, binary PbCl₂ formation requires suppressing oxygen-containing competing phases through acidity and chloride activity [SS1 + Reasonable Inference]. TS and CS are neutral for this route due to insufficient direct hydrothermal evidence.

#### F2 — Route 4 direct chlorination

Direct chlorination is chemically straightforward but operationally severe because it uses dry Cl₂ or sealed halogen systems, supported by CS1 chlorine chloride synthesis and TS5 closed-tube halogenation. Pb and Cl₂ hazards create a substantial safety deduction [CS1/TS5 + Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures can remain below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. SS gives insufficient direct support and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4 direct chlorination

The reaction kinetics for forming PbCl₂ from Pb and Cl₂ should be favorable because direct metal–halogen reactions are highly reactive [Reasonable Inference] and TS5 demonstrates analogous Bi + Br₂ formation of BiBr₃. Still, structural reconstruction from elements is complete, and post-synthesis recrystallization may be necessary to obtain the target Pmmn phase [Target + Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports chlorine-rich chloride formation but not Pb-specific polymorph selection. The main uncertainty is controlling heat release and avoiding stable-polymorph lock-in.

The target has only one cation species and a simple binary PbCl₂ composition, so the ionic-radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered [Target]. The strongest synthetic support comes from TS2 and SS3 for low-temperature crystallization of Pb/halide salts, and from SS1 for PbCl₂-based hydrothermal crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: mild routes must avoid crystallizing the more stable PbCl₂ modification, while vapor/high-temperature routes may equilibrate away from the Pmmn sheet phase [Target + Reasonable Inference].
