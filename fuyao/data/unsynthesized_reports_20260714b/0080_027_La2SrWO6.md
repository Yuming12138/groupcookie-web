### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target E\_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin, justifying a high score. CS and TS analogues show that oxides at or somewhat above this energy can be made: La₂NaTaO₆ and Sr₂HoTaO₆ are on hull (CS1, CS5), while LiLa₂ReO₆ and Ce₂O₃ are synthesized despite E\_hull values around 0.06–0.08 eV/atom (TS1/SS5, TS2/SS2). The remaining uncertainty is that SrLa₂WO₆ appears to require W⁴⁺ [Reasonable Inference], so thermodynamic stability is coupled to oxygen chemical potential rather than just total energy.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The ceramic route inherits high-temperature and multi-step penalties from Sr and double-perovskite analogues, which commonly require 1150–1425 °C, repeated grinding, and pellet refiring (CS2–CS5). A reducing atmosphere penalty is applied because similar valence-sensitive oxides used H₂/N₂ or H₂/Ar conditions (CS2, CS3). The main uncertainty is whether W⁴⁺ can be stabilized under mild H₂ without forming secondary tungsten oxides or SrWO₄ [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory oxide diffusion, as shown by high-temperature ceramic schedules and repeated firings in CS2–CS5. SrCO₃/La₂O₃/WO₂ are less reactive than nitrate/sol-gel precursors [Reasonable Inference], and forming the trigonal ilmenite-derived order requires substantial reconstruction from binary oxides. The uncertainty is whether finer WO₂ or sol-gel-derived precursors could raise the kinetic score [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed-tube route has moderate-to-high severity because vacuum/sealed reduced-oxide syntheses require controlled atmosphere and containment, as in LaTiO₃ and reduced tantalate preparations (TS4, TS3). It avoids high pressure, but sealed ampoules introduce risks from CO₂, oxygen fugacity, and vessel compatibility [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Sr–La–W sealed-tube synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics remain poor because the sealed tube does not provide a liquid transport pathway, and analogues such as LaTiO₃ still required regrinding/reheating and careful stoichiometry (TS4). Re/ReO₃ double perovskite analogues show that refractory redox-active B-site systems can be synthesized but require staged high-temperature processing (SS3/SS5, TS1). The uncertainty is whether an internal W/W oxide buffer improves phase selectivity enough to offset slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth has a lower temperature burden because La₂NaTaO₆ forms at 600 °C in NaOH flux (CS1), and Ce₂O₃ crystallizes from LiCl at 700–800 °C (SS2/TS2). However, sealed vessels, caustic or anhydrous fluxes, slow cooling, and washing steps impose deductions (CS1, SS2/TS2). The largest uncertainty is redox compatibility of W⁴⁺ with hydroxide flux, which is not demonstrated in the provided CS/SS/TS data.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux improves precursor activity and mass transport compared with solid-state diffusion, supported by successful low-temperature crystal growth in CS1 and SS2/TS2. The structural assembly is plausible because CS1 forms a chemically close La-containing octahedral oxide, but W⁴⁺ redox control may require chloride flux or buffering [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 3 receives the best kinetic score but an expert penalty for chemical uncertainty.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target contains cations with substantially different bonding distances, especially W–O at 2.03 Å versus Sr/La–O around 2.43–2.56 Å, so one amorphization trigger is counted [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is crystalline, ordered, and based mainly on octahedral corner sharing, and no face-sharing coexistence is reported in the target description. CS1 and CS5 show that chemically related complex oxides with tilted octahedra can crystallize, reducing concern from the size mismatch.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrLa₂WO₆ is best viewed as a borderline-stable or mildly metastable oxide because its E\_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable cutoff, and TS analogues around 0.06–0.08 eV/atom have been synthesized (TS1, TS2). The CS evidence supports oxide synthesis strongly in a broad sense, especially through Sr/La-containing ceramic routes and La₂NaTaO₆ flux growth (CS1, CS5), but no listed analogue directly proves Sr–La–W⁴⁺ chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is control of oxygen fugacity to stabilize the inferred W⁴⁺ state while allowing octahedral framework crystallization [Reasonable Inference; supported by valence-control precedents in CS2, CS3, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is competing oxidation or secondary phase formation, especially W⁶⁺ tungstate-like products under air or overly oxidizing flux [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine; flux growth offers the best kinetic pathway, while reducing ceramic synthesis is the most conservative bulk backup.
