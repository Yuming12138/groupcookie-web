### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, so it falls within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range where metastable synthesis is generally plausible. TS analogues have similar E_hull values, e.g. TS1 at 0.0404 eV/atom, TS3 at 0.0388 eV/atom, TS4 at 0.0394 eV/atom, and TS5 at 0.0309 eV/atom, supporting that phases in this energy range can be experimentally relevant. CS2 also reports an alkali halide with E_hull ≈0.0316 eV/atom accessed by deposition, which supports metastable halide accessibility. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is a polymorph of NaCl, whose stable bulk rocksalt structure is very competitive; CS/SS/TS do not directly prove the P4/mmm polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with CS2 using −160 to 120 °C vacuum evaporation of alkali halides. The main severity deductions are vacuum operation and vapor deposition control, both directly supported by CS2. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this thin-film route, but they reinforce that dry chloride handling is important in related chloride syntheses. The uncertainty is whether the target P4/mmm phase persists after warming.

#### F3 — Route 1

CS2 gives the strongest kinetic support because metastable or abnormal alkali-halide structures were obtained by low-temperature evaporation. Single-source NaCl evaporation should give good Na:Cl stoichiometry, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from the use of preformed halide sources in CS2. The crystallization score is limited because neither CS nor SS nor TS directly reports tetragonal P4/mmm NaCl. The route is favored because kinetic trapping and substrate templating can bypass equilibrium rocksalt formation.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses standard sealed-ampoule chloride synthesis practices supported by SS1, SS3, SS4, and TS2. It requires vacuum or dry Ar sealing, drying, high-temperature annealing or melting, and quenching, so the process is more severe than Route 1. It does not require high pressure or >1000 °C conditions in the proposed adaptation. The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility and vapor pressure management near molten NaCl.

#### F3 — Route 2

Bulk kinetics are favorable once NaCl is molten, and SS/TS analogues show chloride salts can react or crystallize in sealed ampoules. However, structural reconstruction from stable rocksalt NaCl to a 2D tetragonal P4/mmm polymorph is the limiting kinetic issue. TS2 shows quenching can be used for Na chloride phases, but not for pure NaCl polymorph selection. Therefore, this route is plausible but less compelling than thin-film trapping.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is based on chloride flux/melt chemistry from SS1 and SS5, with CS5 providing broader molten-salt flux precedent. It requires dry/inert handling, sealed or protected equipment, multiple steps, and flux removal. It avoids high pressure and can likely avoid >1000 °C if low-melting chloride eutectics are used, which is a [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is moderate to challenging because chloride fluxes are hygroscopic and corrosive.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux transport gives high ion mobility, supported by SS1 and SS5. However, flux growth generally improves equilibration, which may favor the stable rocksalt NaCl polymorph rather than the metastable P4/mmm sheet structure; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Quenching or templating could improve kinetic trapping, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for flux stabilization of the target structure. The route is therefore kinetically workable but phase-selectivity limited.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization criterion is not triggered. The reported bonding network has corner and edge sharing, but no face-sharing coexistence is described, so the strict C3 criterion is not triggered. SS and TS analogues with similar chloride octahedral/square-pyramidal connectivity are crystalline, e.g. SS2, TS3, and TS5. Uncertainty remains because a 2D polymorph grown at low temperature may form nanocrystalline or textured films rather than large single crystals.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable NaCl polymorph rather than the bulk ground state, because its E_hull is 0.041 eV/atom and conventional NaCl is expected to favor rocksalt in bulk. CS2 provides the most relevant support, showing alkali halides can form metastable abnormal structures by low-temperature vacuum evaporation. SS and TS strongly support dry chloride handling, sealed ampoules, and chloride phase synthesis, but they do not directly demonstrate the target P4/mmm NaCl polymorph. The most critical enabling factor is kinetic trapping by low-temperature deposition or rapid quenching. The most critical limiting factor is competition with stable rocksalt NaCl during annealing or bulk crystallization.
