### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0380 eV/atom, which falls below 0.05 eV/atom and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score. CS gives supportive but mixed evidence: several chemically similar compounds are on or near the hull, such as In₁₀Cl₁₈ at 0.0 eV/atom and CdS₂ at ~0.051 eV/atom, while the CdCl₄ analogue is much higher at ~0.256 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). SS is strongly supportive because all five structural analogues are reported at 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1–SS-5). TS is also supportive because thermodynamic analogues near the target stability range are experimentally synthesized at ~0.019–0.062 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, consistent with related halide syntheses at 340–460 °C and sealed-ampoule Cd chloride CVT near 420 °C (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2). The deductions arise from dry/inert or vacuum sealing, volatile halide containment, multiple operations, and Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference; sealed ampoule support from CS-1, CS-5, SS-2]. CS supports moderate severity; SS supports moderate severity; TS provides indirect support through halide ampoule processing but no direct CdCl₂ polymorph route, so TS is partially informative rather than decisive.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics should be favorable because the precursor can be the same composition, CdCl₂, so only polymorph rearrangement is required [Reasonable Inference]. The chemically similar In chloride synthesis required long tempering, showing that halide phase equilibration can be slow even at moderate temperature (CS-1). SS-2 shows CdCl₂ participates in successful chloride vapor/solid reaction within 48 h, which supports mobility under sealed-ampoule conditions. TS provides insufficient direct kinetic evidence for binary CdCl₂ polymorph formation and is treated as neutral for this route-specific kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires vacuum sealing and a temperature gradient, as in SS-2 and SS-3–SS-5, so it is more operationally demanding than simple annealing. It still avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, which improves the score relative to high-pressure routes such as CS-2. Volatile CdCl₂ containment and Cd toxicity are the main severity penalties [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 supports feasibility of halide sublimation; SS provides the strongest evidence for CVT; TS provides general support for evacuated ampoule halide transport but less direct relevance.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport should improve kinetics because material is recrystallized from mobile vapor species rather than relying only on solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 demonstrates Cd-containing chloride crystal growth by CVT in 48 h, and SS-3–SS-5 demonstrate related halide CVT over 5 days. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the desired Pmmn CdCl₂ polymorph nucleates preferentially rather than a more stable/common CdCl₂ polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 supports vapor purification improving crystal quality, while TS offers only indirect support from halide ampoule methods.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Solution crystallization is mild because CS-4, TS-1, and TS-4 show chloride crystals forming from aqueous solution at room temperature or by slow cooling from hot water. The main penalties are Cd toxicity, multi-step dehydration, and possible need for dry vacuum/N₂/HCl post-treatment [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS support mild solution processing strongly, while SS-1 supports low-temperature chloride solution crystallization under dry inert conditions. However, none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates anhydrous binary CdCl₂ Pmmn formation from solution, so the condition score is high but not fully transferable.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution kinetics are intrinsically favorable because Cd²⁺ and Cl⁻ are mobile in solution [Reasonable Inference], and related chlorides crystallize readily from solution in CS-4, TS-1, and TS-4. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction during dehydration: solution-grown Cd chloride may form hydrates or solvates rather than the desired anhydrous sheet polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 shows solution crystallization can yield well-defined chloride clusters but under highly controlled dry conditions. Therefore, kinetics of crystallization are strong, but kinetics of conversion to the exact anhydrous Pmmn target are only moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation radius mismatch trigger [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives one Cd site and two Cl sites and does not suggest a very large unit cell, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not supported by the target data. The target does have mixed edge- and corner-sharing CdCl₆ octahedra, which triggers one amorphization/structural-complexity point. CS/SS/TS contain many crystalline halide analogues with edge/corner-sharing motifs, which reduces concern that such connectivity prevents crystallization (CS-4, SS-2, TS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a mildly metastable phase rather than a deeply unstable compound because its E_hull is only 0.0380 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS datasets collectively support synthesis of related halides under mild-to-moderate conditions: sealed ampoules, vapor transport, sublimation, and solution crystallization all appear repeatedly (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2–SS-5, TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The most important enabling factor is the simple binary CdCl₂ chemistry, which permits use of an anhydrous single-source precursor and avoids multication diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is polymorph control: the target Pmmn sheet structure may compete with other CdCl₂ polymorphs or hydrates, especially in solution routes [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best practical strategy being dry sealed-ampoule annealing or sublimation after careful precursor purification.
