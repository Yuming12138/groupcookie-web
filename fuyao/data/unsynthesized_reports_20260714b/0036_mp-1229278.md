### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for the highest stability bin, so I assign 90 rather than a higher 95–100 because it is boundary-stable rather than clearly on the hull [Target]. CS entries are strongly supportive because several chemically similar hydrates have E_hull values from ~0.0019 to 0.0376 eV/atom [CS1][CS2][CS3][CS5]. SS and TS are also supportive, although SS5 at 0.145 eV/atom and TS5 at 0.0674 eV/atom show that related hydrates can be metastable [SS5][TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull energy does not fully encode humidity/water-activity stability for a high hydrate [Target][Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Condition Severity**  
Route 1 scores 100 because the closest sulfate analogues crystallize from aqueous solution at 70–105 °C without high pressure, and the Cr argon/vacuum requirement is tied to Cr2+ oxidation rather than Al3+ chemistry [CS1][CS3][Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 scores 100 because slow evaporation analogues use mild aqueous conditions without special atmosphere or pressure [CS2][CS5][SS4][TS4]. Route 3 scores 90 because precipitation itself is mild, but controlled addition, pH/supersaturation management, aging, and isolation make it a multi-step process; insufficient evidence from CS for this factor is scored neutral 50 [SS3][TS2][CS1][CS2][CS3][CS4][CS5]. Route 4 scores 90 because hydrothermal synthesis uses only ~200 °C and no GPa pressure, but it requires sealed autoclave loading, heating, slow cooling, and recovery; insufficient evidence from CS and SS is neutral 50 for hydrothermal transferability [TS1][CS1][CS3][SS1][SS2].

**F3 — Reaction Kinetics**  
Route 1 scores 90 because soluble sulfate precursors and liquid-mediated transport are highly favorable, while the target’s unusual square-coplanar S site lowers the structural-reconstruction score relative to tetrahedral sulfate analogues [CS1][CS3][SS1][SS2][Target]. Route 2 scores 86 because slow evaporation enables ordered hydrogen-bonded oxysalts, but ambient evaporation can take days to weeks and may require careful crystal selection [CS2][CS5][SS4][SS5][TS4]. Route 3 scores 76 because precipitation gives fast mixing but can trap poorly ordered solids, and aluminum hydrolysis risk is not directly resolved by CS data, which is neutral 50 for this route [SS3][TS2][Reasonable Inference]. Route 4 scores 80 because heated aqueous media improves transport, but hydrothermal temperatures may favor less hydrated aluminum sulfate phases over the target H50/O52 hydrate [TS1][Target][Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target triggers C1 if Al3+ and S6+ are both counted as cationic centers, and it triggers C2 because the formula contains 113 atoms [Target][Reasonable Inference]. It does not trigger C3 because the target description reports corner-sharing but not coexisting vertex/edge/face sharing [Target]. CS/SS/TS show that hydrated oxysalts can crystallize despite hydrogen bonding, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS quantifies the atom-count penalty, so the final F4 value is driven by the target checks [CS3][SS1][TS1][Target]. The resulting medium amorphization risk is mainly a crystallization/order risk, not a high-temperature glass-forming risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best interpreted as a low-lying metastable or boundary-stable hydrate rather than an unambiguous ground-state phase because E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS and SS analogues strongly support aqueous crystallization of hydrated metal sulfates, especially CuH6SO7 and CrH6SO7, which are close in chemistry/structure and form under mild solution conditions [CS1][CS3][SS1][SS2]. TS analogues support the thermodynamic plausibility of hydrated oxysalts near this energy range and provide backup hydrothermal or precipitation routes [TS1][TS2][TS3]. The most important enabling factor is liquid-mediated sulfate chemistry, which avoids solid-state diffusion and supports low-temperature hydrate assembly [CS1][CS3][Reasonable Inference]. The most important limiting factor is controlling water activity, acidity, and the unusual target sulfate geometry without forming competing aluminum sulfate hydrates or dehydrated phases [Target][CS3][Reasonable Inference].
