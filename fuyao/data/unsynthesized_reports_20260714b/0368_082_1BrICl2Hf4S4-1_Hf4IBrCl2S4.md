### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. This is consistent with several chemically and thermodynamically similar phases also being hull-stable or nearly hull-stable, including La₄NS₃Cl₃, Nd₄NS₃Cl₃, Pr₃AsS₅Cl₂, LaS, and Sc₄NCl₆ (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5, TS-1, TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or mixed-halide ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses conditions similar to CS-3/CS-5 and SS-4: sealed ampoules, high temperature below 1000 °C, long reaction times, and volatile halogen-containing reagents. The severity score is limited mainly by vacuum sealing, volatile halide species, and air/moisture-sensitive precursors, all supported by CS-3, CS-5, SS-4, and SS-5. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Hf₄S₄IBrCl₂ can form directly without flux or transport, so this route remains challenging.

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state kinetics are penalized because Hf metal is refractory and the target requires ordering of Hf, S, Cl, Br, and I into a specific 2D sheet. Long sintering times in CS-3, CS-4, CS-5, and SS-4 indicate that related chalcogenide-halide phases often require prolonged diffusion. A modest kinetic benefit is inferred from volatile halides, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports fast direct formation of the exact target.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth avoids temperatures above 1000 °C and is directly supported by NaCl/excess chloride flux improving single-crystal formation in CS-1. However, the route still requires evacuated ampoules, volatile halides, glovebox handling, and flux separation, which are comparable to CS-1, CS-5, and SS-4. The main uncertainty is whether flux chemistry will preserve the intended Cl/Br/I ratio.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve mass transport compared with a purely dry solid-state route, as CS-1 explicitly reports faster and more complete reaction with NaCl or excess MCl₃ flux. However, Hf remains a slow-diffusing refractory cation, and target formation likely requires complete reconstruction into a 2D octahedral mixed-halide lattice. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for selective ordering of I and Br on Hf(1) sites and Cl on Hf(2) sites.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT uses sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile iodine/halogen species, matching SS-2, SS-3, and TS-5. The temperature range is moderate for refractory sulfides, but the route is operationally demanding because it requires two-zone furnace control and careful halogen loading. No high pressure is needed, which improves feasibility relative to truly HTHP methods.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT has the best kinetic score because gas-phase halide transport can overcome sluggish Hf diffusion, and Zr sulfide/chalcogenide CVT with iodine is directly supported by SS-3. SS-2 and TS-5 also support iodine transport for sulfide crystals. The major uncertainty is compositional fractionation: Cl, Br, and I may transport at different rates, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS demonstrates stable three-halide incorporation during CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern. The described structure uses corner- and edge-sharing Hf octahedra but no face sharing, so the specified mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered. The formula unit is modest in size; however, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the exact crystallographic cell size beyond the target description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₄S₄IBrCl₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of related sulfide-halide and reduced halide frameworks in sealed ampoules, fluxes, and CVT conditions, especially at 750–950 °C over week-scale durations. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or fluxing, which can overcome the slow diffusion expected for refractory Hf and is supported by CS-1, SS-3, and TS-5. The most critical limiting factor is precise mixed-halide stoichiometry and site ordering, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates simultaneous Cl/Br/I ordering in a Hf sulfide sheet. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible but will likely require careful control of halogen chemical potentials and sealed-tube conditions.
