### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the upper edge of the 90–100 band in the required scoring table. TS provides strong support because several thermodynamic analogues at similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized, including Nd₃ReO₇ at 0.0506 eV/atom (TS1), Sm₃ReO₇ at 0.0498 eV/atom (TS5), and RbPrNb₂O₇ at 0.0559 eV/atom (TS3). CS and SS also support this range through synthesized near-hull oxides such as Sm₂Ti₃Bi₂O₁₂ at 0.0413 eV/atom (CS1) and Pr/Nd rhenates at ~0.041–0.051 eV/atom (SS1, SS4). The remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom suggests likely metastability rather than a deep ground state.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: oxide solid-state route

The condition severity is moderate because the closest chemical analogue Ba₂InTaO₆ requires air firing up to 1500 °C with multiple cycles (CS3), and Ba₂CeTaO₆ uses 1400–1425 °C with regrinding (SS3). No high pressure or special atmosphere is needed in CS3, which helps the F2 score. Kinetics are the main weakness: CS3 explicitly reports that In/Ta ordering is difficult and requires prolonged high-temperature treatment, while Ta₂O₅ is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. The route is credible but likely slow and may produce competing binary/ternary oxides before equilibrating.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: co-precipitation route

The co-precipitation route has similar condition severity to the solid-state route, but avoids the highest 1500 °C temperatures by improving precursor mixing [Reasonable Inference]. SS5 directly supports La nitrate/TaCl₅ co-precipitation at pH ≈9 followed by calcination at 1570 K, making the route chemically relevant. Kinetically, nitrate/chloride-derived precursors are more active than dense oxides, but Ta chemistry remains difficult and SS5 notes metastable/multiphase behavior in related La–Ta oxides. The main uncertainty is whether In co-precipitates homogeneously with La and Ta without forming separate In₂O₃-rich regions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: flux route

Flux-assisted synthesis is supported only indirectly: CS2 demonstrates molten hydroxide flux growth of a La-containing oxide at 600 °C, and TS2 shows molten-salt ion exchange in tantalates. The lower reaction temperature improves transport, but the phase window is uncertain and alkali incorporation is a major risk [Reasonable Inference; SS2 and TS2 show alkali tantalates are readily stabilized]. Kinetically, a flux may help dissolve/reprecipitate Ta-containing species, but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates La–In–Ta oxide formation by flux. Thus F3 is intermediate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch, with La–O distances around 2.39–2.57 Å, Ta–O around 1.97–2.06 Å, and In–O around 2.28–2.73 Å in the provided structure; this triggers C1. The described P1 cell contains 10 crystallographic atoms, so C2 is not triggered from the supplied target structure. The target description mentions corner-sharing OLa₂TaIn tetrahedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity, so C3 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from TS for amorphization tendency is treated as neutral. Overall the amorphization risk is low–medium, not prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La₂TaInO₆ is most likely a **metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than clearly zero. The CS evidence is favorable because Ba₂InTaO₆, with the same In–Ta oxide subchemistry, is synthesized by high-temperature solid-state reaction (CS3), while SS and TS show multiple La/lanthanide tantalate or near-hull oxide phases can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is **highly homogeneous cation mixing**, best achieved by co-precipitation, because refractory Ta-containing oxides and In/Ta ordering are kinetically slow as shown in CS3. The most critical limiting factor is **phase competition and ordering/reconstruction**, especially because the target P1 structure and under-coordinated In environment differ from the more common ordered double perovskite InO₆/TaO₆ framework in CS3. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require optimization rather than a single straightforward firing.
