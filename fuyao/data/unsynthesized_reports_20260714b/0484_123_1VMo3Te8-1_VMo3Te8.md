### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0180 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. This value is comparable to experimentally synthesized analogues such as MoTe₂ at 0.0139 eV/atom (SS1/TS1), VTe₂ at 0.0185 eV/atom (SS4/TS3), and NbPdTe₅ at 0.0213 eV/atom (CS2/TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is not exactly on the hull, so it may require kinetic trapping or Te-rich/transport conditions rather than equilibrium bulk synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Sealed-tube direct solid-state reaction

Route 1 uses conditions strongly supported by CS and SS examples: evacuated tubes, high temperature near 1000 °C, and multi-day anneals are used for Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆, NbIrTe₄, TaPtTe₅, and VTe₂ (CS1/CS3/CS4, SS4/SS5). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, Te volatility, and the likely need for stepwise heating/regrinding, which is explicitly used for NbIrTe₄ and improved TaPtTe₅ formation (CS3/CS4). Insufficient evidence from TS on exact target reaction conditions requires neutral handling of target-specific phase behavior beyond the analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube direct solid-state reaction

The kinetic score is limited because elemental Mo and V are refractory and likely diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference], while CS3 shows long annealing and regrinding may be needed for refractory-metal tellurides. Layered MoTe₂/VTe₂/WTe₂ analogues support the structural pathway (SS1/SS3/SS4/SS5), but V/Mo ordering in VMo₃Te₈ adds a kinetic burden not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS. Excess Te helps, as related tellurides improved under Te-rich conditions (CS3/CS4), but solid-state kinetics remain the main weakness.

#### F2 — Route 2: CVT / halogen-assisted growth

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C temperatures because NbPdTe₅ and WTe₂ CVT are demonstrated at 700/750 °C and 820/700 °C respectively (CS2/TS5, SS3). Deductions still apply for evacuated ampoules, volatile/toxic halogen transport agents, and multi-step two-zone growth (CS2/SS3/TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on VMo₃Te₈-specific halide volatility creates uncertainty in optimal transport-agent choice.

#### F3 — Route 2: CVT / halogen-assisted growth

CVT improves kinetics by transporting species through the vapor phase, supported by successful WTe₂ and NbPdTe₅ crystal growth at moderate temperatures (SS3, CS2/TS5). Structurally similar MoTe₂/WTe₂ layered tellurides are accessible by vapor transport (SS1/SS3, TS1), which supports crystallization of a molybdenite-derived sheet. The key uncertainty is differential transport of V and Mo, which could shift the V:Mo ratio [Reasonable Inference; composition-control issues are hinted by NbPdTe₅ microprobe deviations in CS2/TS5].

#### F2 — Route 3: Te-rich self-flux

Route 3 uses high Te activity, which is supported by Te-rich or excess-Te syntheses in Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆, NbIrTe₄, and TaPtTe₅ (CS1/CS3/CS4). The severity remains moderate because sealed tubes are required to contain volatile Te, and post-growth separation or annealing adds steps [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS on Te-flux growth of the exact V–Mo–Te ternary means the route is plausible but less directly demonstrated than CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3: Te-rich self-flux

A Te-rich melt should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], and excess Te improved related multication tellurides (CS3/CS4). Layered telluride structures are accessible in the relevant binary systems (SS1/SS3/SS4/SS5), so structural reconstruction is moderate rather than severe. The main uncertainty is removal of residual Te and avoiding binary VTe₂/MoTe₂ crystallization, especially because VTe₂ preparations report elemental Te impurity and crystal-growth difficulties (SS4/SS5, TS3).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple layered chalcogenide framework and no evidence for a very large unit cell or mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity in the supplied target description. The structurally similar VTe₂, WTe₂, and MoTe₂ entries are ordered 2D tellurides rather than amorphous-prone complex networks (SS1/SS3/SS4/SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on glass formation in this exact ternary is noted, but the low apparent complexity supports a high F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VMo₃Te₈ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0180 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally accessible MoTe₂, VTe₂, and NbPdTe₅ analogues (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS3, CS2/TS5). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of layered transition-metal tellurides by sealed tubes and CVT, especially with vacuum, Te-rich conditions, and halogen transport (CS1–CS4, SS1/SS3–SS5, TS1/TS3/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high Te chemical potential while allowing V and Mo to order into a molybdenite-derived sheet [Reasonable Inference from excess-Te benefits in CS3/CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with MoTe₂, VTe₂, and other binary/ternary tellurides, as impurity formation is repeatedly reported in related systems (CS1/CS3/CS4, SS4/SS5). Overall, CVT is the best first route because it combines moderate temperature with improved mass transport and precedent for Mo/W telluride crystal growth (SS1/SS3, TS1).
