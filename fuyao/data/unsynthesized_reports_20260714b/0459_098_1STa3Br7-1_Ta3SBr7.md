### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0040 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore strongly favorable for synthesis. Exact-composition monoclinic Ta3SBr7 is also near the hull, E_hull ≈ 0.0049 eV/atom (CS1; SS1; TS1), which supports that the Ta–S–Br composition is thermodynamically viable. Thermodynamic analogues Ta3SeI7 and Ta3TeI7 are also low-energy but still reported with side products, so low E_hull does not guarantee clean phase formation (TS2; TS4). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target P3m1 phase is near-degenerate with the known Cm form, but CS/SS/TS do not prove which polymorph is experimentally preferred.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 550 °C, but requires vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile Br2/S handling, as directly reported for exact Ta3SBr7 (CS1; SS1; TS1). The need for purification, glovebox/vacuum transfer, flame sealing, long annealing, and possible grinding makes the practical severity higher than a simple solid-state oxide reaction (CS1; SS1; TS1). No high pressure or >1000 °C heating is needed, which prevents the score from being lower (CS1; SS1; TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a simpler open-vessel route exists, so the vacuum/sealed system penalty is retained.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The exact synthesis from Ta foil, S8, and Br2 demonstrates feasibility but also slow kinetics, requiring about two weeks at 550 °C (CS1; SS1; TS1). Ta metal is refractory and likely limits reaction kinetics [Reasonable Inference], while Br2 and S8 provide high chemical activity and volatile transport that partially compensates, consistent with synthesis far below Ta’s melting point (CS1; SS1; TS1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the known analogue already has the same composition and very similar edge-sharing TaSBr5 sheet motif (CS1; SS1; TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether kinetics favor the reported monoclinic structure rather than the desired trigonal P3m1 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the same sealed-halogen and vacuum requirements as Route 1, with added temperature-gradient control (CS1; SS1; TS1). Nb3Br8 transport uses high vacuum, Br2/I2, and a 500–760 °C gradient, showing that structurally similar layered bromides can be transported but under controlled conditions (SS2; TS3). The route does not require high pressure or extreme temperature, but volatile halogens and multi-step growth/purification justify a moderate severity penalty. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that P3m1-Ta3SBr7 can be selectively grown by CVT, so the severity score is not improved for selectivity.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport improves mass transfer relative to purely solid-state diffusion, and this is supported by Nb3Br8 purification via chemical transport and Ta3SBr7 crystal growth in a temperature gradient (SS2; TS3; CS1; SS1; TS1). However, exact Ta3SBr7 gradient growth took six weeks and yielded only small crystals, indicating slow nucleation/growth kinetics (CS1; SS1; TS1). The structural pathway is plausible because layered edge-sharing bromide frameworks are common to Ta3SBr7 and Nb3Br8 analogues (CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS3). The key uncertainty is composition control during transport, especially Br/S ratio and possible TaBr5 volatility.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids direct Br2 dosing but introduces TaBr5, which is air-sensitive and likely volatile; related Ta/Nb pentahalide syntheses require dry glovebox handling and sealed tubes (CS3; CS4; SS5). Temperatures of 500–650 °C are inferred from exact Ta3SBr7 at 550 °C and related Ta chloride frameworks at 600–800 °C, so no extreme-temperature penalty applies (CS1; CS4; SS5; TS1). The route is operationally simpler than Route 1 if TaBr5 can be weighed and sealed under Ar, but air sensitivity remains significant (CS4; SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly validates TaBr5/Ta/S for Ta3SBr7, so uncertainty is mainly chemical-transfer rather than equipment severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

TaBr5 should provide a more reactive halide source than elemental Ta + Br2 alone [Reasonable Inference], consistent with related metal + pentahalide reactions forming reduced halide frameworks (CS3; CS4; SS5). Ta metal still imposes a diffusion/redox limitation [Reasonable Inference], but volatile or molten halide species can promote transport, consistent with low-temperature halide framework formation in references (CS1; CS4; SS5; TS1). Structural reconstruction is less certain than Route 1 because no source directly reports Ta3SBr7 from TaBr5/Ta/S; it is inferred from related Ta/Nb halide chemistry (CS3; CS4; SS5). The score is therefore only moderate despite potentially improved precursor activity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Ta, so there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch; exact Ta3SBr7 also contains only Ta as the metal cation (CS1; SS1; TS1). The structural motif is a relatively simple layered edge-sharing TaSBr5 network, not a large multi-cation framework; this is supported by the exact analogue’s two-dimensional sheet description (CS1; SS1; TS1). Mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity is not indicated for the target or exact analogue; both emphasize edge-sharing Ta-centered polyhedra (CS1; SS1; TS1). Remaining uncertainty is stacking/order polymorphism, not amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is very likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable polymorph because its E_hull is only 0.0040 eV/atom, and the exact-composition Ta3SBr7 analogue is also near the hull (CS1; SS1; TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis of the Ta3SBr7 composition, since the exact formula has already been made by direct elemental reaction (CS1; SS1; TS1). The most important enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, which allows refractory Ta to react at only ~450–650 °C in related layered Ta/Nb halides (CS1; SS2; SS3; TS1; TS2; TS3). The main limiting factor is phase and polymorph control: exact Ta3SBr7 synthesis reports TaBr5 impurity and difficult crystal growth, and related Ta3QI7 phases are not obtained as pure phases (CS1; SS1; TS1; SS3; TS2; TS4). Overall, the material is synthesizable, but isolating the trigonal P3m1 polymorph rather than the known monoclinic Cm polymorph will require careful control of Br chemical potential, temperature gradient, and cooling/quenching protocol.
