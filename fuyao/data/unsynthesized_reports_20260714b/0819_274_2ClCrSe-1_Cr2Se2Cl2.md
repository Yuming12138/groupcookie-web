### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0130 eV/atom, placing it close to the convex hull and inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability band. TS provides useful context: NaCr₅Se₈ at 0.0143 eV/atom (TS1) and CrSe₂ at 0.0154 eV/atom (TS3) are comparable low-above-hull materials with reported synthesis routes. CS also supports that related layered chalcogenide halides can be thermodynamic ground states, e.g., CrSBr and NbSe₂Cl₂ have E_hull = 0 (CS1, CS2). Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be a near-hull metastable polymorph rather than the equilibrium Cr–Se–Cl phase.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT

The CVT route avoids high pressure and uses temperatures below 1000 °C, consistent with CrSBr CVT at 880 °C (CS1, SS1) and NbSe₂Cl₂ CVT at 400–515 °C (CS2). Deductions are mainly for vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions, volatile halogen/chalcogen transport species, and toxic or air-sensitive precursors, supported by S₂Br₂ in CS1 and Cl₂/MoCl₅-type chemistry in TS4. Insufficient evidence from SS2–SS5 for a direct CrSeCl CVT route beyond the CrSBr structural analogue, so the severity score remains moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT

Cr metal is kinetically sluggish, but CS1 demonstrates that reactive chalcogen-halogen vapor can convert Cr into a layered Cr chalcogenide halide. Vapor transport reduces diffusion barriers, as shown by the moderate temperatures in CS1 and CS2. The structure is similar but not identical: CrSBr is orthorhombic with CrS₄Br₂ octahedra (CS1, SS1), while the target is trigonal CrSe₃Cl₃, so ordering/reconstruction uncertainty remains.

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux

The flux route has no high-pressure penalty and no >1000 °C penalty because TS3 uses 780 °C. It is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Se/Cl chemistry, multi-step precursor/flux preparation, and air-sensitive chloride handling; these requirements are consistent with sealed carbon-coated fused-silica tubes in TS3 and chloride handling issues inferred from TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS that LiCl/KCl flux directly produces CrSeCl, so the route is more demanding than a demonstrated synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux

TS3 is the strongest kinetic analogue because CrSe₂ has the same omega-like, 2D, edge-sharing Cr–Se octahedral motif and crystallizes from LiCl/KCl flux. The molten flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth compared with direct solid-state reaction, supported by TS3’s phase-pure single-crystal growth. The main uncertainty is whether Cl substitutes/order into the sheet rather than remaining as flux or forming CrCl₃; CS2 supports layered Se–Cl compounds, but not this exact CrSeCl stoichiometry.

#### F2 — Route 3: Direct Sealed-Tube Solid-State

Direct sealed-tube reaction is experimentally simple and does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, consistent with SS4 and CS3. It still requires vacuum sealing and retention of volatile Se/Cl species, and likely uses air-sensitive/toxic chlorine donors. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct synthesis of CrSeCl specifically, so the route is not scored as easy despite simple equipment.

#### F3 — Route 3: Direct Sealed-Tube Solid-State

SS2/TS2 show Cr–Se binaries can form by direct reaction, and SS4 shows ampoule sintering of chalcogenides at 600–1000 °C is feasible. However, elemental Cr has slow diffusion, and ordered mixed-anion CrSeCl formation likely competes with Cr–Se binaries and Cr chlorides. CS1 reports CrSBr synthesis forms CrBr₃ and Cr₂S₃ by-products, and TS4 shows multiple metal chloride phases under similar chloride vapor conditions, supporting a lower kinetics/phase-control score.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation and a simple 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet, so the listed amorphization triggers are not activated by the target structure. SS2 contains mixed edge/corner/face-sharing CrSe₆ octahedra, but that connectivity is not present in the target, so it is not used to penalize F4. The low atom-count and simple connectivity suggest low intrinsic amorphization risk; uncertainty remains for thin films or very rapid quenching, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CrSeCl is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.013 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as NaCr₅Se₈ and CrSe₂ (TS1, TS3). The synthesis case is supported most strongly by CrSBr as a chemically and structurally related Cr chalcogenide halide made by CVT (CS1, SS1), and by omega-like CrSe₂ grown from LiCl/KCl flux (TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to CrSe₂, which suggests that a Cr–Se octahedral sheet can form and then be chemically tuned toward mixed Se/Cl coordination. The main limiting factor is phase competition among Cr–Se binaries, Cr chlorides, and possibly other Cr–Se–Cl stoichiometries, as seen by by-products in CrSBr synthesis (CS1) and multiple chloride phases in Mo chloride transport (TS4). Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with the best chance coming from flux-assisted or vapor-transport growth under tightly controlled halogen chemical potential.
