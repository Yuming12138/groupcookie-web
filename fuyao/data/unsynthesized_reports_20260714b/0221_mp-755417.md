### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, so it sits exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff in the rubric; this supports F1 = 90. CS/SS provide strong positive analogues because Sr₃B₂O₆, the isostructural R-3c analogue, has E_hull = 0.0 (CS-4, SS-5), and Ba₂Ca(BO₃)₂ is also stable with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1). TS supports that phases in the 0.05–0.08 eV/atom range can be synthesized, e.g., Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ at 0.067 eV/atom (TS-1) and RbPrNb₂O₇ at 0.056 eV/atom (TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that Ba₃(BO₃)₂ may be slightly metastable relative to other Ba–B–O compounds, and CS/SS do not directly report this exact Ba phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because the closest structural analogue Sr₃B₂O₆ forms by standard air solid-state sintering below 1300 K (CS-4, SS-5), and Ba₂Ca(BO₃)₂ forms by ceramic heating near 1000 °C with only a Pt crucible (SS-1). No high pressure, reactive atmosphere, or exotic equipment is indicated by these analogues (CS-4, SS-1, SS-5). The only severity deduction is multi-step grinding/calcination/regrinding, supported by SS-1 and TS-3. Insufficient evidence from TS for Ba–borate-specific condition severity, so TS is neutral here.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because carbonate/boric acid ceramic reactions are standard but rely on solid-state diffusion; SS-1 required preheating, regrinding, and 24 h final firing. The exact Sr analogue in R-3c strongly lowers structural reconstruction risk (CS-4, SS-5). The main uncertainty is whether Ba₃(BO₃)₂ competes with other Ba borates or Ba-rich phases during decarbonation [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides general evidence that repeated grinding improves ceramic reaction completion (TS-3), but insufficient evidence from TS for Ba–borate-specific kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because flux growth often needs slow cooling and post-growth washing, as shown by BaRuO₃ flux growth (CS-5). Excess H₃BO₃ flux is directly used in Ba borate chloride analogues at 1000 °C (SS-2/SS-3). A toxicity deduction is included if BaCl₂ flux is used, and a high-temperature deduction is included if the flux route reaches above 1000 °C. TS provides washing precedent from TS-5, but insufficient evidence from TS for borate flux severity specifically.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve kinetics by providing a liquid-mediated transport path [Reasonable Inference], consistent with BaCl₂ flux growth in CS-5 and excess H₃BO₃ flux use in SS-2/SS-3. However, chloride flux can stabilize Ba borate chloride structures instead of the target oxide, as demonstrated by Ba₂Ln(BO₃)₂Cl analogues (SS-2 to SS-4). Thus F3 is better than Route 1 for transport but not decisively better for phase selectivity. Insufficient evidence from TS for Ba₃(BO₃)₂ flux crystallization specifically.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for sealed/dry handling and air-sensitive BaO use [Reasonable Inference], even though the temperature can be lower than Route 1. CS-1 shows a sealed Ar Ni-bomb reaction of CsO₀.₅/B₂O₃ at 600 °C for 38 d, supporting the concept but also showing additional handling complexity. SS-1 supports B₂O₃ use in Ba borates, but not sealed BaO/B₂O₃ synthesis. TS gives insufficient evidence for BaO/B₂O₃ sealed synthesis and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Direct BaO/B₂O₃ avoids CO₂ release and should be chemically active [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 supports oxide + B₂O₃ borate formation. The kinetic score is limited because CS-1 required 38 d, suggesting slow ordering or crystal growth under sealed low-temperature conditions. SS-5 provides structural transferability through the R-3c Sr analogue, but the exact Ba direct-oxide route is not demonstrated. TS provides insufficient route-specific kinetic evidence and is neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a very large alkaline-earth cation with small trigonal BO₃ groups, so radius mismatch is substantial [Reasonable Inference from crystal chemistry]. R-3c conventional cells for A₃(BO₃)₂-type structures are likely to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference], triggering the unit-cell-size criterion. Mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral coexistence is not evident from the target description, unlike some perovskite-like CS/TS examples; therefore that check is not triggered. The major uncertainty is whether Ba-rich composition suppresses glass formation sufficiently; borate systems can vitrify, but crystalline Sr₃B₂O₆ forms by solid-state firing (CS-4/SS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₃(BO₃)₂ is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and TS shows several experimentally synthesized phases in the ~0.05–0.08 eV/atom range (TS-1, TS-5). The strongest support comes from the isostructural R-3c Sr₃B₂O₆ analogue, which has the same BO₃ trigonal planar motif and alkaline-earth coordination environment and is synthesized by standard solid-state sintering in air (CS-4, SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust isolated BO₃ structural chemistry and the availability of reactive H₃BO₃/B₂O₃ precursors, supported by SS-1 and SS-5. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control in a Ba-rich borate system where B₂O₃ volatility and competing borate phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference supported by excess B₂O₃/H₃BO₃ use in SS-1 to SS-3]. Overall, the target should be synthesizable in bulk powder form with conventional ceramic methods, while single crystals may require flux optimization.
