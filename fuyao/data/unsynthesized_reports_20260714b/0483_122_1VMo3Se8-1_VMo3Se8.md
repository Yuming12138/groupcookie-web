### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0190 eV/atom places it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic category, giving 95/100 by the provided rubric. This is consistent with thermodynamic analogues near the hull, including MoSe₂ at ~0.003–0.004 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2), EuMo₆Se₈ at 0.029 eV/atom (TS-4), and NbSe₂ at 0.025 eV/atom (TS-5). Chemically similar phases span stable to modestly metastable values, with CS-1 and CS-4 on the hull but CS-2 at 0.146 eV/atom, indicating that impurity/ordering challenges can occur even when related chemistries are accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS is the exact VMo₃Se₈ composition, so the 0.019 eV/atom prediction must be treated as a strong but not definitive stability signal.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated quartz-tube solid-state conditions, strongly supported by CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5 and TS-5. The main deductions are for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile Se, and the likely need for multiple grinding/annealing steps because CS-2/CS-4 report persistent minor transition-metal selenide impurities. The route avoids high pressure and can likely remain below 1000 °C, supported by 800 °C synthesis in CS-2/CS-4 and 600 °C synthesis in CS-1. Insufficient evidence from SS for the exact V–Mo–Se direct solid-state route, but SS-1/SS-2 support the relevant layered MoSe₂ structural motif.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetic penalty for Route 1 comes from refractory Mo and V starting metals [Reasonable Inference], even though elemental routes are common in CS-1 to CS-5 and TS-5. Formation of a layered molybdenite-derived structure is favorable because MoSe₂ analogues share similar Mo–Se coordination and ~2.53 Å Mo–Se bonds (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). However, V/Mo ordering and avoidance of binary VSeₓ/MoSe₂/NbSe₂-like competing phases are likely slow, consistent with impurity and ordering issues in CS-2, CS-4 and CS-5. The remaining uncertainty is whether VMo₃Se₈ forms congruently or only in a narrow chemical-potential window.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because SS-2/TS-2 require 1040–1120 °C growth, vacuum ampoules, and 168–216 h growth periods. Halogen transport adds toxicity/corrosion risk: iodine is used in CS-1 and bromine in SS-3. No high pressure is required, which is favorable and consistent across SS-2, SS-3 and TS-2. The key uncertainty is the correct transport-agent concentration for maintaining V:Mo:Se = 1:3:8 rather than selectively transporting Mo or V halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by using volatile transport intermediates [Reasonable Inference], and direct evidence exists for high-quality MoSe₂-type layered crystals by vapor transport (SS-2, TS-2). The same molybdenite-like sheet motif in SS-1/SS-2/TS-1/TS-2 supports crystallization into a layered product rather than requiring a completely unrelated framework. The kinetic risk is cation ordering and composition control: V and Mo halides may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference], while complex chalcogenides in SS-3 need long heating and transport-agent control. Overall, Route 2 scores best because it offers the strongest path to crystalline ordering despite harsher conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth inherits sealed-tube and volatile-Se risks from the selenide chemistry, while PbSe flux directly reported in SS-4 introduces toxicity. The temperature may exceed 1000 °C if adapted from Mo chalcogenide single-crystal synthesis in TS-3, so a temperature deduction is applied. Flux methods also require extra steps such as pre-reaction, slow cooling, and flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for a direct V–Mo–Se flux route, so CS is neutral for this specific method.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux can improve mass transport and reduce the diffusion penalty of refractory Mo/V [Reasonable Inference], and Mo–Se crystals have been obtained by flux in SS-4. Slow cooling used for Mo chalcogenide single crystals in TS-3 supports a crystal-growth pathway. However, SS-4/SS-5 are closer to Mo₆Se₈ cluster chemistry than to the target’s molybdenite-derived sheet structure, so structural transferability is only moderate. The major uncertainty is flux compatibility: Pb/Sn/alkali species may incorporate or stabilize different competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple layered chalcogenide motif and does not show the mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity that would strongly promote amorphization; MoSe₂ analogues are layered and structurally simple (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The cation radius mismatch between V and Mo is not expected to exceed 15% in comparable chalcogenide coordination [Reasonable Inference], though CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii directly. The atom count trigger is not clearly met because the target formula contains 12 atoms and no large supercell is specified. Remaining uncertainty is local V/Mo ordering: amorphization risk is low, but antisite disorder or stacking disorder may still occur [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VMo₃Se₈ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strongly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.019 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues are similarly near-hull (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). The synthesis is well supported by analogues: chemically similar V/Nb/Se phases are made in evacuated tubes from elements (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4), and structurally/thermodynamically similar MoSe₂ sheets are made by microwave, solid-state, and CVT routes (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match to molybdenite-like MoSe₂, especially the matching Mo–Se coordination and bond lengths near 2.53 Å (SS-1, SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase purity and V/Mo ordering, because chemically similar selenides report unavoidable minor transition-metal selenide impurities and ordering difficulty (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with CVT best for crystals and sealed-tube solid-state reaction best for powder screening.
