### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0410 eV/atom, which lies in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bracket. CS provides strong support because several chemically related iodides with E_hull ≈0–0.01 eV/atom are synthesized, including Ta₃SeI₇, Ta₃TeI₇, Zr₆CI₁₂, and Zr₆BI₁₂ (CS-1 to CS-4). SS also supports near-hull halide synthesis through ZrI₂ and Zr₆BI₁₂ (SS-4, SS-5), while TS shows a thermodynamically similar synthesized iodide at 0.0477 eV/atom (TS-1). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed I/Br, and analogue hull stability does not guarantee halogen ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because it requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and volatile halogen/halide precursors, as in CS-1, CS-3, and CS-5. It avoids >1000 °C conditions, which is favorable relative to TS-3, but I₂/Br₂/HfX₄ volatility and air sensitivity add severity. CS supports the feasibility of moderate-temperature evacuated ampoules, while SS/TS support the need for inert handling in iodides. The main uncertainty is whether silica ampoules tolerate the mixed Hf halide vapor without an inner liner.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate-to-poor because Hf metal is refractory, but reactive volatile halides can accelerate transport, analogous to Zr/ZrI₄ reactions (CS-2, CS-4, SS-5). The target’s 2D octahedral sheet is structurally related to layered Ta₃QI₇ and ZrI₂ systems (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5), which improves plausibility. However, CS-1, CS-3, and CS-5 report long reaction times and phase-purity difficulties, so structural reconstruction and ordering are not expected to be rapid. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Hf mixed-halide kinetic pathway is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses established high-temperature sealed Ta/Nb-tube halide methods, but still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile halides, and air-sensitive handling (CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, SS-5, TS-1). The 750–850 °C temperature range is below the >1000 °C penalty threshold, unlike Er iodide synthesis in TS-3. Equipment severity is higher than Route 1 because Ta/Nb welding and silica jacketing are needed, as reported in SS-1, SS-3, and TS-4/TS-5. The evidence is strong for iodides but weaker for mixed iodide–bromide Hf sheets.

#### F3 — Route 2

The metal + tetrahalide route is kinetically credible because Zr iodides form from Zr/ZrI₄ in sealed tubes (CS-2, CS-4, SS-5). Diffusion remains challenging because Hf metal is refractory, and solid-state homogenization can be slow; this is consistent with 1–4 week durations in CS-2, CS-4, SS-5, and TS-1. Structural reconstruction is only partially supported because many analogues are cluster or ternary iodides rather than Hf₂I₅Br-type sheets. Insufficient evidence from TS for Hf/Zr mixed I/Br sheet formation is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has similar severity penalties to Routes 1–2 because it requires sealed vacuum/inert tubes, volatile halide sources, and air-sensitive handling, all supported by SS-5 and TS-2. It avoids external high pressure and >1000 °C, which improves feasibility relative to the harsh Er iodide route in TS-3. The temperature-gradient apparatus is specialized but standard for halide CVT, as shown by SS-5 and TS-2. The uncertainty is whether a stable transport window exists for Hf₂I₅Br rather than binary Hf halides.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT receives the best kinetic score because the vapor transport route directly overcomes slow diffusion in refractory group-4 metals; this is demonstrated for ZrI₂ from Zr/ZrI₄ under a 750/850 °C gradient (SS-5). Bromide transport chemistry is also supported by Ti(AlBr₄)₂ CVT (TS-2), though that system is chemically different. The limiting kinetic factor is composition control: mixed I/Br stoichiometry may be narrow, and impurity formation is common in CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, TS-3. CS provides general low-dimensional halide precedent but insufficient direct evidence for Hf mixed-halide CVT, so that part remains uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so cation radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk. The described structure is an ordered 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet, not a highly mixed vertex/edge/face network, and the formula unit is small. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Hf₂I₅Br, so their signal for F4 is insufficient and neutral. The low F4 risk is therefore based primarily on the target crystallographic description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂I₅Br is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.041 eV/atom, within the high-probability stability range. CS and SS strongly support the broader feasibility of low-dimensional transition-metal iodides, including 2D sheet phases and Zr/Hf-analogous metal–tetraiodide chemistry (CS-1 to CS-4, SS-5). TS supports the idea that iodides around this hull energy can be synthesized, but also warns that air sensitivity and phase purity are serious issues (TS-1, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is volatile halide transport, which can overcome the slow diffusion of refractory Hf metal, as shown for ZrI₂ CVT (SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is precise mixed I/Br stoichiometry and avoidance of competing binary Hf iodide/bromide phases, by analogy to impurity issues in CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, and TS-3.
