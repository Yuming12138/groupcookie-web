### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly within the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin that receives 90–100 [Target]. This places it at the boundary between likely ground-state-accessible and metastable, but thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.058–0.074 eV/atom were still synthesized by flux, sealed-tube, high-pressure, or ion-exchange routes [TS1–TS5]. Chemical analogues Sr₃ZnRhO₆ and Sr₃NiInO₆ are much closer to the hull, supporting that Sr–Zn/Sr–In oxides can be stable under ceramic conditions [CS4, CS1]. The main uncertainty is whether the specific triclinic P1 ordering is the equilibrium form or a low-energy metastable polymorph [Target].

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard furnace conditions around 1050–1150 °C, matching Sr₃ZnRhO₆, Sr₃NiInO₆, and SrZnO₂ syntheses [CS4, CS1, SS5]. It is not high-pressure and does not require sealed tubes, but repeated grinding/pelletizing is expected from the analogues [CS1, CS4]. The score is reduced mainly for T >1000 °C and multistep processing [CS1, CS4]. TS gives no better direct condition signal for this chemistry, so insufficient evidence from TS is treated as neutral, score 50.

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

Oxide/carbonate starting materials are robust but not very reactive, consistent with long sintering and repeated regrinding in Sr₃ZnRhO₆ and Sr₃NiInO₆ [CS4, CS1]. SrZnO₂ also required controlled heating and atmosphere, indicating nontrivial solid-state diffusion [SS5]. The target’s P1 arrangement differs from the R-3c chain oxides and binary SrZnO₂/Sr₂In₂O₅ frameworks, so reconstructive crystallization is likely [Target, CS1, SS5]. This route is reliable but kinetically slow.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 still requires a final oxide crystallization step near 950–1100 °C, consistent with Sr₃NiInO₆ and SrZnO₂ syntheses [CS1, SS5]. However, it avoids high pressure, sealed ampoules, and toxic precursors if nitrate/acetate salts are used [CS1, SS5]. The multistep gel decomposition/calcination/sintering sequence costs severity points [CS1]. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for nitrate/gel Sr–Zn–In synthesis, neutral score 50.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Nitrates/acetates and gel chemistry provide high precursor activity and better cation homogeneity, supported by Sr₃NiInO₆ nitrate processing and SrZnO₂ acetate processing [CS1, SS5]. This should reduce diffusion length compared with oxide/carbonate ceramic synthesis [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/SS5]. The target still requires crystallization of a specific low-symmetry P1 framework, so structural reconstruction remains moderate [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is whether a homogeneous gel crystallizes the target or instead forms binary SrZnO₂/Sr₂In₂O₅-type impurities [SS5, SS2].

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Flux or molten-salt processing can operate at ~850–1000 °C, supported by the 925 °C flux analogue and molten-salt ion-exchange analogue [SS1, TS1, TS5]. It avoids high pressure and can avoid toxic Tl/Hg chemistry by selecting benign fluxes [Reasonable Inference; TS1/TS2 toxic species are not required]. The multistep nature—pre-reaction, flux mixing, slow cooling, washing—reduces the score [SS1, TS5]. Chemical similarity evidence for Sr–Zn–In flux synthesis is insufficient from CS, so this route receives an expert penalty despite good numerical F2/F3.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

Liquid flux improves mass transport and can crystallize oxides below typical ceramic temperatures, as shown for flux-grown structurally/thermodynamically similar oxides [SS1, TS1]. Molten salts also enable low-temperature framework transformations in a thermodynamic analogue [TS5]. However, no CS entry demonstrates flux growth for Sr₂ZnInO₆-like chemistry, so the phase-selectivity risk is substantial. The score is therefore good but not exceptional.

#### F2: Route 4 condition severity

The melt/quench route is based on Sr₂In₂O₅ synthesis above 2000 °C using a plasma burner [SS2]. This triggers high-temperature penalties and requires specialized handling [SS2]. ZnO volatility becomes a significant inferred issue at such temperatures because ZnO-containing ceramic analogues already required stoichiometry attention and, in one case, excess ZnO [CS4, Reasonable Inference]. This makes the route challenging despite being plausible for metastable phases.

#### F3: Route 4 reaction kinetics

If fully molten, diffusion is fast, which improves the ion-transport component [SS2, Reasonable Inference]. The difficulty is controlled crystallization: Sr₂In₂O₅ melt synthesis involved rapid cooling and crystal imperfections, and adding Zn increases volatility/composition risk [SS2, CS4]. The P1 target may amorphize or segregate on quench rather than crystallize directly [Target, Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are mixed: fast transport but poor phase selection.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Sr and the smaller Zn/In sites, and the target description shows distinct Sr–O versus Zn–O/In–O bond length regimes [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The atom count in the described P1 cell is not obviously >40, so C2 is not triggered [Target]. The target description explicitly mentions corner-sharing OSr₂ZnIn tetrahedra but does not prove simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered [Target]. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium, mainly due to cation-size mismatch.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂ZnInO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable oxide because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. Chemically similar Sr–Zn and Sr–In oxides are made by standard ceramic routes at 1000–1150 °C, giving strong support for bulk synthesis feasibility [CS1, CS4, SS5]. Thermodynamic analogues show that oxides with comparable or slightly higher hull energies can be synthesized, but often require flux, sealed-tube, high-pressure, or ion-exchange methods [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of stable Sr, Zn, and In oxide/carbonate/nitrate precursors that can be reacted under standard furnace conditions [CS1, CS4, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the low-symmetry P1 target may compete with simpler SrZnO₂, Sr₂In₂O₅, ZnO, or In₂O₃-derived phases [SS5, SS2, Reasonable Inference].
