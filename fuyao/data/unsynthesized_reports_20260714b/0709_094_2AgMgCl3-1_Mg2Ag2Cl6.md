### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0060 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category by the given rubric. Thermodynamically similar halides with comparable low E_hull values were experimentally synthesized: BaZnCl4 at E_hull ≈0.0019 eV/atom by Bridgman methods (TS1), GaAlCl4 at E_hull ≈0.0048 eV/atom by melt growth (TS2), and Cu(AlCl4)2 at E_hull ≈0.0099 eV/atom by CVT (TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee the specific P-31m layered polymorph if competing MgCl2/AgCl-derived phases are kinetically favored.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures below 600 °C, consistent with SS3 and SS5/TS1, so no high-temperature penalty applies. However, sealed vacuum or dry-Ar ampoule handling is required, as in SS3, SS5/TS1, and CS5, and the chlorides must be dried and protected from moisture, consistent with hygroscopic/air-sensitive halide behavior in SS3 and CS1. The main uncertainty is whether MgAgCl3 forms before any AgCl reduction or MgCl2 segregation occurs.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chloride precursors are kinetically favorable compared with oxides; SS3 and SS5/TS1 show that dried binary chlorides can react directly to ternary chlorides. Related halides form at 250–600 °C in sealed or molten conditions (CS5, SS3, SS5/TS1), suggesting cation diffusion is not severely limiting. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target is a layered P-31m sheet rather than a simple salt mixture; no CS/SS/TS source directly confirms the MgAgCl3 topology.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth benefits from lower effective diffusion barriers but increases operational complexity. CS2, TS2, and SS5/TS1 support sealed-ampoule flux/melt growth and slow cooling, but these methods require long reaction times, dry handling, and sometimes volatile Lewis-acid chloride fluxes. The severity score is lower than Route 1 because flux loading/removal and possible flux incorporation add risk.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth has the best kinetic outlook because molten chloride media are demonstrated in CS2, CS5, TS2, and SS5/TS1. Slow cooling can promote single-crystal or layered ordering, as seen in TS2 and SS5/TS1. The uncertainty is compositional: alkali chloride or Al/Ga chloride fluxes could stabilize competing complex chlorides rather than MgAgCl3, a concern inferred from the complex phase behavior in CS2 and phase sensitivity in TS3.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires evacuated sealed tubes and a temperature gradient, as in SS1/SS2/SS4, TS3, TS5, and CS4. Optional transport additives such as I2 or chlorine sources increase volatility, toxicity, and side-reaction risk; I2 transport is supported for NbOBr3 in CS4 but not specifically for MgAgCl3. Thus the condition severity is comparable to Route 2 but with less direct chemical suitability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport can overcome solid diffusion, and low-dimensional halides are frequently grown by CVT in SS1/SS2/SS4, TS3, TS5, and CS4. However, the structural analogues in SS1/SS2/SS4 report low yields, unreacted halides, and phase-purity problems, which lowers the kinetics/phase-control score. Additionally, MgCl2 is not expected to be highly volatile under mild CVT conditions; this is a [Reasonable Inference] and is not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a small formula unit and no described face-sharing network, so the rubric’s amorphization triggers are not strongly activated. CS3 shows that VOCl3 can crystallize from an amorphous phase upon cooling, but this is a molecular oxychloride and provides insufficient evidence for MgAgCl3 amorphization. The remaining uncertainty is that mixed MgCl6 octahedra and AgAgCl3 trigonal pyramids require cation ordering in a 2D sheet, which could broaden crystallization if cooling is too rapid.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgAgCl3 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from thermodynamic and structural/chemical analogues showing that ternary halides near the hull can be synthesized in sealed ampoules, melts, fluxes, and CVT gradients (SS3, SS5/TS1, CS5, TS2, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of anhydrous chloride salts, which allows synthesis at moderate temperatures rather than refractory ceramic conditions, as seen in SS3, SS5/TS1, and CS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control: MgCl2 is hygroscopic, AgCl is photosensitive/redox-active, and the target’s Ag–Ag bonded layered structure has no direct analogue in the provided data. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the direct sealed-ampoule route preferred for first trials and flux growth preferred if single crystals are required.
