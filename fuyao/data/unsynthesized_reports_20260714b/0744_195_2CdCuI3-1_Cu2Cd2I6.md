### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0270 eV/atom, within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range, giving F1 = 94. TS is especially supportive because thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally accessible: TlCdI3 at 0.0167 eV/atom (TS1), CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (TS2), and AgTl2I3 at 0.0272 eV/atom (TS4). CS also supports feasibility through chemically similar stable or near-stable iodides such as CuGaI4 at 0 eV/atom (CS2) and TlCdI3 at 0.0167 eV/atom (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee the exact trigonal P-31m polymorph rather than competing CuI/CdI2-derived phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule melt growth / Bridgman

Condition severity is moderate because related iodides form at low to moderate temperatures, e.g., 573 K for CuGaI4 (CS2), 200 °C for TlGaI4 (CS3), and 250–350 °C for TlFeI3/TlMnI3 (CS5/TS3), but sealed dry/vacuum ampoules are needed. Kinetics are favorable because melt or softened iodide conditions enhance diffusion, and the target’s Cd–I distances match CdI2-type sheets (SS1–SS5), while Cu–I distances are close to CuGaI4 (CS2). The main uncertainty is cation ordering into the target’s CuCuI3/CdI6 layered connectivity, for which no direct CS/SS/TS analogue exists.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: low-temperature solid-state annealing

This route has lower F2 because it likely requires vacuum/Ar, sealed ampoules, toxic CdI2, and possibly repeated grinding/annealing, as repeated annealing was needed for pure TlFeI3 (CS5/TS3). Kinetically, binary iodides are reactive compared with refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and similar ternary iodides form from binary salts at 200–350 °C or 553 K (CS3, CS5, TS2). The limiting kinetic factor is solid-state Cu/Cd ordering in a layered structure, especially because CS5/TS3 show that phase purity may require repeated cycles.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: hydrothermal / solvothermal HI

F2 is relatively favorable in temperature because hydrothermal iodide syntheses operate at 155–220 °C (CS4, TS4), but the use of concentrated HI and autogenous pressure adds handling risk. F3 is strong for transport because solution-mediated growth bypasses solid diffusion, and HI successfully mineralizes iodide systems (CS4, TS4). However, CS4 reports multiphase/intergrown products and TS4 reports twinned/low-yield crystals, so the exact CuCdI3 phase may be hard to isolate cleanly.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: vapor-phase transport

F2 is moderate because vapor transport can be low-temperature but requires sealed/vacuum ampoules and uses volatile toxic iodides; SS5 directly supports vapor growth of CdI2 polytypes. F3 is lower because vapor availability is plausible for CdI2 but direct evidence for stoichiometric Cu–Cd–I vapor transport is insufficient from CS/TS. Composition control is the major concern because CuI and CdI2 may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference], risking CuI-rich or CdI2-rich deposits.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has two F4 triggers: significant cation size/coordination mismatch between Cu⁺ and Cd²⁺ [Reasonable Inference from their distinct target coordination environments], and mixed corner/edge connectivity in the target structure. SS1–SS5 show that CdI2-like sheets themselves crystallize readily and are not amorphous-prone, but the target adds Cu-centered trigonal pyramids and Cu–Cu bonding absent from pure CdI2 sheets. Therefore amorphization risk is medium, not high, because the structure is still a simple iodide network and not a very large-cell glass-former.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuCdI3 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0270 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as AgTl2I3 at 0.0272 eV/atom (TS4) and CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (TS2). The CS/TS data strongly support anhydrous sealed-ampoule synthesis of ternary iodides from binary iodides, especially CuI-containing CuGaI4 (CS2) and Cd-containing TlCdI3 (CS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of soft iodide salts, which allows crystallization at 200–550 °C rather than refractory ceramic temperatures (CS2, CS3, CS5, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: CdI2 has many layered polytypes (SS1–SS5), and related ternary iodides sometimes require repeated annealing or yield twinned/mixed products (CS5/TS3, TS4). Overall, synthesis is feasible but will require careful stoichiometry, dry sealed handling, and slow cooling/annealing to stabilize the ordered trigonal layered phase.
