### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the direct thermodynamic score is very high. TS analogues are largely also at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Pb(OH)I, Pb(OH)Br, FeH₄(OCl)₂, PNF₂, and BiOI, supporting that hydrated/oxyhalide or related phases can be true ground states (TS1–TS5). CS analogues include near-hull compounds but also one at 0.104 eV/atom, so CS gives a mixed thermodynamic signal rather than decisive support. Remaining uncertainty is computational: hydrous/proton positions and acid/base speciation can change relative stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Aqueous precipitation / slow crystallization

Condition severity is low because SS1–SS3 and TS1–TS2 demonstrate Pb hydroxyhalide precipitation from hot aqueous solutions without high pressure, high temperature, or special atmosphere. Deductions are mainly for Pb toxicity and multiple solution-handling/crystallization steps [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because aqueous acetate/chloride/phosphorous acid precursors are highly mobile and reactive; CS3 further supports slow crystallization of P–H–O salts from solution. The main uncertainty is that SS/TS analogues do not include phosphite, and CS3 does not include Pb–Cl, so combined framework assembly is inferred rather than directly proven.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-temperature hydrothermal synthesis

Hydrothermal severity is moderate because CS1 uses 180 °C autogenous pressure and SS5 uses sealed-ampoule heating, which are accessible but require pressure-compatible equipment. The route is penalized for sealed volatile aqueous/acidic conditions, multiple steps, and toxic Pb precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, hydrothermal media should dissolve PbCl₂ better and allow reconstructive crystallization of Pb–O–P–Cl frameworks [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS1 and SS5 metal–P–O–H framework growth. Uncertainty remains because TS provides no direct hydrothermal phosphite chloride example, and hydrothermal conditions may instead favor Pb hydroxychloride or phosphate impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Acidic cooling crystallization / diffusion growth

Cooling/diffusion crystallization has mild conditions, supported by CS2 at 323 K, TS3 at 70 °C, and TS5 at 18 °C diffusion growth. The severity score is limited by Pb toxicity and multistep crystallization handling [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are excellent for ion transport because all species are in solution, but structural selection is less certain than hydrothermal synthesis because TS5 reports fragile crystals and difficult phase control. The main uncertainty is whether H₃PO₃ can be incorporated before simple PbCl₂/Pb(OH)Cl crystallizes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly size- and coordination-mismatched Pb²⁺ and tetrahedral P centers, triggering the cation-radius-difference criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula has 19 atoms, and without explicit Z from CS/SS/TS the unit-cell atom count criterion is uncertain; using likely P2₁/m Z≈2 gives about 38 atoms, below the >40 threshold [Reasonable Inference]. The structural description does not clearly show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so that criterion is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, with uncertainty because no CS/SS/TS source directly evaluates glass formation or amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pb₃P₂H₅O₇Cl₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The best synthesis support comes from SS/TS Pb hydroxyhalide analogues, which show that Pb–O–Cl hydrated frameworks crystallize readily from mild aqueous solution, and from CS3, which supports aqueous crystallization of P–H–O salts. The most critical enabling factor is liquid-mediated crystallization: solution routes avoid high-temperature decomposition or loss of H/O/Cl components and allow Pb, chloride, and phosphite to reorganize at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase competition with simpler Pb hydroxychlorides or PbCl₂, because SS1–SS3/TS1–TS2 show Pb(OH)X phases form readily under related aqueous halide conditions. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, with phase-pure crystallization requiring careful pH, chloride activity, and phosphite stoichiometry control.
