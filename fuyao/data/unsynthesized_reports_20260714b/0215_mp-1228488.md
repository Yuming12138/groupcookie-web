### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score boundary in the required rubric. Stable or nearly stable Ba–Ge analogues exist, including Ba3Ge5 at E_hull = 0.0 and Ba3Ge4 near the hull at 0.0013 eV/atom (CS1, CS2; SS3, SS4). Thermodynamic analogues with similar metastability, such as BaGe2 at E_hull ≈0.0497 eV/atom, are experimentally accessible but require high-pressure synthesis in some cases (CS3, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Ba2Ge3 is an ambient-pressure metastable phase accessible by quenching or a phase that needs pressure stabilization; TS gives strong high-pressure signals, while CS/SS also support ambient sealed-melt Ba–Ge synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1

The melt route is moderately severe because Ba3Ge5 synthesis uses 1470 K under Ar in sealed Ta crucibles with controlled cooling (CS1, SS4). The main deductions come from temperature above 1000 °C, sealed containment for volatile/reactive Ba, and air-sensitive Ba/Ba–Ge handling, all supported by Ba–Ge analogue procedures (CS1–CS3). There is insufficient evidence from TS that the target specifically requires high pressure for this route, so high-pressure deductions are not applied. The uncertainty is whether the target phase crystallizes from the melt before decomposing into lower-hull Ba–Ge phases.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable compared with solid-state diffusion because melt reactions are directly reported for Ba3Ge5 and structurally similar intermetallics (CS1, SS1, SS2, SS4). Elemental precursors are not chemically activated, so precursor activity is only moderate-to-low, but liquid-phase homogenization compensates. Structural reconstruction is partial rather than minimal because the target Imm2 structure differs from the reported Cmcm/P4₂/mnm/I4₁/amd Ba–Ge analogues (CS1–CS3). Remaining uncertainty comes from whether slow cooling selects Ba2Ge3 or more stable neighboring compositions.

#### F2 — Route 2

The solid-state route inherits the Ba3Ge4 protocol: sealed Nb ampoule, 1120 K then 1360 K, intermediate grinding, and inert handling (CS2, SS3). It is more operationally complex than the melt route because it requires at least two heat treatments and grinding under inert gas (CS2, SS3). There is insufficient evidence from TS that ambient-pressure sintering stabilizes the thermodynamic class of Ba2Ge3, so this route is scored lower than melt growth. The main risks are Ba volatility, air sensitivity, and incomplete reaction.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reaction kinetics are slower because elemental Ba/Ge powders require interdiffusion and ordering in the solid or partially molten state, and Ba3Ge4 required 24 h + 24 h with intermediate grinding (CS2, SS3). Diffusion is not extremely poor because Ba melts below these temperatures and Ge is not ultra-refractory, but repeated annealing indicates kinetic barriers. The structural reconstruction score is reduced because the target is not identical to Ba3Ge4 and because phase purity in Ba–Ge systems requires careful control (CS1, CS2). The uncertainty is whether multiple annealing cycles can equilibrate Ba2Ge3 without disproportionation.

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure route is severe because thermodynamic analogues require 40 kbar/1000 °C or 13–15 GPa/1000–1300 K conditions with BN capsules, MgO pressure media, graphite heaters, and glovebox handling (CS3, TS1–TS4). These conditions trigger major deductions for pressure, multi-step handling, air sensitivity, and sealed high-temperature containment. This route is nevertheless plausible because the closest thermodynamic analogue BaGe2 has almost the same E_hull as the target and is made by HPHT treatment (TS1). The uncertainty is whether Ba2Ge3 needs the 4 GPa class of TS1 or the 13–15 GPa class of TS2–TS4.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can accelerate formation of dense Ba–Ge frameworks and stabilize metastable phases, as seen for BaGe2, BaGe3, and hp-BaGe5 (TS1–TS4). Pre-reacted Ba–Ge precursors improve kinetic access but add synthesis steps, as BaGe2(ND) and Ba6Ge25-derived routes show (CS3, TS1, TS4). Structural reconstruction is moderate because HPHT phases often form by transformation and require quenching, rather than by direct crystallization under ambient conditions (TS1, TS4). The kinetic uncertainty is whether quenching retains Ba2Ge3 or permits back-transformation/decomposition.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a binary intermetallic with a small Ba2Ge3 formula and no explicit evidence for a large, glass-forming, mixed-polyhedral framework in the target description. Structurally similar analogues can have mixed edge/face/corner connectivity, but this is not explicitly stated for Ba2Ge3, so C3 is not triggered for the target (SS1, SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40-atom target cell, and the “cation radius mismatch” check is not directly applicable to a single-cation Ba–Ge Zintl/intermetallic phase. The amorphization risk is therefore low, with the larger practical risk being competing crystalline phases rather than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba2Ge3 is likely **metastable or marginally stable**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues near this range include high-pressure Ba–Ge phases (TS1–TS4). The CS and SS data strongly support that Ba–Ge intermetallic frameworks with similar Ba–Ge and Ge–Ge distances can be synthesized from elements by sealed melt or solid-state routes (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4). The TS data, however, warns that phases in this metastability range may require HPHT stabilization or quenching, especially for Ge-rich Ba–Ge frameworks (TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is sealed inert-atmosphere synthesis with controlled cooling, because this directly addresses Ba volatility/reactivity and phase selection (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The most critical limiting factor is competition with nearby Ba–Ge phases such as Ba3Ge4, Ba3Ge5, BaGe2, BaGe3, and BaGe5, several of which are experimentally accessible and/or lower in energy (CS1–CS3, TS1–TS4).
