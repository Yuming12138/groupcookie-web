### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score boundary given in the prompt. CS analogues include stable or near-stable Sr₂BB′O₆ phases, such as Sr₂CaWO₆ at 0.0–0.0208 eV/atom [CS1, CS2] and Sr₂LnMoO₆ at 0.0 eV/atom [CS3–CS5]. TS analogues include metastable Cr perovskites with E_hull up to ~0.084 eV/atom and high-pressure synthesis requirements [TS1, TS2], so the score is high but not perfect.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The ambient ceramic route is strongly supported by CS and SS because Sr-containing perovskite-derived oxides are repeatedly made by grinding, pelletizing, and firing in air at high temperature [CS1, CS2, SS2]. Its severity is reduced by ambient pressure and standard equipment [CS1, CS2], but penalized by >1000 °C firing, multi-step processing, and the need for oxidizing Cr control [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main limitation because carbonates/oxides require long diffusion distances and analogues report repeated grinding, impurity formation, or long firing [CS1, CS2, SS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
High-pressure synthesis is supported by thermodynamic Cr perovskite analogues: BaCrO₃ at 9 GPa [TS1] and Ca/Sr chromate perovskites at 65–70 kbar [TS2]. This route receives a low F2 score because >5 GPa pressure, capsule loading, and oxygen potential control are experimentally demanding [TS1, TS2]. Kinetically it may help stabilize high-valent Cr perovskite frameworks, but TS does not directly prove ordered Sr₂LuCrO₆ formation, so uncertainty remains high.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
The sol–gel route is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so it is a [Reasonable Inference]. It is motivated by the repeated need for long firing and regrinding in ceramic analogues [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS5], which suggests that improved cation mixing should help. The route still requires controlled O₂ annealing and careful burnout to avoid reducing Cr, but its kinetic score is higher than oxide/carbonate ceramic synthesis because precursor homogeneity is much better [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a significant B-site size/charge contrast between Lu³⁺ and high-valent Cr, which can favor ordering but also increases local strain [Reasonable Inference]. The described target topology is corner-sharing LuO₆/CrO₆ octahedra, not mixed edge/face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered by the target description. Unit-cell atom count >40 is not established by the provided target structure; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LuCrO₆ is likely a **near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible** oxide because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-stability cutoff. The CS data strongly supports the feasibility of Sr₂BB′O₆ double-perovskite frameworks by ceramic routes [CS1–CS5], while SS/TS data supports the broader accessibility of Sr/Ba chromium oxides but warns that high-valent Cr perovskites can require careful atmosphere or high pressure [SS1, TS1, TS2, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match to known Sr₂BB′O₆ perovskite-derived oxides [CS1–CS5]. The most critical limiting factor is stabilization of Cr⁵⁺ without forming competing Sr–Cr oxides or reducing to Cr³⁺/Cr⁴⁺ phases [SS1, TS1, TS2, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with sol–gel/O₂ annealing recommended as the best first attempt and high-pressure synthesis as a backup if ambient-pressure attempts fail.
