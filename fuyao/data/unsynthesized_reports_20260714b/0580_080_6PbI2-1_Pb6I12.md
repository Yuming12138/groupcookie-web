### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0370 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range, so a score of 93/100 is appropriate (target). Closest CS/SS PbI₂ polytypes are nearly on the hull, with E_hull ≈ 0.0008–0.0019 eV/atom, showing that PbI₂ layered frameworks are thermodynamically robust (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). TS analogues near 0.027–0.039 eV/atom are experimentally accessible but often polymorphic or phase-control limited, suggesting the target may be metastable but still reachable (TS-1–4). The uncertainty is not bulk decomposition, but whether the monoclinic C2/m stacking variant can be isolated over more common PbI₂ polytypes (CS-3; SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1, silica-gel diffusion growth

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because PbI₂ gel growth occurs at room temperature or 273–323 K, ambient pressure, and ordinary room atmosphere with only test tubes/U-tubes and thermostat control (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). The deductions are for multi-step gel preparation/diffusion/growth and toxic lead acetate/Pb²⁺ chemistry (CS-1–4; SS-1–4; **[Reasonable Inference]** for toxicity). TS provides no useful evidence for this route, so **insufficient evidence from TS** is treated as neutral rather than negative. The resulting severity is low compared with sealed ampoule or melt routes.

#### F3 — Route 1, silica-gel diffusion growth

The kinetics are excellent because Pb²⁺ and I⁻ are supplied as soluble salts and react/diffuse through a gel, avoiding sluggish solid-state diffusion (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). The references report crystals within 2 days or over a few weeks, which supports rapid nucleation and manageable growth kinetics (CS-1, CS-3; SS-3, SS-4). Structural reconstruction is minimal because the products are chemically identical layered PbI₂ analogues with edge-sharing PbI₆ octahedra and similar Pb–I distances (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). The main kinetic uncertainty is slow ordering of the specific monoclinic stacking sequence, not formation of PbI₂ itself (target; CS-3; SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule melt growth

Route 2 is penalized for evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile iodine/PbI₂ vapor control, multi-step heating/slow cooling/annealing, and toxic Pb-containing feedstock (SS-5; TS-5; **[Reasonable Inference]** for Pb/I volatility and toxicity). The temperature is still moderate, since TlPbI₃ melt growth uses 870 K and inferred PbI₂ melt recrystallization would be below 1000 °C (SS-5; TS-5; **[Reasonable Inference]**). CS gives no direct melt-growth evidence for PbI₂, so **insufficient evidence from CS** is neutral. The route is feasible but operationally harsher than gel growth.

#### F3 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule melt growth

Melt growth gives rapid diffusion and good crystallinity potential, as TlPbI₃ is synthesized by direct melting of iodides in evacuated quartz ampoules and then grown by controlled thermal gradients (SS-5; TS-5). PbI₂ as a feedstock should be kinetically accessible in the melt **[Reasonable Inference]**, but it may recrystallize into common PbI₂ polytypes rather than the target monoclinic variant. PbI₂ polytypism and the need for careful conditions are indicated in the gel-growth PbI₂ references (CS-3; SS-3). Thus F3 is good but not as high as Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3, sealed-ampoule solid-state/CVT

Route 3 is penalized for sealed Ar/vacuum handling, volatile iodine or iodide transport, multi-step loading/sealing/heating/cooling, and toxic/volatile reagents (TS-1–4; **[Reasonable Inference]** for Pb/I₂ hazards). TS analogues use moderate temperatures such as 272 °C, 558 K, and 550–650 °C, so no high-temperature deduction applies (TS-1–4). SS-5 supports the use of evacuated ampoules for Pb–I iodide systems, while CS gives no direct sealed-tube route; **insufficient evidence from CS** is neutral. Severity is therefore moderate-to-high relative to gel growth.

#### F3 — Route 3, sealed-ampoule solid-state/CVT

Vapor transport improves kinetics compared with purely solid-state diffusion because iodine and metal iodides can migrate in sealed ampoules and temperature gradients (TS-1–4). However, starting from Pb metal + I₂ or recrystallizing PbI₂ is less uniformly mixed than solution gel chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS analogues also show multiple modifications, narrow stability windows, twinning, and mixed products, which lowers the structural-ordering score (TS-1–3). The route is plausible but more phase-selection sensitive than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Pb, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered (target). The structure is layered and dominated by edge-sharing Pb–I polyhedra, matching close CS/SS PbI₂ analogues and avoiding highly mixed connectivity that often promotes glassy/disordered products (target; CS-1–4; SS-1–4). Exact unit-cell atom count is not fully established from the supplied data, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is treated neutrally for C2, but the described inequivalent sites do not indicate an obviously very large cell. The amorphization risk is therefore low; the more relevant risk is crystalline polytype competition rather than amorphous product formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PbI₂ phase is likely a low-energy metastable polytype rather than the unique ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0370 eV/atom while several chemically identical PbI₂ analogues lie essentially on the hull (target; CS-1–4; SS-1–4). The CS and SS datasets strongly support synthesis of layered PbI₂ under very mild gel-growth conditions, including chemically identical compounds with closely matching PbI₆ edge-sharing networks and Pb–I bond lengths (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). TS analogues support the broader idea that heavy-metal iodide phases near this metastability range can be synthesized, but they also warn that polymorphism and phase control can be difficult (TS-1–4). The most critical enabling factor is the very high chemical/structural match to already grown PbI₂ polytypes in silica gel (CS-1–4; SS-1–4). The most critical limiting factor is selective stabilization of the monoclinic C2/m stacking variant against other PbI₂ polytypes, especially under conditions where multiple polytypes may nucleate (CS-3; SS-3).
