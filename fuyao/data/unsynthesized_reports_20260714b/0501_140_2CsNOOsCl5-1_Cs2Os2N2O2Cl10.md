### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility. TS analogues include Cs₂RhOCl₅ at 0.0037 eV/atom (TS1) and Rb₂PtCl₆ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS2), indicating that related alkali/transition-metal halide frameworks can be thermodynamically stable. CS evidence is less consistent because CS1 and CS2 have high computed hull energies but are experimentally crystallized, so CS is useful for synthetic route evidence but less decisive for thermodynamic scoring. Remaining uncertainty is mainly DFT treatment of Os nitrosyl bonding and possible hydration/solvation competition **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because SS1/TS1, CS2, and SS5 all support mild solution crystallization without high temperature, high pressure, or specialized atmospheres. The only major deduction is toxic Os/nitrosyl chloride chemistry, which is not directly present in the sources but is a necessary physicochemical risk for osmium compounds **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact toxicity profile of the proposed Os precursor, so this deduction is based on chemical knowledge rather than source data.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization avoids long-range cation diffusion, as demonstrated by slow evaporation routes in SS1/TS1 and CS2. Use of a preformed [OsNOCl₅]⁻ precursor should reduce structural reconstruction to counterion exchange and crystallization **[Reasonable Inference]**. The remaining uncertainty is whether the preformed Os nitrosyl chloride anion is stable under the selected HCl concentration and evaporation conditions; no CS/SS/TS source directly proves this for Os.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because in situ nitrosylation adds multiple chemical steps and potential volatile NOx/NOCl handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS3 supports room-temperature nitrosyl/chloride crystallization chemistry for Ru, and TS5 supports hot HCl cooling crystallization, so the temperature and pressure severity remain low. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Os nitrosylation conditions, so the route is penalized for uncertainty and safety.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution kinetics remain good because Ru nitrosyl chloride complexes form/crystallize from aqueous media in SS3, and Cs chloride complexes crystallize from HCl in SS1/SS5. However, the key Os–NO–Cl₅ unit must be generated in solution, which increases reconstruction complexity relative to Route 1 **[Reasonable Inference]**. Uncertainty is moderate because osmium can access multiple oxidation states and volatile OsO₄ under oxidizing conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses low solvothermal temperatures analogous to the 450 K hydrohalic ampoule synthesis in TS4, so it avoids high-temperature or high-pressure penalties. It is penalized for sealed volatile acidic media, multiple handling steps, and toxic Os/nitrosyl chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for sealed-tube Os nitrosyl chloride synthesis specifically, so this route is lower-confidence than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solvothermal acid media should improve dissolution and recrystallization relative to dry solid-state methods, as TS4 demonstrates for Cs/Pd hydrohalide crystallization. If preformed [OsNOCl₅]⁻ is used, the structural reconstruction burden remains modest **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether heating accelerates decomposition or ligand substitution of the Os–NO unit rather than improving crystallization **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Cs⁺ and the Os nitrosyl chloride center, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target formula and structure description do not indicate a very large unit cell or explicitly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered; however, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents a fully certain C2 assignment. Solution crystallization routes in SS1/TS1 and CS2 mitigate amorphization risk by allowing ordered crystal growth under mild conditions.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsOsNOCl₅ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase**, because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues include stable chloride frameworks such as Cs₂RhOCl₅ and Rb₂PtCl₆ (TS1, TS2). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/TS data: Cs₂RhOCl₅ is made by CsCl + hydrated RhCl₃ in 3 M HCl followed by slow evaporation (SS1, TS1), closely matching the target’s Cs/chloride layered coordination environment. CS and SS evidence also support nitrosyl/chloride solution chemistry through KPtNOCl₃ and Ru–NO chloride/nitro complexes (CS2, SS3), though neither is an Os compound. The most critical enabling factor is **high-chloride acidic solution crystallization**, which stabilizes metal chloride complexes and promotes Cs salt crystallization (SS1, SS5, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is **control of osmium nitrosyl speciation and safety**, because Os/nitrosyl reagents are toxic and no direct Os analogue is provided in CS/SS/TS **[Reasonable Inference]**.
