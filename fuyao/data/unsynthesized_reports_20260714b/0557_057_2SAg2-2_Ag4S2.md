### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0340 eV/atom, which falls within the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring range. [Target] Thermodynamically similar Ag–Cu–S analogues have comparable low hull energies, e.g., 0.0306–0.0462 eV/atom, and are reported as synthesizable [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3]. This supports metastable but realistic accessibility rather than a highly unstable hypothetical phase. [TS]

#### F2 — Route 1

Direct elemental sealed-tube synthesis uses standard equipment, as Ag3CuS2 is synthesized from elements in evacuated sealed tubes [SS-4, TS-1]. The main severity deductions are vacuum/sealed operation and sulfur volatility, consistent with chalcogenide sealed-tube practices [CS-1, CS-4]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Ag2S Pmma formation specifically, so route transferability is chemically inferred. [CS neutral for polymorph selectivity]

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic limitation is Ag metal reacting with sulfur and then reconstructing into the desired polymorph. [SS-4, TS-1; Reasonable Inference] Related Ag–Cu–S materials can form by solid-state methods, but long anneals/mechanical activation are sometimes needed [SS-3, TS-2]. This suggests moderate, not easy, kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids elemental sulfur handling if commercial Ag2S is used, but vacuum or inert sealed annealing is still preferred based on Ag-containing sulfide/halosulfide analogues [CS-3, SS-5, TS-3]. The method requires multiple thermal-history steps, as Ag3SI phase selection uses regrinding, pelletizing, quenching/annealing, and prolonged low-temperature anneal [CS-3, SS-5, TS-5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact Pmma Ag2S annealing window, so the temperature program is a reasonable inference.

#### F3 — Route 2

Starting from Ag2S gives excellent precursor activity because the composition is already correct [CS-2, CS-3, TS-3]. The main kinetic task is polymorph rearrangement, not long-range multication diffusion, so kinetics are favorable. However, phase-selection uncertainty remains because Ag3SI analogues show narrow thermal-history dependence and long annealing for ordered phases [CS-3, SS-5, TS-5].

#### F2 — Route 3

Mechanical activation adds processing steps but not extreme temperature or high pressure [SS-3, TS-2]. Vacuum/inert atmosphere is required during annealing/milling to avoid oxidation or sulfur loss [SS-3, TS-2]. The route is more complex than Route 2 due to milling and contamination control. [SS-3]

#### F3 — Route 3

Mechanical alloying improved high-purity Ag3CuS2 preparation [SS-3, TS-2], so it plausibly improves Ag2S polymorph access. Diffusion lengths are reduced and low-temperature annealing becomes more effective [SS-3, TS-2; Reasonable Inference]. The risk is that excessive milling can amorphize or contaminate the product, which is not directly quantified in CS/SS/TS but follows from the milling media used in SS-3.

#### F2 — Route 4

Supercritical ethylenediamine synthesis is more severe because it requires high-pressure autoclave conditions, Ar counterpressure, sealed quartz tubes, and toxic/flammable solvent [SS-1, TS-4]. It is not multi-GPa, so no high-pressure penalty under the rubric. Insufficient evidence from CS for this route; support comes mainly from SS/TS. [CS neutral]

#### F3 — Route 4

The kinetics are attractive because supercritical solvent transport bypasses slow solid diffusion and enabled crystalline KAg3S2 at 300 °C [SS-1, TS-4]. The structural motif is relevant: KAg3S2 has bent 150° two-coordinate Ag–S units similar to the target Ag(2) geometry [SS-1; Target]. The uncertainty is compositional transferability, because the analogue contains K and a different framework topology [SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 5

Chemical bath deposition is mild in temperature and pressure, as CdS films were made at 80 °C in air [CS-5]. Deductions arise from multi-step deposition/annealing and toxic sulfur precursors such as thiourea [CS-5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for solution deposition of Ag2S specifically, so this route is less directly supported by structural/thermodynamic analogues.

#### F3 — Route 5

Solution precursors are highly reactive, and CS-5 supports metal-sulfide thin-film formation from soluble salts and thiourea. However, CS-5 also reports mixed cubic/hexagonal CdS and impurities, indicating phase purity can be difficult. For Ag2S, rapid Ag+–S2− precipitation likely makes composition and nucleation control critical. [CS-5; Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern. [Target] The target description reports simple Ag–S coordination rather than multiple coexisting vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, unlike complex analogues such as Cu9S5 or Cu7Te4 [CS-1, CS-4]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so that factor is treated as non-triggered based on the small number of inequivalent sites in the target description. [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Ag2S Pmma phase is likely metastable rather than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0340 eV/atom, though this is low enough to be experimentally accessible. [Target] The strongest support comes from TS and SS analogues: Ag–Cu–S and K–Ag–S materials with similar Ag–S coordination and comparable hull energies are synthesized under sealed-tube or solvothermal conditions [SS-1, SS-3, SS-4; TS-1–TS-4]. The key enabling factor is that Ag2S itself is a simple binary with mobile Ag+ chemistry and can be used directly as a target-composition precursor [CS-2, CS-3, TS-3]. The key limiting factor is polymorph selection: related Ag-containing sulfides/halosulfides require careful quenching, annealing, or long equilibration to stabilize specific phases [CS-3, SS-5, TS-5]. Overall, synthesis is feasible, but the exact Pmma layered structure will likely require thermal-history screening or thin-film/nanocrystalline stabilization.
