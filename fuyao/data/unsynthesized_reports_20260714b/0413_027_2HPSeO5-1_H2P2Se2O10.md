### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score. Stable or near-stable analogues are common in the reference sets: MgH2(SeO4)2 has E_hull = 0 in CS-2/TS-2, InHSeO4 has E_hull = 0 in SS-5/TS-1, and LiHSeO3 has E_hull = 0 in TS-3. CS-1 is a contradictory high-hull outlier with E_hull ≈ 1.04 eV/atom, but its synthesis evidence is chemically inconsistent with the listed formula and is therefore weighted less heavily. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or hydration-state stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis uses moderate temperatures, typically 70–230 °C in the relevant analogues (SS-4, SS-5/TS-1, SS-2, TS-5), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Autogenous pressure is required but not >5 GPa, and standard Teflon-lined autoclaves are used in SS-5/TS-1 and TS-5. The main penalties are toxic selenium precursors and volatility/containment of SeO2/H2SeO3. Insufficient evidence from CS for a direct phosphate–selenite hydrothermal analogue is treated neutrally in judging exact transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because soluble H3PO4 and SeO2/H2SeO3 species can react through solution, avoiding refractory solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with hydrothermal selenite formation in SS-5/TS-1 and TS-5. Structural reconstruction is moderate because related 2D or protonated selenite frameworks are known (SS-2, SS-5), but PHSeO5 itself is not directly represented. The uncertainty is whether phosphate and selenite condense into the desired sheet instead of forming separate phosphoric/selenious acid hydrates.

#### F2 — Route 2 slow evaporation

Route 2 has the mildest conditions: room temperature to ~370 K slow evaporation is directly demonstrated for hydrogen selenites (CS-5/SS-1, TS-3). No pressure vessel, vacuum, or reactive atmosphere is required in those analogues. The main severity deduction is for selenium toxicity, especially if SeO2 is used. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for an exact P-containing analogue means the condition severity is still low, but phase selectivity remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2 slow evaporation

Reaction kinetics are very favorable because all components can be molecular or ionic in aqueous solution, and analogues crystallize by slow evaporation without high-temperature diffusion (CS-5/SS-1, TS-3). The limiting kinetic factor is nucleation of a mixed P–O–Se sheet rather than separate molecular acid phases [Reasonable Inference]. Slow evaporation allows local ordering and hydrogen-bond equilibration, which supports high F3. Remaining uncertainty is the absence of a direct PHSeO5 crystallization precedent in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3 concentrated acid crystallization

Concentrated acid crystallization is supported by hydrogen selenate and sulfate analogues at 120–200 °C-scale conditions (CS-2/TS-2, CS-3). It avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature but introduces handling severity from concentrated acids and selenium compounds. A dry atmosphere may be useful by analogy to hygroscopic sulfate crystallization under dry N2 (CS-3), but air is sufficient for some selenate concentration syntheses (CS-2/TS-2). The route is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 3 concentrated acid crystallization

Kinetics benefit from liquid acid transport and high precursor activity, as shown by crystallization from concentrated sulfuric/selenic acid media (CS-2/TS-2, CS-3). Low water activity may promote condensation into an anhydrous or low-hydration PHSeO5 framework [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural reconstruction and phase selectivity are less certain because the cited acid-crystallized analogues are hydrogen selenates/sulfates, not mixed phosphate–selenites. Insufficient direct evidence from SS for this exact concentrated-acid P–Se chemistry is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 4 sealed-tube solid-state / flux

Sealed-tube synthesis is more severe because it requires evacuated quartz ampoules, volatile SeO2 containment, and multiple handling steps, as seen for high-temperature selenite solid-state synthesis in TS-4. Although TS-4 uses 750–800 °C, the target likely requires lower temperature to retain H [Reasonable Inference]. The route also inherits risks of toxic SeO2 and possible pressure buildup. CS-1 gives weak support for flux/sealed growth, but its synthesis evidence is not actually for the listed H–Se–N–O material, so it is weighted cautiously.

#### F3 — Route 4 sealed-tube solid-state / flux

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable than solution routes because forming a protonated P–Se oxide sheet from dry precursors requires extensive reconstruction. TS-4 shows that sealed-tube selenite solid-state synthesis is possible but may need rigorous conditions and impurity removal. A flux could improve transport, but high temperature risks SeO2 loss and target dehydration [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore F3 is moderate-low relative to solution routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically distinct P(V) tetrahedra and Se(IV) lone-pair polyhedra, so cation size/coordination mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description does not provide a unit-cell atom count, so C2 cannot be confidently triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for this exact unit-cell-size factor is treated neutrally. The target description shows bridging connectivity but not edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, consistent with many crystalline protonated selenite/selenate analogues in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PHSeO5 is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of protonated selenite/selenate oxides by aqueous, hydrothermal, slow-evaporation, and acid-crystallization methods, especially CS-5/SS-1, TS-3, SS-5/TS-1, and CS-2/TS-2. The most critical enabling factor is that both phosphate and selenite chemistry can be accessed through highly reactive soluble oxoacid precursors under mild acidic conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the system may crystallize separate phosphoric/selenious acid hydrates, isolated hydrogen selenites, or Se(VI)-containing products if oxidation/water activity is not controlled. Overall, the best strategy is mild slow evaporation first, followed by acidic hydrothermal screening if nucleation is poor.
