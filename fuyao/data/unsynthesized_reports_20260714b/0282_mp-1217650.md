### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold for the highest stability bin, so it is likely accessible but may be metastable. Thermodynamically similar rare-earth chalcogenides with E_hull values around 0.046–0.065 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable phases in TS1–TS3 and TS5, supporting the idea that phases in this energy range can be isolated. However, the target is not reported as a known ground state in the provided data, so the score is kept at 90 rather than near 100. CS and SS provide examples of stable or near-stable related phases, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS proves the target is a true ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized heavily because SS1 and SS4 use 1420 K, evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, long annealing, and quenching. The use of Se in sealed ampoules implies volatility management, and Tb metal handling requires inert conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The route is standard for chalcogenide solid-state synthesis but operationally demanding. CS provides lower-temperature alternatives, so Route 1 is not the least severe option.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from the fact that Tb3CuGeSe7 and Y3CuGeSe7 frameworks form from elemental mixtures under similar sealed-tube conditions (SS1, SS4). However, SS1/SS4 require long anneals and report minority phases for some compositions, suggesting slow ordering and phase competition. Dense Tb/Ge reactants reduce precursor activity compared with flux or solution routes [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, its kinetics are moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best severity profile because CS1/CS2 use 1123 K and 1023 K, below 1000 °C, under Ar in sealed ampoules. It is still penalized for Se volatility, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive/toxic precursor handling. The CS route explicitly includes binary formation, selenization, homogenization, and reheating, so the multi-step deduction is well supported (CS1, CS2). TS provides insufficient specific evidence for this two-step Tb–Ge–Se chemistry and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

The two-step route improves chemical intimacy by forming Tb–Ge binaries before adding Se, analogous to Eu–Ge binary precursors in CS1/CS2. This should reduce cation segregation relative to direct mixing [Reasonable Inference], but it remains a solid-state reaction, so diffusion and ordering still limit kinetics. The target’s mixed Ge tetrahedral/octahedral environment is more complex than Eu2GeSe4, so structural reconstruction is not trivial. SS supports that Tb–Ge–Se frameworks can form, but does not specifically validate the binary-selenization pathway (SS1, SS4).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 operates at a relatively mild 1123 K in SS5, but vacuum sealing, Se volatility, slow cooling, and anhydrous halide flux handling add severity. DyCl3 flux use in SS5 directly supports the feasibility of a rare-earth chloride flux route for rare-earth–Ge–Se crystals. CS provides insufficient flux evidence, and TS only indirectly supports slow-cooled sealed-tube chalcogenide growth. The route is moderately severe but not as high-temperature as Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth has the best kinetic score because molten halide flux can enhance mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and SS5 demonstrates successful Dy3CuGeSe7 crystal growth at 1123 K. Very slow cooling in SS5 indicates that nucleation and ordering can be controlled, which is favorable for a complex P3 phase. The main uncertainty is that the target lacks Cu and has a different stoichiometry, so the same flux may instead stabilize other Tb–Ge–Se or Tb–Se phases. Thus, kinetics are strong but phase selectivity remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is severe because TS4 uses high pressure of 14–70 kbar and temperatures up to 2000 °C. Although the proposed exploratory program could remain near 900–1200 °C, the pressure apparatus itself is specialized and strongly penalized. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence that high pressure is needed for Tb–Ge–Se phases, so this route is not favored. It is included mainly as a fallback for metastable-phase stabilization.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can stabilize dense phases and may help if the target lies near the metastability limit [Reasonable Inference], but TS4 only concerns binary rare-earth selenides. There is no direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure promotes the target P3 Tb–Ge–Se framework. Solid-state diffusion in a compressed pellet remains limited unless high temperature is used. Therefore, the kinetics and structural-transferability score is the lowest among the routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains chemically and geometrically distinct cations: large 7-coordinate Tb and much smaller Ge in tetrahedral and octahedral environments, which triggers the ionic-size/polyhedral complexity concern [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Similar SS and TS chalcogenides contain mixed coordination environments and corner/edge/face-sharing motifs, consistent with complex crystallization landscapes (SS1, TS1–TS3). The exact unit-cell atom count is not proven to exceed 40 from the supplied data, so that check is not triggered. Overall, amorphization risk is medium, mainly reflecting complex ordering rather than glass-forming chemistry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tb6Ge3Se14 is probably a borderline metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the upper edge of the high-synthesizability thermodynamic bin. The strongest support comes from CS1/CS2, where rare-earth germanium selenides are synthesized by binary precursor formation followed by selenization, and from SS1/SS4, where Tb–Ge–Se-containing frameworks form in sealed ampoules. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube chalcogenide methods that control Se vapor pressure while allowing long annealing and slow ordering. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target has a specific Tb:Ge:Se ratio and mixed Ge coordination, so competing Tb–Se, Ge–Se, and other Tb–Ge–Se phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth may be especially useful for obtaining crystals, while the two-step binary route is recommended for bulk synthesis screening.
