### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target YSeI is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a high thermodynamic score. CS provides multiple stable related rare-earth chalcogenide iodides/halides with E_hull = 0, including ErSeI, LaSeCl, YI(Ga), GdSI, and DySI (CS1–CS5). SS and TS also show the closest structural/thermodynamic analogues as ground-state phases, especially ErSeI, HoSI, DySI, and GdSI (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: thermodynamic stability of the computed trigonal YSeI does not guarantee that a specific synthesis route will avoid orthorhombic or binary side phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because the analogues require 900 °C, long annealing, and sealed ampoules with iodine/chalcogen volatility (CS4, CS5, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4). CS/SS/TS provide no useful signal for high-pressure requirements; therefore high pressure is scored neutral/no deduction, effectively 50 evidence quality for that factor but no penalty. The main deductions come from vacuum/protective atmosphere, volatile iodine in a sealed system, and toxic/air-sensitive handling. The absence of multistep precursor preparation keeps the severity better than Routes 2–4.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct reaction from elements is kinetically plausible because HoSI, GdSI, and DySI analogues form directly from metal/chalcogen/iodine at ~900 °C (CS4, CS5, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4). However, elemental Y requires diffusion/reaction from a dense metal precursor, and converting elements into a layered YSeI sheet requires complete structural reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**. Long annealing times in analogues, e.g. 7–10 d, indicate slow ordering or transport (CS5, SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). Volatile iodine may aid transport but also complicates stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses chemically matched binaries but requires inert handling and likely prior synthesis/purification of Y₂Se₃ and YI₃. The need for halide dehydration/sublimation is directly supported by ErSeI synthesis details (CS1, SS1, TS1), and Ta/silica sealed-tube handling is supported by YI(Ga) (CS3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any high-pressure requirement, so high pressure is treated as neutral/no deduction. The severity score is lower than Route 1 because precursor preparation and moisture-sensitive halide handling add complexity.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The stoichiometric reaction Y₂Se₃ + YI₃ → 3 YSeI is favorable from a reaction-design standpoint **[Reasonable Inference]**, and it mirrors the Ln₂Se₃/LnI₃ chemistry present in ErSeI-related data (CS1, SS1, TS1). Binary precursors reduce the reconstruction burden relative to elements, but Y₂Se₃ is still a refractory solid and may require long annealing **[Reasonable Inference]**. The 800–950 °C synthesis window is supported by ErSeI/LaSeCl/YI(Ga) analogues (CS1–CS3), suggesting acceptable kinetics. Remaining uncertainty is whether binary mixing stabilizes the target trigonal polymorph or a different layered polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits the controlled-atmosphere and multistep burden of CVT: high-vacuum ampoule preparation, halide dehydration/sublimation, and thermal-gradient control are explicitly reported for ErSeI-related synthesis (CS1, SS1, TS1). The temperature is not extreme because ErSeI CVT is reported near 800 °C (CS1, SS1, TS1). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure or >1000 °C requirements in this route, so those factors are neutral/no deduction. The main severity risks are volatile halides and air-sensitive anhydrous halide precursors.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT has the best kinetic score because vapor transport bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion limitation **[Reasonable Inference]** and is directly validated for the closest ErSeI analogue (CS1, SS1, TS1). The method can yield mm-scale crystals, indicating effective mass transport and crystallization (CS1, SS1, TS1). However, ErSeBr narrow product zones and ErSeCl difficulty show that phase transport windows can be narrow (CS1, SS1, TS1). The target’s different trigonal P3m1 polymorph introduces uncertainty in nucleation selectivity **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Flux growth still requires inert/vacuum sealed-tube conditions because rare-earth iodides and chalcogenides are moisture-sensitive and iodine-bearing charges are volatile **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with CS1, CS3, SS5, and TS5. It adds multistep processing through flux drying, loading, slow cooling, and flux removal **[Reasonable Inference]**. No CS/SS/TS source indicates high-pressure need, so that factor is neutral/no deduction. The route is therefore challenging despite not exceeding 1000 °C.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

A molten halide flux should improve transport and lower effective diffusion barriers **[Reasonable Inference]**, and NaCl flux is mentioned in ErSeI-related work (CS1, SS1, TS1). However, the same Er chemistry shows a serious risk of NaErSe₂ formation in some cases (CS1, SS1, TS1), which lowers the structural-reconstruction/phase-control score. Kinetics may be better than dry solid-state reaction, but selectivity is less reliable. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms successful flux synthesis of YSeI specifically, so this route is not preferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk based on the target composition. The target description indicates a simple layered P3m1 YSeI sheet with edge-sharing YSe₃I₃ octahedra, so the unit-cell-size and mixed-connectivity checks are not triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues are crystalline layered rare-earth chalcogenide iodides rather than amorphous products (CS1, CS4, CS5; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is not amorphization but crystalline polymorph competition **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YSeI is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable. The analogue support is strong chemically and thermodynamically: ErSeI is the closest Y/Ln–Se–I layered analogue and is experimentally accessible by CVT (CS1, SS1, TS1), while multiple rare-earth sulfide iodides are accessible by direct sealed-ampoule synthesis (CS4, CS5, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted mass transport, either through CVT or iodine/iodide-rich sealed-tube chemistry, as shown by ErSeI and related iodide syntheses (CS1, CS3, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: ErSeI-related data show narrow product zones, failed/competing halide phases, and Na-containing side products under some conditions (CS1, SS1, TS1). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful control of iodine chemical potential, moisture exclusion, and temperature gradient/cooling profile.
