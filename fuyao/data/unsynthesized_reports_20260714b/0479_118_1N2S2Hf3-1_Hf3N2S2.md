### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.043 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and therefore within the range often accessible as a metastable or near-ground-state phase. Thermodynamically similar compounds include NaTa₃N₅ at 0.038 eV/atom, SrHfN₂ at 0.031 eV/atom, and Hf₂N₂S at 0.021 eV/atom, all with reported synthesis routes (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). The target is not a computed ground state, so remaining uncertainty is whether it can beat competing HfN/Hf₂N₂S/HfSₓ phases under real chemical potentials **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Topochemical sulfide-for-halide reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the closest analogue uses only 750 °C and sealed quartz tubes, not high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures (CS-1, CS-2, TS-4). The main penalties are vacuum sealing, precursor pre-synthesis, and moisture-sensitive sulfide handling, all indicated or implied by the topochemical Hf₂N₂S route (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory solid-state HfN routes because Hf₂N₂S forms topochemically at 750 °C, whereas HfN-related solid-state equilibration can require 1500–2100 °C and tens of hours (CS-1, SS-1). The uncertainty is that Hf₃N₂S₂ has a different stoichiometry and higher S/Hf than Hf₂N₂S, so a direct halide-exchange reaction may need stoichiometry tuning **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state route

Condition severity is higher because HfN and related refractory nitrides require severe sintering/homogenization conditions in SS analogues (SS-1, SS-2). TS shows SrHfN₂ can be made from HfN at 1000 °C for 5 days under Ar, so binary nitride routes are feasible but slow and atmosphere-sensitive (TS-3). Kinetics are poor because HfN is refractory, sulfur diffusion must be introduced from a separate sulfide phase, and the target layered structure likely requires full reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct solid-state synthesis of Hf₃N₂S₂ from HfN + HfS₂, so this route is less favored.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure route

Condition severity is high because analogous high-pressure nitride-halide synthesis uses 3–5 GPa and 1000–1100 °C (CS-5), while Hf₃N₄ synthesis uses up to 18 GPa and 3000 K in DAC experiments (TS-2). Kinetically, pressure may help stabilize high-coordinate Hf–N frameworks, as seen for Hf₃N₄ (TS-2), but no source directly reports high-pressure Hf–N–S synthesis, so transferability is indirect. This route is useful if ambient-pressure routes fail or if the P-6m2 phase is pressure-stabilized **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether sulfur can be retained without capsule reaction at high pressure and temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film route

Condition severity can be relatively low because deposition could occur below 800 °C and without bulk diffusion distances **[Reasonable Inference]**. However, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports direct thin-film deposition of Hf–N–S phases, so the route is speculative. Kinetics may benefit from non-equilibrium growth and 2D templating, but composition control is difficult because nitrogen and sulfur activities must be balanced and competing HfN/HfSₓ/Hf₂N₂S phases are likely **[Reasonable Inference]**. This route is competitive in score only because it avoids high bulk temperatures, not because the reference data strongly supports it.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any cation disorder problem. The formula and described P-6m2 cell do not imply a >40 atom unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for large-cell glass-forming complexity. The target does have mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, which increases structural complexity and is also seen in some Hf/actinide nitride analogues (TS-2, SS-3, SS-4). Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₃(NS)₂ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.043 eV/atom, below the common metastability threshold but not zero. The strongest synthesis support comes from the chemically and thermodynamically similar Hf₂N₂S compounds made by topochemical α-HfNBr + Na₂S reactions at 750 °C (CS-1, CS-2, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a low-temperature topochemical Hf–N–S pathway that avoids refractory HfN bulk diffusion (CS-1, CS-2 versus SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the closest analogues show strong sensitivity to heating rate and temperature, and the target stoichiometry differs from demonstrated Hf₂N₂S (CS-1, CS-2). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful sulfide chemical potential and stoichiometry screening.
