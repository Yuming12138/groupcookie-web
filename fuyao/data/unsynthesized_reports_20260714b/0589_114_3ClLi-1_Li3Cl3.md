### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.026 eV/atom, which is within the range normally considered experimentally accessible, so the base thermodynamic score is high [Target]. This is supported by thermodynamic analogues Li₆CoCl₈ and Li₆FeCl₈, which are synthesized despite E_hull values of 0.0309 and 0.0388 eV/atom [TS-1, TS-2]. Chemical similarity also provides a positive metastability signal because ScCl is synthesized at E_hull = 0.0214 eV/atom [CS-2], although CS entries other than ScCl provide insufficient direct evidence for LiCl polymorph stabilization and are treated as neutral where not applicable [CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not specify whether the tetragonal LiCl polymorph is kinetically accessible instead of reverting to rocksalt LiCl [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C processing because LiCl melt screening is estimated at ~650–750 °C [Reasonable Inference], consistent with moderate-temperature sealed halide routes in SS-3, SS-5, and TS-3. The penalties come from the need for sealed dry Ar/vacuum handling, dehydration, and controlled cooling, all of which are common in related chloride syntheses [SS-3, SS-5, TS-3]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for pure LiCl melt-polymorph synthesis and is scored neutral for this route-specific condition assessment [CS]. The main uncertainty is whether melt equilibration favors the ordinary stable LiCl polymorph rather than the target P4/mmm structure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The melt route should have favorable ion diffusion because molten halide routes yield Li₆MgBr₈ and RbLiCl₂ using controlled cooling [TS-3, SS-5]. The structural-reconstruction score is lower because the target is an unusual tetragonal two-dimensional LiCl polymorph, not a conventional rocksalt product [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Similar Li chloride frameworks can require long annealing or careful phase identification, as seen for Li₆FeCl₈ and Li₆CoCl₈ [SS-1/TS-2, SS-4/TS-1]. CS provides insufficient kinetic evidence for pure LiCl polymorph formation and is neutral for this route [CS].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures of 200–450 °C, directly inspired by Li₆FeCl₈ at 200 °C for 4 weeks and Li₆CoCl₈ at 400 °C for 1 week [SS-1/TS-2, SS-4/TS-1]. The main severity penalties are dry inert/vacuum conditions, multi-step drying/milling/sealing/annealing, and hygroscopic chloride handling [SS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. TS supports similar low-temperature sealed chloride processing through Li₆CoCl₈ and Li₆FeCl₈ [TS-1, TS-2]. CS provides limited direct condition-transfer evidence to LiCl and is neutral except for the broader precedent that metastable ScCl required sealed high-temperature processing [CS-2].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Solid-state annealing has poorer precursor activity than a melt because LiCl remains a solid below the melt and relies on defect-assisted rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. The long 1–4 week anneals in Li₆FeCl₈ and Li₆CoCl₈ analogues support a penalty for slow ordering kinetics [SS-1/TS-2, SS-4/TS-1]. Ion diffusion is still expected to be easier than in refractory oxides because only Li and Cl must reorganize [Reasonable Inference], but the reconstructive polymorph change is the limiting step. SS/TS therefore support moderate kinetics, while CS is insufficiently specific and neutral for this route [CS].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so the route is explicitly a [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high temperature and high pressure but requires vacuum deposition, dry transfer, and control of hygroscopic LiCl, which is consistent with the general moisture sensitivity of related lithium chlorides [SS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. TS/SS analogues justify the need for controlled dry environments but do not directly verify vapor deposition [TS-1, TS-2; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for thin-film growth]. The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Thin-film growth can bypass bulk diffusion limits by forming the phase directly at a surface or interface [Reasonable Inference]. The crystallization pathway remains uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates the target P4/mmm LiCl film; therefore the crystallization score is only moderate. Composition control is favorable because LiCl is binary 1:1, but film quality could be affected by LiCl hygroscopicity or re-evaporation [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS are insufficient for direct thin-film kinetics and are treated as neutral aside from the dry-environment precedent [SS-1, SS-3, SS-5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Li, so the cation-size-mismatch check is not triggered [Target]. The structure description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell and reports ordered Li/Cl coordination rather than a complex multi-cation network [Target]. The connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not the simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing criterion, so the amorphization risk is low [Target]. The remaining uncertainty is that thin films of hygroscopic salts may become poorly crystalline if exposed to moisture, but that is a processing issue rather than an intrinsic amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference; moisture sensitivity from SS-1, SS-3, SS-5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a true ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.026 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS analogues: Li₆CoCl₈, Li₆FeCl₈, and Li₆MgBr₈ are synthesized lithium halide frameworks with comparable metastabilities or closely related Li–halide octahedral motifs [SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3]. CS support is weaker because chemically similar entries include non-chloride phosphides and sulfides, though ScCl demonstrates that a two-dimensional chloride with E_hull ≈0.021 eV/atom can be synthesized under demanding sealed conditions [CS-2]. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic driving force against the target, combined with facile Li/Cl mobility in halide environments [Target; TS-1/TS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity: ordinary rocksalt LiCl is expected to compete strongly, so epitaxial templating or kinetic trapping is likely required [Reasonable Inference; phase-control difficulty in SS-4/TS-1 and SS-1/TS-2].
