### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
NaScSe₂ is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket. Similar compounds in all three evidence classes are also stable on the hull, including Na₂ZrSe₃, LiYSe₂, LiScS₂, NaErSe₂, LiHoSe₂, and LiTbSe₂ (CS-1/2/4; SS-1/2/3/4/5; TS-1/2/3/4/5). The remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic stability but whether the ordered Na/Sc layered phase can be accessed kinetically.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The elemental route requires sealed/inert handling and high-temperature annealing up to roughly 900 °C, matching Li/Na–Ln–Se protocols that use evacuated silica tubes, Ar atmosphere, long holds, grinding, resealing, and washing (CS-2; SS-2/3/4; TS-2/3/4). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but reactive Na and volatile Se impose sealed-tube and safety penalties. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates elemental NaScSe₂ synthesis, so the Sc substitution is a [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Elemental Na and Se are reactive, but Sc metal is refractory and must form Sc–Se bonds before ordering into R-3m NaScSe₂ [Reasonable Inference]. The long multi-week anneals and regrinding used for Li/Na–Ln–Se analogues indicate nontrivial diffusion and ordering kinetics (CS-2; SS-2/3/4; TS-2/3/4). Thus the elemental route is feasible but kinetically slower than binary-selenide or flux routes.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The binary selenide route is supported by Na₂ZrSe₃ synthesis from Na₂Se/Zr/Se at 800 °C in vacuum and by Sc sulfide ABX₂ formation from binary sulfides at high temperature (CS-1; CS-4; SS-5; TS-5). It still requires sealed ampoules, air-sensitive Na₂Se, and possibly prior synthesis of Sc₂Se₃, so severity remains moderate. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly covers Sc₂Se₃ + Na₂Se to NaScSe₂, so that adaptation is [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Binary selenides preform Na–Se and Sc–Se chemistry and should lower reconstruction demands relative to elements [Reasonable Inference]. Na₂Se works in a closely related Na–M–Se sealed-tube synthesis (CS-1; SS-5; TS-5), and binary sulfide routes work for LiScS₂/NaScS₂ (CS-4). The kinetic limitation is still solid-state diffusion and cation ordering into layered Na/Sc sites.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
NaErSe₂ forms in NaCl flux in sealed Nb ampoules at 850 °C, giving a direct structural and thermodynamic precedent for a Na–M–Se₂ R-3m phase (SS-1; TS-1). This route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure but requires sealed Nb ampoules, dry chloride/selenide handling, and flux separation. Insufficient evidence from CS gives no direct flux synthesis signal for Sc, so the ScCl₃/Sc₂Se₃ substitution remains [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux growth should improve mass transport and crystallinity relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and the NaErSe₂ analogue is explicitly formed in NaCl flux (SS-1; TS-1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because NaErSe₂ and NaScSe₂ share the same R-3m caswellsilverite topology (SS-1; TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether Sc³⁺ chloride/selenide speciation in NaCl flux supplies the correct Na/Sc/Se chemical potentials.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple layered ABSe₂ R-3m framework analogous to multiple crystalline R-3m compounds in SS/TS, which argues against high amorphization risk (SS-1/2/3/4; TS-1/2/3/4). The main trigger is the Na–Sc cation size/charge contrast, reflected in distinct Na–Se and Sc–Se bond lengths in the target and treated as a [Reasonable Inference]. Because only corner- and edge-sharing octahedra are reported, not face-sharing coexistence, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaScSe₂ is likely a true ground-state phase rather than a strongly metastable phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many similar Na/Li–M–Se₂ phases are also on the hull (CS-1/2; SS-1/2/3/4/5; TS-1/2/3/4/5). The structural support is strong: NaErSe₂, LiYSe₂, LiHoSe₂, and LiTbSe₂ all adopt the same R-3m caswellsilverite layered octahedral motif (SS-1/2/3/4; TS-1/2/3/4). The most enabling factor is the robust ABSe₂ layered octahedral structure family, which tolerates alkali + trivalent metal combinations (CS-2; SS-1/2/3/4; TS-1/2/3/4). The main limiting factor is kinetic and synthetic control: Na/Se volatility, air-sensitive precursors, and slow cation ordering require sealed ampoules, careful thermal profiles, and possibly flux assistance (CS-1/2; SS-1/2/3/4/5; TS-1/2/3/4/5). Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with flux or binary-selenide routes preferred over direct elemental reaction.
