### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, indicating a predicted ground-state composition rather than a strongly metastable phase. Thermodynamic analogues Na₃PS₄, Na₂PS₃, Li₃AsS₃, Li₂CdGeS₄, and Li₂PbGeS₄ are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in the TS set, supporting that related thiophosphate/germanium sulfide chemistries can be stable (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic: hull stability does not guarantee easy crystallization of the ordered LGPS polymorph.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The solid-state route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure, consistent with analogues synthesized at 350–650 °C or 823–923 K (CS1, CS2, CS3/TS3, TS5). However, it requires dry inert handling, sealed ampoules, and air-sensitive sulfides, all repeatedly reported for analogous sulfides (CS1, CS2, CS3/TS3). The major uncertainty is whether sulfur/P₂S₅ vapor pressure or side reactions with the ampoule require additional process optimization [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary sulfides provide reasonable precursor activity, and chemically related Li–P–S and Li–Ge–S phases form at moderate temperatures (CS1, CS2, SS5/TS4). Long holds and slow cooling in analogues indicate diffusion/ordering is not instantaneous (CS1, CS2, TS5). The structural requirement for ordered Li sites and both PS₄/GeS₄ tetrahedra lowers the kinetics score [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Mechanochemical activation does not introduce high pressure or high temperature, but it still requires Ar handling and sealed annealing because the same sulfide precursors are moisture-sensitive (CS1, CS3/TS3). Direct mechanochemical evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS, so the method-specific source signal is neutral, but the annealing environment is well supported by CS/SS/TS analogues. The severity score remains limited mainly by atmosphere and sealed-system requirements.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Milling should enhance mixing and reduce diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], improving kinetics relative to purely thermal reaction. The final crystallization still relies on annealing conditions demonstrated for related sulfides (CS1, CS2, CS3/TS3). Uncertainty remains because overmilling can amorphize sulfide electrolytes or trap off-stoichiometric glassy phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperatures, supported by Li₂CdGeS₄ and Li₂PbGeS₄ flux syntheses at 525–650 °C (SS5/TS4, TS5). The route requires sealed sulfur-rich flux, slow cooling, and flux removal, increasing operational complexity (TS5). The source data also indicate flux-ratio sensitivity, so the route is more challenging despite moderate temperature (SS5/TS4).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten lithium polysulfide flux provides high mass transport, supported by dissolution/crystal-growth behavior in Li₂PbGeS₄ and Li₂CdGeS₄ analogues (TS5, SS5/TS4). However, flux analogues show impurity formation when the flux ratio is not tightly controlled (SS5/TS4). The target’s higher Li and P content makes composition control more difficult than in simpler Li–M–Ge–S flux systems [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains multiple cations with different coordination preferences: Li in 4–6 coordinate sulfide environments, GeS₄ tetrahedra, and PS₄ tetrahedra (target structure). The large, Li-rich tetragonal cell and mixed corner/edge sharing increase disorder and glass-forming risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. No face-sharing is described, so the connectivity trigger is only partial, leaving the F4 score in the medium-risk range.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₁₀GeP₂S₁₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis by sealed-tube sulfide chemistry: Li–P–S phases form under dry Ar/vacuum ampoule conditions (CS1, CS3/TS3), and Li–Ge–S tetrahedral phases form by solid-state or flux routes at moderate temperatures (CS2, SS5/TS4, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of highly reactive sulfide precursors and sealed inert processing, which preserves sulfur activity and prevents hydrolysis. The most critical limiting factor is phase/composition control, especially avoiding Li₂S/P₂S₅/GeS₂ residuals, Ge–S secondary phases, or amorphous thiophosphate glass [Reasonable Inference]. Mechanochemical activation followed by a controlled anneal is the best overall route because it improves homogeneity while retaining the proven sealed-ampoule chemistry.
