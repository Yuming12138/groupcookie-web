### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for a 90–100 score in the user-provided rubric. Similar borohydrides span stable or near-stable values: Zr(BH₄)₄ and Cd(BH₄)₂ are at 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS3 / SS2, SS3), while Mn(BH₄)₂ and LiY(BH₄)₄ are near-hull at ~0.01 eV/atom (CS2, CS4 / SS1, SS4 / TS4). The target is therefore likely synthesizable but may be metastable relative to competing Mg–B–H phases because it is not below hull by margin. TS provides mixed support because thermodynamic analogues include both easy recrystallization examples and high-pressure hydrides, so TS is not decisive for the exact Mg(BH₄)₂ chemistry (TS1–TS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because mechanochemical borohydride formation is performed at room temperature or low temperature under Ar in Cd(BH₄)₂ and Zr(BH₄)₄ examples (CS1, CS3 / SS2, SS3). The main severity deduction is air/moisture sensitivity of borohydrides and anhydrous halides, which is implied by repeated glovebox/Ar use across CS1–CS4 and SS1–SS5. F3 is strong because ball milling has demonstrated fast metathesis kinetics for chemically similar borohydrides (CS1–CS3), and the target’s Mg–H/BH₄ units resemble Mn and Cd borohydride frameworks (CS2–CS3 / SS3–SS4). Remaining uncertainty is phase purity, because mechanochemical products can contain several compounds and be hard to identify, as stated for Cd borohydrides (CS3 / SS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has lower F2 because solvent metathesis requires multiple operations and vacuum drying, as shown by the solution mixing/vacuum route in CS5. It has high kinetic potential because solution reactions give intimate mixing and can remove salt byproducts by filtration, consistent with CS5 and [Reasonable Inference] from metathesis chemistry. The uncertainty is that Mg(BH₄)₂ may form solvent adducts, and desolvation may yield amorphous or mixed polymorph products; this risk is consistent with phase-purity limitations in CS5 and metastable borohydride behavior in CS4 / SS1 / TS4. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for Mg(BH₄)₂ solvent chemistry, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is moderately severe because it requires preformed Mg(BH₄)₂ plus controlled heating, quenching, and inert storage, analogous to LiY(BH₄)₄-type synthesis (CS4 / SS1 / TS4). Its kinetics are favorable if the starting material is already Mg(BH₄)₂, since only polymorph rearrangement is needed rather than full chemical metathesis; this is supported by Mn(BH₄)₂ pressure polymorph conversion from preformed α-Mn(BH₄)₂ (CS2 / SS4). TS supports metastability concerns through TS4 but is otherwise insufficient for exact Mg(BH₄)₂ conditions.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has a moderate F2 by the specified deductions because the pressure example is 1.2 GPa, below the >5 GPa penalty, but it remains practically challenging due to specialized press/DAC equipment reported for Mn(BH₄)₂ (CS2 / SS4). F3 is lower than mechanochemistry because pressure-cell transformations may have limited sample size, compacted powders, and recovery issues, although δ-Mn(BH₄)₂ formation at 110 °C for 3 h supports feasibility (CS2 / SS4). #### F4 — Amorphization tendency

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Mg(BH₄)₂ phase is best viewed as a near-hull or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold. CS and SS strongly support synthesis of metal borohydrides by mechanochemical metathesis, especially from metal chlorides and alkali borohydrides, as shown for Zr(BH₄)₄, Cd(BH₄)₂, Mn(BH₄)₂, and KZn borohydride chloride (CS1–CS3 / SS2–SS5 / TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity of BH₄⁻ salts with metal halides under inert ball-milling conditions, which avoids high-temperature diffusion (CS1, CS3 / SS2, SS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase control: borohydrides are air-sensitive, can decompose or disproportionate, and mechanochemical products often contain multiple phases (CS3 / SS3; CS4 / SS1 / TS4). Overall, the best synthesis path is ambient-pressure mechanochemical metathesis followed by careful PXRD-guided optimization and, if needed, mild thermal/quench treatment.
