## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Target RuTe₂ marcasite is exactly at 0.0500 eV/atom, so it falls at the high end of plausible metastability but not clearly at the ground state [Target]. Chemically and structurally similar RuTe₂ pyrite is closer to the hull at 0.0117 eV/atom, which supports RuTe₂ formation but also highlights a lower-energy competing polymorph [CS1][SS1]. TS analogues near 0.034–0.070 eV/atom indicate that phases in this metastability range can be experimentally targeted, although phase purity remains uncertain [TS1][TS2][TS3].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 receives moderate severity because it uses standard sealed ampoules and furnaces, but vacuum sealing, Te volatility, and regrinding/annealing steps impose deductions [CS1][SS2][TS5]. Its kinetics are limited by refractory Ru and solid-state diffusion, but direct RuTe₂ synthesis and related octahedral Ru frameworks support transferability [CS1][SS1][SS2][TS5]. The main uncertainty is whether the reaction equilibrates to pyrite rather than retaining marcasite [CS1][SS1][Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because the IrTe₂ flux analogues require >1000 °C, vacuum sealing, Te-rich flux, slow cooling, and decanting [CS2][CS3][TS4]. Kinetics are better because liquid Te flux can transport Ru species, but structural selectivity is weaker because the flux analogues are layered IrTe₂ rather than marcasite RuTe₂ [CS2][CS3][TS4][Target]. Insufficient evidence from SS for flux growth is treated as neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is condition-severe because high-pressure analogues require 35–89 kbar, specialized capsules, graphite heaters, and quenching [SS3][SS4][TS2]. Kinetically, high pressure and high temperature may help access a metastable polymorph, but elemental Ru/Te still require substantial reconstruction and phase-control expertise [SS3][SS4][TS2][Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for high-pressure RuTe₂ is treated as neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 has moderate condition severity because TS3 uses only ~700 °C and standard sealed silica tubes, but vacuum sealing and iodine toxicity add penalties [TS3][Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, CVT can bypass bulk diffusion and grow crystals at lower temperature, but Ru transport and marcasite RuTe₂ crystallization are not directly demonstrated [TS3][Target][Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for CVT synthesis of RuTe₂ is treated as neutral 50.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius disorder is not a meaningful amorphization driver [Target]. The marcasite Pnnm binary cell is expected to be small and the target description lacks face-sharing coexistence, so no F4 trigger is assigned [Target][Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly report target unit-cell atom count, so that sub-check uses neutral source evidence plus crystallographic inference.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RuTe₂ marcasite is best interpreted as a **near-hull metastable polymorph**, not a clear ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and exact-composition RuTe₂ pyrite is reported closer to the hull [Target][CS1][SS1]. The CS/SS/TS data support making Ru–Te compounds by sealed-tube solid-state synthesis, but they also warn that phase selection is the central difficulty [CS1][SS1][SS2][TS5]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of simple elemental precursors and established sealed-ampoule telluride protocols [CS1][SS2][TS5]. The most important limiting factor is polymorph competition against pyrite RuTe₂, compounded by Te volatility and slow Ru diffusion [CS1][SS1][Target][Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires iterative anneal/quench screening rather than a single equilibrium firing [SS2][TS5][Reasonable Inference].
