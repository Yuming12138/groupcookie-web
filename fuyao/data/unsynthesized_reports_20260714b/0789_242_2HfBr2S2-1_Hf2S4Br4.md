### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. The closest chemical/structural analogue Nb(SBr)₂ is also essentially on the hull at 0.0008 eV/atom. **[CS-2]** Several Hf-containing or structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfides are reported with E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that Hf–S frameworks are thermodynamically viable. **[SS-1/SS-4/TS-1/TS-3]** Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee a broad phase field under finite Br₂/S vapor pressures. **[Reasonable Inference]**

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 400–500 °C, which are directly supported by Ta–S–Br and Nb(SBr)₂ syntheses. **[CS-1/CS-2]** The main severity penalties are the evacuated sealed ampoule, volatile sulfur/bromine, and toxic/corrosive Br₂. **[CS-1/CS-2]** SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for bromine-handling severity in Hf(SBr)₂, so their contribution to this factor is neutral. **insufficient evidence from SS/TS.**

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The exact Nb(SBr)₂ analogue shows that the desired layered M(SBr)₂ framework can form at 500 °C in 2 days. **[CS-2]** However, replacing Nb with refractory Hf may slow reaction from elemental metal because Hf diffusion and oxide removal can be limiting. **[SS-1/TS-1; Reasonable Inference]** Thus the structural match is favorable but precursor activity is only moderate-to-low.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
CVT uses similar moderate temperatures but imposes longer reaction times, controlled temperature gradients, evacuated tubes, and volatile halogen transport. **[CS-3]** Hf sulfide vapor transport also uses evacuated quartz ampoules and gradients. **[SS-4/TS-3]** The condition score is lower than direct synthesis because CVT is operationally more complex and Br-containing volatile species require tighter control. **[CS-3; Reasonable Inference]**

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
CVT improves mass transport via volatile halide species, which is why NbX₂Y₂ single crystals can be grown at 400–515 °C over weeks. **[CS-3]** Hf-containing sulfides are also compatible with transport-growth methods in evacuated quartz ampoules. **[SS-4/TS-3]** The uncertainty is that no source directly reports Hf(SBr)₂ CVT, so the transport chemistry must be optimized. **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Hf(SBr)₂ CVT.**

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Binary precursor synthesis requires sealed evacuated tubes and volatile/air-sensitive halides, similar to NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ chemistry. **[CS-4]** It may also require prior HfS₂ preparation, which adds steps; HfS₂ use is supported in Hf sulfide synthesis. **[SS-1/TS-1]** The chemical hazard is dominated by HfBr₄/Br₂ handling. **[Reasonable Inference]**

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Using HfS₂ should reduce the need to build all Hf–S bonds from elemental Hf, and binary chalcogenide + halide routes are precedented by NbSe₂ + NbCl₄. **[CS-4/SS-1/TS-1]** However, CS-4 reports poor reproducibility and narrow stability for the Nb–Se–Cl product, suggesting phase control can be difficult. **[CS-4]** Therefore kinetics are better than elemental solid-state but less robust than an optimized CVT route.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
Flux synthesis uses standard sealed silica tubes and furnaces, but requires vacuum, dried flux, controlled heating/cooling, and post-growth flux removal. **[TS-5]** If Br₂ or HfBr₄ is added, volatility and toxicity penalties remain. **[CS-1/CS-2; Reasonable Inference]** CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for bromide flux conditions for Hf(SBr)₂, so this is scored conservatively. **insufficient evidence from CS/SS.**

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
A molten halide flux should enhance ion mobility and crystal growth, as demonstrated for Ta–Ge–S compounds in alkali halide fluxes. **[TS-5]** The limitation is poor structural/chemical transferability: TS-5 is a quaternary sulfide flux system, not a layered Hf sulfide bromide. **[TS-5]** Therefore flux growth is plausible but exploratory rather than preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is absent. **Target structure** The reported structure is a simple layered Hf(SBr)₂ sheet rather than a large multi-cation framework, and no >40-atom unit cell or complex mixed sharing network is indicated. **Target structure** The closest Nb(SBr)₂ analogue forms crystals rather than amorphous products, supporting low amorphization tendency. **[CS-2]**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(SBr)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the exact Nb(SBr)₂ analogue, which has the same C2/m layered structure and was synthesized from elements/S/Br₂ in a sealed tube. **[CS-2]** Additional support comes from halogen chemical vapor transport of related NbX₂Y₂ chalcogenide halides and evacuated-ampoule Hf sulfide chemistry. **[CS-3/SS-1/TS-1]** The most critical enabling factor is halogen-mediated transport, because Br₂/Hf–Br vapor species can overcome sluggish Hf solid-state diffusion. **[CS-1/CS-2/CS-3; Reasonable Inference]** The main limiting factor is control of bromine chemical potential: too little Br may leave HfS₂/HfSₓ, while too much Br may favor volatile Hf bromides or over-brominated phases. **[CS-1/CS-4; Reasonable Inference]**
