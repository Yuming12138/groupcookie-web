### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the top of the prompt’s 90–100 scoring range. Many CS and SS analogues are calculated ground states with E_hull = 0 [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4], supporting that ternary metal borides with Ir–B networks can be stable. TS analogues include experimentally reported phases at E_hull values above the target, such as TiCrIr₂B₂ at 0.0677 eV/atom and Pu(IrB)₄ at 0.1717 eV/atom [TS1, TS3], so MgIrB at 0.05 eV/atom is plausible but may be metastable. Remaining uncertainty is whether the Mg–Ir–B phase diagram contains lower-energy competing MgB₂, IrBₓ, or Mg–Ir phases not captured by the analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed solid-state

Route 1 receives moderate severity because the required temperatures, 1050–1100 °C, are directly consistent with Mg–Ru–B and Zn–Ir–B synthesis [SS2, SS1], but Mg volatility requires sealed Ta ampoules and likely excess Mg [SS2]. Kinetics are limiting because Ir and B are refractory, and related phases need 50 h to 6 days plus homogenization [SS1, SS2]. CS supports elemental solid-state routes for Ir borides [CS2], while TS offers insufficient direct Mg-containing evidence and is treated as neutral 50 for Mg volatility transferability. The major uncertainty is whether MgIrB nucleates before competing Mg–B or Ir–B binaries consume the elements [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, arc melting + annealing

Route 2 has harsher severity because arc melting uses very high effective temperatures and a multi-step remelt/anneal process, as reported for Ir-containing borides and intermetallics [TS1, TS3, CS4]. Kinetically, arc melting helps homogenize refractory Ir/B mixtures [TS1], but Mg volatility makes stoichiometry control worse than in Hf/Ti/Th analogues [CS4, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. TS1 also reports side phases and only 78% main phase, indicating that arc melting alone may not produce pure product. SS provides better Mg-retention guidance through sealed Ta ampoules [SS2], so the route should include post-annealing rather than relying on melt quenching.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, Mg-rich self-flux

Route 3 keeps severity moderate because it can use 1000–1150 °C sealed-ampoule conditions similar to SS1/SS2, avoiding the highest arc-melting temperatures. Kinetics are improved because a liquid Mg-rich phase can enhance mass transport, analogous to Al-flux growth of WAlB [CS3] and excess Zn-assisted Zn–Ir–B growth [SS1]. However, structurally similar Zn–Ir–B systems show impurity and phase-width problems [SS4, SS5], so composition control remains difficult. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for Mg-flux behavior and is scored neutral 50 for that specific aspect.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: Mg and Ir have a large size/coordination mismatch, consistent with two inequivalent Mg sites and high-coordinate Ir/B environments in the target [Target description; Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not prove a unit cell larger than 40 atoms, and it shows edge/corner sharing but not full vertex+edge+face coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS analogues show related complex boride frameworks [SS2, SS4], but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates intrinsic amorphization. Thus, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgIrB is likely a **near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support synthesis of Ir-containing ternary borides by elemental solid-state reaction, sealed ampoules, and arc melting [CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed Ta-ampoule or Mg-rich flux conditions that can retain volatile Mg while allowing Ir–B framework formation [SS2, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, especially Mg loss and slow reaction of refractory Ir/B [SS2, SS4, SS5]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require iterative optimization of Mg excess, sealing, and annealing/cooling protocol.
