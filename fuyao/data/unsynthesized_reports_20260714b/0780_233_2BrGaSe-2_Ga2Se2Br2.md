### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0260 eV/atom, so it lies in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic window of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally realized by CVT, sealed-tube solid-state reaction, direct fusion, and melt/anneal routes (TS1–TS5). CS and SS include stable Ga/Se tetrahedral materials and halide/chalcogenide motifs, but they do not directly prove GaSeBr thermodynamic stability; insufficient evidence from CS and SS for exact target hull behavior, neutral 50 if considered alone. Remaining uncertainty is whether the P3m1 2D polymorph is the experimentally accessible polymorph rather than a low-energy competing Ga–Se–Br phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions; the proposed 450–550 °C window is supported by CuSe₂Br synthesis at 550 °C (CS1) and is milder than many selenide analogues requiring 950–1150 °C (CS2, CS4, SS2–SS5, TS4, TS5). However, it requires a sealed evacuated ampoule, which is common in CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, and TS1–TS5, and volatile GaBr₃ handling is a significant inferred penalty [Reasonable Inference]. The multi-step dry loading/sealing/anneal process adds practical complexity compared with simple open-tube synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for bromide volatility specifically, so SS is neutral for that sub-penalty.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Ga₂Se₃ and GaBr₃ should be reactive relative to refractory oxide systems because GaBr₃ can melt/vaporize and contact Ga₂Se₃ efficiently [Reasonable Inference]. The one-cation nature of GaSeBr reduces cation interdiffusion complexity, unlike multication analogues such as Li₂In₂GeSe₆ or Ag₂In₂GeSe₆ (TS2, TS3). Structural reconstruction is still nontrivial because Ga₂Se₃ must convert to a 2D bromoselenide sheet; slow ordering in Ga₂TeSe₂ after long annealing is a warning (CS3/SS1), while rapid CuSe₂Br formation at 550 °C is encouraging (CS1).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct elemental synthesis is well supported for selenides in evacuated ampoules, including CS2, CS4, TS3, TS4, and TS5. The penalty comes from using Br₂ or in-situ GaBr₃, because bromine is volatile/toxic and creates autogenous pressure in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference]. Although the proposed temperature is modest relative to many solid-state selenide analogues, staged heating makes this a multi-step and safety-sensitive route [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct elemental Br₂ use in GaSeBr specifically, so that precursor choice remains extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Elemental routes can work for selenides, as shown by direct fusion or melt/anneal in TS3–TS5 and elemental solid-state synthesis in CS2/CS4. Kinetically, low-melting Ga and volatile Br species should help reaction contact [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic penalty is that the target layered structure must nucleate directly, and competing Ga–Se or Ga–Br phases could form before the ternary sheet orders [Reasonable Inference]. CuSe₂Br’s cooling sensitivity (CS1) and Cd₄GeSe₆’s impurity formation (TS4) justify a lower structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT avoids external high pressure and can operate at moderate temperatures relative to high-temperature solid-state routes, as TS1 demonstrates for MnIn₂Se₄ at 840–820 °C. It still requires high-vacuum sealed tubes, transport-agent loading, and dry/volatile halide handling [Reasonable Inference]. TS1 explicitly used AlCl₃ and optimized conditions, supporting the severity penalty for controlled vapor chemistry. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for GaSeBr CVT specifically, so those sources are neutral for this route’s feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport may bypass bulk diffusion limits, and TS1 shows that a thermodynamically similar layered selenide can be grown as large single crystals by CVT. However, crystallization depends strongly on halide chemical potential and transport equilibria, and TS1 notes that thermodynamic calculations were needed to avoid multiphase condensation. Composition control is likely narrower for GaSeBr because Br is part of the framework, not merely a transport agent [Reasonable Inference]. The lack of direct CS/SS evidence for Ga–Se–Br CVT is the main uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ga, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion does not apply [Target structure]. The reported P3m1 layered structure is simple and built from corner-sharing GaSe₃Br tetrahedra, with no mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity reported [Target structure]. This gives a low amorphization tendency score, consistent with many crystalline tetrahedral selenides in CS2, CS3, SS1–SS5, and TS5. Remaining uncertainty is stacking disorder in the 2D layers, which is not captured by the three F4 checks [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaSeBr is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0260 eV/atom, but that value is within the experimentally accessible range demonstrated by TS1–TS5. The CS evidence is especially useful because CuSe₂Br proves that layered bromoselenides can be made in evacuated ampoules, although with cooling-rate-sensitive phase control (CS1). The SS evidence supports tetrahedral Ga–Se framework accessibility but provides insufficient direct evidence for bromide chemistry, so it is supportive but not decisive (SS1–SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty plus the availability of a stoichiometric binary route, Ga₂Se₃ + GaBr₃ → GaSeBr [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1 and CS3/SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is bromine chemical potential control, because volatile halides can cause off-stoichiometry or competing phases [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1 and TS1].
