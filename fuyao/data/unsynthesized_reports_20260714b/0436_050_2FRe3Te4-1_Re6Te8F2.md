### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. This is consistent with several CS and SS/TS analogues being on or near the hull: Tl₃SiTe₃ and BaU₂Te₅ have E_hull = 0 in CS-3/4, and WTe₂, Mo₃Te₄, ReSe₂, and AgMo₆Te₆ have E_hull = 0 in SS-1/3/4/5 and TS-1/2/3/5. The uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee a broad synthesis window, as SS-5 / TS-5 reports narrow stoichiometry tolerance for AgMo₆Te₆ despite E_hull = 0. Therefore F1 is high but not 100.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses conventional sealed-tube solid-state methods, which are well supported for tellurides by CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, and CS-5. However, vacuum sealing, volatile Te, long anneals, and dry handling are unavoidable, matching the controlled ampoule requirements in CS-2 and SS-2 / TS-4. The main added severity is the fluoride precursor, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS and is inferred to be moisture-sensitive/corrosive. Thus Route 1 receives a moderate-low F2 score of 55.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct reaction is kinetically limited because Re is refractory and diffusion through Re/Te/ReF₃ solids is expected to be slow. Long reaction times in CS-1, CS-3, and SS-2 / TS-4 support the expectation that complex telluride/halochalcogenide phases require extended annealing. Structurally similar Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂ shows difficult phase control and low-yield behavior (SS-2 / TS-4), lowering confidence for direct bulk synthesis. Route 1 therefore has poor F3 = 25.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the severity of CVT: evacuated sealed tubes, toxic/corrosive transport agents, and temperature gradients. These features are directly supported by WTe₂ CVT with TeBr₄ at 820/700 °C (SS-1 / TS-1), Re dichalcogenide CVT with Br/I carriers (SS-4 / TS-3), and AgMo₆Te₆ CVT at 1050–1060 °C with AgCl (SS-5 / TS-5). The possible need for ~1000 °C operation and F-active species makes this route more severe than Route 1. Hence F2 = 45.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT should significantly improve kinetics by transporting Re/Te through volatile halide species rather than relying only on solid-state diffusion. This is directly supported by layered WTe₂, Mo₃Te₄, ReSe₂, and AgMo₆Te₆ being accessible by CVT (SS-1/3/4/5; TS-1/2/3/5). Structural transferability is particularly supported by Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂, which contains Re–chalcogen–halide sheets (SS-2 / TS-4). The uncertainty is F retention and avoiding Cl/Br/I substitution if non-fluoride transport agents are used, so F3 is good but not excellent at 63.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux-assisted synthesis reduces nominal temperature relative to CVT but still requires dry inert loading and sealed ampoules. Halide/flux participation is supported by CS-1, SS-1 / TS-1, SS-2 / TS-4, and SS-5 / TS-5, but the fluoride flux component is an extrapolation. Toxicity/corrosivity of fluorides and possible silica attack add practical severity. F2 is therefore 55, similar to Route 1 but with better kinetic prospects.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux or halide-assisted synthesis is expected to improve Re mobility and promote crystallization of layered sheets, consistent with the widespread use of transport agents or halide fluxes in CS-1, SS-1, SS-2, SS-5, and TS analogues. The method addresses the key Re diffusion problem better than direct solid-state reaction. However, no provided source directly reports a Re–Te–F flux synthesis, and fluoride may be trapped as stable alkali fluoride rather than incorporated into the target. This gives a moderately strong F3 = 64.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target does not contain multiple cations with large ionic-radius mismatch, and its formula unit is small, so the amorphization tendency appears low. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Re₃Te₄F, so their signal for F4 is insufficient and treated as neutral. The layered Re–Te framework resembles crystalline layered analogues such as WTe₂, ReSe₂, and Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂ (SS-1/2/4; TS-1/3/4), which supports crystallization rather than glass formation. Remaining uncertainty comes from possible disorder in F occupancy or Re clustering.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re₃Te₄F is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support high-temperature sealed-tube synthesis of layered tellurides and Re/chalcogen/halide phases, especially via transport or halide-assisted methods. The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for layered transition-metal tellurides and Re dichalcogenides forming under evacuated ampoule/CVT conditions (SS-1/3/4/5; TS-1/2/3/5). The most critical limiting factor is controlled incorporation of F, because the closest direct analogue uses Cl rather than F (SS-2 / TS-4), and fluoride precursors are more reactive and moisture-sensitive. Overall, the phase is reasonably synthesizable but likely requires careful sealed-tube chemistry rather than a simple one-shot elemental reaction.
