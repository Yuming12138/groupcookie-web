### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0070 eV/atom, which lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. Thermodynamic analogues near this energy are experimentally accessible: ZrTe3 at 0.0028 eV/atom is synthesized in evacuated quartz ampoules (SS4/TS1), and TiZrTe4 at 0.0144 eV/atom is synthesized by direct reaction with repeated heating/grinding (TS4). However, TS3 shows that a low E_hull iodide/chalcogenide, MnBiSe2I at 0.0058 eV/atom, may still be non-phase-pure, so the score is high but not perfect.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed ampoule

Condition severity is moderate because analogous syntheses use evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile iodine, as in NbSeI3 (SS1), TaTe4I/NbTe4I (SS2), and TiI3 (SS3). The temperature is not extreme; relevant analogues form from ~480–700 °C for telluride iodides and Zr tellurides (SS2, SS4/TS1), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Kinetically, the main limitation is refractory Zr metal and solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], while phase-purity risks are supported by reported impurities in ZrTe3 (SS4/TS1), phase-pure difficulty in NbSeI3 (SS1), and non-phase-pure MnBiSe2I (TS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Alkali-halide flux growth

Flux growth is more operationally complex because it requires flux drying, ampoule sealing, controlled slow cooling, and flux removal, as reported for NdSeTe2/PrSeTe2 and ZrGeTe4 (CS1, CS3, SS5). The kinetic score is higher than direct solid-state because molten salt flux can enhance mass transport and crystal growth; this is directly supported by ZrGeTe4 growth in KCl flux (SS5) and layered LnSeTe2 flux growth (CS1/CS3). The remaining uncertainty is iodine incorporation: insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a Zr–Te–I phase grown specifically in chloride flux, so iodine chemical potential must be optimized.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Iodine-assisted CVT

CVT has similar severity to flux because it uses sealed evacuated tubes and volatile/toxic I2, as in TiI3 (SS3) and NbPdTe5 (TS5). Kinetically, iodine transport can improve crystal growth and mobility, and I2 was necessary for NbPdTe5 crystal growth (TS5). The limiting evidence is that TiI3 was obtained as part of a mixture (SS3), and NbSeI3 phase-pure samples were difficult (SS1), so CVT may produce crystals but not necessarily phase-pure Zr(TeI)2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Hydrothermal HI

Hydrothermal HI is mild in temperature relative to solid-state routes and avoids refractory solid-state diffusion, as AuTeI and AuTe2I are prepared hydrothermally in HI/hydrohalic acid (CS4/CS5). However, CS4/CS5 provide no Zr analogue, and insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports hydrothermal Zr–Te–I synthesis. The route is therefore numerically attractive but scientifically uncertain; Zr hydrolysis/oxide competition is a major [Reasonable Inference] risk.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered based on the target composition. The structure description is a relatively simple 2D sheet with Zr, Te, and I coordination; it does not report a >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence. Some analogues such as LnSeTe2 have complex mixed sharing (CS1/CS3), but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that the target itself has this amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(TeI)2 is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.007 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized low-hull analogues such as ZrTe3 (SS4/TS1). The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis most strongly through sealed-ampoule telluride/iodide chemistry and flux-assisted layered chalcogenide growth, especially ZrTe3 (SS4/TS1), ZrGeTe4 flux growth (SS5), and TaTe4I/NbTe4I direct synthesis (SS2). The most critical enabling factor is control of iodine chemical potential in a sealed system, because iodine is both a required structural component and a volatile transport/reactive agent, as seen in SS1/SS3/TS5. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Zr–Te binaries and possible over-iodinated Zr iodides, consistent with impurity/phase-control issues reported for ZrTe3 (SS4/TS1), NbSeI3 (SS1), and MnBiSe2I (TS3). Overall, the compound appears synthesizable, with the best practical route being a chloride-flux or carefully buffered sealed-ampoule synthesis.
