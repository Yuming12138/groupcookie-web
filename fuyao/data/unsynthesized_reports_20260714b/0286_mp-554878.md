### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable edge of the required 90–100 scoring bin. CS gives exact-composition PrSbO₄ with E_hull = 0.0, strongly supporting the chemical stability of the Pr–Sb–O composition, but the CS structure is P2₁/c rather than target P2/c. TS analogues with E_hull around 0.06–0.10 eV/atom are experimentally accessible, so the target is likely synthesizable but may be metastable.

**F2 — Route 1 CVT.**  
CS explicitly reports 1373 K to 1223 K, 14–21 days, evacuated quartz ampoules at ca. 10⁻⁴ mbar, and TeCl₄ transport, which imposes deductions for >1000 °C, vacuum, volatile sealed transport agent, multistep processing, and hazardous precursors. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for PrSbO₄ CVT, so CS is weighted most heavily. The route is experimentally established but operationally demanding.

**F3 — Route 1 CVT.**  
CVT reduces diffusion limitations because the transported species move through the vapor phase, as supported by CS. However, CS also notes slow transport rates and phase-control difficulty for RESbO₄ crystals, lowering the kinetics score. The largest uncertainty is whether CVT gives the target P2/c polymorph rather than the CS P2₁/c polymorph.

**F2 — Route 2 solid-state.**  
SS/TS show that many complex oxides can be made by standard solid-state reaction at 800–1150 °C with grinding or regrinding, and CS implies a polycrystalline RESbO₄ feed powder exists. Deductions are applied for high temperature near/above 1000 °C, multistep grinding/calcining, antimony oxide hazards, and likely Sb volatility. The route is simpler than CVT but less selective for polymorph.

**F3 — Route 2 solid-state.**  
Binary oxide precursors are moderately to poorly reactive compared with nitrate/sol-gel reagents, as inferred from SS/TS long firing schedules. Complete reconstruction from Pr oxide and Sb oxide to monoclinic PrSbO₄ is required, and repeated firing may be needed, decreasing the structural-reconstruction score. The remaining uncertainty is whether a ceramic route will access only the thermodynamic P2₁/c phase seen in CS rather than the target P2/c phase.

**F2 — Route 3 flux.**  
CS and TS support sealed-tube flux/halide-assisted oxide growth at lower temperatures than CVT, so no >1000 °C deduction is applied. Deductions remain for evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile/toxic Sb or Te chloride species, and multistep flux workup. Direct PrSbO₄ flux evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so severity is moderately uncertain.

**F3 — Route 3 flux.**  
Flux growth improves kinetics through liquid-phase transport, consistent with CS rare-earth arsenate flux synthesis and TS flux-grown oxide crystals. The lower temperature may help preserve a metastable P2/c arrangement, but this is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly demonstrated. The main uncertainty is flux compatibility with Pr–Sb–O and possible chloride incorporation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between 8-coordinate Pr and 4-coordinate Sb, consistent with the large bond-length contrast in the target and CS. Mixed edge/corner-sharing oxygen environments are reported in the target and in CS RESbO₄ analogues, increasing disorder risk. CS indicates crystallization is possible, but the P2/c target’s unusual O–O contact and lower symmetry make amorphization or competing crystallites plausible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PrSbO₄ P2/c phase is likely a **mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, while CS reports an exact-composition PrSbO₄ P2₁/c phase at E_hull = 0.0. The CS evidence strongly supports synthesis of PrSbO₄ chemistry, especially by TeCl₄-CVT, but it does not conclusively support the exact P2/c polymorph. SS/TS analogues show that oxides in the 0.05–0.10 eV/atom range are experimentally accessible by solid-state, flux, or sealed-tube methods. The most critical enabling factor is halide- or flux-assisted mass transport, which can overcome slow oxide diffusion and enable crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and redox control, particularly because the target has 4-coordinate Sb and an O–O contact unlike the CS SbO₆ PrSbO₄ analogue.
