### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold, so it receives a high but boundary-limited score of 90. Thermodynamic analogues with similar or slightly higher E_hull values are experimentally accessible: Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ has E_hull ≈0.067 eV/atom and is synthesized by solid-state sintering (TS1), and Sr₂UZnO₆ has E_hull ≈0.054 eV/atom and is synthesized by ceramic reaction (TS2). Chemically similar Ba₂BiTaO₆ is near the hull and synthesized by conventional solid-state methods (CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee Tl³⁺ retention or kinetic accessibility; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct Tl evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1: Ceramic route

Ceramic synthesis is strongly supported for Sr/Ba–Ta oxide frameworks by SS2, SS3, CS5, TS4, and TS5, but these examples commonly require >1000 °C and repeated heat/grind cycles. The main added severity for the target is Tl toxicity and likely volatility, which is not directly addressed in CS/SS/TS. TS3 demonstrates the general need to compensate volatile cation loss using excess alkali carbonate, but Tl-specific compensation is a [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the route is feasible but operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 1: Ceramic route

Kinetics are poor because Ta₂O₅ is refractory and Sr/Ba tantalate analogues often require high temperatures, long durations, and repeated grindings (SS2, SS3). CS5 shows that Ba₂BiTaO₆ can be made by conventional solid-state methods, but no temperature/time details are given, and direct Sr₂TaTlO₆ evidence is absent. The target’s triclinic P1 distortion and mixed coordinations imply significant structural reconstruction from binary oxides/carbonates. Consequently, Route 1 has the lowest kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 2: Hydroxide flux route

The flux route avoids >1000 °C processing, using 600–700 °C conditions similar to CS1–CS4 and SS5. However, sealed Ag tubes, caustic hydroxide flux, water-content control, and Tl toxicity increase operational difficulty. CS1–CS4 emphasize acidic/wet hydroxide melts and sealed silver tubes for high-valent oxide crystal growth, while SS5 demonstrates Ta₂O₅ compatibility with KOH/NaOH flux. TS gives insufficient useful signal for hydroxide flux processing of the target chemistry, so TS is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2: Hydroxide flux route

Flux growth improves mass transport and precursor reactivity, which is important for Ta-containing oxides because solid-state Ta diffusion is slow (SS2, SS3). SS5 provides the strongest structural/chemical transferability because it uses Ta₂O₅ in KOH/NaOH flux at 600 °C to grow a Ta oxide. CS1–CS4 show that hydroxide melts can stabilize complex high-valent oxide crystals at relatively low temperature. The uncertainty is whether Tl³⁺ remains soluble and incorporated rather than reducing to Tl⁺ or volatilizing; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct Tl evidence.

#### F2 — Route 3: Wet-chemical route

This route is a [Reasonable Inference] and is not directly reported by CS/SS/TS. It reduces maximum temperature relative to ceramic methods, which should help Tl retention, but it introduces multi-step processing and possible sensitivity of Ta alkoxide/peroxo precursors. The main penalties are Tl toxicity, potential Tl volatility, and the multi-step nature of gel formation, burnout, and crystallization. Because no CS/SS/TS source directly supports wet-chemical synthesis of the analogues, confidence is lower.

#### F3 — Route 3: Wet-chemical route

Reactive soluble precursors should improve cation homogeneity relative to SrCO₃/Ta₂O₅ ceramic mixtures, addressing the diffusion problems suggested by SS2, SS3, and TS5. However, crystallization from an amorphous precursor into a low-symmetry ordered oxide is a substantial structural reconstruction. Organic burnout can also perturb Tl oxidation state, which is a target-specific risk not resolved by CS/SS/TS. Therefore the kinetic score is moderate, not high.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes and coordination environments, with Sr in 6-coordinate environments, Ta in 6-coordinate Ta–O bonding, and Tl in 3-coordinate bonding as stated in the target structure. This size/coordination mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. The provided target structure does not show >40 atoms per unit cell, and it reports corner/edge sharing but not face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. Remaining uncertainty comes from the unusual triclinic P1 distortion and O–O contact, which may indicate a narrow crystallization window.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂TaTlO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of related Sr/Ba–Ta and double-perovskite-derived oxides by both ceramic and flux methods, with especially relevant Ta hydroxide-flux precedent from SS5 and ceramic Ta-perovskite precedent from CS5/SS2/SS3. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature hydroxide flux chemistry, which can dissolve refractory Ta₂O₅ and reduce diffusion limitations shown in solid-state analogues. The most critical limiting factor is Tl chemistry: Tl compounds are toxic, volatile, and redox-sensitive, and no provided CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Tl incorporation. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful stoichiometry, oxidation-state control, and probably iterative phase-purity optimization.
