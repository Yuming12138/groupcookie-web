### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the rubric boundary for the 90–100 range. TS provides a strong positive signal because experimentally synthesized analogues occur at similar metastabilities: HfAu₂In at 0.0458 eV/atom, TbAu at 0.0403 eV/atom, NdAu at 0.0564 eV/atom, and Y₃MnAu₅ at 0.0666 eV/atom. CS is also favorable because YbAuIn₂ is reported synthesized despite E_hull ≈0.0897 eV/atom, while several CS analogues are on hull. SS supports the Lu–Au subchemistry with stable LuAu₂ and related RAu₂ phases, but SS alone gives insufficient evidence for the exact ternary Lu₂Au₂In stability.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for severe thermal exposure during arc melting, multistep processing, possible In volatility, and Lu air sensitivity. CS and SS show arc melting is common for rare-earth intermetallics, but SS also reports 1–2 week homogenizations, indicating nontrivial condition severity. TS supports arc melting/annealing of Au intermetallics but does not eliminate volatility and competing-phase risks. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for high-pressure requirements, so high pressure is scored neutral/no penalty.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate because arc melting creates intimate alloying, but dense metals and high Lu melting point make complete homogenization slow. CS shows anneals of 240 h to 1 month for related R–Pd–In/Pb intermetallics, while SS reports 1–2 week homogenizations for RAu₂, supporting a kinetic penalty. Structural reconstruction is partially helped by CS P4/mbm R₂Pd₂Pb and SS Lu–Au local bonding, but no source directly reports Lu₂Au₂In. TS provides insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Lu–Au–In but supports melt/anneal accessibility of Au-rich intermetallics.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because CS and TS support sealed Ta or induction melting under Ar without high pressure. The main penalties are T >1000 °C, volatile In in a sealed system, multistep melt/anneal processing, and air-sensitive Lu handling. CS directly justifies the sealed Ta approach for R–Au–In compounds, and TS supports induction melting in Ta crucibles. SS provides useful container/inert-atmosphere support but insufficient direct signal for sealed Lu–Au–In ternary formation.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are better than conventional solid-state sintering because induction/HF melting and In/Au liquid phases improve transport. CS directly reports EuAuIn₂/YbAuIn₂ formation from elements in sealed Ta tubes, which is the strongest kinetic analogy. SS supports Lu–Au bonding distances very close to the target Lu–Au distances, suggesting compatible local chemistry. Remaining uncertainty is whether Lu₂Au₂In P4/mbm ordering competes with LuAu₂ or Au–In binaries.

#### F2 — Route 3

The sealed ampoule route avoids the highest temperatures, so it is less thermally severe than arc melting. However, SS uses vacuum ampoules, and the presence of In implies a sealed system is needed to control volatility; Lu metal also remains air-sensitive. CS and TS provide less direct support for this lower-temperature sintering route in ternary Lu–Au–In, so no severity reduction is applied beyond the lower temperature. High pressure is unsupported by all sources and is treated as unnecessary.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are moderate to weak because dense elemental metals react slowly unless In becomes a liquid mediator. SS shows YAu₂ can form after 100 h at 900 °C, but this is a binary, not the target ternary. CS annealing examples near 870–900 K support that long heat treatments can homogenize related phases. Insufficient direct evidence from TS exists for this specific ampoule-sintering kinetic pathway.

#### F2 — Route 4

The flux route can use lower temperatures than arc/induction melting, which improves condition severity. The penalties are sealed handling of volatile excess In, multistep crystal growth/decanting, and air-sensitive Lu. CS supports In-containing R–Au–In melts but does not explicitly support flux growth, so this route is assigned an uncertainty penalty in expert adjustment. TS gives only broad melt-growth support and insufficient direct signal for In self-flux in Lu–Au–In.

#### F3 — Route 4

The kinetic score is highest because liquid In flux should enhance mass transport and reduce diffusion barriers; this is a [Reasonable Inference] based on In’s low melting point and CS use of elemental In in melt syntheses. The main kinetic uncertainty is phase selection: excess In may favor Au–In or Lu–In binaries instead of Lu₂Au₂In. CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct flux-grown Lu₂Au₂In analogue, so structural reconstruction/ordering is scored only moderate. Post-growth annealing is recommended to improve ordering.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small-cell intermetallic with simple coordination descriptions, which generally lowers amorphization risk; SS and CS analogues are crystalline bulk intermetallics formed by melt/anneal routes. One radius-mismatch trigger is assigned from [Reasonable Inference] because Lu and Au metallic radii differ appreciably, but CS/SS show such mismatches are tolerated in crystalline R–Au phases. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has >40 atoms per cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity. Therefore, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Lu₂Au₂In is likely a slightly metastable or marginally stable intermetallic, not a deeply unstable phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and TS/CS include experimentally synthesized analogues at comparable or higher hull energies. The CS data strongly support formation of rare-earth–Au–In compounds from elemental melts in sealed Ta tubes, while SS strongly supports Lu–Au local bonding and stable LuAu₂-type subchemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of melt-mediated synthesis—arc, induction/HF, or In flux—which overcomes slow diffusion in dense Lu/Au metals. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Lu–Au, Au–In, and Lu–In binaries plus In volatility/stoichiometry drift. Overall, synthesis is feasible with careful sealed-tube or flux processing, but obtaining single-phase Lu₂Au₂In may require annealing and composition optimization.
