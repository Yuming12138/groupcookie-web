### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff in the requested scoring rubric [Target data]. CS analogues include stable CaFeO3 and near-stable Ca–Fe ferrites, supporting a high but not perfect F1 score [CS2–CS5]. TS analogues cluster near 0.046–0.064 eV/atom and are synthesized by high-pressure routes, supporting the idea that this metastability range can be accessible [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ca2Fe3O8 is a quenchable metastable phase or decomposes into CaFeO3 / Fe oxide mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

**Route 1 F2/F3 — High-Pressure Oxidative Solid-State / Topotactic Oxidation.**  
The condition severity score is reduced because high oxygen pressure, sealed oxidizer chemistry, and multi-step capsule synthesis are required [CS2/SS1; CS3/SS3]. It avoids the >5 GPa deduction because the closest CaFeO3 analogues use 2–3 GPa, not 15 GPa [CS2/SS1; CS3/SS3]. Kinetics are moderately favorable because CaFeO2.5 is already a Ca–Fe oxide precursor and should reduce cation redistribution relative to starting from carbonates [CS2/SS1; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Ca2Fe3O8 forms topotactically or competes with CaFeO3 plus Fe-rich calcium ferrites [CS2/SS1; CS4/CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Polymerized-Complex Precursor + High-Pressure Oxidation.**  
The F2 score is low because TS and SS high-pressure analogues use 15 GPa, sealed Pt capsules, KClO4, and multi-step precursor processing [TS1–TS5; SS4/SS5]. The F3 score is higher because nitrate/citrate gels provide excellent cation mixing and related Fe oxides crystallize in short high-pressure holds [TS1–TS5; SS4/SS5]. The uncertainty is compositional transferability because TS/SS analogues contain Ln/Cu sublattices absent from Ca2Fe3O8 [TS1–TS5; SS4/SS5; Reasonable Inference].

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Ambient-Pressure Controlled-pO2 Ceramic Screening.**  
CS supports that Ca–Fe–O frameworks can be made by ceramic annealing near 1120 °C under controlled atmospheres [CS4; CS5]. However, those atmospheres are reducing or low-pO2, while the target requires Fe ≈ +4, making the redox direction unfavorable without an added oxidation step [CS4; CS5; CS2/SS1; CS3/SS3; Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for ambient-pressure Ca2Fe3O8 synthesis and are treated as neutral 50 source signals for this route [SS insufficient; TS insufficient]. The route is therefore useful as precursor screening, not as the preferred final synthesis route [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
Only one formal trigger is assigned because cation size mismatch is significant for Ca2+ versus Fe4+ [Reasonable Inference]. The target has mixed corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, but the requested C3 criterion specifically requires vertex + edge + face sharing coexistence, and face-sharing is not reported [Target data]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as neutral 50 source signal and not triggered [CS/SS/TS insufficient]. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca2Fe3O8 is most likely a **borderline metastable** phase rather than a true ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, slightly above the convex hull but within the common accessible metastability window [Target data]. The strongest support comes from CS/SS CaFeO3 analogues, which show that high-valence Ca–Fe–O phases require high oxygen pressure and can be synthesized at 2–3 GPa with KClO4 [CS2/SS1; CS3/SS3]. TS analogues reinforce that materials with similar thermodynamic distance can be isolated, but their Ln/Cu chemistry makes them less directly transferable than the Ca–Fe–O references [TS1–TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is **high oxygen chemical potential under pressure** to stabilize Fe4+ [CS2/SS1; CS3/SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is **phase competition with CaFeO3 and lower-valence Fe-rich calcium ferrites**, especially if pO2 or stoichiometry is slightly off [CS2/SS1; CS4/CS5; Reasonable Inference].
