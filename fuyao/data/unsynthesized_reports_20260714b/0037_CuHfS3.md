### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
HfCuS₃ is at 0.0500 eV/atom, which is exactly at the favorable cutoff for high synthesizability in the provided rubric [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with slightly higher E_hull values are reported as synthesized, so TS strongly supports accessibility of near-hull metastable sulfides [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5]. CS and SS provide additional support because multiple similar sulfides are on-hull or near-hull and synthesized, although none is the exact HfCuS₃ target [CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-3].

**F2 — Route 1 Elemental Solid-State**  
The severity is moderate because the route uses standard evacuated ampoules and 800–900 °C temperatures rather than high pressure or >1000 °C processing [CS-2, SS-2, TS-2]. Deductions come from vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and repeated annealing/grinding [CS-2, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2]. Remaining uncertainty is high because elemental Hf reactivity is not directly tested in CS/SS/TS for HfCuS₃ [Reasonable Inference from SS-2].

**F3 — Route 1 Elemental Solid-State**  
Kinetics are poor because Hf is refractory and elemental diffusion through sulfide products is expected to be slow [SS-2, Reasonable Inference]. The closest Hf-containing analogue required very long annealing and reannealing, supporting a low diffusion/ordering score [SS-2]. The uncertainty is whether HfCuS₃ forms before competing HfS₂/Cu sulfides dominate, which CS/SS/TS do not directly resolve [CS/SS/TS].

**F2 — Route 2 Binary-Sulfide Annealing**  
Condition severity remains moderate because it still uses evacuated sealed ampoules and sulfur-containing solids [CS-5, TS-5]. The route avoids the lowest-reactivity elemental sulfurization step but still requires vacuum, sealed sulfur pressure, and repeated homogenization [CS-5, SS-2, TS-5]. No CS/SS/TS source directly shows HfS₂ + CuS → HfCuS₃, so the transferability is inferred [Reasonable Inference from CS-5, TS-5].

**F3 — Route 2 Binary-Sulfide Annealing**  
Kinetics improve versus elemental synthesis because Hf–S and Cu–S bonds are already present in the precursor set [CS-5, TS-5, Reasonable Inference]. The final Pnma framework still requires cation rearrangement and octahedral/pyramidal network formation, so the structural reconstruction score remains only moderate-low [Target]. The main uncertainty is whether binary sulfides equilibrate into HfCuS₃ or persist as competing phases [CS-5, TS-5, SS-2].

**F2 — Route 3 CVT**  
CVT uses moderate-to-high temperatures and evacuated sealed tubes, with no high pressure and no >1000 °C requirement in the proposed window [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. Deductions are driven by vacuum sealing, sulfur/halogen volatility, and iodine/bromine toxicity [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. TS provides insufficient evidence for CVT-specific severity, so TS is neutral at 50 for that route-specific source signal [TS].

**F3 — Route 3 CVT**  
Kinetics are strongest for CVT because vapor transport can bypass bulk diffusion and has produced crystals in several sulfide analogues [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. The score is not higher because Hf transport chemistry and the exact HfCuS₃ Pnma nucleation pathway are not directly demonstrated [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, SS-3, SS-5]. The key uncertainty is maintaining Hf:Cu:S stoichiometry during transport [CS-1, SS-5, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 Solvothermal/Hydrothermal**  
The route has low thermal severity but higher handling complexity because it uses sealed autoclaves, controlled atmosphere, organic solvents, and likely moisture-sensitive Hf precursors [CS-4, TS-3, CS-3]. The route avoids high-temperature solid-state diffusion in the first step, but a post-anneal is likely needed for crystallinity [Reasonable Inference from CS-4, TS-3]. SS provides insufficient evidence for solvothermal processing and is treated as neutral at 50 [SS].

**F3 — Route 4 Solvothermal/Hydrothermal**  
Solution precursors have high reactivity and good initial mixing, which supports a strong precursor-activity score [CS-4, TS-3]. The structural score is low because CS/TS solvothermal analogues are not dense HfS₆/CuS₆ perovskite-like phases, and Hf hydrolysis/amorphization is likely [CS-4, TS-3, Target, Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether a low-temperature product can be converted to Pnma HfCuS₃ without decomposing [Reasonable Inference from CS-4, TS-3].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only two cations and a compact ABX₃-type formula, so the atom-count and ionic-size disorder checks are not triggered [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The structure contains corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is also not triggered under the stated criterion [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not directly provide target-specific ionic radii or unit-cell atom count, so those source signals are neutral at 50 for the subchecks [CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfCuS₃ is best interpreted as a near-hull, likely metastable but experimentally plausible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom rather than clearly zero [Target]. CS/SS/TS collectively support sulfide synthesis under sealed-tube, solid-state, and CVT conditions, but they do not contain a direct HfCuS₃ synthesis precedent [CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube/CVT sulfide chemistry that maintains sulfur activity and enhances mass transport [CS-1, CS-2, SS-3, SS-5]. The most important limiting factor is phase control around Hf–Cu–S ordering, because the closest Hf-containing structural analogue required prolonged annealing and was described as difficult to make single phase [SS-2]. Overall, Route 3 CVT is the best first attempt for crystals, while Route 2 binary-sulfide annealing is the best backup for bulk powder screening [CS-1, SS-3, SS-5, CS-5, TS-5].
