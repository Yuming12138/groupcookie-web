### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold, so a score of 90 is appropriate. CS analogues include experimentally synthesized uranates at 0.0–0.0706 eV/atom, which supports feasibility near this energy range (CS1–CS5). TS analogues include made phases at comparable metastability, such as Nd3ReO7 at 0.0506 eV/atom and Ce2O3 at 0.0793 eV/atom (TS1–TS2), although they are not chemically identical. SS provides structural precedent for complex oxides but insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence for Sr–U–La, so SS is supportive but not decisive.

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized because it uses >1000 °C firing, controlled Ar/reducing atmosphere, multi-step grinding/sintering, and uranium-bearing precursors; all of these requirements are consistent with CS1, CS3–CS5 and SS1. It avoids high pressure and can avoid sealed volatile precursors if UO2/SrCO3/La2O3 are used [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is moderate-to-high but still within normal ceramic synthesis capability.

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are poor because UO2 and La2O3 are refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference], matching the long firing/regrinding reported in CS1 and CS4–CS5. Structural reconstruction is also nontrivial because the CS uranates are mostly perovskite-derived, while the target is corundum-derived. SS1 shows reduced uranium oxides can form under Ar, but also reports limited stability/purity issues for Ba2U2O7, reducing confidence.

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 has similar thermal and atmosphere penalties to Route 1 because the final phase still likely requires >1100 °C and controlled oxygen fugacity [Reasonable Inference from CS4–CS5 and SS1]. Uranyl nitrate is toxic/radioactive and oxidizing, so a reduction step is required after nitrate decomposition [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains standard laboratory ceramic chemistry, not high-pressure or thin-film specialized.

**F3 — Route 2.** Nitrate-assisted mixing improves precursor activity relative to dense oxides, supported by uranyl nitrate use in CS4–CS5. The main uncertainty is that CS4–CS5 form Ba2SrUO6 perovskites, not SrULa2O6 corundum-derived material, so nucleation of the target structure is unproven. The final reduction and cation ordering steps may still be slow.

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux synthesis reduces temperature relative to ceramic routes, as shown for BaLn2O4 at 1000→820 °C in SS4 and Ce2O3 at 700–800 °C in TS1. However, it introduces sealed or controlled-atmosphere handling, hygroscopic chloride fluxes, possible chloride volatility/corrosion, and uranium handling [Reasonable Inference]. Thus severity is not low despite lower temperature.

**F3 — Route 3.** Flux transport should improve ion mobility and nucleation kinetics relative to dry oxide sintering, as inferred from successful flux-grown BaLn2O4 and Ce2O3 crystals (SS4, TS1). The uncertainty is high because no CS example shows Sr–U–La chloride-flux synthesis, and uranium redox chemistry in chloride flux may be sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth may be best for crystals or phase discovery rather than gram-scale ceramic powder.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has a medium amorphization/kinetic-trapping risk because it combines size-mismatched Sr, La, and U cations and explicitly contains corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. The mixed connectivity is more complex than simple perovskite corner-sharing networks described in many CS analogues (CS1–CS5). The provided target formula does not prove a >40-atom unit cell, so that check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a larger target cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrULa2O6 is likely a low-energy metastable or near-threshold ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is favorable but not deeply stable. The CS data strongly support high-temperature synthesis of Sr/Ba uranate oxides, but mostly in U(VI)-like perovskite-derived structures rather than the target U(IV)-like corundum-derived structure. The SS and TS data support the broader feasibility of complex reduced oxides under controlled atmosphere or flux conditions, but they do not provide an exact Sr–U–La analogue. The most critical enabling factor is control of uranium oxidation state and oxygen fugacity, especially using UO2 and Ar/Ar-H2 conditions. The most critical limiting factor is competition from simpler Sr–U, La–U, La2O3, SrO/SrCO3-derived, or perovskite/fluorite-related phases during slow solid-state ordering.
