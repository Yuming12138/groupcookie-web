### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. CS-2 NiF₃ is also on the hull and CS-1 PdF₃ is only 0.0147 eV/atom above hull, supporting that nearby late-transition-metal trifluorides can be stable or near-stable. TS analogues are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, but they are ternary fluorides rather than binary RhF₃, so their support is indirect. **Insufficient evidence from SS** for binary RhF₃ thermodynamic stability, so SS is neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 Condition Severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because CS-1 reports RhF₃ synthesis at about 400 °C under dry F₂. The main severity comes from elemental fluorine, which is toxic, corrosive, and requires anhydrous specialized gas handling; this is directly supported by CS-1 and by TS-4/TS-5 F₂ fluorination analogues. The process is not heavily multi-step in CS-1, so no multi-step penalty is applied. Remaining uncertainty is the lack of an exact RhF₃ reaction time in CS-1.

#### F3 — Route 1 Reaction Kinetics

CS-1 shows that RhI₃ reacts directly with dry F₂ at ~400 °C, implying favorable gas–solid fluorination kinetics compared with starting from Rh metal or Rh oxide. However, forming the Pmma RhF₃ octahedral sheet arrangement from a halide precursor still requires structural reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS analogues PdF₃, FeF₃, and NiF₃ all show MF₆ octahedral frameworks, supporting the plausibility of the final coordination environment. SS/TS provide no direct kinetic evidence for binary RhF₃, so they are treated as indirect/neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 Condition Severity

CS-2 supports aHF precipitation at low temperature, so thermal severity is low. The severity score is reduced because anhydrous HF is volatile, corrosive, and requires fluoropolymer reactors, and CS-2 describes specialized Teflon FEP tubing reactors and strict low-temperature handling. CS-2 also indicates multiple additions, washing, and decantation steps, so the process is operationally complex. **Insufficient evidence from SS/TS** for aHF precipitation of RhF₃, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F3 — Route 2 Reaction Kinetics

CS-2 indicates that aHF solution chemistry can give rapid precipitation of high-fluoride transition-metal phases, so precursor activity and mass transport are favorable. The major kinetic uncertainty is crystallization of the specific Pmma RhF₃ sheet phase at low temperature; CS-2 notes metastability and fluorine loss in related products, suggesting post-annealing may be needed. This route may yield amorphous or nanocrystalline RhF₃ first **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS do not provide direct support for solution precipitation of RhF₃.

#### F2 — Route 3 Condition Severity

SS-1/SS-2/SS-5 and CS-5/TS-2 support fluoride thermal decomposition or sealed-tube annealing in the 500–750 °C range, so the route avoids ultra-high temperatures and >5 GPa pressures. Severity remains significant because sealed Au/Pt tubes, dry Ar/F₂-compatible handling, and potential volatile fluoride species are involved. TS-2/TS-3 show that some analogous complex fluorides require multi-step fluorination and long annealing, which justifies the multi-step penalty. **Insufficient evidence from SS/TS** for exact RhF₃ thermal decomposition conditions, so the route is less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 Reaction Kinetics

Thermal decomposition of fluorometallates can shorten diffusion distances and promote crystallization, as inferred from SS-1/SS-2/SS-5 decomposition routes. However, CS-5 and TS-2 indicate that long anneals and slow cooling are often required for well-crystallized fluoride phases, lowering the kinetics score. RhF₃ formation from a fluorometallate precursor likely requires partial reconstruction and gas release **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is plausible but less kinetically direct than RhI₃ fluorination.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target structure contains corner- and edge-sharing RhF₆ octahedra but no face-sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face-sharing criterion is not triggered. No evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a very large target unit cell or strong glass-forming complexity; therefore, amorphization risk is low. The remaining uncertainty is the exact crystallization pathway for the orthorhombic Pmma polymorph.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RhF₃ is likely a true ground-state or very accessible ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support is CS-1, which explicitly reports RhF₃ made by dry F₂ reacting with RhI₃ at about 400 °C. SS and TS analogues support the broader idea that fluoride octahedral frameworks can be obtained by fluorination, thermal decomposition, and sealed-tube annealing, but they do not specifically prove the Pmma RhF₃ polymorph. The most important enabling factor is the high thermodynamic stability combined with a known direct fluorination route. The main limiting factor is the operational difficulty and hazard of strictly anhydrous elemental fluorine chemistry.
