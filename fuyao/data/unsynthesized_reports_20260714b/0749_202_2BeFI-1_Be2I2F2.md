### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, placing it in the strongest stability bin. CS supports that chemically related hydroxyhalides can be stable or nearly stable, including Cd(OH)Cl at 0.0 eV/atom (CS1), Pb(OH)Br at 0.0 eV/atom (CS5), and Pb(OH)Cl at 0.0024 eV/atom (CS2). SS/TS also support stable halide frameworks, including CsEuBr₃, Rb₃NaBe₂F₈, Sr(OH)Br, and Sr(OH)I at or near the hull (SS1–SS3/TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly reports BeIF, so kinetic accessibility is not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 needs dry, sealed, controlled-atmosphere handling, which is supported by SS1/TS1 using dry Ar, evacuated silica tubes, and glovebox handling for CsEuBr₃. It also needs Be halides, which are toxic and moisture sensitive by physicochemical inference; the provided sources do not explicitly discuss Be toxicity, so this is [Reasonable Inference]. Temperature is moderate compared with SS1/TS1 at 1123 K and SS2/TS2 at 500 °C, so no high-temperature penalty above 1000 °C is applied. The main uncertainty is compatibility of BeF₂/BeI₂ with ampoule materials.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary halides are generally more reactive than dense refractory oxides, and SS1/TS1 and SS5 show binary halide mixtures can yield ternary halides. Diffusion should be helped by halide volatility or incipient melting, as halide melt routes are demonstrated in SS4/TS4 and SS5. However, forming the target 2D BeIF sheet from BeF₂ and BeI₂ requires reconstructive rearrangement, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an identical BeIF precursor-to-product pathway. Therefore the kinetic score is good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with CS1 at 323 K, CS2 at 350–360 K, and CS4 at 230 °C for related hydroxyhalide crystallization. It still requires sealed-vessel operation like CS1/CS4 and dry inert loading by analogy to SS1/TS1 because anhydrous Be halides are moisture sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. The route is penalized for toxic Be precursors and a sealed volatile halide environment. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for direct aqueous BeIF formation, so an anhydrous solvothermal medium is required by inference.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Solution-phase routes have favorable kinetics because dissolution and recrystallization bypass long-range solid diffusion; CS1, CS2, and CS4 support crystallization of layered halide-related phases from solution/sealed fluids. The 2D nature of the target is consistent with the layered motifs in Cd(OH)Cl and Cu(OH)Cl (CS1/CS3/CS4). The uncertainty is chemical transferability: CS phases are hydroxyhalides, while BeIF is an anhydrous mixed halide, so water must be excluded to prevent Be hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the highest F3 among the routes but with practical sensitivity.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 needs dry sealed halide handling similar to SS1/TS1, SS4/TS4, and SS5. It avoids >1000 °C conditions because halide fluxes should operate around 350–550 °C [Reasonable Inference], which is below SS4/TS4 at 850 °C and comparable to SS5 bromide synthesis at 430–600 °C. Deductions come from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile iodide handling, multi-step flux growth, and Be precursor toxicity. Insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of BeIF makes the route less certain than the solution route.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth provides high ion mobility, and SS4/TS4 plus SS5 demonstrate that ternary halides can crystallize from molten halide media. Be–F bonding is structurally plausible because Rb₃NaBe₂F₈ contains BeF₄ units with Be–F = 1.53–1.59 Å (SS2/TS2), close to target Be–F = 1.56 Å. The limiting kinetic factor is competing phase formation: alkali beryllofluorides like Rb₃NaBe₂F₈ are stable (SS2/TS2), so alkali flux may be incorporated. This lowers the structural reconstruction/composition-control component.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Be²⁺, so cation-radius mismatch is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure has one Be, one I, and one F crystallographic site and no evidence for a >40 atom unit cell; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence to infer a large complex cell. The structure is a 2D sheet but does not report simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that polyhedral connectivity is described in coordination terms rather than full topology.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeIF is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is moderately strong: CS supports low-dimensional halide/hydroxyhalide crystallization under mild solution or hydrothermal conditions, SS2/TS2 supports Be–F structural chemistry, and TS5 supports iodide-containing anhydrous synthesis, but no source directly reports BeIF. The most critical enabling factor is the combination of zero hull energy and short Be–F bonding consistent with known beryllofluoride chemistry (SS2/TS2). The most critical limiting factor is handling and stoichiometry control of toxic, moisture-sensitive, and potentially volatile Be/I halides. The best practical route is anhydrous solvothermal crystallization because it minimizes diffusion barriers while preserving the low-dimensional sheet-forming tendency seen in CS analogues.
