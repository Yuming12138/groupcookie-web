### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0020 eV/atom places it essentially on the convex hull and within the 90–100 scoring band. CS5 provides a chemically similar near-hull sulfide, CdS at 0.0027 eV/atom, that is experimentally synthesized; SS1/TS1 provide a structurally and thermodynamically similar Ag sulfide, KAg₃S₂, at 0.0093 eV/atom, also synthesized. TS2, TS3, and TS5 show that other near-hull compounds are experimentally accessible, although some require harsh or specialized conditions. Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not prove the exact Pbcm Ag₂S polymorph is the equilibrium Ag₂S phase, only that the thermodynamic penalty is very small.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid state

Condition severity is moderate because evacuated sealed tubes are common in Ag/chalcogenide analogues (SS3, SS4, CS3/SS5, TS4), but sulfur volatility requires sealed staged heating [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate: elemental Ag is dense and may react slowly, while Ag–sulfide diffusion/order can require long anneals, as Ag₃CuS₂ needed 2 weeks at ~550 K (SS3) and Ag₈SnSe₆ needed 250 h annealing (TS4). The route is chemically direct for binary Ag₂S, but the specific Pbcm polymorph may be bypassed in favor of more common Ag₂S structures. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Pbcm phase selection by this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Chemical bath / low-temperature deposition

F2 is high because the closest bath analogue, CdS, is synthesized at only 80 °C in air for 60 min (CS5), avoiding vacuum, high pressure, and high temperature. F3 is reduced because CS5 reports mixed cubic/hexagonal CdS and impurities, implying that low-temperature solution growth can suffer from polymorph and impurity control. Ag₂S precipitation from Ag⁺ and sulfide is expected to be kinetically facile [Reasonable Inference], but that same rapid precipitation may make the Pbcm polymorph difficult to select. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms bath synthesis of Pbcm Ag₂S, so this route is best for films/nanocrystals rather than guaranteed bulk phase-pure material.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solvothermal / supercritical amine

F2 is lower because SS1/TS1 require a high-pressure autoclave, Ar counterpressure of 2400 psi, sealed quartz tube, ethylenediamine, and six days at 300 °C. F3 is high because SS1/TS1 demonstrate that supercritical ethylenediamine can crystallize an Ag–S framework at relatively low temperature, and subcritical conditions failed, showing the solvent pathway is kinetically enabling. This route is especially relevant to a 2D Ag–S target because structural analogue KAg₃S₂ has similar soft Ag–S coordination (SS1/TS1). The main uncertainty is avoiding alkali incorporation and steering the product to binary Ag₂S rather than K/Na–Ag sulfides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Melt/Bridgman

F2 is moderate because CS2/SS2 show Ag₃SI can be grown from sealed ampoules around 700 °C, but sealed systems and controlled thermal gradients are still required. F3 benefits from liquid-phase transport, but CS2/SS2 explicitly note phase mixtures from which crystals must be separated, indicating significant phase-selection risk. CS3/SS5 also shows that Ag₃SI polymorph control can require quenching, slow cooling, and long annealing. Insufficient evidence from TS3 on exact Bridgman conditions limits confidence in translating that route to Pbcm Ag₂S.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Ag, so the cation-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered; this is based on the target composition, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct signal and are neutral for this check. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS1/TS1 warn that subcritical solvothermal reactions can yield amorphous products for KAg₃S₂, but that is route-specific rather than an intrinsic amorphization tendency of Ag₂S. Overall amorphization risk is low, with the main risk being poor crystallinity in low-temperature solution products.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely very close to thermodynamic stability, with E_hull = 0.0020 eV/atom, but the exact Pbcm 2D Ag₂S polymorph may still be metastable relative to more familiar Ag₂S structures; CS/SS/TS do not resolve this polymorph question. Chemically and structurally similar Ag–S materials are experimentally accessible by sealed solid-state, melt, and supercritical routes (SS1, SS3, SS4, CS2/SS2, CS3/SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, mobile Ag–S chemistry, which allows low-temperature solution chemistry and solvothermal crystallization [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1/TS1 and CS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection: several analogues report mixed phases, prolonged annealing, quenching/slow-cooling dependence, or amorphous products under nonideal conditions (CS2/SS2, CS3/SS5, SS1/TS1, SS3, CS5). Overall, synthesis is feasible, but isolating phase-pure Pbcm Ag₂S will require careful thermal or solution-chemical control.
