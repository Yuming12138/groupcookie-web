### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is only 0.028 eV/atom above the hull, so it falls in the 90–100 score range by the required rubric [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues support near-hull fluoride phases: Rb2KInF6 is 0.016 eV/atom and Cs2KMnF6-related phases are around 0.044–0.045 eV/atom [TS2, TS3, TS5]. Chemical evidence is mixed because RbF is close at 0.0516 eV/atom but Rb2O2 is much less stable at 0.301 eV/atom [CS1, CS2]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the P4/mmm CsF polymorph is kinetically recoverable at ambient conditions, which CS/SS/TS do not directly establish.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate practical severity because it needs high-pressure equipment, but the required pressure suggested by RbF is only 9–15 kbar, below the >5 GPa deduction threshold [CS1/SS1/TS1]. BaO shows that some 2D tetragonal analogues may require much higher DAC pressures, so the pressure window is uncertain [CS3]. The main rubric deduction is for moisture-sensitive CsF handling, inferred from strict dry CsF/Cs-fluoride protocols [SS2, TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact pressure needed for tetragonal P4/mmm CsF.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the route starts from the same binary composition, analogous to pressure-induced RbF polymorphism from anhydrous RbF [CS1/SS1/TS1]. A same-composition transformation should avoid long-range chemical diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural: the RbF analogue is cubic Pm-3m, not tetragonal P4/mmm, so the target may require a more reconstructive transition than the analogue [CS1/SS1/TS1; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high pressure and very high temperature because Cs-containing fluoride analogues are made at ~500–700 °C under Ar [SS2, SS3/TS4]. Deductions arise from sealed dry handling, multi-step drying/pelletizing/sealing/annealing, and moisture-sensitive CsF [SS2, SS3/TS4]. The route is experimentally accessible but not directly supported for the target polymorph, because no CS/SS/TS example reports ambient-pressure P4/mmm CsF.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetically, Route 2 benefits from using CsF directly, so no cation interdiffusion between separate reagents is needed [Reasonable Inference; CsF precursor handling supported by SS2]. Near-melting or sub-melting fluoride annealing can improve ion mobility, as alkali fluoride analogues were sintered near melting points [SS3/TS4]. The limiting kinetic factor is phase selection rather than diffusion: ambient annealing is likely to favor the thermodynamic ambient polymorph unless quenching traps the target [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the highest condition severity because Bridgman/self-flux work needs sealed or vacuum/inert high-temperature containment [SS5/TS2]. Alkali fluoride systems are handled in Ag/Pt hardware under Ar or vacuum due to moisture and melt reactivity [SS3/TS4, SS5/TS2]. Although temperatures need not exceed 1000 °C for CsF self-flux by inference, the sealed melt and vacuum/inert procedure lead to significant deductions [Reasonable Inference; SS5/TS2].

#### F3 — Route 3

Molten or near-molten CsF should have excellent mass transport, so diffusion kinetics are strong [Reasonable Inference; Bridgman fluoride growth supported by SS5/TS2]. Flux/Bridgman methods are credible for obtaining crystalline fluorides [SS5/TS2]. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the P4/mmm CsF polymorph nucleates from the melt rather than the stable CsF polymorph, so structural-selection kinetics remain uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation radius mismatch does not trigger the first amorphization criterion [Target]. The structure has mixed corner- and edge-sharing Cs/F polyhedra, triggering one amorphization/complex-connectivity check [Target]. Similar mixed-connectivity fluoride analogues are nevertheless crystalline, such as Cs2CaF4 and Cs2NaMnF6 [SS2, TS5]. The overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a low-energy metastable CsF polymorph rather than a clear ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.028 eV/atom rather than zero [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from high-pressure alkali fluoride polymorphism in RbF, which is chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically the closest recurring analogue [CS1/SS1/TS1]. Structurally similar and thermodynamically similar fluoride analogues also show that Cs/Rb fluorides can be synthesized under dry, sealed, inert, or high-pressure conditions [SS2, TS3, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is same-composition polymorphic access from anhydrous CsF, which minimizes chemical diffusion and stoichiometry complexity [CS1/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection and recoverability of the exact tetragonal P4/mmm layered phase, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct experimental precedent.
