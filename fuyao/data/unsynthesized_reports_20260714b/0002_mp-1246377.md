### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the threshold of the highest stability bin, so it is scored 90 rather than closer to 100 [Target]. CS contains several Ca ternary nitrides that are on or very near the hull, SS contains stable H-phase/P6₃/mmc nitride analogues, and TS shows synthesized metastable analogues around ~0.075–0.096 eV/atom [CS1–CS5, SS2, SS5, TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether CaMnN2 is a true low-temperature ground state or a kinetically accessible metastable polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Solid-state sintering avoids GPa pressure and can use temperatures similar to Ca3MnN3 at 900 °C, but it still requires dry N2/Ar control, air-sensitive nitrides, and likely repeated annealing [CS5, CS1, CS2]. The main severity comes from handling Ca3N2/Li3N and maintaining nitrogen activity without losing volatile/mineralizer components [CS1, CS2]. SS gives insufficient evidence from SS for Ca-containing binary-nitride sintering, so SS is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are moderate because Ca3N2/Li3N are reactive, but reconstructing into ordered H-phase CaMnN2 from binary solids is slower than flux growth [CS1, CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Long holds in Ca2VN3 and Ca2PN3 show that Ca ternary nitride ordering can require extended sintering [CS1, CS2]. The uncertainty is whether Mn reaches the higher nitride coordination/oxidation state required by CaMnN2 rather than forming Ca3MnN3 [CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Flux growth uses relatively moderate temperatures, matching 700 °C for H-phase MgMoN2 and 760 °C for Ca–Ga nitrides [SS5, CS4]. The severity remains significant because NaN3/alkali metals require sealed systems, drybox handling, and post-reaction flux removal [SS5, CS4]. TS gives insufficient evidence from TS for this flux route, so TS is neutral.

**F3 — Route 2.** Kinetics are the best among the proposed routes because flux/azide chemistry improves nitrogen activity and cation transport, and SS5 gives a direct H-phase analogue [SS5]. Phase control is still not trivial because Ca–Ga flux nitrides show multiple competing phases and Ca–Mn–N chemistry can favor Ca3MnN3 under related conditions [CS4, CS5]. The route’s key uncertainty is controlling Ca:Mn ordering in the H-phase rather than crystallizing a Ca-rich nitride [CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.** Ammonolysis has a favorable temperature window near 625–750 °C and avoids sealed azide decomposition pressure [SS2]. Its penalty comes from flowing NH3, multi-step oxide preparation, and safety/handling issues associated with nitriding gases and moisture-sensitive nitride products [SS2, CS4]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for CaMnN2 ammonolysis and are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.** A homogeneous Ca–Mn oxide precursor should reduce cation diffusion distances, which helps kinetics [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. However, oxide-to-nitride conversion is reconstructive and SS2 explicitly notes a narrow temperature range for pure P6₃/mmc nitride products [SS2]. The largest uncertainty is whether Ca–O bonds can be fully converted without leaving CaO or oxygen-containing byproducts [SS2; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.** The high-pressure ammonothermal route uses lower temperature but requires NH3 pressure, a specialized autoclave, long dwell times, and air-sensitive amide chemistry [SS1, TS1]. It is below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, but it is still operationally demanding [SS1, TS1]. CS provides insufficient evidence from CS for this route and is neutral.

**F3 — Route 4.** Fluid-mediated amide/NH3 chemistry can enhance nitrogen activity and crystal growth, as shown by Mn3N2 crystal synthesis [SS1, TS1]. Kinetic confidence is reduced because the analogue requires weeks to months and contains no Ca, so Ca/Mn ordering into H-phase CaMnN2 is unproven [SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. This route is better viewed as a crystal-growth fallback than the primary powder synthesis path [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** Two checks trigger: large Ca/Mn size mismatch and mixed face/edge/corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity [Target, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. The small H-phase unit cell keeps the score from falling into the high-risk amorphization range [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence from TS for amorphization tendency and is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaMnN2 is best described as a **near-hull, likely metastable or borderline ground-state nitride**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from structurally matched H-phase MgMoN2 made by sealed NaN3 solid-state/autoclave chemistry and chemically matched Ca3MnN3 made from Li/Ca/Mn/N2 [SS5, CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high nitrogen activity while allowing cation mobility, which is why the sealed azide/alkali flux route is preferred [SS5, CS4, CS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Ca-rich Ca–Mn nitrides, binary Mn nitrides, and possible poorly ordered products [CS5, SS1/TS1, CS4]. Overall, the target appears **moderately synthesizable** with careful sealed-flux or ammonolysis optimization rather than simple one-step open-tube firing [SS5, CS4, SS2; Reasonable Inference].
