### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold, so it receives 90/100. CS analogues with E_hull ≈ 0.0049–0.0597 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by standard ceramic routes [CS-1–CS-5], and TS analogues around 0.043–0.076 eV/atom are also reported [TS-1–TS-5]. The main uncertainty is that La₂NbZnO₆ may be metastable relative to binary/ternary oxides even if close to the hull; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS identifies the specific competing phases.

#### F2 — Route 1

The conventional ceramic route is moderately severe because analogues require >1000 °C and often long times/regrinding, especially Sr₂InTaO₆ at 1200 °C for 5 days [CS-2–CS-5]. It avoids high pressure and controlled atmosphere because many chemical analogues are made in air [CS-1–CS-5]. The score is limited by multi-step processing and the likely need for repeated firings; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that a sealed system is mandatory for Zn retention.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are weak because La₂O₃/Nb₂O₅/ZnO are oxide precursors, and analogous refractory oxide syntheses require high temperatures, prefiring, quenching, or repeated cycles [SS-4, SS-5]. CS double-perovskite analogues require up to 5 days and intermittent regrinding to reach homogeneity [CS-2–CS-5]. The low-symmetry Cm target and Nb/Zn ordering imply substantial structural reconstruction from binaries; this is [Reasonable Inference] based on the target structure and analogue kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2

The sol-gel/Pechini route has lower condition severity because it can plausibly crystallize below or near 1000 °C, reducing the temperature penalty relative to ceramic analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Air/O₂ processing is compatible with the oxide chemistry because CS analogues are air-fired [CS-1–CS-5]. The main deduction is multi-step gel preparation/burnout/firing; no CS/SS/TS source directly reports sol-gel, so that evidence is indirect.

#### F3 — Route 2

The precursor activity is high because nitrates, acetates, and soluble Nb complexes provide molecular-level cation mixing by the rubric; this is [Reasonable Inference]. This directly addresses the slow diffusion and homogeneity problems seen in CS ceramic analogues requiring long firing and regrinding [CS-2–CS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the gel crystallizes into the desired Cm La₂NbZnO₆ rather than simpler LaNbO₄/ZnO-type impurities; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms the phase selection.

#### F2 — Route 3

The sealed/flux route is more severe because it uses sealed or vacuum conditions like TS-3 and TS-4 and includes controlled thermal profiles. It is justified only if Zn loss or phase competition prevents air synthesis; the need for sealing for this exact target is [Reasonable Inference]. It does not require high pressure, but the controlled atmosphere, sealed containment, and multistep workflow produce a lower F2 score.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux can improve mass transport and crystallinity, consistent with the success of high-temperature sealed methods for related oxides [TS-3, TS-4], but no source directly demonstrates La–Nb–Zn oxide flux synthesis. Kinetics may improve versus dry oxide ceramic reaction, but Nb chemistry remains refractory and cation ordering is still required. The route is therefore useful as a troubleshooting route rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination contrast, with La–O bonds much longer than Nb–O/Zn–O bonds in the target structure, giving C1. It also has mixed connectivity, including corner-sharing NbO₅/ZnO₅ units and O-centered edge/corner-sharing motifs, giving C3; similar mixed connectivity appears in SS oxides [SS-3–SS-5]. Cell size is uncertain, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40 atom unit cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La₂NbZnO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strongly guaranteed ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom at the practical metastability boundary. The CS data strongly support air-fired oxide synthesis of related multication oxides [CS-1–CS-5], while SS/TS data show that structurally or thermodynamically related complex oxides can require repeated firing, sealed tubes, or controlled cooling [SS-3–SS-5, TS-3, TS-4]. The most critical enabling factor is the low energy above hull combined with oxide-stable La/Nb/Zn chemistry under air firing. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: forming a low-symmetry ordered La–Nb–Zn oxide with five-coordinate Nb/Zn from refractory oxide components may be slow and impurity-prone. A homogeneous sol-gel/Pechini precursor route is therefore recommended as the best balance of thermodynamic plausibility and kinetic assistance.
