### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold for likely synthesizability under the given rubric. TS analogues with very similar hull energies, such as Ca₂LaSbO₆ at ~0.0504 eV/atom and Sr₂YSbO₆ at ~0.0519 eV/atom, were synthesized by solid-state routes, supporting that this metastability range is experimentally accessible (TS1, TS2). CS/SS analogues include multiple hull-stable Bi–Ln double perovskites, which further supports the structural chemistry (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5; SS1–SS5). The main uncertainty is that the target is not clearly below the hull, so competing Sr–La–Bi–O phases may appear.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Flux Growth

The flux route has mild severity because the closest analogue Sr₂BiNdO₆ was made at 900 °C in air with no high pressure or special gas (CS1, SS1). Its kinetic score is good because molten SrCl₂/KCl should accelerate ion transport and enable crystal growth, as shown by the successful single-crystal preparation of Sr₂BiNdO₆ (CS1, SS1). The penalty comes from the long 4-day dwell, flux replenishment, and the need to separate crystals from flux (CS1, SS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether La³⁺, being larger than Nd³⁺, shifts the stability window or favors a different tilt variant [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Solid-State Ceramic Reaction

The ceramic route has moderate severity because related Bi–Ln double perovskites require 1000–1050 °C or even 1300 °C with repeated grinding and pressing (CS2, CS4, CS5; SS2–SS4). Kinetics are slower because oxide/carbonate precursors require long-range diffusion and B-site ordering, as reflected by long holds and intermediate regrinding in the analogues (CS2, CS5; SS2, SS4). The route is strongly supported as a general family method for Ba₂LnBiO₆ and Sr/Ba–Lu–Bi phases, but the closest Sr₂NdBiO₆ evidence is flux rather than ceramic (CS1 vs. CS2/CS5). The key uncertainty is whether pure Sr₂LaBiO₆ forms before Bi loss or secondary Sr/La/Bi oxides become competitive [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sol–Gel/Pechini-Assisted Route

The sol–gel route receives a favorable kinetics score because soluble nitrate/citrate precursors should reduce diffusion lengths compared with the oxide/carbonate ceramic routes that require repeated firing (CS2, CS5; SS2, SS4; [Reasonable Inference]). Its severity remains modest because final crystallization can likely be attempted near 850–1000 °C in air, consistent with the 900–1050 °C window of close analogues (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS2). However, there is insufficient direct evidence from CS, SS, and TS for sol–gel synthesis of these exact Bi–Sr–Ln double perovskites, so confidence is lower than the numerical score alone suggests. The main uncertainty is Bi hydrolysis/segregation during solution processing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains substantially different cation sizes, especially Sr versus octahedral La/Bi, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference based on ionic radii; structural accommodation supported by CS1/SS1 and CS4/SS3]. The unit cell is not evidently >40 atoms based on the formula and analogous doubled descriptions, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a larger-cell penalty. The target has corner-sharing octahedra and some edge/corner-sharing O-centered tetrahedral descriptions, but no face-sharing network is specified, so C3 is not triggered (target description; CS1/SS1). Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiSr₂LaO₆ is best viewed as a marginally metastable but likely accessible double-perovskite-related oxide because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the favorable synthesis threshold. The strongest support comes from the nearly identical Sr₂BiNdO₆ analogue, which was obtained by chloride flux growth at 900 °C in air, and from multiple Ba/Sr–Ln–Bi double perovskites made by ceramic routes (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing LaO₆/BiO₆ double-perovskite framework, which is repeatedly observed across Ln analogues (CS1, CS4, CS5; SS1, SS3–SS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: long anneals, repeated grinding, flux replenishment, and possible Bi volatility all indicate that stoichiometry and ordering will need careful control (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1, SS2, SS4; Bi volatility is [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, the material should be synthesizable, with flux growth being the best experimentally supported route and sol–gel-assisted ceramic synthesis being the most kinetically attractive extrapolated route.
