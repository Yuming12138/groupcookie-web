### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.016 eV/atom, which falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range associated with high synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues include VS₂ at 0.019 eV/atom, V₅S₈ at 0.0376 eV/atom, NiV₄S₈ at 0.0376 eV/atom, and NiV₂S₄ at 0.0396 eV/atom, all with reported synthesis routes (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar sulfides are often on the hull or near hull, including GaMo₄S₈ at 0.042 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that V₃WS₈ may be metastable with respect to competing VS₂/WS₂ or V-rich sulfides despite its low hull energy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate because many CS/TS analogues use evacuated tubes at 800–1000 °C with repeated grinding rather than high pressure or exotic equipment (CS1, CS2, CS5, TS4). The main F2 penalties are vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and multistep grinding/refiring, all directly supported by CS1/CS2/CS5/TS4. Kinetics are the weakness: elemental W is refractory and slow diffusing [Reasonable Inference], while analogues often require long reaction times or repeated firings (CS1, CS4, TS4). This route is plausible but less attractive for phase-pure V/W ordering.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Iodine-assisted CVT

CVT conditions are moderately severe because V₅S₈ and related V sulfides use evacuated tubes, iodine, sulfur, and temperature gradients around 900–800 °C (SS3, TS2). The route improves kinetics by vapor transport and is proven to yield single crystals for V sulfides (SS3, SS5). The uncertainty is W transport: CS/SS/TS provide strong evidence for V sulfide transport but insufficient direct evidence that W incorporates uniformly into V₃WS₈ under the same conditions. Thus the F3 score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: H₂S oxide sulfidation

This route is more severe because H₂S is toxic/corrosive and the process involves several steps, as in oxide-to-sulfide routes for NiV₄S₈/NiV₂S₄ and Mn(NbS₂)₄ (SS4, TS3, TS5, CS3). It may offer better chemical mixing than elemental W/V reaction because oxides can be premixed and converted under reactive H₂S [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural reconstruction from oxides to a layered sulfide is substantial, and analogous V–M sulfides require sulfur adjustment and long anneals (SS4, TS5). The score therefore reflects feasible but operationally challenging synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Layered sulfide precursor / topochemical route

Route 4 has moderate condition severity because it uses sealed-tube annealing at 700–900 °C, similar to dichalcogenide precursor syntheses in CS4 and evacuated-tube chalcogenide syntheses in CS5. Its kinetic advantage is structural: VS₂ is a molybdenite-type structural analogue with V–S sheets (SS1), and the target is molybdenite-derived. The main kinetic limitation is still W/V cation interdiffusion and ordering within a sulfide framework [Reasonable Inference]. This is why Route 4 is recommended even though its numerical F3 ties Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target description emphasizes a relatively simple 2D sheet with edge-sharing V-centered polyhedra and 6-coordinate W, not a highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing 3D network. CS/SS/TS analogues include more complex V₅S₈/NiV₄S₈ structures with mixed sharing (SS3–SS5, TS2–TS5), but that evidence does not directly imply high amorphization risk for the target. Ionic-radius and unit-cell-size evidence is not directly provided by CS/SS/TS, so those source signals are treated as insufficient/neutral. Based on the target’s low hull energy and layered structural motif, the amorphization tendency is assessed as low [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃WS₈ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly unstable hypothetical phase, because its E_hull is only 0.016 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues at similar hull energies have reported syntheses (TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis of related layered and mixed transition-metal sulfides by evacuated sealed-tube reactions, CVT, and H₂S sulfidation, with especially strong support for V–S layered motifs from VS₂ and V₅S₈ analogues (SS1, SS3, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the molybdenite-derived layered framework, which suggests that VS₂/WS₂-like sulfide precursors can reduce structural reconstruction (SS1, CS4). The most critical limiting factor is cation ordering and diffusion involving refractory W, which may make direct elemental synthesis slow and impurity-prone [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is realistically synthesizable, with the best chance coming from a layered sulfide precursor route under controlled sulfur chemical potential.
