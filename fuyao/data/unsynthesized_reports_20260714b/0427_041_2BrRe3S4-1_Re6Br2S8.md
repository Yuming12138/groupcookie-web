### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0140 eV/atom, which places it well within the generally synthesizable near-hull regime; this directly supports a high F1 score. Similar low-hull or stable analogues are reported across all reference categories: CS2–CS4 and SS2–SS4 are at 0 eV/atom, SS1/TS4 is 0.0016 eV/atom, and TS1 is 0.028 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially for halide-containing layered Re phases, as SS1/TS4 reports phase-control difficulty.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits high condition severity from TS1, where Mo₆S₆Br₂ requires ~1100 °C, vacuum-sealed silica tubes, compression, and annealing. Use of volatile sulfur and bromide species requires sealed ampoules; this is consistent with TS1 and with sealed-tube practices in SS1/TS4 and CS2. The major uncertainty is whether Re₃S₄Br can form at lower temperature than the Mo analogue; if so, the severity would decrease.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Re metal and the need to reconstruct a layered Re sulfide bromide framework from elemental/halide precursors. TS1 shows that a sulfide bromide can be made by this route, but it also requires annealing for purity, indicating nontrivial kinetic/phase evolution. Re is less mobile than many transition metals at comparable temperatures, so diffusion likely remains the limiting factor by **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions because SS1/TS4 demonstrates formation of a close Re chalcogen-halide at 900 °C, but it still requires inert handling, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and halide precursors. The use of Re halides and possible alkali bromide mineralizers introduces air/moisture sensitivity and sealed-tube hazards. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact S/Br target in this route, so the score remains moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 2

This route has better kinetics than Route 1 because SS1/TS4 uses ReCl₅ and halide salts, suggesting halide-assisted transport/mineralization can help assemble Re chalcogen-halide frameworks. The structural match to the target is strong: both are triclinic layered Re chalcogen-halides with ReQ₅/ReQ₄X square-pyramidal motifs, from SS1/TS4. However, SS1/TS4 reports low yields and multiple products, so phase selectivity is a significant uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses evacuated ampoules and volatile Br₂, so it incurs deductions for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed systems, multistep processing, and toxicity; these are directly supported by Br₂ CVT in CS2 and evacuated CVT in CS1/CS4. The proposed 850–950 °C growth window avoids the >1000 °C penalty and is consistent with CS1/CS4 and SS1/TS4 temperature scales. The uncertainty is whether Br₂ transport will preserve, rather than over-brominate or decompose, Re₃S₄Br.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT should improve mass transport relative to bulk diffusion because halogen transport is demonstrated in chemically similar sulfides using Br₂ or Cl₂ (CS1, CS2, CS4). Crystallization of layered sulfide/chalcogenide crystals by CVT is strongly supported by CS1/CS2/CS4, and bromine is chemically compatible with the target composition. Composition control is the main kinetic/compositional weakness because Br₂ is both transport agent and constituent; phase-control issues in SS1/TS4 support this caution.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests no radius-driven amorphization risk. The structure is low-dimensional but built from well-defined ReS₅/ReS₄Br square pyramids, and analogous crystalline Re/Mo chalcogen-halide structures exist in SS1/TS4 and TS1. The remaining uncertainty is that layered phases can stack-fault or disorder, but that is more a crystallinity/phase-purity issue than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re₃S₄Br is likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0140 eV/atom, and many CS/SS/TS analogues are stable or near-stable. The strongest support comes from TS1, a sulfide bromide with MoS₄Br square pyramids, and SS1/TS4, a triclinic layered Re chalcogen-halide with closely related ReQ₅/ReQ₄Cl square-pyramidal sheets. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or mineralization, because it can overcome slow Re diffusion and promote layered crystal growth, as shown by CS2 and SS1/TS4. The most critical limiting factor is Br/S chemical-potential control: sulfur and bromine are volatile, and excess bromine can alter transport, stoichiometry, or competing halide formation. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with CVT or halide-assisted sealed-tube synthesis preferred over a purely diffusion-controlled elemental reaction.
