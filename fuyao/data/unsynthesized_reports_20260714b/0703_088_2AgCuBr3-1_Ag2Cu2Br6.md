### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is **0.0170 eV/atom**, placing it well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Comparable synthesized analogues include CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom (SS1/TS2), CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom (SS3/TS1), CuHgSI at 0.0210 eV/atom (SS5/TS3), and CuBr₂ at 0.0084 eV/atom (CS5/TS5). This supports the view that AgCuBr₃ is near-stable rather than highly metastable. Remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated low-hull phase is accessible before competing AgBr/CuBr₂ phases crystallize [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate score because it requires vacuum sealing, directly supported by SS3–SS5/TS1/TS3, and because bromide volatility makes sealed containment prudent [Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 is penalized for corrosive HBr, sealed hydrothermal operation, and multistep crystallization, all consistent with CS2 and SS1/TS2. Route 3 is more severe because molten/fluxed bromides need sealed tubes and may require quench or flux removal, as in CS1 and CS3. Route 4 has the mildest conditions because CS4/CS5/TS5 show room-temperature evaporation for CuBr₂, but it still requires acidic bromide chemistry for Ag solubility [Reasonable Inference]. Route 5 is penalized for vacuum, volatile transport chemistry, and uncertain transport-agent hazards, based only weakly on TS4.

#### F3 — Reaction kinetics

Route 1 benefits from reactive halide precursors and low-temperature analogues in SS3–SS5, but solid-state Ag/Cu ordering still requires diffusion over days to weeks [Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 has the best kinetic profile because hydrothermal HBr can dissolve/transport metal bromide complexes, and SS1/TS2 reports crystalline mixed-metal bromide-containing product after 24 h. Route 3 also has favorable transport because melts and fluxes bypass solid diffusion, as indicated by CS1 and CS3, but phase selection is more delicate because CS1 highlights metastable quench dependence. Route 4 is kinetically easy but chemically uncertain, since CS4/CS5/TS5 only prove CuBr₂ solution crystallization and not ternary AgCuBr₃ formation. Route 5 has the weakest kinetics/composition score because TS4 supports halide CVT generally, but not Ag/Cu bromide co-transport.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines large Ag(I)-centered AgBr₆ octahedra with smaller Cu-centered CuCuBr₃ trigonal pyramids, so cation size/coordination mismatch is significant [Reasonable Inference from target coordination and ionic-radius trends]. The structure also includes both corner-sharing and edge-sharing polyhedral connectivity, explicitly stated in the target description. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the target unit-cell atom count exceeds 40, so C2 is not triggered. These factors justify a medium amorphization/disorder risk score rather than a low-risk score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCuBr₃ is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0170 eV/atom, and several similar experimentally synthesized halide/chalcogenide phases lie in the same low-hull range (SS1/TS2, SS3/TS1, SS5/TS3, CS5/TS5). The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis most strongly through sealed-tube and hydrothermal bromide chemistry, especially because SS1/TS2 demonstrates successful mixed-metal bromide-containing hydrothermal growth and SS3–SS5 show sealed-tube mixed-metal halide/chalcogenide formation. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and complexation chemistry of bromides, which allows low-temperature crystallization in sealed, acidic, molten, or flux media [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1, CS2, SS1/TS2, and CS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against AgBr, CuBr₂, CuBr, and possible redox-shifted copper bromides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is realistically synthesizable, with hydrothermal HBr growth and sealed binary-bromide reaction as the most defensible first attempts.
