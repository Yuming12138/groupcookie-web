### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic-stability score. Several analogues are also on or very near the hull, including CS2, CS4, CS5, SS5/TS3, and TS4, which supports the broader feasibility of stable halide/chalcogenide phases. However, thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for a low-symmetry mixed-anion Hf phase; this uncertainty is reflected in F2/F3 rather than F1.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile S/Se/Br/I components, which is consistent with CS1, CS4, CS5, and SS1/TS2 but imposes handling and pressure-control penalties. The proposed temperature is not extreme compared with CS1 at 900 °C and SS1/TS2 at 550 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied. The main uncertainty is whether Hf metal reacts sufficiently without excessive formation of HfS₂/HfSe₂ or Hf halides.

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state reaction from Hf metal is kinetically difficult because Hf is refractory and surface-oxide-prone; this is a physicochemical inference, while long sealed-tube times in CS1, CS4, CS5, and SS1/TS2 support slow kinetics for related systems. Halogens can act as mineralizers, as suggested by SS1/TS2 and SS2, but full ordering of S/Se/I/Br over multiple anion sites likely remains slow. Therefore Route 1 receives the lowest kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits the vacuum, volatile transport-agent, and multi-step penalties from CVT methods in SS2, SS3, and TS5. Its temperatures are moderate relative to refractory Hf chemistry and do not exceed 1000 °C in the preferred program. The main severity issue is sealed-ampoule pressure and corrosive halogen vapor.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by forming mobile halide/chalcogenide vapor species; this is directly supported by iodine transport in SS2 and SS3. Structural transferability is good but not perfect: SS2 involves Zr–S/Se chemistry and SS1/TS2 involves a 2D transition-metal sulfide bromide, while the target requires simultaneous S/Se/I/Br ordering. The route is promising for crystals but may suffer from composition drift across the transport gradient.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has similar severity deductions to Routes 1 and 2 because it still requires sealed, anhydrous, volatile-halide conditions. CS2 explicitly supports halide flux use in evacuated silica vessels at 850 °C, and CS4 supports slow cooling of sulfide chloride crystals. The flux may add post-synthesis handling complexity but does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten halides can increase precursor activity and ion mobility, as CS2 reports that NaCl or excess metal chloride flux secures complete and fast reaction and single-crystalline products. Slow cooling in CS4 and CS1 supports crystal growth from such sealed high-temperature reactions. The remaining uncertainty is whether alkali halide fluxes contaminate or destabilize the Hf mixed-anion phase.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses lower temperatures, but it still requires air-free handling of Hf tetrahalides and sealed conditions, so the same atmosphere/volatility/toxicity penalties apply. CS3 supports the concept that metal tetrahalides can undergo lower-temperature anion-exchange chemistry, but that evidence is for ammonolysis rather than chalcogenide-halide formation. Therefore severity is not lower enough to offset the uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 4

Metathesis from HfS₂/HfSe₂ could reduce Hf metal diffusion limitations, a [Reasonable Inference] supported indirectly by chalcogenide-feed use in SS3. However, conversion into the target requires substantial anion rearrangement and mixed-halide ordering, with no direct CS/SS/TS analogue. This gives moderate but not high kinetic confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise. The described structure contains corner- and edge-sharing Hf octahedra but no face-sharing, and the formula unit is relatively small, so amorphization risk is low. Related 2D crystalline chalcogenide halides in CS1 and SS1/TS2 further support crystallizability, although mixed-anion disorder remains possible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through sealed-ampoule halide/chalcogenide chemistry: SS1/TS2 is structurally relevant as a 2D sulfide bromide, SS2 supports Zr/Hf-family chalcogenide transport with iodine, and CS2/CS4 support flux or slow-cooled sulfide halide crystallization. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted mobility, either through a reactive halide flux or vapor transport, because it can overcome slow Hf diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometric control of four anions, especially volatile Br/I and S/Se, during sealed high-temperature synthesis. Overall, the target should be synthesizable with careful ampoule design and chemical-potential control, with flux growth the most attractive first attempt.
