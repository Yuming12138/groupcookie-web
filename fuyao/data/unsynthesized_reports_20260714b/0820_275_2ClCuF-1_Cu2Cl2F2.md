### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E above hull is 0.0440 eV/atom, placing it in the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom category. CS gives useful support because synthesized analogues include CuOCl at 0.079 eV/atom [CS1] and CdOCl at 0.321 eV/atom [CS5], indicating that metastable layered oxyhalide/halide-related phases can be isolated. TS gives especially relevant support because thermodynamic analogues sit near the same metastability range, including 0.0216 eV/atom [TS1], 0.0492 eV/atom [TS3], and 0.0734 eV/atom [TS4]. SS is structurally supportive but provides insufficient direct evidence for CuClF thermodynamics, so SS is treated as partially neutral for F1.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Hydrothermal analogues are made at modest temperatures of 210–250 °C [CS1, CS5, SS4], so no high-temperature deduction applies. The route does require sealed hydrothermal equipment [CS1, SS4, CS5] and likely fluoride/HF handling [Reasonable Inference], so deductions were applied for sealed volatile/corrosive chemistry and toxic fluoride reagents. CS and SS strongly support the general conditions; TS2 supports hydrothermal growth but also indicates that some hydrothermal systems may be very slow.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Solution-mediated transport gives high precursor activity and fast ion redistribution relative to solid-state routes, consistent with hydrothermal Cu phases forming at 230–250 °C [CS1, SS4]. The structural reconstruction score is not maximal because known hydrothermal Cu–Cl analogues are oxy/hydroxychlorides [CS1, SS4, TS2], while the target requires anhydrous F substitution. The largest uncertainty is whether water-containing conditions can avoid Cu(OH)Cl/CuOCl.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis is supported by CS3 at 320 °C, CS4 at 550 °C, and TS5 using evacuated ampoules, so temperatures are moderate. Deductions are needed for vacuum/dry sealed conditions [CS4, TS5], volatile halide precursors [CS3, CS4, TS5], and moisture-sensitive/toxic fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids hydrolysis but increases equipment/material compatibility constraints.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Anhydrous copper halides should react more readily than refractory oxides, and analogous sealed halide reactions occur at 320–550 °C [CS3, CS4]. However, solid-state anion ordering between F and Cl may be kinetically limited, and TS5 shows that sealed ampoule reactions can require grinding and long annealing to reach purity. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the final Cu–Cl–F sheet is not directly inherited from a simple precursor.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires dry halogen/vacuum handling, sealed tubes, and multistep operations; these requirements are directly illustrated by FeCl₃ CVT [TS4]. Toxic/corrosive gas handling is significant because TS4 uses chlorine gas, and fluoride-containing transport would add further corrosion risk [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the condition severity is higher than Routes 1–2.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
TS4 confirms that layered metal chlorides can be grown by vapor transport, but no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates mixed Cu–Cl–F CVT. Composition control is likely difficult because Cl and F chemical potentials must both be maintained [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore this route is better viewed as a crystal-growth refinement strategy than as the primary synthesis route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Cu, so cation-size mismatch is absent; this is directly from the target composition. The structure is a simple 2D sheet and no large unit cell or mixed face/edge/vertex connectivity is reported in the target description. CS/SS/TS analogues broadly support layered crystalline products [CS1, SS4, TS2], but they do not provide exact target amorphization evidence, so uncertainty remains neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuClF is likely a **slightly metastable but experimentally accessible** phase because its E above hull is only 0.0440 eV/atom, within the range where many inorganic metastable phases can be isolated. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the formation of layered Cu–Cl frameworks under hydrothermal conditions [CS1, SS4], while TS analogues show that phases with similar thermodynamic stability can be synthesized [TS1–TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature, solution-mediated halide transport, which can assemble layered Cu–halide sheets without high-temperature decomposition [CS1, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is anion competition: hydrothermal Cu–Cl chemistry readily forms O/OH-containing CuOCl or Cu(OH)Cl [CS1, SS4, TS2], so fluoride activity and water/basicity must be carefully controlled. The sealed-tube route avoids hydrolysis but introduces volatility, fluoride compatibility, and anion-ordering challenges [CS3, CS4, TS5].
