### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0220 eV/atom, which lies well inside the commonly synthesizable metastability window and receives 95/100 by the required rubric. CS1 and SS1 support this because the closest chemical/structural analogue AgTlSe is on the hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. TS1–TS4 also support this because thermodynamic analogues have E_hull ≈0.0199–0.0462 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized. The main uncertainty is whether the reported Pmn2₁ AgTlS polymorph is the same experimentally obtained TlAgS polymorph in CS1/SS1.

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 is mild in temperature because CS1/SS1 specify 570 K, but it requires evacuation and sealing, so vacuum and volatile-component deductions apply. Thallium compounds are toxic, so a toxicity deduction is necessary [Reasonable Inference]. CS1/SS1 describe a simple annealing route with no high pressure or complex equipment, so no high-temperature, high-pressure, or multi-step deductions are applied. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for this exact AgTlS route and is neutral for Route 1 severity transferability.

#### F3: Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because CS1/SS1 explicitly report preparation of TlAgS-family compounds at only 570 K, indicating that diffusion/reaction is feasible at low temperature. The precursor activity is moderate because Ag metal is less reactive than preformed sulfide powders, but thallium chalcogenide plus sulfur likely provides sufficient chalcogen chemical potential [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is scored high because the source includes TlAgS itself, although the target Pmn2₁ structure is not explicitly confirmed in the synthesis snippet. Remaining uncertainty is phase identity and whether the target layered polymorph needs a particular cooling protocol.

#### F2: Route 2

Route 2 follows TS2, where AgCuS is made at 300 °C in an evacuated ampule from binary sulfides. It shares the vacuum/sealed-system penalty and adds a thallium toxicity penalty [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high temperature and high pressure, and if commercial Ag₂S/Tl₂S are used, it is not a ≥3-step route. CS1/SS1 only indirectly support this precursor choice because they use thallium chalcogenide but not explicitly Ag₂S + Tl₂S.

#### F3: Route 2

Binary sulfides should be more kinetically active than elements because Ag–S and Tl–S bonding already exists [Reasonable Inference], and TS2 demonstrates low-temperature Ag sulfide metathesis at 300 °C. Diffusion is favorable because both TS2 and CS1/SS1 show related phases forming at low temperatures. Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-high, but TS2 reports minor elemental Ag impurity, and TS3 shows long annealing/mechanical alloying may be needed for high-purity Ag sulfides. The uncertainty is whether Tl₂S and Ag₂S interdiffuse fast enough without a liquid/flux component.

#### F2: Route 3

Route 3 is based on CS5, which uses evacuated carbon-coated fused-silica tubes, 723–773 K temperatures, long cooling, and flux removal. The route is not high-pressure or extremely high-temperature, but vacuum, volatile Tl/chalcogen species, toxicity, and multi-step flux handling all apply. SS and TS provide insufficient direct flux-growth evidence for AgTlS specifically, so they are neutral-to-indirect for condition severity. The route is experimentally more complex than Route 1.

#### F3: Route 3

Flux transport gives the best kinetic environment because CS5 demonstrates Tl₂Q flux growth over long cooling schedules. Ion diffusion should be excellent because the reacting species are transported in a melt/flux rather than solely through solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is less certain because CS5 is Hg–Tl–S rather than Ag–Tl–S, even though it is a layered thallium sulfide. The main uncertainty is whether AgTlS has sufficient solubility and crystallizes before competing Tl–S or Ag–S phases.

#### F2: Route 4

Route 4 is supported by high-temperature elemental sealed-tube syntheses of Ag chalcogenides in SS5/TS1 and complex Ag selenides in SS4, but it is harsher than necessary for TlAgS. Vacuum, volatile components, toxicity, and multi-step thermal control penalties apply. No high-pressure deduction is needed because the analogues use evacuated sealed ampoules/tubes rather than multi-anvil pressure. The main uncertainty is ampoule safety and stoichiometry retention with elemental Tl and S.

#### F3: Route 4

Elemental reactions require complete compound formation and are therefore kinetically less favorable than binary-sulfide or flux routes [Reasonable Inference]. SS5/TS1 show elemental Ag–Cu–S compounds can form in sealed tubes, but TS4/TS5 show Tl–Cu–Se systems can yield multiple phases and phase mixtures, indicating phase-selection risk in Tl chalcogenides. Diffusion is not expected to be refractory for Ag/Tl sulfides, but volatility limits useful temperature. This route is feasible but inferior to the directly evidenced low-temperature TlAgS route.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically and geometrically distinct cation sites: Ag is distorted trigonal planar with Ag–S ≈2.56–2.65 Å, while Tl is 2-coordinate with Tl–S ≈3.06 Å, supporting one amorphization trigger through cation size/coordination mismatch [Target description; Reasonable Inference]. The target appears structurally simple and 2D, and no >40-atom cell is reported; however, exact atom count is not provided, so CS/SS/TS are insufficient and neutral for that check. Mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity is not described, so that check is also insufficient/neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgTlS is likely synthesizable and probably either a near-ground-state phase or a very low-energy metastable phase, because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS1/SS1, which explicitly state that TlAgS was prepared by annealing thallium chalcogenide, silver, and sulfur/chalcogen at 570 K in sealed evacuated silica tubes. TS analogues support the general idea that Ag sulfides and low-E_hull chalcogenides with E_hull ≈0.02–0.05 eV/atom can be synthesized, but TS does not directly prove the Ag–Tl–S target. The most important enabling factor is the low-temperature sealed-tube chalcogenide chemistry demonstrated for TlAgS itself. The main limiting factor is maintaining Tl/S stoichiometry and avoiding competing Ag₂S/Tl₂S or alternate AgTlS polymorphs during sealed-tube annealing.
