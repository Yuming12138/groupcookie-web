### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0270 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability stability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom [Target]. Several chemically or thermodynamically related phases are on or near the hull: NbSe₂Cl₂ is 0.0 eV/atom [CS1], Nb(SBr)₂ is 0.0008 eV/atom [CS2/SS1], and thermodynamic analogues fall around 0.02–0.04 eV/atom [TS1–TS5]. The main uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee clean phase formation in volatile halide systems, as phase-control issues are reported for related chloride/halide syntheses [CS1; TS1].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 500 °C, supported by Nb(SBr)₂ synthesis at 500 °C [CS2/SS1] and Nb chloride/chalcogenide sealed-tube syntheses near 530 °C or 750 K [CS3/SS4; SS5]. The severity penalty comes from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/chloride components, and likely air-sensitive TiCl₄/Cl₂ handling [CS2/SS1; SS5; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for the exact Ti(SCl)₂ solid-state route, so TS is used only as a general warning about chloride vapor-pressure control [TS1].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are moderately favorable because the exact structural analogue Nb(SBr)₂ forms in only 2 days at 500 °C [CS2/SS1]. However, Ti metal is refractory and may react slowly unless activated by chloride vapor or finely divided powder [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether Ti⁴⁺, S–S dimers, and bridging chloride sheets assemble directly rather than forming TiS₂, TiCl₃, or TiCl₄-derived phases [Reasonable Inference; phase competition supported generally by TS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

CVT conditions are not extremely hot, with NbSe₂Cl₂ made at 400–515 °C and Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ at ~530 °C [CS1; CS3/SS4]. The penalties arise from evacuated ampoules, volatile chlorides/halogens, and air-sensitive/toxic chloride sources [CS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The method is operationally more demanding than simple sintering but remains standard for layered chalcogenide halides [CS1].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT should improve kinetics by transporting Ti/Cl/S species through the vapor phase, analogous to NbX₂Y₂ CVT growth [CS1]. Direct crystallization of related layered chalcogenide halides in a gradient supports a favorable crystallization pathway [CS1; CS3/SS4]. The main kinetic and compositional uncertainty is narrow phase stability, since CS1 notes contamination/phase-window issues and TS1 shows multiple chloride phases under similar vapor-transport conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Flux growth uses standard evacuated silica tubes and temperatures around 650–750 °C, consistent with Ti sulfide flux synthesis at 750 °C and Nb sulfide flux synthesis at 500–700 °C or 973 K [TS3; SS2; SS3]. It is penalized more heavily because it includes flux addition, slow cooling, and likely flux removal, making it more multi-step [SS3; TS3]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS for Ti(SCl)₂ flux growth, so this route is more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth can significantly improve mass transport and crystal growth, as shown for CuTi₂S₄ and Nb sulfide frameworks [TS3; SS2/SS3]. The diffusion score is helped by the flux/liquid mechanism but limited by Ti’s refractory character and the need to maintain chloride stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is less certain because the flux analogues are not the same M(SCl)₂ sheet type [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation size disorder does not drive amorphization [Target]. The structure is a relatively simple 2D sheet with Ti, S, and Cl sites and no evidence for a very large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is low, although defects or off-stoichiometry from volatile S/Cl loss could broaden diffraction peaks without true amorphization [Reasonable Inference from volatile-component issues in CS1/CS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti(SCl)₂ is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from the isostructural Nb(SBr)₂ analogue made by sealed-tube synthesis and from chemically similar Nb chalcogenide chlorides grown by CVT [CS2/SS1; CS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated stability of 2D M–chalcogen–halide sheets with 8-coordinate transition metals, chalcogen–chalcogen dimers, and bridging halides [CS1; CS2/SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is control of sulfur and chlorine chemical potentials in sealed ampoules, because related halide systems show multiple competing phases and sensitivity to temperature gradients [CS1; TS1; SS5]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with CVT or sealed-tube reactive halogenation preferred over flux growth for first attempts.
