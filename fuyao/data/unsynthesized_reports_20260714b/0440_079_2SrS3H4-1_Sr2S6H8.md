### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is only 0.0040 eV/atom above hull, placing it in the highest stability bin, so F1 = 95. Thermodynamic analogues include NaHS and KHS at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [TS1, TS4] and Sr(NH₂)₂ near the hull at 0.0013 eV/atom [CS1; TS5]. The main uncertainty is that near-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or room-temperature persistence, especially for H₂S-containing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature because alkali hydrogensulfides form at 50–150 °C [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The severity comes from H₂S toxicity/volatility, sealed autoclave handling, Ar glovebox operations, and Sr metal air sensitivity [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct sulfur-route evidence and is neutral for the H₂S handling details, but CS supports that Sr hydrogen-rich salts can require controlled reactive-fluid synthesis [CS1; TS5].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because direct metal + H₂S reactions form hydrogensulfides at low temperature over 1–3 d [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The S–H distances in the analogues, 1.34 Å, closely match the target 1.35–1.38 Å, supporting local bonding transferability [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The uncertainty is structural: the target’s SrS₇ sheet with mixed face/edge/corner sharing is more complex than simple alkali hydrogensulfides, so complete phase ordering may be harder [Target; SS1–SS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 shares the same H₂S sealed-autoclave severity as Route 1, supported by hydrogensulfide syntheses requiring autoclaves, gloveboxes, gas handling, and sometimes liquid-N₂ condensation [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. It avoids Sr metal as the main reagent if SrS is used, but SrS is moisture-sensitive and H₂S remains toxic and volatile [Reasonable Inference; SS1–SS4]. CS gives insufficient evidence for SrS hydrosulfidation and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

SrS + H₂S is chemically well matched to the target formula by the formal reaction SrS + 2H₂S → SrH₄S₃ [Reasonable Inference]. The hydrogensulfide analogues show that S–H phases can be made at low temperature with fluid/gas-assisted transport [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The uncertainty is whether dense SrS fully converts without leaving SrS cores or forming simpler Sr(HS)₂-type products; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for SrS conversion.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure solvothermal H₂S is operationally challenging because analogous Sr/NH₃ and amide syntheses use 80–5000 bar reactive-fluid conditions and specialized autoclaves [CS1; CS3–CS5; SS5; TS5]. Under the specified rubric, pressure below 5 GPa does not trigger the high-pressure deduction, but practical difficulty remains high [CS1; TS5]. H₂S toxicity and corrosion add additional severity by [Reasonable Inference] based on the H₂S autoclave analogues [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4].

#### F3 — Route 3

Reactive-fluid high-pressure chemistry supports formation and crystallization of hydrogen-rich salts at moderate temperatures, as shown for amides in NH₃ [CS1; CS3–CS5; SS5; TS5]. A high H₂S chemical potential could stabilize the target’s H₂S-rich SrH₄S₃ composition [Reasonable Inference]. However, no direct high-pressure Sr–S–H analogue is provided, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity remain uncertain; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation type, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger [Target]. The provided description does not establish a unit cell atom count above 40, so C2 is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for the target’s full cell size. Mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing SrS₇ polyhedra are explicitly present, giving one amorphization-risk trigger and F4 = 80 [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrH₄S₃ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0040 eV/atom, comparable to stable hydrogensulfide analogues [TS1, TS4] and near-hull Sr amide chemistry [CS1; TS5]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar alkali hydrogensulfides made by low-temperature H₂S autoclave reactions [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high H₂S chemical potential in a sealed, dry, oxygen-free system to preserve S–H bonding and the H-rich stoichiometry [SS1–SS4; TS1, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with simpler Sr–S phases such as SrS or lower-H hydrogensulfides, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because the exact SrH₄S₃ stoichiometry is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. Overall, the material appears synthesizable but requires careful gas handling and phase-control experiments.
