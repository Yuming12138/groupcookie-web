### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound. Chemical analogues Gd₂Te₃ and Er₂Te₃ are also listed with E_hull = 0 in CS-2 and CS-3, supporting the stability of rare-earth sesquitellurides. SS/TS analogues are ternary BaLn₂Te₄ phases rather than binary Y₂Te₃, but their on-hull status supports general Y/Ln–Te bonding stability (SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: on-hull thermodynamics does not guarantee the trigonal P-6m2 polymorph will be the easiest kinetic product.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The condition severity is substantial because the direct Gd₂Te₃ analogue required evacuated tubes, two heat treatments, Ar grinding, and several days at up to 1313 K (CS-2). Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Y metal is comparatively dense and oxidation-prone, and solid-state diffusion through Y–Te product layers can be slow [Reasonable Inference supported by multi-day CS-2 and SS-1/TS-1 reactions]. The main advantage is chemical directness: CS-2 demonstrates single-phase Ln₂Te₃ formation from elements. The main uncertainty is whether Y₂Te₃ adopts the desired trigonal layered polymorph rather than another Y–Te binary or polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT / vapor-assisted growth

CVT avoids the most severe bulk-diffusion limitations and is directly supported by Er₂Te₃ single-crystal growth using ErCl₃ at 950→800 °C for 3 days (CS-3). Condition severity remains moderate because it requires sealed ampoules, halide transport chemistry, dry/inert handling, and possibly high-vacuum preparation of anhydrous chloride transport agent (CS-3). Kinetics are favorable due to vapor transport and recrystallization, but polymorph selection is uncertain because the CS-3 Er₂Te₃ analogue is orthorhombic rather than trigonal. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for binary CVT, so this route relies mainly on CS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure synthesis

High-pressure synthesis has the harshest condition penalty because related lanthanide tellurides were synthesized at up to 100 kbar and 1200 °C (CS-1, CS-5). It may be useful if the trigonal omega-like layered polymorph requires pressure or rapid quench stabilization [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 explicitly includes Y among the lanthanide metals used. However, CS-1/CS-5 also report difficult phase windows, impurity formation, and instability/decomposition concerns for related tellurides. SS/TS give insufficient high-pressure evidence, so this route is a fallback rather than preferred first attempt.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: flux growth

Flux growth has the best numerical balance because similar BaLn₂Te₄ crystals were obtained at only 850 °C in BaBr₂/KBr flux for 6 days (SS-2, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). The molten medium should improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to direct solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is that the provided flux examples are Ba-containing ternaries, not binary Y₂Te₃, and some were obtained unexpectedly with phase-control challenges (SS-2/TS-2). Therefore, flux is promising for crystals but less proven for bulk phase-pure Y₂Te₃.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not an amorphization driver. The structure is likely a small-cell binary phase, so the >40 atom unit-cell risk is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula/space group]. The main disorder risk is mixed face/corner/edge-sharing YTe₆ connectivity in the target, and mixed octahedral connectivity is also common in SS/TS structural analogues (SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). This gives a low–medium amorphization/defect risk rather than a high glass-forming tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y₂Te₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and chemically related Gd₂Te₃ and Er₂Te₃ are also stable/on-hull in the CS set (CS-2, CS-3). The strongest direct synthesis support comes from CS: Gd₂Te₃ can be made phase-pure by sealed-tube sintering, and Er₂Te₃ can be grown by CVT (CS-2, CS-3). SS/TS strongly support the stability and crystallization of Y/Ln–Te octahedral frameworks, but mostly through Ba-containing ternary analogues, so their transferability to binary Y₂Te₃ is indirect (SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is sealed/inert handling that maintains Te activity and prevents Y oxidation. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Y–Te binaries and polymorphs, especially because direct analogues show intermediate or impurity telluride phases before reaching the desired composition (CS-2).
