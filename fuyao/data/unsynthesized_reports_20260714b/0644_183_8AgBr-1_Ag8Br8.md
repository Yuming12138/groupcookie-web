### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0240 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom category [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally reported: CuHgSCl has E_hull ≈0.0238 eV/atom [TS2], CuHgSBr ≈0.0309 eV/atom [TS1], Ag3CuS2 ≈0.0388 eV/atom [TS4], and AgCuS ≈0.0306 eV/atom [TS5]. This supports experimental accessibility, but it does not prove the Pc AgBr polymorph is the ambient ground state because common AgBr polymorphism is not addressed by CS/SS/TS; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct AgBr ground-state assignment, neutral 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because it uses standard evacuated ampoules, which are common in CS1, CS4, SS2, TS2, and TS5, but vacuum sealing and Br retention add severity. Kinetically, AgBr should be more mobile than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and Ag-containing sulfide reactions at 300 °C in TS5 support relatively favorable cation mobility. The main penalty is reconstructive phase formation: the target is a 2D monoclinic Pc sheet [Target], whereas simple AgBr feedstock may crystallize in a competing dense phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Melt growth is supported by Ag3SI Bridgman growth in sealed quartz ampoules around 700 °C [SS1], so liquid transport gives good kinetic access. The route is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile bromide containment, and multi-step thermal-gradient handling [SS1]. The major uncertainty is phase selection: SS1 reports phase mixtures from melt-grown Ag3SI, and SS2 shows Ag3SI phases depend on annealing/quenching, so melt crystallization may not directly yield the target Pc phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal bromide chemistry is strongly supported by CuHgSBr formation in concentrated HBr at 670 K, where the reaction proceeded quantitatively with no observed side products [TS1]. This route avoids high temperature and solid-state diffusion limitations, giving a strong F3 score [TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that no provided source reports hydrothermal AgBr Pc formation specifically; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for AgBr-specific hydrothermal polymorph selection, neutral 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Chemical bath/solution-film synthesis is mild and experimentally validated for CdS at 80 °C in air [CS3], giving a high F2 score. Solution precipitation of AgBr from Ag+ and Br− is chemically plausible [Reasonable Inference], and thin-film/substrate effects may stabilize a metastable 2D polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. The F3 score is reduced because CS3 reports phase mixing and impurity formation in bath-deposited CdS, implying that crystallinity and phase purity may be difficult to control.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Ag, so there is no cation radius mismatch trigger [Target]. The described asymmetric unit has four Ag and four Br sites, and no evidence is provided for a >40-atom cell [Target]. Although the target has varied Ag coordination and a 2D sheet motif [Target], the description does not explicitly indicate simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral networks, so amorphization risk is assessed as low; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to override this target-structure assessment, neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target AgBr phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than an unambiguously stable ambient ground-state phase, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.024 eV/atom [Target]. The thermodynamic analogues strongly support accessibility of phases in this energy range, as several TS examples with E_hull ≈0.02–0.04 eV/atom have reported syntheses [TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. The chemical and structural analogues support sealed-tube, melt, hydrothermal, and solution-film processing, but none directly confirms monoclinic Pc AgBr, so polymorph control remains the central uncertainty [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and soft-lattice chemistry expected for Ag–halide systems, aided by low-temperature solution or hydrothermal transport [TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is competition with the common dense AgBr phase and possible bromine stoichiometry/light sensitivity during processing [Reasonable Inference].
