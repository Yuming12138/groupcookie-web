### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0270 eV/atom, placing it in the high-stability rubric range of 90–100 (Target). Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, including K2RuN3O5Cl3 at 0.025 eV/atom and Rb2PdN2(O2Cl)2 at 0.036 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized phases, supporting accessibility of slightly metastable complex salts (TS5, TS1). Structurally similar CsRuF6 and RbRuF6 are on the hull, supporting robust Cs/Ru fluoride chemistry, but they lack N–S ligands (SS1, SS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether the N–S ligand destabilizes the phase kinetically even if the computed hull distance is small [insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is penalized heavily because the closest Cs/Ru fluoride analogues require elemental fluorine, dry handling, and specialized fluorination setups (SS1, SS3). The inferred N–S reagent such as NSF3 is likely volatile/toxic and may require sealed or controlled handling [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is modest at ~350 °C, which helps, but hazardous fluorination and multistep control make this route challenging (SS1, SS3).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Solid-state fluorination can form CsRuF6 rapidly at 350 °C, showing reactive gas chemistry can overcome some diffusion limitations (SS1). However, transforming a binary/ternary fluoride network into a RuF5(NS) sheet requires partial structural and ligand reconstruction, and no SS analogue demonstrates N–S insertion [insufficient evidence from SS]. Thus kinetics are only moderate despite good fluoride-framework precedent.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
HF precipitation/evaporation is mild in temperature and pressure because CS1 and CS4 operate at room temperature. The major severity comes from HF toxicity and possible volatile N–S reagents [CS1, CS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS1 also notes hydrolysis issues in related attempts, so moisture and speciation control are nontrivial (CS1).

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and avoids solid-state diffusion, as shown by precipitation/slow evaporation in CS1, CS4, and SS5/TS2. The weak point is ligand assembly: the sources support fluoride octahedral formation but do not directly show Ru–N–S formation in HF [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Therefore kinetics are favorable for crystallization but uncertain for correct anion formation.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Solvothermal synthesis uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed-vessel conditions, as in CS2 at 200 °C for 4 days. It avoids elemental F2 but still involves HF/HBF4-like corrosive media and possibly volatile N–S donors [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. The sealed system is a benefit for retaining volatile components but adds pressure and safety considerations.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Liquid-mediated transport and slow cooling should support ordering and crystal growth, as shown by large hydrothermal crystals in CS2. Acetonitrile recrystallization of CsOIF4 from CsI/IF5 also supports Cs fluorinated salt crystallization from molecular media (CS5). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the N–S ligand survives solvothermal temperatures [insufficient evidence from CS].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 is mild during the final crystallization because Ru complex salts in TS5 crystallize at room temperature and TS1 precipitates N-ligand metal salts under mild aqueous conditions. It is penalized for requiring a preformed fluorinated Ru–NS complex, which is a multistep and potentially hazardous precursor [Reasonable Inference]. If the precursor is isolated, final Cs⁺ metathesis should not require severe temperature or pressure [Reasonable Inference based on TS5].

**F3 — Route 4.**  
This route has the best kinetics because the target RuF5(NS) coordination sphere is assumed to be preassembled, so crystallization mainly involves Cs⁺ packing and fluoride coordination [Target; Reasonable Inference]. TS5 supports solution crystallization of Ru–N ligand complexes, and TS1 supports mild precipitation of thermodynamically similar N-ligand salts. The uncertainty is precursor availability, because no provided source reports [Ru(NS)F5]⁻ directly [insufficient evidence from TS].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Cs/Ru cation-size contrast, indicated by 11-coordinate Cs–F distances near 3.05–3.40 Å versus octahedral Ru–F/Ru–N distances near 1.76–1.95 Å (Target). This triggers one amorphization-risk criterion, but the target description does not show a >40 atom cell or explicit vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so those checks are not triggered [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The low-dimensional sheet structure may increase stacking/disorder risk, but 2D crystallinity is not itself a rubric trigger [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsRuNSF5 is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom (Target). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support Cs–fluoride coordination, Ru–fluoride octahedra, and mild crystallization of Ru/N-ligand salts, but no source directly validates the specific Ru–N–S fluoride ligand environment (CS1, CS5, SS1, SS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the ability to preform or preserve the RuF5(NS)⁻ coordination unit, which would make Cs⁺ crystallization comparatively straightforward [Reasonable Inference based on TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is N–S ligand compatibility with strongly fluorinating or HF-rich conditions, because the closest fluoride syntheses either use F2 or HF and could over-fluorinate or hydrolyze the N–S unit (CS1, SS1, SS3). Overall, molecular-complex metathesis is the preferred strategy, with solvothermal fluoride crystallization as the best fallback.
