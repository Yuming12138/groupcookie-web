### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom, which falls within the high synthesizability stability bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, such as Ag₃CuS₂ at 0.046 eV/atom and other Ag/Cu/S phases near 0.03–0.039 eV/atom, have reported syntheses (**TS1–TS3**). However, a chemically similar near-hull material at 0.0425 eV/atom is reported to have reproducibility/phase-selection difficulty (**CS3**), so the score is high but not perfect.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed-ampoule chalcogenide conditions supported by Pt–Se, Bi–Se, and Ag/Cu/S analogues (**CS2**, **CS4**, **TS1–TS3**). The main deductions come from vacuum/inert sealed conditions, Se volatility, and multi-step heating/annealing; these requirements are consistent with sealed-tube chalcogenide syntheses in **CS4** and **TS1–TS3**. There is no high-pressure requirement and the proposed temperatures are below 1000 °C, so the severity is moderate rather than extreme.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct reaction of Au metal with Se is likely limited by Au’s density and diffusion resistance **[Reasonable Inference]**, although Se melt/vapor can improve contact. Similar sealed-tube chalcogenide analogues often require long anneals, quenching, or regrinding (**CS2**, **TS1**, **TS4**), indicating nontrivial ordering kinetics. Because the target structure must form from elements rather than from a structurally related precursor, the structural reconstruction score is modest.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 also requires sealed vacuum handling and volatile Se control, as in **SS1**, **CS5**, and **CS4**. The flux route adds procedural steps such as flux drying, slow cooling, and product recovery, but it avoids very high temperature and high pressure. The severity score is therefore similar to Route 1, but the practical kinetics are better.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth improves mass transport and nucleation relative to direct Au + Se diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**. This is supported by LiCl/KCl flux growth of layered BiSe-related sheets (**CS5**) and by slow-cooled Au chalcogenide synthesis in a sealed tube (**SS1**). Remaining uncertainty is phase selection: the target is a specific tetragonal Au₂Se sheet, and near-hull analogues sometimes show competing polymorphs or reproducibility problems (**CS3**, **SS2**, **SS4**, **TS4**).

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Thin-film selenization avoids high bulk diffusion temperatures but still requires vacuum or controlled Se vapor, so the controlled-atmosphere and volatile-precursor deductions apply. No direct thin-film evidence is present from **CS**, **SS**, or **TS**, so this route is assessed mainly by **[Reasonable Inference]**. The process is multi-step but not high pressure or >1000 °C.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Au and Se can be supplied as standard vapor/film sources **[Reasonable Inference]**, but no direct Au₂Se thin-film analogue is provided by **CS/SS/TS**. Crystallization may require post-annealing and careful Se chemical potential control, and composition control is expected to be difficult because Se is volatile **[Reasonable Inference]**. AuBr analogues show low-temperature Au compound transformations (**SS2**, **SS4**), but they also show polymorph and reproducibility issues, limiting confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Au, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The structure description is simple, with one Au site and one Se site, and does not indicate a >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network; insufficient evidence from **CS/SS/TS** is needed because this is primarily target-structure-based. The amorphization risk is therefore low, though thin-film deposition could still produce amorphous material kinetically **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Au₂Se is likely a slightly metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom. The analogue data support synthesis reasonably well: metal selenides and Au chalcogenides are accessible in sealed tubes or fluxes (**CS2**, **CS4**, **SS1**, **CS5**), and thermodynamically similar near-hull materials have been synthesized (**TS1–TS3**). The most important enabling factor is the low energy above hull, which places the compound within a commonly synthesizable metastability window. The most important limiting factor is phase control against competing Au–Se compositions or polymorphs, as near-hull/polymorphic analogues can show sluggish transitions and reproducibility issues (**CS3**, **SS2**, **SS4**, **TS4**). The best experimental strategy is therefore a sealed flux or self-flux screen with tight Se chemical-potential control and slow cooling.
