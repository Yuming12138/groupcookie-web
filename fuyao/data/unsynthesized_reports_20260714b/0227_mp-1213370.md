### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-scoring boundary of the rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.04–0.08 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable intermetallics, supporting that this energy range is experimentally accessible (TS1–TS5). The major uncertainty is polymorphism: the same RuGa₂ composition has a stable orthorhombic Fddd phase at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS1), so the cubic Fd-3m form is likely metastable.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state sintering.**  
Condition severity is favorable because the direct RuGa₂ analogue uses only Ru, Ga, quartz ampoules, and 1000 °C without high pressure (CS1, SS1). Kinetics are less favorable because elemental Ru is refractory and the reported reaction requires 170 h (CS1, SS1; refractory Ru [Reasonable Inference]). The largest uncertainty is whether the cubic polymorph can be selected rather than the known orthorhombic product (CS1, SS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, Ga flux.**  
Ga flux routes are moderately severe because they require sealed tubes, excess Ga, slow cooling, and flux removal (CS4, SS4). Kinetically, the molten Ga medium should improve Ru transport relative to dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], and related Ga intermetallic crystals are successfully grown from Ga flux (CS4, SS4). The uncertainty is that no source demonstrates cubic RuGa₂ growth by flux, and flux may still equilibrate to orthorhombic RuGa₂ (CS1, SS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 3, arc melting/annealing.**  
Arc melting is a standard route for intermetallics and improves homogeneity through remelting, as shown for structurally and thermodynamically similar materials (SS2, TS2). Severity is higher because arc melting involves very high local temperatures and post-annealing steps (SS2, TS2, TS3). The limiting factor is that high-temperature equilibration may favor the ground-state RuGa₂ polymorph, so quenching or controlled annealing is needed [Reasonable Inference; CS1, SS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 4, CVT.**  
CVT has chemical precedent in Pd₂Ga using GaI₃ and a temperature gradient (CS2). It could enable single-crystal growth and kinetic polymorph selection [Reasonable Inference], but there is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for CVT growth of Ru–Ga phases. The main uncertainty is whether Ru can be transported efficiently by GaI₃/iodide chemistry and whether cubic RuGa₂ nucleates instead of orthorhombic RuGa₂ (CS1, SS1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple binary intermetallic with one Ru site and one Ga site, and the structure is described as edge-sharing RuGa₆ octahedra rather than highly mixed polyhedral connectivity. Ru–Ga crystalline phases are experimentally known (CS1, SS1), which argues against strong amorphization risk. Direct amorphization data are absent from CS/SS/TS, so this score relies partly on [Reasonable Inference] from structural simplicity.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target cubic Fd-3m RuGa₂ is likely a **metastable polymorph**, because the same RuGa₂ composition is reported as a stable orthorhombic Fddd phase with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS1), while the target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS data strongly support that Ru–Ga intermetallic chemistry is experimentally accessible, especially from elemental Ru and Ga in sealed ampoules (CS1, SS1). TS data support that intermetallics with similar modest positive hull energies can be synthesized by melt/anneal or flux-like methods (TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong chemical compatibility of Ru and Ga and the existence of known RuGa₂ synthesis conditions (CS1, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: ordinary equilibrium annealing may yield orthorhombic RuGa₂ rather than the cubic calaverite-like phase (CS1, SS1).
