### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0080 eV/atom, which is deep within the ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket and therefore scored 95 [Target]. TS analogues cluster near the hull, including AuBr at 0.0178 eV/atom and KAg₃S₂ at 0.0093 eV/atom, supporting the idea that low-energy heavy-metal halide/chalcogenide phases can be isolable [TS1, TS3]. CS also contains low-hull binary analogues such as CdS and CuS, although sulfide chemistry does not directly guarantee AgCl polymorph stability [CS1, CS2]. Remaining uncertainty is structural rather than compositional: monoclinic 2D AgCl may be metastable relative to ordinary bulk AgCl despite its low computed hull energy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, solution precipitation / chemical-bath thin film

Condition severity is low because CS1 demonstrates deposition at 80 °C in air with simple wet-chemical handling, and the proposed AgCl route similarly avoids high temperature, high pressure, and sealed ampoules [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. The main F2 deductions are for multistep handling and the need to control silver salts/light sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, soluble Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ precursors should react rapidly, but CS1’s phase/impurity observations warn that chemical-bath deposition does not automatically produce a single desired structure [CS1]. The largest uncertainty is whether a 2D monoclinic AgCl sheet can be nucleated rather than conventional AgCl particles [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, vapor transport / sublimation

F2 is lower because SS2 requires a quartz tube, Cl₂ atmosphere, and a temperature gradient for AuCl vapor transport, while TS2 uses evacuated quartz ampoules for related heavy-metal halide crystal growth [SS2, TS2]. Volatile halide species and toxic chlorine control add significant severity [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. F3 is moderate because vapor transport can provide high-purity crystals, as shown for AuCl and Hg halide systems, but phase purity/polymorph selectivity is a recurring issue in SS/TS halide analogues [SS2, TS1, TS2]. The target’s simple AgCl stoichiometry helps, but the desired 2D polymorph is not directly validated by any vapor-growth reference [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, melt / Bridgman growth

F2 is moderate-to-challenging because SS1 uses sealed quartz ampoules, melting, and slow pulling through a temperature gradient, and similar sealed-tube handling appears in SS4 [SS1, SS4]. Kinetics in a melt should be favorable, and SS1 supports Ag-containing halide crystal growth from a melt [SS1]. However, SS1 also reports phase mixtures from which crystals must be separated, so phase selectivity is a significant limitation [SS1]. The main uncertainty is that melt crystallization is likely to favor the most stable bulk AgCl polymorph rather than a layered monoclinic polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, mechanochemical / low-temperature solid-state metathesis

F2 is moderate because the route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but it uses multiple processing steps and controlled atmosphere/dark handling [TS4, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. TS4 demonstrates that related heavy-metal halide-containing materials can form by long low-temperature sealed solid-state annealing, and SS4 confirms AgCl can be used in sealed solid-state synthesis [TS4, SS4]. Kinetics are aided by reactive salt metathesis and milling, but structural ordering into a specific 2D polymorph is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. SS3 and TS1 show related halide polymorph transformations can be sluggish and poorly reproducible, which reduces confidence [SS3, TS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is chemically simple, with one cation and one anion, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The described structure has one Ag site and one Cl site in a 3-coordinate sheet, and no evidence is provided for a large >40-atom unit cell or mixed polyhedral sharing [Target]. This supports a low amorphization tendency and a high F4 score. The remaining uncertainty is that low-temperature precipitation may produce nanocrystalline or poorly ordered AgCl if nucleation is too fast, even though the structural descriptors themselves do not predict high amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0080 eV/atom [Target]. CS/SS/TS analogues support the general feasibility of low-hull binary and heavy-metal halide/chalcogenide synthesis, including mild bath deposition, sealed-tube solid-state synthesis, melt growth, and vapor transport [CS1, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the simple 1:1 AgCl chemistry combined with very low computed thermodynamic penalty [Target; TS analogues]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control, because structurally similar halides such as AuCl/AuBr show metastability, sluggish transitions, or phase-control difficulty [SS2, SS3, TS1]. The best practical approach is therefore a low-temperature thin-film/solution route that can kinetically trap the 2D sheet before conversion to the ordinary bulk AgCl phase [CS1; Reasonable Inference].
