### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0460 eV/atom, which lies just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bracket [Target]. The exact CsNaSe analogue in the CS and SS data is calculated/reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, strongly supporting the stability of this chemical/structural family [CS1, SS1]. Thermodynamic analogues include experimentally realized phases around E_hull ≈0.020–0.075 eV/atom, showing that small positive hull energies do not preclude synthesis [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target’s 4-coordinate Cs variant is a distinct metastable polymorph relative to the reported 5-coordinate CsNaSe ground-state description [Target, CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 870 K / 597 °C, and no high-pressure apparatus [CS1, SS1]. The main deductions are for sealed handling of volatile/reactive alkali selenides and air/moisture-sensitive precursors/products [CS1, CS3]. Ar atmosphere is required but not penalized under the rubric because the listed atmosphere penalty applies to H₂, NH₃, or vacuum [CS1, SS1]. Uncertainty is mainly ampoule compatibility and pressure control during scale-up [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary Na₂Se and Cs₂Se are already close in chemistry to the product, so the reaction avoids carbonate/oxide decomposition and should be kinetically favorable relative to refractory solid-state synthesis [CS1, SS1]. The alkali cations are mobile, and analogous phases form at 688–870 K rather than very high temperatures [CS1, CS3, CS4]. However, reported anneals of 3–14 days imply that long-range ordering and crystal growth are not instantaneous [CS1, CS3]. Thus kinetics are favorable but not trivial.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the same sealed Ar annealing conditions as Route 1 but adds synthesis of Na₂Se/Cs₂Se from elements, which increases procedural complexity [CS3, TS1]. Elemental Cs/Na and alkali selenides are air-sensitive, and Se can be volatile/toxic during precursor preparation [TS1, CS3]. No high pressure is required in the final ternary anneal [CS3]. The route is therefore more severe than Route 1 despite similar final temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Freshly prepared binary selenides should be reactive and compositionally well-defined, which improves precursor activity [CS3]. RbLiSe single crystals were obtained by long low-temperature annealing after preparing binary selenides from elements, indicating that slow equilibration can be effective for alkali selenide systems [CS3]. The kinetic penalty is that multiple precursor formation and ordering steps can introduce inhomogeneity or residual binary phases if incomplete [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics remain good once the binary selenides are formed.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The carbonate/H₂–Se route uses moderate high temperatures, typically 925–1030 K, and flowing hydrogen charged with chalcogen [TS2–TS5]. The H₂ atmosphere is explicitly penalized because it is reactive and requires safety controls [TS2–TS5]. Se vapor/toxicity and air-sensitive products add another deduction [TS2–TS5]. However, no high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required in the cited analogues [TS2–TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Carbonate/chalcogen/H₂ routes can rapidly form alkali transition-metal chalcogenides within 6–10 h, showing good kinetic accessibility for some selenides [TS2–TS5]. However, there is insufficient evidence from CS and SS that carbonate chalcogenization directly forms CsNaSe or any matlockite Cs–Na selenide [insufficient evidence from CS; insufficient evidence from SS]. The route likely requires complete reconstruction from carbonates and removal of oxygen/carbonate byproducts, which makes phase selection uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore F3 is lower than for binary-selenide routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Cs and Na sites, reflected in very different Cs–Se and Na–Se bond lengths [Target]. That triggers one amorphization-risk criterion, but the structure is a small, ordered P4/nmm matlockite-type phase and does not have a >40-atom cell [CS1, SS1]. The polyhedral connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but no reported face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered [Target]. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for amorphization in CsNaSe, so it is treated as neutral for this factor [insufficient evidence from TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.046 eV/atom, while the exact reported CsNaSe analogue is on the hull in CS/SS data [Target, CS1, SS1]. The CS and SS evidence is unusually strong because the closest entry is not merely similar but essentially the same Cs–Na–Se P4/nmm matlockite chemistry made by a documented solid-state route [CS1, SS1]. TS evidence is supportive mainly in showing that related alkali chalcogenides with small positive hull energies can be synthesized, but TS structures are less directly transferable [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive binary selenide precursors Na₂Se and Cs₂Se, which directly map onto the target stoichiometry [CS1, SS1]. The most critical limiting factor is air/moisture sensitivity and stoichiometry control during sealed-ampoule synthesis, especially because alkali selenides and products are hygroscopic/reactive [CS1, CS3, TS1].
