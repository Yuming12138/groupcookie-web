### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.013 eV/atom, which is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues with similar small positive hull energies are reported as synthesizable, including MnSe at 0.0035 eV/atom (SS1/TS1), TlMnI₃ at 0.017 eV/atom (SS3/TS2), and Li₂MnBr₄ at 0.0148 eV/atom (TS4). CS analogues also include stable/near-stable halide materials such as CrSBr at 0 eV/atom (CS1), Nb(SBr)₂ at 0.0008 eV/atom (CS3), and NbSeI at 0.0083 eV/atom (CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy does not guarantee the mixed Cl/I ordering or the Pmc2₁ layer topology.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state metathesis/annealing

The severity score is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires a sealed dry/vacuum or Ar environment, as in many halide references (CS3, CS4, SS3/TS2). The precursor chemistry is favorable because binary manganese halides are directly relevant to Mn iodide syntheses (SS3/TS2) and anhydrous binary halide melting is used for Li₂MnBr₄ (TS4). Kinetics should be acceptable at 350–500 °C because related halide systems form in this range (CS3, CS4, SS3/TS2), but long annealing/regrinding may be required, as repeated processing is needed for phase purity in SS3/TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact MnICl phase diagram, so phase competition remains the main uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Chemical vapor transport / vapor-assisted crystallization

CVT has higher condition severity because it requires volatile halogens/halides, evacuated sealed ampoules, a thermal gradient, and often long times, as shown for CrSBr (CS1), NbSe₂Cl₂ (CS2), and Hg/Zr halides (SS4). Vapor transport is credible for low-dimensional halides because multiple layered/ribbon halide-containing analogues crystallize this way (CS1, CS2, SS4). The limiting kinetic factor is composition control: mixed-halide MnICl requires maintaining a 1:1 Cl:I ratio, while CS2, CS4, and SS4 all report phase-purity or contamination difficulties in halide transport chemistry. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a specific Mn–Cl–I transport agent, so I₂-assisted transport is only a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Melt growth / Bridgman-type crystallization

Melt growth is supported by Mn iodide and bromide analogues: TlMnI₃ uses melted binary iodides plus long annealing (SS3/TS2), Li₂MnBr₄ is made by melting anhydrous binary bromides (TS4), and TlCdI₃ crystals are grown by Bridgman method (TS5). Kinetically, molten halides should give high diffusion and good mixing, which is why the F3 value is slightly higher than Route 1. The severity is still significant because sealed ampoules, dry handling, volatile iodine species, and multi-step cooling/annealing are required, as suggested by SS3/TS2 and TS4. The largest uncertainty is whether MnICl melts congruently or whether the melt instead crystallizes MnCl₂, MnI₂, or other mixed-halide phases; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for congruent MnICl melt growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains a single cation, Mn, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target structure is a simple 2D sheet of corner-sharing MnI₂Cl₂ tetrahedra, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not triggered; this differs from some more complex analogues such as CrSBr with edge/corner-sharing octahedra (CS1) and Na₂Mn₂Se₃ with mixed polyhedral sharing (SS2). The atom-count criterion is not evidently triggered from the target description, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a large-cell MnICl analogue. Thus amorphization risk is low, although stacking disorder in 2D halides remains possible by [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnICl is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.013 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as TlMnI₃ and Li₂MnBr₄ (SS3/TS2, TS4). The CS data strongly support sealed halide/chalcogen-halide synthesis and CVT for low-dimensional compounds, while SS/TS data specifically support manganese halide chemistry through MnSe, TlMnI₃, and Li₂MnBr₄ analogues (CS1–CS4, SS1/SS3, TS1/TS2/TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of anhydrous metal halides under sealed-tube conditions, supported by binary halide melt/anneal syntheses in SS3/TS2 and TS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control of a mixed chloride/iodide layer, because related halide systems often show polymorphism, byproducts, poor reproducibility, or difficult crystal growth (CS2, CS4, SS3/TS2, TS4). Overall, the material appears experimentally accessible, with the best chance coming from low-temperature sealed-tube annealing before attempting CVT or melt growth.
