### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E above hull of 0.010 eV/atom is within the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom window, so the assigned score is 95. This is consistent with Nb(SBr)₂, the closest structural analogue, being nearly on the hull at 0.0008 eV/atom [CS-3], and with several structurally or thermodynamically similar compounds being experimentally known at 0–0.035 eV/atom [SS-2 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5]. The main uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field, as NbSe/Cl systems show narrow phase windows and competing polymorphs [CS-4, CS-5].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, 450–550 °C, which are directly supported by 500 °C Nb(SBr)₂ synthesis [CS-3] and 400 °C Ta₄S₉Br₈ synthesis [CS-1/SS-1]. The deductions arise from evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/halogen components, and toxic or moisture-sensitive chlorine/HfCl₄ handling, consistent with sealed halogen syntheses [CS-1, CS-3, CS-4]. There is insufficient evidence from TS that direct Hf(SCl)₂ solid-state synthesis is straightforward, so TS is neutral for this route’s practical severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The exact Nb(SBr)₂ structural analogue strongly supports formation of the M(SX)₂ sheet with S–S dimers and bridging halide [CS-3]. However, Hf metal is refractory and likely slower to react than volatile halide/chalcogenide precursors [Reasonable Inference], so precursor activity is modest. The halogen-rich sealed environment partially offsets diffusion limitations, as Nb(SBr)₂ and Ta₄S₉Br₈ form at only 400–500 °C [CS-1, CS-3].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature and pressure, using conditions analogous to NbX₂Y₂ CVT at 400–515 °C [CS-4]. It still requires vacuum sealing, volatile transport agents, and toxic/air-sensitive chlorides, so the score remains moderate rather than high. TS supports chlorine transport for Hf sulfides [TS-2], but also indicates high-temperature gradient control is needed, so the route is experimentally controlled rather than simple.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT provides the best kinetic pathway because it bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion through vapor-phase transport, as demonstrated for NbX₂Y₂ chalcogenide halides [CS-4]. Hf sulfide chlorine transport is directly plausible because PbHfS₃ crystals were grown using chlorine as transport agent [TS-2]. The uncertainty is the target’s chlorine chemical potential: too much chlorine may favor volatile HfCl₄ or other chloride-rich phases [Reasonable Inference; phase sensitivity in Nb chloride/chalcogenide systems from CS-4, CS-5, SS-4].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth uses standard sealed ampoule/furnace equipment, similar to halide flux sulfide growth [SS-5] and Cd-assisted Nb(SBr)₂ synthesis [CS-3]. The route receives extra deductions for flux drying, post-reaction washing/separation, volatile chlorides, and possible toxic CdCl₂. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux growth of Hf–S–Cl phases, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A halide flux should improve mass transport and reduce the kinetic penalty of Hf’s high melting point [Reasonable Inference], consistent with reactive halide flux growth of sulfides [SS-5]. The Cd-containing exact analogue synthesis [CS-3] suggests mineralizer assistance may be beneficial. The uncertainty is compositional control: flux components can be incorporated or stabilize competing phases, as phase-control difficulty is common in related chloride/chalcogenide systems [CS-4, CS-5, SS-4].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a single cation, Hf, so the mixed-cation radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered. The reported C2/m layered structure appears chemically simple, and the provided description does not indicate a large unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral complexity. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for glass-forming or amorphization tendencies in this exact Hf–S–Cl phase, so no additional penalty is applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(SCl)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.010 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the exact layered C2/m analogue Nb(SBr)₂, which was experimentally synthesized in a sealed tube at 500 °C [CS-3], and from NbX₂Y₂ chloride/chalcogenide CVT chemistry [CS-4]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/mineralization, which allows refractory transition-metal chalcogenide-halides to crystallize at 400–550 °C [CS-1, CS-3, CS-4]. The main limiting factor is chlorine chemical-potential control: HfCl₄ volatility and competing Hf–S–Cl or Hf–Cl phases may narrow the synthesis window [Reasonable Inference; analogous phase-control issues in CS-4, CS-5, SS-4]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable but likely requires sealed-tube/CVT optimization rather than simple open solid-state heating.
