### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0070 eV/atom, placing it very close to the convex hull and within the highest stability band specified by the rubric [Target]. CS references show many chemically similar Cu halides/chalcogenide halides at E_hull = 0 [CS-1–CS-5], while SS and TS references include stable or near-stable low-dimensional halides with E_hull values up to ~0.031 eV/atom that were experimentally synthesized [SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Ni/Cu ordering [Reasonable Inference].

Source-specific factor signal: CS = useful positive signal; SS = useful positive signal; TS = useful positive signal. No neutral 50 assignment is needed for F1.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature but requires vacuum sealing, similar to CuHgSX and CuHgSI syntheses in evacuated Pyrex tubes [SS-1, TS-2, TS-4]. The halide precursors are expected to require dry handling and a sealed ampoule because related bromide/halide syntheses use sealed tubes or ampoules [CS-1, CS-3, SS-1]. The main uncertainty is whether NiBr₂/CuBr react cleanly without melting or transport assistance [Reasonable Inference].

Source-specific factor signal: CS supports sealed halide handling but not direct solid-state Ni–Cu bromide synthesis; SS supports the sealed-tube solid-state route; TS supports moderate-temperature evacuated-tube synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halides are more reactive than refractory oxides, and related Cu halides form at moderate temperatures [CS-2, CS-3, SS-1]. However, long dwell times of 2 weeks in structurally/thermodynamically similar CuHgSX/CuHgSI syntheses indicate that ordering and diffusion can be slow [SS-1, TS-2, TS-4]. Insufficient evidence from CS for Ni/Cu cation ordering specifically; that uncertainty is treated by not assigning a very high structural-reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Melt/quench synthesis requires vacuum sealing and volatile halide containment, as shown in CuAlBr₄/CuAlCl₄ and CuBrSe₂ examples [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5]. Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies [CS-3]. The main severity comes from sealed ampoule operation and toxic/hygroscopic metal bromides [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference].

Source-specific factor signal: CS gives strong positive evidence for melt routes; SS gives insufficient evidence for melt synthesis of comparable mixed Ni/Cu bromides and is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor; TS gives insufficient evidence for melt synthesis and is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

The melt route should have favorable kinetics because liquid halide media enhance mixing and diffusion [CS-1, CS-3]. The key limitation is phase selection during cooling, because CuAlBr₄/CuAlCl₄ and CuBrSe₂ analogues show metastable phase formation and cooling-rate sensitivity [CS-1, CS-3]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for target-like melt crystallization, so the route remains moderately uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal synthesis avoids vacuum but uses concentrated HBr in sealed ampoules/autoclaves, which imposes pressure and corrosivity hazards [SS-2, TS-5]. The temperature is moderate, around 670 K in the analogue, and no high-pressure >5 GPa condition is indicated [SS-2, TS-5]. The route has multiple operations—loading, sealing, heating, controlled cooling, washing/crystal selection—so a multistep penalty is appropriate [SS-2, TS-5].

Source-specific factor signal: SS and TS strongly support the hydrothermal route; CS provides insufficient hydrothermal evidence for this factor and is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal conditions give high precursor activity and solution-mediated transport, as CuHgSBr was obtained as high-quality crystals with no observed side products [SS-2, TS-5]. This directly supports a high kinetics score relative to dry solid-state synthesis [SS-2, TS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target lacks S/Hg chemistry and requires Ni²⁺/Cu⁺ ordering in a purely bromide lattice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile bromine or halide transport species, and a temperature gradient [CS-2, SS-3, TS-3]. PdBr₂ CVT specifically used added Br₂, which increases handling severity [SS-3]. The route is also multistep if pre-reacted NiCuBr₃ powder is first prepared [Reasonable Inference].

Source-specific factor signal: CS supports moderate-temperature CVT for Cu bromide selenide; SS supports Br₂-assisted bromide CVT; TS supports halide ampoule transport. No neutral source assignment is needed for Route 4 F2.

#### F3 — Route 4

Gas-phase transport should provide strong mass transport, as demonstrated by CuBrSe₃ and PdBr₂ crystal growth [CS-2, SS-3]. However, transport-agent chemical potential can shift phase equilibria, and TS halide examples include metastable phases or low yields under some transport/cooling conditions [TS-3]. The target’s exact transport chemistry is unknown, so this route is better for crystal growth after a powder phase is identified [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a crystalline low-dimensional sheet structure with defined NiBr₆ octahedra and CuCuBr₃ trigonal pyramids [Target]. Mixed edge- and corner-sharing connectivity is present, which increases structural specificity and amorphization/defect risk [Target]. However, the formula and crystallographic description do not indicate a very large cell, and the cations occupy distinct coordination environments, limiting the amorphization penalty [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly measures amorphization tendency, so F4 is primarily target-structure-based with CS/SS/TS treated as neutral 50 for direct amorphization evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NiCuBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.007 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue set supports synthesis of low-dimensional bromides and mixed halides under sealed ampoule, melt, hydrothermal, and CVT conditions [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is the soft, mobile bromide chemistry, which allows moderate-temperature crystallization in sealed or solution-mediated environments [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is selective stabilization of Ni²⁺/Cu⁺ ordering without forming binary NiBr₂, CuBr, or CuBr₂ impurities [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with the hydrothermal HBr route offering the best balance of kinetic mobility, moderate temperature, and crystal-growth control [SS-2, TS-5].
