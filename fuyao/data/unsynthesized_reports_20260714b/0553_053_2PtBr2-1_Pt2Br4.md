### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

PtBr₂ has E above hull = 0.0170 eV/atom, placing it well within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.011–0.034 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by CVT, sealed ampoule, solvothermal, or sublimation methods (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that low hull energy does not guarantee the specific monoclinic P2₁/c layered polymorph; polymorph sensitivity is seen for PdCl₂, where α-PdCl₂ is metastable and requires controlled cooling (TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 600–700 °C sealed-ampoule Br₂-assisted CVT, which is moderate rather than extreme because PdBr₂ CVT occurs at 700 °C and PtCl₂ at 550 °C (SS-1, CS-4/SS-2). The deductions arise from reactive Br₂ atmosphere, volatile Br₂ in a sealed system, and toxic/corrosive halogen handling, all supported by bromide CVT analogues (SS-1, SS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS specifically addresses PtBr₂ CVT conditions, so TS is neutral for this route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because preformed PtBr₂ powder and Br₂ vapor transport avoid direct Pt metal bromination; this is supported by same-halide transport of PdBr₂ and PtCl₂ (SS-1, CS-4/SS-2). The close structural analogue PdBr₂ has square-planar PdBr₄ units and similar M–Br distance, supporting low structural reconstruction (SS-1). The uncertainty is whether PtBr₂ vapor species have an optimal transport window comparable to PdBr₂, for which direct evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not penalized for ultra-high temperature or high pressure because analogous elemental halogenations and bromide CVT occur below 1000 °C in sealed systems (CS-1, SS-4, SS-5). It is penalized for Br₂ reactive atmosphere, volatility/sealing, and toxicity, as in IrBr₃ and RuBr₃ bromination examples (CS-1, SS-4, SS-5). SS-4 shows that RuBr₃ powder synthesis sometimes used autoclave pressure, but because PdBr₂/PtCl₂ routes avoid this (SS-1, CS-4/SS-2), external high pressure is not assumed for PtBr₂.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic score is lower because direct reaction starts from Pt metal, a dense refractory noble metal, while analogous Ir/Ru routes explicitly use finely divided metal to enable halogenation (CS-1, SS-4). Halogen vapor can assist transport and conversion, but the process still requires construction of the PtBr₂ sheet from elements, unlike same-compound recrystallization. Insufficient evidence from TS addresses direct Pt metal bromination to PtBr₂, so TS is neutral for this kinetic factor.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses 550–670 °C sealed-tube sublimation/recrystallization, based on PtCl₂ transport at 550 °C and PdCl₂ sublimation at 670 °C (CS-4/SS-2, TS-5). The route is penalized for vacuum/controlled Br₂ atmosphere, sealed volatile halide conditions, and possible Br₂ toxicity if trace Br₂ is added, supported by PdCl₂ vacuum sublimation and PdBr₂ Br₂-assisted transport (TS-5, SS-1). It avoids multi-step penalties because it starts from PtBr₂ and primarily recrystallizes rather than chemically converts, by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are best for Route 3 because same-composition recrystallization minimizes chemical reaction and structural reconstruction, consistent with PdCl₂ sublimation from PdCl₂ and PtCl₂ transport from PtCl₂ (TS-5, CS-4/SS-2). Vapor-phase growth reduces solid diffusion limitations, as shown across PdBr₂, PtCl₂, and PdCl₂ analogues (SS-1, CS-4/SS-2, TS-5). The main uncertainty is whether PtBr₂ sublimes/transports cleanly without decomposition, for which direct evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Pt, so cation radius mismatch is not a concern by composition. The target description gives a relatively simple PtBr₂ sheet with square-coplanar Pt and bent bridging Br, not a mixed network of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, but the simple binary formula and layered description indicate low amorphization risk by [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PtBr₂ is likely **near-ground-state or only weakly metastable**, because its E above hull is 0.0170 eV/atom, inside the high-stability window used for F1. The strongest support comes from the square-planar bromide analogue PdBr₂, which is grown by Br₂-assisted CVT (SS-1), and from PtCl₂, which demonstrates transportable Pt(II) halide chemistry (CS-4/SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a controlled bromine chemical potential in a sealed ampoule, as Br₂ is explicitly used to transport PdBr₂ and RuBr₃ (SS-1, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control during heating, because PdCl₂ shows metastable phase sensitivity under sublimation conditions (TS-5), and bromide analogues require halogen pressure/gradient control (SS-1, SS-5). Overall, PtBr₂ should be synthesizable with moderate difficulty, with sealed-tube vapor growth or Br₂-assisted CVT preferred over direct metal bromination.
