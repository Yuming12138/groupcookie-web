### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, so it sits at the high-synthesizability boundary according to the required rubric. CS analogues include several stable oxide phases, such as CS-1, CS-3, and CS-5 at E_hull = 0, and CS-2 at 0.024 eV/atom. SS analogues are also stable or nearly stable, including SS-1 and SS-3 at E_hull = 0, while TS analogues cluster close to the target at ~0.05–0.058 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee the unusual Rb–Y–Cr oxidation/coordination motif will be kinetically accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because repeated grinding, pelletizing, and firing are required in analogous solid-state syntheses (CS-1, CS-3). A controlled oxidizing atmosphere is a reasonable requirement because the target composition implies highly oxidized chromium [Reasonable Inference], and high-valent oxide synthesis can require strong oxidizing conditions as shown by the high-pO₂ Ru analogue in CS-5. CrO₃/chromates are toxic oxidizers, and Rb/Cr volatility risks are inferred from high-temperature alkali chromate chemistry. SS and TS give insufficient direct evidence for this exact ceramic route and are neutral for that route-specific transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are limited by the refractory Y₂O₃ precursor, even though RE₂O₃ use is established in CS-2. CS-1 and CS-3 show that repeated regrinding/firing is common for complex oxide formation, indicating diffusion and homogenization limitations. The target’s mixed Rb–Y–Cr oxide framework requires substantial reconstruction from carbonate/oxide/chromate reagents. This gives Route 1 the weakest kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Hydrothermal synthesis operates at modest temperatures, consistent with TS-3 at 493 K and TS-4 at 175–225 °C. It avoids GPa pressures and external reactive gas atmospheres, although it requires a sealed Teflon-lined autoclave as in TS-3/TS-4. The main severity penalties are multi-step solution handling/pH control and toxic CrO₃/chromate chemistry, supported by CrO₃ use in SS-4 and TS-1/TS-3. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for this solution route and is neutral for route-specific transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution and hydrothermal media provide high precursor activity and bypass solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated for Rb chromates by SS-1–SS-4 and for hydrothermal chromates by TS-3. Soluble Y salts are a reasonable inference to improve Y incorporation compared with Y₂O₃. The kinetic uncertainty is structural selectivity: TS-3 reports competing phases in a hydrothermal chromate system, and the target is an anhydrous Rb–Y–Cr oxide rather than a simple Rb chromate. Overall, Route 2 has the best kinetic outlook but still requires careful phase control.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route uses moderate temperatures relative to ceramic synthesis and is supported indirectly by Rb chromate/dichromate crystallization in SS-1, SS-4, and SS-5. It is penalized for toxic Cr(VI) fluxes, multi-step growth and flux removal, and possible Rb/Cr volatility at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference]. It does not require GPa pressure or necessarily a reactive gas atmosphere. TS provides insufficient direct molten-flux evidence and is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A chromate/dichromate flux should provide a liquid pathway and high Rb–Cr–O chemical activity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the ease of Rb chromate crystallization in SS-1–SS-4. The key kinetic advantage is reduced diffusion length compared with Route 1. The main limitation is dissolution and transport of Y, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Y incorporation into a Rb chromate flux phase. Controlled slow cooling is supported by CS-3 and SS-5.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, Rb, Y, and Cr, with different preferred coordination environments, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports corner-sharing YO₅ and CrO₅ polyhedra, but not simultaneous edge and face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve it, so C2 is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂YCrO₆ is likely a borderline metastable or very weakly stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the cutoff between highly accessible and moderately metastable compositions. The SS and TS analogues strongly support the accessibility of Rb chromate-type chemistry by solution, slow cooling, and hydrothermal methods, while CS supports the general use of alkali/rare-earth oxide precursors in solid-state synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a highly oxidizing Rb–Cr–O chemical environment while keeping Y soluble or mobile enough to enter the framework. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition from simpler Rb₂CrO₄/Rb₂Cr₂O₇, yttrium chromates, hydrated phases, or Rb-deficient products. Overall, hydrothermal/solution crystallization is the most promising first attempt, with chromate flux growth as the best backup for obtaining crystalline material.
