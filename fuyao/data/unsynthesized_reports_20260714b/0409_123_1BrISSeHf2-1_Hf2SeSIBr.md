### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0140 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic category. TS provides direct support because similar low-energy halide/chalcogenide systems include Ta₃SBr₇ at 0.0049 eV/atom and Sc₇Cl₁₂ at 0.0145 eV/atom (TS1, TS4). CS also supports stability of related chalcogenides because several chemically similar selenides are reported at 0 eV/atom (CS2–CS5). SS is mixed: Ta₃SBr₇ is near-stable, but Zr₃S₄ is high above hull, so SS contributes moderate rather than decisive confidence (SS1, SS2).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits sealed-tube vacuum and volatile halogen/chalcogen handling from Ta₃SBr₇ and related chalcogenide syntheses (SS1/TS1, CS2–CS5). It avoids >1000 °C if the halide chemistry is effective, but it still requires vacuum sealing, multi-step grinding/annealing, and toxic or air-sensitive halide sources [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1 and SS2]. CS indicates sealed-ampoule high-temperature chalcogenide reactions are common, but phase control can be difficult (CS1–CS5). Therefore the condition severity is challenging but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic barrier is moderate-to-high because Hf is refractory and solid-state diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 provides a positive counterexample: Ta₃SBr₇ forms at 550 °C despite refractory Ta, likely aided by halogen transport chemistry. CS1 required 900 °C and long duration for a 2D selenide chloride, showing that long annealing may be needed. Remaining uncertainty is whether Br/I and S/Se order cleanly rather than forming HfS₂, HfSe₂, or Hf halides.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses vacuum, volatile iodine/bromine species, sealed ampoules, and several controlled steps, all supported by CVT precedents (SS1/TS1, SS2, TS2, SS5). The temperature can likely remain below 1000 °C because Zr–S/Se CVT used 870→770 °C and Ta₃SBr₇ crystal growth used lower gradients after prereaction (SS2, SS1/TS1). The severity is still penalized for toxic/volatile halogens and pressure buildup in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS for CVT specifically, so CS is neutral for this route’s condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by converting refractory metals into volatile metal-halide species, as indicated by Zr–S/Se iodine transport and TiOCl gas transport (SS2, TS2). The structural match to layered halide/chalcogenide analogues supports direct crystallization of 2D sheets (SS1/TS1, TS2). The largest uncertainty is composition control: S/Se and Br/I chemical potentials may differ substantially, and no provided source demonstrates quaternary Hf–S–Se–Br–I ordering directly. Thus the route receives the best F3, but not a near-perfect score.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-temperature reactive sintering is more severe because it may require 900–1050 °C or higher to activate Hf-containing binaries [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 shows group-4 chalcogenide synthesis at 1570 K in sealed Ta tubes, and CS2–CS5 show 1420 K fusion with long annealing for selenides. The route also inherits sealed-tube vacuum/Ar, volatile halogen components, and possible Ta containment from TS4–TS5. Therefore F2 is lower than Routes 1 and 2.

#### F3 — Route 3

Preformed HfS₂/HfSe₂ can reduce structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference], but Hf diffusion and halide incorporation remain slow compared with CVT. TS5 implies high-temperature group-4 chalcogenide formation can require robust containers and significant thermal activation. CS2–CS5 also show long annealing and impurity phases in complex selenides, indicating moderate-to-difficult phase control. This route is plausible but kinetically less attractive than vapor transport.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hf, so there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch trigger. The described structure is a small ordered layered phase with corner- and edge-sharing octahedra and no face-sharing, which lowers amorphization risk. Layered analogues such as TiOCl and Ta₃SBr₇ are crystalline under vapor/solid-state conditions (TS2, SS1/TS1). The main uncertainty is the actual crystallographic cell size, since the source description does not explicitly list atom count.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂SeSIBr is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0140 eV/atom, a range where many inorganic phases are experimentally accessible. The synthesis is reasonably supported by analogues: Ta₃SBr₇ demonstrates a 2D transition-metal sulfide bromide sheet made in sealed ampoules, Zr–S/Se compounds demonstrate iodine CVT, and TiOCl demonstrates layered octahedral halide sheets grown by gas transport (SS1/TS1, SS2, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport, which can overcome slow Hf diffusion and promote layered crystal growth [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1 and SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of four anions/chalcogens—S, Se, Br, and I—because their volatilities and chemical potentials differ strongly [Reasonable Inference]. Competing HfS₂, HfSe₂, HfBr₄/HfI₄-derived phases, or mixed halide segregation are likely practical obstacles.
