### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0010 eV/atom, which is essentially thermodynamically stable by the required rubric. CS, SS, and TS analogues are also predominantly at or very near the hull, including Ta₃SBr₇ at 0.0049 eV/atom [SS1, TS1] and several CS/TS analogues at 0–0.004 eV/atom [CS1–CS3, TS3–TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that low calculated hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Hf/Ti and S/Se ordering [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions by analogy to Ta₃SBr₇ at 550 °C [SS1/TS1], but it requires vacuum sealing and volatile Br₂ handling [SS1/TS1]. Br₂/Se toxicity and multi-step sealed-ampoule processing justify the major deductions [CS5, SS1/TS1]. CS provides some support for high-temperature ampoule synthesis [CS1, CS5], while SS/TS give the closest condition match [SS1/TS1].

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
The main kinetic penalty is the use of refractory Hf/Ti metals, for which solid-state diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 shows that a refractory transition metal can react with S/Br₂ at 550 °C, but only after long reaction/crystal-growth times and with phase-control issues. CS5 also reports demanding S/Se/halide ampoule processing and difficult phase control, supporting a low-to-moderate kinetics score.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses air-sensitive and volatile halide/flux components, so controlled dry sealed handling is required [SS3/TS2]. It is not high-pressure and can plausibly operate below 1000 °C based on Zr halide fluxes at 220–300 °C and Ta sulfide bromide formation at 550 °C [SS3/TS2, SS1/TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact HfBr₄/TiBr₄ flux temperatures in this target, so the score is conservative.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
The flux route receives the best kinetics score because molten halide media can dissolve/transport refractory metal species, as shown for Zr tetrahalides in aluminum halide melts [SS3/TS2]. Fluxes also improve reaction completion and single-crystal formation in related lanthanide sulfide chlorides [CS3, SS4]. The uncertainty is that mixed Hf/Ti and S/Se ordering in a 2D Cm sheet is not directly demonstrated in any source.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 resembles conventional sealed-ampoule sulfide/halide synthesis at 700–900 °C, which is supported by CS1 and CS5. It still requires vacuum sealing, volatile halide/chalcogen control, and likely multiple grinding/annealing steps [CS1, CS5, TS4]. The score is similar to Route 1 because condition severity is dominated by sealed reactive halide/chalcogen handling.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Binary sulfides/selenides reduce some elemental diffusion burden, but the reaction remains a solid-state reconstruction into a mixed-cation, mixed-anion 2D sheet [Reasonable Inference]. TS4 and TS5 show sulfide solid-state syntheses often require high temperatures, long holds, regrinding, and annealing. CS1 supports binary sulfide + halide chemistry, but does not prove facile formation of Hf–Ti mixed bromide chalcogenide layers.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a meaningful Hf/Ti size mismatch, triggering the cation-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. However, the structure is dominated by corner- and edge-sharing octahedra rather than a highly diverse face/edge/corner network, and the formula size is not obviously >40 atoms per cell from the provided information. SS1/TS1 demonstrates that closely related layered halide sheets can crystallize, but phase purity and crystal size can be difficult [SS1/TS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom, well inside the stability window. The strongest support comes from the structural and thermodynamic analogue Ta₃SBr₇, which is also a 2D monoclinic Cm sulfide bromide synthesized in evacuated ampoules [SS1, TS1]. Chemical analogues support sealed-ampoule chalcogenide/halide synthesis and flux-assisted crystallization, but they are less directly matched to Hf/Ti bromide selenosulfide chemistry [CS1, CS3, CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a controlled Br chemical potential in a sealed system or bromide flux [SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is slow, selective ordering of refractory Hf/Ti cations and S/Se anions without forming binary Hf/Ti sulfides, selenides, or bromides [Reasonable Inference; phase-control concerns supported by SS1/TS1 and CS5].
