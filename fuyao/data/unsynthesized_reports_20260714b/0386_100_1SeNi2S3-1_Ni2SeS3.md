### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.017 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom category [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues at similar or higher E_hull are reported as synthesizable, including AgVP₂Se₆ at 0.009 eV/atom [TS2], CuSbSe₂ at 0.0147 eV/atom [TS5], TlNi₂Se₂ at 0.0223 eV/atom [TS4], and Cu₃SbSe₃ at 0.0346 eV/atom [TS3]. The main uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly identifies Ni₂SeS₃ as experimentally known.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard evacuated quartz/silica tube conditions, which are widely supported for chalcogenides [CS1–CS3, TS3, TS5]. The severity is moderate because S/Se volatility requires sealing, and Se-containing mixtures are toxic/volatile **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with sealed-tube use in CS3/TS3/TS5. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or >1000 °C is required, so those penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental chalcogen reactions are common and successful [CS2, CS3, TS3, TS5], but dense Ni metal introduces diffusion limitations **[Reasonable Inference]**. Long anneals of days to weeks in analogues indicate slow ordering or diffusion constraints [CS1, CS2, CS4, TS3]. The target’s low-dimensional structure may need careful reconstruction, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms this Ni₂SeS₃ topology forms from elements.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar vacuum and volatile-chalcogen requirements to Route 1, supported by binary-precursor sealed ampoule analogues [TS3, CS5]. It receives an additional multi-step penalty because binary NiS/NiSe precursor preparation or verification plus final annealing is likely **[Reasonable Inference]**; multi-step annealing is common in TS3 and TS5. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that inert gas alone is sufficient for the target, so vacuum is preferred.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary chalcogenides improve kinetic matching because metal–chalcogen bonds are already present; this inference is supported by successful binary-precursor synthesis of Cu₃SbSe₃ [TS3] and CuPS₃ [CS5]. Partial reconstruction should be easier than forming the framework directly from Ni metal **[Reasonable Inference]**. Remaining uncertainty is that NiS/NiSe binaries are stable competitors, so incomplete reaction may leave persistent impurity phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth lowers the synthesis temperature to ~500–600 °C, supported by the KCl/LiCl flux route at 550 °C [CS4]. The method is still moderately severe because it requires vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogen control, long duration, and flux washing [CS4]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly supports halide flux for Ni₂SeS₃, so transferability is less certain than Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux should enhance diffusion and crystallization, as implied by successful low-temperature chalcogenide crystal growth in KCl/LiCl [CS4]. However, the six-week duration in CS4 suggests slow growth or a narrow crystallization window. For Ni₂SeS₃, flux may stabilize crystals but may also dissolve components unevenly or introduce halide contamination **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT uses moderate temperatures and vacuum tubes, supported for structurally similar CuBrSe₃ [SS5]. The penalties arise from vacuum, volatile halogen/chalcogen species, multi-step pre-synthesis/growth, and toxic/corrosive transport agents **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS supports direct Ni₂SeS₃ CVT, so this is a secondary route.

#### F3 — Route 4

CVT can bypass bulk diffusion and grow crystals, as shown for CuBrSe₃ [SS5]. However, the target lacks halide in its formula, and Ni halide formation could compete with transport of the desired sulfide–selenide **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thus, CVT is better viewed as a crystal-growth route after powder synthesis rather than the primary discovery route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Ni, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent [Target]. The target description gives corner-sharing NiSeS₃ trigonal pyramids and does not report mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity, so C3 is not triggered [Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes the exact target unit-cell atom count, but the simple formula and described single sheet do not indicate a >40-atom cell; uncertainty remains moderate.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₂SeS₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.017 eV/atom [Target]. The CS and TS analogues strongly support sealed-ampoule chalcogenide synthesis using elemental or binary precursors, with many related sulfides/selenides prepared under vacuum at 350–900 °C [CS1–CS3, CS5, TS3, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic driving force for decomposition, comparable to successfully synthesized thermodynamic analogues [TS2–TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in the Ni–S–Se system, especially because S and Se are volatile and Ni chalcogenide binaries may persist **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with the binary-precursor sealed-ampoule route offering the best balance of thermodynamics, kinetics, and phase control.
