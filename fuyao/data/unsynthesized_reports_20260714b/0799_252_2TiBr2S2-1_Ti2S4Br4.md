### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E above hull is **0.021 eV/atom**, placing it well inside the commonly synthesizable metastability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom (target data). Thermodynamic analogues with similar or slightly higher hull energies are experimentally known, including Nb₂Tl₅S₄Br₉ at 0.014 eV/atom and Bi₂Te₄Br₈ at 0.028 eV/atom (TS3; TS5). CS/SS data further support this conclusion because the exact Nb(SBr)₂ structural analogue is nearly on-hull at 0.0008 eV/atom and experimentally synthesized (CS1; SS1). Remaining uncertainty is that Ti(SBr)₂ is not reported directly in the provided data, so local competing Ti–S, Ti–Br, or Ti–S–Br phases may still intervene ([Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a relatively favorable F2 score because the exact analogue Nb(SBr)₂ is synthesized at only 500 °C for 2 days in a sealed glass tube, without high pressure or very high temperature (CS1; SS1). The main severity penalties arise from Br₂ volatility and the hazards of Br₂/Cd, both implied by the CS1 precursor list and sealed-tube requirement (CS1; SS1; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and SS provide strong evidence here; TS gives no route-specific Ti–S–Br condition signal beyond general sealed-tube feasibility, so TS is neutral for Route 1 severity (50).

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is helped by volatile bromine and possible Cd mineralization, because Nb(SBr)₂ forms directly at 500 °C despite Nb being a refractory metal (CS1; SS1). For Ti, the refractory metal precursor may still slow reaction, so the precursor-activity component is not scored very high ([Reasonable Inference]). The exact structural match to Nb(SBr)₂ strongly improves the structural-reconstruction score, but simultaneous polymorph formation in CS1 suggests some phase-selection difficulty (CS1; SS1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because CVT analogues require evacuated ampoules, long duration, controlled temperature gradients, and volatile halogen transport (CS2; SS5). The temperature remains moderate, generally around 400–515 °C for NbX₂Y₂ and 500–460 °C for NbSeBr₃, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (CS2; SS5). CrSBr demonstrates sulfide bromide CVT but at much higher 880 °C, which is a conflicting signal; because the closer NbX₂Y₂ and NbSeBr₃ analogues are more chemically/structurally similar to Ti(SBr)₂, they are weighted more heavily (CS2; CS4; SS5).

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics through volatile halide species and long-range mass transport, supported by NbX₂Y₂ and NbSeBr₃ crystal growth in sealed gradient tubes (CS2; SS5). However, CS2 explicitly indicates long 3–4 week times, polymorphism, and sensitivity to phase windows, which lowers the reaction-kinetics/phase-ordering score (CS2). Ti-specific vapor transport chemistry is not directly shown, so the role of Ti bromide volatility is a reasonable inference rather than direct evidence ([Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses reactive halide/chalcogenide precursors and likely an evacuated sealed tube, similar to NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ and NbCl₅/Nb/Se routes (CS3; CS5). The temperature range is moderate, around 500–550 °C by analogy, but the route uses volatile or air-sensitive Ti bromides and may require flux handling, slow cooling, or post-reaction separation (CS3; CS5; SS2; SS4; [Reasonable Inference]). TS provides insufficient evidence for the specific TiS₂/TiBr₄ route, so it is neutral for this condition score (50).

#### F3 — Route 3

Using TiS₂ and TiBr₄/TiBr₃ should increase precursor activity relative to Ti metal, analogous to NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ generating Nb chalcogenide halides (CS3; [Reasonable Inference]). The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction from binary TiS₂/TiBr₄ components into the specific 2D Ti(SBr)₂ sheet, which is not directly demonstrated in the references ([Reasonable Inference]). Additional concern comes from poor reproducibility and side-product issues in some Nb chalcogenide halide syntheses, including CS3 and CS5.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ti, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply (target description; [Reasonable Inference]). The structure appears small and ordered, with the isostructural Nb analogue represented as Nb₂S₄Br₄, below the >40 atom trigger threshold (CS1; SS1). No source reports the target as having simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral networks, and the sheet-like structure with simple S–S and Br-bridging motifs should have low amorphization tendency (target description; CS1; SS1). Uncertainty remains because the exact crystallographic cell size of Ti(SBr)₂ is not explicitly provided beyond the structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti(SBr)₂ is likely a **mildly metastable but readily accessible** phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E above hull is only 0.021 eV/atom (target data). The support from analogues is strong: the closest analogue, Nb(SBr)₂, is both chemically and structurally almost identical and is synthesized directly in a sealed tube at 500 °C (CS1; SS1). The most important enabling factor is halogen-assisted sealed-tube chemistry, where Br₂/Cd or Br₂ vapor can transport/react with refractory metal and sulfur to form low-dimensional chalcogenide halide sheets (CS1; CS2; SS1; SS5). The main limiting factor is phase control, because related systems show polymorphism, side phases, and sensitivity to stoichiometry/transport gradients (CS1; CS2; CS5). Overall, the target should be experimentally plausible, with the best initial attempt being a sealed-tube elemental synthesis adapted from Nb(SBr)₂ (CS1; SS1; [Reasonable Inference]).
