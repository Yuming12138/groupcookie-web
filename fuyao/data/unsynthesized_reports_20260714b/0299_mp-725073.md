### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold for high synthesizeability. CS and SS are strongly supportive because FeBr₂·4H₂O, the closest Fe-halide tetrahydrate analogue, has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and is synthesized from aqueous solution (CS1, SS1). TS is also supportive because the closest thermodynamic analogue CoBr₂·4H₂O has E_hull ≈0.048 eV/atom and is experimentally crystallized by cooling (TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom is borderline, so the target may be mildly metastable relative to another FeCl₂ hydrate or solution state.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The condition severity is low because directly analogous halide tetrahydrates crystallize at 50–65 °C from aqueous solution (CS1, SS1), and FeCl₂ hydrate crystals are grown at 70 °C from saturated FeCl₂ solution (SS3). No high pressure, high temperature, or required controlled atmosphere is reported in CS/SS. A small deduction is applied because Fe²⁺ salts can oxidize in air [Reasonable Inference], even though the sources do not require inert atmosphere.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics should be excellent because the process is solution-mediated, not diffusion-limited, and FeBr₂·4H₂O forms directly as crystals suitable for X-ray diffraction (CS1, SS1). The target has the same basic molecular hydrate motif, FeX₂(H₂O)₄, requiring only ligand/water organization during crystallization [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1]. The main uncertainty is hydrate selection between FeCl₂·4H₂O and other FeCl₂ hydrates, as FeCl₂·2H₂O is also accessible at 70 °C (SS3).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Slow evaporation is mild because MgCl₂·6H₂O grows at room temperature in a desiccator (CS3, SS5), ZnBr₂ hydrate/complex crystallizes from saturated solution at 297 K (TS4), and Cu/Cd chloride hydrate crystals form at 299 K by evaporation (TS5). No special pressure or high-temperature equipment is needed in these analogues. The same Fe²⁺ oxidation risk justifies a small deduction [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution mobility and precursor activity are high, as chloride hydrates crystallize directly from saturated aqueous solutions in CS/SS/TS examples (CS3/SS5, TS4, TS5). Growth may be slower than Route 1 because TS5 reports about four weeks for evaporation-grown chloride hydrate crystals. The structural assembly remains simple, but slow evaporation can increase the risk of hydrate conversion or over-concentration [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-vial or capillary growth still uses mild temperatures and ambient pressure, consistent with FeCl₂ hydrate growth at 70 °C (SS3) and CoCl₂ hydrate capillary handling (SS4). Sealing is not a high-pressure method; it is a hydration-state and handling control measure (SS4). The same oxidation concern for Fe²⁺ remains [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The kinetic pathway is favorable because FeCl₂ is soluble and hydrate crystals can form from saturated solution (SS3). However, hydrate-state control is more delicate because SS4 explicitly notes sealing CoCl₂·2H₂O to avoid conversion to tetrahydrate, indicating that water activity can shift the hydrate phase. This route is therefore slightly less kinetically straightforward than direct cooling crystallization.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a discrete molecular hydrate rather than a network solid, so amorphization risk is low [target description]. CS/SS reinforce this because FeBr₂·4H₂O is zero-dimensional and crystallizes directly as X-ray-quality crystals (CS1, SS1). No mixed cation radius mismatch or mixed polyhedral connectivity exists in the target [target description]. The only uncertainty is practical crystal quality, not amorphization; SS3 notes FeCl₂ hydrate crystals can form confused aggregates/twins.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeCl₂(H₂O)₄ is likely a near-ground-state or only very mildly metastable hydrate, given its E_hull of exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and the successful synthesis of close analogues near this energy range (CS2, TS1). The CS and SS evidence are especially strong because FeBr₂·4H₂O is nearly identical in coordination geometry, space group, dimensionality, and synthesis route (CS1, SS1). The most critical enabling factor is that the phase can assemble directly from aqueous FeCl₂ solution by low-temperature crystallization, avoiding solid-state diffusion barriers (CS1, SS3). The most critical limiting factor is hydrate-state and oxidation control: FeCl₂ can form other hydrates such as FeCl₂·2H₂O under different conditions (SS3), and Fe²⁺ may oxidize in aerated solution [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target should be highly synthesizable with careful solution crystallization.
