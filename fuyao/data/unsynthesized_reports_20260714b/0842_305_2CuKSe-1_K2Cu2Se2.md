### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0270 eV/atom, which lies well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability band. Experimentally reported CS and SS analogues such as KCuS, KAgSe, KNaSe, K₃AuSe₂, and RbAuSe are ground-state or near-ground-state phases [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3]. TS analogues include several synthesized compounds at comparable E_hull values, including K₆Cd₄Sn₃Se₁₃ at 0.0306 eV/atom and RbMnSe₂ at 0.0243 eV/atom [TS1, TS2]. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact decomposition boundary of Pmma KCuSe.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because KCuS forms at 600 °C [CS1, SS1], KAgSe at 800 °C [CS2], and alkali Au chalcogenides at 600 °C [SS5, TS5]. The main severity penalties are sealed handling of volatile K/Se and air-sensitive alkali reagents, supported by KAgSe’s excess K practice [CS2] and moisture sensitivity in alkali chalcogenides [CS1, CS5]. No high pressure, H₂, NH₃, or >1000 °C condition is required by the closest analogues [CS1, CS2]. Uncertainty remains over whether Pmma KCuSe needs a narrow K chemical potential to avoid K loss or Cu–Se binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct elemental synthesis has good kinetic precedent because KCuS is made directly from elements [CS1, SS1], and KAgSe is similarly made from elements [CS2]. K and Se become mobile/reactive at the proposed temperatures, but Cu is a dense metallic precursor, so precursor activity is not maximal [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable due to the close KCuS analogue with linear Cu–chalcogen bonding [CS1, SS1], but not perfect because the target is a Se-containing Pmma phase rather than the reported KCuS Pna2₁ phase [CS1]. The residual uncertainty is whether the orthorhombic Pmma polymorph is the kinetically selected product.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures based on K₂Se + Au + Se synthesis at 823 K [SS2] and Rb₂Se + Au + Se at 873 K [SS3]. It receives larger severity deductions because evacuated ampoules are explicitly used for K₃AuSe₂ [SS2], K₂Se is air-/moisture-sensitive [CS5, TS1], and K₂Se may require prior synthesis [TS2]. The route avoids direct K metal handling but introduces a binary selenide precursor that is itself reactive. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms a Cu-specific K₂Se + Cu + Se reaction pathway.

#### F3 — Route 2

K₂Se should improve K–Se homogeneity relative to direct K + Se reaction [Reasonable Inference], and A₂Se + metal + Se routes are validated for Au analogues [SS2, SS3]. Cu diffusion remains the principal kinetic bottleneck, but the temperature range and possible chalcogen-rich transport are favorable [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the route is chemically analogous but not Cu-specific; SS provides Au analogues rather than Cu analogues [SS2, SS3]. The main uncertainty is whether Cu prefers KCuSe over binary Cu₂Se/CuSe phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux/self-flux growth is more operationally complex than direct reaction because it requires flux design, slow cooling, and product separation [Reasonable Inference]. Related flux/fusion routes and extended tempering are reported in TS3, SS5, and TS5. The route still uses sealed systems and air-sensitive K–Se reagents, consistent with KNaSe and K₃AuSe₂ preparation [CS5, SS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates a K–Cu–Se flux-growth window, so the severity remains moderate-challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth has the best diffusion kinetics because a molten K–Se-rich environment should enhance Cu transport and crystal nucleation [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is common in KAgSe, K₃AuSe₂, RbAuSe, and alkali Au chalcogenide growth [CS2, SS2, SS3, SS5]. However, flux routes can complicate phase selectivity and may stabilize off-stoichiometric K-rich or Cu–Se phases [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore high for diffusion but only moderate for structural selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size mismatch between K and Cu, which triggers C1 by ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference], and mixed edge/face/corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity triggers C3 from the target structural description and related mixed-sharing analogues [CS1, SS4]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is treated as not triggered and neutral with respect to risk. Mixed connectivity and size mismatch increase the risk of disorder or glassy/poorly crystalline products during rapid quenching, so slow cooling/annealing is recommended [CS1, CS2, SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the Pmma layered structure orders readily during annealing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KCuSe is likely a slightly metastable but experimentally accessible phase because its E_hull is only 0.027 eV/atom, and TS analogues with comparable hull energies have been synthesized [TS1, TS2, TS5]. The CS/SS support is strong: KCuS provides a direct K–Cu–chalcogen analogue [CS1, SS1], while KAgSe and alkali Au selenides validate the broader alkali–coinage-metal–selenide chemistry [CS2, SS2, SS3, SS5]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed, inert-atmosphere synthesis that preserves K and Se stoichiometry, as repeatedly used in analogous alkali chalcogenides [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control against K loss and Cu–Se binary formation, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from Se volatility and Cu–Se chemistry and is consistent with the phase-control concerns reported for air/moisture-sensitive alkali chalcogenides [CS1, CS4, SS5]. Overall, the target should be synthesizable in a well-equipped solid-state laboratory, with Route 1 recommended first.
