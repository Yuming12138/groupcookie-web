### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0470 eV/atom, which is just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability band (Target). Thermodynamic analogues include RbBr at E_hull = 0.0316 eV/atom, which was associated with low-temperature evaporation access to a metastable alkali-halide structure (TS1; also CS1). CS provides a useful positive signal because chemically similar RbBr and CsBr have low E_hull values and reported synthesis routes (CS1, CS2); SS provides a useful positive signal because the closest structural CsBr analogue is near the hull at 0.0051 eV/atom (SS1); TS provides a useful positive signal because the top thermodynamic analogue is close in metastability and method (TS1). Source scores: CS = 85, SS = 80, TS = 90.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses vacuum and cryogenic/low-temperature substrate control, which is moderately demanding but avoids high temperature and high pressure (CS1, CS2, CS5; TS1). The analogues explicitly require vacuum evaporation, low-temperature bases, slow deposition, and controlled heating (CS1, CS2; SS1; TS1). CS and TS give strong evidence for the relevant severity; SS gives useful evidence through the structurally similar CsBr evaporation route (SS1). Source scores: CS = 75, SS = 75, TS = 75.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Vapor availability is favorable because alkali halides are directly evaporated in the analogue studies (CS1, CS2, CS5; TS1). The limiting kinetic issue is crystallization pathway: the analogues show metastable/abnormal structures and temperature-sensitive transitions, implying the target P4/mmm phase may require a narrow kinetic window (CS1, CS5; TS1). Composition control is easier than multicomponent bromides because KBr is binary and stoichiometric [Reasonable Inference], while SS1 supports simple alkali-bromide structural transferability. Source scores: CS = 75, SS = 70, TS = 75.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is not high-pressure and does not exceed 1000 °C, but it requires sealed/inert handling and high-temperature halide processing [Reasonable Inference based on SS2 and TS2]. SS2 and TS2 show related bromides are melted in glass ampoules under Ar with slow cooling at 1–2 °C h⁻¹. CS gives only indirect support for alkali-halide volatility/metastability via evaporation studies, so it is less informative for melt growth than SS/TS (CS1, CS5). Source scores: CS = 60, SS = 70, TS = 70.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The liquid phase should provide rapid ion transport, and analogous bromides are made by melting binary bromides and slow cooling (SS2; TS2). However, the target is a metastable 2D tetragonal KBr polymorph, so the main kinetic challenge is avoiding crystallization of the normal KBr phase [Reasonable Inference supported by polymorph sensitivity in CS1, CS5, TS1]. SS and TS support melt kinetics well, while CS warns that alkali-halide polymorph selection is sensitive. Source scores: CS = 60, SS = 80, TS = 78.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures similar to 373–583 K annealing in structurally similar halides, but sealed evacuated ampoules and repeated cycling add complexity (SS3–SS5). TS analogues show that some solid-state routes can require extended heat treatments or specialized vacuum/inert handling (TS3, TS4). CS gives only indirect evidence because its best matches emphasize thin-film deposition rather than bulk annealing (CS1, CS5). Source scores: CS = 50 neutral/indirect, SS = 65, TS = 60.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solid-state polymorph conversion in KBr should have reasonable ionic mobility at moderate temperatures [Reasonable Inference], but formation of a specific 2D P4/mmm polymorph from a bulk salt is structurally demanding. Structurally similar solid-state halides required long annealing, intermediate grindings, and sometimes showed impurities, implying slow ordering/phase-control limitations (SS3–SS5). TS4 also reports difficult phase control and unreacted material in a related halide system, though its rare-earth chemistry is less directly applicable; CS thin-film analogues suggest kinetic trapping is more promising than bulk equilibration (CS1, CS5). Source scores: CS = 55, SS = 65, TS = 60.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern (Target). The described structure is a small binary KBr polymorph with two K sites and two Br sites, not a large complex cell, and it has edge/corner sharing but no face-sharing coexistence (Target). CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify amorphization tendency for the target; they provide indirect evidence that alkali halides can crystallize under evaporation or melt conditions rather than necessarily amorphize (CS1, CS2; SS1; TS1). Source scores: CS = 70, SS = 70, TS = 70, with target-structure evidence dominating the final 95.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target KBr P4/mmm phase is likely metastable rather than a ground-state bulk phase because its E_hull is 0.0470 eV/atom, just below the common kinetic-accessibility threshold but still above the hull (Target). The best support comes from CS and TS analogues showing metastable RbBr/CsBr/CsI alkali-halide polymorphs can be accessed by low-temperature vacuum evaporation and controlled warming (CS1, CS2, CS5; TS1). Structurally similar CsBr also supports the relevance of alkali-bromide octahedral/corner-edge connectivity, though it is cubic rather than tetragonal P4/mmm (SS1). The key enabling factor is the chemical simplicity of KBr: a single binary salt precursor gives exact 1:1 composition and avoids multication ordering problems [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/CS2 and SS2/TS2 binary-halide precursor use]. The key limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because analogues explicitly report temperature-sensitive abnormal structures and metastable phase windows (CS1, CS5; TS1).
