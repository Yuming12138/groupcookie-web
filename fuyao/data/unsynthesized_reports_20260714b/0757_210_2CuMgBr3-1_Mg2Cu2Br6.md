### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.018 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability thermodynamic window by the given rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values—Li₂ZnBr₄ at 0.017 eV/atom (TS-1), Li₂MgBr₄ at 0.0146 eV/atom (TS-2), Li₂MnBr₄ at 0.0148 eV/atom (TS-3), and Li₂FeBr₄ at 0.0287 eV/atom (TS-4)—all have reported syntheses. CS and SS also show multiple low-hull or stable halide frameworks accessible by sealed ampoule/melt routes (CS-3 to CS-5; SS-1/SS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is above hull, not exactly on hull, so it may require kinetic trapping or careful cooling.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to halide melt routes: 250–400 °C for AMBr₄ bromides (CS-4/SS-4) and melting/cooling in evacuated quartz ampoules for Li₂MgBr₄ (TS-2). The main penalties are controlled atmosphere/vacuum, sealed volatile halides, and air-sensitive anhydrous bromides, all consistent with dry Ar/evacuated ampoule handling in TS-2 and CS-4. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS requiring high pressure or >1000 °C for analogous bromides, so those penalties are not applied. The uncertainty is whether MgCuBr₃ melts congruently or forms competing MgBr₂/CuBr phases.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary bromides should react faster than oxide ceramics because halide melts/soft salts enable mobility, as shown by Li₂MgBr₄ synthesis from LiBr/MgBr₂ melts (TS-2) and clear-melt AMBr₄ bromide growth (CS-4/SS-4). MgBr₆ octahedra in Li₂MgBr₄ (TS-2) provide a useful local-structure match to the MgBr₆ octahedra in MgCuBr₃. However, the target’s Cu–Cu bonded trigonal-pyramidal unit is not directly reproduced in TS-2 or CS-4, so structural reconstruction is only moderate. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact MgCuBr₃ crystallization pathway.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is supported only indirectly by elemental sealed-ampoule synthesis of Mg₂Al₂Se₅ (CS-1) and metal/halide sealed-ampoule synthesis of CrInBr₃ (CS-2). It has greater severity because Br₂ is volatile, toxic, and pressure-generating [Reasonable Inference], and staged heating is needed to avoid uncontrolled bromination [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 uses 1000 °C, but the proposed bromide route can likely operate near the lower CS-2 temperature of 450 °C because bromides are more mobile than chalcogenide solids [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports direct elemental Mg–Cu–Br synthesis specifically, so this route is scored lower.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental reactions can work in sealed ampoules, as CS-1 shows for Mg-containing chalcogenides, and CS-2 shows metal plus bromide reactions at 450 °C. However, starting from Mg and Cu metals requires full bromination and long-range ordering, which is kinetically less favorable than starting from binary bromides [Reasonable Inference]. The route may benefit from in situ formation of molten/volatile bromides, but controlling Cu(I) rather than Cu(II) is a significant uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that direct elements yield phase-pure MgCuBr₃.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses molten bromide flux conditions analogous to CS-2, where CrInBr₃ was prepared at 450 °C for 5 days in evacuated glass ampoules, and CS-4/SS-4, where bromide salts form clear melts at 250–400 °C under dry Ar. The severity comes from sealed ampoules, air-sensitive anhydrous bromides, and flux-separation steps [Reasonable Inference]. There is no high-pressure or ultrahigh-temperature requirement in the supporting CS/SS/TS data. The remaining uncertainty is whether excess CuBr/MgBr₂ flux can be removed without decomposing or hydrating MgCuBr₃.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A molten flux should provide the best diffusion kinetics because CS-2 and CS-4 show bromide compounds crystallizing from molten media. Slow cooling is likely beneficial for layered crystal growth, as slow cooling is used in CS-2, CS-4/SS-4, and TS-2. The structural reconstruction score is not maximal because no CS/SS/TS analogue exactly matches the trigonal P-31m MgCuBr₃ sheet with Cu–Cu dimers. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the solubility window and flux composition needed for MgCuBr₃.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger from cation size/coordination mismatch between Mg²⁺ in octahedra and Cu⁺ in trigonal-pyramidal coordination [Reasonable Inference], while the target description shows only corner and edge sharing, not face sharing. The cell-size penalty is not triggered because the described formula and space group do not imply a >40 atom cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates otherwise. Structurally related bromides in SS/TS often crystallize from melts rather than becoming amorphous, supporting a low–medium rather than high amorphization risk (SS-2, TS-2). The uncertainty is that layered halides can form glassy or poorly crystalline products if quenched too quickly [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgCuBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.018 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues in TS-1 to TS-4. The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports low-to-moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule bromide synthesis, especially using anhydrous binary halides and slow cooling, as shown by TS-2, CS-4/SS-4, and CS-2. The most important enabling factor is high halide mobility in molten or soft bromide media, which lowers diffusion barriers and supports ordered crystallization. The most important limiting factor is phase and redox control: MgCuBr₃ requires Mg²⁺ and Cu⁺, while Cu can oxidize or disproportionate under brominating or air/moisture-contaminated conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the compound appears realistically synthesizable, with best prospects in sealed, anhydrous, slow-cooled bromide melts.
