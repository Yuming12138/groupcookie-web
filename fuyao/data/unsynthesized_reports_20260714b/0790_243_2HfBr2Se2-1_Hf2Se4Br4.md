### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score [target]. This is reinforced by several related phases that are stable or nearly stable: Nb(SBr)₂ is near the hull [CS2], NbSe₂Cl₂ is on the hull [CS3], NbSeBr₃ is on the hull [SS1, TS1], and Hf–Se compounds in the thermodynamic set are also on the hull [SS2, TS2]. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase-pure synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, because analogous sealed-tube bromochalcogenides form at 400–500 °C [CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1]. The main severity penalties are the need for evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile Br₂/Se handling [CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1]. There is no evidence for high pressure or >1000 °C synthesis in the analogues [CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by starting from refractory Hf metal [Reasonable Inference], whereas analogous Nb/Ta elemental reactions succeed because Br₂/Se vapor transport assists reaction [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The structural match to Nb(SBr)₂ is strong because both are layered M(ChBr)₂-type materials with 8-coordinate metal and water-like Br coordination [CS2, target]. The uncertainty is that Hf may form stable binary HfSe₂ or HfBr₄ before the ternary phase forms [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 still requires vacuum sealing and Br₂ containment, as do the analogous halide/chalcogenide routes [CS3, CS4, SS3, TS3]. It is penalized for possible multi-step preparation of HfSe₂, because the analogous Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ route uses a preformed NbSe₂ precursor [CS4, SS3, TS3]. The temperature severity is moderate because the relevant NbSe₂/NbCl₄ and NbX₂Y₂ reactions occur around 400–530 °C [CS3, CS4, SS3, TS3].

#### F3 — Route 2

This is kinetically preferred because HfSe₂ provides a preformed Hf–Se layered framework, reducing reconstruction relative to elemental Hf [Reasonable Inference]. The analogy to NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ chemistry supports the use of a layered dichalcogenide precursor for metal chalcogenide halide formation [CS4, SS3, TS3]. The key uncertainty is whether bromination of HfSe₂ gives the desired Hf(SeBr)₂ rather than HfBr₄, HfSe₂, or other Hf–Se–Br phases [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct HfSe₂ bromination].

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT uses similar moderate temperatures but adds gradient control and long dwell times [CS3, SS1, TS1]. It requires vacuum sealing and volatile Br₂, both of which are explicitly used in Nb/Ta–Se–Br analogues [SS1, TS1]. The route is not extreme in pressure or temperature, but operational control is more demanding than a simple bulk reaction [CS3, SS1, TS1].

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT benefits from volatile Br₂ and Se transport chemistry, and NbSeBr₃ crystals were obtained this way [SS1, TS1]. However, phase control in NbX₂Y₂ systems is reported as difficult, with polymorphism and narrow phase windows [CS3]. For Hf(SeBr)₂, vapor-phase composition control may be the limiting kinetic factor because Hf transport likely depends on volatile bromide formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [target]. The structure is a relatively simple layered Hf(SeBr)₂ sheet and does not show the complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing networks that would strongly favor amorphization [target]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so the C2 check has some uncertainty, but no CS/SS/TS evidence indicates a large complex cell for this target [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(SeBr)₂ is likely a true thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target]. The synthesis outlook is supported by close analogues: Nb(SBr)₂ has the same layered M(ChBr)₂ motif and is made in a sealed tube at 500 °C [CS2], while Nb/Ta–Se–Br phases are accessible by CVT around 500 °C [SS1, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of sealed-ampoule halogen/chalcogen vapor chemistry, which repeatedly succeeds for related bromochalcogenides [CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under volatile Br₂/Se conditions, because NbX₂Y₂ and Nb–Se–Cl analogues show polymorphism, contamination, and reproducibility issues [CS3, CS4, SS3, TS3]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but the best practical route likely uses HfSe₂ as a preorganized precursor rather than relying entirely on refractory Hf metal reaction [Reasonable Inference].
