### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
LiFeF4 has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff and therefore likely metastable but close to accessible stability. [Target] CS and SS analogues are mostly on-hull or near-hull and experimentally synthesized, supporting the score. [CS1–CS5][SS1–SS5] TS analogues include metastable fluorides up to ~0.110 eV/atom that are still synthesized, supporting accessibility around the target hull value. [TS1–TS5]

**Route 1 F2/F3 — High-Pressure Fluorination**  
The severity score is reduced by F2 toxicity, reactive atmosphere, and pressure containment, but not by high temperature or GPa pressure because LiCoF4 formed at 400 °C and 300 bar. [CS1] Kinetics score high because F2 raises fluorine chemical potential and the analogue is isostructural Hydrophilite-derived P2_1/c. [CS1][Target][Reasonable Inference] Remaining uncertainty is Fe-specific side-phase formation, since Li–Fe–F chemistry produced Li3FeF6 by-product in another route. [CS2]

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Sealed-Tube Solid-State**  
Severity is moderate because sealed Pt/Ni tube fluoride reactions are established but require dry containment and high temperatures. [CS3][TS1][TS3] Kinetics are less favorable because binary fluorides can be diffusion-limited and analogues require 620–950 °C or long heating. [CS3][TS2][TS3] The main uncertainty is whether LiFeF4 forms before competing Li–Fe–F phases. [CS2][Reasonable Inference]

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Chloride Flux**  
Severity is lowered by the need for sealed Ar tubes, corrosive ZnCl2 flux, and post-growth recovery. [SS1][SS2][Reasonable Inference] Kinetics are favorable because flux growth produced Hydrophilite-derived LiMnFeF6 at only 580 °C with slow cooling. [SS1][SS2] The uncertainty is cation/oxidation-state control in a Mn-free Li–Fe–F system. [SS1][SS2][CS2][Reasonable Inference]

**Route 4 F2/F3 — HF Hydrothermal**  
Severity is significant because concentrated HF and autogenous pressure up to ~2500 bar are required. [CS2] Kinetics are favorable because HF hydrothermal media enhance dissolution and transport, and LiFe2F6 is a close Li–Fe–F Hydrophilite-derived analogue. [CS2][Reasonable Inference] The limiting uncertainty is phase selectivity, since Li3FeF6 by-product was reported. [CS2]

**Route 5 F2/F3 — GPa HP–HT**  
Severity is high because 5–5.5 GPa pressure, dry handling, and Pt capsules are required. [CS4][TS5] Kinetics are moderate because HP–HT can stabilize dense fluoride modifications at 300–575 °C, but reaction scale and transport are constrained. [CS4][TS5][Reasonable Inference] The uncertainty is transferability, since the best direct LiMF4 analogue uses F2 pressure rather than GPa pressure. [CS1][CS4]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the cation-size mismatch check is triggered, because Li+ and Fe3+ in octahedral coordination differ by >15%. [Target][Reasonable Inference] The target does not exceed the >40 atom-cell threshold by analogy to Hydrophilite LiCoF4, and it reports corner/edge sharing without face sharing. [CS1][Target] Similar corner/edge-sharing fluoride frameworks are crystallized in CS and SS analogues, so amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. [CS1][CS2][SS1][SS2]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiFeF4 is best treated as a **near-hull metastable phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than clearly zero. [Target] The strongest synthesis support comes from isostructural Hydrophilite-derived LiCoF4, which was made directly by high-pressure fluorination. [CS1][Target] Additional support comes from Li–Fe fluoride hydrothermal synthesis and Hydrophilite-derived flux-grown Fe-containing analogues. [CS2][SS1][SS2] The key enabling factor is maintaining high fluoride chemical potential while preserving dry, sealed conditions. [CS1][CS2][Reasonable Inference] The main limiting factor is phase competition in Li–Fe–F chemistry, especially Fe-rich or Li-rich phases such as LiFe2F6/Li3FeF6. [CS2]
