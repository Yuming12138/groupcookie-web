### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so by the requested rubric it receives a very high thermodynamic score [target]. Thermodynamic analogues include on-hull and near-hull chloride materials such as Rb₂PtCl₆, Cs₂Pd(ICl₂)₂, CsAuCl₄, and Cs₂RhOCl₅ [TS1–TS4]. The main uncertainty is that several chemically similar materials in CS have high computed hull energies while still being reported crystallographically [CS1–CS4], suggesting that solution-grown molecular/ionic salts may not be perfectly captured by bulk hull metrics.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because closest analogues crystallize by room-temperature or low-temperature evaporation from HCl/water without high pressure or inert atmosphere [SS1/TS1, SS2, CS4]. The only significant deduction is hazardous/corrosive chemistry: Ru nitrosyl compounds and concentrated HCl require careful handling [SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact CsRuNOCl₅ toxicity is treated as neutral beyond the general Ru/HCl hazard.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because solution crystallization bypasses solid-state diffusion, and analogues form crystals in days under ambient evaporation [SS1/TS1, CS2]. The target’s local Ru–NO and Ru–Cl geometry is close to the Ru nitrosyl complex in SS4 and Ru oxychloride octahedra in SS2, reducing structural reconstruction difficulty. Remaining uncertainty is the exact chloride/nitrosyl speciation of Ru in strong HCl [SS4; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains mild in temperature, atmosphere, and pressure because SS4 reports room-temperature aqueous synthesis in air. It is penalized for multiple operations and specialized/hazardous reagents: Ru nitrosyl precursor preparation, sulfamic acid reaction, BaCl₂ addition, filtration, and crystallization [SS4]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact ligand-substitution pathway to CsRuNOCl₅, so they are neutral for the extra procedural complexity.

#### F3 — Route 2

The route has good solution-phase kinetics because SS4 shows Ru–NO complexes react and crystallize at room temperature. However, conversion from nitro/hydroxo ligands to a pentachloride nitrosyl environment likely requires more speciation control than Route 1 [SS4; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Cs⁺ promotes the desired sheet structure or precipitates competing Cs/Ru chloride salts [SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids high temperature and high pressure in the GPa sense, and TS3 demonstrates sealed acidic halide synthesis at 450 K. It is penalized for volatile/corrosive HCl in a sealed system and hazardous Ru nitrosyl chemistry [TS3; SS4; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS for sealed solvothermal synthesis of the exact Ru–NO pentachloride, so this route is less preferred.

#### F3 — Route 3

Liquid/solvothermal transport should be kinetically easier than bulk solid-state reaction, as supported by TS3 acidic ampule crystallization. The limitation is structural and chemical: TS3 involves Pd/I/Cl halide complexes, not Ru–NO complexes, so nitrosyl retention under sealed heating is uncertain [TS3; insufficient direct TS evidence for Ru–NO]. Higher temperature may improve crystal growth but could increase nitrosyl decomposition risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Cs/Ru size contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion; the target’s Cs–Cl distances are much longer than Ru–Cl distances [target]. The formula unit is small and no >40 atom unit cell is provided, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports treating this check as neutral. Mixed vertex/edge/face sharing is reported in some analogues [SS2, SS5], but not explicitly in the target description, so C3 is not triggered for the target.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsRuNOCl₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target]. The analogue support is strong for mild solution crystallization: Cs/Rh oxychloride forms from CsCl and metal chloride in HCl over a few days [SS1/TS1], Ru chloride oxy-complexes crystallize from concentrated HCl [SS2], and Ru–NO complexes can be prepared and crystallized in aqueous acid at room temperature [SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is high chloride activity in acidic solution, which should stabilize the Ru–Cl coordination sphere and promote Cs salt crystallization [SS1/TS1, SS2, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Ru nitrosyl speciation, since Ru–NO complexes can undergo ligand substitution and redox/acid reactions [SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the best-supported path is slow evaporation from concentrated chloride acid rather than high-temperature solid-state synthesis.
