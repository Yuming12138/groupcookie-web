### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.041 eV/atom, which lies in the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom category. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values are experimentally synthesized, including Li₆FeCl₈ at 0.0388 eV/atom, Li₂CrCl₄ at 0.0394 eV/atom, Li₆CoCl₈ at 0.0309 eV/atom, and Na₂CrCl₄ at 0.0404 eV/atom (TS1–TS3, TS5). Structurally similar Li–Cl salts are stable or nearly stable and synthesized from LiCl-containing mixtures (SS1–SS3). The main uncertainty is that the target is not ordinary rocksalt LiCl but a PbO-like P4/nmm polymorph, so thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee bulk phase selection [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to SS1–SS3 and TS1–TS3, so no >1000 °C penalty applies. However, sealed dry Ar/vacuum handling is required, as structurally and thermodynamically similar Li–chlorides are handled in gloveboxes, Schlenk lines, evacuated tubes, or sealed ampoules (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The route also involves multiple operations—drying, glovebox loading, sealing, annealing, and quenching—which justifies the multi-step deduction [Reasonable Inference supported by SS1/SS3 procedures]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for pure LiCl polymorph processing, so its severity signal is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary chlorides are generally more mobile than refractory oxides, and Li–chloride analogues are synthesized at modest temperatures of 200–450 °C or 573–673 K (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The kinetic weakness is that the desired product is a polymorph of LiCl, so structural reconstruction from the ordinary LiCl lattice into a 2D P4/nmm sheet is not guaranteed [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing times of 1–4 weeks in TS1–TS3 and up to 1 month in SS1/SS3 indicate that ordering and crystallization in related chlorides can be slow. CS gives insufficient direct kinetic evidence for pure LiCl, so it is neutral for this specific route.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses the best-supported experimental window from SS2, namely sealed-tube flux growth around 400 °C with slow cooling, and from SS1/SS3, sealed dry LiCl/CsCl reactions at 573–673 K. These are not high-temperature or high-pressure conditions, but they do require dry Ar/vacuum sealing and moisture exclusion (SS1–SS3). The use of LiCl/RbCl/CsCl fluxes requires drying, sealed-tube reaction, controlled cooling, and post-growth separation, so the process is multi-step [Reasonable Inference from SS1–SS3]. TS analogues also support glovebox/vacuum handling for Li–chlorides (TS1–TS3).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth has the strongest kinetic support because SS2 explicitly reports RbLiCl₂ formation from RbCl + LiCl at ~400 °C with slow cooling, and SS1/SS3 show Cs–Li–Cl crystallization from LiCl/CsCl at 573–673 K. The LiCl₄ tetrahedral environments in SS1–SS3 have Li–Cl distances close to the target’s 2.40 Å, suggesting local structural compatibility. The limitation is that the same evidence also shows strong formation of ternary alkali–Li–Cl phases, so the flux may stabilize double salts instead of binary P4/nmm LiCl (SS1–SS3). CS provides insufficient direct signal for alkali-chloride flux growth of LiCl, so it is neutral here.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Thin-film synthesis avoids high bulk temperatures and high pressure [Reasonable Inference], so the thermal severity is low. However, high vacuum or controlled inert deposition is required, and moisture-sensitive handling is consistent with SS1/SS3 and TS1 even though those sources are not thin-film studies. LiCl volatility/desorption and air sensitivity justify penalties for sealed/controlled environment and sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for thin-film processing, so all thin-film-specific severity estimates remain uncertain and neutral relative to the references.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Epitaxial growth is a plausible way to access a metastable 2D P4/nmm polymorph that is only 0.041 eV/atom above hull [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source reports PLD, sputtering, MBE, or vapor-deposited LiCl polymorphs, so the crystallization pathway score is limited by insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS. The simple binary stoichiometry favors composition control [Reasonable Inference], but LiCl volatility and possible relaxation to rocksalt LiCl lower the score [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore potentially enabling but less evidence-backed than Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Li, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The structure description indicates a simple LiCl sheet and does not suggest a large >40-atom cell, so C2 is not triggered. The target does contain mixed corner- and edge-sharing LiCl₄ tetrahedra and ClLi₄ trigonal pyramids, so C3 is triggered directly by the target description. SS analogues also show mixed corner/edge-sharing LiCl₄ tetrahedral frameworks that are crystalline, which reduces concern that mixed connectivity alone prevents crystallization (SS1–SS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the ordinary ground-state LiCl phase, because its E_hull is 0.041 eV/atom and its PbO-like P4/nmm layered structure differs from common bulk LiCl [Reasonable Inference from target data]. The thermodynamic case is favorable: several TS analogues with nearly identical energy-above-hull values have reported syntheses (TS1–TS3, TS5). The structural case is also encouraging because Li–Cl tetrahedral environments and Li–Cl distances near 2.3–2.4 Å occur in synthesized Cs/Rb–Li–Cl compounds (SS1–SS3). The most critical enabling factor is the low formation-energy penalty combined with chloride flux mobility at 300–450 °C (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with stable rocksalt LiCl or ternary alkali–Li–Cl salts, because SS1–SS3 show that LiCl readily forms other crystalline chloride frameworks under the proposed conditions.
