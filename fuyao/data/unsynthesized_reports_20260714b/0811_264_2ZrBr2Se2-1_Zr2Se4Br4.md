### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target thermodynamic data. This is supported by chemically similar hull-stable chalcogenide halides such as NbSe₂Cl₂, NbSeBr₃, and CrSBr (CS-1; CS-4; CS-5), and by structurally/thermodynamically similar hull-stable phases in SS/TS (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide kinetic formation window, especially in volatile halogen systems [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 450–550 °C, supported by Nb(SBr)₂ at 500 °C and Nb–Se–Br at 500–460 °C (CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). The score is reduced because the reaction requires vacuum/sealed-tube handling and volatile/toxic Br₂ and Se, which are also present in the analogous syntheses (CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). Insufficient evidence from TS for a lower-severity open-atmosphere route; TS analogues also use sealed/vacuum ampoules (TS-1 to TS-5).

#### F3: Route 1

Direct elemental reaction has moderate-to-slow kinetics because Zr metal is refractory and likely oxide-passivated [Reasonable Inference], while related syntheses still require sealed heating for days to weeks (CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). Volatile Br₂ can improve transport and reactivity, as Br₂-containing analogues form at only ~500 °C (CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). Phase competition is a concern because analogues sometimes yield multiple polymorphs or require careful phase control (CS-2; CS-3; SS-5 / TS-5).

#### F2: Route 2

CVT avoids very high temperatures and high pressure, consistent with 400–515 °C and 500–460 °C windows for Nb chalcogenide halides (CS-1; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). The severity remains moderate because CVT requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile halogens, and precise gradients (CS-1; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a nonsealed vapor route; the structurally and thermodynamically similar references overwhelmingly use sealed ampoules (SS-1; SS-3; SS-5; TS-1; TS-3; TS-5).

#### F3: Route 2

CVT is kinetically favorable relative to direct solid-state reaction because halide vapor transport can move Zr/Se/Br species without long-range diffusion through dense Zr metal [Reasonable Inference]. Related chalcogenide halides form as single crystals under gradients over 15 days to 3–4 weeks (CS-1; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1), indicating effective but slow crystal-growth kinetics. Remaining uncertainty is whether Zr forms volatile transport species under the same Br₂ chemical potential without overforming ZrBr₄ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 3

The binary-precursor route is chemically attractive but operationally more severe because it may require prior ZrSe₂ synthesis and handling of air-sensitive/volatile ZrBr₄ or Br₂ [Reasonable Inference]. The route is supported by NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ forming a Nb–Se–Cl phase in sealed Pyrex at 530 °C for 2–3 weeks (CS-3). SS/TS provide additional support for sealed binary/element reactions but not specifically for ZrBr₄ chemistry, so there is partial uncertainty (SS-4 / TS-4).

#### F3: Route 3

Starting from ZrSe₂ could reduce structural reconstruction and improve local Zr–Se ordering [Reasonable Inference], analogous to NbSe₂ acting as a precursor in Nb–Se–Cl synthesis (CS-3). Reactive halide precursors should improve bromination kinetics relative to elemental Zr [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is stoichiometry: ZrSe₂ + Br₂ may produce ZrSe₂Br₂, but excessive bromine activity could instead form ZrBr₄-rich or selenium-deficient products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Zr, so cation-size mismatch is not a meaningful amorphization driver. The described structure is a relatively simple layered C2/m sheet rather than a large complex multication framework, so C2 and C3 are not triggered from target data; however, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly quantifies amorphization behavior, so those sources are neutral for this factor. Low amorphization risk is also consistent with many related crystalline layered/ribbon products being isolated by sealed-tube or CVT methods (CS-1; CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr(SeBr)₂ is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state phase**, because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom from the target data. The CS evidence is strong: Nb(SBr)₂ has the same monoclinic C2/m layered chalcogenide-halide motif and forms at 500 °C in a sealed tube (CS-2), while NbSe₂Cl₂ and Nb–Se–Br analogues are accessible by CVT/sealed-tube methods (CS-1; CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport/reactivity, because Br₂ or other halogens enable formation and crystallization of related low-dimensional chalcogenide halides at moderate temperatures (CS-1; CS-2; CS-4 / SS-1 / TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is control of bromine chemical potential and phase purity, since analogous systems report polymorph coexistence, poor reproducibility, or difficulty obtaining phase-pure samples (CS-2; CS-3; SS-5 / TS-5). Overall, synthesis is plausible and best attempted by sealed-tube CVT or vapor-assisted growth rather than open solid-state heating.
