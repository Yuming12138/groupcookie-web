## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the favorable threshold in the rubric, so a score of 90 is assigned. CS supports feasibility because several chemically similar Sr–Re/Sr–Os/Sr–Ru perovskite-derived phases are stable or nearly stable, including Sr₂LiReO₆ and Sr₂YOsO₆ (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). SS gives particularly strong support because Ca₃ReO₆ and Sr₂MgReO₆ are stable/near-stable and structurally related Re oxides (SS-1, SS-3). TS indicates that phases around ~0.06–0.08 eV/atom can still be experimentally realized, but it is indirect because some TS analogues are different chemistries such as tantalates or osmates (TS-1 to TS-5).

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled evacuated/Ar atmosphere, sealed-tube handling, ReO₃ volatility, and multi-step grinding/pelletizing, all of which are directly reflected in SS-1 and SS-3. It avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the closest Re analogues formed at 800–850 °C, not >1000 °C (SS-1, SS-3). Remaining uncertainty is whether Sr₂CaReO₆ needs a narrower oxygen fugacity than Ca₃ReO₆ or Sr₂MgReO₆; no exact target synthesis is provided, so this is a [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because oxide/carbonate + ReO₃ solid-state reactions require cation diffusion over pellet length scales, and SS-1/SS-3 report 24–40 h anneals. The structural match is favorable because ReO₆ octahedral perovskite-derived motifs occur in Ca₃ReO₆ and Sr₂MgReO₆ (SS-1, SS-3). The limiting kinetic factor is likely B-site/Ca–Re ordering and distortion into triclinic P1, which is inferred from the lower symmetry of the target relative to many analogues [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has milder equipment demands because CS-2/CS-3 describe a straightforward ceramic route at 600–800 °C with only intermediate regrinding. However, CS-2/CS-3 do not specify atmosphere, so evidence for oxygen fugacity control is insufficient from CS. Because the target likely requires high-valent Re⁶⁺ by charge balance, controlled oxidation is inferred and the route is penalized modestly [Reasonable Inference].

### F3 — Route 2

Re metal is refractory and can oxidize slowly, so precursor activity and diffusion scores are lower than for ReO₃ or solution routes [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2/CS-3 show that Sr₂LiReO₆ can form from Re metal at 800 °C, which supports feasibility, but Ca²⁺ substitution changes both size and charge balance relative to Li⁺. The remaining uncertainty is whether Re metal fully oxidizes to the needed state without producing ReO₂/ReO₃ secondary phases.

### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for multi-step processing and likely need for final sealed annealing, which is inferred from ReO₃ sealed-tube analogues SS-1/SS-3. CS-4 supports pH-controlled co-precipitation and stepwise heat treatment for complex Sr-containing oxides, but it does not contain Re, so Re-specific solution chemistry has insufficient evidence from CS. The route is therefore plausible but less directly proven.

### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic advantage of Route 3 is high precursor homogeneity, because solution mixing or gel formation reduces diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports co-precipitation as a route to complex oxides, but no CS/SS/TS entry directly validates Sr/Ca/Re sol–gel chemistry. The major uncertainty is whether Re remains uniformly distributed during nitrate/organic burn-out or volatilizes as high-valent Re oxide species.

### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 benefits from low temperature because TS-5 used hydroxide flux at 600 °C. It is penalized for caustic flux handling, washing/isolation steps, possible sealed-container requirements, and uncertain Re volatility control [Reasonable Inference]. Direct evidence for Re flux growth is insufficient from TS because TS-5 is Eu–Os–O, not Sr–Ca–Re–O.

### F3 — Route 4

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂CaReO₆ is likely a **borderline stable or low-energy metastable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the cutoff between highly stable and moderately metastable materials. The strongest support comes from structurally similar Re oxides and Sr–Re double perovskites synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods, especially Ca₃ReO₆ and Sr₂MgReO₆ (SS-1, SS-3). The most critical enabling factor is that ReO₆ octahedral perovskite-derived networks form at accessible temperatures around 800–850 °C in closely related systems (SS-1, SS-3; CS-2, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is Re volatility/redox control, since ReO₃-containing syntheses use evacuated or sealed tubes and careful handling (SS-1, SS-3). Phase purity may also be challenged by competing Ca–Re–O or Sr–Re–O phases, as Re oxide analogues often require repeated grinding or careful stoichiometry (SS-1, SS-3, TS-3).
