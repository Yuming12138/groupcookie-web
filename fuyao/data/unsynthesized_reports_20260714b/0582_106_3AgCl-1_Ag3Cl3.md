### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. SS and TS strongly support the idea that Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide compounds near or on the hull can be experimentally synthesized, including E_hull = 0 analogues Ag₂HgSI₂, Ag₅PS₄Cl₂, and AgHgSI (SS-3/4/5; TS-1/2/3). CS is less directly relevant to AgCl but includes stable or near-stable binary compounds such as CuS and CdS (CS-1/2). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not by itself select the orthorhombic Pmm2 polymorph over conventional AgCl polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1  
Route 1 uses mild temperature and ambient pressure, consistent with CS-1 chemical bath deposition at 80 °C in air. Deductions are mainly for multistep solution handling and optional controlled atmosphere/complexation during annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for aqueous AgCl deposition, so their contribution to F2 for this route is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1  
Kinetically, solution precipitation of AgCl should be rapid because Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ react directly in solution [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 supports low-temperature bath formation of a binary semiconductor film. The weakness is crystallization of the specific orthorhombic 2D polymorph: CS-1 reports mixed CdS phases and impurities, and SS/TS do not confirm solution access to Pmm2 AgCl. Thus the route has good precursor and stoichiometry kinetics but uncertain polymorph kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed-ampoule equipment, consistent with SS-2 to SS-5 and TS-1 to TS-3. It is penalized for vacuum/sealed atmosphere, volatile halide management, and multistep grind/seal/anneal/regrind procedures, all seen in SS-2 and related SS/TS analogues. CS-2 also supports evacuated sealed-tube synthesis as feasible but not as mild as solution deposition.

#### F3 — Route 2  
Solid-state AgCl annealing benefits from a simple binary precursor and relatively mobile Ag⁺ chemistry [Reasonable Inference], and SS/TS analogues form at moderate temperatures, including 120 °C to 725 K (SS-3/4/5; TS-1/2/3). However, transformation from conventional AgCl to a layered Pmm2 polymorph likely requires structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 and TS-3 show that Ag-halide/chalcogenide phases may require specific thermal histories or long anneals, lowering the kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 3  
Melt recrystallization is not high-temperature by ceramic standards, but it requires a sealed dry ampoule and careful thermal-gradient handling, as in SS-1 Bridgman growth. SS-1 supports feasibility of melt growth for an Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide, while SS-4/TS-2 confirm AgCl can be used in sealed-tube reactions. Deductions come from volatile halide containment, controlled atmosphere, and multistep crystal-growth handling.

#### F3 — Route 3  
A molten AgCl charge should have excellent mass transport and fast ionic rearrangement [Reasonable Inference], and SS-1 shows Ag₃SI crystals can be grown from a melted sealed charge. The limitation is nucleation selectivity: SS-1 reports mixtures of phases from which crystals are separated, and CS-3 to CS-5 show high-temperature growth can generate multiple polytypes. Therefore, melt growth is kinetically favorable but phase-selectivity limited.

#### F2 — Route 4  
Vapor transport requires sealed dry/vacuum operation and management of volatile halide species, consistent with sealed/vacuum halide synthesis in SS/TS and with vacuum/dry N₂ handling in TS-5. Proposed AgCl temperatures are inferred below the 1200 °C ZnS vapor-growth cases in CS-3 to CS-5 [Reasonable Inference]. The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, volatility, multistep gradient operation, and optional hazardous transport additives.

#### F3 — Route 4  
Vapor growth can access layered or polytypic crystals in principle, supported by ZnS vapor/sublimation examples in CS-3 to CS-5. However, those same CS examples emphasize difficult polytype control, and TS-5 reports sluggish/non-reproducible phase changes in AuBr. Direct vapor-growth evidence for the target AgCl polymorph is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the kinetic score remains moderate-low.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency  
The target is a simple binary with one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure has only two inequivalent Ag and two inequivalent Cl sites and corner-sharing connectivity, so it does not meet the high-complexity or mixed-connectivity amorphization checks. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for pure AgCl, but analogous crystalline Ag halide/chalcogenides in SS/TS indicate crystallization is generally feasible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase by the provided E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, although experimental access may still be polymorph-limited. The CS data support mild low-temperature deposition for chemically similar binary compounds, while the SS/TS data more strongly support sealed-tube and melt approaches for Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide frameworks. The most important enabling factor is the very simple Ag:Cl stoichiometry combined with predicted thermodynamic stability. The most important limiting factor is selective formation of the unusual orthorhombic 2D Pmm2 AgCl structure rather than conventional AgCl or other Ag/AgCl products. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, but structural verification by PXRD/GIXRD and careful light/stoichiometry control will be essential.
