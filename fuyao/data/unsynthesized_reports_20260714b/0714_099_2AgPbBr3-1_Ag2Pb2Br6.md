### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target is calculated at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving the maximum thermodynamic score by the required rubric. TS analogues include E_hull = 0 and near-zero layered halides [TS-1–TS-4], and CS/SS analogues include multiple stable or low-hull bromides/halides [CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-4]. The main uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing AgBr/PbBr₂ ordering variants; this uncertainty is not resolved by CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-ampoule solid-state.** Severity is moderate because analogous bromides commonly require vacuum-sealed ampoules and multi-day heating [CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-4, TS-1], but temperatures remain far below 1000 °C. Kinetics are favorable relative to oxide synthesis because binary halides are reactive and low-melting [SS-1, SS-4; Reasonable Inference], but ordering Ag and Pb into the 2D P-31m phase may require annealing. Phase impurities reported for Rb/Cs–Bi–Br ampoule syntheses reduce confidence [SS-1, SS-4].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, melt/self-flux.** Severity remains moderate because evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, and toxic Pb halides are required, as in TlPbI₃ melt growth [SS-3]. Kinetics are the strongest among dry routes because molten halides improve mass transport, and TlPbI₃ demonstrates direct ternary Pb-halide crystallization from binary halide melts [SS-3]. The uncertainty is whether AgPbBr₃ melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically forms AgBr/PbBr₂-rich phases; no CS/SS/TS source directly answers this.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, hydrothermal/solvothermal.** Severity is lowest because CS-5 demonstrates low-pressure hydrothermal bromide synthesis at 120–200 °C without high vacuum or high temperature. Kinetics can be good through solution transport, and PbI₂ analogues crystallize under mild gel/solution conditions [SS-2, TS-2–TS-4]. The main uncertainty is AgBr’s low solubility and the need for bromide-rich acidic complexation; this is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly proven by CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, CVT.** Severity is comparable to sealed-ampoule routes because CVT requires vacuum, volatile transport species, and multi-day thermal gradients [TS-1, SS-5]. Kinetics are favorable due to vapor-phase transport, but TS-1/SS-5 report low yields and equilibrium mixtures, indicating phase-control challenges. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ag–Pb–Br transport chemistry specifically, so this route is less preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target’s small formula, lack of face-sharing in the described connectivity, and comparable Ag/Pb cation sizes suggest low amorphization tendency [Target structure; Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar layered halides crystallize as ordered solids [SS-1–SS-4], and thermodynamic PbI₂ polytypes crystallize readily [TS-2–TS-4]. Remaining uncertainty is polytypism or stacking disorder, which is plausible for layered halides and is seen in PbI₂ polytype examples [SS-2, TS-2–TS-4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgPbBr₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also at or near the hull [TS-1–TS-4]. The CS/SS/TS data strongly support halide synthesis in sealed ampoules, melts, hydrothermal media, and CVT, but they do not contain a direct Ag–Pb–Br precedent, so the transfer is plausible rather than proven. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of soft halide salts, supported by low-temperature bromide and iodide syntheses [CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against binary AgBr/PbBr₂ and possible layered stacking variants, consistent with impurity and polytype issues in structurally similar halides [SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, TS-2–TS-4]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with hydrothermal/solvothermal crystallization as the lowest-severity first attempt and melt/ampoule methods as robust backup routes.
