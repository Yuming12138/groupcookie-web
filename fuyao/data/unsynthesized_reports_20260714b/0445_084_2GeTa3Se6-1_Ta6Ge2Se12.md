### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin and suggests a predicted ground-state phase. Several chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar Ta–Se phases also have near-zero hull energies, including Ta₂PdSe₆ [CS1, TS5], Ta₂NiSe₅ [CS4, SS5, TS3], TaSe₂ [SS2, TS1], and Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂ [TS4]. The main uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for a Ge-containing ordered ternary; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Ta–Ge–Se synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated ampoule conditions, which are well supported for Ta–Se and Ta–M–Se analogues [CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, TS2–TS5]. Temperatures are moderate for solid-state chemistry, typically 650–850 °C in close analogues [CS4, SS1, TS2–TS3]. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and Se toxicity; high pressure and >1000 °C conditions are not required by the preferred route. The uncertainty is whether Ge incorporation requires higher temperature than the analogues; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction suffers from refractory Ta kinetics, while Se provides a reactive chalcogen environment [Reasonable Inference] and analogues still require days-long anneals [CS1, CS4, TS5]. TaSe₂ can form from elements at 850 °C [SS1, TS2], supporting formation of Ta–Se networks, but the target requires additional Ge ordering not directly shown in CS/SS/TS. Regrinding may be needed because Ta₂PdSe₆-related materials show inhomogeneity and impurity issues [CS1, TS5].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT requires vacuum ampoules, volatile halogens, and a multi-step workflow, all of which increase severity [SS2, SS3, TS1, TS4]. However, the temperatures remain in a conventional range for Ta selenides, with 900–700 °C gradients for TaSe₂ [SS2, SS3, TS1] and 875–800 °C for Ta₂NiSe₅ crystal growth [CS4, SS5, TS3]. No high pressure or ultrahigh temperature is indicated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by moving material through volatile species, which is why it is effective for TaSe₂ single crystals [SS2, SS3, TS1] and more complex Ta–Co/Pd–Se crystals [TS4]. This mitigates refractory Ta diffusion limitations relative to direct bulk reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The principal uncertainty is whether Ge forms compatible volatile transport species and incorporates uniformly; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ge transport.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The binary-selenide-assisted route remains within standard sealed-ampoule chemistry supported by TaSe₂ and Ta₂PdSe₆ analogues [SS1, TS2, CS1, TS5]. It is penalized for multi-step processing, vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and possible volatile Ge–Se intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and does not require temperatures above 1000 °C based on the selected analogue window [CS4, SS1, TS2–TS3].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Using TaSe₂-like precursors should reduce the need to form all Ta–Se bonds from refractory Ta metal [Reasonable Inference], and TaSe₂ phases are well established by SS/TS analogues [SS1–SS4, TS1–TS2]. The route still requires conversion to a more complex orthorhombic ternary and likely repeated grinding/annealing, as complex Ta–M–Se analogues show phase-control issues [CS1, TS5]. Thus kinetics are better than fully elemental direct reaction but not as favorable as CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has significant cation size/coordination mismatch between Ta and Ge [Reasonable Inference], giving one amorphization trigger. The structure is ordered and 2D, and many 2D Ta–Se analogues crystallize readily rather than amorphize [CS1, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. However, unit-cell atom count and detailed polyhedral sharing are not sufficiently specified by CS/SS/TS, so those checks are not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃GeSe₆ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of layered Ta–Se frameworks by evacuated ampoule reactions and CVT, especially TaSe₂, Ta₂NiSe₅, Ta₂PdSe₆, and Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂ [CS1, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The most important enabling factor is that Ta–Se layered networks form under accessible sealed-tube conditions, often at 650–900 °C [CS4, SS1, TS1–TS3]. The main limiting factor is the absence of direct Ge-containing analogue evidence in CS/SS/TS, making Ge incorporation, transport chemistry, and competing Ge–Se/Ta–Se phase equilibria uncertain. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will require careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry and likely CVT optimization for phase-pure crystals.
