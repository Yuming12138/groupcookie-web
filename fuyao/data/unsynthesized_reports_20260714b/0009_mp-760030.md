### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, so it falls at the high-score boundary for thermodynamic accessibility. [Target] CS and SS analogues include several V fluorides at or near the hull, supporting the plausibility of crystalline V–F frameworks. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-4, SS-5] Remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom is borderline rather than deeply stable, so the phase may require kinetic or redox stabilization. [Target, Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and GPa pressure, but it requires dry handling, sealed containment, and controlled vacuum/Ar/N2 conditions. [SS-1, CS-5, TS-1] The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy. [SS-1, SS-4] Main uncertainty is whether VF2 can be obtained and handled without oxidation or hydrolysis. [TS-1, SS-4]

**F3 — Route 1**  
The VF2/VF3 precursors are chemically close to the target and should preserve local VF6 chemistry. [SS-1, CS-5, Target] Kinetics are limited by solid-state diffusion and cation/valence ordering, as structurally similar fluorides require long anneals. [SS-1, SS-4, SS-5] This gives Route 1 the best balance of chemical transferability and manageable conditions. [SS-1, CS-5]

**F2 — Route 2**  
HF hydrothermal synthesis uses a lower temperature than solid-state sintering, but it introduces a severe safety and containment burden. [CS-1] The score is capped by volatile HF, sealed Pt-tube requirements, and corrosive/toxic reagent handling. [CS-1] SS and TS do not provide useful HF-growth evidence for this route, so their route-specific signals are neutral 50. [SS, TS]

**F3 — Route 2**  
HF should improve transport and crystallization kinetics relative to dry powders. [CS-1, Reasonable Inference] However, the only direct CS evidence is VF3 crystallization, while V5F11 requires lower F:V and mixed-valence control. [CS-1, Target] The uncertainty is phase selectivity rather than crystallization rate. [CS-1, Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V5F11 is likely a **borderline metastable or weakly stable** phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. [Target] The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the existence of crystalline transition-metal fluoride frameworks under sealed, dry, controlled conditions. [CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, TS-5] The most enabling factor is the direct stoichiometric match from **4 VF2 + VF3**, which provides a chemically simple path to the target F:V ratio. [Target, SS-1, Reasonable Inference] The most limiting factor is mixed-valence fluoride phase control, since related AVF3 and VF4 systems show disproportionation, oxidation, or long optimization windows. [CS-3, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1] Overall, synthesis appears feasible but not routine, with sealed-tube solid-state annealing as the best first attempt. [SS-1, CS-5, Reasonable Inference]
