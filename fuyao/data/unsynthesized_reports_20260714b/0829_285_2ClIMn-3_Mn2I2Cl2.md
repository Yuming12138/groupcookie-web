### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

MnICl has an energy above hull of 0.0370 eV/atom, placing it in the high-score ≤0.05 eV/atom bin by the provided rubric. Thermodynamic analogs show experimentally reported compounds with similar or slightly higher hull energies, including TlMnI₃ at 0.017 eV/atom and NaHgCl₃ at 0.055 eV/atom (TS2; TS3). CS analogs include many on-hull or near-hull halide/chalcogenide phases (CS1–CS5), but none directly proves MnICl stability; therefore the remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated metastability corresponds to an accessible low-temperature kinetic basin.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state annealing

The severity is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C temperatures and high pressure, but it requires dry handling and sealed ampoules for volatile/moisture-sensitive halides, as in Mn iodide and halide analog syntheses (SS2; SS4; TS2). Kinetically, binary MnCl₂/MnI₂ should be more reactive than elemental Mn metal [Reasonable Inference], and related Mn iodide chemistry proceeds at 350 °C over weeks (SS2; TS2). The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction from binary salts into a 2D mixed-halide tetrahedral sheet; repeated annealing may be needed because pure Mn iodide analogs required long annealing/powdering (SS2; TS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: direct elemental sealed-tube reaction

The route has comparable condition severity to Route 1 because it still requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile halogens, as in direct elemental iodide synthesis (CS5). Its kinetics are worse because Mn metal is less reactive than binary halide salts [Reasonable Inference], although halogen vapor can assist reaction transport in sealed tubes, as seen in CS3 and CS5. The largest uncertainty is overhalogenation or segregation into MnCl₂/MnI₂ rather than ordered MnICl, because no SS/TS example directly confirms elemental synthesis of mixed Mn–Cl–I sheets.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT / vapor-assisted growth

CVT is more operationally demanding because it needs sealed evacuated ampoules, transport-agent loading, temperature-gradient control, and long growth durations, all seen in layered halide/chalcogenide analogs (CS1; CS2). Kinetically it is attractive because gas-phase transport reduces bulk diffusion limitations and has produced layered single crystals in related systems (CS1; CS2). The major uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS1 reports byproducts requiring separation, and CS2 reports polymorph/stoichiometry sensitivity, so MnICl may require careful Cl/I chemical-potential tuning.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple single-cation composition and corner-sharing tetrahedral connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers is activated. This contrasts with several analogs that have multiple cations, mixed polyhedra, or complex connectivity (SS5; TS1), but those complexities are not present in the target description. Remaining uncertainty is small for amorphization specifically, though crystallinity may still be limited by halide volatility or phase competition.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnICl is likely a **slightly metastable but accessible** phase rather than a strongly unstable one, because its E above hull is only 0.037 eV/atom. The CS data strongly support sealed-tube/CVT methods for layered halide-containing compounds, while SS/TS data specifically support Mn iodide chemistry and low-temperature halide annealing routes (CS1–CS3; SS2; TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, volatile, chemically mobile halide chemistry, which allows synthesis at a few hundred °C rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (SS2; CS2). The most critical limiting factor is **mixed Cl/I stoichiometry control**, since iodine volatility and possible MnCl₂/MnI₂ competing phases could narrow the phase window [Reasonable Inference], analogous to phase-control issues in CS1, CS2, and TS4. Overall, synthesis appears feasible in a well-equipped solid-state/halide chemistry lab.
