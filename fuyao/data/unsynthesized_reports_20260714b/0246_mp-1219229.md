### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold where the rubric assigns a high score, so it is plausibly synthesizable but may be slightly metastable. CS gives strong support because close Sm-containing chalcogenides such as Sm₃Si₂S₈Br and Sm₃CuGeS₇ are on the hull in the supplied data (CS-1; CS-2). SS also supports stability because the closest structural analogues are on or near the hull, including Sm₃Si₂S₈Br at E_hull = 0 and NaSm₃SiS₇ at 0.026 eV/atom (SS-1; SS-2). TS is consistent because thermodynamically similar sulfides have E_hull values around 0.052–0.068 eV/atom and are experimentally accessible (TS-1 to TS-4), but TS also implies these phases can be near the metastability boundary.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because the closest halide-thiosilicate CVT analogues require evacuated sealed quartz ampoules, a 1000→800 °C gradient, and 10 days in a two-zone furnace (CS-1; SS-2). The volatile halogen component is directly evidenced for Br₂ in the analogue (CS-1), while Cl substitution is a **[Reasonable Inference]** for the target. TS provides insufficient evidence for halide CVT conditions, so TS is neutral for this route. The main uncertainty is whether Cl₂/SmCl₃ will reproduce the Br-based transport chemistry without forming excessive SmCl₃ or binary sulfides.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by moving material through vapor or volatile transport species, and the Sm–Si–S–Br analogue gives direct evidence that single crystals can form in 10 days (CS-1; SS-2). The local SiS₄ tetrahedra and Sm–S/halide environments in CS-1/SS-2 closely resemble the target’s SiS₄ and Sm–S/Cl coordination. However, the target stoichiometry Sm₃SiS₆Cl differs from Sm₃Si₂S₈Br, so structural reconstruction is not identical. TS gives insufficient evidence for vapor-transport kinetics in this chemical space and is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C because flux analogues crystallize at 800–850 °C (SS-1; CS-5; TS-1). It still requires evacuated sealed silica ampoules and volatile sulfur-containing mixtures, which are explicitly reported for these flux syntheses (SS-1; CS-5; TS-1). Sm metal and anhydrous chloride/SiS₂ handling add moderate hazard; the air/moisture sensitivity of SmCl₃/SiS₂ is a **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is flux compatibility with the exact Sm₃SiS₆Cl stoichiometry.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because SiS₂ and molten halide fluxes promote formation and transport of thiolosilicate units, as shown by SS-1, CS-5, and TS-1. The use of SmCl₃/NaCl-type fluxes is particularly relevant because the target contains Cl, and SS-1 explicitly includes SmCl₃ among flux agents. Flux synthesis at 800–850 °C indicates diffusion is assisted by a liquid or molten salt medium rather than purely solid-state diffusion (SS-1; TS-1). The uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS explicitly reports Sm₃SiS₆Cl itself, so chloride incorporation rather than merely flux mediation remains to be optimized.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Direct solid-state synthesis is more severe because related compounds require high temperatures around 1050–1150 °C or 1420 K in evacuated sealed tubes (CS-2; CS-3; SS-4; TS-2 to TS-4). Long annealing up to 240 h is reported for rare-earth Cu/Si/S analogues (CS-2; CS-3; SS-4). Volatile sulfur and chloride activity require sealed ampoules; sulfur volatility is supported by the sealed-tube sulfide syntheses, while chloride-specific concerns are a **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS reports impurities and substitution limitations in related solid-state sulfides, supporting a harsher phase-control assessment (TS-2 to TS-4).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are only moderate because elemental rare-earth metals and Si require high-temperature interdiffusion, and the analogues often use long anneals (CS-2; CS-3; SS-4). The 240 h annealing in CS-2/CS-3/SS-4 indicates slow ordering or diffusion. TS-2 to TS-4 show direct reactions can work, but also report impurity sensitivity for related rare-earth sulfides. The uncertainty is whether using SiS₂ and SmCl₃ instead of elemental Si can sufficiently improve precursor activity; that improvement is a **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly proven for the target.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very different cation roles: Sm in high-coordinate sulfide/chloride environments and Si in tetrahedral SiS₄ units, which triggers the cation-radius/coordination mismatch check by fundamental crystal-chemical reasoning. CS and SS analogues nevertheless crystallize well as single crystals, showing that such rare-earth thiolosilicate networks are not intrinsically glass-forming under appropriate ampoule or flux conditions (CS-1; SS-1; SS-2). The unit-cell size and exact sharing topology of the target are not fully established from CS/SS/TS, so those checks are not counted. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the low-symmetry P2₁ chloride phase requires slow ordering that could be bypassed by quenching.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sm₃SiS₆Cl is likely either a very low-energy metastable phase or a near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is favorable: CS and SS provide strong support from close Sm–Si–S–halide and Sm–Si–S framework analogues, while TS shows that phases in the same thermodynamic stability range can be synthesized experimentally (CS-1; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1 to TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the use of halide flux or vapor transport to mobilize Sm/S/Si species and stabilize chloride-containing coordination environments. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, especially avoiding Sm₂S₃, SmCl₃-rich residues, Na/alkali incorporation, or bromide/alkali analogue phases. Overall, the best practical strategy is chloride flux growth around 800–850 °C in evacuated silica ampoules.
