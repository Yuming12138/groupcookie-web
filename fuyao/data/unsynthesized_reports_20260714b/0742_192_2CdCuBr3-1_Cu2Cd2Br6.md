### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which strongly supports synthesizability as a ground-state or near-ground-state phase [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similarly low hull energies are experimentally synthesized by solid-state, CVT, and sintering routes [TS1–TS5]. Chemical analogues include several E_hull = 0 phases, supporting that mixed Cu halides can be thermodynamically accessible [CS1–CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or rule out competing CuBr/CdBr₂ phase separation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to 250–400 °C halide ampoule syntheses [CS1–CS3] and 573 K sealed-tube synthesis [SS4]. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing and volatile halide retention, both common in analogous systems [CS1, CS3, SS4]. Cd toxicity and moisture sensitivity add handling burden [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides strong condition evidence; SS provides moderate condition evidence; TS is less directly relevant to this exact route but supports sealed ampoule halide chemistry [TS3–TS5].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halide precursors should be more reactive than refractory oxides, consistent with low-temperature Cu–metal halide syntheses [CS1–CS3]. Diffusion is likely aided by soft/partially molten halide phases, but cation ordering into Cu trigonal pyramids plus CdBr₆ sheets requires structural rearrangement [Target, SS5]. Long holds in analogous solid-state syntheses imply that ordering may not be instantaneous [CS2, SS4]. TS gives insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Cu–Cd bromides and is treated as neutral 50 for this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses only moderate temperatures, as related halide/chalcogenide-halide systems grow around 340–540 °C [CS4, SS1, TS5]. However, it requires vacuum ampoules, temperature gradients, and volatile halide transport, all of which increase severity [CS4, SS1, TS5]. Toxic Cd bromide handling is an additional non-negotiable constraint [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS all support feasibility of vapor/ampoule methods, but none directly demonstrates CuCdBr₃ transport.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor availability is favorable because many related halide compounds are grown from gas phase or CVT [CS4, SS1, TS2, TS4, TS5]. Crystallization can be direct, but structural analogue SS1 reports low yields and phase-control issues, and TS5 reports polymorph competition [SS1, TS5]. Composition control is the key kinetic/transport uncertainty because CuBr and CdBr₂ may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports CVT in Cu–Br chemistry [CS4], while TS supports low-hull vapor-grown halides [TS2, TS4, TS5].

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal Route 3 avoids vacuum and high furnace temperature, matching analogous concentrated HX syntheses at 220 °C to 670 K [CS5, SS2]. The main deductions are autogenous pressure/sealed HBr handling, multiple steps, and toxic/corrosive precursors [CS5, SS2]. No high-pressure GPa equipment is required in the analogues [CS5, SS2]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Cu–Cd bromide synthesis and is neutral 50 for this route’s condition-specific transferability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution-mediated transport should give fast kinetics relative to dry solid diffusion, as hydrothermal analogues crystallize in 24 h to 5 days [SS2, CS5]. Concentrated HBr can maintain bromide-rich chemistry and suppress hydrolysis, which is inferred from successful HX hydrothermal growth in related systems [SS2, CS5, Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether the anhydrous CuCdBr₃ sheet phase crystallizes instead of hydrated Cd bromides or acid adducts [Reasonable Inference]. SS2 is the strongest positive signal, while CS5 warns of multiphase hydrothermal products.

#### F2 — Route 4

Melt growth is supported by chemically similar halides forming from melts at 250–400 °C [CS1, CS2]. It still requires sealed ampoules or inert/vacuum conditions to retain volatile bromides and prevent hydrolysis/oxidation [CS1, CS2]. Multi-step cooling optimization is likely because CS1 reports quench-sensitive metastability [CS1]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct melt-growth evidence for CuCdBr₃ and are treated as neutral 50 for this route-specific condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 4

Kinetics in a molten bromide medium should be favorable because liquid mixing and low-temperature halide mobility are demonstrated in CS1–CS2. Structural ordering remains uncertain because the target requires a specific 2D arrangement of Cu–Cu and CdBr₆ units [Target]. CS1 shows that cooling rate can determine metastable versus stable framework forms in a related Cu halide [CS1]. Therefore, Route 4 has good kinetic mobility but moderate phase-selection uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains distinct Cu and Cd coordination environments, and the Cu/Cd ionic-size mismatch likely triggers one amorphization/disorder risk criterion [Target, Reasonable Inference]. The structure also combines corner-sharing Cu–Cd connectivity with edge-sharing CdBr₆ octahedra, adding mixed-connectivity complexity [Target]. The unit cell is not indicated to be very large, and layered halide analogues such as CdI₂ are readily crystalline [SS5]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence and are scored neutral 50 for that specific risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuCdBr₃ is likely a near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue set supports synthesis well: chemically similar Cu–metal halides form at low temperatures in sealed ampoules [CS1–CS3], structurally related layered Cd halides exist [SS5], and thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible [TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of soft metal bromides under sealed, bromide-rich, molten, vapor, or hydrothermal conditions [CS1–CS5, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control because volatile bromides, Cu⁺ oxidation, Cd toxicity, and possible competing CuBr/CdBr₂ or solvated phases must be managed [CS1, CS5, SS1, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with the best initial approach being bromide-rich hydrothermal crystallization followed by dry sealed-ampoule annealing confirmation.
