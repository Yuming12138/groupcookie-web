### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.023 eV/atom, which is within the generally synthesizable metastability window and receives a high score by the provided rubric. Structurally/thermodynamically similar Nb₂S₂C phases have E_hull = 0.0168–0.0222 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized by topochemical methods (SS-1/SS-2, TS-1/TS-2), supporting the idea that this energy range is accessible. Chemically similar Zr₂N₂S includes one polymorph at 0.0108 eV/atom and another at 0 eV/atom (CS-2/CS-3), also supporting stability. The remaining uncertainty is that Ti₃N₂S₂ may compete with very stable TiN, TiS₂, TiS, or Ti₂S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: anion metathesis

The route is strongly supported by CS because ZrNCl reacts with Na₂S/K₂S/Rb₂S at 800 °C for 72 h in vacuum to yield layered Zr₂N₂S (CS-2, CS-3). Its condition severity is moderate because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but vacuum sealing, air-sensitive sulfides, and post-reaction washing are required (CS-2/CS-3). Kinetics should be better than direct solid-state sintering because halide-to-sulfide metathesis forms stable ACl salts, driving reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-3]. The main uncertainty is whether TiNCl/TiNBr can transform to the Ti-rich Ti₃N₂S₂ sheet rather than forming TiN + Ti sulfides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: topochemical deintercalation

SS and TS provide strong structural precedent: Nb₂S₂C is made by preparing a Cu-containing layered precursor and extracting Cu without oxidizing the slab (SS-1/SS-2, TS-1/TS-2). The route is conditionally severe because it requires 1200 °C precursor synthesis, repeated treatments, vacuum, and iodine/dry acetonitrile extraction (SS-1/SS-2). Kinetically, the final extraction step is favorable if the correct precursor exists, but making that precursor is likely the limiting step [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for Ti–N–S topochemical extraction specifically, so chemical transferability is less certain than Route 1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: direct solid-state sintering

SS shows Ti₂AlN and Ti₂GaN can be produced by solid-state reactions of nitrides/metals/metalloids in ampoules or crucibles, but the anneals are very long and phase control can be difficult (SS-3, SS-5). TS-5 shows mononitride + metal powder routes can yield ternary nitrides at high temperature under N₂, supporting the general bulk approach. The kinetic score is low because TiN is refractory and the target requires reconstructing a layered Ti–N–S framework from binary solids [Reasonable Inference]. The route is experimentally simple but likely to produce competing TiN/TiSₓ phases unless extensively optimized.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVD/thin film

SS/TS show Nb₄N₅ can be deposited by reacting NbCl₅ vapor with NH₃/H₂ at 950–1000 °C (SS-4, TS-3), which supports vapor delivery of transition-metal nitrides. CS-4 supports high-temperature halide/sulfide vapor transport for a layered sulfide-halide, though not a nitride sulfide. The route has moderate condition severity but difficult gas handling due to NH₃/H₂ and inferred H₂S/TiCl₄ [Reasonable Inference]. Its major uncertainty is composition control: Nb–N CVD itself has difficult phase windows (SS-4/TS-3), and adding sulfur introduces another volatile anion.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: high pressure

CS and TS show high-pressure synthesis is feasible for chemically or thermodynamically related nitrides/chalcogenides, including 3 GPa ZrNCl/HfNCl processing (CS-1), 65 kbar NbPS (CS-5), and up to 18 GPa Zr₃N₄ (TS-4). This route scores poorly in severity because it requires specialized high-pressure equipment and controlled quenching. Kinetics may be helped by pressure and temperature, but reaction volume is small and phase windows can be narrow, as reported for high-pressure nitride phases (TS-4). Because the target E_hull is already low, high pressure is likely a fallback rather than the primary route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so it avoids large cation-radius mismatch; this lowers amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is not indicated to be large, and the formula has only seven atoms, so no large-cell penalty is applied. However, the target explicitly contains mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, triggering one amorphization/defect-risk check. SS/TS layered analogues are crystalline despite related edge/corner-sharing motifs (SS-1/SS-2, TS-1/TS-2), so the risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₃N₂S₂ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.023 eV/atom rather than zero. The CS evidence is especially useful because Zr₂N₂S layered nitride sulfides are made by ZrNCl/A₂S metathesis at accessible temperatures (CS-2, CS-3). The SS/TS evidence supports the structural plausibility of layered transition-metal anion sheets and shows that topochemical methods can preserve such slabs (SS-1/SS-2, TS-1/TS-2). The key enabling factor is the low hull energy combined with layered nitride/sulfide chemical analogues. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: TiN and Ti sulfides are stable, refractory competitors, and the exact Ti₃N₂S₂ stoichiometry is not directly demonstrated in the references [Reasonable Inference].
