### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0450 eV/atom, placing it just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. The closest SnO analogue is experimentally synthesized even at E_hull = 0.1254 eV/atom, showing that metastable SnO polymorphs can be isolated under kinetic control (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Thermodynamic analogues include experimentally reported phases near the target instability, such as P₄SeO₇ at E_hull ≈ 0.0499 eV/atom (TS3). The main uncertainty is that the target is probably metastable rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses very mild temperature, ambient pressure, and standard solution equipment, as shown by the 95 °C precipitation synthesis of red SnO (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The main deductions are for multiple wet-chemical steps and corrosive/volatile reagents such as HCl and NH₃ (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). There is no evidence from CS1 / SS1 / TS1 that high pressure, high temperature, or specialized atmosphere is needed. The remaining uncertainty is whether the exact Pmc2₁ stacking can be selected rather than the reported Cmc2₁ red SnO.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the reference SnO forms from solution at only 95 °C over 5 d, indicating high precursor activity and no need for long-range refractory solid diffusion (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Structural reconstruction is modest because the target and analogue are both layered orthorhombic SnO, but not identical in space group or sheet count (CS1 / SS1 / TS1; target structure). The narrow pH and metastability window lowers confidence, since CS1 / SS1 / TS1 report pressure and pH sensitivity. Overall, kinetic access is good but phase selectivity is the key uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits mild sealed-tube conditions from Sn₂OF₂ formation at 200 °C for 24 h (SS2). It requires controlled oxygen activity and sealed-tube handling, which adds condition severity (SS2; [Reasonable Inference]). It avoids high temperature and high pressure, and the precursor is already SnO, so severity remains moderate. The main uncertainty is that SS2 demonstrates Sn(II) oxyfluoride formation, not direct SnO polymorph conversion.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are plausible because the composition is unchanged and only polymorph rearrangement is targeted [Reasonable Inference from target and CS1 / SS1 / TS1]. The 200 °C sealed reaction of SnO with SnF₂ shows Sn(II)-oxide units can rearrange at low temperature (SS2). However, a dry solid-state polymorph transition may be slower than solution precipitation, and insufficient evidence from SS2 proves formation of Pmc2₁ SnO specifically. This route is best viewed as a post-synthesis tuning strategy.

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route avoids extreme temperature if adapted to SnO, but it likely requires vacuum/inert sealing, dehydration, slow cooling, and flux washing, as in Sn₂TiO₄ growth (SS4). SnCl₂ volatility/hygroscopicity and Sn(II) redox sensitivity make the route more severe [Reasonable Inference], and SS4 uses sealed quartz tubes in vacuo. CS3 supports flux oxide growth but uses nitrate flux, which may be chemically mismatched for Sn(II). Thus, condition severity is higher than for Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth can provide high precursor mobility and good crystallinity, as shown by Sn₂TiO₄ single-crystal growth with KCl flux (SS4) and PdO formation in molten NaNO₃ (CS3). Liquid-mediated transport greatly reduces diffusion limitations compared with solid-state reactions (SS4 / CS3). The uncertainty is phase selectivity: for binary SnO, oxygen potential must be narrow to avoid Sn metal or SnO₂ [Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically strong but chemically delicate.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film deposition operates at moderate substrate temperatures, with CS2 reporting 350–400 °C and 10⁻⁵ Torr. The main severity penalty is vacuum/controlled oxygen atmosphere (CS2). It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, and no toxic precursor is inherently required [Reasonable Inference]. However, specialized deposition equipment is needed, as CS2 used a vacuum coater and substrate heater.

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor deposition can access metastable oxides, as demonstrated by CS2, but CS2 also reports simultaneous metallic particles and multiple oxide phases. For SnO, oxygen chemical potential must be tightly controlled to avoid Sn or SnO₂ [Reasonable Inference]. Epitaxial stabilization may help select the Pmc2₁ polymorph [Reasonable Inference], but there is insufficient evidence from CS2 or TS5 that this exact SnO polymorph crystallizes directly. Thus, kinetic feasibility is moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Sn, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger (target structure). The target structure is simple and layered, not a >40-atom complex mixed-cation framework, and no mixed edge/vertex/face-sharing polyhedral coexistence is described (target structure). The closest SnO analogue is crystalline and layered, supporting low amorphization tendency (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The remaining uncertainty is not amorphization but polymorph competition.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Pmc2₁ SnO is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a fully robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0450 eV/atom and the closest synthesized SnO analogue is itself metastable and phase-sensitive (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The CS, SS, and TS data strongly support synthesis of layered orthorhombic SnO chemistry, especially through low-temperature precipitation, but they do not directly prove the exact Pmc2₁ stacking. The most critical enabling factor is the very mild Sn(II) solution chemistry that can crystallize SnO at 95 °C without high-temperature equilibration (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among SnO polymorphs and oxidation/disproportionation toward Sn/SnO₂ [Reasonable Inference], compounded by the reported pH and pressure sensitivity of red SnO (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Overall, the material is quite synthesizable if kinetic control is maintained.
