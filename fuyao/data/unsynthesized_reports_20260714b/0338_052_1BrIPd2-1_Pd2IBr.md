### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom, which is within the high-probability ≤0.05 eV/atom window specified in the rubric. CS analogues include both on-hull Te₃(PdBr)₄ (CS1) and experimentally reported phases at ~0.043–0.061 eV/atom (CS4/CS5), indicating that small positive hull energies do not preclude synthesis. TS analogues are especially supportive because several experimentally synthesized compounds have E_hull values similar to the target, such as CuHgSBr at 0.0309 eV/atom and CuHgSCl at 0.0238 eV/atom (TS1/TS2). Remaining uncertainty is that Pd₂IBr is not directly reported, so the hull value supports metastability but does not guarantee kinetic accessibility.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is moderate severity because the closest analogue Te₃(PdBr)₄ forms at 700 K in sealed quartz under reduced Ar/vacuum-like conditions (CS1/SS1), and related halide ampoule syntheses occur at moderate temperatures in evacuated tubes (TS2/TS3). The main severity deductions are the sealed/vacuum atmosphere and volatile halide component control, supported by SS2 showing Br₂ transport in closed ampoules and TS4 using sealed Br₂-containing ampoules. The route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C treatment, unlike some high-temperature chalcogenide analogues in CS2/CS3. Uncertainty remains in whether PdI₂ reacts cleanly without segregating binary iodide.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are limited by Pd metal diffusion, a [Reasonable Inference], but halide precursors should improve reactivity because PdBr₂ is directly used in CS1/SS1. The 2D Pd–Br structural motif in CS1/SS1 reduces the reconstruction penalty, but target Pd₂IBr requires forming Pd–Pd bonds and ordered I/Br sublattices not explicitly shown in any source. CS2/CS3/CS5 and TS3/TS5 show that related solid-state products often require long heating, regrinding, or annealing, supporting a moderate rather than easy kinetics score. Insufficient evidence from TS for exact Pd–I–Br diffusion kinetics, so TS is neutral **50** on that sub-aspect.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 has lower condition score because SS2 uses Br₂ in a closed quartz ampoule at 700 °C with an imposed temperature gradient, which requires careful sealed-tube handling. Toxic/volatile halogen transport agents are a major penalty, and use of I₂/IBr for mixed-halide transport is a [Reasonable Inference] based on SS2 and SS4. It does not require GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. CS provides insufficient evidence for CVT growth of chemically similar Pd₂IBr-like compounds, so CS is neutral **50** for the route-severity transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Vapor transport can overcome bulk diffusion barriers because PdBr₂ is demonstrably recrystallized by CVT in SS2. However, composition control is likely difficult because the target contains both I and Br, and CS1/SS1 shows phase sensitivity to stoichiometric deviations while TS4 reports multiphase Pd–Se–Br products. The crystallization pathway is therefore plausible for crystals but not robust for phase-pure bulk. Insufficient direct evidence from TS for mixed I/Br CVT, so TS is neutral **50** on the vapor-transport mechanistic detail except for the cautionary multiphase signal from TS4.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 avoids high temperature and GPa pressure because related halide materials are synthesized at 180–420 °C or 670 K in SS4, SS5/TS1, and TS4. Its severity comes from sealed autogenous pressure, corrosive halide acids/halogens, and multi-step handling/cooling/washing, all reflected in SS4/SS5/TS1/TS4. SS4 and TS4 specifically require thick-walled ampoules, steel autoclaves, or counterpressure, making the equipment more demanding than Route 1. CS gives insufficient evidence for solvothermal synthesis of chemically similar Pd–I–Br phases, so CS is neutral **50** for Route 3 severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solvothermal conditions improve precursor activity and mass transport because hydroiodic acid yields AuTeI crystals (SS3), HBr medium yields CuHgSBr crystals (SS5/TS1), and Br₂/CCl₄ solvothermal chemistry forms Pd–Se–Br products (TS4). This route should reduce diffusion limitations relative to dry Pd metal reactions, but Pd activation and reduction state control remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is plausible for low-dimensional halide phases, but TS4 shows Pd–halogen solvothermal chemistry can produce multiphase mixtures requiring manual crystal separation. The score is therefore the best of the three routes but still not “easy.”

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation element, Pd, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from the target composition]. The structure is a simple Pd₂IBr layered formula in trigonal P3m1 rather than a large complex unit cell, and the provided target description does not indicate coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. CS1/SS1, SS2, and SS3/SS4 show analogous low-dimensional halide compounds crystallize as ordered sheets/ribbons rather than amorphous phases. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically on amorphization, so TS is neutral **50** for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pd₂IBr is most likely a near-ground-state metastable phase rather than a clearly stable ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogue set supports synthesis moderately well: CS1/SS1 is a strong precedent for a 2D Pd–Br-containing sheet made by sealed-tube reaction, while SS3/SS4/SS5/TS1/TS4 support halide-rich hydrothermal or solvothermal crystallization. The most critical enabling factor is the low target hull energy combined with known stability of low-dimensional Pd/noble-metal halide frameworks in CS1/SS1 and SS2–SS4. The most critical limiting factor is mixed I/Br chemical-potential and site-order control, because CS1/SS1 reports by-products from stoichiometry deviations and TS4 reports multiphase products in Pd–halogen solvothermal chemistry. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require screening of halogen activity and cooling/annealing conditions.
