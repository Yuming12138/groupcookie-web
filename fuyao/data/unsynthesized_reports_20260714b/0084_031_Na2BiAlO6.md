### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the edge of the highest stability scoring bin and indicates a plausible ground-state or near-ground-state phase. TS provides the strongest support because TS-1, TS-4, and TS-5 are experimentally synthesized analogues with E_hull values around 0.056–0.084 eV/atom. CS also supports oxide synthesizability because several chemically similar complex oxides are stable or near-stable, but CS analogues are mostly perovskite-like Os/Bi oxides rather than Na–Al–Bi peroxides. SS gives mixed thermodynamic evidence because structurally similar peroxide compounds exist but some have significantly higher E_hull values.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses mild-to-moderate temperature solution/hydrothermal conditions, supported by SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, and TS-2. It avoids >1000 °C firing, high pressure above 5 GPa, and vacuum/H₂/NH₃ atmospheres. The main deduction is procedural complexity because preparation, hydrothermal treatment, washing, and drying are multiple steps. Remaining uncertainty is whether Bi and Al dissolve or recrystallize sufficiently under the same alkaline peroxide conditions.

#### F3: Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because solution and hydrothermal media reduce cation diffusion distances, as shown by TS-2 hydrothermal single-crystal growth and SS-4 alkaline peroxide crystallization. SS-1, SS-2, and SS-4 show that peroxide O–O frameworks can form under low-temperature conditions. The structural reconstruction penalty remains because no source demonstrates direct formation of Na₂AlBiO₆ or a close Na–Al–Bi peroxide analogue. The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the triclinic P1 target instead of simpler Na–Al or Na–Bi oxides.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids the high temperatures used in CS-1 to CS-5 and TS-5, but it requires sealed peroxide/oxygen control. CS sealed-tube syntheses show that controlled oxygen sources such as Ag₂O or PbO₂ are common for complex oxides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). The route is penalized for sealed/reactive atmosphere, volatile/pressure-sensitive sodium peroxide chemistry, multistep reheating, and air-sensitive oxidizing precursors. The uncertainty is high because peroxide units may decompose during even moderate solid-state heating.

#### F3: Route 2 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are weaker than solution kinetics because Al and Bi must interdiffuse through a solid sodium oxide/peroxide matrix. CS and TS solid-state analogues often require 20–48 h, repeated grinding, or quenching to control phases (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5, TS-5). Use of Na₂O₂ and Al hydroxide improves precursor activity, but structural reconstruction into a low-symmetry peroxide framework is not strongly supported by CS/TS ceramic analogues. SS evidence suggests peroxide materials are better accessed by low-temperature solution routes.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 operates below 1000 °C and benefits from flux-assisted crystallization, supported by TS-1 and TS-4. It is still penalized for sealed sodium peroxide/NaOH flux handling, multistep processing, and moisture-sensitive oxidizing precursors. SS-4 supports alkaline peroxide crystallization, but SS-4 is solution cooling rather than molten sodium peroxide flux. The remaining uncertainty is containment/corrosion control and avoidance of flux-derived impurity phases.

#### F3: Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth should provide strong mass transport, analogous to TS-4 Bi₂O₃ flux growth and TS-1 sealed flux/melt growth. It is kinetically more favorable than dry solid-state synthesis because dissolution/reprecipitation can bypass refractory Al₂O₃ diffusion. SS-4 supports peroxide complex formation in alkaline peroxide media, which is relevant to retaining O–O units. The kinetic limitation is phase selection during cooling, since Na–Al–Bi peroxide topology is not directly shown in the sources.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target combines cations with very different size and bonding preferences, which increases disorder/amorphization risk by [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS, SS, and TS analogues show many crystalline complex oxides and peroxide-containing materials can form despite mixed coordination environments, including Na₂TeO₅-like, K₃TaO₈-like, and Na₃Cu₂SbO₆-like structures (SS-1, SS-4, TS-5). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has a >40 atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂AlBiO₆ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues with comparable E_hull values are experimentally accessible. The strongest synthesis support comes from SS peroxide/oxo-peroxide analogues with O–O distances around 1.50 Å and from TS hydrothermal/flux examples showing that metastable complex oxides can crystallize under moderate conditions. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature peroxide chemistry, because high-temperature oxide sintering risks destroying the target’s peroxide-like O–O units. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among Na–Al, Na–Bi, Bi oxide, and peroxide decomposition products. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, but the best chance is a CO₂-free alkaline peroxide hydrothermal route rather than conventional high-temperature ceramic synthesis.
