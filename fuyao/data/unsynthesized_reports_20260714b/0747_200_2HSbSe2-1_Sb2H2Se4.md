### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied data. TS strongly supports that stable Bi/Sb–Se-related chalcogenides are experimentally accessible: TS4 Cs(SbSe₂)₂, TS3 K₂Bi₈Se₁₃, TS2 Bi₂Se₃, and TS5 MnBi₂Se₄ all have E_hull = 0. SS also supports accessible low-dimensional Bi–Se frameworks, although several are slightly above hull, such as SS1 Bi₃Se₄Br at 0.0276 eV/atom. CS is chemically hydrogen-rich but not Sb–Se-specific, so it provides insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence and is treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 scores well because TS4 demonstrates Sb–Se hydrothermal crystallization at only 115 °C in a sealed ampoule, avoiding high temperature and high pressure. The main penalties arise from the likely need for volatile/toxic H–Se or hydroselenide chemistry, which is a physicochemical inference from the target H–Se bond rather than direct CS/SS/TS evidence. CS gives insufficient direct support for hydrothermal Sb–Se–H synthesis and is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal chemistry should provide good kinetics through dissolution/reprecipitation rather than solid-state diffusion, consistent with TS4 producing large crystals at low temperature. The target still requires partial reconstruction from Sb₂Se₃ into a 1D SbHSe₂ ribbon, so structural reconstruction is not trivial. SS gives structural support through related square-pyramidal Bi/Sb–Se motifs, but no direct protonated analogue, leaving uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized because SS1/SS3 sealed-tube methods require evacuated ampoules, controlled thermal profiles, and volatile chalcogen/halide transport. Adding H–Se chemistry increases the sealed-tube hazard and decomposition risk **[Reasonable Inference]**. However, the temperatures can remain below 1000 °C, as SS1 and SS3 show chalcogenide growth at 623–823 K and 790 K.

#### F3 — Route 2

Sealed-tube kinetics are moderate: SS1 and SS3 demonstrate that vapor transport or melt cooling can crystallize low-dimensional Bi–Se/halide frameworks, but dense Sb₂Se₃ plus elemental Se is less reactive than hydrothermal precursors. The target’s H–Se bond is the main kinetic/chemical uncertainty because it may not survive the temperatures used for SS1/SS3. CS provides insufficient direct signal for Sb–Se–H vapor-assisted crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits demanding handling from TS1/TS3: glovebox loading, air-sensitive K₂Se/K₂Se₃, evacuated sealed tubes, long heating, washing, and then an additional proton-exchange step. These are not high-pressure or >1000 °C conditions, but the sequence is multi-step and chemically sensitive. TS1/TS3 directly support the flux portion, while proton exchange is a reasonable inference.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux synthesis has favorable kinetics because TS1 and TS3 show complex selenide frameworks forming at 290–540 °C through liquid/flux-mediated transport. If an alkali antimony selenide framework closely related to the target can be made, proton exchange could preserve Sb–Se connectivity **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is whether the alkali precursor has the correct topology and whether acid treatment dissolves rather than protonates it.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is most severe because SS2 requires 5.5 GPa and up to 1200 °C in specialized cubic-anvil equipment. The target’s H–Se component would require sealed containment and adds toxic/volatile risk **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS and CS provide insufficient direct evidence that high pressure benefits SbHSe₂ specifically, so their route-specific support is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure and high temperature can give fast diffusion and crystallization, as SS2 reports 10–30 min high-pressure melt growth. However, the required structural reconstruction is large and H retention is uncertain. Because the target is already on the hull, high pressure may be unnecessary and could destabilize the Se–H bonding **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Sb, so cation-size disorder is not expected. The target description is a relatively simple 1D edge-sharing SbSe₅ ribbon with H bound to Se; it does not describe mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral networks. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for SbHSe₂, so source-specific direct evidence is insufficient and neutral, but the structural simplicity supports low amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SbHSe₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The best support comes from TS4, which directly shows low-temperature hydrothermal formation of an Sb–Se framework from Sb₂Se₃, and from SS1/SS3/SS5, which show that low-dimensional Bi/Sb-like selenide frameworks are crystallizable. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution or flux transport, because it can form Sb–Se frameworks while preserving H–Se bonding. The most critical limiting factor is hydrogen/selenium chemical potential control: H–Se species are volatile, toxic, redox-sensitive, and not directly represented in the analogues. Overall, the target appears synthesizable, but the best route is likely hydrothermal rather than high-temperature sealed-tube synthesis.
