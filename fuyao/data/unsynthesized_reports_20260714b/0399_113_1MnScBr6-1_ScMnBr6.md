### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

ScMnBr₆ is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability class. [Target] CS includes TiBr₃ and Zr(InBr₃)₂ with E_hull = 0, showing chemically related bromides can be stable ground-state phases. [CS1, CS5] SS/TS include several E_hull = 0 halide analogues, reinforcing that related halide frameworks are not necessarily metastable. [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or moisture stability. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because CS TiBr₃ forms at only 500–550 °C and NbBr₅-related ampoule chemistry occurs at 370 °C. [CS1, CS4] The score is reduced because vacuum/sealed systems and volatile bromine/air-sensitive bromides are likely required. [CS1, CS4, SS5, TS5] The main uncertainty is whether ScBr₃ and Mn bromides react cleanly before forming binary bromide side phases. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderately favorable because reactive bromides and Br₂ are more mobile than refractory oxides. [CS1] Structural reconstruction is favorable because TiBr₃ is a 2D edge-sharing octahedral bromide sheet similar to the target’s edge-sharing ScBr₆/MnBr₆ sheet. [CS1, Target] The penalty comes from possible slow Sc/Mn ordering and oxidation-state control for Mn(III). [Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT conditions in analogues are moderate in temperature, typically 200–475 °C, and do not require >5 GPa pressure. [CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] Deductions apply because the process uses vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile halides, and air/moisture-sensitive precursors. [CS2, SS2, TS2] The route is operationally standard for halide synthesis but still requires sealed-ampoule competence. [CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT has the best kinetic score because vapor transport reduces solid-state diffusion limitations and supports crystal growth at low-to-moderate temperature. [CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] Structurally similar halide systems crystallize by CVT, but they are not exact ScMnBr₆ analogues. [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] The main uncertainty is whether Sc and Mn transport rates can be matched well enough to preserve 1:1 cation stoichiometry. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures, as Zr(InBr₃)₂ forms at 475 °C and Ti(AlBr₄)₂ forms at 200–250 °C. [CS5, CS2] The route still requires vacuum sealing, air-free handling, and volatile Lewis-acid bromides. [CS2, CS5] No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty applies. [CS2, CS5]

#### F3 — Route 3

Molten AlBr₃/InBr₃ flux should enhance reaction kinetics by increasing ion mobility. [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS5] Zr(InBr₃)₂ demonstrates that oxidation and crystallization in a bromide melt can yield crystalline halide products. [CS5] The main kinetic/phase-control risk is flux incorporation or formation of complex bromometallate salts rather than ScMnBr₆. [CS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 4

The pressurized Br₂ route is more severe because SS/TS TlFeBr₄ uses an evacuated quartz ampoule at about 200 bar inside an autoclave. [SS5, TS5] The formal rubric does not penalize 200 bar as >5 GPa, but practical safety and multi-step containment reduce the score. [SS5, TS5] Br₂ toxicity and volatility are major penalties. [SS5, TS5]

#### F3 — Route 4

Br₂ pressure can provide high bromine chemical potential and rapid bromination. [SS5, TS5] However, the structural match of TlFeBr₄ to ScMnBr₆ is weaker than the TiBr₃ and CVT halide analogues. [SS5, TS5, CS1] This route is therefore plausible but not kinetically or operationally preferred. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple edge-sharing octahedral sheet rather than a mixed-connectivity framework, which lowers amorphization risk. [Target] The likely octahedral Sc³⁺/Mn³⁺ ionic-radius mismatch is not clearly above 15% under high-spin Mn³⁺ assumptions. [Reasonable Inference] There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered. [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScMnBr₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. [Target] The synthesis analogues support feasibility mainly through low-to-moderate-temperature sealed halide chemistry, including TiBr₃ solid-state bromination, Ti(AlBr₄)₂ CVT, and Zr(InBr₃)₂ flux growth. [CS1, CS2, CS5] Structurally and thermodynamically similar halides are frequently grown by CVT in evacuated ampoules, making sealed gradient growth the best-supported route. [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] The key enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of bromide/Lewis-acid bromide media, which lowers diffusion barriers. [CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3] The key limiting factor is maintaining the correct Mn oxidation state and Sc:Mn ordering without forming binary bromides or complex bromometallates. [Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Sc–Mn phase control]
