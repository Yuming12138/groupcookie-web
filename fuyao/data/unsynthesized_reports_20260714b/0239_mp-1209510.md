### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the high-synthesizability range and supports a score of 90 [Target]. Chemically similar arsenides are often calculated on the hull and have reported syntheses, including Rb₂SnAs₂, Rb₂SiAs₂, K₂NiAs₂, Cs₂PdAs₂, and Cs₂PdSb₂ [CS1–CS5]. Thermodynamic analogues at comparable hull energies, such as Na₃As at 0.0425 eV/atom and CsMnAs/KMnAs at ~0.055 eV/atom, are synthesizable [TS1–TS3]. SS evidence is less decisive because some structurally similar compounds are high-energy/metastable or require specialized methods [SS1, SS3, SS5].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires sealed vacuum or inert ampoule conditions, which is consistent with Rb₂SiAs₂ and K₂NiAs₂ syntheses [CS4, CS2]. The main severity penalties arise from vacuum/sealing, volatile As/Rb, and air-sensitive Rb/Ca plus toxic As [CS4, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature severity penalty is applied [CS2, CS4, SS2]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ca reacts with the ampoule or forms stable competing Ca–As binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by reactive Rb and moderate reaction temperatures comparable to successful arsenide analogues [CS1, CS4, SS2]. Ca has a relatively low melting point compared with refractory transition metals, so long-range diffusion should not be severe [Reasonable Inference]. However, elemental reaction requires full structural reconstruction into a P4/mmm zero-dimensional phase that is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Target; insufficient direct structural evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate-high rather than very high.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids external high pressure and ultra-high temperatures, matching the molten-mixture synthesis of Rb₂SnAs₂ and related phases [CS1]. The major penalties are volatile Rb/As and air-sensitive/toxic starting materials [CS1, CS4, TS2]. Ar atmosphere itself is not penalized as reactive in the provided F2 table, unlike H₂/NH₃/vacuum. The route is operationally moderate because alkali removal and sealed handling are required [CS1, SS2].

#### F3 — Route 2

Rb-rich melt/flux conditions should strongly improve transport and crystallization, as molten mixtures yielded Rb₂SnAs₂ and related A₂MAs₂ phases [CS1]. Slow cooling and controlled heating rates are known to produce crystals in related systems [CS1, CS3]. The structural mismatch between target P4/mmm clusters and orthorhombic CS analogues prevents assigning a near-perfect score [CS1, CS4, Target]. The main uncertainty is whether the target is the primary crystallizing phase or a narrow-window metastable product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The binary-precursor route is more step-intensive because it requires making Rb–As and Ca–As precursors before ternary annealing [Reasonable Inference]. This added complexity is analogous to K₄ZnAs₂, where preformed KZnAs and K₃As were needed to obtain pure phase [SS4]. The temperature and pressure requirements remain moderate and below 1000 °C [SS4]. Air sensitivity and arsenide toxicity remain significant [SS4, TS2].

#### F3 — Route 3

Preformed arsenides should reduce As volatility and improve local chemical matching, supported by the K₄ZnAs₂ precursor strategy [SS4]. Diffusion should be reasonable at 600–800 °C because related K₄ZnAs₂ forms at 923 K and K₄CdAs₂ at 973 K [SS4, SS2]. Structural reconstruction is only partial, not complete, because binary arsenides do not share the exact target structure [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is the availability and purity of Rb₃As/RbAs and CaAs/Ca₃As₂ precursors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a strong Rb/Ca cation size mismatch, so one amorphization-risk criterion is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. However, the reported target structure is a small zero-dimensional cluster, not a >40-atom complex unit cell [Target]. It also lacks mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, unlike some SS analogues with tetrahedral or polyhedral networks [SS2, SS4, Target]. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂CaAs₂ is best viewed as a marginally metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the usual metastability threshold [Target]. Chemical analogues strongly support synthesis of alkali metal ternary arsenides by direct or melt-based methods, especially Rb₂SnAs₂ and Rb₂SiAs₂ [CS1, CS4]. Thermodynamic analogues also show that compounds around 0.04–0.06 eV/atom can be made under standard sealed/inert solid-state conditions [TS1–TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a Rb-rich melt or sealed alkali flux to overcome diffusion limits and compensate volatile Rb/As loss [CS1, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Rb–As, Ca–As, and possible Rb–Ca intermetallic/arsenide phases, especially because no direct Ca analogue is provided in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].
