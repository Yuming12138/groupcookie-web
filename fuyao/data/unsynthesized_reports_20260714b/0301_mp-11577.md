### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, which falls at the upper edge of the rubric’s 90–100 band [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values from about 0.025 to 0.112 eV/atom are reported as synthesized, including CaZn2-related high-pressure phases and Ca3Hg [TS1, TS4, TS5]. CS analogues are mostly stable intermetallics and support general binary alloy formation [CS1–CS5], but they do not prove this exact Fm-3m YbZn polymorph [insufficient evidence from CS]. SS analogues include Yb intermetallics, some requiring high pressure, so the remaining uncertainty is whether rock-salt YbZn is an ambient metastable phase or a pressure-stabilized phase [SS1, SS2].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses vacuum sealed ampoules, volatile Zn, and air-sensitive Yb [CS1, CS2, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, Zn-mediated vapor/liquid transport should improve reaction rates, and related intermetallics form at 400–800 °C [CS1, CS2, SS5]. The main kinetic penalty is structural selection: no analogue confirms rock-salt YbZn, and repeated reheating is sometimes needed for related compounds [CS1, CS2; insufficient evidence from TS]. This route is therefore the best compromise between accessible conditions and stoichiometry control.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Arc/induction melting is well supported for rare-earth intermetallics [CS3–CS5, SS5], but condition severity is increased by high local temperature, remelting, sealed annealing, and volatile Zn/Yb loss [CS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because melting homogenizes the binary alloy rapidly [CS3–CS5]. The uncertainty is whether the target rock-salt polymorph survives cooling or transforms to a competing ambient structure [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for Yb–Co/Ni intermetallics and CaZn2 polymorphs at 7–10 GPa [SS1, SS2, TS5]. The route is severe due to specialized multianvil conditions, capsule assembly, and quenching [SS1, SS2, TS4, TS5]. Kinetically, short high-P/T holds may be adequate for low-melting Zn alloys [TS5], but phase control is difficult because high-pressure products can be metastable and narrow-window [SS1, SS2, TS5]. This route is most appropriate if ambient-pressure attempts fail.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Thin-film synthesis has no direct support from the supplied CS/SS/TS data, so all thin-film claims are extrapolated [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]. Its advantage is kinetic trapping and possible epitaxial stabilization of a metastable Fm-3m phase [Reasonable Inference]. Its main drawbacks are Yb oxidation, Zn volatility, and precise 1:1 flux control [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore limited despite moderate nominal temperatures.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple rock-salt Fm-3m structure with a small atom count, which lowers amorphization risk [Target]. The only trigger is the large Yb/Zn size mismatch, inferred from metallic radii [Reasonable Inference]. The target polyhedra are edge- and corner-sharing octahedra, not edge/corner/face sharing all together, so the connectivity trigger is not counted [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is that size mismatch may instead favor alternative intermetallic structure types rather than amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YbZn in the specified rock-salt structure is likely **marginally metastable rather than a robust ground-state phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS data support direct rare-earth intermetallic synthesis from elements under vacuum or Ar, especially for Zn-containing or Yb-containing alloys [CS1, CS2, CS3]. The SS and TS data suggest that high pressure can stabilize unusual Yb or Zn intermetallic phases, but they do not prove that rock-salt YbZn is accessible at ambient pressure [SS1, SS2, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature reactivity and transport enabled by metallic Zn and sealed-ampoule conditions [CS1, SS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition/stoichiometry control caused by Zn volatility and the absence of a directly demonstrated rock-salt YbZn analogue [CS1, CS3, SS5; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target polymorph selectivity].
