### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. This is supported by thermodynamic analogues: FeRh₂Se₄ at 0.045 eV/atom is synthesized by direct solid-state reaction [TS2], and Cu₃SbSe₃ at 0.0346 eV/atom is phase-pure after annealing [TS1]. Chemical analogues also show that a slightly higher-energy chalcohalide, CuClCu₂TeS₃ at 0.066 eV/atom, is experimentally accessible [CS3]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is quinary and compositionally more complex than most analogues, which may increase competing-phase density despite favorable E_hull [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated ampoule synthesis, which is strongly supported by CS and SS/TS data [CS1, CS3, SS1, TS2]. The severity score is reduced mainly because vacuum sealing and volatile Se/Te/Br retention are necessary, as in CuClCu₂TeS₃ and CuSe₂Br [CS3, SS4]. Multi-step grinding/annealing is likely needed because structurally similar CrAuTe₄ and thermodynamic analogue FeRh₂Se₄ required repeated annealing or multiple firings [SS1, TS2]. CS provides useful condition evidence; SS provides structural-process evidence; TS provides kinetics/process evidence.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Co and Ni are dense metal precursors, so their solid-state reaction kinetics are slower than solution or flux routes [Reasonable Inference], although chalcogen melts can aid transport as seen in chalcohalide/telluride syntheses [CS3, SS1]. The structural reconstruction burden is moderate because the target’s edge-sharing octahedral layer resembles CrAuTe₄ [SS1]. However, Co/Ni ordering over two metal sites and mixed Te/Se/Br anions likely require long annealing, consistent with FeRh₂Se₄ and CrAuTe₄ requiring multiple firings/anneals [TS2, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 avoids very high temperatures because related flux/melt systems form at 180–550 °C [SS2, SS4, TS3]. It still requires evacuated ampoules and careful volatile/halide handling; CuSe₂Br explicitly requires evacuated quartz ampoules and fast cooling [SS4], while Pd selenium-halides require closed ampoules/autoclave [TS3]. Toxic/air-sensitive reagents such as alkali chalcogenides or selenium/tellurium bromides make the practical severity comparable to Route 1 [SS2, TS3; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives less direct flux evidence, so CS is neutral-to-weak for this route; SS and TS provide the main support.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux or reactive-melt synthesis offers the best kinetic pathway because mass transport occurs through a liquid phase, as shown by AuCuSe₄ growth in K₂Se/Se flux [SS2] and Pd selenium-halide formation in Se/SeX₄ melt [TS3]. CuSe₂Br shows that bromide–selenium melts can produce layered chalcohalides at 550 °C [SS4]. The main kinetic uncertainty is phase selection: AuCuSe₄ flux growth produced impurity phases [SS2], and Pd selenium-halide products required careful impurity control [TS3]. Thus F3 is highest for Route 2 but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Solvothermal synthesis uses the lowest nominal temperature, supported by Pd selenium-halides at 180 °C [TS3] and AuTe₂I hydrothermal synthesis in hydrohalic acid [SS5]. The score is reduced because autogenous pressure, corrosive HBr/bromide media, and toxic selenium species create practical hazards [TS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS that Co/Ni telluroselenide bromide sheets form solvothermally; CS2 only shows solution diffusion for Cu–As–S–Cl using specialized organometallic precursors [CS2]. Therefore, severity is moderate but uncertainty remains high.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solvothermal media can activate chalcogen/halide chemistry at low temperature, which should help volatile Br and Se retention [TS3, SS5]. However, direct crystallization of the target’s edge-sharing Co/Ni octahedral sheet is not demonstrated by the provided solvothermal analogues; SS5 is Au–Te–I, and TS3 is Pd selenium-halide ribbons rather than Co/Ni layers [SS5, TS3]. CS2 suggests solution routes can be precursor-sensitive and phase-control-limited [CS2]. Thus kinetics are plausible but less predictable than flux growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains similar-sized Co/Ni cations in octahedral environments, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure uses edge-sharing octahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, and the closest structural analogue CrAuTe₄ also uses edge-sharing octahedra [SS1]. There is insufficient explicit unit-cell atom-count information from CS/SS/TS, but the target formula and description do not imply a >40-atom complex cell, so the amorphization risk is rated low. The main uncertainty is possible anion disorder among Te/Se/Br, which could broaden diffraction but does not necessarily imply amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoNiTe₂SeBr is likely a **mildly metastable or near-ground-state phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom, very close to the high-stability cutoff and comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues [TS1, TS2]. The CS data support sealed-tube chalcohalide synthesis using moderate temperatures and slow thermal profiles [CS1, CS3, CS4], while the SS data strongly support the structural motif through edge-sharing octahedral tellurides such as CrAuTe₄ [SS1]. The most critical enabling factor is a sealed, halogen-retaining synthesis environment with liquid chalcogen/halide transport, as seen in CuSe₂Br, AuCuSe₄, and Pd selenium-halides [SS2, SS4, TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in a quinary Co–Ni–Te–Se–Br system, especially because flux analogues report impurity phases and cooling sensitivity [SS2, SS4, TS3]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires careful screening of Br activity, chalcogen excess, and cooling/annealing schedule.
