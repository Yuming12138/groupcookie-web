### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0390 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability scoring bin given in the prompt. TS is especially supportive because thermodynamically similar halides with E_hull values around 0.028–0.062 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable by CVT, aqueous crystallization, or direct halogen reaction (TS2–TS4). CS is mixed: several chemically similar halides are on the hull (CS1, CS3, CS5), but a Cd chloride analogue is much higher at 0.256 eV/atom (CS4), so CS gives moderate support rather than unequivocal support. SS gives moderate support because structurally similar 2D chloride phases include stable compounds and a slightly metastable 0.055 eV/atom phase (SS1–SS5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because it requires vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile halide transport, and multiple preparation steps; these features are directly reflected in CS5, SS1–SS3, TS2, and TS4. Temperatures are not extreme because analogous chloride CVT conditions are 360–475 °C (SS1–SS3), and thermodynamic analogues use 170→100 °C gradients or flame/sublimation conditions (TS2, TS4). CS2 provides insufficient useful signal for this route because it concerns high-pressure CdS₂ rather than chloride vapor growth, so CS2 is treated neutral for this route. Remaining uncertainty is whether vapor growth selects the Pbcm CdCl₂ sheet or a more common CdCl₂ polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Vapor availability is favorable because multiple halide analogues form crystals by sublimation or CVT (CS5, SS1–SS3, TS2, TS4). Crystallization is plausible but not guaranteed because no reference directly reports Pbcm CdCl₂, so structural selection is inferred. Composition control is easier than multication analogues because CdCl₂ is binary [Reasonable Inference], but chlorine volatility in sealed transport systems remains a concern by analogy to volatile halide synthesis (CS5, SS1–SS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact CdCl₂ vapor species or transport coefficient, so the kinetic score is not higher.

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because CS4, SS5/TS1, and TS3 all demonstrate aqueous chloride crystallization at room temperature, slow evaporation, or boiling-water cooling. It avoids high temperature, high pressure, vacuum, and sealed volatile transport, so the only major deduction is Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining severity risk is mainly chemical safety and hydration control rather than equipment difficulty.

Solution precursors should have high activity because chloride salts crystallize directly from aqueous media in CS4, SS5/TS1, and TS3. Diffusion limitations are minimal in solution, and analogous crystallizations occur without long high-temperature anneals (CS4, SS5/TS1, TS3). Structural reconstruction is still nontrivial because the target is a specific anhydrous 2D tetrahedral CdCl₂ sheet rather than just any Cd–Cl solid [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for anhydrous Pbcm CdCl₂ nucleation from water, so this route receives a strong but not perfect F3 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **slightly metastable** phase rather than a strict ground state because its E_hull is 0.0390 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well in a broad sense: chemically similar halides can be grown by ampoule or solution routes (CS1, CS4, CS5), structurally similar chloride sheets can crystallize from solution or vapor transport (SS1–SS5), and thermodynamically similar metastable halides are experimentally accessible (TS2–TS4). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: the Pbcm tetrahedral CdCl₂ sheet may compete with other CdCl₂ polymorphs or hydrates [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, synthesis is feasible, but identifying the correct water activity, chloride activity, and cooling/dehydration pathway will be decisive.
