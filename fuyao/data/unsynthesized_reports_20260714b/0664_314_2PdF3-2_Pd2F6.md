### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0400 eV/atom, which lies inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range, so a high F1 score is justified by the required scoring rubric. Chemically similar PdF3 is also low above hull at 0.0147 eV/atom [CS1], and PdF2 analogues span 0–0.0357 eV/atom [CS2, CS3], supporting the idea that Pd–F phases near this energy range are experimentally accessible. TS contains similarly metastable phases around 0.020–0.056 eV/atom [TS1–TS5], but SS mainly supports structural motifs rather than target thermodynamic stability; therefore there is insufficient evidence from SS for F1 beyond neutral support. Remaining uncertainty is whether the 0.040 eV/atom triclinic polymorph is kinetically isolable rather than converting to the known trigonal PdF3 [CS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because exact PdF3 synthesis occurs at about 400 °C, not extreme temperature or pressure [CS1]. The large penalties come from dry F2 and toxic/reactive halide chemistry [CS1]. Kinetics are favorable because PdI2-to-PdF3 conversion is experimentally reported [CS1], but the target polymorph differs from the reported trigonal R-3c PdF3 [CS1], lowering the structural-reconstruction score. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact PdI2 fluorination route, so their route-specific contribution is neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by structurally similar Pd fluorides synthesized via staged direct fluorination under diluted F2 [SS3, SS4]. The severity is similar to Route 1 because F2 remains necessary, but the process is more explicitly multi-step with pre-fluorination, through-fluorination, tempering, and cooling in F2 [SS3, SS4]. Kinetics benefit from reactive Pd salts and intimate mixing [SS3, SS4], but transfer to binary PdF3 is uncertain because SS3/SS4 are ternary fluoropalladates rather than PdF3. CS1 conflicts slightly by showing PdI2 as the proven precursor for binary PdF3, so CS1 is weighted more strongly for the best route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has lower condition score because sealed Pt/Au tube procedures, long anneals, and dry inert handling are repeatedly associated with Pd fluoride and complex fluoride crystal growth [CS2, TS4, SS1, SS2]. Kinetically, starting from preformed PdF3 is attractive because stoichiometry is already correct [CS1], but long annealing and slow cooling in analogues indicate slow ordering and difficult phase control [CS2, TS4]. This route may be best for crystals or polymorph selection rather than first synthesis. SS supports sealed-tube fluoride crystal growth but not PdF3 directly [SS1, SS2], so its evidence is useful but indirect.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is penalized heavily for high-pressure requirements because a PdF2 high-pressure phase required 50 kbar and a belt-type device [CS3]. Kinetics can be acceptable because preformed PdF3 would only need a polymorph transformation [Reasonable Inference], and CS3 shows Pd fluoride frameworks can transform at 400 °C under pressure. However, no source directly demonstrates high-pressure PdF3 polymorphism, so there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for this exact transformation. This route is a fallback if ambient-pressure fluorination yields only the known trigonal phase [CS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Pd, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [target description]. The described bonding contains Pd(1) octahedra and Pd(2) square-planar sites connected through F bridges, but no simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing mixture is stated [target description]. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, but the asymmetric description with two Pd and three F sites gives no evidence for a >40-atom cell; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on this target-specific check. Thus amorphization tendency is low, though the layered 2D nature may still make stacking disorder possible [target description; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PdF3 phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the established ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.040 eV/atom and CS1 reports a different trigonal PdF3 polymorph. The chemical-similarity data strongly supports the feasibility of making PdF3 itself by direct dry fluorination of PdI2 at ~400 °C [CS1], while structurally similar Pd fluorides support F2-based oxidative fluorination and Pd–F bond lengths near the target values [SS3, SS4]. The most critical enabling factor is access to sufficiently high fluorine chemical potential under rigorously dry conditions, which is common to the successful PdF3 and Pd fluoropalladate syntheses [CS1, SS3, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: available evidence supports PdF3 formation, but not specifically the triclinic 2D P-1 sheet structure [CS1; insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful temperature, fluorine activity, and quench/anneal control.
