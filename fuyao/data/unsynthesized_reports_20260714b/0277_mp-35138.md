### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest scoring bin in the provided rubric, so F1 = 90. The closest exact structural analogue Tl4SnTe3 is nearly on the hull at 0.0055 eV/atom (CS1/SS1), and several Tl–Te chemical analogues are on or near the hull (CS2/CS4/CS5). Thermodynamic analogues show moderate metastability in the 0.037–0.080 eV/atom range (TS1–TS4), supporting likely accessibility but not guaranteeing equilibrium stability. Remaining uncertainty is mainly the Mo substitution, because CS/SS analogues contain Sn, Ag, Ge, Hg, Pt, or Tl–Te frameworks rather than Mo.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state / melt reaction

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because evacuated sealed ampoules, toxic Tl, volatile Te/Tl, and multi-step heating/cooling are required, all consistent with sealed Tl–Te syntheses (SS2/CS4/SS5). The route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, as related direct syntheses occur at 500–550 °C (CS4/SS5/CS2/SS4). Kinetically, this route is penalized because elemental Mo is refractory and may not react fully at 500–650 °C [Reasonable Inference], whereas the cited direct analogues use more reactive Ge/Sn/Ag systems (CS1/CS2/CS4). Its main advantage is that the desired I4/mcm framework is strongly supported by the Tl4SnTe3 analogue (CS1/SS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Tl–Te self-flux / Tl2Te-rich flux growth

The severity score is similar to Route 1 because it still requires vacuum sealed ampoules, toxic Tl, volatile chalcogen control, and multiple processing steps, as reported for AgTl3Te2 and Hg3Tl2Te4 (CS2/SS4/CS5). Kinetics are better than direct reaction because a molten Tl–Te flux can improve mass transport, as demonstrated by Tl2Q flux growth over slow cooling (CS5). The proposed use of MoTe2 is a [Reasonable Inference] to reduce the Mo reaction barrier. Remaining uncertainty is whether Mo has sufficient solubility in Tl–Te flux to form MoTl4Te3 rather than leaving MoTe2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Molten-salt / molten-polytelluride-assisted route

This route has the best F2 because the closest analogue Tl4SnTe3 is synthesized under dry Ar at 600–700 °C without high pressure (CS1/SS1), and TS1 reports similar conditions for Tl4SnSe3. Kinetically, molten media improve contact and can promote crystal growth at temperatures far below refractory-cation melting points, as shown for the Tl4SnTe3/Tl4SnSe3 analogues (CS1/SS1/TS1). The structure-transfer argument is strongest here because the target and Tl4SnTe3 share I4/mcm symmetry, Tl-rich framework, and corner-sharing MTe6 octahedra (CS1/SS1). The key uncertainty is the Sn→Mo substitution, which may change oxidation-state preference and reaction kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film co-evaporation / sputtering

The condition severity is significant because high vacuum, post-deposition annealing, and volatile Tl/Te management are required, consistent with evaporation-deposition analogues (SS3/TS5). Kinetically, thin films can bypass bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], but SS3/TS5 indicate that post-heating is often needed and phase control can be difficult. Composition control is the limiting issue because Tl and Te are volatile relative to Mo [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for thin-film MoTl4Te3 synthesis, so the route is plausible but less supported than melt/flux synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: High-pressure contingency route

TS3/TS4 support high-pressure telluride synthesis at 30 kbar and 400–500 °C, but these are In–Te phases, not Tl–Mo–Te. The route is therefore scored as a contingency, with CS and SS giving insufficient direct high-pressure signal and treated as neutral where applicable. Kinetics may remain problematic because the temperature is relatively low for Mo diffusion [Reasonable Inference], although pre-reacted feed could help as in TS3/TS4. The high-pressure route may be useful if MoTl4Te3 is metastable and not obtainable under ambient-pressure flux conditions.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one triggered amorphization check: a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Tl and Mo [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the very different Tl–Te and M–Te environments in the Tl4SnTe3 analogue (CS1/SS1). The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms based on the Tl8Sn2Te6 analogue description (CS1/SS1), and the octahedral network is corner-sharing rather than mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity. Therefore, amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high. Uncertainty remains because Mo substitution could introduce local disorder or mixed valence [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MoTl4Te3 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong structurally: Tl4SnTe3 has the same I4/mcm Tl4MTe3 framework with corner-sharing MTe6 octahedra (CS1/SS1), and thermodynamic analogues occupy similar low-metastability ranges (TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is use of a liquid Tl–Te or molten-salt medium, which is supported by Tl-rich telluride flux/melt syntheses (CS1/CS2/CS5/SS1/SS4). The most critical limiting factor is Mo incorporation, because no provided analogue demonstrates Mo in this framework and elemental Mo is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful sealed-ampoule flux chemistry and strict stoichiometry/volatility control.
