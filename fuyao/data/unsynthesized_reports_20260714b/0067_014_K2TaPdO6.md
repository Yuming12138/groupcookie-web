### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 bin in the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues include synthesized phases with higher hull energies, such as Na₂Ca₃Ta₂O₉ at 0.067 eV/atom (TS1), CsV₂O₅ at 0.115 eV/atom (TS2), and Ba₃WO₆ at 0.0675 eV/atom (TS3), supporting that phases in this metastability range can be experimentally obtained. CS analogues are mostly on or near the hull (CS1–CS5), but they are chemically different perovskite-derived W oxides; insufficient evidence from CS for the specific K–Ta–Pd–O phase. Remaining uncertainty is the O–O/Pd redox chemistry, not the hull energy alone.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because CS ceramic analogues routinely use air, standard furnaces, grinding, pelletizing, and Pt crucibles/boats without high pressure or special atmospheres (CS1–CS5). TS1 similarly uses Ta₂O₅/carbonates in air on a Pt plate, supporting accessible equipment and atmosphere. The only formal deduction is multi-step processing, since repeated grinding/refiring is common in CS1–CS5. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for ceramic synthesis of the exact K–Ta–Pd–O composition.

#### F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has poor-to-moderate kinetics because Ta₂O₅ is refractory and ceramic routes in CS1–CS5 require long, repeated high-temperature treatments. The target is structurally unlike the CS double perovskites because it has triclinic P1 symmetry, TaO₄ tetrahedra, PdO₄ geometry, and an O–O bond; this implies substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. TS1 supports Ta oxide formation by solid-state reaction, but also reports phase mixture requiring crystal picking, indicating phase control can be difficult. SS gives insufficient evidence for dry ceramic formation of this exact topology.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized for reactive/controlled atmosphere, sealed containment, multi-step handling, and air-sensitive oxidizing precursors. SS1 directly supports KO₂ as a K/oxidant precursor but under demanding encapsulated high-pressure conditions. The target’s O–O bond makes peroxide/superoxide chemistry attractive [Reasonable Inference], but CS gives insufficient direct evidence for such precursors. The route is chemically compelling but operationally more severe than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

KO₂/K₂O₂ should be more reactive than dense oxide mixtures, so precursor activity is higher [Reasonable Inference], and SS1 demonstrates reaction of KO₂ with Re metal. However, Ta₂O₅ remains diffusion-limiting, and no SS analogue contains both Ta and Pd. Structural reconstruction is partly aided because the precursor chemistry matches the O–O motif [Reasonable Inference], but the exact triclinic K₂TaPdO₆ structure lacks precedent. The main uncertainty is whether Pd reaches the needed oxidation state without forming PdO/Pd metal side phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids high pressure and very high temperature, consistent with SS2, SS4, SS5, and TS2 flux/slow-cooling alkali oxide syntheses. Deductions arise from controlled oxidizing conditions, K volatility/sealed or covered crucible needs, and multi-step crystal-growth processing. SS evidence is strong for alkali flux growth but weak for Ta–Pd chemistry specifically. CS provides insufficient evidence for flux synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux improves kinetics by dissolving/reprecipitating oxides, as implied by K₂V₃O₈ flux growth (SS2), K₄CuV₅O₁₅Cl melt growth (SS4), and RbVO₃ slow-cooling growth (SS5). It may mitigate Ta₂O₅’s refractory behavior [Reasonable Inference], though Ta solubility in K-rich flux is uncertain. Structural reconstruction is more plausible than dry ceramic reaction because flux growth can select low-symmetry crystals [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes rather than decomposes the O–O/Pd oxidation state.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses specialized high-pressure equipment, but the required scoring table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa; the proposed 1.5–2.0 GPa is based on SS1 and TS5 and does not trigger that specific deduction. It is still penalized for reactive sealed oxidizing chemistry, volatile K/O containment, multi-step operations, and air-sensitive precursors. SS1 and TS5 both support Au/gold capsule high-pressure routes, but also indicate practical difficulty. CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

The high-pressure route scores best for reaction kinetics among the non-ceramic routes because KO₂/K₂O₂ are active oxidants and pressure can retain volatile K/O species [Reasonable Inference]. SS1 demonstrates that KO₂ can form alkali transition-metal oxides under pressure, while TS5 shows sealed high-pressure alkali tungstate crystal formation. However, both SS1 and TS5 report phase purity or serendipitous/byproduct issues, limiting confidence. The target’s exact Ta–Pd ordering remains unproven.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The radius mismatch between large K⁺ and smaller Ta/Pd cations triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The target formula unit has only 10 atoms, and the provided structure description does not establish a >40 atom cell, so C2 is not triggered. The target has mixed local coordination and an O–O bond, but there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS or the target description for simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing networks. Thus the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂TaPdO₆ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase**, sitting exactly at 0.0500 eV/atom above the hull rather than clearly on the ground-state hull. The CS analogues strongly support that multication oxide ceramics can be synthesized by repeated carbonate/oxide firing in air, but they do not capture the K volatility, Pd redox, or O–O bond chemistry. SS analogues provide the best support for K-rich flux and KO₂-based chemistry, while TS analogues show that Ta-containing and modestly metastable oxides can be experimentally isolated. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high oxygen chemical potential and/or peroxide/superoxide chemistry to preserve the target O–O unit and oxidized Pd state. The most critical limiting factor is competing phase formation from K loss, refractory Ta₂O₅, and PdO/Pd redox instability.
