### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0280 eV/atom places it well inside the commonly synthesizable metastability window, giving a high score by the required rubric. TS provides the strongest supporting signal because TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, and TS-5 are experimentally synthesized at similar E_hull values around 0.024–0.034 eV/atom. CS also supports feasibility because CS-1 to CS-4 are on the hull, and SS supports halide stability via SS-1, SS-4, and SS-5. Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity does not guarantee the specific Au–Hg–Br–Cl ordering pattern.

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed-ampoule solid-state

The severity is moderate because temperatures can remain below 300–350 °C, consistent with CS-1, SS-1, and TS-5, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Hg/Au halides, toxic Hg precursors, and multiple operations; these constraints are directly analogous to CS-1/CS-4/SS-1/TS-5. The key uncertainty is whether the target requires a narrow halide chemical potential window, as phase-control problems are reported for CS-2/CS-4.

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed-ampoule solid-state

Kinetics are better than typical ceramic solid-state synthesis because halide precursors are reactive and volatile, as shown by Hg halide chemistry in CS-1/CS-4 and SS-1. However, the target requires ordered Au/Hg placement and a 2D mixed-halide sheet, so structural reconstruction is only moderate. Long anneals in SS-1 and phase-purity issues in CS-2/CS-4 reduce confidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 CVT / halide vapor transport

The CVT route has similar condition-severity penalties to Route 1: evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile halide precursors, toxic Hg compounds, and multi-step setup are all present in CS-2/CS-4 and TS-2. It avoids high temperatures and high pressure, which keeps F2 from being lower. The uncertainty is sealed-ampoule pressure and composition drift from unequal HgCl₂/HgBr₂/AuCl₃ volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 CVT / halide vapor transport

CVT receives the best kinetic score because vapor transport bypasses slow solid diffusion, and CS-2/CS-4 demonstrate Hg halide materials grown under temperature gradients. TS-2 further supports low-temperature chloride transport in sealed ampoules. The limitation is phase selectivity: CS-2/CS-4 report low yields and equilibrium mixtures, so the target may require careful optimization of gradient and halide excess.

#### F2 — Route 3 solvothermal / reactive-halide solution

This route also avoids high temperature and high pressure, but sealed reactive solvents such as SOCl₂ or concentrated HX introduce volatility, corrosivity, and handling penalties, as shown by TS-1 and TS-4. Hg toxicity is common to all routes using HgCl₂/HgBr₂, supported by CS/SS/TS precursor lists. The main uncertainty is compatibility of Au halide speciation with hydrothermal or SOCl₂ media [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 solvothermal / reactive-halide solution

Kinetically, solution routes are favorable because dissolved halide complexes can reorganize rapidly; SS-3 forms Hg chloride sheets by slow evaporation, and TS-1 reports nearly quantitative chloride reaction in SOCl₂. TS-4 shows high-quality crystals from concentrated HX hydrothermal media. The score is slightly below CVT because no source directly demonstrates Au–Hg mixed-halide crystallization from solution, and Au hydrolysis/redox side reactions are plausible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically and geometrically different cations: Au is four-coordinate while Hg is six-coordinate in the given target structure, so one amorphization trigger is assigned. The formula-level structure is not large, and no face-sharing/edge-sharing/vertex-sharing coexistence is explicitly described, so the other checks are not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the target’s exact unit-cell atom count and polyhedral sharing classification, so those source signals are treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuHgBrCl₃ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground state**, because its E_hull is 0.0280 eV/atom but not zero. The CS and TS analogues strongly support that Hg halide/chalcogenide-halide materials with similar thermodynamic stability can be synthesized in sealed ampoules, while SS provides additional support for Hg chloride and Au bromide halide chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and volatility of metal halides, especially HgCl₂/HgBr₂, which makes low-temperature vapor or sealed-ampoule crystallization plausible. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control in a volatile, toxic, mixed Au/Hg/Br/Cl system, especially because no provided analogue exactly matches the Au–Hg mixed-halide chemistry. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with CVT/halide vapor transport being the most promising first route.
