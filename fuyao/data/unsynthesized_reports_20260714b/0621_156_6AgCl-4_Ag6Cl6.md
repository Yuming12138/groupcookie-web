### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0190 eV/atom, placing it in the highly accessible metastability range and giving F1 = 95. TS provides strong supporting evidence because experimentally synthesized thermodynamic analogues include CuHgSCl at E_hull ≈0.024 eV/atom and AuBr at E_hull ≈0.018 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2). SS also supports synthesizability of Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide phases, including experimentally prepared Ag₃SI variants with metastable energy values (SS-1, SS-2). CS gives mixed signal: near-ground-state CdS/ZnS analogues are accessible, but ZnS polytypes show metastable polymorph complexity; thus CS is useful but not decisive for AgCl.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives F2 = 65 because vacuum-sealed ampoule processing, volatile halide chemical potential control, and multistep grinding/annealing are required, as seen in SS-2 and SS-5. Temperatures are modest, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main uncertainty is whether low-temperature annealing can access the Pmmn ribbon phase rather than simply preserving or recrystallizing conventional AgCl [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient route-specific evidence for AgCl sealed polymorph conversion and is treated as neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are mixed: using AgCl as a single-source precursor helps stoichiometry, but converting to a 1D ribbon structure requires significant reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 and TS-2 indicate halide polymorphs can require long anneals, quenching, or controlled decomposition, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. Diffusion in silver halides should be relatively favorable compared with refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], so the route is not kinetically impossible. Remaining uncertainty is high because the exact Pmmn AgCl phase has no direct analogue in the dataset.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives F2 = 80 because aqueous precipitation/slow evaporation can occur near ambient temperature and pressure, consistent with SS-3 and CS-1. The only deductions are for multistep control of complexation/aging/isolation and handling of silver salts. No vacuum, high pressure, or high temperature is required. TS-3 supports halide-rich crystallization but at higher hydrothermal severity, so it is supportive but not directly identical.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives F3 = 80 because soluble Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ precursors provide very high precursor activity and solution-mediated nucleation avoids bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. SS-3 demonstrates chloride crystallization from aqueous solution, and CS-1 demonstrates controlled bath deposition using pH/complexation. The structural score is lower because ordinary AgCl precipitation is likely unless supersaturation, complexation, or templating biases nucleation toward the metastable 1D polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms direct Pmmn AgCl formation, so this remains the central uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives F2 = 65 because sealed ampoule, halide vapor control, and temperature-gradient crystallization impose moderate severity. SS-1 supports sealed melt growth of Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide crystals, while CS-3 to CS-5 support vapor/sublimation growth of polymorphic binary compounds. The temperature is far below the >1000 °C penalty threshold for the proposed AgCl route [Reasonable Inference]. The main practical risk is volatile chlorine loss and Ag⁰ formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 receives F3 = 60 because vapor/melt routes can yield crystals but often suffer from polymorph competition. CS ZnS vapor-grown polytypes show narrow phase windows and reproducibility challenges (CS-3 to CS-5), while TS-2 reports AuBr phase-control difficulty and sluggish transformations. Binary stoichiometry helps composition control, but halide volatility narrows the useful window [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the route is plausible but less attractive than solution or hydrothermal crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 receives F2 = 70 because hydrothermal synthesis uses sealed vessels, volatile/corrosive chloride media, and multistep loading/heating/cooling/washing. TS-3 directly supports sealed hydrothermal halide synthesis using concentrated HX and controlled cooling. The route avoids high temperature above 1000 °C and avoids high pressure above 5 GPa. CS offers insufficient direct hydrothermal AgCl evidence and is treated as neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 4

Route 4 receives F3 = 80 because hydrothermal dissolution–reprecipitation gives high precursor activity and avoids solid-state diffusion barriers. TS-3 reports quantitative reaction and high-quality single crystals for related halide-containing phases, supporting favorable kinetics and phase formation. However, the Pmmn AgCl ribbon polymorph may be metastable against conventional AgCl, so structural selectivity remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Chloride activity, fill fraction, and cooling rate are likely critical, based on TS-3.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

F4 = 95 because the target is a simple binary AgCl with only one cation species, a likely small cell, and simple 2-coordinate/linear connectivity. There is no mixed-cation radius mismatch, no large unit cell evidence, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network in the target description. SS-5 and TS-2 show related low-dimensional halide/chalcogenide materials can crystallize rather than amorphize, but CS gives insufficient specific evidence for amorphization in AgCl and is neutral 50. The remaining uncertainty is polymorph competition, not glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **metastable** AgCl polymorph rather than the conventional ground-state AgCl phase, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.019 eV/atom. The thermodynamic analogues strongly support accessibility of near-hull metastable halide/chalcogenide phases, especially TS-1 and TS-2, while SS supports sealed-ampoule synthesis of Ag-containing halide-related materials. The most critical enabling factor is the very low energy above hull combined with the high mobility/reactivity of silver halide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: ordinary AgCl is expected to nucleate readily, so the 1D Pmmn ribbon phase likely requires slow supersaturation, templating, confinement, or carefully controlled annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is feasible but will require polymorph-directed synthesis rather than merely combining Ag⁺ and Cl⁻.
