### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0390 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range by the provided rubric. CS gives strong support because multiple chemically close Ta–M–S phases are computed on the hull, including Ta₃SnS₆, Ta₄FeS₈, Ta₃PbS₆, FeTa₃S₆, and Ta₂PdS₆. SS and TS also support accessibility of slightly metastable layered Ta sulfides, including TaS₂ polytypes and TaS₂-derived phases with E_hull values up to ~0.03 eV/atom. The main uncertainty is that Ta₃SiS₆ itself is not experimentally reported in the provided sources.

#### F2 — Route 1 solid-state severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed quartz/silica tube conditions, which are directly supported by TaS₂ synthesis in evacuated quartz tubes at 600–1000 °C (SS) and Ta₂PdS₆ synthesis in sealed evacuated silica tubes near 1000–1075 K (CS). Severity deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and repeated grinding/annealing, all consistent with CS/SS workflows. TS provides only indirect support through high-temperature Ta chalcogenide processing, so its severity signal for this exact solid-state route is weaker. No high pressure or >1400 °C condition is indicated by any source.

#### F3 — Route 1 solid-state kinetics

The main kinetic penalty is the refractory nature of Ta and Si [Reasonable Inference], which makes direct solid-state diffusion slow. CS and SS partially mitigate this concern because Ta sulfides form at 600–1000 °C and chemically similar phases are obtained by long annealing, but those same sources also show long reaction times, repeated homogenization, and phase-control difficulty. Using TaS₂/TaS₃ precursors is inferred from CS/SS to preserve Ta–S connectivity and improve reaction kinetics. The remaining uncertainty is Si incorporation into the layered Ta–S framework.

#### F2 — Route 2 CVT severity

CVT requires evacuated ampoules, iodine, volatile sulfur, a temperature gradient, and multiple steps, all supported by Ta₄FeS₈ and TaS₂ CVT syntheses (CS, SS, TS). These conditions are moderate rather than extreme because temperatures are around 800–1000 °C and no high pressure is used. The toxic/corrosive nature of iodine adds operational severity, and possible silicon iodide formation is a route-specific concern [Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful evidence for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 CVT kinetics

CVT has better kinetic prospects than dry solid-state reaction because vapor transport reduces the need for long-range solid diffusion, as shown by Ta₄FeS₈ and TaS₂ single-crystal growth (CS, SS, TS). A prereacted charge should help maintain the desired Ta–Si–S ratio during transport [Reasonable Inference based on CS Ta₄FeS₈ workflow]. The limiting kinetic uncertainty is whether Si transports and incorporates congruently with Ta and S, since no CS/SS/TS example directly reports Ta–Si–S CVT. Therefore F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3 flux severity

TS supports KCl-melt reactions for layered TaS₂/NbS₂-derived phases at 900 °C under N₂ or H₂S, which keeps the temperature moderate. Severity arises from controlled atmosphere, possible H₂S toxicity, sulfur volatility, and post-reaction flux removal. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for KCl-flux synthesis of chemically similar Ta–Si–S phases, so they are neutral for this route. No high pressure is required.

#### F3 — Route 3 flux kinetics

Flux growth may improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, and TS directly shows TaS₂-derived layered chemistry in KCl melt. However, TS also notes issues such as crystal quality, stacking disorder, and thermal mobility for related layered phases, indicating phase-control challenges. CS/SS do not directly validate flux incorporation of Si into Ta sulfide sheets, so the flux-route F3 remains moderate. The route is plausible but less directly supported than CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The Ta–Si cation size and bonding mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference], because Ta is a large transition metal in high coordination while Si is much smaller and often covalent. The target description does not provide unit-cell atom count >40, and CS/SS/TS do not directly prove mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence in the target, so those checks are not triggered. CS shows related complex Ta sulfides can crystallize rather than amorphize, including Ta₄FeS₈ and Ta₂PdS₆, which reduces concern. The remaining uncertainty is whether orthorhombic ordering of Si is kinetically sluggish.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃SiS₆ is likely a slightly metastable but accessible phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.039 eV/atom while many synthesized Ta sulfide analogues lie between 0 and ~0.03 eV/atom (CS, SS, TS). The CS analogues strongly support the existence of Ta-rich ternary sulfides with similar Ta–S bonding, while SS and TS strongly support layered TaS₂-like frameworks and CVT growth. The most important enabling factor is the well-established high-temperature sealed-tube/CVT chemistry of Ta sulfides, especially iodine-assisted transport of layered Ta–S phases (CS, SS, TS). The most important limiting factor is the lack of direct evidence for Si incorporation and congruent Si transport in a Ta–S CVT environment. Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful sulfur fugacity and stoichiometry control.
