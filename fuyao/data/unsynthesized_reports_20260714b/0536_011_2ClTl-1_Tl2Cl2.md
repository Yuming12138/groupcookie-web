### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.021 eV/atom lies well below 0.05 eV/atom, giving a high intrinsic stability score by the required rubric [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally reported, including TlCl at 0.0062 eV/atom [TS1], TlBr at 0.0047 eV/atom [TS3], and AgTl₂I₃ at 0.0272 eV/atom [TS4]. The uncertainty is that the target is a P4/nmm zero-dimensional polymorph of TlCl, whereas the direct TlCl analogue is rocksalt Fm-3m [CS1, SS1, TS1], so the target is likely metastable even though low in energy.

#### F2 — Route 1

Low-temperature evaporation avoids high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The main severity penalties come from vacuum operation, cryogenic substrate control, multi-step deposition/warming, and thallium toxicity [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide strong evidence for feasibility of the equipment and conditions but also warn that phase control is difficult for metastable halide polymorphs [CS1, CS2].

#### F3 — Route 1

TlCl vapor availability is directly supported because TlCl is included in the evaporation-deposition precursor set [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The kinetic path is favorable for trapping metastable phases at low temperature, but the reported direct TlCl structure is rocksalt rather than the target P4/nmm zero-dimensional form [CS1; target data]. Composition control is easier for binary TlCl than multicomponent halides, but polymorph selection is very sensitive to deposition rate, base temperature, and warming rate [CS1, CS2].

#### F2 — Route 2

The hydrothermal route uses mild temperatures, 120–200 °C, and no high pressure beyond autogenous sealed-ampoule pressure [SS3, TS2]. It still requires evacuated ampoule preparation, sealing, solvent freezing/handling, and toxic TlCl, which produce the same practical penalties as other sealed thallium-halide routes [SS3; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal pure TlCl polymorph synthesis, so the condition score is based mainly on SS/TS analogues [insufficient evidence from CS; SS3, TS2].

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal synthesis provides high precursor mobility and avoids slow solid-state diffusion, as shown by crystallized Tl₂PdCl₄ and Tl–Hg halide analogues [SS3, TS2]. Structurally, SS3 is encouraging because it is an indium-derived, zero-dimensional Tl chloride, but it contains PdCl₄ units and is not pure TlCl [SS3]. The remaining uncertainty is phase selection: CS/TS do not show that hydrothermal treatment stabilizes the P4/nmm TlCl polymorph, so structural reconstruction/selectivity is the limiting kinetic term [insufficient evidence from CS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 3

Sealed-ampoule melt or vapor treatment uses moderate temperatures compared with refractory solid-state syntheses, consistent with halide melt/ampoule analogues [SS4, SS5, TS5]. The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile halide vapor pressure, multistep ampoule processing, and thallium toxicity [SS5; Reasonable Inference]. CS3 shows CVT can grow low-dimensional monohalides, but it uses AuCl/Cl₂ chemistry and therefore only partially transfers to TlCl [CS3].

#### F3 — Route 3

The melt/vapor environment gives good mass transport and should not be diffusion-limited [SS4, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. However, equilibrium thermal processing is likely to favor dense TlCl polymorphs, because the direct TlCl analogue in CS/SS/TS is rocksalt Fm-3m [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Thus, this route has reasonable kinetics for crystallization but weak kinetic selectivity for the target metastable zero-dimensional P4/nmm structure [target data; CS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple binary composition with one cation type, so the ionic-radius-mismatch check is not triggered [target data]. The target description contains two TlCl clusters and no large complex framework, so the >40 atom unit-cell risk is not triggered [target data]. The target has isolated single Tl–Cl bonds rather than mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, so the amorphization risk is low; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data for the exact P4/nmm polymorph and are treated as neutral for this factor [target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target TlCl P4/nmm phase is likely a metastable polymorph rather than the ground-state TlCl structure, because its E_hull is nonzero at 0.021 eV/atom and the direct TlCl analogue in CS/SS/TS is rocksalt Fm-3m [target data; CS1, SS1, TS1]. The analogues support synthesis of thallium halides very well in general, especially by low-temperature evaporation and sealed hydrothermal methods [CS1, SS1, TS1; SS3, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the low energy above hull, which places the target within the experimentally accessible metastability window [target data; TS1–TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, since the known TlCl evaporation analogue forms rocksalt and metastable halide phases depend strongly on temperature, substrate, and heating history [CS1, CS2]. Overall, the material is plausible but will likely require kinetic trapping or careful low-temperature crystallization rather than conventional equilibrium annealing.
