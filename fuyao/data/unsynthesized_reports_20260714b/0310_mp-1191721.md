#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the top of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” stability bin, giving 90/100. CS and SS analogues are mostly on or very near the hull, including REAlH₆ and BaAlH₅ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1–CS5) and Ba–Mg–Ru/Os/Fe hydrides at E_hull ≈0–0.002 eV/atom (SS1–SS3). TS analogues with E_hull ≈0.060–0.084 eV/atom have reported synthesis routes (TS1–TS5), so a 0.050 eV/atom BaReH₉ phase is plausibly metastable-accessible rather than thermodynamically prohibitive. Remaining uncertainty is that the hull value does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as impurity and decomposition issues are reported in CS1–CS3 and SS2–SS3.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct BaH₂ + Re hydrogenation

F2 is moderately favorable because the route avoids multi-GPa pressure and uses temperatures comparable to SS5 and TS5, but it still requires controlled H₂ and air-sensitive hydride handling. F3 is low because Re is refractory [Reasonable Inference] and the route requires long-range reaction between BaH₂ and Re, analogous to solid-state routes where educt impurities or long sintering are reported (TS2, SS5). The route is experimentally straightforward compared with DAC synthesis, but kinetic sluggishness and phase competition are likely.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: LiH/LiD flux-assisted synthesis

F2 is lower than Route 1 because the flux route introduces additional steps and air-sensitive LiH/LiD handling, but conditions are directly supported by SS1–SS3. F3 improves relative to direct sintering because LiH/LiD flux enables Ba–transition-metal hydride formation at 500–530 °C and 100–150 bar (SS2–SS3), reducing diffusion barriers. However, SS2–SS3 report impurity phases such as BaLiD₃ or Mg-containing hydrides, so phase purity remains a meaningful risk.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Mechanochemical metathesis

F2 is moderate because room-temperature milling from CS1–CS3 avoids severe temperature/pressure, but controlled atmosphere, air-sensitive hydrides, and prior synthesis of K₂ReH₉/Na₂ReH₉ add penalties. F3 is the best among routes because a preformed [ReH₉]²⁻ precursor would bypass Re metal hydrogenation and long-range cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference], analogous to NaAlH₄ chloride metathesis in CS1–CS3. The main uncertainty is that CS1–CS3 report low yields and decomposition even for REAlH₆, and no SS/TS source directly validates ReH₉ salt metathesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Ba–Re alloy hydrogenation

F2 is penalized because Ba alloy preparation can involve Ba loss, explicitly noted for BaAlH₅ precursor alloying (CS5), and the route requires multiple operations. F3 is limited because even though alloying improves contact, CS5 required 5–30 days and had crystallization challenges for BaAlH₅. The route may be useful as a screening backup, but the absence of a demonstrated Ba–Re alloy hydride in CS/SS/TS makes transferability uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Extreme high-pressure synthesis

F2 is the most severe because >5 GPa pressure triggers the largest condition penalty, based on TS1 high-pressure anvil synthesis. F3 is also weak because TS1 gives only broad high-pressure hydride formation, with insufficient evidence for Ba/Re chemical compatibility, product scale, or phase-pure recovery. This route is best viewed as a discovery/proof-of-existence path rather than a practical bulk synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Ba/Re size and bonding mismatch [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the strong Ba–H versus transition-metal–H distance contrast seen in SS2–SS5. The structure also contains multiple inequivalent H sites and mixed hydride coordination environments, comparable to complex mixed-connectivity hydride frameworks in SS1 and SS5 and CS1–CS3. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, so that check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents a stronger amorphization penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaReH₉ is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the metastability threshold. The CS analogues support mechanochemical access to complex hydrides but warn of low yield and decomposition (CS1–CS3), while SS and TS analogues strongly support high-H₂-pressure synthesis of Ba/alkaline-earth transition-metal hydrides (SS1–SS5, TS2, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high hydride chemical potential, either through a preformed [ReH₉]²⁻ precursor in Route 3 or high-pressure H₂/LiH flux in Routes 1–2. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: Re is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and analogous systems report impurities, long reaction times, or poor crystallization (CS5, SS2–SS3, TS2). Overall, the target is plausible but likely requires careful inert handling and either a chemically preorganized ReH₉ precursor or high-pressure hydride flux chemistry.
