### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has Eₕᵤₗₗ = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability boundary of the rubric and supports a score of 90 [Target]. The closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue is the same nominal Sr₂UZnO₆ composition with Eₕᵤₗₗ ≈ 0.054 eV/atom and reported solid-state synthesis [CS1, SS1, TS1]. This suggests the target is near the metastability threshold but likely experimentally accessible [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is the C2/m versus P2₁/c polymorph relation; the references support Sr₂UZnO₆ formation but not uniquely the C2/m setting [Target, CS1].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses high-temperature ceramic processing up to 1150 °C, so the >1000 °C penalty applies [CS1, SS1, TS1]. It also requires multiple heating/regrinding steps, so the multistep penalty applies [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Uranium precursors impose toxicity/radiological handling penalties [Reasonable Inference based on uranium chemistry; uranium precursor listed in CS1/SS1/TS1]. No high pressure, sealed volatile system, or reactive atmosphere is required for Zn because air synthesis was reported [CS1, SS1, TS1].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The uranyl acetate/carbonate precursor set is relatively reactive and was directly used to form Sr₂UZnO₆ [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Repeated grinding and staged calcination indicate that long-range diffusion and B-site ordering are still kinetically relevant [CS1]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target and analogue share corner-sharing UO₆/ZnO₆ double-perovskite topology [Target, CS1, SS1, TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether the C2/m polymorph forms directly or converts from a P2₁/c-like variant during annealing/cooling [Target, CS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The oxide/carbonate route is also high-temperature, with reported Sr₂AᴵᴵUO₆ synthesis at 900–1150 °C and repeated grinding over long durations [CS3, CS4, SS2, SS3]. Air is generally acceptable for non-Mn/Fe/Co members, so no controlled-atmosphere deduction is applied for Zn [CS3, CS4, SS2, SS3]. Uranium oxide handling is the major safety severity factor [Reasonable Inference based on uranium chemistry; uranium oxides listed in CS3/CS4/SS2/SS3]. No source requires high pressure for this family [CS3, CS4, SS2, SS3].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Oxide/carbonate precursors are less reactive than acetate-containing mixtures, and the reported 50 h calcination with grinding every 10 h supports a moderate kinetic penalty [CS3, CS4, SS2, SS3]. The family-level evidence is strong for forming Sr₂AᴵᴵUO₆ double perovskites, including Zn-relevant temperature guidance [CS3, SS2]. However, oxide precursors require more complete solid-state reconstruction than the acetate/carbonate route [Reasonable Inference]. Phase monitoring by XRD is important because related ceramic syntheses can show minor SrUO₄/Sr₂UO₅ impurities [CS2].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route uses 950–1150 °C in air and ambient pressure, so it is not extreme but still exceeds 1000 °C in the likely optimized window [SS5]. It requires a multistep heat/hold/slow-cool/flux-removal process [SS5]. Uranium oxide handling again imposes toxicity/radiological risk [Reasonable Inference based on uranium chemistry; U₃O₈ listed in SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports flux growth of Sr₂UZnO₆ directly, so those source categories are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Molten carbonate flux should improve ion transport relative to solid-state diffusion, and related Ba₂ZnUO₆ crystals were grown from such fluxes [SS5]. Slow cooling supports crystallization but introduces composition and polymorph sensitivity [SS5]. The key uncertainty is that the demonstrated flux analogue is Ba₂ZnUO₆, cubic Fm-3m, whereas the target is Sr₂UZnO₆, monoclinic C2/m [SS5, Target]. Thus the kinetics are favorable, but transferability is less certain than Route 1 [SS5 versus CS1/SS1/TS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large A-site Sr cation and smaller octahedral U/Zn cations, so the ionic radius contrast check is triggered [Target, CS1, SS1, TS1]. The octahedral network is corner-sharing rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, which lowers amorphization risk [Target, CS1, SS1, TS1]. No provided source states a unit cell atom count above 40 for the target; therefore that check is not triggered, with insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a larger-cell amorphization penalty. Overall, the ordered double-perovskite topology should crystallize under ceramic conditions, but B-site ordering and tilt polymorphism remain possible complications [CS1, SS1, TS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂UZnO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable oxide rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because the target Eₕᵤₗₗ is 0.0500 eV/atom and the closest same-composition analogue is ~0.054 eV/atom [Target, CS1, TS1]. The CS, SS, and TS evidence strongly support synthesis because the closest entry in all three categories is the same nominal Sr₂UZnO₆ double perovskite, already made by a standard ceramic route [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most important enabling factor is the robust Sr₂AᴵᴵUO₆ double-perovskite family chemistry, where UO₆ and ZnO₆ octahedra form a corner-sharing ordered framework under air firing [CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2]. The main limiting factor is phase/polymorph control, especially avoiding Sr–U oxide impurities and obtaining the target C2/m variant rather than a closely related P2₁/c distortion [Target, CS1, CS2]. Overall, the material is highly plausible by ceramic synthesis, with the best route being the directly reported SrCO₃/ZnCO₃/uranyl acetate process [CS1, SS1, TS1].
