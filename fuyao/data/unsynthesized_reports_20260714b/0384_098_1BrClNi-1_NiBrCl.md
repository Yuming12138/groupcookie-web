### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.006 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore receives a high score of 98. TS analogues are close in thermodynamic distance and also lie near the hull, with TS-1 at ~0.011 eV/atom, TS-2 at ~0.017 eV/atom, and TS-5 at ~0.020 eV/atom, supporting near-equilibrium accessibility. SS analogues include several stable bromides at E_hull = 0 or nearly 0, such as PdBr₂ and RuBr₃, reinforcing that related halide frameworks can be thermodynamically robust. CS evidence is mixed because CS-1 has E_hull ~0.103 eV/atom while CS-4 is stable, so CS supports accessibility but not uniformly.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Route 1 receives F2 = 65 because it avoids extreme temperature and high pressure but requires vacuum/Ar sealing, moisture exclusion, and toxic/hygroscopic nickel halides. This severity profile is consistent with CS-1, where a related 2D halide-containing phase was made in a sealed tube at 320 °C, and TS-5, where binary halides were reacted in evacuated quartz ampoules. Its F3 = 76 because anhydrous binary halides should be moderately reactive, and the target’s 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet is structurally closer to layered metal halides than to dense refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether Br/Cl order forms cleanly or whether the product becomes a disordered NiCl₂–NiBr₂ solid solution.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: chemical vapor transport

Route 2 receives F2 = 55 because it uses sealed ampoules, controlled halogen vapor, multistep feed preparation/growth, and hazardous Br₂/Cl₂ handling, all of which are supported by SS-1 and SS-5. Its F3 = 67 because bromide CVT is well established for structurally similar PdBr₂ and RuBr₃, but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates mixed Br/Cl vapor transport for NiBrCl. The main kinetic advantage is vapor-phase mass transport, as shown for PdBr₂ and RuBr₃ crystals in SS-1 and SS-5. The main uncertainty is composition control, since small changes in Br₂/Cl₂ chemical potential could favor NiBr₂, NiCl₂, or nonstoichiometric mixed halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: melt/flux-assisted sealed ampoule

Route 3 receives F2 = 55 because it is hotter and more operationally complex than Route 1, requiring sealed ampoule heating, quenching/annealing or flux handling, and toxic hygroscopic halides. Its F3 = 76 because molten or fluxed halides should greatly improve mass transport, and TS-5 demonstrates melt/quench/anneal access to thermodynamically similar halides. However, CS-4 shows that cooling rate can strongly determine which halide phase forms, so phase selection could be difficult. This route is therefore kinetically attractive but less controlled than direct low-temperature annealing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ni, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described P3m1 NiBrCl sheet is compositionally simple and does not suggest a >40-atom unit cell, so the unit-cell-size criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The target contains edge-sharing NiBr₃Cl₃ octahedra but not mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, so the polyhedral-connectivity criterion is not triggered by the provided structure description. CS-1 supports crystallization of chemically related 2D halide-containing sheets, while TS provides insufficient direct evidence for amorphization tendency and is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NiBrCl is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.006 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through sealed halide ampoule chemistry: CS-1 shows a 2D halide-containing compound formed at 320 °C, TS-5 shows binary halide ampoule annealing, and SS-1/SS-5 show that bromide crystals can be stabilized/grown under halogen vapor. The most important enabling factor is the target’s simple Ni²⁺ mixed-halide chemistry and low thermodynamic driving force for decomposition. The most important limiting factor is precise Br/Cl stoichiometry and ordering, because the references mainly demonstrate single-halide or more complex molecular halide phases rather than a directly analogous mixed NiBrCl sheet. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with sealed-ampoule annealing of dry NiCl₂/NiBr₂ as the best first attempt.
