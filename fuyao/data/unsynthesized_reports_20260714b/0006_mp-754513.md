### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target sits at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is the favorable cutoff for high thermodynamic synthesizability [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally synthesized, including MnTiO₃ at ~0.06 eV/atom and several TS analogues near 0.05–0.066 eV/atom [CS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is whether MnZrO₃ competes strongly with MnO + ZrO₂, which is not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS data [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is conditionally severe because high temperature and reducing/inert atmosphere are supported by Mn oxide analogues [CS1, SS1, SS2, SS3]. Its kinetic score is limited by refractory ZrO₂ diffusion, although the ilmenite-like structural precedent from Mn₄Nb₂O₉ and Mn₄Ta₂O₉ is strong [CS1, SS2; Reasonable Inference]. TS gives useful solid-state precedent for high-temperature oxide formation but not direct MnZrO₃ kinetics [TS3, TS4].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has the best condition score because flux growth avoids very high temperatures and high pressure, and MnTiO₃ was directly grown from Na₂B₄O₇ flux [CS2, SS4, TS1]. Its kinetic score is moderate-high because liquid flux should improve transport, but exact temperature, atmosphere, and Zr solubility are not given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on those details is scored neutral at 50 [CS2, SS4, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is whether the flux selects the desired ilmenite-like MnZrO₃ rather than MnO/ZrO₂ or another Mn–Zr oxide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 improves precursor activity because EDTA-type precursor chemistry is reported for RMnO₃ preparation [CS3]. Reducing annealing is justified by Mn-containing analogues where H₂/Ar or reducing gas was required for phase purity and oxidation-state control [SS1, SS3]. SS and TS do not directly demonstrate sol-gel MnZrO₃, so their route-specific signal is insufficient and neutral at 50 [SS, TS].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is harsh because CS3 uses 5 GPa and high-pressure apparatus for Mn oxide phase conversion [CS3]. It may help if MnZrO₃ is metastable, but CS3 also shows phase-control failures for some compositions, so the route is not guaranteed [CS3]. SS and TS provide insufficient high-pressure evidence for MnZrO₃ and are scored neutral at 50 for this route [SS, TS].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: a borderline Mn²⁺/Zr⁴⁺ size mismatch inferred from octahedral radii and reflected in longer Mn–O than Zr–O bonds [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure does not explicitly show mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, unlike some ilmenite-like analogues that contain edge/face sharing [Target, CS1, SS2]. Unit-cell atom-count evidence is not directly provided by CS/SS/TS, so that specific source signal is insufficient and neutral at 50 [CS, SS, TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnZrO₃ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from ilmenite-like Mn₄Nb₂O₉/Mn₄Ta₂O₉ solid-state analogues and the MnTiO₃ flux analogue with similar thermodynamic stability [CS1, SS2, CS2, SS4, TS1]. The most important enabling factor is the availability of octahedral Mn–O / high-valent-cation–O frameworks that tolerate large tilts and distortions [Target, CS1, SS2]. The most important limiting factor is kinetic and redox control: ZrO₂ is refractory, while Mn must remain near Mn²⁺ during crystallization [SS1, SS3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, MnZrO₃ appears synthesizable but will likely require flux assistance or highly homogeneous precursors rather than a single simple oxide firing [CS2, SS4, TS1; Reasonable Inference].
