### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0380 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bracket, so it is likely accessible as a metastable or near-ground-state phase [Target]. Thermodynamic similar materials with comparable hull energies have reported sealed-ampoule syntheses, especially Cu₂FeGeSe₄ at ~0.038 eV/atom [TS1]. Chemically related NbSe₂Cl₂, NbSeBr₃, and CrSBr are on the hull and experimentally synthesized [CS1, CS4, CS5], supporting the idea that chalcogen-halide layered phases are synthetically realistic. The uncertainty is that Ti(SeBr)₂ itself is not directly reported in the provided CS/SS/TS data.

#### F2 — Route 1

CVT uses moderate temperatures, but it requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, temperature gradients, volatile Br₂/Se, and long dwell times [CS1, CS4, SS1]. These conditions match well-established chalcogen-halide CVT syntheses but impose safety and stoichiometry penalties [CS2, CS4]. TS gives no direct route-specific condition for Ti–Se–Br CVT; insufficient evidence from TS, neutral signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

CVT improves kinetics by moving material through volatile halide/selenide species rather than relying only on bulk diffusion [CS1, CS4]. The target’s topology is strongly supported by Nb(SBr)₂ and NbSe₂Cl₂ analogues [CS1, CS2], but Ti may form volatile TiBr₄ or stable TiSe₂ side phases [Reasonable Inference]. Long transport durations in CS1 and CS4 suggest crystal growth is feasible but slow.

#### F2 — Route 2

Direct sealed-tube reaction is less procedurally complex than CVT and is directly supported by Nb(SBr)₂ at 500 °C for 2 days [CS2]. It still requires sealed-tube handling of Br₂ and Se, which are volatile and hazardous [CS2, CS4]. SS has limited direct support for Ti–Se–Br direct solid-state synthesis; insufficient evidence from SS, neutral signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

The major kinetic penalty is Ti metal activation because dense/refractory metals react slowly in solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Halogen-assisted reaction can partially overcome this, as Nb(SBr)₂ and Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ form at ~500–530 °C in sealed tubes [CS2, CS3]. However, CS2 reports simultaneous polymorph formation, so obtaining a single Ti(SeBr)₂ phase may require optimization.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures similar to Ti selenide flux synthesis [SS2] and selenophosphate flux synthesis [TS5]. The process is more complex because it involves flux preparation, slow cooling, possible glovebox handling, and flux removal [SS2, TS5]. CS does not provide a direct Ti–Se–Br flux analogue; insufficient evidence from CS, neutral signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux media can enhance precursor activity and reduce diffusion barriers, as shown for Ti-containing selenides at 490 °C [SS2]. However, the target contains Br and no P/alkali component, whereas many demonstrated flux systems introduce alkali or phosphorus chemistry [SS2, TS5]. Thus flux growth is plausible for discovery crystals but carries higher impurity and phase-selection uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Target]. The reported structure is a simple layered Ti–Se–Br sheet rather than a very large or highly mixed polyhedral framework [Target]. Related low-dimensional chalcogen-halides crystallize as ordered single crystals or crystalline powders [CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, SS5], so amorphization risk is low, although exact unit-cell atom count is not specified in the prompt.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti(SeBr)₂ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase** rather than a guaranteed ground state because its E_hull is 0.0380 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis is well supported by chemically and structurally related early-transition-metal chalcogen-halides, especially Nb(SBr)₂ with the same C2/m layered motif [CS2] and NbSe₂Cl₂/NbSeBr₃ CVT examples [CS1, CS4, SS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a sealed halogen-containing environment, which can provide both Br chemical potential and vapor-transport kinetics [CS1, CS2, CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: Ti may form competing TiSe₂ or titanium bromides, and related systems show polymorph coexistence or impurity sensitivity [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is feasible but will likely require careful Br₂ loading, temperature-gradient tuning, and product separation.
