### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic criterion. Chemically close Eu₂H₃Cl and Eu₂H₃Br are at or near the hull, supporting that the P-3m1 rare-earth hydride framework is thermodynamically plausible (CS1, CS2). Structurally and thermodynamically similar La₂H₅ and LaH₃ are also stable or nearly stable, supporting favorable La–H bonding environments (SS1/SS2, TS1/TS2). The main uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly reports synthesized La₂NH₃.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate high temperature, 850–950 °C, directly based on Eu₂H₃Cl at 900 °C/13 h in sealed Ta/silica ampoules (CS1). It avoids >1000 °C and avoids high pressure, but it requires sealed ampoules, inert handling, and air-sensitive hydride/nitride reagents, which justify the deductions. CS4 supports similar sealed ampoule rare-earth hydride/deuteride handling at 850 °C. SS/TS are insufficient for exact ampoule conditions because their listed synthesis metadata for LaH₃/La₂H₅ is chemically mismatched to oxide ceramic routes.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable compared with refractory oxide ceramic synthesis because hydrides are reactive and the closest analogue crystallizes in 13 h at 900 °C (CS1). Structural reconstruction should be limited because CS1 has a nearly identical P-3m1 rare-earth hydride topology with two H sites and octahedral/tetrahedral H coordination. The uncertainty is the replacement of halide by nitride, which changes charge balance and may favor LaN or LaH₃ impurities by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is lower temperature than Route 1, using 400–750 °C conditions inspired by Eu hydride synthesis under H₂ up to 10 MPa (CS5). However, it requires reactive H₂/NH₃ atmospheres, pressure-rated equipment, and likely multiple steps. CS5 supports H₂ autoclave hydride chemistry, but NH₃ nitriding is not present in the data and is therefore [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Gas-phase H₂/NH₃ provides high chemical activity, and CS5 shows rare-earth hydrides can be accessed by high-pressure hydrogenation. Diffusion may be acceptable through hydride phases, but ordering N and H into the exact La₂NH₃ P-3m1 topology is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether ammonolysis produces the ordered nitride-hydride rather than a LaN + LaH₃ mixture.

#### F2 — Route 3

Mechanochemical synthesis avoids high temperature and high pressure, and REAlH₆ analogues form by ball milling under Ar/H₂ at room temperature for 4–10 h (SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5). The method still requires air-sensitive salts/hydrides, controlled atmosphere, and likely post-annealing/salt removal. The evidence supports rare-earth hydride metathesis generally, but not the exact La–N–H phase.

#### F3 — Route 3

Precursor activity is high because salt-metathesis and ball milling can generate intimate mixing and reactive intermediates, as shown for REAlH₆ mechanochemical products (SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5). Diffusion is aided by nanoscale milling and possible low-temperature annealing. Structural ordering is the weak point because the REAlH₆ analogues are reported with low yield, impurities, and partial decomposition, suggesting difficult phase purity control (SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, La, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The structure is small by formula size and P-3m1 symmetry, so the >40 atom-count criterion is not triggered by [Reasonable Inference]. Mixed corner/edge/face sharing is explicitly present in the target and also appears in the close CS1/CS2 and SS1/SS2 analogues, so crystallization remains plausible despite a low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

La₂NH₃ is likely a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The best support comes from the chemically close Eu₂H₃Cl/Eu₂H₃Br P-3m1 rare-earth hydride frameworks, especially Eu₂H₃Cl synthesized in sealed Ta ampoules at 900 °C (CS1, CS2). La–H structural motifs are additionally supported by stable LaH₃ and near-stable La₂H₅ analogues with HLa₄ and HLa₆ polyhedra (SS1/SS2, TS1/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high H chemical potential while allowing La–N incorporation in a sealed, oxygen-free environment. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among LaH₃/La₂H₅, LaN, and possible off-stoichiometric La–N–H products, because direct La₂NH₃ synthesis is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.
