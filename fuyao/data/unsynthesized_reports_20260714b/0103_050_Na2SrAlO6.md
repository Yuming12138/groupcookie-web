### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable threshold for likely synthesizability and receives 90/100 by the required rubric. CS supports this because chemically similar CS-4 has E_hull ≈0.0568 eV/atom and is experimentally synthesized by solid-state reaction. TS also supports metastable accessibility because several thermodynamically similar phases with E_hull ≈0.056–0.074 eV/atom are synthesized (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). SS gives mixed evidence because some structurally similar peroxide-like phases are stable, such as SrO₂ (SS-2), while others are higher above hull, so uncertainty remains regarding the target’s O–O-containing framework.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses ordinary furnace temperatures below 1000 °C, ambient pressure, carbonates/oxides, O₂ or air, and repeated grinding, consistent with CS-1, CS-3, and TS-5. The main deductions are multi-step processing and Na volatility/stoichiometry control, the latter inferred from sodium oxide synthesis issues and high-T Na-containing solid-state processing in TS-5. CS provides strong evidence for feasibility, TS provides supportive Na-oxide precedent, and SS provides insufficient evidence for complex bulk synthesis by this route. Overall severity is moderate, not severe.

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

Carbonate/oxide precursors have moderate activity, and CS-1/CS-3 require staged heating up to 950 °C for as long as two weeks, indicating slow kinetics. TS-5 shows Na-containing complex oxides can be made by solid-state reaction but require calcination, regrinding, firing, quenching, and sometimes annealing. The target likely requires substantial structural reconstruction from simple precursors [Reasonable Inference], especially if O–O units must be retained or formed. Therefore kinetics are only moderate to poor.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high pressure and can plausibly remain below 1000 °C, while using standard wet-chemistry and furnace steps. CS-5 directly supports citrate-gel synthesis with dehydration, incineration, and final heat treatment for a chemically similar complex oxide. The only severity deduction is the multi-step process; SS and TS do not provide direct sol-gel evidence and are treated as neutral for this condition assessment. The route is operationally moderate but not harsh.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Citrate-gel processing gives high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths, supported by CS-5. This should help overcome the slow diffusion seen in solid-state CS-1/CS-3 and CS-4 [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS-5 still uses prolonged high-temperature treatment and intermediate grinding, so crystallization and cation ordering are not trivial. The unresolved issue is whether peroxide-like O–O bonding survives the anneal, for which CS-5 gives insufficient evidence.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is low-temperature but chemically more demanding because it uses concentrated base/peroxide media and possibly sealed oxidizing capsules. SS-1, SS-2, and SS-3 support peroxide synthesis under mild temperatures, while TS-1 supports sealed gold-tube oxide flux growth using peroxide precursors. Deductions arise from air/moisture-sensitive oxidizers, sealed-system handling, and multiple operations. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for this route and is neutral.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution or flux media should provide very high precursor activity and good ion transport, as SS-3 crystallized K₃TaO₈ from KOH/H₂O₂ solution and SS-1/SS-2 formed simple peroxides under mild conditions. The route directly targets the O–O motif indicated by the target structure and supported by SS peroxide analogues. The limiting kinetic factor is structural ordering of Na, Sr, and Al into a monoclinic framework, which no SS source directly demonstrates. Thus the route has good chemical kinetics but uncertain crystallographic selectivity.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with very different sizes and preferred coordinations: Na in 5- and 8-coordinate sites, Sr in 8-coordinate sites, and Al in octahedral sites, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference from the target description]. The target description does not establish a unit-cell atom count above 40, and CS/SS/TS do not supply that missing target-cell information, so C2 is not triggered. Mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity is common in some CS/TS analogues, but the target description only states corner-sharing NaO₅–AlO₆ polyhedra, so C3 is not triggered. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂SrAlO₆ is likely a marginally metastable or near-threshold phase rather than an unambiguous ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS and TS analogues strongly support that oxides in this stability range can be synthesized by solid-state or related bulk methods, especially CS-4 and TS-5. SS analogues are particularly important because the target contains short O–O distances, and SS-1/SS-2/SS-3 demonstrate peroxide-type O–O chemistry, although mostly in simpler compounds. The most critical enabling factor is achieving homogeneous Na/Sr/Al mixing while maintaining an oxidizing environment. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: avoiding Na loss, carbonate impurities, ordinary aluminates, and decomposition or loss of the peroxide-like oxygen substructure.
