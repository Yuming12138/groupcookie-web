### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.015 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range normally considered experimentally accessible. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.018–0.032 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable, including Sr₃Co₂O₄Cl₂ [TS1], Sr₃Fe₂O₅Br₂ [TS2], and HgBa₂CaCu₂O₆-type oxides [TS4]. This supports a high score, but the hull value does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or Ag oxidation-state stability; insufficient direct Ag–Sr–I–O thermodynamic precedent from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, matching Sr₂CuO₂I₂ synthesis at 600 °C in evacuated ampoules [CS1, SS1]. The main severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile/moisture-sensitive iodide chemistry, and air-sensitive SrO/SrI₂ handling [CS1, CS2]. The conditions are standard for oxyhalides but still nontrivial; insufficient evidence from TS for Ag-specific solid-state severity, so TS contributes only indirect neutral-to-moderate support.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, the route is plausible because SrO/SrI₂/CuO analogues form layered oxyiodides at only 600–650 °C [CS1, CS2, SS1]. Structural reconstruction should be modest because the target shares the layered OSr₄M₂ octahedral framework with Sr₂CuO₂I₂ and Ca₃Cu₂O₄Br₂ [SS1, SS4]. Remaining uncertainty is the Ag precursor: AgO may decompose or reduce, and no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Sr–Ag–O–I solid-state synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth is more operationally complex because it requires flux excess, slow cooling, and flux removal, as seen in Sr₂CuO₂Br₂, Ba₃In₂O₅Br₂, and Sr₃Fe₂O₅Br₂ routes [SS2, CS4, TS2]. It still avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but sealed/dry conditions are recommended because SrI₂/SrO iodide systems are moisture sensitive [CS1, CS2]. Air flux is reported for some bromide/iron/indium oxyhalides [CS4, TS2], but for the iodide Ag target this is risky by inference; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct support for air processing of Sr–Ag–O–I.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve kinetics because halide melts enable transport and crystal growth in several oxyhalide families [SS2, CS4, TS2]. Slow cooling can aid ordering and crystallinity, while excess SrO can suppress competing phases in Sr₂CuO₂Br₂ growth [SS2]. The main uncertainty is composition control: SrI₂ flux may dissolve or volatilize iodide and Ag oxide may reduce, which is inferred but not directly quantified by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Topochemical synthesis uses low temperature, as Sr₃Co₂O₄Cl₂ forms at 200 °C under vacuum [TS1], so thermal severity is low. However, the route is multi-step and requires a precursor, sealed vacuum, glovebox handling, reactive NaH if reduction is used, and washing steps [TS1]. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for topochemical Ag iodide exchange, so this route is exploratory.

#### F3 — Route 3

Topochemical kinetics can be favorable because they avoid long-range cation diffusion and preserve a layered framework, as demonstrated for Sr₃Co₂O₅Cl₂ → Sr₃Co₂O₄Cl₂ [TS1]. If an appropriate Sr–Ag–O–I or Sr–Ag–O–Cl/Br precursor exists, structural reconstruction could be minimal [Reasonable Inference from TS1]. The limiting uncertainty is precursor availability; no CS/SS/TS source gives a known Sr–Ag layered oxyhalide precursor.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing OSr₄Ag₂ octahedra, which increases structural ordering complexity [Target], and similar mixed connectivity appears in close analogues [SS1, SS4]. The Sr/Ag size mismatch is large by ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference], but the unit cell is not large, and layered oxyhalide analogues are crystalline under conventional conditions [CS1, SS1, SS4]. Thus the amorphization/poor-ordering risk is medium rather than high; insufficient direct amorphization evidence from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₃Ag₂(O₂I)₂ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.015 eV/atom [Target], comparable to experimentally accessible thermodynamic analogues [TS1, TS2, TS5]. The CS and SS evidence strongly supports layered Sr oxyhalide formation by sealed solid-state reaction, especially from SrO/SrI₂/transition-metal oxide mixtures [CS1, CS2, SS1]. The most important enabling factor is the close structural match to known Sr/Ca layered oxyhalides with OSr₄M₂ or OCa₄M₂ octahedral networks [SS1, SS4]. The most important limiting factor is Ag chemistry: stabilizing Ag in the required oxide/iodide coordination is not directly demonstrated in the provided references, and Ag oxide precursors can decompose or reduce [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful dry sealed-tube stoichiometry and likely optimization of oxygen/iodine chemical potentials.
