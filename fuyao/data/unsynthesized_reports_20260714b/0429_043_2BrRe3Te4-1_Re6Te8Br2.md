### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so the intrinsic thermodynamic score is near maximum. Similar materials in all three source classes are frequently on-hull or near-hull: WTe₂, Mo₃Te₄, ReSe₂, NbIrTe₄, and Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂ are reported with E_hull ≈ 0–0.002 eV/atom in SS/TS, while CS analogues are also mostly stable or slightly metastable [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature stability under Br/Te vapor pressure, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 requires vacuum sealing, a two-zone gradient, volatile TeBr₄/halogen chemistry, and multiple handling/heating steps, all of which match CVT precedents [CS1/CS4/SS1/TS1]. It avoids >1000 °C if the Re telluride CVT range is adapted to 900–980 °C [SS4/TS3]. The main uncertainty is whether Br incorporation requires higher halogen activity than standard TeBr₄ transport, which is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly proven.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
CVT improves kinetics because volatile halogen species transport refractory metals and chalcogens, as shown for WTe₂-like tellurides and Re chalcogenides [CS1/SS1/SS4/TS1/TS3]. Crystallization of layered phases by CVT is well supported [SS1, SS4]. Composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because the target has a low Br content and close Re chalcogen-halide analogues report phase/yield challenges [SS2].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The solid-state route uses conditions close to the structurally closest analogue: 900 °C, sealed fused-silica ampoule, dry N₂ grinding, slow cooling, and quenching [SS2]. It does not require high pressure or >1000 °C, but volatile Te/Br species and air-sensitive halide sources demand sealed, controlled-atmosphere processing [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. Multiple heating/cooling and possible regrinding steps lower the condition score [SS2, SS5/TS4].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are poor because Re is refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference], and close analogues needed long dwell times and controlled cooling [SS2]. The strongest positive is structural transferability from Re₃Tl(Se₂Cl)₂, which has Re₃ chalcogen-halide sheets in P-1 [SS2]. Phase purity remains uncertain because SS2 reports low-yield/phase sorting issues for related phases.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The Te-rich flux route still requires sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile Te/Br species, similar to sealed-tube telluride and Re chalcogenide routes [SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4]. It avoids high pressure and can likely operate near 950–1000 °C, as in NbIrTe₄ synthesis [SS5/TS4]. Complexity arises from slow cooling, possible decanting/removal of Te flux, and bromide handling [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten Te and bromide species should improve precursor contact relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and excess chalcogen is explicitly reported to stimulate Re chalcogenide transport [SS4/TS3]. However, Re remains diffusion-limited and the exact flux window for Re₃Te₄Br is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. The route is therefore kinetically better than pure solid-state synthesis but less directly validated than CVT.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Re, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for cation-disorder-driven amorphization in this target. The described Re₃Te₄Br formula is compact, and the target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell or explicit vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence. Layered chalcogenides can crystallize well by CVT in analogues [SS1, SS4], so amorphization risk is low but not zero.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re₃Te₄Br is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong: layered tellurides are routinely made by CVT [CS1/CS4/SS1/TS1/TS5], Re chalcogenides are made by halogen CVT [SS4/TS3], and a close Re₃ chalcogen-halide sheet compound exists in P-1 [SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is halogen-mediated transport, which can overcome the low mobility of refractory Re and promote layered crystal growth [SS4/TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is Br/Te chemical-potential control, since too little Br may give Re–Te binaries and too much halogen may favor volatile Re bromides or competing halide phases [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, CVT is the best-supported route, while sealed solid-state synthesis is a useful fallback for phase mapping.
