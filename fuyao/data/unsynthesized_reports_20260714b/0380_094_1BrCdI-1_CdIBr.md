### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0240 eV/atom, which lies within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range specified in the rubric. This is consistent with many low-energy halide analogues: HgBrI is 0.0105 eV/atom (CS1, TS2), TlCdI₃ is 0.0167 eV/atom (TS1), and CdI₂ polytypes are essentially on the hull (SS1–SS4). The material is therefore likely experimentally accessible either as a near-ground-state phase or as a low-energy metastable polymorph. Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not prove I/Br ordering or exclude competing disordered Cd(I,Br)₂ phases.

#### F2 — Route 1, solution evaporation

The mildness score is high because CdI₂ structural analogues crystallize from saturated or aqueous solution under room-temperature/ambient evaporation conditions (SS1–SS4). No high pressure, high temperature, or vacuum is required by analogy to SS1–SS4. The main deduction is toxicity of cadmium salts, which is a physicochemical safety issue **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for direct CdIBr solution synthesis, so CS and TS are neutral for this route-specific severity except as broader mixed-halide support.

#### F3 — Route 1, solution evaporation

Kinetics should be favorable because solution growth bypasses solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CdI₂ polytypes nucleate/crystallize readily from solution (SS1–SS4). Structural reconstruction is minimal because the target retains the edge-sharing CdX₆ octahedral layer motif of CdI₂, with Cd–I distances of 3.00 Å in the target versus 3.01–3.02 Å in CdI₂ analogues (SS1–SS4). The uncertainty is whether Br and I order into the CdIBr P3m1 arrangement rather than forming a disordered mixed-halide Cd(I,Br)₂ phase **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The route avoids extreme temperature because related halides form at moderate temperatures, such as CuHgSI at 553 K (TS4) and Te₃(PdBr)₄ at 700 K (CS5). However, evacuated or sealed ampoules are commonly used for related halides (CS5, TS4), causing a controlled-atmosphere/sealed-system deduction. Volatile halides and toxic Cd salts add further deductions **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS for sealed solid-state synthesis of mixed CdIBr specifically; SS mainly supports structural compatibility, not this route.

#### F3 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Binary halide precursors should be substantially more reactive than refractory oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**, and direct mixed-halide formation is supported by HgBrI solid-state synthesis (CS1, TS2). Moderate-temperature sealed-tube syntheses in CS5 and TS4 support manageable diffusion and reaction kinetics. Structural reconstruction is limited because CdI₂-like sheets already exist in the closest structural analogues (SS1–SS4). The main uncertainty is whether halide ordering occurs during annealing or whether kinetic trapping gives a random mixed-halide solid solution.

#### F2 — Route 3, chemical vapor transport

CVT/gas-phase methods require evacuated sealed ampoules, as seen for Cd₂AsCl₂ (CS3), BiSeI (CS4), and Hg₃S₂ICl (TS5). The temperature is moderate rather than extreme, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile-halide environment, and Cd toxicity justify the deductions **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS gives insufficient evidence for CVT because the closest CdI₂ structural analogues were solution-grown, not vapor-grown.

#### F3 — Route 3, chemical vapor transport

Halide vapor transport is plausible because several related halides are grown from gas phase or chemical transport in sealed ampoules (CS3, CS4, TS5). Kinetics for crystal growth can be good, but composition control is less favorable because I- and Br-containing species may transport at different rates **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS5 reports by-products when stoichiometry deviates in a halide/chalcogenide-halide system, supporting concern about narrow composition windows. The uncertainty is that no provided source directly demonstrates CdI₂/CdBr₂ co-transport to ordered CdIBr.

#### F2 — Route 4, melt growth / Bridgman

Melt growth is supported by InTeBr grown from the melt at 520 °C using Bridgman (CS2) and by Tl-containing iodide analogues grown by Bridgman/melt methods (TS1, TS3). The temperature is below 1000 °C, so severity is not from heat but from sealed ampoule, volatile halides, and toxic Cd salts **[Reasonable Inference]**. TS3 also indicates dry glovebox/inert handling for iodide melt/anneal synthesis, supporting controlled-atmosphere concerns. SS provides structural support but insufficient direct evidence for melt synthesis of CdIBr.

#### F3 — Route 4, melt growth / Bridgman

A melt or partial melt should provide fast mass transport and good crystallization kinetics **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with successful melt/Bridgman growth of InTeBr (CS2) and TlCdI₃/TlMnI₃-type iodides (TS1, TS3). The target’s CdI₂-derived layer should be structurally accessible because the CdI₂ analogues are extremely close in geometry (SS1–SS4). The limiting kinetic issue is phase selection: TS3 reports repeated annealing/powdering for purity in related iodides, and CS2 notes incongruent melting concerns for InTeBr, so melt processing may need careful thermal control. Composition drift from volatile halides remains uncertain **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Cd, so the cation-size-mismatch check is not triggered. The described P3m1 CdIBr cell is simple and layered, not a >40-atom complex unit cell, and it contains only edge-sharing CdI₃Br₃ octahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity. This low amorphization risk is consistent with easy crystallization of CdI₂ layered polytypes from solution (SS1–SS4). Remaining uncertainty is not amorphization but possible stacking/polytype disorder, which SS2–SS5 show can occur in CdI₂-related materials.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CdIBr is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase, because its E_hull is only 0.0240 eV/atom and related halides with similar small hull energies have been synthesized (CS1, TS1, TS2). The strongest support comes from SS1–SS4: CdI₂ has almost the same layered edge-sharing octahedral framework and crystallizes easily from solution. CS and TS sources further support mixed-halide and sealed-tube/vapor/melt routes, especially HgBrI solid-state formation (CS1, TS2) and halide ampoule growth (CS3–CS5, TS4–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the structural match to CdI₂-type sheets, which should make the CdX₂ framework easy to form. The most critical limiting factor is I/Br ordering and stoichiometry control, because mixed-halide volatility, possible disordered solid solutions, and CdI₂ polytypism may complicate isolation of the exact P3m1 CdIBr phase.
