### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.017 eV/atom places it well within the commonly synthesizable metastability window, giving a high score of 95. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are reported as synthesized, including CeMo₆Se₈ at 0.024 eV/atom (TS-1), Nb₃NiSe₆ at 0.0119 eV/atom (TS-2), NbSe₂ at 0.024–0.025 eV/atom (TS-3, TS-5), and TaSe₂ at 0.0317 eV/atom (TS-4). Chemically and structurally similar compounds also include many hull-stable or near-hull selenides, such as Ta₂PdSe₆ and Ta₂Pd₃Se₈ at 0 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-5, SS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports VW₃Se₈, so the score reflects thermodynamic feasibility rather than demonstrated synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated sealed-tube conditions, which are broadly supported by CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-5. The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed ampoule operation and volatile/toxic Se, both recurring in CS/SS/TS selenide syntheses. The proposed temperature of 850–950 °C avoids the >1000 °C penalty, although related materials sometimes require up to 1050 K or 1075 K (CS-1) and 600–1100 °C for NbSe₂ polymorphs (TS-5). Uncertainty remains because W-rich ternary ordering may require a higher temperature than proposed [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental W and V powders are relatively low-activity precursors compared with solution or salt precursors, and the CS/SS/TS solid-state examples often require days to weeks, e.g., 10 days for Ta₂PdSe₆ (CS-1 / SS-3), 7–14 days for Ta₂PtSe₇ (CS-2), and 7 days for Ta₂NiSe₅ (SS-5). W’s very high melting point implies slow cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference], even though analogous W/Mo/Nb/Ta selenides form below the cation melting point by reaction with Se and/or vapor transport (SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). Full reconstruction from elemental powders and possible inhomogeneity justify a low-to-moderate kinetic score; impurity and repeated-grinding issues are specifically noted in CS-1/SS-3. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct VW₃Se₈ kinetics, so this remains the largest uncertainty for Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 adds halogen-assisted CVT, which increases complexity relative to direct reaction because it requires prereaction, transport-agent addition, sealed vacuum ampoules, and a controlled temperature gradient. These features are directly supported by Ta₂Pd₃Se₈/Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂ CVT using Br₂ and 1000–900 K gradients (CS-5, SS-1), MoSe₂ vapor transport (SS-4), and Nb/Ta/W AB₂ chalcogenide transport with I₂/Br₂ at 900–700 °C (TS-3, TS-4). The use of volatile/toxic halogens and Se accounts for major deductions. No high-pressure or >1400 °C condition is supported or required by the analogues.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT improves effective mass transport by forming volatile metal-halide/chalcogen species, which is why it is widely used for layered dichalcogenide single crystals (SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). It is also demonstrated for more complex layered Ta–Pd–Se and Co–Ta–Pd–Se phases (CS-5, SS-1), supporting transferability beyond simple MX₂. The kinetic score is still only moderate because V and W may transport at different rates, creating stoichiometry-control risk [Reasonable Inference], and TS-3/TS-4 note that transport-agent choice matters. Insufficient direct evidence exists for V–W–Se CVT speciation, so cation ratio control is the main uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is more severe because it likely requires two-stage annealing and may exceed 1000 °C. This is supported by pellet sintering of RExMo₆Se₈ at 950 °C then 1250 °C (TS-1) and Pu/Am/Np molybdenum selenides at 1140 °C after an initial heating step (CS-4). It also uses sealed vacuum ampoules and volatile Se, as in TS-2 and TS-5. The route remains pressure-simple because no CS/SS/TS analogue requires high pressure.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Binary selenide precursors should improve chemical compatibility and reduce the need to create all M–Se bonds from dense elemental W/V [Reasonable Inference], analogous to rare-earth or actinide selenide precursors in TS-1 and CS-4. However, solid-state cation redistribution across W/V selenide domains can still be slow, and the analogues require high temperatures and long anneals (TS-1, CS-4). The structural score is moderate because WSe₂/VSe₂-like layers are related to the target molybdenite-derived motif [Reasonable Inference], but competing binary phases and polymorph retention are realistic risks, as seen in TS-2 and TS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly validates binary-selenide conversion to VW₃Se₈, so this route is less favored than CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure is crystalline, layered, and relatively simple: a molybdenite-derived VW₃Se₈ sheet with 6-coordinate V/W and 3-coordinate Se, matching the general connectivity of MoSe₂/NbSe₂-like layered analogues (SS-2, SS-4, TS-3, TS-5). Similar V–Se and W–Se bond lengths in the target description suggest the cation-size mismatch is not large enough to trigger severe amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence for a large >40-atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing framework in the target; CS/SS/TS do not provide contrary evidence. The remaining uncertainty is that orthorhombic cation ordering in Pmm2 may require slow crystallization, but that is more a kinetics/phase-control issue than an amorphization issue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VW₃Se₈ is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.017 eV/atom, and many CS/SS/TS analogues with comparable hull energies are experimentally synthesized (CS-1, CS-2, TS-1–TS-5). The analogue support is strong for layered selenide chemistry and vacuum sealed-tube/CVT methods, especially from MoSe₂/NbSe₂/TaSe₂/W-containing chalcogenide transport examples (SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is that molybdenite-derived transition-metal selenide sheets are synthetically well established under evacuated sealed-tube or CVT conditions (SS-2, SS-4, TS-3, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is cation ordering and stoichiometry control between V and W, especially because W is refractory and V/W halide transport rates may differ [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material should be synthesizable with moderate difficulty, with CVT after prereaction being the most promising route for crystalline material.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy from CS/SS/TS |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Se volatility and loss at high temperature | High | Use evacuated sealed silica/quartz ampoules as in CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-5, TS-5; use slight chalcogen excess as reported for Ta₂PtSe₇ and Ta₂NiSe₅ crystal growth (CS-2, SS-5). |
| 2 | All | Competing binary phases such as WSe₂/VSe₂ or other transition-metal selenides | High | Use long anneals, regrinding, and phase checks; impurity/homogeneity issues in Ta₂PdSe₆-type reactions motivate repeated grinding (CS-1 / SS-3), and trace transition-metal selenide impurities are noted in NbSe₂-intercalate syntheses (TS-2). |
| 3 | All | Slow W/V cation diffusion and ordering | High | Use fine powders, pelletization, and long annealing; pelletization is used in TS-1, TS-2, CS-4, while long holds are common in CS-1, CS-2, SS-4. CVT can reduce diffusion limitations by vapor transport (CS-5, SS-1, TS-3, TS-4). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Inhomogeneous elemental reaction from refractory W | Medium–High | Apply staged heating and intermediate grinding, following the repeated grinding/heating logic reported for Ta₂PdSe₆-related synthesis (CS-1 / SS-3). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Halogen transport may fractionate V and W, shifting V:W ratio | Medium–High | Screen I₂ vs Br₂ and tune gradient/loading; transport-agent dependence is reported for Nb/Ta/Mo/W chalcogenides (TS-3, TS-4), and Br₂ loading is specified for Ta–Pd–Se CVT (CS-5, SS-1). |
| 6 | Route 2 | Crystal growth may be sensitive to temperature gradient and transport-agent amount | Medium | Use 1000–900 K-type gradients from Ta–Pd–Se/Co–Ta–Pd–Se CVT (CS-5, SS-1) or 900–700 °C AB₂ selenide gradients (TS-3, TS-4) as starting points; adjust after powder XRD/EDS analysis [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Binary selenide precursors may remain unreacted | Medium | Use two-stage heat treatment and pelletization, as in RExMo₆Se₈ and actinide Mo₆Se₈ syntheses (TS-1, CS-4); include regrinding between anneals [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 8 | All | Polymorph or metastable phase retention | Medium | Test slow cooling vs quenching; NbSe₂ polymorph synthesis shows temperature-dependent phases and quenching limitations (TS-5), so cooling protocol should be treated as a control variable. |
| 9 | All | Ampoule safety due to Se/halogen vapor pressure | Medium | Use conservative Se excess, staged low-temperature prereaction, adequate ampoule volume, and high-quality quartz/silica tubes; sealed-tube approaches are standard across CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5. |
