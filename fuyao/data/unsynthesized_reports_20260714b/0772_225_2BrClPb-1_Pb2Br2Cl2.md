### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0350 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability ≤0.05 eV/atom window. CS gives a strong positive signal because SnClI is on hull and BiSeI is near hull (CS-1, CS-2). SS gives a strong positive signal because layered PbI₂ polytypes lie essentially on the hull (SS-2 to SS-5). TS gives a positive but slightly more cautionary signal because thermodynamic analogues include metastable halides up to ~0.0617 eV/atom, but several are experimentally made (TS-1 to TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because CS-1 uses aqueous room-temperature dropwise addition, SS-1 uses cooling of aqueous component salts, and TS-2 uses boiling-water dissolution followed by slow cooling. No high pressure, high temperature, or controlled atmosphere is required in those references. The main severity penalty is toxicity from Pb salts, and the remaining uncertainty is that PbClBr may require stricter halide activity control than the simple analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution-phase kinetics should be fast because ions are mobile and precipitation/crystallization occurs without long-range solid diffusion; this is supported by CS-1 and TS-2. SS-1 and TS-2 show Pb-halide crystallization from aqueous solution, supporting rapid nucleation and growth. The uncertainty is structural ordering of Cl and Br into the orthorhombic Pmn2₁ sheet, since CS-1 reports phase mixtures when halide amount is varied.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains mild because SS-2, SS-3, and SS-5 report room-temperature gel growth under ordinary conditions, while SS-4 uses only 273–323 K with thermostat control. The route receives penalties for multiple operations—gel preparation, setting, diffusion, and growth—and for toxic Pb salts. SS provides strong route-specific evidence; CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for gel synthesis, so their signal for this route is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Gel diffusion slows mass transport but enables controlled supersaturation, and SS PbI₂ crystals grow within two days to weeks (SS-2 to SS-5). The layered Pb-halide structural motif strongly supports crystallization of a related 2D sheet. The main uncertainty is that gel-grown PbI₂ analogues are single-halide iodides, while the target requires Cl/Br ordering and may form compositional gradients.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is more severe because TS halide syntheses commonly use vacuum- or Ar-sealed ampoules, dry handling, temperature gradients, and slow cooling (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). Temperatures remain moderate and far below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies. The penalties arise from sealed/vacuum handling, possible halide volatility, and Pb toxicity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solid-state/melt-assisted kinetics are reasonable for Pb halides because they are low-melting and not refractory, and TS-5 demonstrates halide salt reaction at 523 K with slow cooling. However, diffusion and ordering are still less favorable than in solution, especially if the reaction remains partly solid rather than molten. SS layered PbI₂ analogues support the structural motif, but no source directly demonstrates PbCl₂ + PbBr₂ forming ordered PbClBr, so uncertainty is moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check does not apply. The target formula and described crystallographic sites indicate a small ordered cell rather than a >40 atom complex cell. SS PbI₂ and CS SnClI show that low-dimensional heavy-metal halides can crystallize under mild conditions rather than amorphize, while TS gives insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PbClBr is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0350 eV/atom, which is within the range of experimentally accessible halides supported by TS analogues. The strongest support comes from SS layered Pb-halide compounds and CS mixed group-14 halides, especially the aqueous SnClI route and PbI₂ gel-growth analogues. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of Pb²⁺/halide species in solution, which should allow Pb–Cl/Br coordination to assemble at low temperature. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: separate PbCl₂/PbBr₂, hydrolyzed Pb phases, or halide-rich plumbate salts may compete if Cl⁻/Br⁻ activities are not tuned. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with solution crystallization preferred over sealed solid-state reaction.
