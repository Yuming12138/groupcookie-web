### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the hull at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the provided stability rubric. Chemically similar Eu₂H₃Br is also on the hull and Eu₂H₃Cl is only 0.0049 eV/atom above hull (CS-1, CS-2), while several structurally/thermodynamically similar rare-earth hydrides are also on or essentially on the hull (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct H/N ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it likely requires sealed Ta ampoules, inert handling, and air-sensitive hydride/nitride precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is not extreme because the closest ampoule analogues form around 850–900 °C (CS-2, CS-4). SS and TS provide insufficient direct synthesis evidence for this exact route and are treated as neutral 50 for Route 1 condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from a strong structural analogue: Eu₂H₃Cl and Eu₂H₃Br share P-3m1 rare-earth hydride-halide topology with similar 10-coordinate rare-earth sites (CS-1, CS-2). The limiting kinetic factor is likely nitrogen mobility from refractory GdN and H retention from GdH₂ [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide hydride polyhedral similarity but insufficient direct evidence for GdN/GdH₂ reaction kinetics, so their route-specific kinetic signal is neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids very high temperature but introduces reactive NH₃/H₂ gas handling, toxicity, flammability, and pressure-control issues [Reasonable Inference]. H₂ autoclave hydrogenation up to 10 MPa is supported for Eu–Pd hydrides (CS-5), but NH₃ nitridation is not directly shown in CS/SS/TS. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for ammonolysis or nitridation-hydridation and are neutral 50 for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Gd, GdH₂, or GdN can reorganize into the ordered Gd₂NH₃ P-3m1 phase rather than stopping at binary GdN/GdH₂ [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 shows hydrogen uptake into rare-earth intermetallic systems can occur, but it also notes multiple hydride phases and phase-control difficulty. There is insufficient SS/TS evidence for direct gas-phase nitrogen incorporation into this target, so their route-specific kinetic signal is neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest temperature profile because structurally/thermodynamically similar REAlH₆ compounds were prepared mechanochemically at room temperature under Ar/H₂ (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). It still requires glovebox handling, air-sensitive salts/hydrides/nitrides, and possibly H₂ pressure. The main uncertainty is whether the target survives post-milling annealing without decomposing or losing H [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Mechanochemistry improves precursor activity and reduces diffusion lengths, supported by RE trichloride + NaAlH₄ metathesis examples (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). However, those analogues also report low product yields, NaCl/Al byproducts, and partial decomposition, so structural reconstruction and phase purity are serious concerns. The specific substitution of AlH₆ chemistry by nitride/hydride chemistry is a [Reasonable Inference], not directly proven.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Gd, so cation-radius disorder is not expected [Reasonable Inference]. The structure contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion and is directly stated for the target; similar mixed sharing appears in Eu₂H₃Cl/Br analogues (CS-1, CS-2). The compact P-3m1 formula suggests a small unit cell, but exact crystallographic atom count is not provided, so the C2 non-trigger is a [Reasonable Inference].
