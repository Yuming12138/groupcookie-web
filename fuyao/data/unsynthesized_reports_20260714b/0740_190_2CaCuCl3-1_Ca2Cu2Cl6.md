### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.022 eV/atom, which is inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. Thermodynamically similar chlorides with comparable E_hull values are reported as synthesized, including BaPdCl4 at 0.019 eV/atom and BaBrCl at 0.048 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). Chemical and structural analogues are often on the hull or nearly on the hull, supporting a high score (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy does not prove the specific P-31m 2D Cu–Cu bonded polymorph is the experimentally selected polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures near 550 °C, directly supported by phase-pure SrZnCl4/BaZnCl4 sealed-ampoule synthesis (CS4, SS2, SS3). The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, volatile chloride retention, and moisture-sensitive precursors, all consistent with the ampoule chloride literature (CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1). No high pressure or >1000 °C heating is required, so the route is not extreme. Uncertainty remains in CuCl volatility and Cu(I) stability.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chlorides are more reactive than oxide precursors, and CS4/SS2/SS3 show that such salts can form phase-pure ternary chlorides at 550 °C. Ion transport should be favorable because chlorides can partially melt or flux at moderate temperatures, consistent with SS1 and TS1. Structural reconstruction is the weak point because Zn/Pd analogues do not reproduce the target’s Cu–Cu trigonal-pyramidal sheet. Therefore, kinetics are rated good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 can operate at low temperature if AlCl3/GaCl3 flux is used, as shown by CS1 at 100–125 °C. However, the method requires glovebox loading, sealed ampoules, volatile/corrosive fluxes, and difficult flux separation, all directly supported by CS1. These penalties make condition severity worse than the direct route despite the lower temperature. The source evidence is indirect because CS1 involves complex chlorometallate/chalcogenide products rather than CaCuCl3.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth has excellent kinetic advantages because molten chlorides provide high ion mobility and strong chloride complexation; CS1 demonstrates crystal growth in viscous chloride melts. Diffusion limitations are minimal in a liquid flux, and slow cooling has precedent in SS1/TS1. The structural-reconstruction score is limited because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates this exact Ca–Cu sheet topology in flux. Thus, kinetics are strong but phase selectivity is uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

CS5 and TS3 show that some chloride crystals can be grown by mild solution evaporation or cooling from aqueous chloride media, so the route has low thermal and equipment severity. The main penalty is chemical handling: concentrated HCl and copper chlorides are hazardous, and Cu(I) control is a reasonable concern for the target. Insufficient evidence from SS supports this route for the target structural family, so its mildness should not be overinterpreted. The route is experimentally easy but chemically risky.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution transport gives excellent precursor activity and eliminates solid-state diffusion barriers, as shown for KCuCl3 in concentrated HCl (CS5) and K–Pb chlorides from aqueous cooling (TS3). However, structural reconstruction toward an anhydrous CaCuCl3 layered phase is poorly supported. Ca2+ hydration, hydrolysis, and alternate chlorocuprate formation are likely complications by physicochemical inference. Therefore, kinetics are mixed: fast transport but weak target-phase selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 resembles established Bridgman/slow-cooling chloride syntheses for BaZnCl4, NaScCl4, and BaPdCl4 (SS1, CS3/SS5, TS1). It requires evacuated ampoules, controlled cooling, dry handling, and volatile chloride containment, giving significant severity deductions. Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, so the route avoids extreme thermal conditions. It is best suited for crystal growth rather than rapid powder synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

Melt growth offers excellent diffusion and homogenization, supported by NaScCl4 and BaPdCl4 melt/Bridgman syntheses (CS3/SS5, TS1). Controlled cooling can help select ordered chloride polymorphs, as shown for BaZnCl4 polymorphs (SS1). The target’s unique Cu–Cu bonded 2D layer is still not directly represented, so structural selection remains uncertain. Kinetically, the route is strong but experimentally less forgiving.
