### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the convex hull and justifying a score of 98/100. This is reinforced by on-hull analogues MoO₂Br₂, VOBr₂, and VOCl, which are chemically or structurally related low-dimensional oxyhalides (CS1 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2; SS5 / TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Br/Cl ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives 65/100 because it avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires vacuum sealing, volatile halide/oxyhalide transport, and moisture-sensitive/toxic reagents. The temperature and vacuum requirements are directly supported by MoO₂Br₂ CVT at 820 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), while the temperature-gradient transport concept is supported by VOCl and NbSeBr₃ (SS5 / TS3 / TS5). The main uncertainty is the optimal Br/Cl vapor chemical potential for a mixed-halide phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives 80/100 because vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion, and close analogues form ordered 2D oxyhalide crystals. MoO₂Br₂ is a 2D Mo oxybromide grown by apparent transport (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), and VOCl/VOBr₂ are 2D oxyhalide sheets with similar anion coordination motifs (SS2 / SS5 / TS2 / TS3). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether mixed Br/Cl ordering equilibrates before competing Mo oxyhalides crystallize [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives 55/100 because it likely requires vacuum/inert sealed-tube processing, volatile halide containment, air-sensitive Mo halides, and possibly regrinding/annealing. Sealed high-temperature solid-state synthesis is supported by LaCrOS₂ in evacuated silica ampoules (CS5) and multi-stage Mo oxide synthesis under inert/sealed conditions (SS3). The exact MoO₃ + MoCl₃ + MoBr₃ route is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so its practical severity has higher uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives 50/100 because bulk solid-state diffusion involving Mo is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], and the structure must be assembled from separate oxide and halide precursors. Multi-day high-temperature treatments and anneals are seen in CS5 and SS3, indicating that related solid-state systems can require long equilibration. Halide volatility may help transport, but it also increases stoichiometry-control difficulty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives 65/100 because its temperature is mild, supported by 150 °C MoSeOCl₇ solvothermal synthesis in SOCl₂ (SS4), but it requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile/toxic thionyl halide media. Low-temperature oxyhalide processing is supported by SS4 and SS2 / TS2, yet mixed SOCl₂/SOBr₂ chemistry is extrapolated. The main severity comes from pressure, corrosion, and moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 receives 77/100 because solution/solvothermal media should provide high precursor mobility and fast halide exchange [Reasonable Inference], and SS4 demonstrates nearly quantitative Mo–O–Cl chemistry at 150 °C. However, SS4 forms a zero-dimensional cluster rather than a 2D layered solid, so the crystallization pathway is less certain. This route may be kinetically fast but phase-selectivity-limited.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has low amorphization tendency because it contains a single cation and a simple low-dimensional oxyhalide motif [Reasonable Inference from target formula and structure]. Ordered 2D oxyhalide analogues exist for MoO₂Br₂, VOBr₂, and VOCl (CS1 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2; SS5 / TS3), supporting crystallization rather than glass formation. Remaining uncertainty comes from possible stacking disorder or poor van der Waals crystal quality, which is explicitly noted for MoO₂Br₂ (CS1 / SS1 / TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MoOBrCl is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related oxyhalide or halide phases are also on the hull (CS1 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2; SS5 / TS3). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through sealed-ampoule vapor transport: MoO₂Br₂ is the closest Mo–O–Br analogue and forms by apparent CVT at 820 °C (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), while VOCl confirms that 2D oxychloride sheets can be grown by vapor transport (SS5 / TS3). The most critical enabling factor is halide/oxyhalide volatility, which permits crystal growth below refractory Mo diffusion limits, as seen in CVT/gradient analogues (CS1 / SS5 / TS5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the correct 1:1 Br:Cl chemical potential without forming separate Mo–O–Br, Mo–O–Cl, or higher-valent Mo oxyhalide phases [Reasonable Inference]. Moisture sensitivity and weakly bonded layered crystals are additional practical concerns, supported by the MoO₂Br₂ and MoSeOCl₇ reports (CS1 / SS4).
