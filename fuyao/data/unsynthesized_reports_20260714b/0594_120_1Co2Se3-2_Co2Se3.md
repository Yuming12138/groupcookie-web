### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0460 eV/atom, which is inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. The closest chemical/structural/thermodynamic analogue, Co₃Se₄, has E_hull ≈0.0458 eV/atom and is experimentally accessible by sealed-tube reaction, supporting that this metastability range is synthesizable in Co–Se chemistry. [CS1, SS1, TS1] The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a different Pmm2 2D polymorph, so the thermodynamic score does not guarantee phase selectivity. [SS1; target structure]

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed-ampoule route is penalized because Co–Se analogues require >1000 °C, evacuated sealed tubes, volatile Se containment, quenching, and multi-step annealing. [CS1, CS3, TS1] It is not penalized for GPa pressure because the Co₃Se₄/Co₉Se₈ routes use conventional sealed ampoules. [CS1, CS3] The main uncertainty is whether the target Pmm2 sheet phase forms on cooling or transforms into more common Co–Se phases. [CS1, CS3, SS1]

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Elemental Co has relatively slow solid-state reaction kinetics compared with soluble precursors, but molten/vapor Se improves reaction contact at elevated temperatures. [CS1, CS3; Reasonable Inference] The synthesis temperatures used for Co–Se analogues are high enough for diffusion but the repeated annealing and regrinding steps imply nontrivial ordering kinetics. [CS1, TS1] Structural reconstruction is the weak point because the closest Co₃Se₄ analogue is not the same 2D Pmm2 topology. [SS1]

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Hydrothermal/solvothermal selenization is mild in temperature and avoids high vacuum/high pressure, which greatly reduces condition severity. [CS5, SS3] It still receives deductions for multistep precursor formation/selenization and toxic Se/fluoride/reducing reagents. [CS5; reducing reagents = Reasonable Inference] The evidence is less direct for Co₂Se₃ because TS provides insufficient hydrothermal support and is scored neutral for this route. [TS: insufficient evidence]

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solution-phase Co salts and reactive Se species should give much higher precursor activity than elemental Co metal. [CS5; Co substitution = Reasonable Inference] Nanoscale precursor conversion and hydrothermal sheet growth reduce diffusion limitations, as shown for sheet-like NiSe₂. [CS5] The uncertainty is phase specificity: hydrothermal conditions may produce CoSe, CoSe₂, amorphous Co–Se, or mixed hydroxide/selenide unless Se activity and redox potential are controlled. [CS5; competing phases = Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3.**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized for >5 GPa conditions, possible >1400 °C operation, encapsulation, and specialized apparatus. [CS2, CS4] These routes are experimentally validated for some transition-metal selenides but not for the target 2D Co₂Se₃ phase. [CS2, CS4; insufficient SS/TS evidence] The severity score is therefore low despite plausible access to metastable phases. [CS2, CS4]

**F3 — Route 3.**  
HPHT conditions can overcome diffusion barriers and quenching can retain metastable products. [CS2, CS4] However, the high-pressure analogues are dense pyrite/marcasite-type structures, which may be structurally mismatched to a 2D sheet target. [CS2, CS4; target structure] Thus the kinetic pathway may preferentially yield dense Co–Se phases rather than Pmm2 Co₂Se₃. [Reasonable Inference based on CS2/CS4]

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger. [target; insufficient contrary CS/SS/TS evidence] The described connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, unlike Co₃Se₄ which has edge/corner/face-sharing octahedra. [CS1, SS1, TS1] Therefore the amorphization risk is low, although low-temperature hydrothermal products may still be poorly crystalline if post-annealing is omitted. [CS5; Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Co₂Se₃ at E_hull = 0.046 eV/atom is most likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a strongly unstable phase; this metastability level is comparable to experimentally synthesized Co₃Se₄. [CS1, SS1, TS1] The analogues strongly support elemental sealed-tube Co–Se synthesis, while hydrothermal support is more indirect but attractive because the target is two-dimensional and sheet-like NiSe₂ has been made hydrothermally. [CS1, CS3, CS5, TS1] The most critical enabling factor is maintaining selenium chemical potential in a sealed or chemically buffered environment. [CS1, CS3, CS5] The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among many possible Co–Se compositions/polymorphs, especially because the target Pmm2 sheet topology is not the same as the closest bulk Co–Se analogues. [CS1, CS3, SS1] Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with hydrothermal selenization best for accessing the 2D topology and sealed-tube annealing best for crystallinity and compositional equilibration. [CS5, CS1, CS3]
