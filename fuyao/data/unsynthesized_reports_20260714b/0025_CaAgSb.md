## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** CaAgSb at **0.0500 eV/atom** is near the ground-state boundary and receives a high but not perfect score [Target]. TS analogues demonstrate that phases near this hull distance can be synthesized, especially CaAuBi at 0.0478 eV/atom and Ba11Cd6Sb12 at 0.0395 eV/atom [TS-1, TS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition, because TS-2 reports non-uniform products and moisture-sensitive decomposition for a related antimonide family [TS-2].

**F2 — Route 1.** The sealed-tube route avoids >1000 °C conditions but requires controlled atmosphere, sealed handling, long heat treatments, and air-sensitive/toxic elements [CS-2, SS-2, SS-3, Reasonable Inference]. The score is moderate because this is standard intermetallic synthesis rather than extreme-pressure chemistry [CS-2, SS-3].

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are helped by relatively low-melting Ca/Ag/Sb chemistry and by Pnma structural precedent in NaCdAs [CS-2, Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in SrAg4Sb2 and CaAgBi imply ordering/diffusion still need time [SS-2, SS-3].

**F2 — Route 2.** Arc-melting gives strong homogenization but penalizes condition severity because it uses very high local temperature, controlled Ar, remelting, and sealed annealing [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5]. The limiting concern is Ca/Sb mass loss under melt conditions, which is inferred from their volatility/reactivity rather than directly reported for CaAgSb [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** Melt processing gives high precursor activity and diffusion scores because CS analogues are homogenized by repeated remelting [CS-1, CS-3]. Structural transfer is weaker because most arc-melted CS analogues are hexagonal RAgGe phases rather than Pnma CaAgSb [CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5].

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux growth stays below 1000 °C but requires vacuum sealing, slow cooling, decanting, and toxic/air-sensitive materials [SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-5]. Its condition score equals Route 1 because both require sealed controlled synthesis, but Route 3 adds flux-handling complexity [SS-1, TS-1].

**F3 — Route 3.** Flux strongly improves kinetics by dissolving reactants and enabling slow crystal growth, as shown in Ca2CdSb2, Ba/Sr–Cd–Sb, and CaAuBi syntheses [SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-5]. The score is not higher because SS-1 explicitly indicates stoichiometric reactions failed and flux optimization was required [SS-1].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior, so direct source evidence is **insufficient from CS/SS/TS** and neutral source signal = 50. The target’s small Pnma ABX cell, lack of reported mixed polyhedral sharing, and crystalline Pnma analogue NaCdAs support low amorphization risk [Target, CS-2, Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is the ambiguous ionic-radius check for Ca/Ag in an intermetallic/Zintl-like environment [Reasonable Inference].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaAgSb is best interpreted as a **near-ground-state or low-lying metastable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is good: CS provides an isostructural-looking Pnma ABX precedent, SS provides Ca–Ag–pnictide and Ag–Sb antimonide routes, and TS shows near-hull Ca/antimonide-like phases can be synthesized [CS-2, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is access to liquid or near-liquid reaction pathways by flux or melt, which improves diffusion and ordering [CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Ca/Ag/Sb stoichiometry under sealed high-temperature conditions because Ca is reactive and Sb is volatile/toxic [SS-3, CS-2, Reasonable Inference]. The evidence is mildly contradictory: CS arc-melted Ag germanides look easy, while SS/TS antimonides often need sealed tubes, flux, and slow cooling; SS/TS are weighted more heavily because they contain Ca/Sr/Ba and Sb-bearing chemistry closer to CaAgSb [CS-1, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1].
