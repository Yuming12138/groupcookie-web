### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored at the top of the requested stability range. TS analogues include several E_hull = 0 nitrides or diazenides, including MnMoN₂, CaN₂, SrN₂, and MgMoN₂ (TS1–TS4), supporting that nitrogen-rich or octahedral nitride frameworks can be thermodynamic ground states. CS and SS include both metastable and stable nitrides, but they do not directly determine the target’s hull; therefore, direct CS/SS evidence for F1 is insufficient and treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for NH₃ atmosphere, high-pressure volatile NH₃ containment, and reactive NaNH₂/NH₃ chemistry because CS1 and CS5 explicitly use 6 kbar NH₃ autoclaves. It avoids the >5 GPa penalty because 6 kbar is only 0.6 GPa, and temperatures of 400–600 °C are moderate (CS1, CS5). The main uncertainty is whether ammonothermal conditions can raise nitrogen chemical potential enough to form MnN₂ rather than Mn₃N₂, since CS1 directly reports Mn₃N₂ rather than MnN₂.

#### F3 — Route 1

MnI₂/NaNH₂/NH₃ are reactive and already produce Mn nitrides, supporting a moderately high precursor activity score (CS1). However, CS1 reports long reaction times from weeks to months, indicating slow crystallization/ordering. Structural reconstruction from salt/amide precursors into corundum-like MnN₂ with N–N units is substantial, so kinetics are not scored as easy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives a large severity penalty because TS2/TS4 use multianvil pressures up to 12 GPa and glovebox handling. It also involves hazardous azide precursors, which TS2/TS4 identify as controlled decomposition precursors requiring careful handling. Temperature is not severe by the provided rubric because the relevant range is 450–800 °C in TS2/TS4.

#### F3 — Route 2

Azides are highly activated nitrogen reservoirs, and TS2/TS4 demonstrate that controlled azide decomposition can produce MN₂ phases with N–N units. The short reaction times of 15–20 min in TS2/TS4 support relatively favorable reaction kinetics once high pressure is applied. The uncertainty is Mn-specific: Mn may form competing lower nitrides such as Mn₃N₂ known from CS1, so pressure/temperature must suppress competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from ambient-pressure equipment and moderate temperatures because TS1, SS2, and SS3 use tube furnaces or flow reactors at 625–800 °C. It is penalized for flowing NH₃ and possible multi-step precursor preparation, especially because SS3 describes a three-step precursor method. Evidence is insufficient from CS for this exact route to binary MnN₂; CS1 instead supports high-pressure ammonothermal Mn nitride formation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Ammonolysis is proven for Mn-containing ternary nitrides such as MnMoN₂ and MnWN₂ (TS1, SS2), but SS2 reports persistent impurity phases and TS1 reports a narrow temperature window. These observations lower the structural reconstruction and phase-control aspects. The biggest uncertainty is whether ambient-pressure NH₃ can stabilize N–N bonded MnN₂ rather than lower Mn nitrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is severe because SS5 uses about 10 GPa and 1800 K with DAC laser heating. It is also penalized for sealed high-pressure gas loading. It avoids toxic/air-sensitive precursor penalties because Mn metal and N₂ gas are simpler precursors than azides or NaNH₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

SS5 proves that direct metal + nitrogen reactions can form 3d transition-metal nitrides, but dense metal plus N₂ has lower precursor activity than azide or amide chemistry. High laser temperature improves diffusion, but the small reaction volume and complete crystallization from elements make phase selection difficult. Evidence is insufficient from SS5 for this exact MnN₂ corundum-like phase, so the route is considered exploratory.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation size disorder does not trigger amorphization risk. The described R-3m structure has one Mn site and one N site with edge-sharing MnN₆ octahedra, so no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network is described. The compact corundum-like topology gives low amorphization risk, but the N–N bonded sublattice still leaves some uncertainty during rapid quenching [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthetic analogue is not the chemically closest Mn₃N₂ route alone, but the high-pressure diazenide chemistry of CaN₂/SrN₂/BaN₂, because the target contains an explicit N–N bond and has MN₂ stoichiometry (TS2, TS4). Mn nitrides are experimentally accessible under nitrogen-rich ammonothermal conditions, supporting chemical feasibility for Mn–N bond formation (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is high nitrogen chemical potential under pressure, supplied either by azide decomposition or dense NH₃/N₂. The most critical limiting factor is competing formation of lower manganese nitrides, especially Mn₃N₂, and the need to stabilize the N–N bonded MnN₂ topology rather than a conventional Mn–N framework [Reasonable Inference from CS1 and TS2/TS4].
