### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.013 eV/atom, which is well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. Thermodynamic analogues are also near the hull, including AgSe₃I at 0.016 eV/atom and Pd(Se₃Br)₂ at 0.0035 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2), supporting plausibility. CS and SS also include several stable or near-stable halide/chalcogenide analogues, but CS-1 and SS-4 show that some related phases can be higher in energy (CS-1, SS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Sealed-ampoule direct solid-state reaction

The severity score is reduced because sealed vacuum ampoules and volatile Se/Br species are needed, as in CoPSe solid-state synthesis and Cu/Se/Br sealed-tube synthesis (CS-2, CS-3). Repeated heat treatments and intermediate grinding are explicitly used in CoPSe synthesis, which justifies a multi-step penalty if the target requires similar homogenization (CS-2). Kinetically, elemental Co is relatively sluggish **[Reasonable Inference]**, but halide/selenide sealed systems can react at moderate temperatures in CS-3 and SS-5. The main uncertainty is whether dry solid-state conditions can form the specific 2D P3m1 CoSeBr sheet rather than CoSe, CoBr₂, or selenium-rich bromoselenides.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: CVT / temperature-gradient growth

The CVT route has moderate condition severity because it uses vacuum ampoules and volatile halide transport species, as in CuBrSe₃ and WCl₆Se₆I syntheses (SS-5, TS-3). Kinetically, CVT is favorable because vapor transport reduces bulk diffusion limitations, and single crystals of CuBrSe₃ were grown under a 340→290 °C gradient (SS-5). However, the exact transport chemistry for Co–Se–Br is inferred, not demonstrated, and too much Br activity may stabilize CoBr₂-rich products **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct CVT of Co analogues gives a neutral CS route-specific signal of 50.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Hydrothermal / solvothermal HBr

This route scores best because Pd–Se–Br and Ag–Se–I analogues form under acidic hydrothermal conditions at only 180–220 °C (SS-1, TS-2, TS-1). The solution medium should improve precursor activity and avoid solid-state diffusion barriers, as demonstrated by hydrothermal formation of low-dimensional chalcogenide halides (SS-1, TS-1). The severity is moderate due to concentrated HBr, sealed vessels, and toxic SeO₂/Co species, but there is no high temperature or GPa pressure penalty. The largest uncertainty is phase purity, since SS-1 and TS-1 report contamination/intergrowth or multi-phase products.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Melt/quench

The melt/quench route is supported by CuSe₂Br synthesis at 550 °C for 6 h followed by rapid cooling, with cooling rate critical to phase selection (CS-3). Pd–Se–halides also form from selenium/SeX₄ melts at 180 °C, but phase-pure growth was difficult (SS-4). Kinetically, molten Se/halide media should enhance reaction rates **[Reasonable Inference]**, but quench-sensitive behavior increases risk of metastable or competing phases. Insufficient evidence from TS for Co–Se–Br melt/quench specifically is treated as neutral TS signal of 50 for this route.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation, a small formula, and only edge-sharing octahedral connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers is activated. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for CoSeBr, so each is neutral at 50 for this sub-factor. Physicochemically, a simple 2D ordered sheet with one transition metal and two anions should have lower glass-forming tendency than multication, large-cell, mixed-connectivity frameworks **[Reasonable Inference]**. Remaining uncertainty is that halide/chalcogenide melts can vitrify if quenched too rapidly, especially in selenium-rich compositions **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoSeBr is likely a near-ground-state or only mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.013 eV/atom, within the commonly synthesizable range. The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis most strongly through sealed halide–selenide chemistry: Co mixed-anion octahedra are known in CoPSe (CS-2), Br–Se low-dimensional phases form in sealed tubes/CVT/hydrothermal media (CS-3, SS-1, SS-5), and thermodynamic analogues are close to the hull (TS-1 to TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is control of Br and Se chemical potentials in a sealed environment, which is repeatedly used in the analogues (CS-3, SS-1, SS-5, TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among CoSe, CoBr₂, and selenium-rich bromoselenides, inferred from phase-control difficulties in CuSe₂Br, Pd–Se–halides, and hydrothermal Ag/Pd analogues (CS-3, SS-1, SS-4, TS-1). Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed-vessel chemistry and screening of redox/cooling conditions.
