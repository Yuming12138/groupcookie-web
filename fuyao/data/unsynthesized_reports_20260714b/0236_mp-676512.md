### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly within the highest rubric bin, so a score of 90 is assigned. Chemically and structurally related fluorides are often stable or near-stable: NaFeF3 is ~0 eV/atom in CS-1/CS-2, Na2LiAlF6 is ~0.010 eV/atom in CS-3/SS-1, and Na2ZnAlF7 is ~0.003 eV/atom in SS-4. Thermodynamic analogues TS-1–TS-4 show that metastable fluorides around 0.033–0.038 eV/atom can be synthesized, but TS-5 at 0.082 eV/atom shows some thermodynamic analogues are less stable. Remaining uncertainty is whether the R-3 ilmenite-like polymorph is the ground-state polymorph or a low-energy metastable polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because it requires high-temperature fluoride handling, sealed or controlled atmosphere processing, and drying/grinding/sealing/heating/cooling steps, consistent with SS-4 and CS-1/CS-2. It avoids high pressure, and its temperature is comparable to CS-3/SS-1 and SS-4 rather than the more severe high-pressure CS-1/CS-4/CS-5 conditions. The main deductions come from sealed volatile fluoride chemistry and toxic/hygroscopic fluoride salts. Insufficient evidence from TS for ambient-pressure Na–Al–F melt synthesis specifically, so TS is neutral for this route’s exact condition choice.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary fluoride precursors are not as reactive as solution precursors, but CS-3/SS-1 and SS-4 show they react effectively at 850–950 °C. Melt/sintering should improve ion transport, and the low melting points of Na/Al relative to refractory cations support favorable diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural similarity to Na2LiAlF6 is strong from CS-3/SS-1, but the target’s exact R-3 ordering may require careful cooling. The uncertainty is phase competition among Na3AlF6 polymorphs and possible NaF/AlF3 secondary phases.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 but emphasizes sealed flux/crystal growth, supported by SS-2, SS-3, and TS-5. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions but needs sealed noble-metal ampoules and slow cooling. Volatile fluoride components and toxic fluoride salts remain important deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS for Na3AlF6 self-flux crystal growth specifically, so CS contributes mainly general NaF handling constraints.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth improves mass transport substantially, as SS-3 reports K2LiAlF6 crystals growing from fluoride melt and TS-5 reports single-crystal formation from sealed fluoride mixtures. However, long tempering in TS-5 and phase transformations in SS-3 imply slow ordering and possible polymorph competition. The target’s simple binary chemistry helps, but exact R-3 polymorph selection is uncertain. Therefore kinetics are good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is mild in temperature because TS-4 forms Na3VF6 at 180 °C and TS-2 uses 175–225 °C hydrothermal conditions. It avoids high pressure above 5 GPa and avoids vacuum or reducing/reactive gas atmospheres. The major severity penalty is chemical safety: HF/NH4HF2 are toxic/corrosive and volatile in sealed systems, as directly supported by TS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for hydrothermal Na–Al–F synthesis makes the route more uncertain but not more severe by the rubric.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal fluoride media provide high precursor activity and solution-mediated transport, supported by TS-4 and TS-2. Na3VF6 in TS-4 is especially relevant because it is an A3MF6-type sodium fluoride with octahedral connectivity, suggesting hydrothermal assembly of Na–M–F frameworks is feasible. For Al3+, formation of fluoroaluminate complexes should aid transport [Reasonable Inference], but it may also stabilize soluble or hydrated byproducts. Thus the kinetic score is high, with phase selectivity as the primary uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 is the most severe because it requires >5 GPa high pressure, as in CS-1 and TS-3, and specialized high-pressure assemblies like those in CS-4/CS-5. It does not necessarily require >1000 °C for the proposed Al system, but high pressure and encapsulation dominate the difficulty. CS analogues explicitly report multianvil/DAC equipment and difficult phase control. SS evidence suggests ambient-pressure fluoroaluminate routes may suffice, so high pressure is not justified unless polymorph stabilization fails.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

High pressure can drive dense fluoride polymorph formation, as shown for NaFeF3/NaCoF3 analogues in CS-1/CS-4/CS-5 and LiScF4 in TS-3. Binary fluoride precursors are workable but not highly reactive, and diffusion at 575–850 °C should be acceptable. However, CS-1 and CS-4/CS-5 indicate phase transitions, metastability, and recovery issues can complicate product isolation. The target may not need high pressure, so kinetic benefit is offset by polymorph recovery uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly size- and charge-mismatched cations, Na+ and Al3+, and its target bond lengths show distinct Na–F and Al–F environments; this triggers the ionic-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description only reports corner-sharing NaF6/AlF6 octahedra, so mixed edge/face/corner connectivity is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for >40 atoms, so it is treated as neutral/non-triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na3AlF6 at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is likely either a near-ground-state phase or a readily accessible low-energy metastable polymorph, based on the provided stability threshold and the successful synthesis of similarly low-energy fluorides in CS-3/SS-1 and TS-1–TS-4. The strongest support comes from SS/CS fluoroaluminate analogues, especially Na2LiAlF6 made directly from NaF/LiF/AlF3 and structurally similar AlF6 networks (CS-3/SS-1), while TS-4 supports a low-temperature hydrothermal route for A3MF6 sodium fluorides. The key enabling factor is the high fluoride compatibility of Na+ and Al3+ and the existence of stable AlF6 octahedral fluoroaluminate frameworks in SS-1/SS-2/SS-3. The main limiting factor is polymorph/phase selectivity: the target is specifically ilmenite-like R-3, whereas analogues show multiple perovskite-derived, trigonal, monoclinic, and hydrothermal fluoride frameworks (CS-3/SS-1, SS-2/SS-3, TS-4). If hydrothermal synthesis yields competing hydrated or complex fluoroaluminates, the solid-state sealed-ampoule route is the most reliable fallback.
