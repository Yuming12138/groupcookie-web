### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.0130 eV/atom is within the highly synthesizable ≤0.05 eV/atom range. The closest analogue, MoTe₂ mp-7459, has E_hull = 0.0139 eV/atom and is experimentally synthesized by vapor transport (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), strongly supporting the thermodynamic score. Similar low-hull tellurides such as WTe₂, Mo₃Te₄, VTe₂, NbPdTe₅, and FeTaTe₃ are also experimentally accessible (CS2 / CS3 / CS5 / TS4 / TS5). The main uncertainty is not bulk thermodynamic stability but polymorph selection between nearby MoTe₂ structures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1, Br/TeBr₄ CVT

The route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, with WTe₂ CVT occurring at 820/700 °C for 8 days under vacuum (CS2). Penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Te/halogen species, multistep ampoule/CVT handling, and toxic/corrosive Br₂ or TeBr₄ [Reasonable Inference supported by bromine/TeBr₄ transport in CS1 / CS2]. The exact MoTe₂ analogue confirms that these conditions are viable, but CS1 / SS1 / TS1 do not provide detailed temperatures, so the recommended thermal profile is inferred from WTe₂ (CS2).

#### F3 — Route 1, Br/TeBr₄ CVT

Kinetics are favorable relative to direct solid-state synthesis because halogen transport can move Mo/Te species through the vapor phase [Reasonable Inference], and bromine transport is directly reported for molybdenum/tungsten tellurides (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). WTe₂ crystal growth at 820/700 °C shows that refractory transition-metal ditellurides can be grown below the metal melting point by CVT (CS2). Structural reconstruction is minimal because the target is nearly the same as the synthesized MoTe₂ analogue in composition, bonding, and energy above hull (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Remaining uncertainty is controlling the specific P2₁/m monoclinic polymorph rather than a closely related MoTe₂ stacking variant [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2, Direct solid-state ampoule

Direct sealed-tube reaction uses simpler reagents and avoids toxic halogen transport agents, so its condition severity is milder than CVT in precursor hazard. VTe₂ is prepared from elements in evacuated quartz ampoules at ~1000 K for one week with slow cooling (CS5 / SS3 / TS3), supporting the feasibility of a binary telluride ampoule route. However, vacuum sealing and Te volatility still apply, and high-temperature ampoules are required (CS5 / SS3 / TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows exact MoTe₂ P2₁/m formation by this direct route, so the score is not higher.

#### F3 — Route 2, Direct solid-state ampoule

The kinetic limitation is significant because Mo metal is refractory and diffusion from dense elemental powders is slow [Reasonable Inference]. VTe₂ forms by direct reaction (CS5 / SS3 / TS3), but V is not the same as Mo, and VTe₂ analogues report elemental Te impurity/crystal-growth difficulty (CS5 / SS3 / TS3). The route may still work for polycrystalline MoTe₂ because binary tellurides often form from elements in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference supported by CS5 / SS3 / TS3], but phase purity and ordering are less assured than in CVT. Regrinding and longer anneals may be required [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3, I₂ CVT

I₂ CVT is supported for several layered tellurides, including TaTe₂ at 873→773 K for 28 days and NbPdTe₅ at 700/750 °C for 8 days under vacuum (CS4; SS4 / TS4). Like Br/TeBr₄ CVT, it requires vacuum sealing, volatile halogen handling, a multi-step ampoule process, and a controlled thermal gradient. It avoids high pressure and can operate below 1000 °C based on the telluride analogues (CS4; SS4 / TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms iodine as the optimal transport agent for binary MoTe₂ specifically.

#### F3 — Route 3, I₂ CVT

I₂ transport improves kinetics over direct solid-state diffusion and is demonstrated for layered telluride crystal growth (CS4; SS4 / TS4; TS5). However, the strongest exact MoTe₂ evidence uses bromine rather than iodine (CS1 / SS1 / TS1), so the transferability is slightly weaker than Route 1. Structural reconstruction remains favorable because layered transition-metal tellurides are formed by I₂ CVT in multiple analogues (CS4; SS4 / TS4), and the target is structurally close to the synthesized MoTe₂ analogue (SS1). The main uncertainty is whether iodine forms competing molybdenum iodide/telluride species or alters stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a simple binary MoTe₂ layered material with one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The structure description does not indicate a large >40-atom unit cell or a complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, and the closest MoTe₂ analogue is a crystalline 2D material synthesized by vapor transport (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). Layered telluride analogues are repeatedly obtained as crystals rather than amorphous products (CS2 / CS4 / SS4 / TS4). The uncertainty is mainly stacking disorder or polymorphism, not amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target MoTe₂ phase is likely a very low-energy metastable or near-ground-state polymorph rather than a deeply metastable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.0130 eV/atom and the closest MoTe₂ analogue has nearly identical E_hull and is experimentally synthesized (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The CS, SS, and TS datasets all give unusually strong support because their top analogue is essentially MoTe₂ with nearly identical bonding, dimensionality, and energy above hull (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The most critical enabling factor is halogen-mediated vapor transport, which overcomes slow Mo diffusion and has direct precedent for Mo/W tellurides (CS1 / CS2 / SS1 / TS1). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding Te loss, Te-rich impurities, or a nearby MoTe₂ stacking/symmetry variant [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control concerns in CS1 and impurity issues in CS5 / SS3 / TS3]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible in a standard solid-state/CVT laboratory, with Br₂ or TeBr₄ CVT being the most defensible route.
