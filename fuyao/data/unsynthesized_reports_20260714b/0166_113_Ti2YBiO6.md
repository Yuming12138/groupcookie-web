### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold, so the rubric supports a high score of 90. Chemically similar materials include stable and near-hull oxides, such as CS2 at 0.0 eV/atom and CS1/CS3/CS4 near 0.04–0.05 eV/atom. Structurally and thermodynamically similar references also include synthesizable phases around this metastability level, including SS4 at 0.0429 eV/atom and TS3 at 0.0506 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be redox-metastable if it contains Ti³⁺/Bi³⁺ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is supported by CS1, which uses ordinary oxide precursors and 700–900 °C multi-step firing, and by CS3/CS4, which show conventional oxide ceramic routes for rare-earth titanates. The penalty comes from needing sealed or controlled atmosphere to preserve Ti³⁺ **[Reasonable Inference]**, supported indirectly by sealed/vacuum thermodynamic analogues TS2/TS3. Bi₂O₃ volatility is not explicitly quantified in CS1/CS2, but the presence of Bi₂O₃ at high temperature makes Bi loss a plausible control issue **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct Y–Bi–Ti oxide solid-state conditions, so SS is neutral for this route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS1 required three 72 h firings, and CS5 reports 2–30 days for some solid-state oxide equilibrations, so slow diffusion/order formation is a real risk. The target combines Y, Ti, and Bi in a low-symmetry P1 structure with multiple inequivalent oxygen and Ti sites, implying substantial reconstruction from binary oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thermodynamic analogues TS3 and TS5 also show that some near-hull complex oxides need controlled schedules or yield mixtures. Insufficient evidence from SS for dry-oxide kinetics of this exact composition, so SS contributes little beyond general structural complexity.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has no direct CS/SS/TS precedent, so the sol–gel/Pechini part is explicitly a **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its lower proposed crystallization temperature is justified by improved precursor mixing, while the need for final Ar/vacuum annealing is inferred from charge balance and supported indirectly by TS2/TS3 sealed/vacuum redox methods. Multi-step processing is unavoidable, but high pressure and >1000 °C firing are avoided. CS supports the relevant oxide chemistry but not the solution processing; SS provides insufficient direct evidence and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Nitrate/alkoxide/citrate routes receive high precursor-activity scores by the required rubric and are chosen to address the long diffusion times in CS1 and CS5. Even with excellent mixing, the target requires crystallization of a low-symmetry ordered oxide framework, so structural reconstruction is still only moderate **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS1 and CS2 suggest Bi–Ti oxide chemistry can crystallize below 1000 °C, but no source proves that YTi₂BiO₆ crystallizes from an amorphous gel. TS gives only indirect support that near-hull complex oxides can be recovered if the oxygen potential is controlled.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses Bi₂O₃ as both reagent and flux, which is inferred from Bi₂O₃ being a real precursor in CS1/CS2 but is not directly reported for YTi₂BiO₆. Controlled/sealed atmosphere is recommended because thermodynamic analogues TS2/TS3 show sealed/vacuum conditions for redox-sensitive phases. The route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressures, but Bi volatility, flux handling, and Ti³⁺ stability create penalties. SS evidence is insufficient for Bi₂O₃ flux transferability and is neutral here.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux assistance should improve mass transport compared with dry oxide sintering **[Reasonable Inference]**, and controlled cooling/crystal selection in TS5 shows that near-hull complex oxides may be accessible by cooling from high-temperature conditions. However, no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Bi₂O₃-flux growth of this target family, and excess Bi₂O₃ may instead stabilize Bi-rich titanates similar to CS1. Structural reconstruction remains significant because the target P1 framework is not topotactically inherited from a known precursor. Thus kinetics are better than Route 1 but less predictable than Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains large Y/Bi cations and much smaller Ti cations, satisfying the ionic-radius mismatch check by physicochemical inference. The target structure contains corner- and edge-sharing distorted YO₅/TiO₅ units, but no face-sharing is specified, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not provided; from the formula alone it is not necessarily >40, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for that check. The resulting one-trigger score gives a low–medium crystallization/amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YTi₂BiO₆ is likely a borderline metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and the target sits exactly at the favorable/metastable cutoff. The CS analogues strongly support that Bi–Ti–rare-earth oxides can be synthesized by ordinary ceramic routes, especially CS1, but they do not prove the same Ti oxidation state or P1 structure. The TS analogues support that phases near 0.05–0.07 eV/atom can be made if redox and thermal schedules are controlled. The most important enabling factor is intimate cation mixing, best achieved by Route 2, because CS1/CS5 show long diffusion times for conventional routes. The limiting factor is control of Ti oxidation state and Bi volatility, both inferred from the target charge balance and Bi₂O₃ high-temperature chemistry.
