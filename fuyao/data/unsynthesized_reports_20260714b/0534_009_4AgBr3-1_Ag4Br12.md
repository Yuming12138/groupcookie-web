### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.025 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable near-hull range, so F1 = 95. CS supports this because chemically related bromides include stable or near-stable phases such as CuBr2 with E_hull = 0–0.008 eV/atom and InBr3/GaBr3 at 0 eV/atom (CS1–CS3, CS5). TS also supports the score because thermodynamically similar bromide-rich phases lie at 0.011–0.033 eV/atom and have reported syntheses (TS1–TS5). SS is less decisive because some structurally similar phases are substantially above hull, e.g., SS1 and SS3, so the structural analogy is treated as mixed rather than strongly favorable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, similar to 150–180 °C sealed ampoule/CVT analogues (SS2, TS1, TS4, TS5), so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty is applied. Deductions are applied for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation and volatile Br2, consistent with evacuated ampoules and inert handling used in SS2/TS1/TS5 and moisture-sensitive bromide handling in TS4. Toxic/corrosive bromine adds another penalty **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS5 and CS3 also support that anhydrous/vacuum sublimation of metal bromides is feasible but moderately demanding.

#### F3 — Route 1

Vapor availability is moderate because Br2 is volatile but AgBr transport is uncertain **[Reasonable Inference]**. Crystallization is plausible because related bromide-rich materials are grown in gradients in sealed ampoules (SS2, TS1, TS4, TS5). Composition control is the main kinetic/compositional limitation because AgBr3 requires high bromine chemical potential and Br–Br bonding **[Reasonable Inference]**; TS5 reports by-products in related bromide CVT reactions, supporting a moderate phase-control risk.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because CS1/CS2 demonstrate room-temperature slow evaporation of CuBr2 and SS1/SS3 demonstrate solution precipitation/evaporation of layered halides. No high-pressure, high-temperature, or vacuum requirement is supported for the core solution route. Penalties remain for Br2/HBr volatility and toxicity **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct AgBr3 solution stability, so this source gap is treated as uncertainty rather than an added formal deduction.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable because solution chemistry avoids long-range cation diffusion, as demonstrated by immediate precipitation/recrystallization in SS1 and ambient slow evaporation in CS1/CS2/SS3. Precursor activity is high in concentrated HBr/Br2, although AgBr precipitation may reduce effective Ag solubility **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural assembly into a 2D sheet is plausible from solution because SS1 and SS3 include 2D halide sheets, but no CS/SS/TS source directly proves AgBr3 formation. Therefore F3 is high but not near-perfect.

Route 3 uses low temperatures and no external high pressure, consistent with low-temperature closed ampoule bromide reactions in TS4/TS5 and sealed-tube solid-state chemistry in SS4. The main severity deductions are for vacuum/sealed ampoule use, volatile Br2, and toxic/corrosive bromine. Unlike CS4 high-pressure pyrite-type chalcogenides, the relevant bromide analogues do not require 65–89 kbar pressure; CS4 is chemically less transferable and is not weighted strongly.

Solid–gas bromination has moderate kinetics because Br2 is reactive, but AgBr is a relatively insoluble/dense solid and may transform only at surfaces **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction is the largest uncertainty: AgBr to layered AgBr3 with Br–Br units likely requires nontrivial anion reorganization **[Reasonable Inference]**, and no direct CS/SS/TS analogue reports this exact transformation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgBr3 is likely a near-ground-state metastable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is small but nonzero at 0.025 eV/atom. CS and TS analogues strongly support the feasibility of bromide-rich and near-hull halide phases using mild solution, sublimation, or sealed-ampoule routes (CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5), while SS analogues support layered halide crystallization but give mixed thermodynamic evidence (SS1–SS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high bromine chemical potential to stabilize the Br-rich composition and Br–Br bond **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most critical limiting factor is suppression of ordinary AgBr and other Ag–Br/bromine-rich competing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the best first attempt is low-temperature HBr/Br2 solution crystallization, followed by sealed-ampoule vapor or solid–gas bromination if solution solubility is inadequate.
