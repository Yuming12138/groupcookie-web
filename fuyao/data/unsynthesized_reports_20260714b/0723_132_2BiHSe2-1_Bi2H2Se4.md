### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E above hull is 0.0060 eV/atom, which places it very close to the convex hull and within the highest scoring range. Thermodynamic analogues with similar small hull energies are reported as synthetically accessible, including AgBi₂Se₃Cl at 0.0041 eV/atom (TS-1), BiSe at 0.0038 eV/atom (TS-2), Bi₄Se₃ at 0.0067 eV/atom (TS-4), and Pb₇Bi₄Se₁₃ at 0.0115 eV/atom (TS-5). CS evidence is less directly useful for Bi–Se thermodynamics because CS mainly concerns hydroxy/halide or amide salts, so insufficient evidence from CS for BiHSe₂ thermodynamic stability and CS is treated as neutral. Remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated H-containing phase competes with Bi₂Se₃ + Se + H₂/H₂Se under real chemical potentials.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from low temperature, consistent with CS precipitation at hot aqueous conditions around 350–360 K (CS-1, CS-4) and CS hydrothermal crystallization at 160 °C (CS-5). The major penalties arise from sealed hydrothermal handling and toxic/air-sensitive selenide or HSe⁻ precursors, which are [Reasonable Inference] because CS does not involve HSe⁻. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for low-temperature BiHSe₂ synthesis, but SS-2 confirms that the desired Bi–Se framework chemistry is plausible. The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solution precursors should be kinetically active, analogous to CS precipitation from soluble Pb salts and sodium halides (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). Hydrothermal reaction reduces diffusion barriers, supported by CS-5 where an H-bearing framework crystallized at 160 °C in sealed solution. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction: SS-2 shows the target-like BiSe₆ layered motif, but no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates direct assembly of Bi–H–Se sheets from solution. This gives good but not maximal kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses sealed quartz/fused silica tubes and moderate temperatures similar to SS/TS Bi–Se syntheses: Bi₂Se₃ by sealed high-vacuum melt/Bridgman methods (SS-2) and Pb–Bi–Se at 573–773 K in sealed fused silica (TS-5). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires vacuum/sealed operation and volatile Se/H₂Se control, which matches the sealed-vapor nature of SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, and TS-5. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for high-temperature H retention and is treated as neutral. The route is practically challenging because H₂Se is hazardous and Se–H bonds may not survive high annealing temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Bi₂Se₃ is a strong structural parent because it contains 2D BiSe₆ sheets with Bi–Se bond lengths close to the target (SS-2). Diffusion is helped by low-melting Bi and Se and by the fact that related Bi–Se systems form at moderate sealed-tube temperatures (SS-2, TS-5). However, incorporating H into a crystalline Bi–Se lattice is a partial topochemical reconstruction with no direct analogue in SS/TS. Thus kinetics are reasonable but less favorable than solution synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT of Bi–Se compounds is well supported by SS-1 for Bi₃Se₄Br and SS-4/TS-3 for BiSeI, all in evacuated sealed quartz ampoules with controlled temperature gradients. The target route inherits the same sealed/vacuum and volatile transport-agent penalties. The key extra challenge is that halogen transport agents may substitute into the structure or favor known Bi–Se–halide phases (SS-1, SS-4; TS-3). CS provides insufficient evidence for CVT of H-containing phases, so this route is scored cautiously.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport can strongly improve mass transfer, as shown by single-crystal growth of Bi₃Se₄Br and BiSeI (SS-1, SS-4; TS-3). Bi–Se structural motifs are therefore likely transportable and crystallizable. However, preservation of H–Se bonding under CVT is unproven by CS/SS/TS and may require lower temperatures than standard Bi–Se–halide CVT. The kinetic score is moderate-good for crystallization but limited by H volatility/decomposition risk.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one framework cation, Bi, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise. The described structure is a relatively simple P2₁/m 2D sheet with BiSe₆ octahedra and H–Se bonds, and there is no evidence of a >40 atom unit cell in the target description. Mixed corner and edge sharing exists, but the criterion requires vertex + edge + face sharing coexist; no face sharing is described. Similar Bi–Se layered structures with edge/corner sharing are known crystalline phases (SS-2; TS-5), supporting low amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiHSe₂ is likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase because its E above hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom, consistent with many experimentally accessible TS analogues in the 0–0.013 eV/atom range (TS-1 through TS-5). The SS/TS analogues strongly support formation of Bi–Se layered and octahedral frameworks, especially Bi₂Se₃-like sheets (SS-2) and Bi–Se binaries (TS-2, TS-4). The CS analogues support the general feasibility of H–anion bonds and low-temperature crystallization of H-bearing inorganic salts, but they do not directly establish Bi–H–Se chemistry (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural/thermodynamic compatibility of Bi–Se layered motifs. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining H–Se bonding without losing volatile/toxic H₂Se or relaxing to Bi₂Se₃/BiSe/elemental Se competing phases.
