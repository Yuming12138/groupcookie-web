### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored as a predicted ground-state phase. Multiple oxide-halide analogues are also on the hull, including VOBr₂, NbOI₃, MoO₂Br₂, and VOCl (SS/TS), which supports the general stability of low-dimensional transition-metal oxide halides. CS contains low-hull materials but they are mostly La/Ga/Cr/Hg/Pt chalcogenide or halide salts rather than V oxyhalides, so there is **insufficient evidence from CS** for direct thermodynamic transfer; CS neutral score 50.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT / Sealed-Ampoule Halogen Transport

CVT analogues require evacuated ampoules and temperature gradients, as shown for WOI₃, NbOI₃, MoO₂Br₂, and VOCl (SS/TS). The severity score is reduced because vacuum, sealed volatile halogens, and toxic/air-sensitive halide reagents are needed (SS/TS + Reasonable Inference). Temperatures are moderate rather than extreme because closest iodide analogues form at 275–440 °C (SS/TS), although MoO₂Br₂ shows that some bromide oxide-halide transport can require higher temperature up to 820 °C (SS/TS).

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT / Sealed-Ampoule Halogen Transport

Vapor transport lowers diffusion barriers, as NbOI₃ and WOI₃ form far below refractory metal/oxide diffusion temperatures (SS/TS). Structural reconstruction is favorable because VOBr₂ and VOCl₂ have 2D V–O–halide sheets with linear O and L-shaped halides similar to the target (SS/TS). The uncertainty is mixed-halide ordering among I, Br, and Cl, which is not directly shown by CS/SS/TS and may require careful halogen chemical-potential tuning [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Solution Precipitation / Dehydration

The low synthesis temperature is favorable because VOBr₂/VOCl₂ analogues are accessed near 80 °C with Ar/vacuum processing (SS/TS). Deductions arise from the controlled atmosphere/vacuum, multiple operations such as precipitation, centrifugation, concentration, washing, and drying, and chemically sensitive V/halide reagents (SS/TS). CS provides examples of slow evaporation under mild conditions, but those are Hg/Pt/Re-containing salts rather than V oxyhalides, so **insufficient evidence from CS** for this exact route; CS neutral score 50.

#### F3 — Route 2: Solution Precipitation / Dehydration

The reaction kinetics are strong because soluble VOSO₄ and halide salts allow molecular-level mixing and rapid metathesis, as in VOBr₂/VOCl₂ analogue preparation (SS/TS). Ion diffusion is essentially bypassed at the precipitation stage, which justifies a high kinetics score (SS/TS). The main kinetic/structural risk is that the known route may form hydrates and may decompose during isolation or dehydration, which lowers the reconstruction score (SS/TS + Reasonable Inference).

#### F2 — Route 3: Direct Solid-State / Reactive Sealed-Ampoule Synthesis

This route is more severe because it combines sealed-tube handling, volatile halogens, vacuum, and likely air-sensitive V halides (SS/TS + Reasonable Inference). It is still not penalized for >1000 °C because oxide-halide CVT analogues commonly form at 275–820 °C (SS/TS), and the proposed solid-state window is below 650 °C [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports sealed ampoule practice at higher temperatures, but CS materials are chemically less direct; CS is secondary evidence only.

#### F3 — Route 3: Direct Solid-State / Reactive Sealed-Ampoule Synthesis

Dense V oxides/metals have slower solid-state kinetics than solution or CVT precursors [Reasonable Inference], even though halide transport can assist. The structural target is related to known layered V oxyhalides (SS/TS), so complete novelty is not required, but arranging three different halides on bridging sites may be slow and defect-prone [Reasonable Inference]. Analogues also report phase-control issues such as disorder or by-product crystal formation for NbOI₃/MoO₂Br₂ (SS/TS), lowering confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, V, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Target]. The formula is small, and neither target nor close SS/TS analogues imply a large, glass-prone unit cell; however, exact target cell atom count is not provided, so that subclaim has residual uncertainty. VOBr₂/VOCl₂ analogues show edge/corner sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing (SS/TS), so amorphization tendency is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close oxide-halide analogues are also on the hull (SS/TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar V oxyhalides VOBr₂ and VOCl₂, which share the 2D V–O–halide sheet chemistry (SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halide/vanadyl chemistry or halogen vapor transport, both of which can assemble oxyhalide frameworks without extreme diffusion requirements (SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is precise mixed-halide stoichiometry and ordering across I, Br, and Cl sites, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS and may lead to competing single-halide phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with solution metathesis/dehydration preferred for low kinetic barriers and CVT preferred if single crystals or better ordering are required.
