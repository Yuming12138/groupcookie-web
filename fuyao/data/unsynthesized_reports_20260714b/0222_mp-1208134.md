### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV atom⁻¹, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff; this supports a high F1 score of 90. CS gives strong support because the closest Tl–Cu–H–Se analogue has E_hull = 0.019 eV atom⁻¹ [CS2], while SS/TS include structurally related C2/m phases at ~0.048–0.078 eV atom⁻¹ [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that 0.05 eV atom⁻¹ suggests possible metastability rather than a deep ground state, so kinetic route control may matter.

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal severity

Route 1 uses moderate hydrothermal temperatures, ~180–240 °C or ~500 K, reported across close analogues [CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4]. No high pressure beyond autogenous hydrothermal pressure, no inert atmosphere, and no >1000 °C treatment are required in the cited analogues [CS1, CS2, CS5]. The only deduction is for toxic/corrosive Tl and selenium reagents, inferred from the use of Tl₂CO₃ and H₂SeO₄ in CS1/CS2.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics

Hydrothermal solution transport should overcome the otherwise slow diffusion of CuO and Tl-containing solids, and the closest analogues crystallize under these conditions [CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target sheet of edge-sharing CuO₆ octahedra and corner-sharing SeO₄ tetrahedra closely matches SS1–SS5 and TS1/TS4. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: multiple phases are reported in similar hydrothermal systems [CS2, SS3, SS4, TS2].

#### F2 — Route 2 evaporation severity

Route 2 is mild in temperature and pressure because TS3 reports room-temperature to 50 °C aqueous evaporation/crystallization [TS3]. There is no high-pressure vessel or controlled atmosphere requirement in TS3, so condition severity is low. The same toxic Tl/selenium chemistry remains, so a −10 deduction is retained by [Reasonable Inference] from CS1/CS2 precursor chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2 evaporation kinetics

Aqueous precursors can be highly reactive, and TS3 supports crystallization of hydrogen selenate/sulfate compounds from solution [TS3]. However, CS and SS provide insufficient evidence that the specific Tl–Cu C2/m layered CuO₆–SeO₄ framework forms by simple evaporation, so CS and SS are scored neutral 50 for this route-specific kinetic transferability. The major uncertainty is whether evaporation yields the desired extended Cu–selenate sheet rather than simple hydrated salts.

#### F2 — Route 3 precursor-conversion severity

Route 3 remains hydrothermal and uses the same moderate temperature window as Routes 1 and the closest analogues [CS2, SS1–SS5]. It receives an added multi-step penalty because a preformed natrochalcite-type precursor must first be synthesized before conversion [Reasonable Inference from SS1/SS2/TS4]. Toxic Tl/selenium reagents again impose a safety penalty based on CS1/CS2.

#### F3 — Route 3 precursor-conversion kinetics

The structural match is excellent because SS1 is C2/m Tl(CuSeO₅)₂ and the target is also C2/m with a closely related Tl–Cu–Se–O sheet [SS1]. Hydrothermal transport should allow proton/selenate re-equilibration at low temperature [CS2, SS3, SS4]. The uncertainty is that a dense preformed precursor may resist conversion or remain as an impurity, and no CS/SS/TS source directly reports this exact topochemical conversion.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very different cation sizes and coordination types, including large Tl environments, CuO₆ octahedra, and SeO₄ tetrahedra, so C1 is triggered [target description; SS1–SS5]. C2 is not triggered because the formula has 16 atoms and the provided CS/SS/TS data do not prove a >40-atom target cell. C3 is not triggered because the target and close analogues show corner and edge sharing, but no face sharing [target description, SS2–SS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlCu₂H(SeO₅)₂ is likely a near-threshold metastable or weakly stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV atom⁻¹. The CS and SS analogues strongly support synthesis: CS2 gives nearly identical Tl–Cu–H–Se–O chemistry, and SS1/SS2 give very close C2/m Cu–selenate layer frameworks [CS2, SS1, SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is hydrothermal chemistry, which provides low-temperature dissolution/recrystallization of CuO, Tl⁺, and Se(VI) into layered CuO₆–SeO₄ structures [CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among closely related Tl/Cu selenates, hydroxyselenates, CuO/Cu₂O, and possible hydrated phases, which is reported for analogous systems [CS2, SS3, SS4, TS2]. Toxic Tl and oxidizing selenium acid handling also limits practical synthesis despite favorable structural precedent [CS1, CS2].
