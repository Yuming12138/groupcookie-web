### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0050 eV/atom, placing it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic range. Chemically identical VTe₂ analogues are reported at E_hull = 0.0 and 0.0185 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2), structurally similar VTe₂ entries are also near the hull (SS-1, SS-2), and thermodynamic analogues cluster near the hull (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). This strongly suggests the target is either a ground-state-adjacent polymorph or a very low-energy metastable phase. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph competition, not bulk decomposition.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state ampoule

The direct route is strongly supported because VTe₂ itself has been made from V + Te in evacuated ampoules at ~1000 K for one week with slow cooling (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). F2 is reduced mainly by the need for vacuum sealing and volatile/toxic Te handling. F3 is moderate because elemental V is refractory and slow to diffuse, but the reported success at 1000 K implies Te-assisted transport or sufficient reactivity under sealed conditions (CS-1, CS-2). The main uncertainty is phase purity: residual elemental Te and poor crystal growth are reported for close VTe₂ analogues (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, high-temperature anneal/quench

This route is supported by V₃Te₄ synthesis using V + Te powder, evacuated silica tube, high-temperature heating, pelletization, long annealing, and quenching (SS-3, TS-3). F2 is lower because the route is multi-step and still requires sealed-tube Te handling. F3 is also moderate because long annealing indicates slow homogenization/order formation, and the route may favor non-1:2 V–Te phases if stoichiometry or Te vapor pressure is imperfect (SS-3, TS-3). Its advantage is that quenching may help trap the desired monoclinic polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, CVT

CVT has no direct VTe₂ example in the provided data, but it is strongly precedented for layered tellurides such as TaTe₂ and NbPdTe₅ using I₂, and MoTe₂/WTe₂ using halogen transport (CS-4, SS-4, TS-4, TS-5). F2 is moderate because CVT requires vacuum sealing, a transport agent, and a two-zone furnace. F3 is relatively high because vapor transport bypasses much of the refractory-metal diffusion limitation and is known to grow layered telluride crystals. The key uncertainty is whether iodine transport stabilizes the target P2₁/m VTe₂ polymorph rather than another VTe₂ form or iodine-containing byproduct.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, Bridgman

Bridgman growth is only weakly supported by the Cr₂Te₃ analogue (CS-5); insufficient evidence from SS and TS for Bridgman growth of VTe₂ specifically. F2 is penalized because melt-growth with Te likely requires sealed ampoules and careful pressure control [Reasonable Inference]. F3 could be favorable because melt growth overcomes diffusion limitations, but polymorph and stoichiometry control are less certain than in direct solid-state or CVT approaches. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth exploration rather than the first powder-synthesis attempt.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple binary composition with only one cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target structure is layered and described as edge-sharing VTe₆ octahedra, so the mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity check is not triggered; mixed connectivity is instead found in V₃Te₄ analogues (SS-3, TS-3). There is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for the exact target unit-cell atom count, but close VTe₂ analogues are simple layered structures (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2), supporting a low amorphization risk. The remaining uncertainty is stacking/polymorph disorder rather than true amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target VTe₂ phase is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable polymorph because its E_hull is only 0.0050 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS datasets strongly support synthesis of VTe₂-like layered phases from elemental V and Te, especially by sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction at ~1000 K for one week. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple chemically identical or nearly identical VTe₂ analogues with edge-sharing VTe₆ sheets and near-zero hull energies. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, since reported VTe₂ analogues mention elemental Te impurity and unsuccessful crystal growth attempts. CVT is recommended for improved crystallinity, while direct sealed-tube synthesis is the most conservative powder route.
