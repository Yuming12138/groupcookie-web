### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and the exact BiOI reference is also reported with E_hull = 0.0 in CS-1, SS-1, and TS-1. This indicates a ground-state thermodynamic phase rather than a strongly metastable one. There is insufficient uncertainty from CS/SS/TS regarding stability because all three categories include the same on-hull BiOI reference.

#### F2 — Route 1: Solution diffusion / controlled hydrolysis

The conditions are very mild: ~18 °C, ambient atmosphere, simple glassware, and no high pressure are reported for the direct BiOI diffusion synthesis (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). Deductions are mainly for corrosive/hazardous acidic precursors and the multi-step diffusion/water/heating sequence [Reasonable Inference from listed HCl/HI/BiCl₃ and CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 procedure]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the exact historical diffusion procedure is optimal for powders versus single crystals, because CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 emphasize crystal-growth difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 1: Solution diffusion / controlled hydrolysis

Kinetics are favorable because transport occurs through solution rather than solid-state diffusion, and BiOI forms directly under aqueous hydrolysis/diffusion conditions (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The main kinetic limitation is nucleation/morphology control: the references report re-solution, polycrystalline formation, and fragile crystals for BiOI (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). Therefore, the kinetics score is high for phase formation but not perfect for controlled large-crystal growth.

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule solid-state

The solid-state route uses moderate temperatures, inferred at ~350–450 °C from Pb oxide iodide synthesis at 408 °C and BiOI-containing ampoule synthesis at 400 °C (CS-3; TS-4; SS-5). However, it requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile iodine/iodide control, and possibly regrinding/annealing, as seen in related sealed-tube oxycompound syntheses (CS-3; TS-4; SS-4; TS-3). The main uncertainty is that direct BiOI solid-state synthesis is not explicitly provided in CS/SS/TS, so transferability is moderate rather than definitive.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule solid-state

Solid-state kinetics are moderate because Bi₂O₃ is less reactive than soluble Bi salts [Reasonable Inference], although Bi/I species can be mobile at the proposed temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. Analogous oxide iodides and Bi oxycompounds form at moderate temperature in evacuated ampoules (CS-3; TS-4; SS-4; TS-3; SS-5), supporting feasibility. Remaining uncertainty concerns competing Bi₂O₃/BiI₃/Bi-rich oxyiodide phases and iodine loss if the ampoule chemistry is not well buffered.

#### F2 — Route 3: Aqueous precipitation / hydrothermal

The route is mild, with no need for high temperature, high pressure, vacuum, or sealed iodine ampoules [Reasonable Inference], and this is consistent with direct low-temperature BiOI solution formation in CS-1/SS-1/TS-1. The only substantial severity deduction is chemical handling of Bi salts and acidic/iodide solutions, based on the listed BiCl₃/HCl/HI precursors (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The uncertainty is that the precipitation/hydrothermal version is inferred rather than directly listed in the provided data.

#### F3 — Route 3: Aqueous precipitation / hydrothermal

The expected kinetics are excellent because soluble Bi³⁺ and I⁻ can react directly, avoiding slow lattice diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and solution formation of BiOI is directly demonstrated (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). Crystallization may still be sensitive to pH, supersaturation, and iodide concentration because the diffusion route notes difficulty avoiding polycrystalline material and re-solution (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). Thus, powder formation is likely easy, while morphology/single-crystal control remains the main kinetic challenge.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple single-cation layered oxyiodide with no multi-cation radius mismatch, and the reference BiOI cell/formula is small compared with the >40-atom threshold (target description; CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The connectivity is corner- and edge-sharing OBi₄ tetrahedra only, with no face-sharing coexistence reported (target description; CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The amorphization risk is therefore low; the remaining uncertainty is mostly morphology/disorder in rapidly precipitated powders rather than true glass-forming tendency [Reasonable Inference].
