### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is exactly at the high-feasibility threshold and suggests a marginally metastable but experimentally plausible phase. Chemically similar Sr₂ZnMoO₆ and Sr₂CoMoO₆ have low E_hull values of 0.0109 and 0.0331 eV/atom and are synthesized by solid-state routes (CS1, CS3), while Sr₂CuMoO₆ at 0.0680 eV/atom required HP synthesis and showed impurity sensitivity (CS2, TS1). Structurally similar Sr₂MgWO₆ is at or near the hull and made by ceramic methods (SS2, SS3), which supports the target strongly. Remaining uncertainty is whether Mo⁶⁺/Mg²⁺ ordering in Sr₂MgMoO₆ is as favorable as W⁶⁺/Mg²⁺ ordering in Sr₂MgWO₆; insufficient evidence from TS specifically for Mg–Mo double perovskite ordering.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses high but ordinary ceramic temperatures, consistent with Sr₂ZnMoO₆ at 800–1350 °C and Sr₂MgWO₆ at up to 1200 °C or 1473 K (CS3, SS1–SS3). It avoids high pressure and controlled atmospheres, as many analogues are synthesized in air (CS1, CS4, SS2, SS4). The main severity penalty is the multistep grinding/firing sequence, which is repeatedly reported for analogous perovskites (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1–SS3). Insufficient evidence from TS that ambient pressure is always adequate, because TS1 indicates a thermodynamically similar Sr₂CuMoO₆ required HP.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are moderate because SrCO₃/MgO/MoO₃ are standard but MgO is refractory and cation diffusion/order is slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with repeated firings in CS3 and SS1–SS3. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target is a double perovskite like Sr₂ZnMoO₆ and Sr₂MgWO₆ (SS1–SS3). The uncertainty is MoO₃ volatility and possible SrMoO₄ formation, which are not directly quantified for Sr₂MgMoO₆; volatility concerns are inferred from WO₃ sublimation mitigation in SS4 and volatile TeO₂ handling in TS3.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has similar temperature severity to Route 1 but adds precursor synthesis, so it remains a multistep ceramic process. Its advantage is that preforming MgMoO₄ is directly analogous to the MgWO₄ precursor route used for Ca₂MgWO₆, where sublimation of WO₃ was a concern and single-phase powder was obtained (SS4). It still requires no high pressure or reactive atmosphere, matching many CS/SS ceramic analogues (CS3, SS1–SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/TS directly confirms MgMoO₄ as the best precursor for Sr₂MgMoO₆, so this is a reasoned extrapolation.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The MgMoO₄ precursor should improve Mg/Mo mixing and reduce the kinetic penalty of reacting refractory MgO directly with Sr/Mo oxides [Reasonable Inference from SS4]. The structural pathway remains favorable because the final product is closely related to Sr₂MgWO₆ and Sr₂ZnMoO₆ (SS1–SS3). The limiting kinetic factor is still long-range B-site ordering in an A₂BB′O₆ lattice, and repeated firings are likely needed as in CS3 and SS1–SS3. Remaining uncertainty is whether MgMoO₄ is too stable and persists as an impurity under the selected firing schedule [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The strict scoring table gives a high F2 because the analogous Sr₂CuMoO₆ HP route uses ca. 4 GPa, below the >5 GPa deduction threshold, and only 900 °C for 60 min (CS2, TS1). In practice, the route is challenging because it requires cubic-anvil equipment and Au capsules, explicitly reported for Sr₂CuMoO₆ (CS2, TS1). It is useful as a fallback if ambient-pressure synthesis fails or if the target polymorph is kinetically inaccessible. Insufficient evidence from SS that HP is needed, because the closest Mg/W and Zn/Mo structural analogues form at ambient pressure (SS1–SS3).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
High pressure can stabilize dense perovskite structures and suppress volatilization [Reasonable Inference], but the short HP dwell may not allow full Mg/Mo ordering. Sr₂CuMoO₆ HP synthesis still showed impurity and phase-purity issues for high-Mo compositions (CS2, TS1), lowering the kinetic/phase-control score. The target has better structural analogues made without HP, so HP is not the preferred kinetic pathway (SS1–SS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether high pressure would preserve the desired tetragonal P4 ordering or quench a different polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization/disorder tendency.**  
The target has large cation size and charge contrast between Sr²⁺, Mg²⁺, and Mo⁶⁺ [Reasonable Inference], which promotes ordered double-perovskite chemistry but can also drive antisite or secondary-phase formation. The connectivity is not purely simple corner-sharing when all coordination polyhedra are considered; target and analogues include corner/edge/face-sharing descriptions involving Sr/O-centered polyhedra (CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3). The atom-count criterion is not triggered based on the formula and lack of evidence for a very large unit cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact target conventional cell size. The resulting risk is medium, more likely manifesting as cation disorder or impurity phases than true glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgMoO₆ is likely a marginally metastable phase rather than a deep ground-state compound, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom at the edge of the high-feasibility window. The CS and SS analogues strongly support synthesis: Sr₂ZnMoO₆, Sr₂CoMoO₆, Sr₂CaMoO₆, and Sr₂MgWO₆ are all accessible by ceramic routes using oxides/carbonates and repeated high-temperature firing (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1–SS3, SS5). The most important enabling factor is the robust A₂BB′O₆ double-perovskite framework with strong charge contrast between Mg²⁺ and Mo⁶⁺, analogous to Mg²⁺/W⁶⁺ ordering in Sr₂MgWO₆ (SS2, SS3). The most important limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: MgO is refractory, MoO₃ can be volatile, and SrMoO₄/MgMoO₄-type competing phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference supported by impurity/volatility concerns in SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3]. Overall, ambient-pressure synthesis should be attempted first, preferably via a pre-reacted MgMoO₄ precursor; HP synthesis is a fallback if the tetragonal P4 phase cannot be isolated.
