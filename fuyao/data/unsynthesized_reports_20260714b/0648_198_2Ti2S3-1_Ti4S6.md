### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is on the convex hull with E\_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving the maximum thermodynamic score [Target]. This is consistent with thermodynamic analogues such as Nb₃S₄ and Ti₃Se₄ at E\_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [TS-1, TS-3] and low-energy Ti sulfides near the hull [CS-1, CS-2]. CS includes higher-hull TiS-like polymorphs [CS-4, CS-5], so competing Ti–S phases are plausible, but this does not reduce the target’s thermodynamic score [CS-4, CS-5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS on the exact Ti₂S₃ polymorph stability beyond analogous low-hull sulfides; neutral source confidence is assumed for exact polymorph selectivity.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because direct Ti–S solid-state syntheses use evacuated tubes and high temperatures, including 1000–1100 °C for TiS [CS-5] and up to 1300 °C for Ti-rich TiS [CS-4]. Sulfur volatility requires sealed tubes, and repeated heat treatments/grinding are used in related Ti sulfides [CS-4, SS-2]. There is insufficient evidence from TS that direct Ti + S specifically forms Ti₂S₃ under milder conditions, so TS is neutral for this route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate-to-slow because Ti metal is refractory and related TiS syntheses need long anneals, up to 8 weeks [CS-5]. The target’s TiS₆ octahedral framework resembles TiS and TiS₂ motifs, which helps structural compatibility [CS-1, CS-4, CS-5], but achieving the Ti₂S₃ stoichiometry likely requires careful ordering/reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS supports the need for staged firing/intermediate grinding in Ti-containing sulfides [SS-2]; insufficient evidence from TS for direct Ti₂S₃ kinetics is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C but still requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile iodine/sulfur species, and a controlled gradient [CS-1, CS-3]. I₂ adds toxicity/corrosion risk, and CVT typically requires prereaction plus transport/recrystallization steps [CS-1, CS-3]. SS provides indirect support through related sealed-tube solid-state syntheses but no direct CVT Ti₂S₃ evidence, so SS is neutral for route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by vapor-mediated transport, as shown for TiS₂-related material crystallization at 850–900 °C [CS-1] and V₅S₈ at 900→800 °C [CS-3]. The structural similarity of layered TiS₆ octahedral TiS₂-like analogues supports nucleation of a Ti–S sheet phase [CS-1, CS-2]. The main uncertainty is whether iodine transport stabilizes Ti₂S₃ rather than TiS₂ or TiS, because phase-window data for Ti₂S₃ itself are not provided [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact Ti₂S₃ CVT selectivity].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is most severe because thermodynamic analogues using oxide-to-sulfide conversion require multiple high-temperature atmosphere steps including H₂S at 1150 °C [TS-4]. H₂S is toxic/reactive, and LiTiS₂ processing also involves evacuated tubes, quenching, H₂S treatment, and vacuum sulfur removal [SS-4, TS-5]. CS gives little direct support for oxide/H₂S Ti₂S₃ preparation, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are poor-to-moderate because TiO₂ is refractory and oxygen removal can leave oxide impurities; LiTiS₂ analogues explicitly report TiO₂-related side phases and difficulty obtaining phase-pure material [SS-4, TS-5]. The oxide-to-sulfide route requires a complete anion-exchange/reconstruction rather than simple Ti–S ordering [Reasonable Inference]. TS-4 shows the route can work for related sulfides, but not specifically for binary Ti₂S₃, leaving significant uncertainty [TS-4; insufficient evidence from CS for this route].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Ti, so cation-radius disorder is absent [Target]. The structure is not described as a large-cell complex phase, but CS/SS/TS do not provide exact atom count for the target, so C2 is treated as not triggered with uncertainty [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing TiS₆ octahedra are explicitly present, which raises amorphization/defect risk, but similar mixed-connectivity sulfides are crystalline in the references [Target; CS-4; CS-5; SS-3].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂S₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target has E\_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The CS data strongly support titanium sulfide synthesis in sealed tubes and by iodine-assisted CVT, especially for layered TiS₂-like phases and TiS-related phases [CS-1, CS-4, CS-5]. SS and TS analogues show that mixed octahedral transition-metal sulfides are routinely accessible by sealed-tube, high-temperature, or vapor-transport methods [SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2]. The most important enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with close Ti–S chemical analogues [Target; CS-1; CS-2]. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity among TiS, Ti₂S₃, and TiS₂ under sulfur-rich or sulfur-poor conditions, as related Ti–S systems show narrow homogeneity ranges and multiple modifications [CS-4, CS-5].
