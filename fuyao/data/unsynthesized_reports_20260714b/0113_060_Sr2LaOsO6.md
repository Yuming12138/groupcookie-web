### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the most favorable scoring bin and suggests near-ground-state or mildly metastable stability [Target]. The direct analogue Sr₂YOsO₆ is calculated on the hull and experimentally synthesized [CS1, SS1], and thermodynamic analogues near 0.04–0.06 eV/atom are also experimentally accessible [TS1, TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that small DFT errors or oxygen chemical potential could shift Sr₂LaOsO₆ slightly above or below the practical stability threshold [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 uses moderate temperature, but the Ar sealed tube, glove-box handling, long firing, and OsO₂ toxicity impose significant severity deductions [CS1, SS1]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Sr–La–Os route, so TS is neutral for the detailed route severity. The main uncertainty is whether La substitution changes oxygen potential requirements relative to Y [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.** Sr₂YOsO₆ required ~100 h with intermittent regrinding, indicating slow bulk diffusion and ordering kinetics [CS1, SS1]. The structural match is excellent because both are Sr₂B′OsO₆-type corner-sharing octahedral networks with similar Os–O distances [CS1, SS1]. The score is limited by refractory oxide diffusion and possible B-site ordering requirements [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Ba₂LnOsO₆ synthesis shows that Ln–Os double perovskites can be made in evacuated silica tubes at ~897 °C for only 5 h [SS2]. However, vacuum sealing and OsO₂/Os redox control still impose moderate severity [SS2]. CS supports Sr–Os sealed-tube chemistry [CS1], while TS gives only indirect support through related evacuated Re/Ln oxide routes [TS4].

**F3 — Route 2.** Short firing in Ba₂LnOsO₆ suggests favorable crystallization kinetics for Ln–Os double perovskites [SS2]. The presence of Os metal may slow homogenization relative to all-oxide precursors, but it can help tune oxygen stoichiometry [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because Ba-site perovskites are less tilted than the Sr₂LaOsO₆ target and may have easier crystallization [SS2; Target].

**F2 — Route 3.** The hydroxide flux route operates at much lower temperature, reducing thermal severity [SS3]. It still requires sealed metal tubes, caustic flux, precursor activation, and Os handling [SS3]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of Sr₂LaOsO₆, so CS is neutral-to-weak for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.** Flux transport strongly improves kinetics by dissolving reactants and enabling crystallization at 600 °C [SS3]. The route is structurally plausible because NaLa₂OsO₆ is a La–Os perovskite-derived oxide [SS3]. The main uncertainty is compositional transfer: Sr₂LaOsO₆ has no Na and needs Sr incorporation rather than the NaLa₂OsO₆ topology [SS3; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.** Air synthesis avoids vacuum/Ar equipment and has been used for Ba₂CaOsO₆ at 1000 °C [CS4]. However, Os evaporation was significant enough to require 10 mol% excess Os and repeated processing [CS4]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct support for open-air Sr–La–Os synthesis, so they are neutral or cautionary.

**F3 — Route 4.** The Ba₂CaOsO₆ analogue shows that air-fired Os double perovskites can crystallize [CS4]. Kinetics are limited by Os metal reactivity and repeated grinding/firing, and phase control is likely less reliable for Os⁵⁺ Sr₂LaOsO₆ than for the cited Ba₂CaOsO₆ [CS4; Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore backup rather than preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The large cation size and bonding contrast between Sr/La and Os triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is still a regular corner-sharing perovskite-derived network, not a highly mixed edge/face-sharing topology [Target]. CS/SS analogues show crystalline ordered perovskites are accessible despite similar size mismatch [CS1, SS1, SS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LaOsO₆ is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from Sr₂YOsO₆, which is a directly chemically and structurally similar Sr–Os double perovskite synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state reaction [CS1, SS1]. Additional support comes from Ba₂LnOsO₆, showing that Ln–Os double perovskites are accessible in evacuated silica tubes [SS2]. The most critical enabling factor is controlled Os oxygen chemistry in a sealed environment, which suppresses volatile Os loss and stabilizes the desired Os oxidation state [CS1, SS1, SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially avoiding Os volatilization, La₂O₃ hydration effects, and competing Sr–La–Os or binary oxide phases [CS1, CS4, TS5].
