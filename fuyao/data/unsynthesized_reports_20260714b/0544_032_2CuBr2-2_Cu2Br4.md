### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull of 0.040 eV/atom places it inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability window. Chemically identical CuBr₂ polymorphs are reported at 0.0 and 0.0084 eV/atom and are experimentally accessible by slow evaporation (CS-1, CS-2). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.02–0.03 eV/atom are also synthesized by hydrothermal, solid-state, and CVT methods (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5), supporting accessibility of slightly metastable bromide phases. The uncertainty is polymorph competition: the target is not the lowest-energy CuBr₂ polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Slow Evaporation

The severity is minimal because CS-1/CS-2 and SS-1/SS-3 use room-temperature water evaporation with no high pressure, no controlled atmosphere, and no specialized equipment. Kinetics are favorable because solution growth avoids solid-state diffusion, and CuBr₂ is already in a mobile ionic solution (CS-1, CS-2). The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the target Pmc2₁ sheet polymorph rather than the known monoclinic CuBr₂ forms; reported twinning also reduces practical reliability (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sublimation / Vapor Transport

The route is more severe because analogous FeBr₂ sublimation uses 773 K under Ar (CS-4), and halide CVT analogues use sealed evacuated ampoules and temperature gradients over days (TS-4). Kinetics can be adequate for crystal growth because vapor transport forms single crystals in analogues (CS-4, TS-4). However, composition control is uncertain because CuBr₂ may lose bromine or reduce to CuBr at elevated temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**, and no direct CuBr₂ sublimation evidence is provided by SS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal HBr

This route has moderate severity due to sealed hydrothermal pressure and concentrated HBr, supported by CuHgSBr synthesis in concentrated HBr at 670 K for 24 h with controlled cooling (TS-1, SS-4). Kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal solution transport enables dissolution and recrystallization, and TS-1/SS-4 report good crystal formation and no side products. The key uncertainty is that CS provides no direct hydrothermal CuBr₂ evidence, so this route is an extrapolation from related Cu bromide-containing systems.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Sealed-Ampoule Annealing

Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis is supported by thermodynamic analogues heated at 553–573 K for two weeks in evacuated Pyrex tubes (TS-2, TS-3; SS-5), and by a chemically similar Cu chalcogenide sealed-ampoule synthesis at 200 °C for 6 days (CS-5). Severity arises from vacuum/sealed ampoules and possible volatile bromine-containing species. Kinetics are slower than solution methods because solid-state ordering may require long anneals, as seen in TS-2/TS-3, and CS-5 explicitly reports difficulty obtaining single crystals of CuSe₂.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size mismatch is not an issue **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structure is a simple binary CuBr₂ sheet made of corner-sharing CuBr₄ tetrahedra, so it lacks the mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity associated with high amorphization risk. Exact target unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, but chemically identical CuBr₂ analogues are simple structures (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3), so the amorphization tendency is scored low-risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target Pmc2₁ CuBr₂ phase is likely a metastable polymorph rather than the ground-state form because its E_hull is 0.040 eV/atom, while chemically identical CuBr₂ references include lower-energy polymorphs at 0.0 and 0.0084 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2). The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis of CuBr₂ crystals from aqueous slow evaporation, including structurally related CuBr₂ forms (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the extremely simple chemistry: a binary CuBr₂ composition can crystallize directly from solution under mild conditions. The main limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because the known CuBr₂ solution products are monoclinic or ribbon/sheet variants rather than explicitly the target Pmc2₁ tetrahedral sheet. Twinning and crystal-quality issues reported for CuBr₂ also indicate that isolating high-quality single crystals may be harder than simply forming CuBr₂ solid (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3).
