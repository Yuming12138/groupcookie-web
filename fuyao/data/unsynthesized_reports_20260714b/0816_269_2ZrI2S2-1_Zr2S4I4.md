### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase and receives 95/100. CS supports this because several chemically related transition-metal chalcogen-halides are hull-stable or nearly hull-stable: NbSe₂Cl₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS1), Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS3), and Nb(SBr)₂ at 0.0008 eV/atom (CS4), although Ta₄S₉Br₈ is less stable at 0.132 eV/atom (CS2). SS/TS support is also strong because multiple low-dimensional iodide/halide analogues are reported at 0.0 eV/atom, including NbSeI₃, CrSBr, CsTaI₆, TiI₃, and HfI₄ (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide finite-temperature phase field, and several analogues show difficult phase purity (CS3, SS1 / TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is not severe in temperature because close analogues form at 400–530 °C in sealed tubes or CVT (CS1–CS4), and the proposed 450–550 °C is far below the >1000 °C penalty threshold. However, vacuum sealing and volatile iodine/sulfur are required, as in NbSeI₃ and Ta/Nb chalcogen-halide syntheses (SS1 / TS1, CS2, CS4). Toxic/corrosive iodine imposes an additional handling penalty, consistent with iodine ampoule use in NbSeI₃, TiI₃, Ta iodides, and HfI₄ (SS1 / TS1, SS3 / TS3, SS4 / TS4, TS5). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that high pressure is needed, so high-pressure penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct solid-state kinetics are penalized because Zr metal is refractory and oxide-passivated [Reasonable Inference], while analogous element-based reactions often need long times: NbSeI₃ requires two weeks (SS1 / TS1), and NbX₂Y₂ transport can require 3–4 weeks (CS1). The volatile S/I₂ components may assist transport, as seen in sealed ampoule syntheses (CS2, CS4, SS1 / TS1), but the target framework must form completely from elements. Remaining uncertainty is whether a transient ZrI₄ vapor phase accelerates Zr transport enough to offset the refractory metal starting material; TS5 suggests group-4 iodides can be volatile, but it is HfI₄ rather than ZrS₂I₂.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT/vapor-assisted growth uses moderate temperatures but requires vacuum, sealed ampules, volatile iodine, and a temperature gradient. These requirements are directly supported by NbX₂Y₂ CVT at 400–515 °C with evacuated tubes and small gradients (CS1), CrSBr CVT in evacuated quartz (SS2 / TS2), and iodide transport in Ta/Ti systems (SS3 / TS3, SS4 / TS4). The multi-step nature—loading, sealing, gradient growth, product separation—matches CS1 and SS3 / TS3. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for high pressure, so no high-pressure deduction is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable relative to direct reaction because vapor transport can bypass long-range solid diffusion. CS1 is particularly strong because NbX₂Y₂ layered chalcogen-halides are explicitly grown by chemical transport under similar temperature ranges. Composition control remains the main kinetic/phase issue: phase-pure NbSeI₃ is reported difficult (SS1 / TS1), CrSBr forms CrBr₃/Cr₂S₃ byproducts (SS2 / TS2), and NbX₂Y₂ requires stoichiometry/halogen control (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is the correct iodine chemical potential window for ZrS₂I₂, which is not directly provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate condition severity: it uses sealed evacuated tubes and volatile iodine, like the iodine-based routes in SS1 / TS1, SS3 / TS3, SS4 / TS4, and TS5. It is treated as multi-step because ZrS₂ must be obtained/dried and then iodinated, analogous in spirit to the NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ binary-precursor reaction in CS3. The temperature window of 400–550 °C is supported by CS3 at 530 °C and CS1/CS4 at 400–515/500 °C. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure or >1000 °C requirements.

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 scores best mechanistically because ZrS₂ supplies pre-existing Zr–S connectivity, reducing Zr diffusion relative to elemental Zr [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 supports binary chalcogenide-to-chalcogen-halide conversion through NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ at 530 °C, although it also warns that reproducibility and phase stability can be poor. The target still requires S–S dimer formation and iodide incorporation, so reconstruction is only partial, not trivial. Remaining uncertainty is whether iodine oxidizes/reconstructs ZrS₂ to the desired S₂-containing layer instead of producing ZrI₄, residual ZrS₂, sulfur, or other iodide-rich phases.

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux/self-flux growth has moderate temperature but significant handling complexity. CS5 supports molten halide flux growth for a sulfide bromide at ~700 K, and TS5 supports closed-system group-4 iodide sublimation with moisture exclusion. Vacuum/dry handling, volatile iodine/ZrI₄, and flux-removal steps lead to the same severity deductions as Routes 2–3. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high-pressure synthesis, so no high-pressure penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 4

A molten/volatile iodide flux should improve mass transport, as flux crystallization is demonstrated for Nd sulfide bromide in NdBr₃ (CS5) and sublimation growth is effective for HfI₄ (TS5). However, this route is less directly supported for the exact Zr–S–I layered topology than Routes 2–3. Flux chemistry can also shift composition toward iodide-rich or sulfur-poor phases, and CS5 notes air sensitivity/alteration in the flux-grown sulfide bromide. The main uncertainty is whether ZrI₄/I₂ acts as a benign flux or consumes Zr/S into competing iodides.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger the amorphization check; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on cation disorder and are treated as neutral 50 for that sub-check. The target structure is simple and layered, with one Zr, two S, and one I site described, so a >40-atom unit cell is not clearly triggered; CS/SS/TS again provide insufficient direct unit-cell-count evidence and are neutral 50. Mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral coexistence is not described for the target, unlike some structurally more complex analogues such as CsTaI₆ or KVP₂S₇ (SS3, SS5), so amorphization risk is judged low. Remaining uncertainty is that layered materials can exfoliate or disorder under vapor growth, but the CS/SS/TS data do not indicate amorphous-product tendencies.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrS₂I₂ is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related chalcogen-halide/iodide analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, CS3, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: the closest structural/chemical analogue Nb(SBr)₂ adopts the same monoclinic C2/m layered motif and is made in a sealed glass tube at 500 °C (CS4), while NbX₂Y₂ chalcogen-halides are grown by chemical transport at 400–515 °C (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is iodine/metal-iodide volatility, which should promote transport and crystallization at temperatures far below Zr metal’s melting point, consistent with iodide transport and sublimation examples (SS1 / TS1, SS3 / TS3, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/composition control: several analogues report byproducts, mixed polymorphs, or difficulty obtaining phase-pure material (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1 / TS1, SS2 / TS2). Overall, the target is quite synthesizable but likely requires sealed-ampule iodine chemical-potential control rather than a simple open solid-state reaction.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy (from CS/SS/TS) |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile iodine and sulfur loss / ampule pressure | High | Use evacuated sealed quartz/fused-silica ampules and staged low-temperature holds, as used for NbSeI₃, Ta/S/Br₂, TiI₃, and Ta iodide systems (SS1 / TS1, CS2, SS4 / TS4, SS3 / TS3). |
| 2 | All | Iodine chemical potential may favor ZrI₄ or iodide-rich phases | High | Use small gradients and carefully controlled I₂ charge; related NbX₂Y₂ transport needs halogen/stoichiometry control (CS1), and iodide systems rely on gradients to separate products (SS3 / TS3, TS5). |
| 3 | All | Competing binary/ternary phases and poor phase purity | High | Expect screening of stoichiometry and gradient; phase-pure NbSeI₃ is difficult (SS1 / TS1), CrSBr forms CrBr₃/Cr₂S₃ byproducts (SS2 / TS2), and Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ reproducibility is poor (CS3). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow reaction of refractory Zr metal | Medium–High | Use fine Zr powder, prereact at low T with iodine/sulfur, or switch to ZrS₂ precursor; long sealed-tube durations are used for NbSeI₃ and NbX₂Y₂ (SS1 / TS1, CS1). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Crystal growth requires stable thermal gradient | Medium | Use two-zone furnace or long tube with 5–80 °C gradient; CS1 uses 5–20 °C gradients for NbX₂Y₂, and SS3 / TS3 uses 500→180 °C gradient for Ta iodide growth. |
| 6 | Route 3 | Topochemical conversion may stop at partially iodinated ZrS₂ or decompose | Medium | Extend reaction to 1–2 weeks and use slight I₂ excess; binary precursor conversion in CS3 required 2–3 weeks at 530 °C. |
| 7 | Route 4 | Flux removal and air/moisture sensitivity of iodide-rich products | Medium | Handle in dry box and remove excess iodide by sublimation/temperature gradient; dry-box handling is used in Ta iodide synthesis (SS3 / TS3), and HfI₄ purification uses sublimation in a closed dry system (TS5). |
| 8 | All | Polymorph or stacking disorder in layered product | Medium | Favor slow cooling and crystal growth rather than quench; analogues show polymorph formation in Nb(SBr)₂ (CS4) and phase-purity difficulty in NbSeI₃ (SS1 / TS1). |
