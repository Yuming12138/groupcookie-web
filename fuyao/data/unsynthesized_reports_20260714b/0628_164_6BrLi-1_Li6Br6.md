### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.034 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS and CS include metastable LiI at ~0.0289 eV/atom, and SS/TS include Li₆MgBr₈ at ~0.020 eV/atom, showing that halide phases with similar small hull energies can be relevant. However, CS/SS/TS do not directly confirm the target Pc LiBr phase, so the score is high but not 100.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for high-vacuum cryogenic deposition, which is explicitly required in CS and TS. CS also requires precursor drying and protected XRD handling, making the process multi-step. The temperature itself is moderate, since CS uses 430 °C and TS uses 300–600 °C, so no high-temperature deductions apply.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

LiBr vapor availability is favorable because CS directly evaporates LiBr and TS supports alkali-halide evaporation from heated tungsten bands. The crystallization pathway is uncertain because CS/TS demonstrate metastable Li-halide trapping but not the exact monoclinic Pc sheet. Composition control is relatively favorable for a single-source binary LiBr route, but CS/TS do not give a quantitative composition window.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has moderate severity because SS/TS bromide syntheses require evacuated or Ar-sealed ampoules, anhydrous precursors, and controlled cooling or quenching. SS gives slow cooling at 2–5 °C/h for bromide melts, and TS gives evacuated-ampoule annealing/quenching for Li₂FeBr₄. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalties apply based on SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Bulk melt kinetics should be good because SS/TS show bromide salts can react or crystallize from molten/annealed states at moderate temperatures. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction: the Pc 2D LiBr target is not the common equilibrium rock-salt structure, and CS/TS suggest metastable Li-halide polymorphs are most accessible by low-temperature deposition. Therefore Route 2 is kinetically plausible but phase-selectivity-limited.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the same severity deductions as Route 1 because it uses high-vacuum LiBr evaporation, dry handling, and multiple processing steps. CS supports the need for high vacuum, cryogenic substrate cooling, and protected characterization. The added substrate-templating step increases experimental complexity, but the formal F2 deduction table does not include an extra substrate penalty.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Route 3 receives the best F3 score because it combines the demonstrated LiBr vapor route from CS with a [Reasonable Inference] that a substrate may stabilize the target’s two-dimensional sheet. TS supports low-temperature condensation for metastable alkali-halide polymorphs, which is consistent with kinetic trapping. The uncertainty is that neither CS nor TS identifies a substrate that specifically templates monoclinic Pc LiBr.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered. The target description lists three Li and three Br inequivalent sites, and CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms. The structure has corner- and edge-sharing LiBr₄ tetrahedra, but not stated face sharing, so the strongest amorphization criterion is not fully triggered; the remaining risk is mainly low-temperature deposition disorder, as TS notes broadened diffraction lines for cryogenic alkali-halide deposits.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a metastable rather than ground-state LiBr polymorph because its E_hull is 0.034 eV/atom and CS/TS show related metastable Li-halide polymorphs can require low-temperature deposition. The CS evidence is particularly relevant because it directly involves LiBr/LiI evaporation and cryogenic deposition, while SS/TS bromide ampoule routes support general anhydrous bromide handling but less directly support the Pc 2D target. The most critical enabling factor is kinetic trapping at low temperature, supported by CS and TS. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection against ordinary equilibrium LiBr, since CS/TS indicate metastable Li-halide structures can transform on warming. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires thin-film or cryogenic methods rather than conventional equilibrium bulk synthesis.
