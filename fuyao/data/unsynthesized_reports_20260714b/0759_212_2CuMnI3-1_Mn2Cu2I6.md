### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0140 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues cluster in the same low-hull region: TlMnI3 has E_hull = 0.0170 eV/atom (TS1), TlCdI3 has E_hull = 0.0167 eV/atom (TS2), CuHgSI has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom (TS3), and CuTl2I3 has E_hull = 0.00686 eV/atom (TS5). Chemical and structural analogues also include on-hull or near-on-hull iodides such as CuGaI4 and CdI2 polytypes (CS2; SS3; SS4; SS5). The remaining uncertainty is that low computed hull energy does not guarantee that the specific trigonal P-31m Mn/Cu ordered layer is the easiest kinetic product.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule melt growth / Bridgman-type annealed melt

Severity is moderate-high because related Mn iodides required dry glovebox/Ar handling, melting, powdering, and long annealing at 350 °C for 3 weeks (CS1; SS1; TS1). CuGaI4 shows that CuI-containing iodide melts can be crystallized in sealed ampoules by slow cooling from 573 K (CS2), supporting feasible but controlled conditions. Kinetically, binary iodides are relatively reactive, but target formation requires Mn/Cu ordering into a 2D mixed-polyhedral sheet, and repeated annealing in TlMnI3/TlFeI3 suggests nontrivial phase equilibration (CS1; CS3; TS1). The main uncertainty is whether MnCuI3 melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically, since incongruent behavior is noted for TlMnI3 (CS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal / acidothermal iodide crystallization in concentrated HI

This route receives the best F2 because TS4/TS5 demonstrate iodide framework crystallization at only 160–175 °C in autoclaves, with no high pressure, no >1000 °C heating, and no inert gas atmosphere specified. Kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated transport in concentrated HI should bypass solid-state diffusion **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CuTl2I3 formed in about 3 h at 160 °C (TS5). The route is strongly supported for Cu–iodide crystallization but only indirectly supported for Mn–Cu–I, because the closest Mn analogue TlMnI3 was prepared by melt/anneal rather than hydrothermal synthesis (CS1; TS1). The remaining uncertainty is Mn²⁺ iodide speciation and whether the desired layered P-31m network nucleates instead of binary MnI2/CuI or other iodocuprate species.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Low-temperature sealed-tube solid-state reaction / sintering

This route is supported by multiple low-temperature sealed-tube iodide syntheses: TlGaI4 at 200 °C for 96 h (CS4), CuHgSI at 553 K for 2 weeks (TS3), Ag2HgSI2 at 120 °C for 4 months (SS2), and TlMnI3 at 350 °C for 3 weeks (CS1; TS1). F2 is limited by vacuum/Ar sealed handling, volatile iodide concerns, and likely air-sensitive precursors, all of which are reflected in CS1/CS4/SS2/TS3. Kinetics are moderate: soft iodide lattices help reaction at low temperature, but long holds and possible repeated grinding indicate slow cation ordering (CS1; SS2; TS3). The uncertainty is whether the solid-state route can fully interdiffuse Mn and Cu at low temperature without forming persistent MnI2/CuI or alternative ternary iodides.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one structural complexity trigger: Mn and Cu have substantially different preferred coordination environments, with MnI6 octahedra and CuCuI3 trigonal pyramids in the provided target structure. The target does not trigger the >40 atom unit-cell criterion from the provided formula/description, and it has corner and edge sharing but no reported face-sharing coexistence. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior, so each source category is neutral for F4. The assigned score of 80 reflects low–medium amorphization risk but some risk of cation/coordination disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCuI3 is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0140 eV/atom, comparable to experimentally synthesized low-hull iodide analogues such as TlMnI3 and CuTl2I3 (TS1; TS5). The analogue support is strong chemically for Mn–I and Cu–I bonding motifs: TlMnI3 provides MnI6 octahedral iodide chemistry (CS1; SS1; TS1), while CuGaI4 and CuTl2I3 support Cu–I framework formation from CuI (CS2; TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of iodide salts or iodometalate species at low temperature, demonstrated by sealed-ampoule and HI hydrothermal syntheses (CS2; CS4; TS4; TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target requires ordered Mn²⁺/Cu⁺ incorporation into a 2D P-31m layer, whereas close analogues often required long annealing, repeated powdering, or yielded twinned/phase-sensitive crystals (CS1; CS3; TS4). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with hydrothermal HI crystallization recommended first for crystals and sealed-tube annealing as the most conservative powder route.
