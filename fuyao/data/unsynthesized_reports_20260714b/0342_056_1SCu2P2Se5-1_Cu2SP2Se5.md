### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Thermodynamic analogues CuInP2Se6, AgGa(PSe3)2, and AgVP2Se6 also have low E_hull values of 0.0173, 0.0161, and 0.0090 eV/atom, respectively (TS1–TS3). CS and SS also include low-hull layered P2Se6 analogues, supporting the idea that this structural/chemical family can be experimentally accessible (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is mixed S/Se and Cu-only, so its competing phases may differ from the heterometallic M′M″P2Se6 analogues.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate-low severity score because the required temperature, ~700–750 °C, is not extreme, but the route uses evacuated sealed tubes and volatile P/Se/S components (CS1–CS3; SS1, SS4; TS1; volatility is Reasonable Inference). Bridgman-type analogues require controlled cooling, quenching/annealing, or long post-anneals, which adds procedural complexity (CS2, CS3; SS1; TS1). CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signal for the vacuum/melt route. The main uncertainty is whether Cu2P2Se5S melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically like some analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, the melt route benefits from liquid or partially liquid transport, which is consistent with Bridgman synthesis of layered selenodiphosphates at 750 °C (CS1–CS3; TS1). The structural reconstruction burden is modest because the target preserves P–P and P–Ch tetrahedral units similar to P2Se6 analogues (CS2; SS4). However, controlled cooling and annealing are often needed in this family, implying nontrivial phase-selection kinetics (CS2, CS3; TS1). The mixed Se/S sublattice may slow ordering relative to pure selenide analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less severe than the melt route because direct annealing analogues are made at 600–700 °C without high pressure or specialized growth equipment (SS2, SS3, CS5; TS3). It still requires evacuated sealed ampoules because P/Se/S are volatile and oxidation-sensitive at high temperature [Reasonable Inference; sealed ampoules in SS2/SS3/CS5]. CS5 and SS2/SS3 provide strong route evidence; TS gives supportive evidence through TS3. Remaining uncertainty is whether the Cu-only target needs a narrower thermal window than Ag/V/Sc/In analogues.

#### F3 — Route 2

The long anneal route has slower kinetics because it relies primarily on solid-state diffusion, and analogues require 7–17 days at 600–700 °C (SS2, SS3, CS5). The P2Se6/P2S6 layered framework is structurally supported by SS2/SS3/CS5, but by-products are reported in related rare-earth analogues, showing phase competition (SS3). The score is therefore moderate rather than high. Use of prereacted binaries or intermediate grinding could improve kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Iodine-assisted synthesis uses standard evacuated ampoules but introduces I2, making the atmosphere more reactive and chemically more complex (CS4). Temperatures remain moderate, as CS4 uses 600–950 °C for Cu chalcogenides, and the proposed target route can likely stay near 650–750 °C [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides the main signal; SS and TS have insufficient direct evidence for iodine-assisted P2Ch6 synthesis and are neutral for this route. The main severity penalty is possible iodide impurity formation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Iodine can enhance mass transport in Cu chalcogenide systems, as CS4 explicitly uses iodine to accelerate reaction. This should help overcome slow Cu/chalcogen diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS4 is structurally a stannite-type Cu2FeSiSe4, not a layered P2Ch6 analogue, so structural selectivity is uncertain. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for iodine transport in the target structure type and are treated as neutral for that specific kinetic transfer.

#### F2 — Route 4

The flux route is proposed only by [Reasonable Inference]; CS, SS, and TS provide insufficient direct evidence and are neutral for this route. It could operate at lower temperatures, but it still requires sealed vacuum handling due to volatile P/Se/S and involves multiple steps such as flux soak, slow cooling, and flux removal. The severity is therefore similar to Route 3 despite lower temperature. The uncertainty is high because external flux compatibility with Cu2P2Se5S is unknown.

#### F3 — Route 4

A chalcogen-rich flux should improve precursor activity and diffusion by creating a reactive liquid medium [Reasonable Inference]. However, no provided CS/SS/TS example confirms flux growth of the P2Ch6 layered analogues. Structural reconstruction and phase selectivity therefore remain uncertain, especially for the mixed S/Se target. This route is best considered exploratory rather than primary.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically distinct Cu and P sites, CuSe5/CuSe4S square pyramids, and PSe/PSeS tetrahedra, implying a significant cation size and bonding mismatch [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. However, the structure is built from well-defined crystalline P–P chalcogenophosphate tetrahedra and Cu polyhedra, and analogues crystallize as layered compounds rather than glassy phases (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS5). The target shows corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, reducing the amorphization penalty. The main uncertainty is mixed S/Se disorder, which may broaden diffraction peaks or create stacking disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu2P2Se5S is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0210 eV/atom, within the common synthesizable range. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of layered P2Se6-type chalcogenophosphates in evacuated ampoules at 600–750 °C, especially CuInP2Se6 and Ag/V/Sc P2Se6 phases (CS2; SS2–SS4; TS1, TS3). The most important enabling factor is the robust P–P chalcogenophosphate tetrahedral motif, which appears repeatedly in experimentally synthesized layered analogues (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS5). The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control for volatile P/Se/S and mixed S/Se occupancy, which may lead to Cu chalcogenides, P–chalcogen binaries, or chalcogen-deficient products [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, a sealed-ampoule long-anneal route is the most practical first attempt, with Bridgman/melt growth as a follow-up for larger crystals.
