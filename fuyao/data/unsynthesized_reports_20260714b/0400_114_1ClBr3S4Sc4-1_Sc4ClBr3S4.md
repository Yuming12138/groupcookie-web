### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin [Target]. The closest SS/TS analogues DySBr, HoSI, GdSI, DySI, and MnSc₂S₄ are also all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the idea that this structural/chemical family can be thermodynamic ground-state accessible [SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5]. CS analogues are less structurally relevant but show mostly low hull energies, including 0.0 eV/atom examples [CS1, CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that mixed Br/Cl ordering in Sc₄S₄Br₃Cl may have a narrow stability window not directly sampled by the unary-halide SS/TS analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the same sealed-ampoule, moderate-high-temperature regime as the strongest structural and thermodynamic analogues [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile halide components, and air/moisture-sensitive halides [SS1/SS4; TS1/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct signal for Sc–S–Br–Cl sealed-ampoule chemistry because the CS examples are mainly non-halide sulfides or unrelated synthesis routes, so CS is neutral 50 for F2 transferability. The route avoids >1000 °C temperatures and avoids high pressure, so it is not severe by solid-state standards [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate because Sc₂S₃ is a refractory sulfide precursor, but halide-assisted sealed-tube synthesis can promote transport and reaction at 750–900 °C [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4; SS5/TS5]. The structural reconstruction penalty is limited because the product is closely related to DySBr/LnSI layered MSX phases with octahedral MS₄X₂ sheets and L-shaped halide coordination [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. Mixed Br/Cl ordering is the main kinetic uncertainty because no SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates ordered mixed Cl/Br occupancy [Reasonable Inference]. CS is neutral 50 for this point due to insufficient direct structural/halide relevance.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 follows the direct elemental logic of Ln + S + I sealed-ampoule reactions that produced phase-pure LnSI [SS2–SS3; TS2–TS3]. It has the same vacuum/sealed-tube and volatile-component penalties as Route 1, and potentially higher practical hazard if Br₂/Cl₂ are used rather than ScBr₃/ScCl₃ [SS1/SS4; TS1/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids external high pressure and can remain below 1000 °C, which is favorable [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. CS gives insufficient evidence for elemental Sc sulfide halide synthesis and is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are less favorable than Route 1 because elemental Sc is dense/refractory and may react more slowly than preformed Sc halides/sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. The route is nevertheless plausible because SS/TS direct elemental routes produce phase-pure lanthanoid sulfide iodides at 900 °C [SS2; TS2]. Formation of the same layered motif is strongly supported structurally [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4], but reaching the ordered mixed Br/Cl target from elemental or semi-elemental sources likely requires longer annealing or regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. CS is again insufficiently specific and neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because the Sc-containing CVT analogue uses 1050 °C for one month after an initial high-temperature treatment [SS5; TS5]. It also requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile iodine/halide transport, and a multi-step workflow [SS5; TS5]. The route is not high-pressure, but it is operationally more demanding than the direct sealed-ampoule route [SS5; TS5]. CS provides no useful signal for Sc sulfide-halide CVT and is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic advantage of CVT is enhanced mass transport, which is exactly why SS5/TS5 used iodine transport to improve Sc-containing sulfide crystal quality [SS5; TS5]. This can help overcome slow Sc diffusion and may yield better single crystals [SS5; TS5]. The main risk is incongruent transport or iodine substitution/contamination in a mixed Br/Cl phase [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, Route 3 has good kinetics but worse condition severity than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The formula contains 12 atoms, and no evidence suggests a >40-atom unit cell in the provided description [Target]. The structure contains corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not met [Target]. Remaining uncertainty is that mixed Br/Cl ordering could produce local disorder, but that is a crystallographic ordering issue rather than a strong amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₄S₄Br₃Cl is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis because multiple 2D rare-earth sulfide halides with MS₄X₂ octahedral sheets and L-shaped halide coordination are made as phase-pure or single-phase crystalline materials in sealed ampoules [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to DySBr/LnSI-type layered sulfide halides, which suggests the target topology is chemically realistic [SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling mixed Br/Cl stoichiometry under sealed high-temperature conditions, because halides are volatile and no SS/TS example directly demonstrates ordered mixed Br/Cl occupancy [SS1/SS4; TS1/TS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS data are mostly useful only as weak confirmation that complex sulfides can be made by solid-state or sealed routes, but CS is insufficient for the key halide-ordering question and is treated as neutral 50.
