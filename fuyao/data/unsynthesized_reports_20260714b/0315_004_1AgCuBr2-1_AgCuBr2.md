### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0050 eV/atom, so it is essentially on the convex hull and receives a high stability score by the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar small positive hull energies are experimentally accessible, including KAg3S2 at 0.0093 eV/atom (TS-1), Hg3Se2I2 at 0.0053 eV/atom (TS-3), and Hg2SnS2Br2 at 0.0057 eV/atom (TS-5). CS also supports high thermodynamic feasibility because AgGaSe2 and one AgGaS2 entry are hull-stable or nearly hull-stable and synthesized by conventional bulk routes (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). SS gives mixed signal because structurally similar Ag3SI entries include metastable energies up to 0.116 eV/atom (SS-1), so structural analogy alone does not guarantee equilibrium stability.

#### F2 — Route 1: Vacuum-Sealed Solid-State Annealing

The route uses moderate temperature and no external pressure, supported by evacuated-ampoule solid-state synthesis in SS-2 and TS-5. The score is reduced for vacuum sealing, sealed volatile bromide control, multiple grinding/annealing steps, and Cu(I) air-sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 specifically shows that Ag-halide solid-state reactions may require regrinding/pelletizing and long annealing. Remaining uncertainty is whether AgCuBr2 forms before AgBr/CuBr phase separation or cation disorder dominates.

#### F3 — Route 1: Vacuum-Sealed Solid-State Annealing

Binary bromides should be more reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and low-temperature halide/chalcogenide solid-state reactions are demonstrated by SS-3 and TS-5. However, solid-state diffusion and Ag/Cu ordering in a 1D ribbon are likely slower than melt or solution routes [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 reports long annealing and phase-specific treatments for Ag3SI, lowering the structural reconstruction score. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact Ag–Cu bromide solid-state reaction, so uncertainty remains moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2: Melt-and-Anneal / Bridgman Growth

This route avoids extreme pressure and uses temperatures similar to CS-1 and SS-1. Deductions come from vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile bromide containment, and multi-step melt/slow-cool/anneal processing, all consistent with sealed melt protocols in CS-1 and SS-1. It is less severe than high-pressure routes in CS-2 or CS-3’s pressure-study context, because no >5 GPa condition is required. The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility and bromide vapor pressure at the melt temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Melt-and-Anneal / Bridgman Growth

Melt processing gives high precursor activity and rapid mass transport, as demonstrated by Ag-containing melt-and-anneal synthesis in CS-1 and Ag3SI Bridgman growth in SS-1. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because crystallizing an ordered 1D Ag/Cu bromide phase may require controlled cooling or annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 notes mixtures of phases from which crystals are separated, indicating phase-purity challenges. Overall kinetics are favorable compared with direct solid-state annealing.

#### F2 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / Halobromide Growth

Hydrothermal bromide synthesis operates at moderate temperature, with SS-4 using 670 K and sealed ampoules/autoclaves. It avoids vacuum and high temperature, but the sealed corrosive HBr medium, autogenous pressure, and multi-step loading/cooling/washing justify deductions. Toxic heavy elements in SS-4 are not transferred to AgCuBr2, but concentrated HBr and Cu(I) air sensitivity remain hazards [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether AgBr is sufficiently soluble in the bromide medium to nucleate AgCuBr2 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / Halobromide Growth

Solution-mediated transport can overcome diffusion barriers, and SS-4 reports quantitative reaction and high-quality crystals for CuHgSBr in concentrated HBr. This supports good ion mobility and precursor activity for bromide-containing Cu(I) systems. The structural reconstruction score is limited because SS-4 is not chemically identical and includes Hg/S, while the target is a binary mixed-metal bromide. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for direct AgCuBr2 hydrothermal formation, so this route is plausible but less certain than melt growth.

#### F2 — Route 4: Chemical Vapor Transport / Bromine-Assisted Transport

CVT uses moderate temperatures and no external high pressure, supported by gas-phase growth of thermodynamic halide analogues in evacuated ampoules (TS-4). Deductions arise from vacuum control, volatile/corrosive transport agents, multi-step gradient operation, and hazardous brominating species [Reasonable Inference]. TS-2 also indicates that one-dimensional AuBr formation can require weeks and controlled dry/vacuum conditions. The uncertainty is high because no source directly reports Ag–Cu bromide CVT.

#### F3 — Route 4: Chemical Vapor Transport / Bromine-Assisted Transport

Vapor transport can provide high mobility, and TS-4 demonstrates halide framework crystal growth from the gas phase. The 1D AuBr analogue in TS-2 supports the feasibility of linear M–Br chain motifs. However, TS-2 also reports sluggish transformations and phase-control difficulty, lowering the structural reconstruction score. Cu(I)/Cu(II) redox control under bromine chemical potential is an additional inferred kinetic/chemical uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple 1D ribbon structure and no evidence of a large >40-atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing in the provided target description. One amorphization trigger is assigned because Ag(I) and Cu(I) have significantly different sizes and the target bond lengths differ substantially, Ag–Br 2.57 Å versus Cu–Br 2.28 Å [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for amorphization tendency in AgCuBr2, so their contribution is neutral except for analogous phase-control issues in SS-1/SS-2. The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCuBr2 is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0050 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from sealed melt/anneal and Bridgman precedents for Ag-containing halide/chalcogenide materials in CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, and SS-1, while TS analogues show that low-Ehull soft halide frameworks can be obtained in evacuated ampoules or vapor routes (TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the soft, low-melting, highly mobile bromide chemistry, which should allow equilibration by melt, flux, or solution-mediated routes [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1 and SS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control: AgBr/CuBr residual phases, Cu(I) oxidation, and Br volatility may prevent clean formation unless sealed conditions and slow cooling are controlled [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-1, SS-2, TS-2]. Overall, the material appears synthetically accessible, with sealed melt-and-anneal the most robust first attempt.
