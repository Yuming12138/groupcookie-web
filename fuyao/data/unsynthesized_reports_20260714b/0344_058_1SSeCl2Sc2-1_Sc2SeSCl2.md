### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0070 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore merits a 90–100 score. Structurally similar LnSBr/LnSI/ErSeI phases are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in SS1–SS5, supporting the plausibility of this layered rare-earth/scandium chalcogenide-halide structure family. TS1/TS3/TS5 include low-hull halide or sulfide-halide analogues near 0.007–0.019 eV/atom, indicating that small positive hull energies can still correspond to isolable phases. The remaining uncertainty is whether the mixed S/Se/Cl ordering in Sc₂SeSCl₂ is entropically or kinetically accessible rather than decomposing into Sc₂S₃, Sc₂Se₃, and ScCl₃.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions and high pressure, consistent with SS1–SS4 analogues synthesized at 750–900 °C in sealed ampoules. The severity penalties come from vacuum sealing and air-/moisture-sensitive ScCl₃, analogous to vacuum ampoule and glovebox practices in CS1–CS2 and sealed quartz reactions in SS3–SS4. Insufficient evidence from TS for binary Sc₂S₃/Sc₂Se₃/ScCl₃ synthesis specifically, so TS contributes a neutral 50 signal for the detailed condition severity. Overall condition severity is moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary precursors should reduce the chemical reconstruction compared with elements, and structurally similar DySBr/DySI routes use binary Dy₂S₃ plus Dy metal and halogen (SS3–SS4). The layered edge/corner-sharing octahedral motif is very close to SS1–SS5, which improves the structural reconstruction score. However, CS1–CS2 show that chalcogenide solid-state products may require multiple heating/regrinding cycles, and SS2–SS4 require 5–10 d anneals, so kinetics are not rated excellent. TS provides insufficient evidence for Sc-specific diffusion, so its diffusion signal is treated as neutral 50 except for the general flux/solid-state trends in TS4.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is operationally simple and follows elemental sealed-ampoule precedents from SS1–SS2 and CS5. It is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile S/Se/ScCl₃, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. It is not penalized for temperature above 1000 °C or high pressure, because the proposed conditions remain 750–850 °C and ambient sealed-ampoule pressure, consistent with SS1–SS4. TS gives insufficient evidence for elemental Sc + S/Se + ScCl₃ synthesis specifically, so it is neutral 50 for this route’s condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental Sc is likely the kinetic bottleneck because dense metal particles and surface oxide layers slow reaction; this is [Reasonable Inference] from solid-state chemistry. SS1–SS2 demonstrate that elemental rare-earth metals can form LnSI at 900 °C over days, but Sc is smaller and less fluxing than heavy lanthanoids. Vapor transport from S/Se and ScCl₃ can help, but the initial metal-to-chalcogenide/chloride conversion is more reconstructive than Route 1. Hence Route 2 is feasible but kinetically less favorable than the binary route.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more conditionally severe because it adds flux/CVT optimization, possible transport agents, and post-growth separation. SS5 and TS1/TS5 support high-vacuum halide transport but also indicate that halide systems may require specialized handling and phase-window control. CS5 and TS4 show flux methods can be done with standard sealed ampoules, so the route does not require high pressure or ultra-high temperature. The main severity is chemical handling rather than thermal extremity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux or CVT should improve kinetics by providing liquid/vapor-mediated mass transport, as supported by CS5, TS3, TS4, and SS5. This gives Route 3 the highest F3 score despite its condition complexity. The drawback is phase selectivity: TS1/TS5 show chloride CVT can stabilize different polymorphs at different deposition temperatures, and SS5 notes product-zone and competing-phase challenges in related ErSeX chemistry. Thus Route 3 is excellent for crystal growth but less straightforward for bulk phase-pure synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The target structure is described as a simple layered Pmm2 phase with two Sc sites, one S, one Se, and two Cl sites, and no evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40 atom unit cell. The target and closest SS analogues have mixed edge/corner sharing, but not the vertex + edge + face coexistence required to trigger C3. Therefore amorphization tendency is low, although stacking disorder in a 2D layered phase remains a possible minor risk by [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂SeSCl₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0070 eV/atom, which is well within the experimentally accessible range. The structural evidence is strong: SS1–SS5 describe closely related layered lanthanoid chalcogenide halides with edge/corner-sharing LnCh₄X₂ octahedra and L-shaped halide coordination, directly paralleling the target ScSe₂S₂Cl₂ octahedral sheets. The chemical evidence is moderately supportive because CS1–CS2 demonstrate vacuum sealed sulfide/selenide solid-state synthesis, while CS5 supports flux-assisted sulfide crystal growth. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known 2D rare-earth chalcogenide halides in SS1–SS5. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the exact S/Se/Cl stoichiometry with volatile, moisture-sensitive chloride and chalcogen components.
