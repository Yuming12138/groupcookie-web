### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. The closest α-HfNBr analogue is also on the hull and structurally nearly identical in Hf–N and Hf–Br bonding (SS-1, TS-1). CS analogues include multiple stable nitride chloride/chalcogenide halides, but because they are lanthanide sulfide-containing phases rather than Hf nitride halides, CS provides only general support; insufficient evidence from CS for exact Hf4N4Br3Cl stability beyond the target E_hull.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because the closest analogue α-HfNBr is made at only 760 °C for 12 h in a fused silica tube, with no high pressure or specialized apparatus (SS-1, TS-1). The main severity deduction is volatile ammonium halide chemistry in a sealed system, especially because NH4Cl is added for the target [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate rather than excellent: Hf metal is refractory [Reasonable Inference], but the structural reconstruction is minimal because the target is an ordered Br/Cl derivative of α-HfNBr (SS-1/TS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate F2 severity because CS flux/azide syntheses require evacuated silica vessels, 850 °C, 7 d, controlled heating, and hazardous NaN3 (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). Fluxing is beneficial for reaction completion and crystal growth, as CS-3 explicitly states NaCl or excess MCl3 secures complete/fast reaction and single crystals. However, F3 remains moderate because the CS examples are lanthanide sulfide chloride nitrides, so structural transfer to Hf4N4Br3Cl is indirect; insufficient evidence from SS/TS confirms this exact flux route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because it is inherently multi-step: α-HfNBr must first be prepared, then partially chloride-exchanged or annealed with a chloride source (SS-1, SS-3; Reasonable Inference). The topochemical precedent is encouraging because α-HfNBr reacts with Na2S to form a related Hf–N layered sulfide at 750 °C, but SS-3 reports phase purity depends strongly on rapid heating and temperature. Kinetically, starting from α-HfNBr minimizes framework reconstruction, yet selective 25% Cl ordering is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has moderate-to-high condition severity due to NH3 atmosphere, volatile/moisture-sensitive Hf halides, and purification needs; CS-1 specifically notes ammonolysis and subsequent sublimation or thermal treatment in NH3. Kinetically, ammonolysis benefits from reactive molecular precursors and low reaction temperature, but crystallization into the ordered α-type Hf4N4Br3Cl sheet may require post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for ammonolysis of the Hf mixed Br/Cl compound, so this route is less preferred than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so there is no cation-radius mismatch penalty. The described formula unit is small and the reported connectivity involves edge- and corner-sharing but not face-sharing polyhedra, giving low amorphization risk. SS-1/TS-1 support low structural complexity by showing the parent α-HfNBr has a simple 2D layered sheet with edge/corner-sharing NHf4 tetrahedra.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf4N4Br3Cl is likely a true ground-state or very strongly stable ordered mixed-halide phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS: α-HfNBr is nearly isostructural, thermodynamically stable, and experimentally synthesized by a simple Hf + NH4Br sealed-tube reaction at 760 °C (SS-1, TS-1). CS data support the broader feasibility of nitride-halide formation in sealed ampoules, fluxes, and ammonolysis, but are less directly applicable because they focus mainly on lanthanide sulfide chloride nitrides (CS-1 to CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a very close α-HfNBr parent structure accessible under moderate conditions (SS-1/TS-1). The main limiting factor is controlling Br/Cl stoichiometry and ordering without losing volatile ammonium halides or forming separate HfNBr/HfNCl domains [Reasonable Inference].
