### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest scoring bin and suggests it is near synthesizable metastability rather than strongly unstable. CS strongly supports stability because the closest listed Tb5Au2Bi analogue has E_hull = 0.0068 eV/atom, and Dy/Er analogues are also near the hull (CS-1 to CS-3). SS also supports this because the closest structural match is the same Tb5Au2Bi-type phase (SS-1), while TS shows similar materials can be synthesized even at ~0.03–0.06 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that the target’s local coordination description differs from the CS/SS Tb5Au2Bi entry, implying possible polymorphism or structure-model ambiguity.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate annealing temperature, 700 °C for 15 days, but also requires arc-melting, evacuated-tube annealing, and quenching (CS-1, SS-1). The penalties arise mainly from vacuum sealing, multi-step processing, Bi volatility risk, and Tb/product air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference; air instability noted in CS-1/CS-5]. CS and SS provide strong direct evidence that these conditions are sufficient for related phases, while TS gives supportive but less specific evidence through Tb5Au2Sb and related thermodynamic analogues (TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether the target’s exact local coordination is obtained or whether the known CS/SS structural variant dominates.

#### F3 — Route 1

Dense metals have low initial precursor activity, but arc-melting creates a homogenized alloy button, improving reaction kinetics (CS-1, SS-1). The 15-day anneal indicates cation ordering and phase equilibration are not instantaneous, so kinetics are only moderate (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2). The strongest positive kinetic evidence is that the same structure family is reported across Tb, Dy, Er, Ho and Sb/Bi variants (CS-1 to CS-5; SS-1 to SS-4). The remaining uncertainty is Bi loss during melting and whether repeated remelting fully eliminates local inhomogeneity.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because the thermodynamic analogue route uses 1000–1050 °C annealing, Ta tubes, evacuated silica jackets, and long durations (TS-1). CS/SS give insufficient evidence that such high-temperature Ta-tube conditions are necessary for Tb5Au2Bi, so CS/SS are neutral for this route. The method may be valuable if silica attack or incomplete ordering occurs, but it increases volatility and containment risks [Reasonable Inference]. Its severity score is therefore lower than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Higher temperature should improve diffusion, and TS-1 shows rare-earth–Au–p-block phases can be annealed for hundreds of hours in Ta/silica assemblies. However, TS-1 is not the same structure type as Tb5Au2Bi, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity are less certain. CS/SS do not directly validate high-temperature Ta-tube synthesis for the target, making the route plausible but not preferred. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether heating near 1000 °C promotes the desired I4/mcm phase or instead competing Tb–Au/Tb–Bi binaries.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from a simpler melting concept but uses high induction-melting temperatures comparable to rare-earth–Au alloy synthesis, 1200–1500 °C (TS-5). SS-5 supports alloy melting and homogenization in Ta crucibles/quartz, but it also reports phase-control complexity in lanthanide–Au systems. CS gives insufficient evidence for induction melting of the ternary target, so CS is neutral for this route. The route is penalized for high temperature, possible Bi loss, air-sensitive Tb, and the likely need for post-annealing.

#### F3 — Route 3

Full melting should help overcome diffusion limitations, and induction melting is established for rare-earth–Au alloys (TS-5). However, SS-5 indicates multiple competing/polymorphic R–Au phases and occasional need for special cooling, reducing confidence in direct formation of the ternary Tb5Au2Bi phase. Without the direct 700 °C anneal/quench protocol from CS/SS, induction melting alone is likely insufficient [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, Route 3 is plausible mainly as a variant of Route 1 rather than a standalone optimized route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The structure is an ordered intermetallic with a relatively small conventional formula in the closest CS/SS representation, Tb10Bi2Au4 = 16 atoms (CS-1, SS-1), so the unit-cell-size amorphization trigger is not met. The target description does not indicate simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing networks; it mentions corner-sharing trigonal bipyramids, while analogues mention corner-sharing octahedra (CS-1, SS-1). A radius-mismatch trigger is assigned from metallic-size differences among Tb/Au/Bi [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct radius data. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tb5Au2Bi is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline stable intermetallic, because the target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while the closest CS/SS Tb5Au2Bi entry is very near the hull (CS-1, SS-1). The analogue support is strong: multiple R5Au2Bi/Sb phases with the same tetragonal I4/mcm family are reported from arc-melting plus 700 °C annealing and quenching (CS-1 to CS-5; SS-1 to SS-4; TS-2 to TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is liquid/alloy homogenization by arc-melting followed by long annealing, which addresses slow diffusion and ordering (CS-1, SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase/composition control, especially Bi volatility, Tb oxidation, and possible competition with Tb–Au or Tb–Bi binaries [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues supported by SS-5]. The target should be experimentally accessible in a standard intermetallic synthesis laboratory, but phase verification and careful handling are essential.
