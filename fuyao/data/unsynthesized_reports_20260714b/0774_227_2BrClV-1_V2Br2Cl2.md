### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. CS analogues CrSBr, NbSe₂Cl₂, Mo₂SBr₂, and Nb₃Se₅Cl₇ are on the hull or near-hull, indicating that low-dimensional halide/chalcogenide-halide phases are not intrinsically thermodynamically inaccessible (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). SS/TS vanadium halide analogues are also on or very near the hull, supporting stable V–halide octahedral chemistry (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is alkali-free and mixed-halide ordered, which is not directly demonstrated in the analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule direct solid-state reaction

The severity score is moderate because the proposed temperature is below 1000 °C, consistent with CS-3 and CS-5 sealed-tube reactions, but the route still requires evacuated ampoules and air-sensitive/volatile halide handling as in SS/TS vanadium halide syntheses. Kinetically, VCl₂/VBr₂ should be more reactive than oxides, and SS/TS analogues show closely matching V–Cl and V–Br bond lengths, which lowers the reconstruction burden. However, diffusion and Br/Cl ordering in an alkali-free 2D sheet may require extended annealing; annealing needs are seen in CS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS directly confirms VBrCl formation by this route, so that uncertainty limits F3.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT / vapor-assisted crystallization

The severity score remains moderate because CVT requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile halogen species, directly supported by CS-1 and CS-2. The kinetic score is the best because vapor transport can overcome slow solid-state diffusion, and CS-1/CS-2 demonstrate crystallization of 2D halide-containing materials through temperature gradients. The main physicochemical advantage is that volatile vanadium halide species can redistribute V, Br, and Cl under controlled chemical potentials by [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS-2 and CS-5 show narrow phase windows and reproducibility issues in related halide systems.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Bridgman / melt growth

The severity score is lower because SS/TS Bridgman analogues involve high temperatures, precursor synthesis, fusion, dry-box handling, and controlled growth rates. Kinetically, a melt or partial melt would be favorable for ion transport, and SS/TS sources demonstrate Bridgman growth of V-halide octahedral frameworks. However, the structural transferability is weaker because SS/TS analogues are alkali-containing AVX₃ phases, while the target is alkali-free and 2D. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS/TS confirms congruent melting of VBrCl, so Bridgman growth is plausible but risky.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered by Target data. The structure is described as a simple 2D sheet of edge-sharing VBr₃Cl₃ octahedra, with no mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity stated in the target description. SS/TS analogues have more complex connectivity in AVX₃, but that complexity does not transfer directly to the target because the target lacks alkali cuboctahedral sublattices. The remaining uncertainty is the exact crystallographic cell size, but the formula and site description suggest a small cell rather than >40 atoms.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VBrCl is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS data support sealed-tube and CVT synthesis of low-dimensional halide-containing materials, while the SS/TS data strongly support stable V–Br and V–Cl octahedral bonding with bond lengths matching the target. The most critical enabling factor is the excellent chemical and geometric compatibility of V–Br and V–Cl octahedra, evidenced by SS/TS vanadium halide analogues. The most critical limiting factor is mixed-halide stoichiometry and ordering under volatile halide conditions, because CS-2 and CS-5 show that related halide systems can have narrow phase windows. Overall, CVT or vapor-assisted sealed-tube crystallization is the most defensible first attempt.
