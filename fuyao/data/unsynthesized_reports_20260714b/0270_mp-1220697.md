### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold in the requested rubric. TS provides the most relevant thermodynamic signal: HfB12 at 0.056 eV/atom, ThB12 at 0.028 eV/atom, and Nb4B3C2 at 0.0502 eV/atom are all reported as synthesized, though some require HPHT or flux. CS and SS contain many stable borides, but they do not directly prove Nb2AlB6 stability; insufficient evidence from CS/SS for exact thermodynamic stability, scored neutral as supporting context only. Remaining uncertainty is whether 0.050 eV/atom corresponds to a quenchable metastable phase or to a phase only stable at finite temperature.

#### Route 1 — F2/F3 Rationale

CS ScNiB4 and CS Ce2NiB10, plus SS LuOsB4 and SS UFe2B6, support arc melting of elemental boride mixtures under Ar, so the route is chemically plausible. The severity score is reduced because SS/CS analogues use high temperatures and repeated melting/annealing, and Al volatility at high T is a [Reasonable Inference] risk requiring containment. Kinetics are limited by refractory Nb/B diffusion, consistent with SS Nb2B3 and CS MoB4 requiring high-temperature treatment. The favorable structural point is SS TiB2, which has the same P6/mmm omega motif and B–B distance matching the target.

#### Route 2 — F2/F3 Rationale

TS Nb4B3C2 and TS Nb3B3C show that Cu–Al auxiliary-bath flux can crystallize Nb–B-containing frameworks at ~1600 °C with slow cooling, and SS Nb2B3 shows Nb borides can be grown from Cu solution. F2 remains moderate because the route still needs >1400 °C, controlled Ar, multiple steps, and Al-rich melt handling. F3 is better than solid-state because molten flux mitigates refractory diffusion and supports crystal growth, as explicitly described in TS. Uncertainty remains because no CS/SS/TS entry is exactly Nb–Al–B P6/mmm, so Al incorporation versus formation of NbB2/Nb–Al binaries is not guaranteed.

#### Route 3 — F2/F3 Rationale

CS MoFe2B4 and CS MoB4 demonstrate that refractory borides can be made by vacuum sintering and long annealing, and SS TiB2 confirms omega-type boride frameworks are accessible. However, CS MoFe2B4 required extended annealing and CS MoB4 used 1500 °C vacuum sintering, indicating severe conditions and slow kinetics. F3 is low because elemental Nb and B are not highly reactive and solid-state diffusion is slow. The main uncertainty is whether Al can be retained during long vacuum anneals without segregation or volatilization.

#### Route 4 — F2/F3 Rationale

TS HfB12 and TS ThB12 indicate that near-hull borides can require HPHT synthesis at ~65 kbar and 1440–2100 °C, especially when metastable. F2 is therefore poor because high pressure, high temperature, encapsulation, and quenching are severe. F3 is moderate because pressure and temperature can accelerate formation, but TS also reports impurity/phase-control issues in HPHT borides. Since SS provides a strong ambient-pressure structural analogue in TiB2, HPHT is not the first choice; it is a contingency route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a compact P6/mmm omega-derived cell, and SS TiB2 has the same simple P6/mmm omega framework, lowering amorphization risk. The target contains both NbB12 and AlB12 cuboctahedra with edge/face sharing; no vertex+edge+face coexistence is described, so C3 is not triggered. C1 is conservatively triggered if using ionic radii for Nb vs Al, although metallic radii would reduce that concern; this uncertainty keeps the score in the low–medium risk range. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly addresses amorphization, so F4 relies mostly on target crystallography and structural analogy to SS TiB2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb2AlB6 is likely a mildly metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest structural support comes from SS TiB2, which shares the P6/mmm omega-type motif and identical 1.75 Å B–B distances, while TS supports synthesis of near-hull borides by flux or HPHT. The most critical enabling factor is using a molten Al-rich or Cu–Al flux to overcome refractory Nb/B diffusion and maintain Al chemical potential. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with NbB2/Nb–B binaries or Nb–Al intermetallics, plus possible Al loss at high temperature. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful stoichiometry and slow-cooling optimization.
