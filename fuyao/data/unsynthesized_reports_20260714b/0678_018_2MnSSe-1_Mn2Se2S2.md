### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a top thermodynamic stability score. This is reinforced by multiple stable chemically similar and thermodynamically similar chalcogenides with E_hull = 0 [CS1, CS3, CS5, TS1–TS5]. Structurally similar omega-like CrSe₂ and CrS₂ are near the hull, at 0.015 and 0.008 eV/atom respectively [SS3, SS4], supporting the plausibility of the layered edge-sharing octahedral motif. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee easy kinetic access or S/Se ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The route uses moderate high temperatures, not >1000 °C, consistent with MnSe₂ at 520–850 °C [SS1] and mixed transition-metal chalcogenides at ~800–850 °C [CS5, TS4]. The score is penalized because evacuated sealed tubes are required [SS1, CS5, TS4], S/Se are volatile at synthesis temperatures [Reasonable Inference], and analogues often use multiple firings or grinding cycles [SS1, CS5]. The remaining uncertainty is whether MnSeS forms directly rather than separating into MnS/MnSe/MnSe₂.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Direct elemental synthesis is proven for Mn–Se compounds [SS1], but dense metallic Mn requires solid-state diffusion, lowering precursor activity. Multiple firings and long anneals in SS1 and CS5 indicate kinetic barriers for phase homogenization. Complete reconstruction from elements into a layered ordered S/Se sheet is likely slower than flux-mediated crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Halide/salt flux growth

The flux route avoids >1000 °C conditions because the close structural analogue CrSe₂ is grown at 780 °C [SS3]. It still requires vacuum-sealed fused-silica tubes, dry flux handling, volatile chalcogen control, and multistep processing [SS3]. The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2: Halide/salt flux growth

Kinetics are favorable because LiCl/KCl flux provides a liquid medium, and phase-pure single crystals of omega-like 2D CrSe₂ were obtained by this approach [SS3]. The target shares the key edge-sharing octahedral sheet topology with SS3, so structural reconstruction is less severe than in direct solid-state synthesis. The main uncertainty is whether Mn²⁺/Mn³⁺ redox and mixed S/Se ordering fit the same flux chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Chemical vapor transport

CVT conditions are moderate in temperature but require evacuated ampoules, volatile halogens, and sealed-tube gradients [CS1, SS2/TS1]. Toxic/corrosive Br₂ or I₂ and possible volatile chalcogen pressure impose safety and stoichiometry penalties [CS1, SS2/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is needed, so the route remains feasible.

#### F3 — Route 3: Chemical vapor transport

Vapor transport improves mass transfer and can grow chalcogenide crystals, as shown for CrSBr and Nb₂Pd₃Se₈ [CS1, SS2/TS1]. However, the exact Mn–S–Se ternary has no direct CVT evidence from CS/SS/TS, and halogen chemistry may stabilize Mn halides or altered compositions [CS1; Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are good, but phase selectivity is less certain than flux growth.

#### F2 — Route 4: Chalcogen-rich alkali flux

This route benefits from low temperature, as K₂Se/Se flux growth occurs at ~310 °C [SS5/TS2]. The score is reduced by air-sensitive K₂Se/K₂S, glovebox/vacuum handling, sealed-tube operation, and post-growth washing [SS5/TS2]. Insufficient evidence from CS supports this route for MnSeS, so its condition advantages do not fully offset chemical risk.

#### F3 — Route 4: Chalcogen-rich alkali flux

A reactive alkali chalcogenide flux can strongly enhance diffusion and crystallization at low temperature [SS5/TS2]. However, SS5/TS2 explicitly reports mixed phases and difficulty obtaining pure AuCuSe₄, so phase selectivity may be poor. For MnSeS, the absence of direct Mn/S/Se evidence makes this route more speculative.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple one-cation composition and a small described structural motif, so it does not trigger the cation-radius or large-cell amorphization criteria [target structure]. It contains edge-sharing MnSe₃S₃ octahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity [target structure]. Remaining uncertainty is local S/Se disorder, but this is more a configurational-ordering issue than a strong amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnSeS is likely a **ground-state phase** because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from the structurally similar omega-like 2D CrSe₂ flux-grown phase [SS3] and from direct sealed-tube Mn–Se and mixed chalcogenide syntheses [SS1, CS5, TS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to flux-grown omega-like edge-sharing octahedral CrSe₂ [SS3]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with flux growth preferred for phase selectivity and crystallinity.
