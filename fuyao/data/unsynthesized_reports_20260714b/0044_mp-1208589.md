### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
TaMnO₄ has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff for high synthesizability [Target]. Similar synthesized oxides exist at comparable or slightly higher hull energies, including ReMnO₄ and FeNbO₄ [CS2, TS1]. The remaining uncertainty is that 0.0500 eV/atom is borderline and may indicate low-energy metastability rather than an unambiguous ground state [Target, TS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
The solid-state route scores well on condition severity because it uses ambient pressure and standard furnace processing, but loses points for >1000 °C firing and multi-step regrinding [TS1, CS5]. Kinetics are less favorable because Ta₂O₅ is refractory and analogous ceramic routes require long firing, controlled cooling, or repeated pellet treatment [TS1, CS5]. The main uncertainty is whether oxidizing conditions can suppress Mn(II)-rich Ta–Mn phases like the Ar-synthesized Mn₄Ta₂O₉ analogue [CS5, Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Flux growth has the best combined score because CS1 directly demonstrates Hydrophilite-derived MnWO₄ flux growth at 1050 °C with controlled cooling [CS1]. The liquid flux should improve mass transport relative to ceramic Ta₂O₅ diffusion, and TS3/TS4 show that flux methods can crystallize related complex oxides [TS3, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is flux chemistry: CS1 used W-containing flux, so TaMnO₄ needs a non-W flux whose Ta solubility and Mn valence control are not directly proven [CS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
High pressure is strongly supported by ReMnO₄, which is a Mn-containing Hydrophilite-derived analogue synthesized at 5.5 GPa and 1473 K [CS2, TS5]. The route loses F2 points because >5 GPa equipment is specialized and operationally difficult [CS2, TS5]. Kinetically, HPHT improves phase stabilization but does not remove Ta₂O₅ diffusion limitations unless a pre-reacted precursor is used [SS1, Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Laser-assisted melt synthesis is supported only indirectly by Ta-containing NiTa₂O₆ and Ti/Fe oxide laser analogues [SS3, SS4]. It is severely penalized because temperatures exceed 1700–1800 °C and Mn/O volatility or redox drift is likely under melt conditions [SS3, SS4, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for this route is treated as neutral source score 50 for those categories [CS, TS].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target is a compact octahedral oxide framework with corner and edge sharing but no reported face-sharing, so the connectivity check does not trigger [Target]. The cation-size mismatch is inferred to be small for octahedral Ta⁵⁺/Mn³⁺, and the Hydrophilite analogue CS1 is a small-cell P2/c structure rather than a large >40-atom cell [CS1, Target, Reasonable Inference]. The residual uncertainty is Mn³⁺ Jahn–Teller distortion, but the target structure already accommodates distorted MnO₆ octahedra [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaMnO₄ is best viewed as a borderline low-energy metastable phase rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis outlook is favorable because close Hydrophilite analogues are experimentally accessible by flux, ceramic, and high-pressure methods [CS1, TS1, CS2, TS5]. The most enabling factor is the strong structural match to MnWO₄ and FeNbO₄ Hydrophilite-type frameworks [CS1, TS1, Target]. The most limiting factor is maintaining Ta⁵⁺/Mn³⁺ chemistry without forming competing Mn(II)-tantalates, especially because the known Ta–Mn solid-state analogue under Ar forms Mn₄Ta₂O₉ instead [CS5, Target, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, flux growth under oxidizing conditions is the most balanced route because it combines structural precedent with reduced diffusion barriers [CS1, TS3, TS4].
