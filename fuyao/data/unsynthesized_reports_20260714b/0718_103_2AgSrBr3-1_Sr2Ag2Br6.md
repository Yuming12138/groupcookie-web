### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0150 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues at very similar hull energies are synthesized as crystalline bromides, including Na3GdBr6 at 0.0156 eV/atom and Na3YBr6 at 0.0148 eV/atom [TS1, TS2]. Chemically similar sources also include stable or near-stable ternary bromides, but they differ in cation chemistry from Sr/Ag, so CS is supportive but not definitive [CS1–CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the reported P-31m SrAgBr3 polymorph is the experimentally selected low-temperature phase or a metastable polymorph [Target; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures rather than high-pressure or ultrahigh-temperature methods, which is favorable and consistent with bromide analogues synthesized at 450–600 °C or 873 K [CS1, CS2, TS1/TS2]. The severity penalty comes mainly from vacuum sealing, volatile/corrosive halide containment, and anhydrous bromide handling [CS1, CS3, TS5]. CS and TS give strong evidence for sealed-ampoule feasibility, while SS gives moderate evidence for bromide ampoule annealing [CS2/CS3, SS2/SS5, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is AgBr loss or segregation, which is inferred rather than directly reported for SrAgBr3 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar atmosphere and sealed-ampoule penalties, but it intentionally uses a melt/flux and slow cooling, which are documented in KInBr3 and Li–Mg bromide analogues [CS1, TS5]. No high pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required, so the main operational difficulty is controlling halide volatility and maintaining stoichiometry in a sealed ampoule [CS1, CS3, TS5]. Evidence from CS is particularly relevant because CS1 is a layered ternary bromide made by slow cooling, and CS3 involves Ag/Cu halide systems in evacuated ampoules [CS1, CS3]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Ag-containing flux chemistry, so SS contributes neutral-to-moderate support for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary bromide precursors should react more readily than refractory oxides, and analogues show ternary bromides form from binary halide mixtures under moderate conditions [CS2, SS2, TS5]. However, a dry solid-state route still requires reconstruction from binary SrBr2/AgBr into the target 2D Sr–Ag–Br sheet, and analogous systems often need long anneals or repeated grindings [CS1, CS3, SS3]. The F3 score is therefore good but not excellent. The key uncertainty is whether SrAgBr3 nucleates directly or competes with persistent SrBr2/AgBr residual phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

A bromide self-flux should improve kinetics by providing high ionic mobility during the melt/slow-cooling stage [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by the successful melt/slow-cool synthesis of KInBr3 and Li–Mg bromides [CS1, TS5], and by Ag/Cu halide analogues synthesized near melting points with annealing [CS3]. The structural reconstruction penalty is lower than in Route 1 because melt growth can promote ordering, but it is not eliminated because the exact SrAgBr3 P-31m sheet is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is whether excess AgBr stabilizes the desired ternary phase or simply crystallizes as an impurity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains two very different cation environments, SrBr6 octahedra and AgAgBr3 trigonal pyramids, indicating a radius/coordination mismatch that triggers one amorphization-risk check [Target]. The structure is ordered and relatively simple, with no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence [Target]. Crystalline bromide analogues across SS and TS suggest ordered halide frameworks are accessible rather than strongly glass-forming [SS2–SS5, TS1–TS5]. Direct amorphization evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the F4 score remains moderately high but not perfect.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrAgBr3 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0150 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is fairly strong: chemically similar and thermodynamically similar bromides are routinely synthesized from binary halides in evacuated ampoules or melts [CS1–CS3, TS1–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the soft, low-temperature bromide chemistry, which allows reaction and ordering at 450–600 °C rather than requiring refractory ceramic processing [CS1, CS3, TS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control, especially avoiding residual SrBr2/AgBr or alternative Sr–Ag–Br polymorphs during cooling [Reasonable Inference based on phase-control issues in CS3 and SS1]. Overall, the material appears experimentally accessible, with the best chance coming from sealed-ampoule AgBr-rich self-flux growth.
