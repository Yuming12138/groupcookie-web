### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. CS supports this because chemically similar CsAuSe and Cs₂Ni₃Se₄ are on the hull, while Cs₂CoSe₂ is only slightly above hull at 0.021 eV/atom (CS2, CS3, CS1). SS and TS also support high stability because KCuS, K₃AuSe₂, CsAuSe, Rb₂Se, and CsAuTe analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 phases (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 600 °C, directly supported by KCuS and CsAuSe/CsAuTe syntheses (SS1/TS1; CS2/SS3/TS3/TS5), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main deductions come from sealed handling of volatile Cs/Se, multi-step tempering, and air-sensitive/toxic precursors; sealed ampoule and tempering are explicitly used for related alkali coinage chalcogenides (CS2/SS3/TS5; SS1/TS1). CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signals here; no source is neutral for Route 1 severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetic feasibility is good because KCuS forms from elements at 600 °C and contains linear Cu–S coordination analogous to target linear Cu–Se coordination (SS1/TS1). CsAuSe/CsAuTe demonstrate that Cs–coinage-metal–chalcogen frameworks crystallize from elemental melts/ampoules at similar temperatures (CS2/SS3/TS3/TS5). The uncertainty is that Cu–Se chemistry may generate competing Cu₂Se or Cs₂Se phases before the ternary phase orders [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate thermal severity because CsNaSe and K₃AuSe₂ are synthesized at about 550–600 °C equivalents (CS4; SS2/TS2). It is penalized more than Route 1 because evacuated sealed tubes, prior synthesis/handling of Cs₂Se, and hygroscopic binary chalcogenides introduce additional operational complexity (CS4, SS5). CS and SS/TS give useful signals; the exact Cu₂Se + Cs₂Se route is a [Reasonable Inference], so direct evidence for this exact precursor pair is absent.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary selenides should improve kinetics by pre-forming Cs–Se and Cu–Se bonds [Reasonable Inference], and related ternary alkali chalcogenides are made from binary chalcogenides (CS4, CS5, SS2/TS2). Diffusion should be manageable at 550–600 °C because alkali selenides are reactive/ionic and analogues require only moderate temperatures (CS4; SS2/TS2). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: Cs₂Se and Cu₂Se could remain as stable competing binaries if ternary ordering is slow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses temperatures below 1000 °C, consistent with Cs₂CoSe₂ at 1030 K and Cs/Ni chalcogenide fusion at 800–850 °C (CS1, CS3). However, H₂/Se or chalcogen-loaded gas is a reactive/controlled atmosphere, and Se vapor control adds a volatility penalty (CS1, CS3). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for carbonate/H₂ synthesis of Cu-containing CsCuSe specifically, so their signal for this route is neutral relative to CS.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are less favorable because carbonate must convert to reactive cesium selenide species and Cu metal must be selenized before ternary ordering [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 and CS3 show that such carbonate/metal/chalcogen routes can form ternary alkali transition-metal chalcogenides, but no Cu-specific carbonate route is provided. The route may work, but uncertainty in competing oxide/carbonate residues and Cu selenide formation lowers the score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Cs/Cu cation size mismatch, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii, supported qualitatively by target bond distances]. The structure is simple CsCuSe rather than a large complex cell, and the target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity; thus C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS contain analogues with mixed polyhedral sharing such as CsAuTe (TS5), but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that this applies to the target itself.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsCuSe is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar alkali coinage-metal chalcogenides are also on the hull (CS2/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS5). The analogue support is strong: KCuS provides the closest Cu–chalcogen structural precedent with linear Cu coordination (SS1/TS1), while CsAuSe and CsAuTe provide direct Cs–coinage-metal–selenide/telluride ampoule-synthesis precedents (CS2/SS3/TS3/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule routes around 600 °C, where alkali/chalcogen melts or vapor assist diffusion (CS2/SS3/TS5; SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is handling and retaining volatile, air-sensitive Cs/Se chemistry while avoiding binary Cs₂Se and Cu₂Se impurities [Reasonable Inference, consistent with air/moisture sensitivity noted in SS1/TS1 and CS4/SS5]. Overall, the material should be synthesizable with careful sealed-tube solid-state practice.
