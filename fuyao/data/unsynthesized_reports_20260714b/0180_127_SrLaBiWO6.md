### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the high-score boundary of the rubric, so F1 = 90. TS1–TS5 show that thermodynamically similar oxides with E_hull values around 0.06–0.115 eV/atom can be synthesized under controlled conditions, supporting the plausibility of a 0.05 eV/atom phase. CS1/CS2/CS3/CS4 are stable reference oxides, but they are not the same Bi-containing chemistry. SS gives insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence for SrLaWBiO6 and is treated as neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

CS1/CS2 provide strong evidence that chemically similar W-containing perovskite-derived oxides can be prepared below 1000 °C using citrate-derived precursors and H2-containing atmospheres. The main severity penalties are the reducing atmosphere, multistep resin/decomposition/anneal procedure, and Bi precursor toxicity/volatility by [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides only general support for controlled-atmosphere perovskite synthesis, while TS provides mainly metastability context rather than exact conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1

The citrate route scores best kinetically because CS1/CS2 explicitly use solution homogenization to create reactive precursors. The W–O network remains diffusion-limited because W is refractory, but the successful 850–900 °C formation of related W double perovskites in CS1/CS2 indicates that homogeneous precursors reduce the kinetic barrier. Structural reconstruction is moderate rather than severe because CS1/CS2 and CS5 are perovskite-derived La–W oxides, although Bi coordination/redox is uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2

CS5 supports a straightforward oxide/carbonate route at 1050 °C, but that temperature triggers the >1000 °C penalty. Additional penalties arise because Bi volatility likely requires sealed/covered conditions by [Reasonable Inference], supported indirectly by CS3/CS4 closed systems for Tl oxides and TS4 sealed reduced oxide synthesis. SS3/SS4 show that some perovskite-derived oxides require much harsher 1400+ °C reducing conditions, but the recommended route avoids those initially.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetics are weaker because stable oxides and carbonates are less reactive than citrate precursors, consistent with CS5 being a high-temperature solid-state route. Repeated regrinding/refiring is likely needed by analogy with SS3/SS4, which reduces the structural reconstruction score. The main uncertainty is whether SrLaWBiO6 orders before Bi loss or decomposition into simpler tungstates/bismuthates.

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route avoids >1000 °C temperatures, but it requires sealed/vacuum or inert ampoule handling, so it is still conditionally severe. CS3/CS4 support closed systems for Sr/Ba heavy p-block oxides, and TS4 supports sealed-tube high-temperature reduced oxide growth. Flux chemistry itself is [Reasonable Inference] because no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports SrLaWBiO6 flux growth.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux/self-flux growth can improve mass transport and crystal growth, giving better kinetics than dry solid-state reaction by [Reasonable Inference]. However, W–O framework formation and cation ordering remain difficult, and fluxes may stabilize competing Sr/Bi tungstates. TS5 supports long controlled cooling for single-crystal growth, but not this exact chemistry.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large cation size and bonding mismatch among Sr, La, W, and Bi triggers the ionic-radius criterion by [Reasonable Inference]. The target primitive description has only one formula unit, so the atom-count criterion is not triggered. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is plausible from the low-symmetry P1 target, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves edge/face/vertex coexistence, so only one F4 trigger is counted.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrLaWBiO6 is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state oxide, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data support synthesis most strongly: CS1/CS2 show La–W perovskite-derived oxides from citrate precursors under mild reducing atmospheres, and CS5 shows La–W double perovskites from solid-state firing. The SS data support general perovskite-derived oxide formation but give insufficient direct evidence for Bi/Sr/W chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is homogeneous cation mixing from a citrate route, which should lower the formation temperature and reduce Bi loss. The main limiting factor is the probable need to stabilize a Bi(I)-like environment while retaining W(VI), which imposes a narrow oxygen-potential window.
