### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull = 0.028 eV/atom places it in the highly synthesizable thermodynamic range ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS gives particularly relevant evidence: AgTl2I3 at 0.0272 eV/atom was synthesized hydrothermally (TS1), ThPbI6 at 0.0334 eV/atom was prepared by sealed-tube melting/quenching/tempering (TS4), and TlMnI3/TlCdI3 at ~0.017 eV/atom were synthesized by Bridgman/melt methods (TS3, TS5). CS and SS also show many closely related iodides are on-hull and experimentally accessible by melt growth or sealed-tube methods (CS1–CS4; SS1, SS2, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee the specific trigonal layered polymorph of CaCuI3 rather than competing CaI2/CuI-derived phases.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because analogous iodides are made at only ~300–400 °C or 573 K in sealed ampoules, not at high pressure or very high temperature (CS1–CS4; SS1/SS5). Deductions are mainly for volatile/air-sensitive iodide handling and sealed ampoule requirements, since CS2 and CS3 explicitly require strict moisture exclusion or dry Ar, and CS4 uses glovebox handling. Insufficient evidence from TS for the exact CaCuI3 melt temperature, so the 450–600 °C range is a [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains favorable because melt homogenization minimizes diffusion limitations.

#### F3 — Route 1

Reaction kinetics should be good because liquid or self-flux conditions are repeatedly successful for iodometallate crystals in CS1–CS4. The target structure shares general iodide polyhedral motifs with CS1/SS5 and CS3/SS1, but it is not isostructural, so nucleation of the 2D trigonal sheet is a partial reconstruction rather than direct topotaxy. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for CaCuI3-specific phase boundaries; therefore F3 is high-moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

The solid-state/annealing route avoids high temperature but is more operationally severe because analogous iodides can require repeated grinding, annealing, or long tempering (SS4/TS3, TS4). Evacuated sealed ampoules, dry glovebox handling, and air-sensitive iodides are common in SS2, SS4/TS3, and TS4. The route is not penalized for high pressure or >1000 °C because no analogue requires those conditions. The main uncertainty is whether CaCuI3 forms by diffusion at 250–450 °C or needs partial melting.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state reaction between CaI2 and CuI has moderate kinetics: iodides are softer and more mobile than oxides, but solid-state diffusion is still slower than in a melt. SS4/TS3 shows that related ternary iodides may require weeks or repeated powdering/annealing for purity, and TS4 shows tempering up to weeks for iodide phases. The target’s 2D ordered sheet requires rearrangement of both Ca and Cu coordination, so structural reconstruction is nontrivial. Thus F3 is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal HI synthesis is low-temperature, as TS1 used 155–175 °C, but it requires corrosive HI and a sealed pressure vessel. It does not trigger the high-pressure >5 GPa penalty because TS1 uses ordinary autogenous hydrothermal pressure. Deductions are assigned for sealed volatile/corrosive medium, multistep hydrothermal processing, and toxic/corrosive HI. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for hydrothermal Ca–Cu iodide synthesis, so this route is less secure.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution-mediated transport in HI can greatly improve ion mobility, as TS1 demonstrates ternary iodide crystallization from TlI and AgI in aqueous HI. However, TS1 also reports twinned crystals and low yield of untwinned crystals, signaling difficult crystal/phase control. For CaCuI3, the hydrothermal route must also maintain Cu+ and prevent hydrolysis/oxide formation, which is [Reasonable Inference] from Cu iodide chemistry rather than directly shown in CS/SS/TS. Kinetics are therefore plausible but uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant Ca/Cu cation size and coordination mismatch, which can promote disorder; this is [Reasonable Inference] from the target CaI6 octahedra and CuCuI3 tetrahedra. The target description shows corner and edge sharing but not simultaneous corner/edge/face sharing, so the strict mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully met; CS/SS/TS analogues include various corner/edge/face connectivities, but this does not replace the target-specific connectivity. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, but the simple CaCuI3 formula and high-symmetry P-31m description suggest it is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCuI3 is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.028 eV/atom and TS contains synthesized iodide analogues in the same energy range (TS1, TS4). The CS and SS evidence strongly supports sealed-ampoule binary-iodide melt growth as the most transferable route, since multiple chemically and structurally similar iodides are obtained by slow cooling of melts in sealed ampoules (CS1–CS4; SS1/SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and low-temperature processability of iodide melts/fluxes, demonstrated across CS1–CS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under moisture-sensitive, volatile iodide conditions, as shown by moisture-handling requirements in CS1/CS3/CS4 and long or repeated annealing in SS4/TS3 and TS4. Hydrothermal HI synthesis is possible but less supported for Ca–Cu iodides and may suffer from twinning or low-yield issues similar to TS1.
