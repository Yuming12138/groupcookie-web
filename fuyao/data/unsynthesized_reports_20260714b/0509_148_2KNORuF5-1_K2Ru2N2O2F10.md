### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound by the provided target data. This is reinforced by structurally and thermodynamically close Ru fluoride analogues CsRuF6 and RbRuF6, both with E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). CS analogues also include stable or nearly stable fluoride/oxyfluoride compounds (CS1, CS3, CS5), but insufficient evidence from CS for the specific Ru–NO–F chemistry. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed ground state remains stable under real chemical potentials of F2, HF, NOx, and moisture.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits severe handling requirements because SS1/SS2 and TS1/TS2 use elemental fluorine at 350 °C for Ru fluoride formation. The route also requires an added nitrosylation step using NOF/NOCl-type reagents, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** and not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. The temperature itself is moderate, but toxic/reactive gases and sealed or dry apparatus dominate the severity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct KRuNOF5 gas-phase synthesis lowers confidence.

#### F3 — Route 1

Gas fluorination can overcome some diffusion limitations, as Ru fluoride analogues form at 350 °C in SS1/SS2 and TS1/TS2 rather than by very high-temperature ceramic routes. However, insertion and ordering of a linear nitrosyl ligand is not shown in any CS/SS/TS source. The kinetic bottleneck is likely not Ru–F formation but selective conversion to RuF5NO without over-fluorination to KRuF6-type products or decomposition to Ru fluorides. Thus F3 is moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 2

Hydrothermal fluoride routes are less severe thermally: CS3 uses 473 K/20 bar, and CS4 uses 175–225 °C. However, HF-containing autoclave chemistry is hazardous, as CS1 and CS2 explicitly use HF media. The need for sealed vessels and multiple workup steps follows CS1/CS3/CS4. The severity is moderate because pressure is autogenous rather than >5 GPa and no mandatory reactive gas atmosphere is required by CS.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal solution transport gives favorable kinetics, as CS1–CS4 show complex fluorides/oxyfluorides forming under liquid-mediated conditions. Fluoride-rich media should promote Ru–F coordination **[Reasonable Inference]**, and low-to-moderate temperatures reduce volatilization relative to F2 gas routes. The weakness is that no CS analogue demonstrates Ru nitrosyl fluoride assembly, so redox/nitrosyl chemistry may be slow or competing. This justifies a moderately high but not excellent F3.

#### F2 — Route 3

The crystallization part of Route 3 is mild because SS4/SS5 and TS4/TS5 show alkali hexafluorometallates crystallizing by room-temperature slow evaporation from fluoroacid solutions. The major severity is hidden in precursor preparation: H[RuNOF5] or a soluble [RuNOF5]− salt is not provided by CS/SS/TS and may require HF/NOF chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore the route scores high on crystallization conditions but loses points for multi-step and toxic precursor handling. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for preformed RuNOF5 fluoroacid availability remains the largest uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3

If the [RuNOF5]− anion is preformed, kinetics should be excellent because crystallization involves assembling K+ and an intact molecular/complex anion, analogous to H2[IrF6] + Cs2CO3/Rb2CO3 in SS4/SS5 and TS4/TS5. Solution crystallization avoids refractory solid-state diffusion and should allow better ordering of the linear NO ligand. The score is not perfect because the existence and solution stability of the Ru nitrosyl pentafluoride anion are not established by CS/SS/TS. Precursor synthesis, not final crystallization, is the likely kinetic risk.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between 11-coordinate K and octahedral Ru, so C1 is triggered based on the target structural description. No evidence shows a unit cell atom count above 40, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that criterion. The target description does not explicitly report simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so C3 is not triggered, although SS/TS analogues do contain complex sharing motifs. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KRuNOF5 is likely a true ground-state or very low-energy phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Ru fluoride analogues in SS/TS are also on the hull. The CS/SS/TS data strongly support the feasibility of forming alkali transition-metal fluoride frameworks, especially Ru–F octahedral compounds by fluorination (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2) and complex fluorides by solution or hydrothermal routes (CS1–CS4; SS4/SS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a highly fluoride-rich environment while controlling K:Ru stoichiometry and preserving an octahedral Ru–F coordination sphere. The most critical limiting factor is incorporation and stabilization of the linear nitrosyl ligand, because no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates Ru–NO–F synthesis. The best practical approach is likely solution crystallization from a preformed [RuNOF5]− species, provided that precursor can be generated cleanly.
