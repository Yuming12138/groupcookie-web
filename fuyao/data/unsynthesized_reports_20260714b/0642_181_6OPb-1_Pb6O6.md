### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0260 eV/atom, so it is low-energy metastable rather than far above the hull, giving a high F1 score by the required rubric. Similar low-energy metastable or near-ground-state oxides are experimentally realized in the references: Pb₂O₃ at 0.009 eV/atom (CS1), AgPb₄O₄Cl at 0.012 eV/atom (SS3/TS1), and HP-Bi₂O₃ at 0.039–0.051 eV/atom (CS5, TS3). The uncertainty is that the target is PbO and polymorph competition is not directly addressed by any CS/SS/TS source.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis has mild condition severity because SS5 and TS2 use 90–210 °C autoclave conditions without high pressure above 5 GPa or controlled external atmosphere. The main F2 penalty is lead toxicity, since PbO/Pb salts are involved in SS5 and TS2. Kinetically, hydrothermal transport should help overcome PbO solid-state diffusion limitations, and TS2/SS5 show crystallization of Pb–O frameworks in water; however, exact P-3 PbO has insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule solid-state/topotactic annealing

The condition severity is moderate because SS3/TS1 require evacuated sealed ampoules and long annealing at 540 °C, and sealed conditions introduce both atmosphere and stoichiometry-control penalties. Solid-state kinetics are acceptable because PbO is not a refractory oxide and PbO-containing analogues form at 540–910 °C in SS1–SS4/TS1. The uncertainty is polymorph selectivity: SS1–SS4/TS1 show Pb–O layered compounds, but none proves that trigonal PbO forms rather than known PbO polymorphs.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Thin-film deposition

CS2 and CS3 support low-temperature binary monoxide film formation at 200–400 °C using vacuum or O₂/Ar sputtering, so F2 is not heavily penalized for temperature. F3 is lower because CS2/CS3 explicitly indicate phase-control difficulty and narrow oxide formation windows for Pt–O, and Pb volatility/composition drift are likely [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports thin-film PbO growth specifically, so this route is less strongly supported than hydrothermal or solid-state routes.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-pressure/high-temperature quench

CS5 and TS3 strongly support high-pressure stabilization of metastable trigonal heavy p-block oxide polymorphs at 6–6.3 GPa and 850–900 °C. F2 is low because >5 GPa pressure, multistep capsule loading/quenching/release, and toxic PbO are significant burdens. F3 remains good because TS3 reports successful metastable polymorph formation in ~0.5 h, but transfer to PbO is uncertain because the direct examples are Bi₂O₃ rather than PbO.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple binary PbO composition with one cation type, so the ionic-radius-mismatch criterion is not triggered. The target description indicates a simple 2D sheet with Pb in 3-coordinate geometry and O in trigonal planar geometry, so large-cell and mixed-connectivity amorphization triggers are not supported. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any amorphization tendency for this exact PbO phase, so F4 is scored high but not perfect.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a metastable phase rather than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0260 eV/atom, which is low but nonzero. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of low-energy metastable Pb/Bi oxides and layered Pb–O frameworks, especially through hydrothermal and sealed solid-state routes (CS1, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty combined with the ability of Pb²⁺ lone-pair chemistry to form low-coordinate layered Pb–O motifs, as seen in SS3/SS5/TS2. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because no provided source directly demonstrates trigonal P-3 PbO, and known PbO polymorphs may nucleate preferentially [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with hydrothermal crystallization being the best first attempt.
