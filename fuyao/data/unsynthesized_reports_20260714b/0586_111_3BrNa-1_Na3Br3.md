### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0360 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. Similar metastable alkali-halide phases with E_hull values near this level include LiI at 0.0289 eV/atom and RbBr at 0.0316 eV/atom, both associated with low-temperature or abnormal polymorph preparation (CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). This supports metastable accessibility but not necessarily bulk stability. Remaining uncertainty is whether the P4/mmm sheet phase survives at room temperature.

#### F2 — Route 1: Low-temperature vacuum evaporation

The main severity penalties are high vacuum, volatile halide evaporation, and multistep cryogenic deposition/annealing, all supported by LiI/LiBr and RbBr deposition analogues (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). The route avoids >1000 °C processing and high pressure, so the intrinsic thermal severity is moderate. Insufficient evidence from SS for binary NaBr low-temperature vapor deposition, so SS is neutral for this route-specific condition choice.

#### F3 — Route 1: Low-temperature vacuum evaporation

Vapor availability is favorable because multiple alkali halides were evaporated directly from boats/bands in vacuum (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). The crystallization score is reduced because the analogues show metastable polymorphs often form only at low temperature and transform toward stable cubic/rocksalt phases on warming (CS-2, CS-3; TS-3, TS-5). Composition control is relatively good for binary NaBr [Reasonable Inference], though deposition rate and substrate temperature remain important, as shown by RbBr abnormal phase work (CS-4; TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule melt crystallization

The sealed-ampoule method uses controlled Ar/vacuum and a sealed system, both common for bromide melts and anneals (SS-1, SS-3; TS-2, TS-4). It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, but NaBr must be heated near its melting point [Reasonable Inference], and dry sealed handling is required. CS provides insufficient direct support for melt growth of the target polymorph and is neutral regarding this bulk route.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule melt crystallization

Kinetically, melt processing gives excellent diffusion and homogenization, consistent with structurally similar bromides prepared by melting and slow cooling (SS-1; TS-4). However, structural reconstruction into a 2D tetragonal polymorph is the weak point: equilibrium cooling of binary NaBr is expected to favor stable rocksalt NaBr [Reasonable Inference], whereas CS/TS metastable alkali-halide polymorphs were accessed by low-temperature deposition (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). Phase selectivity, not reaction completion, is the limiting factor.

#### F2 — Route 3: Thin-film epitaxial/substrate-stabilized deposition

This route has the same vacuum, volatility, and multistep penalties as Route 1, supported by low-temperature alkali-halide deposition analogues (CS-2, CS-3; TS-5). The additional substrate selection does not add a rubric penalty but increases experimental complexity [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for epitaxial stabilization of NaBr and is neutral for this route-specific feature.

#### F3 — Route 3: Thin-film epitaxial/substrate-stabilized deposition

Precursor vapor availability is high because alkali halides are routinely evaporated in the relevant analogues (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). The crystallization pathway is scored slightly higher than Route 1 because substrate stabilization could favor the target’s two-dimensional sheet motif [Reasonable Inference], but there is no direct CS/SS/TS evidence for P4/mmm NaBr epitaxy. Composition control is good for binary NaBr but still sensitive to vapor flux, substrate temperature, and post-deposition relaxation, as suggested by abnormal RbBr and LiI phase behavior (CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no mixed-cation radius mismatch, no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell, and no stated face-sharing connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers apply. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for NaBr amorphization tendency, so the source-specific signal is neutral; the score is based on the target structural description. The main uncertainty is thin-film disorder at cryogenic deposition, which may broaden diffraction peaks as seen in low-temperature LiI analogues (CS-3; TS-5), but this is not the same as intrinsic amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target NaBr P4/mmm phase is likely metastable rather than a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0360 eV/atom, although this is still within a range where metastable phases are often experimentally accessible. CS and TS analogues strongly support the idea that alkali-halide polymorphs with comparable metastability can be trapped by low-temperature vapor deposition, especially LiI and RbBr abnormal phases (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; TS-3, TS-5). SS analogues support the general ease of bromide handling in sealed ampoules and melt/anneal routes, but they are less convincing for stabilizing a binary NaBr polymorph rather than the stable rocksalt phase (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3; TS-4). The key enabling factor is the simple binary chemistry and low E_hull. The key limiting factor is phase retention: the P4/mmm two-dimensional sheet structure may transform to conventional rocksalt NaBr during warming, annealing, or bulk crystallization.
