### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is **0.0500 eV/atom**, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom category, so a high score of 90 is assigned from the target thermodynamic data. CS gives strong support because TbF₃, ErF₃, and EuF₃ analogues are all listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). TS gives a cautionary but positive signal: phases with E_hull around 0.03–0.05 eV/atom can be synthesized, but some require HP–HT or special atmospheres (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific hexagonal P6₃/mcm PrF₃ polymorph is the ambient accessible form or a slightly metastable polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Aqueous precipitation + anneal

F2 is moderate because precipitation itself is mild, but the route may involve HF/H₂F₂ or toxic fluoride salts, and EuF₃ processing used vacuum drying/aging (CS-3). CS-4/SS-3 show very simple YF₃ precipitation with NaF, but also warn that the specific cubic phase was non-reproducible, so phase control is the main uncertainty. F3 is high because soluble Pr salts and fluoride precipitation avoid solid-state diffusion, analogous to EuF₃ precipitation (CS-3). The remaining risk is that an as-precipitated product may be hydrated, poorly crystalline, or not the desired P6₃/mcm polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Hydrothermal synthesis

F2 is high because TbF₃ was made at only 180 °C for 24 h in a standard Teflon-lined autoclave without special atmosphere (CS-1 / SS-1), and LiThF₅ was similarly made at 225 °C in a Teflon-lined autoclave (SS-5). F3 is high because hydrothermal media provide solution transport and crystallization at low temperature, as demonstrated for TbF₃ and LiThF₅ (CS-1 / SS-1; SS-5). The route is chemically very transferable from TbF₃ to PrF₃, but structurally not identical because the cited TbF₃ is orthorhombic Pnma while the target PrF₃ is hexagonal P6₃/mcm. The uncertainty is whether hydrothermal nucleation favors the desired hexagonal phase or another lanthanide trifluoride polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solid-state fluorination / annealing

F2 is lower because sealed/dry conditions, possible NH₄HF₂ or HF fluorination, and 750–950 °C annealing create more operational severity; analogous fluoride reactions use dry Ar sealed ampoules at 900 °C (SS-2) and sealed tubes at 800–1000 °C for days (TS-4). F3 is moderate because binary fluoride/oxide powders require more diffusion and longer annealing than solution routes, and SS-2/TS-4 show long holds or several-day reaction times. The route is useful for densification or phase purification but may be less efficient for initial PrF₃ formation. The main uncertainty is whether high-temperature annealing stabilizes or destabilizes the target P6₃/mcm arrangement.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Molten flux / crystal growth
