### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-stability/metastability boundary, so it receives 90/100. CS evidence is favorable because CS-1/CS-4 are 0 eV/atom K–Ln–Nb oxides and CS-5 is only 0.0098 eV/atom. SS evidence is also favorable because SS-1/SS-2 are low-hull K–Ta/Nb oxides, while TS evidence shows similar thermodynamic analogues around 0.06–0.09 eV/atom can be synthesized. Remaining uncertainty is whether the target is a true ground-state phase or a low-energy metastable phase requiring kinetic trapping.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, hydroxide flux

CS-1/CS-4 and SS-2 provide strong evidence that sealed silver-tube hydroxide flux synthesis at 400–600 °C can crystallize K-containing Nb/Ta oxides. The condition severity is moderate because the temperature is low, but the sealed corrosive hydroxide system and multi-step handling reduce the F2 score. Kinetics are favorable because flux transport bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, as shown by SS-2/SS-4. The key uncertainty is In incorporation and the target’s possible peroxide-like O–O unit, neither of which is directly demonstrated in CS/SS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, ceramic solid-state

CS-2, CS-5, and TS-2 support that Ta/Nb perovskite-derived oxides can be made by ceramic heating in air. However, those examples use less volatile A-site cations such as Ba/Sr/Na/Ca, while target K₂TaInO₆ contains volatile K, so K stoichiometry is a major concern [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are poor because Ta₂O₅ and In₂O₃ are refractory oxides, and CS-2/CS-5 require repeated high-temperature processing. This route is plausible but not preferred.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, metathesis/salt-assisted

CS-3 directly supports a metathesis-type route for ordered Nb/Ta perovskite oxides at 900–1100 °C in air. A KCl/K₂CO₃ molten-salt analogue could lower diffusion barriers relative to dry ceramic processing [Reasonable Inference]. The main limitation is that CS-3 involves Ba₃MM′₂O₉ compositions rather than K–In–Ta peroxide-like chemistry, so structural transferability is incomplete. This route is a good backup if hydroxide flux fails.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, hydrothermal

TS-3 shows that alkali-containing complex oxides can crystallize hydrothermally at 230 °C, and CS/SS sources show alkali hydroxide media are broadly effective. The low temperature makes F2 favorable, but F3 is limited by uncertain Ta₂O₅ dissolution and lack of direct K–Ta–In hydrothermal precedent. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS exists for hydrothermal synthesis of target-like K–Ta–In oxides, so this route is speculative. It may be useful if the phase decomposes at high temperature.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5, high pressure

TS-5 and SS-5 demonstrate that high pressure can access unusual In/Re or K/Re oxides. However, the target is K-rich with large K coordination, and high pressure may favor denser competing phases rather than the desired open K-containing framework [Reasonable Inference].
