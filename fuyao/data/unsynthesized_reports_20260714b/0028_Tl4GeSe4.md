## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score threshold, so it is likely accessible but may be slightly metastable rather than a guaranteed ground state [Target]. CS and SS strongly support stability because Tl₂GeSe₃ is on the hull and Tl₄SnSe₃/Tl₄PbSe₃ are near-hull [CS-1][CS-2][CS-3][SS-1][SS-2][SS-3]. TS supports a similar near-hull regime, with several analogues in the ~0.037–0.083 eV/atom range [TS-1][TS-2][TS-3][TS-4]. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition against known Tl–Ge–Se compositions such as Tl₂GeSe₃ [CS-1][SS-1].

### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it uses standard evacuated ampoules but requires vacuum, staged heating, and volatile/toxic Tl/Se containment [CS-1][CS-4][SS-1][Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory oxides because Tl and Se become mobile at modest temperature, while Ge remains the main slower-diffusing component [CS-1][SS-1][Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is only partial because Tl₂GeSe₃ already has GeSe₄ tetrahedra and similar Tl–Se bonding, but the target has a different stoichiometry and monoclinic C2/c arrangement [Target][CS-1][SS-1].

### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the same F2 deductions as Route 1 because it still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoules, multiple operations, and toxic/air-sensitive handling [CS-2][CS-3][TS-1]. F3 is highest because melt growth provides better mass transport than solid-state annealing and is directly demonstrated for Tl-rich selenides [CS-2][CS-3][CS-5][TS-1]. The main uncertainty is whether the monoclinic Tl₄GeSe₄ phase crystallizes preferentially instead of related Tl–Ge–Se or Tl-rich selenide phases [Target][CS-1][CS-2][SS-1].

### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is kinetically attractive because molten flux can lower diffusion barriers and promote crystal growth at 450–600 °C [TS-2][Reasonable Inference]. However, insufficient evidence from [CS] and [SS] supports flux growth specifically for Tl–Ge–Se, so those source categories are neutral for this route (50) [CS][SS]. The main risk is flux-induced stoichiometry drift or incorporation of foreign ions if non-self-flux chemistry is used [TS-2][Reasonable Inference].

### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 uses a proven thermodynamic selenide CVT concept with iodine and a temperature gradient [TS-3]. Insufficient evidence from [CS] and [SS] supports CVT for Tl₄GeSe₄, so those source categories are neutral for this route (50) [CS][SS]. Kinetics can be good through vapor transport, but iodine may over-stabilize volatile Tl/Ge iodides instead of transporting the target congruently [TS-3][Reasonable Inference].

### F2/F3 — Route 5

Route 5 is a contingency because TS shows high-pressure chalcogenide synthesis at 30 kbar, but CS/SS do not indicate that Tl–Ge–Se requires pressure [TS-5][CS][SS]. The route has weak structural transferability and lower F3 because pressure annealing at 400–500 °C may not provide enough diffusion unless the feed is already pre-reacted [TS-5][Reasonable Inference]. It is mainly useful if ambient-pressure synthesis yields a competing phase and pressure is needed to access a metastable polymorph [TS-5][Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

F4 is medium risk because the target combines a very large Tl cation with much smaller Ge, creating a strong cation-size mismatch [Target][Reasonable Inference]. The C2/c structure with five inequivalent Tl sites and four inequivalent Se sites suggests a large and ordered unit cell, which can slow crystallization and increase disorder risk [Target][Reasonable Inference]. Mixed edge/corner connectivity is seen in analogues, but simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing is not confirmed for the target, so C3 is not triggered and CS/SS/TS evidence is insufficient for that specific check [CS-1][SS-1][TS-4].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl₄GeSe₄ is best viewed as a near-hull, possibly low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS and SS evidence is strong because Tl₂GeSe₃ is both chemically and structurally close and has a demonstrated sealed-ampoule synthesis route [CS-1][SS-1]. TS evidence supports general near-hull chalcogenide accessibility, but it is less chemically specific than CS/SS [TS-1][TS-2][TS-3]. The key enabling factor is liquid or semi-liquid Tl–Se chemistry in sealed ampoules, which should improve diffusion and crystal growth [CS-2][CS-3][TS-1][Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is controlling Tl/Se volatility and avoiding competing Tl–Ge–Se phases such as Tl₂GeSe₃ [CS-1][SS-1][Reasonable Inference].
