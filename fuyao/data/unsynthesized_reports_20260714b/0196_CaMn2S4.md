### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.068 eV/atom, placing it in the moderately metastable but often synthesizable range, so the assigned score is 78. CS provides support because several chemically similar sulfides are stable and one FeCr2S4 analogue has E_hull ≈0.078 eV/atom, close to the target (CS-1 to CS-5). TS strongly supports this stability window because the closest thermodynamic analogues span about 0.058–0.090 eV/atom and have reported syntheses (TS-1 to TS-5). SS is mixed because one close structural analogue CuCrS2 has E_hull 0.223 eV/atom while others are near hull; therefore SS supports structural accessibility but not uniformly thermodynamic robustness (SS-1 to SS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for evacuated ampoule operation, sulfur volatility, repeated grinding/heating, and air/moisture-sensitive sulfide handling. Evacuated ampoule reactions at 750–1000 °C are common in SS-2, SS-3, TS-3, and TS-4, so the conditions are standard rather than extreme. The main uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly show CaMn2S4 formation from CaS + Mn2S3, so the severity is manageable but the phase-control risk remains.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfides provide moderate precursor activity, supported by MnS/Sc2S3 use in CS-3 and binary chalcogenides in SS-2. Diffusion is plausible at 850–950 °C because related chalcogenides form at comparable temperatures in SS-2, TS-3, and TS-4. However, forming the target tetragonal edge-sharing MnS4 framework likely requires substantial reconstruction and cation ordering [Reasonable Inference], and repeated cycles are often required to reach single phase in TS-4.

#### F2 — Route 2

H2S sulfurization receives deductions for toxic/reactive gas handling and possible operation slightly above 1000 °C. CS-1 and CS-4 use dry H2S at 1270–1370 K for 12 h, while CS-2 uses 900–1000 °C with N2/H2S switching. This route is operationally more hazardous than sealed sulfide ampoules, but it may be chemically effective for sulfurizing Ca/Mn precursors.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics benefit from a reactive sulfurizing atmosphere, as CS-1, CS-2, and CS-4 show ternary sulfide formation under H2S. The precursor activity is only moderate because carbonate/metal mixtures still require extensive conversion to sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction and phase selection are uncertain; CS-2 explicitly notes narrow phase windows and transformations among related sulfide phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth avoids temperatures above 1000 °C but requires sealed/reactive sulfur-rich conditions, volatile sulfur/polysulfide components, slow cooling, and moisture-sensitive flux handling. The use of flux/polysulfide melts is supported by CS-2, and high-temperature solution growth by TS-5. The route is therefore not easy, but its conditions are less harsh than H2S flow or month-long CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3

This is the kinetically best route because molten flux increases precursor activity and bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, consistent with CS-2 and TS-5. Slow cooling gives the target a chance to nucleate as a crystalline metastable phase rather than remain as a multiphase solid-state product [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is phase selectivity: CS-2 reports that closely related sulfide phases interconvert depending on temperature and time.

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT is penalized for >1000 °C operation, evacuated iodine atmosphere, volatile iodine, multistep processing, and long duration. CS-3 demonstrates exactly this kind of demanding sequence for MnSc2S4: initial H2S treatment followed by iodine CVT at 1050 °C for one month. SS-1 also supports iodine as a crystallization aid, but no TS source provides CVT-specific evidence for this target family.

#### F3 — Route 4

CVT improves crystallinity by vapor transport and can bypass bulk diffusion, as shown for MnSc2S4 in CS-3. However, it is best viewed as a crystal-growth/post-synthesis route rather than the first attempt at making phase-pure CaMn2S4. The key uncertainty is volatile transport balance: Ca iodide and Mn iodide species may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference], and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ca–Mn–S CVT compatibility.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Ca and Mn, which triggers C1 and gives a low–medium amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description does not imply a very large unit cell, and it reports edge-sharing MnS4 tetrahedra rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS contain many crystalline sulfide analogues with related Mn/Cr/Fe–S polyhedra, supporting crystallization rather than glass formation, but none directly addresses CaMn2S4 amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca(MnS2)2 is most likely a moderately metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.068 eV/atom. The TS set supports synthesis in this metastability range, since several thermodynamic analogues with E_hull ≈0.058–0.090 eV/atom have reported solid-state, melt, or solution-growth syntheses (TS-1 to TS-5). The CS and SS sets support general sulfide chemistry and Mn/transition-metal sulfide formation, but they do not directly demonstrate the Ca–Mn–S tetragonal phase, so the evidence is indirect. The most important enabling factor is high sulfur chemical potential combined with liquid/flux-mediated transport, as inferred from H2S and flux/polysulfide successes in CS-1/CS-2/CS-4 and solution growth in TS-5. The main limiting factor is phase competition among CaS, MnS/Mn2S3, and other Ca–Mn sulfides, especially because Mn must likely be stabilized near +3 in sulfide coordination [Reasonable Inference].
