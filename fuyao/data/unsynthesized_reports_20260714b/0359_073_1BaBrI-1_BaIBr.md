### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0420 eV/atom, placing it below the 0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore in the high synthesizability range. This is strongly supported by BaBrCl, a chemically and thermodynamically close mixed Ba halide with E_hull ≈ 0.0484 eV/atom that was experimentally synthesized (CS-1/TS-1). Structurally similar Ba/Sr oxyhalides are hull-stable and synthesizable, but because they contain O, their relevance to pure BaBrI is indirect (SS-1/SS-2/SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that BaBrI may be metastable relative to separate BaBr₂/BaI₂ or another ordered polymorph, since the target is not a ground-state phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires high temperature near 970 °C and very slow cooling, directly following BaBrCl synthesis (CS-1/TS-1). It does not require >1000 °C or high pressure, but it does require vacuum/Ar handling and likely sealed containment due to halide volatility and hygroscopicity, consistent with BaBrCl and Ba oxyhalide syntheses (CS-1/TS-1, SS-2/SS-5). The main uncertainty is whether BaBrI’s iodide component requires lower temperature or excess iodide to prevent loss [Reasonable Inference]. Condition severity is therefore moderate.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

BaBr₂/BaI₂ are much more reactive than refractory oxides because they can form a molten/ionically mobile phase near the reaction temperature, analogous to BaBrCl being heated above precursor melting points (CS-1/TS-1). Diffusion should be favorable, but reconstructing binary halides into the target 2D trigonal omega-derived sheet and achieving Br/I ordering may require slow cooling, as BaBrCl required 0.1 °C/min (CS-1/TS-1). SS oxyhalides also use controlled cooling, reinforcing the need for crystallization control but not indicating a prohibitive kinetic barrier (SS-1/SS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C temperatures but introduces more hazardous reagents, especially Br₂/I₂ and possibly Ba metal [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed ampoule vacuum conditions are directly supported by DySI/DySBr syntheses (CS-5), and long anneals are supported by ordered bromide analogues (TS-3/TS-4). The condition score is not worse than Route 1 in temperature, but the chemical handling risk is higher. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that elemental Ba + Br₂/I₂ is necessary for BaBrI, so this route remains less preferred.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Reactive halogens should promote formation of Ba halides, and sealed ampoule reactions successfully make related rare-earth halide/chalcogenide phases at 750–900 °C (CS-4/CS-5). However, starting from elements or reactive halogens requires complete structural reconstruction, and TS bromide analogues show that ordering can require several-week anneals (TS-3/TS-4). The kinetic uncertainty is whether BaBrI forms directly or instead crystallizes as BaBr₂/BaI₂ domains [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses a halide flux/self-flux, which improves transport but adds practical severity: sealed tubes, dry glovebox loading, potential container compatibility issues, and possible upper temperatures near or slightly above 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference]. BaI₂ flux growth and Ba oxyhalide single-crystal synthesis use sealed evacuated tubes, carbon-coated silica, Ta containers, and controlled thermal programs (TS-5, SS-2/SS-5). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that BaBrI requires a flux, so this route is scored as more complex than Route 1. Its condition severity is moderate-challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth should have the best kinetics because a molten BaBr₂/BaI₂-rich medium provides high halide mobility [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by BaI₂ flux growth of Ba₄Fe₂I₅S₄ (TS-5) and melt/slow-cool formation of BaBrCl (CS-1/TS-1). The remaining limitation is structural selectivity: flux can crystallize competing BaBr₂/BaI₂ or other mixed-halide polymorphs if stoichiometry and cooling rate are not optimized [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is high, but not near-perfect.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, Ba²⁺, so cation-radius mismatch is absent by composition. The described P3m1 BaBrI sheet is simple and edge-sharing, with no evidence for large unit cells or highly mixed polyhedral connectivity from the target description. SS and TS analogues contain more complex mixed-sharing polyhedra (SS-1/SS-5, TS-2), but those features are not present in BaBrI. Remaining uncertainty is small because halide salts generally crystallize readily rather than amorphize under slow cooling [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaBrI is likely a mildly metastable phase rather than a strict ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0420 eV/atom, but this is still within the commonly synthesizable range. The strongest support comes from BaBrCl, a chemically and thermodynamically close mixed barium halide synthesized from BaBr₂/BaCl₂ under melt-assisted slow cooling (CS-1/TS-1). Structurally similar Ba/Sr oxyhalides and thermodynamically similar Ba halide/chalcogenide phases further support high-temperature sealed-tube halide chemistry, though they are less chemically direct (SS-1/SS-2/SS-5, TS-2/TS-5). The key enabling factor is the likely high mobility of molten or partially molten BaBr₂/BaI₂ salts, which should allow Br/I ordering during slow cooling [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1/TS-1]. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: BaBrI may compete with BaBr₂, BaI₂, or alternative mixed-halide orderings unless stoichiometry, halide vapor pressure, and cooling rate are tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference; slow-cooling sensitivity in CS-1/TS-1].
