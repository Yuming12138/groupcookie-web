### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0160 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin and therefore receives a high F1 score. Thermodynamically similar materials with comparable small positive hull energies are experimentally accessible, including WTeBr₉ at E_hull ≈ 0.011 eV/atom (TS-1), CuHgSCl at E_hull ≈ 0.024 eV/atom (TS-3), and α-PdCl₂ at E_hull ≈ 0.032 eV/atom (TS-2). The uncertainty is that the known PdBr₂ C2/c analogue is hull-stable at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4), so the target P2₁/c 2D phase may be metastable relative to the 1D PdBr₂ polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1: Br₂-assisted CVT

The direct PdBr₂ reference uses 700 °C, a closed quartz ampoule, an ~80 °C temperature gradient, and a small Br₂ amount as transport agent (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). This is not extreme temperature and does not require high pressure, but Br₂ is volatile and hazardous, so sealed-tube handling and safety dominate the severity [Reasonable Inference from CS-1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on the exact transport duration and background atmosphere, so the score remains moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1: Br₂-assisted CVT

Kinetics should be favorable because the feed is already PdBr₂ and the transport mechanism bypasses slow solid-state diffusion (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The known PdBr₂ analogue has square-planar Pd–Br coordination and similar Pd–Br bond lengths, which supports local structural compatibility (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The main kinetic/structural uncertainty is whether the vapor-grown product selects the target 2D P2₁/c sheet rather than the known 1D C2/c ribbon polymorph (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2: Vacuum sublimation / recrystallization

The PdCl₂ analogue is grown by sublimation at 670 °C in a vacuum-sealed silica tube followed by controlled slow cooling (SS-4; TS-2), and PdBr₂ itself is vapor-transportable near 700 °C (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The vacuum requirement and volatile halide vapor chemistry reduce the score, but no high pressure or ultra-high temperature is required. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct PdBr₂ sublimation without Br₂, so bromine loss risk remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2: Vacuum sublimation / recrystallization

Vapor recrystallization generally has good kinetics because transport occurs through volatile species rather than bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference from SS-4/TS-2 and CS-1]. The route is structurally plausible because PdCl₂ and PdBr₂ analogues share square-planar PdX₂ connectivity (SS-4; CS-1). The uncertainty is that vacuum may lower bromine chemical potential and favor decomposition or the already known 1D PdBr₂ polymorph [Reasonable Inference from Br₂ use in CS-1].

#### F2 — Route 3: Bromination + anneal/CVT

The PdBr₂ reference notes a multi-step process involving preparation of PdBr₂ powder from Pd metal and HBr/Br₂ followed by CVT crystallization (CS-1 detailed reasoning; SS-1; TS-4). The route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but Br₂/HBr handling and sealed-tube pressure are significant hazards [Reasonable Inference from CS-1]. Its multi-step nature makes it more severe than starting directly from purified PdBr₂.

#### F3 — Route 3: Bromination + anneal/CVT

Pd metal is less kinetically active than preformed PdBr₂, so initial bromination can be interface-limited unless finely divided [Reasonable Inference]. The later CVT step can improve crystallinity because PdBr₂ is known to form single crystals by transport (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). However, this route must both form PdBr₂ and then select the target metastable 2D polymorph, making the kinetic score lower than direct CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary PdBr₂ with one cation site and square-planar PdBr₄ units, so the mixed-cation radius mismatch and mixed-polyhedral-connectivity triggers do not apply based on the target description. Related complex bromides with multiple cations and mixed polyhedral motifs, such as Te₃(PdBr)₄ and ZrHg₃(SeBr₃)₂, show more phase-control issues (SS-2; SS-5), but those complexities are absent in the target. Remaining uncertainty is the exact unit-cell atom count; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40 atom cell for the target.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target PdBr₂ P2₁/c sheet phase is likely a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than the global ground state, because its E_hull is 0.016 eV/atom while a chemically identical PdBr₂ C2/c polymorph is reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The CS/SS/TS data strongly support that PdBr₂ itself can be synthesized and crystallized by Br₂-assisted CVT, but they do not directly prove formation of the target 2D P2₁/c polymorph (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is vapor transport in a sealed bromine-rich ampoule, which provides high mobility and maintains bromine chemical potential (CS-1; SS-1; TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity against the known 1D PdBr₂ structure, likely requiring careful control of temperature gradient, Br₂ activity, and cooling path [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-4, TS-2]. Overall, synthesis is quite plausible, but obtaining the exact P2₁/c sheet phase as a pure bulk or single-crystal product is the main challenge.
