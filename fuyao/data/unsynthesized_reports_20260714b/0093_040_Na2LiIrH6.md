### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff, so it is likely thermodynamically accessible or only weakly metastable. The closest Ir hydrides NaCaIrH6 and NaBaIrH6 are on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS2; SS1), which supports the IrH6 framework stability. Thermodynamic analogues around E_hull ≈0.054–0.083 eV/atom are reported synthesized under H₂ pressure (TS1, TS3, TS4), supporting the feasibility of slightly metastable hydrides. Remaining uncertainty is whether Na/Li ordering introduces competing decomposition to NaH, LiH, Ir, or binary/ternary Ir hydrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but uses reactive H₂ pressure and air-sensitive NaH/LiH handling (CS1, CS2, SS1; CS4/CS5). Kinetically, it benefits from the closest structural/chemical precedent: NaCaIrH6 has the same Ir–H distance and similar Na–H cuboctahedral framework (CS1/SS1). The main uncertainty is whether Li can order into the octahedral site without forming Li-rich competing hydrides or leaving unreacted Ir [Reasonable Inference]. Phase optimization is likely because analogous quaternary hydrides required multiple attempts (CS1/CS2/SS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 uses similar moderate temperatures but longer autoclave holds under 10–100 bar H₂, which is supported by Li4OsH6, Li4RuH6, and Na4RuH6 syntheses (CS3; SS2–SS4). The longer time improves diffusion and homogenization, but the structural analogues are trigonal rather than cubic Fm-3m, lowering structural transferability relative to Route 1 (SS2–SS4). It remains attractive if Route 1 gives residual Ir because several-day H₂ exposure can drive metal hydrogenation and alkali diffusion (CS3/SS4). Uncertainty remains in phase selectivity between cubic Na2LiIrH6 and other alkali iridate hydride stoichiometries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized heavily for >8 GPa pressure, sealed capsule work, internal hydrogen source handling, and multi-step high-pressure recovery, all directly seen in Li2PtH6 synthesis (SS5). Kinetically, high pressure and BH3NH3 can enable dense Fm-3m complex hydrides that are not accessible at lower pressure, as demonstrated for Li2PtH6 (SS5). However, the same reference reports side-phase formation and optimized purity limits, suggesting narrow phase windows (SS5). For the target, high pressure is best viewed as a fallback stabilization strategy rather than the first attempt [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization/Disordering

The target combines very different cation sizes and environments: Na is 12-coordinate, Li and Ir are octahedral, and H participates in mixed face/edge/corner-sharing networks; this activates cation-size and connectivity disorder risks [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Mixed connectivity is also present in close analogues such as NaCaIrH6 and Li4RuH6 (CS1/SS1; SS2), so crystallization is still plausible. The unit-cell atom count does not clearly exceed 40, so that amorphization check is not counted; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact conventional-cell complexity. The resulting F4 score is medium, reflecting likely crystallinity with possible antisite/disorder or multiphase products.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiIrH6 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable hydride because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and comparable hydrides around this range are experimentally realized (TS1, TS3, TS4). The synthesis case is strongly supported by chemically and structurally close NaCaIrH6/NaBaIrH6, which are made from binary hydrides plus Ir powder under moderate H₂ pressure and contain the same IrH6 octahedral chemistry (CS1, CS2; SS1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficient hydrogen chemical potential under compressed H₂ to form and preserve Ir–H octahedra (CS1/CS2/SS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity and cation ordering between Na and Li, since the target is mixed-alkali and may compete with Li-rich or Na-rich hydride phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is realistically synthesizable in a specialized hydride laboratory, with direct H₂-pressure sintering as the preferred first route.
