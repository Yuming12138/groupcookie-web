### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score threshold and suggests it is near the convex hull rather than strongly unstable. CS supports this because chemically similar Sr–Ru oxides such as Sr₂YRuO₆ and Sr₃CoRuO₆ are at 0–0.027 eV/atom (CS1, CS3), although CS5 shows that chemical similarity alone can include less stable polymorphs. SS gives particularly strong support because several structurally similar Sr–Ru oxides are exactly on or very near the hull (SS1–SS5). TS supports synthesizability at comparable metastability because synthesized thermodynamic analogues appear around 0.054–0.075 eV/atom (TS1–TS4).

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Conventional ceramic route

The severity score is favorable because CS1/SS4 demonstrate Sr₂YRuO₆ synthesis in air using standard ceramic processing, and SS2/SS5 use similar SrCO₃/RuO₂/oxide routes without exotic atmospheres. The main severity deductions come from T >1000 °C and multistep grinding/pelleting, both explicitly present in CS1, SS2, and SS5. Kinetics are the main weakness: MgO is refractory by [Reasonable Inference], and repeated long firings in CS1/SS2/SS5 imply slow solid-state diffusion/order. Remaining uncertainty is the target’s P1 MgO₅/RuO₅ structure, which is not the same as the octahedral Sr₂LnRuO₆ analogues in CS1/SS2/SS5.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Controlled-O₂ / high-pressure oxygen route

The route is more severe because CS3 uses controlled oxygen partial pressure and 5000 bar O₂ post-annealing for Sr–Co–Ru oxides, indicating oxygen fugacity can be decisive in related ruthenates. This route may be chemically better for Sr₂MgRuO₆ because charge balance implies more oxidized Ru than in Sr₂YRuO₆-type analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics remain limited by oxide/carbonate diffusion, although oxygen annealing may improve oxygen stoichiometry rather than cation ordering. SS gives insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure O₂ in Sr₂BRuO₆ double perovskites, and TS gives insufficient direct evidence for Ru⁶⁺ stabilization in Sr–Mg–Ru oxides, so those signals are neutral for this specific route.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Alkali flux route

Flux growth reduces kinetic barriers because SS1 and SS3 show Sr–Ru oxides can be grown from molten alkali fluxes at 700–1150 °C, and SS1 explicitly states Na₂O₂ functions as both oxidizer and flux. The severity score is reduced by reactive/air-sensitive flux components, sealed tubes, slow cooling, and post-synthesis washing/sorting, all supported by SS1/SS3/TS2. The largest uncertainty is composition control: SS1 and SS3 flux products include Na/Li-bearing ruthenates, so alkali incorporation is a realistic risk by [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for this route for Sr₂MgRuO₆, and TS2 supports flux growth generally but not Sr–Mg–Ru composition control.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has one amorphization-risk trigger due to large cation size disparity, inferred from Sr–O, Mg–O, and Ru–O bond-length differences in the Target and standard ionic-radius trends [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell appears small from the target P1 site list, so the >40-atom criterion is not triggered. The connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face-sharing coexistence in the target description, so C3 is not triggered. CS/SS analogues show ordered Sr–Ru oxides can crystallize despite large A/B size mismatch (CS1, SS2, SS4), but they do not directly resolve amorphization risk for triclinic Sr₂MgRuO₆.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgRuO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the feasibility of making Sr–Ru oxides by ceramic methods, especially Sr₂YRuO₆, Sr₂GdRuO₆, and Sr₂DyRuO₆ (CS1, SS2, SS4, SS5). The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated robustness of Sr₂BRuO₆-type oxide synthesis from SrCO₃/RuO₂/oxide precursors under accessible furnace conditions (CS1, SS2, SS5). The most important limiting factor is oxidation-state/structure specificity: replacing trivalent rare-earth B-site cations with Mg²⁺ likely pushes Ru to a higher formal oxidation state and the target has unusual MgO₅/RuO₅ trigonal-bipyramidal coordination [Reasonable Inference from Target charge balance and structure]. If the phase fails under simple air firing, controlled oxygen annealing based on CS3 is the most defensible escalation.
