### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category. SS/TS reinforce this because the closest Cs–Ru–F analogue CsRuF₆ is also on the hull (SS-1/TS-1), and several Cs/Ba fluoride analogues are stable or near-stable (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4/TS-4). CS is mixed because some chemically similar oxyfluorides are above hull, such as CsMn(OF₂)₂ at 0.388 eV/atom and BaMnOF₅ at 0.263 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-4), so CS alone would not guarantee stability. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether nitrosyl ordering and fluoride stoichiometry are captured accurately in the computed ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS-1 and SS-4/TS-4 demonstrate room-temperature slow evaporation in HF/aqueous fluoroacid media. The deductions come from HF toxicity and likely need for preparing or handling a nitrosylruthenium precursor, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because CS/SS/TS do not list a Ru–NO precursor. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct nitrosyl stability under solution evaporation, so that source signal is neutral for the NO-specific hazard and phase-control component. Overall condition severity is moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because solution routes avoid solid-state cation diffusion, as shown by CS-1 overnight slow evaporation and SS-4/TS-4 room-temperature evaporation. Precursor activity should be high in HF because fluoride complexes are soluble and reactive, consistent with CS-1 and SS-4/TS-4. The lower structural-reconstruction score reflects that none of CS/SS/TS directly show CsRuNOF₅ or a Ru nitrosyl fluoride sheet, so the nitrosyl ordering step is inferred rather than proven. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the kinetics of Ru–NO ligand incorporation specifically.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because SS-1/TS-1 and SS-2/TS-2 require elemental fluorine at 350 °C for CsRuF₆/RbRuF₆. Fluorine and NOF/NOCl are toxic/reactive, and the process likely requires multiple staged operations, which justifies deductions. The temperature itself is not high by ceramic standards, so no >1000 °C deduction applies. The main uncertainty is whether nitrosylation can be performed without decomposing the NO ligand or over-fluorinating to CsRuF₆.

#### F3 — Route 2

Gas fluorination gives acceptable kinetics because CsRuF₆ forms at only 350 °C for 2 h in SS-1/TS-1, far below typical diffusion-limited ceramic temperatures. However, solid salt precursors are less reactive than solution complexes, so precursor activity is lower than Route 1. Structural reconstruction is only moderate because CsRuF₆ is structurally and chemically close in Cs–Ru–F bonding (SS-1/TS-1), but replacing one fluoride with linear NO is a major ligand-chemistry change not evidenced in SS/TS. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for direct F₂/NOF nitrosylation of Ru fluorides.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because SS-5 demonstrates CsVOF₃ formation at 100 °C hydrothermally, while CS-5 shows harsher HF hydrothermal synthesis at 350 °C and high pressure for BaTiOF₄. HF toxicity and sealed autogenous pressure justify deductions. It avoids elemental F₂, making it less severe than Route 2. The uncertainty is whether a PTFE-lined hydrothermal environment is compatible with the exact Ru–NO fluoride chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal HF conditions offer high precursor mobility and solution-mediated crystallization, supported by SS-5 for CsVOF₃ and CS-5 for BaTiOF₄. Ion diffusion is not expected to limit reaction strongly because crystallization occurs from dissolved fluoride complexes. Structural reconstruction is moderately favorable due to shared octahedral oxyfluoride/fluoride motifs in SS-5 and CS-5, but no source directly supports nitrosyl ligand retention. Thus, kinetics are good but phase selectivity remains uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large Cs/Ru cation size contrast triggers one amorphization risk factor, consistent with the large-cation/small-octahedral-cation motif in CsRuF₆ and CsVOF₃ analogues (SS-1, SS-5). The target formula and described P2₁/m sheet do not indicate a very large unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct target cell-size evidence, treated neutrally. Mixed edge/face/vertex coexist is not clearly described for the target, unlike CsRuF₆ where multiple sharing modes occur (SS-1/TS-1), so C3 is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsRuNOF₅ is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogues support synthesis well for the Cs–Ru–F chemical subspace, especially CsRuF₆ from SS-1/TS-1, and they support mild solution growth of Cs fluoride/oxyfluoride crystals via CS-1 and SS-4/TS-4. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of fluoride-rich solution or gas-phase chemistry that stabilizes octahedral Ru–F coordination. The most critical limiting factor is preservation and controlled incorporation of the linear nitrosyl ligand, because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ru–NO fluoride synthesis. The best practical route is therefore low-temperature HF slow evaporation using a preformed Ru–NO precursor, rather than high-temperature fluorination.
