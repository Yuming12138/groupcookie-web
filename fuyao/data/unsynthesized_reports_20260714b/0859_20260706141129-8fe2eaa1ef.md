### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which indicates a predicted ground-state phase under the computed thermodynamic model. Similar halide and chalcogenide references also include many on-hull compounds, such as CS1, SS5/TS1, and TS2–TS5, supporting the idea that related alkali-halide frameworks can be thermodynamically stable. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or hydration/solution stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because structurally closest K₂ZnCl₄/Rb₂ZnCl₄ analogues were grown by aqueous slow evaporation without high temperature, high pressure, or controlled atmosphere (SS1–SS3). CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for aqueous synthesis of K₃ZnICl₄, so they are neutral for this route. The only practical concerns are ZnCl₂ hygroscopicity and possible iodide oxidation, which are [Reasonable Inference] handling issues rather than severe-condition triggers.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solution growth has favorable kinetics because all ions are mobile in solution, and SS1–SS3 show that ZnCl₄-based alkali halides crystallize directly from aqueous solutions. The target’s ZnCl₄ tetrahedra are closely matched to those in K₂ZnCl₄/Rb₂ZnCl₄, so structural reconstruction is modest (SS1–SS3). The main uncertainty is whether iodide occupies the intended K-coordinated site rather than producing competing KI-rich or ZnI₂-containing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is moderate because analogous solid-state halide reactions use evacuated ampoules, controlled annealing, intermediate grindings, and sometimes long reaction times (CS3, TS2). Dry/inert handling is also supported by thermodynamic chloride analogues requiring glovebox or Schlenk procedures (TS3, TS4). The route avoids high temperature and high pressure, but sealing and volatile/mixed-halide control introduce deductions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary halide precursors are more reactive than refractory oxides, and analogous halides form at moderate temperatures in sealed ampoules (CS3, TS2–TS4). However, CS3 and TS2 report repeated grindings and extended anneals to reach single-phase material, indicating nontrivial solid-state ordering and diffusion. Mixed Cl/I ordering in K₃ZnICl₄ is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS and remains a kinetic uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 requires dry handling, sealed ampoules, melting, and controlled cooling or Bridgman withdrawal, matching the complexity described for K₂MgCl₄ and Rb₂CdCl₄ crystal growth (SS4, SS5, TS1). It does not require high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, so the thermal severity is moderate rather than extreme. Volatile halide/ZnCl₂ management is a [Reasonable Inference] risk that increases severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Melt growth should provide faster ion transport than solid-state diffusion, and related halide frameworks crystallize from molten salts under controlled cooling (SS4, SS5, TS1). The structure-building units are chemically compatible with the target’s alkali-halide framework and ZnCl₄ tetrahedra, but SS4 notes crystallization challenges for Rb₂CdCl₄ and SS5/TS1 requires controlled Bridgman conditions. The key uncertainty is whether the melt preserves the target mixed-halide stoichiometry rather than losing or segregating iodide/chloride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has strongly different cation sizes and coordination preferences, with K in 8-coordinate environments and Zn in tetrahedral ZnCl₄ units, so one amorphization trigger is applied [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description reports corner and edge sharing but not face-sharing, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. CS/SS/TS show related frameworks can crystallize well, including single crystals from solution or melt (SS1–SS5), lowering concern about amorphization.

K₃ZnICl₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related halide references are also on or near the hull (CS1, SS5/TS1, TS2–TS5).
