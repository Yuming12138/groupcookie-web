### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability threshold, so F1 is scored 90 rather than higher [Target]. Synthesized analogues include stable perovskite/chromite phases and metastable thermodynamic analogues up to ~0.084 eV/atom, indicating that this stability window is experimentally plausible [CS4][SS2][TS3][TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target may be a mixed-valent Cr phase, which can impose redox constraints not captured by E_hull alone [Target][SS1][TS1].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C firing and multi-step regrinding/sintering, both common in ceramic analogues [CS2][CS3][SS2][TS2]. It avoids high pressure and specialized reducing/vacuum atmospheres, so its severity remains moderate [CS2][SS2]. The uncertainty is whether air/O2 is sufficient to stabilize the target’s inferred Cr3+/Cr4+ mixture [Target][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are only moderate because oxide/carbonate routes rely on slow diffusion through refractory Cr2O3/La2O3-type solids [SS2][TS2][Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because CaCrO3 and LaCrO3 analogues share the same perovskite CrO6 framework and similar tilt angles [Target][SS1][SS2]. Repeated firing requirements in the analogues justify a reduced kinetic score [CS2][CS3][SS2].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 receives the largest condition penalty because the closest CaCrO3 analogue requires 65–70 kbar high pressure, and BaCrO3 analogues require similarly specialized high-pressure apparatus [SS1][SS4][SS5][TS1]. The temperature itself is not extreme relative to ceramic routes, but dry handling, capsules, and CrO2/CaO handling add operational burden [SS1][TS1]. CS gives insufficient direct high-pressure evidence and is neutral 50 for this route [CS1–CS5].

**F3 — Route 2**  
The structural-kinetic advantage is strong because high pressure directly stabilizes Cr-rich perovskite frameworks similar to the target [SS1][TS1][Target]. Precursor activity and diffusion are not outstanding because the route still uses solid oxide powders, but short HP reaction times in BaCrO3/CaCrO3 analogues suggest feasible crystallization once pressure stabilizes the framework [SS1][SS4][TS4]. The uncertainty is scale and accessibility, not structural transferability [SS1][TS1].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 avoids high pressure but is penalized for >1000 °C crystallization, multiple processing steps, and toxic Cr nitrate handling [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct sol-gel evidence and therefore are neutral 50 for direct route precedent [CS1–CS5][SS1–SS5][TS1–TS5]. Its severity is moderate because the hard part shifts from equipment to redox-controlled crystallization [Reasonable Inference][SS2].

**F3 — Route 3**  
The nitrate/citrate route has high precursor activity by general sol-gel chemistry, but that is a reasonable inference rather than direct CS/SS/TS precedent [Reasonable Inference]. It should improve cation homogeneity relative to repeated ceramic grinding, yet the final perovskite crystallization still requires high-temperature reconstruction [CS2][CS3][SS2][Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether the amorphous precursor crystallizes into Pmn2₁ CaLaCr2O6 rather than LaCrO3/Ca–Cr secondary phases [Target][SS1][SS2].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization check is triggered: broad A/B cation radius contrast exceeds 15%, but this is normal for perovskites and less severe than same-site cation mismatch [Target][Reasonable Inference]. The target CrO6 network is corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing, lowering amorphization risk [Target]. Exact target unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that evidence channel is neutral 50, but analogous perovskite cells are compact [CS2][SS1][TS3].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaLaCr2O6 is best viewed as a borderline metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS and SS analogues showing that related perovskite-derived chromites/double perovskites can be made by conventional ceramic routes [CS2][CS3][SS2]. The closest Ca–Cr structural/thermodynamic analogue warns that Cr-rich Ca chromites may require high pressure to stabilize Cr4+-containing perovskites [SS1][TS1]. The most important enabling factor is the strong structural match to known corner-sharing CrO6 perovskites with similar tilt angles [Target][SS1][SS2]. The key limiting factor is Cr redox/oxygen-fugacity control, because the target charge balance implies average Cr near +3.5 [Target][Reasonable Inference]. Overall, an ambient O2 ceramic route is the best first attempt, with high-pressure synthesis as the most credible fallback if phase-pure material is not obtained [CS2][SS2][SS1][TS1].
