### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E above hull is 0.039 eV/atom, placing it in the high-synthesizability metastable range. This is supported by thermodynamically similar Nd₃N₂SeBr at 0.0417 eV/atom, which is experimentally obtained by flux growth (TS1). Chemically similar Ca₂NCl and Y₂NCl₃ are on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS2, CS3), while Sc₇Cl₁₀C₂ is nearly on the hull and synthesized in high yield (CS4). Remaining uncertainty is that the exact Sc₃N₂Cl₂ composition is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so competing ScN/ScCl₃/reduced chloride phases may still intervene.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate high temperatures comparable to 800–1000 °C nitride/chloride and Sc chloride-carbide syntheses (CS3, CS4), avoiding high pressure. The main severity penalties come from sealed ampoule handling, volatile/moisture-sensitive chloride precursors, and inert-atmosphere requirements, all supported by CS3, CS4, and SS4. CS2 supports that nitride chlorides can be made in ampoules under Ar, but it also shows long anneals and high-temperature single-crystal growth. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for Sc–N–Cl ampoule chemistry and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because ScCl₃ is reactive but ScN and Sc metal are diffusion-limited refractory solids [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing in Ca₂NCl and Y₂NCl₃ syntheses indicates that nitride chloride ordering is slow (CS2, CS3). Sc₇Cl₁₀C₂ forms quantitatively under optimized sealed conditions (CS4), which supports feasibility, but the target’s 2D Sc–N–Cl sheet likely requires substantial reconstruction from the precursors. SS/TS offer insufficient direct kinetic data for Sc₃N₂Cl₂ and are therefore secondary.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar temperature and atmosphere severity to Route 1, but the flux does not add a severe equipment penalty because flux growth in sealed ampoules is common for the analogues (CS1, SS5/TS1, TS4). It still requires sealed dry conditions because halide fluxes and nitride chlorides are moisture-sensitive, as shown in CS3, SS4, and SS5/TS1. There is no high-pressure penalty. Remaining uncertainty is whether flux removal can be performed without hydrolyzing Sc₃N₂Cl₂.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux improves mass transport and crystal growth, which is strongly supported by CsCl/NaBr flux syntheses of single crystals at 900 °C in CS1, SS5/TS1, and TS4. The flux route is therefore scored higher than direct solid state for ion diffusion and structural ordering. However, precursor activity is not ideal because ScN remains refractory [Reasonable Inference], and the exact P-6m2 Sc₃N₂Cl₂ sheet is not demonstrated by the references. SS4 supports layered halide/interstitial formation from CsCl/LuCl₃/Lu/C, improving confidence in flux transferability.

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure route is more severe because it needs specialized multianvil equipment and reactive sealed capsules, as shown for ZrNCl at 3 GPa (SS2). The scoring rubric does not penalize pressure below 5 GPa, but practical difficulty remains high because SS2 requires Au/Pt/BN cells and controlled cooling. TS5 shows that some nitride phases require much harsher HP conditions, but that is not directly required here. CS data actually suggest ambient-pressure synthesis may be sufficient, so HP is treated as a fallback.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetically, HP reactive flux can be effective because ZrNCl is converted at 3 GPa and 900 °C within 1 h using NH₄Cl flux (SS2). Structural transfer is strong because ZrNCl is a 2D nitride chloride with Cl three-coordinate to metal, similar in broad motif to the target (SS2). However, the Sc₃N₂Cl₂ stoichiometry and P-6m2 sheet differ from ZrNCl, so the route may stabilize the wrong layered polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient support for requiring HP and therefore tempers this route’s priority.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so there is no cation radius mismatch penalty [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for cation-disorder-driven amorphization. The unit cell is likely small because the target formula has seven atoms and few inequivalent sites [Reasonable Inference], so the large-cell penalty is not triggered. The mixed face/edge/corner connectivity is explicitly present in the target and is also associated with phase-control sensitivity in complex chloride/interstitial analogues such as Sc₇Cl₁₀C₂ and Gd₆C₃Cl₅ (CS4, SS3). This gives a low–medium amorphization risk rather than a high one.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₃(NCl)₂ is likely a **low-energy metastable phase**, not a guaranteed ground state, because its E above hull is 0.039 eV/atom. The CS data strongly support feasibility of Sc/Y/Ca nitride or chloride interstitial compounds under sealed high-temperature conditions, especially CS2, CS3, and CS4. The SS data support the layered nitride-chloride motif through ZrNCl and related layered chloride interstitials, especially SS2 and SS4. The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive chloride/flux environments that promote transport and stabilize layered halide sheets, as shown in CS1, SS4, SS5/TS1, and TS4. The main limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control among ScN, ScCl₃-rich phases, and reduced Sc chloride/interstitial phases, a concern supported by the narrow phase behavior in Y₂NCl₃ and Lu chloride-carbide analogues (CS3, SS4).
