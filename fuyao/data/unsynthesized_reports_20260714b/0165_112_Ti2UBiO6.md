### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the boundary of the most favorable rubric bin, giving a high thermodynamic score. CS analogues include stable or near-stable Bi–Ta and U–Pb oxides with E_hull values from 0 to ~0.038 eV/atom (CS1–CS5), while SS/TS analogues demonstrate synthesis of titanates and related oxides at comparable or higher hull energies, including LaTiO₃ at 0.0676 eV/atom and YTiO₃ at 0.0580 eV/atom (SS2; TS1, TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a low-symmetry quaternary phase, not a directly reported ground-state analogue.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard air sintering near 1000–1100 °C, matching CS Bi–Ta oxide syntheses and U double-oxide syntheses (CS1–CS5). It avoids vacuum, H₂, high pressure, and specialized melt-growth equipment, unlike several SS/TS titanate analogues (SS3, SS4; TS2, TS5). Deductions arise from high temperature, repeated grinding, and uranium precursor toxicity.

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are only moderate because binary oxides require long diffusion distances and the target requires substantial cation rearrangement. CS analogues need 30–50 h and repeated/intermittent grinding (CS1–CS5), while SS titanates often require vacuum firings, multiple firings, or long treatments (SS2, SS3, SS5). Bi₂O₃ may assist diffusion by partial melting or fluxing [Reasonable Inference], but this is not directly proven for UTi₂BiO₆.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because it uses evacuated or sealed-tube redox control, similar to LaTiO₃ and NaUO₃ syntheses (SS2, SS5; TS1). CS U double oxides also allow evacuated quartz ampoules for redox-sensitive compositions (CS3, CS4). The penalties are vacuum/sealing, Bi volatility in a sealed system, multi-step handling, and toxic uranium precursors.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Sealed-tube synthesis improves oxygen-fugacity control but does not strongly improve solid-state diffusion. LaTiO₃ and NaUO₃ analogues require long high-temperature treatments and/or multistage reaction schedules (SS2, SS5; TS1), and U double oxides use 50 h with intermittent grinding (CS3, CS4). Therefore, kinetics remain slower than a solution route.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest thermal profile because sol–gel mixing should allow crystallization below the 1000–1100 °C used for CS solid-state Bi oxides [Reasonable Inference; CS1, CS2, CS5]. It remains a multi-step route and uses toxic uranium nitrate/uranyl precursors [Reasonable Inference; U precursors in CS3, CS4, SS5]. No CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates sol–gel synthesis, so its score is high but not maximal.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

The molecular-level mixing in citrate/Pechini chemistry should substantially reduce diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], addressing the long firing/regrinding requirements seen in CS/SS analogues (CS1–CS5; SS2, SS3, SS5). Precursor activity is high because nitrates/alkoxides/citrates fall in the most reactive rubric category. The main kinetic uncertainty is crystallization selectivity: an amorphous gel may crystallize competing Bi–Ti, U–Ti, or U–Bi oxides before UTi₂BiO₆.

#### F4: amorphization tendency

The main amorphization trigger is the large cation size/coordination mismatch among U, Ti, and Bi, consistent with the target’s diverse U–O, Ti–O, and Bi–O bond environments. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is neutral rather than triggered. The target description does not prove coexisting vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so C3 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

UTi₂BiO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than an obviously robust ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS data strongly support that Bi-containing complex oxides and U-containing oxides can be made by high-temperature solid-state routes, while SS/TS data warn that Ti/U redox and oxygen stoichiometry may require vacuum or controlled atmospheres. The most important enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty, supported by synthesized analogues with comparable or higher hull energies (SS2; TS1–TS3). The most important limiting factor is control of phase selection and oxidation state in a quaternary U–Ti–Bi oxide with unusual coordination. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance coming from chemically homogeneous precursor routes followed by careful oxygen-fugacity optimization.
