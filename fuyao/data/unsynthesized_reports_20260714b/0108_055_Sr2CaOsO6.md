### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold in the requested rubric. CS/SS analogues include several stable or near-stable double perovskites: Sr₂YOsO₆ has E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS2), Sr₂MgOsO₆ has E_hull = 0.0 (SS1), and Ba₂CaOsO₆ has E_hull = 0.0 (SS5). TS analogues cluster around E_hull ~0.06–0.08 eV/atom yet are experimentally synthesized (TS1–TS5), supporting that a 0.05 eV/atom oxide is experimentally plausible. Remaining uncertainty is whether Sr₂CaOsO₆ is the targeted ground-state polymorph or a metastable polymorph relative to competing Sr/Ca/Os oxides.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity penalty because the closest Sr–Os analogue Sr₂YOsO₆ requires sealed quartz, Ar atmosphere, glovebox handling, long firing, and intermittent regrinding (CS1, SS2). The synthesis temperature is below 1000 °C, so the temperature penalty is avoided, but sealed handling and toxic Os chemistry are significant (CS1, SS2, SS5). Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact sealed Sr₂CaOsO₆ route; TS mainly supports high-temperature oxide synthesis generally, not this composition.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate-to-slow because the route uses oxide/peroxide powders and relies on solid-state diffusion through a tilted double-perovskite lattice (CS1, SS2). The favorable point is structural transferability: Sr₂YOsO₆ is also a monoclinic tilted Sr₂BOsO₆ perovskite-derived structure with similar octahedral connectivity (CS1, SS2). The limiting factor is long reaction time and repeated regrinding in the analogue, which indicates slow B-site ordering and diffusion (CS1, SS2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has relatively mild equipment demands because Ba₂CaOsO₆ was made in air at 1000 °C in a Pt crucible (SS5). The main penalties are repeated processing and Os toxicity/volatility, since SS5 reports additional Os was used due to evaporation. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS that open-air synthesis is optimal for Sr₂CaOsO₆; CS1 instead favors sealed Ar for Sr–Os perovskites.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetic performance is limited by the use of Os metal and CaO, both less reactive than solution-derived or flux precursors [Reasonable Inference; supported by repeated grinding/heating in SS5]. The structural match is strong because Ba₂CaOsO₆ has the same Ca–Os double-perovskite B-site pair (SS5). The uncertainty is the A-site substitution: replacing Ba²⁺ with smaller Sr²⁺ increases octahedral tilting and may require longer ordering or different oxygen chemical potential [Reasonable Inference from target and SS5 structures].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is severe because Sr₂MgOsO₆ synthesis required 6 GPa and 1500 °C in a belt-type high-pressure apparatus with Pt capsules (SS1). The high-pressure and high-temperature penalties dominate. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS that high pressure is required for Sr₂CaOsO₆; this route is included as a fallback for phase stabilization.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure and high temperature can improve crystallization and B-site ordering, and Sr₂MgOsO₆ is the closest structural analogue (SS1). However, the route still begins from dense Os/MgO-type oxide/metal precursors and uses a short 1 h dwell, so complete reaction may be sensitive to mixing and capsule conditions (SS1). The kinetic score is therefore moderate, not high.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 avoids >1000 °C conditions because Os/Ir flux analogues form at 600–700 °C (SS4, TS2, TS5). Penalties arise from sealed Ag tubes, caustic hydroxide flux, toxic Os reagents, and multi-step flux removal (SS4, TS2, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS for flux synthesis of Sr₂CaOsO₆ specifically, but SS/TS provide useful evidence for related Os/Ir oxide flux chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux growth has the best kinetics because molten hydroxide greatly improves cation transport at low temperature, as shown for Ba–Os, Ln–Os, and Ln–Mg–Ir oxides (SS4, TS2, TS5). The major uncertainty is structural selectivity: SS4 and TS2 produce non-Sr₂CaOsO₆ Os oxide frameworks, so the flux may crystallize competing osmates rather than the desired double perovskite. Careful Sr/Ca/Os ratio, hydroxide identity, water content, and cooling rate screening are therefore essential [Reasonable Inference based on SS4/TS2/TS5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination contrast between Sr, Ca, and Os, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference from target bond lengths and standard ionic-radius trends]. However, the target description shows a well-defined corner-sharing CaO₆/OsO₆ double-perovskite network rather than mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is treated as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂CaOsO₆ is likely near the boundary between a ground-state and metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability threshold. The CS and SS datasets strongly support synthesis of related Sr–Os and Ca–Os double perovskites, especially Sr₂YOsO₆ (CS1, SS2), Sr₂MgOsO₆ (SS1), and Ba₂CaOsO₆ (SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple Os double-perovskite analogues synthesized by conventional or sealed solid-state methods (CS1, SS1, SS2, SS5). The most critical limiting factor is Os volatility/oxidation-state control, especially because Os⁶⁺ is required by charge balance in Sr₂CaOsO₆ [Reasonable Inference] and Os loss was explicitly observed in Ba₂CaOsO₆ synthesis (SS5). Overall, the material should be synthesizable with moderate difficulty, with hydroxide flux or sealed-tube solid-state synthesis being the most promising approaches.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Os volatility and toxic volatile oxide formation | High | Use sealed quartz/Ag/Pt containment as in Sr₂YOsO₆ and flux Os oxides (CS1, SS2, SS4, TS2); use Os excess if open-air firing is attempted, as in Ba₂CaOsO₆ (SS5). |
| 2 | All | Correct Os oxidation state, likely Os⁶⁺ for Sr₂CaOsO₆ charge balance | High | Use oxidizing SrO₂/BaO₂-type precursors or KClO₄ oxidizer; SrO₂ is supported by CS1/SS1/SS2 and KClO₄ by SS1. |
| 3 | Routes 1–2 | Slow B-site ordering and solid-state diffusion | Medium | Use long dwell times, pelletizing, and intermittent regrinding as in Sr₂YOsO₆ (~100 h) and Ba₂CaOsO₆ repeated processing (CS1, SS2, SS5). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Open-air Os loss causing nonstoichiometry | High | Add ~5–10 mol% Os excess and repeat grinding/pressing, following the Ba₂CaOsO₆ approach where extra Os was used due to evaporation (SS5). |
| 5 | Route 1 | Quartz-tube compatibility and internal oxygen pressure | Medium | Place pellets in corundum/alumina containers inside sealed quartz, as done for Sr₂YOsO₆ (CS1, SS2). |
| 6 | Route 3 | Specialized high-pressure equipment requirement | High | Use high pressure only as fallback; if needed, follow Sr₂MgOsO₆ conditions: Pt capsule, 6 GPa, 1500 °C, rapid quench (SS1). |
| 7 | Route 4 | Flux may stabilize competing Os oxide frameworks rather than Sr₂CaOsO₆ | Medium–High | Screen NaOH/KOH/LiOH ratios, Sr-rich flux, water content, and cooling profile; flux optimization is implied by Os/Ir flux growth examples (SS4, TS2, TS5). |
| 8 | Route 4 | Hydroxide flux handling and sealed Ag tube pressure | Medium | Use sealed Ag tube protocols from Ba₃MOs₂O₉, Ln₃OsO₇, and Ln₂MgIrO₆ growth; dissolve flux with water/sonication after cooling (SS4, TS2, TS5). |
| 9 | All | Competing binary/ternary phases such as SrO/SrCO₃ residues, CaO, OsO₂, or Sr–Os/Ca–Os oxides | Medium | Use stoichiometric control, pre-calcine hygroscopic oxides, repeat XRD-guided regrinding/annealing; repeated annealing to single phase is supported by CS1, SS2, SS5, and TS1. |
| 10 | All | Moisture/CO₂ uptake by SrO₂/CaO/Ca(OH)₂ precursors | Medium | Handle in glovebox or dry Ar, as glovebox handling was used for Sr₂YOsO₆ and Sr₂MgOsO₆ analogues (CS1, SS1, SS2). |
