### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.040 eV/atom, which places it in the high-synthesizability stability bin of 90–100 by the requested rubric. Structurally similar Hg-containing phases are often on or very near the hull [SS1–SS5], and thermodynamic analogues are also low-hull [TS1–TS5], supporting the plausibility of isolating related Hg chalcogenide frameworks. CS evidence is mixed because layered BiSe analogues can be much higher in energy, ~0.27 eV/atom [CS3, CS4], so CS gives a cautionary metastability signal rather than a direct stability guarantee. Remaining uncertainty is whether this exact P2/m HgSe polymorph can be selected over more common HgSe structures.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis.**  
The severity score is reduced mainly because SS2/SS3/TS2 require evacuated sealed ampoules and Hg-containing toxic precursors, but temperatures are moderate and no high pressure is used. Kinetics are reasonably favorable because HgSe is already a precursor in structurally similar syntheses [SS2, SS3], and Hg–Se chalcogenide networks form in sealed tubes [TS2]. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction into the 2D P2/m polymorph, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS. This route is therefore the most conservative starting point.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, CVT / vapor transport.**  
Severity is higher because CVT adds a multistep prereaction/transport process and uses volatile iodine or Hg halides [CS2, SS3, SS5]. Kinetics are strong because vapor transport bypasses solid-state diffusion and has precedent in layered PbSe CVT [CS2] and Hg chalcogenide halide gas-phase growth [SS5]. The limiting factor is phase selectivity: SS2/SS3 show Hg–Se–I/Br phases are stable, so iodine may incorporate rather than merely transport. Thus this route is best for crystal growth after bulk phase screening.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, salt flux growth.**  
The flux route has moderate-to-high severity because it requires vacuum sealing, slow cooling, flux handling, and toxic HgSe [CS3, CS4]. Kinetically, it is attractive because molten LiCl/KCl enabled layered BiSe-related crystals and slow growth from 780 °C [CS3, CS4]. The uncertainty is transferability to binary HgSe, since SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for flux growth of P2/m HgSe specifically. This route is valuable if Route 1 gives microcrystalline or multiphase products.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, thin-film deposition.**  
CS1 supports the general concept that vacuum evaporation at low temperature can trap abnormal or metastable phases, but it is a halide example rather than HgSe. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for thin-film stabilization of this specific target, so the crystallization and composition-control scores are low. This route may be useful only if bulk methods relax to competing HgSe phases. The main risk is maintaining exact Hg:Se stoichiometry during deposition and annealing.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple binary composition with one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern by the given criterion. The described structure has two inequivalent Hg and two inequivalent Se sites, not a large >40 atom unit cell, and it contains edge/corner sharing but no reported face-sharing coexistence. SS analogues show edge/corner-sharing Hg polyhedra can crystallize in ordered phases [SS1, SS2, SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether low-temperature thin-film deposition could produce amorphous HgSe before crystallization, especially in Route 4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **metastable but accessible** HgSe polymorph rather than a guaranteed ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.040 eV/atom rather than zero. SS and TS analogues support synthesis strongly: multiple Hg chalcogenide or Hg–Se-containing frameworks form in evacuated ampoules at moderate temperatures with standard solid-state or vapor-growth methods [SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2]. CS analogues support the broader idea that layered selenides can be grown by CVT or flux, but they also show that layered phases may require careful crystallization and can be metastable [CS2–CS4]. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/volatility of Hg–Se chemistry in sealed ampoules, which should allow equilibration at relatively low temperatures [SS2, SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selection against more stable HgSe polymorphs or halogen/flux-derived side phases [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull, SS2/SS3, CS1].
