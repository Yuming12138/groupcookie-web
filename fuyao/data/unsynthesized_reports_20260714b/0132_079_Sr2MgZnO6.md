### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff, so F1 = 90. CS supports accessibility because most chemically similar oxides are near the hull, including CS-1 at 0.028 eV/atom, CS-2 at 0.0018 eV/atom, CS-3 at 0.0327 eV/atom, and CS-4 at 0.0234 eV/atom; however, CS-5 at 0.492 eV/atom shows that chemically related polymorphs can also be highly metastable. TS strongly suggests metastable accessibility because the closest thermodynamic analogues are often around 0.059–0.074 eV/atom and have reported syntheses (TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the target’s O–O-containing P1 arrangement is a true low-temperature metastable phase or a computationally close competing variant.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: conventional solid-state ceramic synthesis

The condition severity is moderate because CS-3, CS-4, and SS-5 show that similar alkaline-earth oxides can be made at 1000–1150 °C or equivalent 1373 K ceramic conditions, but repeated firing/pelletizing is usually needed. Kinetics are the main limitation: solid SrCO₃/MgO/ZnO must undergo full reconstruction into ordered MgO₄/ZnO₄ tetrahedra, and CS-3/CS-4 show long or repeated firings. Insufficient evidence from TS indicates whether ambient-pressure ceramic synthesis alone will retain the short O–O contacts.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: peroxide-assisted sealed oxygen-rich synthesis

This route is conditionally severe because SS-2 and TS-2 use sealed systems, and SS-1/SS-3 show peroxide phases are sensitive to hydration/decomposition. Kinetically it is favorable for forming O–O motifs because SrO₂/BaO₂ already contain O–O bonds similar to the target, but Mg/Zn ordering is not demonstrated in the peroxide analogues. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for peroxide-assisted synthesis of Sr–Mg–Zn oxides, so this route is best treated as a targeted rescue route if air/O₂ annealing fails.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: molten flux

Flux growth has moderate condition severity because CS-1 uses K₂CO₃ flux at 1050 °C with controlled slow cooling, and SS-4/TS-1 use sealed gold-tube flux-like growth at 925 °C. Kinetics are comparatively strong because liquid transport reduces diffusion length and can promote ordered crystals, as shown by single-crystal products in CS-1 and SS-4/TS-1. The uncertainty is flux compatibility with Mg/Zn/Sr and whether the oxygen chemical potential is high enough to preserve O–O bonding.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: sol-gel / nitrate / acetate route

This route has the best severity score because it can plausibly crystallize at ≤1000 °C using reactive, homogeneous precursors; reactive oxide generation from nitrates is supported by CS-3, and acetate-derived SrZnO₂ synthesis is supported by SS-5. Kinetically, nitrate/acetate/sol-gel precursors score high for precursor activity and cation mixing, but structural reconstruction into the low-symmetry P1 phase remains nontrivial. Direct sol-gel evidence for Sr₂MgZnO₆ is absent, so the low-temperature crystallization window is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: oxygen-pressure / high-pressure route

TS-3 to TS-5 show that thermodynamically similar metastable oxides may require oxygen pressure or GPa synthesis, so this route is credible as a fallback. Its severity is high because controlled oxygen pressure, sealed capsules, and specialized equipment are involved, even though the recommended pressure may be below the >5 GPa scoring cutoff. The key uncertainty is transferability: TS high-pressure examples are mostly Hg/Tl/Cu or Cu oxides, not Sr–Mg–Zn oxides.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Sr²⁺ and Mg²⁺/Zn²⁺, which triggers C1 by ionic-radius reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. It does not clearly trigger the >40 atom-count check because the described P1 cell contains two Sr, one Mg, one Zn, and six O sites, and it does not explicitly contain face-sharing in addition to edge/corner sharing. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high, though low symmetry may still broaden the crystallization window.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂MgZnO₆ is most likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues around 0.06–0.07 eV/atom have reported syntheses. The CS and SS data support synthesis of chemically related alkaline-earth oxides and structurally related peroxide/O–O-containing phases, but no source directly demonstrates the full Sr–Mg–Zn–O composition. The most critical enabling factor is the use of highly homogeneous or flux-mediated precursors to overcome Mg/Zn ordering and diffusion limitations, supported by reactive nitrate/acetate routes in CS-3 and SS-5 and flux routes in CS-1. The most critical limiting factor is preservation or correct formation of the short O–O contacts under conditions that also crystallize the MgO₄/ZnO₄ tetrahedral network, as suggested by SS-1 to SS-3. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires careful oxygen activity and cation-homogeneity control.
