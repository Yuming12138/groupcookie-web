### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target data. This is reinforced by multiple synthesized analogues at E_hull = 0, including SrCu₂Sb₂, SrNi₂Sb₂, LaNi₂As₂, and SrSn₂As₂ in CS (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5), and structurally/thermodynamically similar CaGa₂As₂, Ca₃(InAs₂)₂, CaIn₂P₂, and SrIn₂P₂ in SS/TS (SS1, SS3–SS5, TS1, TS3–TS5). Insufficient contrary thermodynamic evidence from CS/SS/TS; the only caution is CS5’s failed Ca/Ba analogue attempts, which affects route selectivity rather than hull stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: In-rich self-flux

The severity score is low-moderate because the closest same-structure AIn₂P₂ flux analogues require vacuum sealing, inert handling, high temperature near 1100 °C, slow cooling, and hot centrifugation (SS4, SS5, TS4, TS5). Kinetics are favorable because a liquid In flux should enhance transport, and the target is nearly isostructural with CaIn₂P₂ and SrIn₂P₂ except P→As [Reasonable Inference] from SS4/SS5/TS4/TS5. Remaining uncertainty is the As substitution: SS4/SS5/TS4/TS5 provide P analogues, while direct CaIn₂As₂ flux synthesis is not explicitly reported, so As volatility and phase competition remain [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct CS evidence for this exact In-flux route, so CS is neutral for Route 1 severity/kinetics except for general elemental pnictide handling (CS3–CS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state reaction

This route is supported by a direct Ca–In–As analogue, Ca₃(InAs₂)₂, made under dry Ar at 1270 K with slow heating/cooling and slight Ca excess (SS3, TS3). Chemically similar pnictides are also commonly prepared by elemental solid-state sintering under Ar or vacuum (CS1–CS4). Kinetics are only moderate because stoichiometric solid-state reaction from elements can be limited by interdiffusion and by competing Ca–In–As phases [Reasonable Inference], and the closest Ca–In–As analogue is not the same P6₃/mmc structure (SS3, TS3). Insufficient SS/TS evidence for a single-step route to the exact target structure; this is why F3 is lower than Route 1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Stoichiometric melt growth

Melt growth has the best severity score because SrSn₂As₂ was synthesized by a short 925 °C vacuum-ampoule treatment with controlled heating/cooling (CS5). Kinetically, liquid-assisted reaction should reduce diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and the target’s E_hull = 0 supports thermodynamic capture on cooling. The main uncertainty is that CS5 explicitly notes failed attempts for analogous Ca or Ba compounds in that SrSn₂As₂ family, conflicting with the positive Ca-containing SS/TS analogues; I weight the Ca–In–As and CaIn₂P₂ SS/TS evidence more heavily for target feasibility, but penalize the route by expert adjustment. Insufficient direct SS/TS evidence for stoichiometric melt growth of the exact arsenide target, so SS/TS are supportive mainly through phase stability and related structures rather than the precise route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a compact P6₃/mmc layered structure, and same/similar P6₃/mmc AIn₂Pn₂ analogues exist for CaIn₂P₂ and SrIn₂P₂ (SS4, SS5, TS4, TS5), which lowers amorphization concern. The only triggered check is the significant Ca/In cation size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference] from the target’s CaAs₆ octahedra and In 3–4-coordinate environment. The target has corner/edge sharing but no reported face sharing, unlike the more complex Ca₃(InAs₂)₂ analogue with face-sharing motifs (SS3, TS3), so C3 is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is local In–In ordering because the target contains an In–In bond, whereas the P analogues list In–P coordination without In–In bonding in the same way (SS4, SS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca(InAs)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest supporting analogues are CaIn₂P₂ and SrIn₂P₂, which share the P6₃/mmc AIn₂Pn₂ structure type and are grown by In-flux methods (SS4, SS5, TS4, TS5), plus Ca₃(InAs₂)₂, which confirms accessible Ca–In–As chemistry (SS3, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of liquid In or melt-assisted transport, which can overcome solid-state diffusion limitations and promote layered crystal growth [Reasonable Inference] supported by AIn₂P₂ flux growth (SS4, SS5, TS4, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Ca and As stoichiometry in a sealed environment, because Ca is air-sensitive and As is volatile/toxic [Reasonable Inference], while CS5 also warns that Ca analogues can be difficult in related SrSn₂As₂-type chemistry. Overall, the material is feasible but requires careful sealed-ampoule or flux-growth control rather than open-atmosphere ceramic processing.
