### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score band. [Target][Rubric] Several close analogues are on or near the convex hull, including K₂NaFeF₆, K₂NaCuF₆, Rb₂NaAlF₆, and K₂NaYF₆, supporting thermodynamic accessibility. [CS3][CS4][SS2][TS1] The main uncertainty is that the target sits at the stability cutoff, so it may require kinetic stabilization or careful phase selection. [Target][RI]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C, GPa pressure, and reactive gas, matching the moderate fluoride solid-state conditions of K₂LiAlF₆ and K₂NaMF₆ analogues. [CS1][CS4][TS1] The only major deduction is the need for sealed corrosion-resistant containment to suppress fluoride loss. [CS1][TS1][RI]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Binary fluorides are moderately reactive but not as active as solution/sol-gel precursors, so precursor activity is not maximal. [CS1][CS4][RI] Kinetics are helped by the strong structural match to K₂NaMF₆ and Rb₂NaAlF₆ analogues, but multi-day annealing in analogues implies cation ordering may be slow. [CS3][CS4][SS2][TS1]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 uses lower temperature than solid-state melting routes, but KHF₂/NaHF₂ introduce HF volatility and toxicity. [CS3][RI] The fusion–reaction–leaching sequence also triggers a multi-step deduction. [CS3]

**F3 — Route 2**  
The molten hydrofluoride flux should accelerate ion transport and structural assembly. [CS3][RI] The exact K₂NaMF₆ framework forms by this route, so structural reconstruction is favorable. [CS3]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 remains below 1000 °C in the preferred K₂LiAlF₆ melt-growth window but requires high-temperature molten fluoride containment. [CS2][SS3][RI] Controlled cooling and optional annealing make it more operationally involved than direct sealed-tube synthesis. [CS2][SS3][SS5]

**F3 — Route 3**  
A fluoride melt gives strong diffusion and homogenization. [CS2][SS3][RI] The uncertainty is whether Na substitution changes the K₂LiAlF₆ melt-growth phase window or promotes competing Na/K fluoroaluminates. [SS5][RI]

**F2 — Route 4**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂NaAlF₆ is best viewed as a borderline metastable-to-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, but many close elpasolite analogues are stable or synthesized. [Target][CS3][CS4][SS2][TS1] The CS evidence strongly supports the K₂NaMF₆ framework, the SS evidence strongly supports the Na–Al fluoride framework through Rb₂NaAlF₆, and the TS evidence supports similar near-hull fluoride elpasolites. [Target][SS2][RI] The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and fluoride volatility during high-temperature synthesis, especially because the target is only marginally above the hull.
