### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the favorable threshold for likely synthesizability but not deeply stable [Target]. CS analogues include synthesized phases at 0–0.071 eV/atom [CS], SS analogues include synthesized structural matches near 0–0.024 eV/atom [SS], and TS analogues include synthesized thermodynamic neighbors up to 0.123 eV/atom [TS]. The remaining uncertainty is whether Y–Os–In ordering lowers the accessible kinetic window despite the acceptable hull energy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized for >1000°C heating, sealed Ar/vacuum control, multiple firings, and Os precursor risk [CS][SS][TS][Reasonable Inference]. CS supports high-temperature multi-step oxide synthesis [CS], while SS/TS support sealed/vacuum processing for reduced oxides [SS][TS]. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS route directly reports Os–In–Y oxide synthesis, so Os-specific handling is inferred [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 is strongly penalized by >5 GPa pressure and capsule handling because the CS Ir analogue uses 9 GPa synthesis [CS]. Insufficient evidence from SS for high-pressure synthesis gives SS a neutral source score of 50 [SS], and insufficient evidence from TS for high-pressure synthesis gives TS a neutral source score of 50 [TS]. This route is a fallback for dense-phase stabilization rather than the lowest-severity method [CS][Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 has the same Os atmosphere/toxicity penalties as Route 1 but gains no high-pressure penalty [CS][TS][Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS support nitrate/ceramic precursor processing [CS][TS], while insufficient evidence from SS for nitrate/gel synthesis gives SS a neutral source score of 50 [SS]. The route remains conditionally challenging because Os solution chemistry and redox control are inferred [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.** Route 4 avoids >1000°C temperatures but still requires sealed ampoule handling, volatile Os control, and post-growth washing [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide sealed/vapor or transport-like precedents [SS][TS], while insufficient evidence from CS for flux/vapor synthesis gives CS a neutral source score of 50 [CS]. The reduced temperature helps severity, but phase selectivity is uncertain [SS][TS].

**F3 — Route 1.** Dense Y2O3/In2O3/OsO2/Os powders imply slow solid-state diffusion, consistent with long and repeated firing in several CS/SS/TS analogues [CS][SS][TS]. The target’s mixed Y/Os/In polyhedral framework requires significant reconstruction from binary oxides [Target][Reasonable Inference]. Therefore kinetics are the weakest part of Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** High pressure can stabilize dense 5d oxide frameworks, as shown by the CS Ir oxide route [CS]. However, the starting materials remain refractory oxides/metals and the target ordering is not directly demonstrated [CS][Reasonable Inference]. The short HP dwell improves practicality but does not fully solve cation ordering uncertainty [CS][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.** Nitrate/citrate processing receives the highest kinetic score because CS/TS nitrate routes provide more reactive precursor mixing than oxide/metal grinding [CS][TS]. The final anneal still requires crystallization of an ordered R3 ilmenite-derived phase [Target][Reasonable Inference]. This route balances improved precursor activity against Os redox uncertainty [CS][TS][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4.** Flux/vapor-assisted synthesis may lower diffusion barriers, supported indirectly by SS/TS sealed Re oxide and CVT examples [SS][TS]. SS also reports multiphase material in a sealed Re/Pb oxide case, so phase selectivity is a major kinetic and thermodynamic uncertainty [SS]. This route is more useful for exploratory crystals than reliable bulk synthesis [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target triggers the radius-mismatch check because Y–O and Os–O bond lengths differ substantially and ionic-radius comparison implies >15% mismatch [Target][Reasonable Inference]. The target triggers the mixed-connectivity check because corner, edge, and face-sharing polyhedra coexist [Target]. The unit-cell atom count trigger is not applied because the formula unit has 10 atoms and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific unit-cell evidence, so that source signal is neutral 50 [Target][CS][SS][TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YOsIn2O6 is most likely a **borderline metastable or near-ground-state accessible phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold but not strongly below it [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of related complex oxides by solid-state, sealed-atmosphere, nitrate/ceramic, and high-pressure routes [CS][SS][TS]. The most important enabling factor is that 4d/5d octahedral oxides with M–O distances near ~2.0 Å are repeatedly synthesized in the analogues, matching the Os–O scale in the target [Target][CS][SS][TS]. The most important limiting factor is Os redox/volatility control, which is not directly benchmarked in CS/SS/TS and therefore must be managed by sealed, buffered conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The reference data are mildly contradictory because some CS analogues form by ambient solid-state routes while one close 5d oxide requires 9 GPa; I weight ambient soft-chemistry as best first attempt and high pressure as the fallback stabilization route [CS].
