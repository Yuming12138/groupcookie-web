### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.022 eV/atom, which is within the common synthesizable/metastable window and within the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring band. Similar halides in CS and SS are often on or near the hull: CS2/CS4 are 0 eV/atom, CS1 is ~0.0048 eV/atom, and SS1–SS4 are 0 eV/atom. TS analogues with E_hull values of ~0.01–0.03 eV/atom are still associated with reported synthesis routes (TS1–TS5), supporting feasibility. Remaining uncertainty is that no source explicitly reports Ga₂BrCl₅.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, consistent with 190–250 °C halide syntheses in CS1–CS4 and related sealed-ampoule conditions in SS3. The severity comes mainly from controlled atmosphere, volatility, and air/moisture sensitivity, all supported by evacuated ampoules/glove boxes and hygroscopic halide handling in CS4, SS1–SS5, and TS4–TS5. No high pressure or high temperature is required; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a complex multistep sequence is required for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics should be favorable because GaCl₃/GaBr₃ are reactive molecular halides and analogous compounds form at low temperature (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS3). The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: the target is 2D edge-sharing octahedral, whereas GaCl₃/GaBr₃ entries in SS1–SS2 are zero-dimensional molecular structures, though InBr₃ in SS4 supports an AlCl₃-type layered trihalide framework. This gives high but not perfect structural-reconstruction confidence.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Sublimation/transport uses low temperatures and no external pressure, as shown for GaCl₃/GaBr₃ and InBr₃ in SS1–SS4. It is penalized more heavily than Route 1 because SS1–SS2 describe multiple preparation/purification/crystal-selection operations, plus sealed evacuated ampoules and inert handling. Insufficient evidence from CS/TS for an easier mixed-halide vapor route.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Vapor-mediated crystallization bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, and structurally similar trihalides are grown by slow sublimation or gradients (SS1–SS4). The major concern is composition control: GaCl₃ and GaBr₃ may transport at different rates, so the deposited solid may deviate from Ga₂BrCl₅. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence on mixed Cl/Br vapor stoichiometry in gallium trihalides.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 keeps the same low-temperature advantage seen in SS3 and TS4–TS5 while using a gradient to improve crystallization. Deductions arise from dry/vacuum ampoule operation, volatile halides, and air-sensitive precursors, all supported by SS1–SS5 and TS4–TS5. It avoids the extra precursor-synthesis burden of Route 4 and the full sublimation-purification burden of Route 2.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
This route has favorable kinetics because it combines melt equilibration with vapor/gradient recrystallization, both of which are effective in low-temperature halide systems (CS1–CS4, SS3, TS4–TS5). Structural selection is plausible because layered/extended halide systems such as InBr₃ and Cu(AlCl₄)₂ are accessible by gradient methods (SS4, TS4). The uncertainty remains the lack of direct Ga₂BrCl₅ evidence and the possible need for long annealing to order Br and Cl.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 is not thermally severe but is operationally more challenging due to Br₂ handling and multiple precursor-purification steps. SS1–SS2 explicitly support GaBr₃ formation from Ga and Br₂ and purification by sublimation, but this adds toxicity and step complexity. CS and TS give insufficient evidence that direct elemental halogenation is advantageous for the final target phase.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Precursor formation is chemically facile because GaBr₃ formation from Ga/Br₂ is documented (SS1–SS2), and final reaction would still proceed through reactive halide chemistry. However, this route does not inherently solve mixed-halide ordering or layered phase selection. Therefore, it is less attractive than using prepurified GaCl₃/GaBr₃ directly.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, so cation radius mismatch is not a concern; this follows from the target composition. The structure is described as edge-sharing octahedra, not a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, reducing glass-forming/amorphization risk; SS4 supports that AlCl₃-type layered trihalides can crystallize. The exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ga₂BrCl₅ is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a highly unstable compound, because its E_hull is only 0.022 eV/atom and comparable TS analogues at 0.01–0.03 eV/atom have reported syntheses (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from SS data: GaCl₃/GaBr₃ are readily sublimed/grown in sealed ampoules (SS1–SS2), and AlCl₃-type InBr₃ forms a 2D layered trihalide structure by low-temperature gradient sublimation (SS4). CS data supports low-temperature sealed halide melt chemistry but is less structurally direct because many CS analogues are heterometallic tetrahalide salts rather than binary layered gallium mixed halides (CS1–CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of volatile gallium trihalides at 150–200 °C, supported by CS1–CS4 and SS1–SS4. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the exact Br:Cl = 1:5 stoichiometry and ordering during sealed-tube/vapor growth, for which there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS.
