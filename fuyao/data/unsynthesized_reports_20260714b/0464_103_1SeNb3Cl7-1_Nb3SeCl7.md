### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the calculated convex hull and giving a high thermodynamic score. The closest Nb₃TeCl₇ analogue also has E_hull = 0.0 and was synthesized quantitatively (CS1/SS1/TS1), while several Nb chloride thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS2–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of nearby Nb–Se–Cl competing phases, especially given the multiphase result in CS3.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate high temperature, around 800–830 °C, inferred from Nb₃TeCl₇ at 1090 K (CS1/SS1/TS1), so no >1000 °C penalty applies. The penalties arise from controlled Ar/glovebox handling and a sealed ampoule because NbCl₅ is moisture-sensitive and volatile, consistent with sealed-tube handling in CS1/CS3 and inert handling in CS2/SS5. The route is not high-pressure and is essentially single-step, as in the Nb₃TeCl₇ synthesis (CS1).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are helped by volatile/reactive NbCl₅ and by the fact that the target is structurally very close to Nb₃TeCl₇ (CS1/SS1/TS1). Kinetics are limited by Nb metal diffusion and the need to select Nb₃SeCl₇ rather than other Nb–Se–Cl phases; CS3 specifically shows NbSeCl₃ formation as a low-yield side product in a multiphase reaction. Overall, the route is plausible but likely requires optimization of stoichiometry and thermal profile.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is less thermally severe because CS3 demonstrates Nb–Se–Cl reactivity at 750 K. However, it requires vacuum sealing, volatile reagents, and air-sensitive/toxic precursor handling, so the severity score remains moderate rather than easy. Insufficient evidence from SS for exact low-temperature Nb₃SeCl₇ formation; SS mainly supports related Nb chloride sealed-tube practice rather than this specific phase.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The exact NbCl₅/Nb/Se chemistry is supported by CS3, but CS3 reports NbSeCl₃ as a side product in <2% yield in an inhomogeneous reaction mixture. That is a strong negative kinetic and phase-selection signal for this lower-temperature route. The route may still be useful for crystal growth or transport screening, but it is not the most reliable bulk route.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The flux route operates at moderate temperatures, 600–800 °C, based on molten salt synthesis of K₄Nb₆Cl₁₈ (TS5) and sealed-tube Nb chloride analogues around 720–800 °C (SS4/SS5/TS3). It is penalized for vacuum/inert handling, sealed volatile chemistry, and multiple steps such as flux drying, melting, sealing, staged heating, and recovery, as described in TS5. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten chloride flux should improve transport relative to direct solid-state diffusion, supported by TS5 where K₄Nb₆Cl₁₈ crystallizes from KCl/LiCl flux. The limitation is phase competition: structurally and thermodynamically similar alkali niobium chlorides are readily formed with K/Rb/Cs chloride environments (SS3/SS4; TS4/TS5). Thus, flux may improve kinetics but worsen composition control.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, Nb, so the cation-size mismatch trigger is absent. The target description gives an ordered 2D sheet of edge-sharing NbSeCl₅ octahedra rather than a highly mixed polyhedral network, and the closest analogue Nb₃TeCl₇ is also a 2D ordered sheet (CS1/SS1/TS1). Exact crystallographic cell multiplicity is not fully resolved from CS/SS/TS for the target, so that part is treated conservatively, but there is no strong amorphization warning.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₃SeCl₇ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Nb₃TeCl₇ analogue is also hull-stable and experimentally synthesized in quantitative yield (CS1/SS1/TS1). The strongest support comes from the nearly isostructural Nb₃TeCl₇ route, which uses the same NbCl₅/Nb/chalcogen synthetic logic and produces the same Nb₃QCl₇-type sheet topology (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the use of NbCl₅ in a sealed ampoule, because it supplies chlorine and volatile/reactive niobium chloride species that help overcome Nb metal diffusion limitations, as seen across many Nb chloride syntheses (CS1, CS2, SS2–SS5, TS2–TS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in the Nb–Se–Cl system, since the direct NbCl₅/Nb/Se reaction in CS3 produced NbSeCl₃ only as a low-yield side product in a multiphase mixture. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but it should be approached with rigorous stoichiometry, sealed-ampoule control, and screening of cooling rate/temperature.
