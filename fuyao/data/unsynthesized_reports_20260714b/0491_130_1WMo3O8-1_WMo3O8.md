### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.012 eV/atom, which falls well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS supports this because chemically similar W/Mo and W-containing oxides are known at or near the hull, including UMo₅O₁₆ at 0.027 eV/atom and Cr₂WO₆/V₂WO₆ at 0 eV/atom (CS1–CS3). TS supports that oxides with E_hull ≈0.017–0.037 eV/atom are experimentally accessible by electrochemical, ceramic, or vapor routes (TS1–TS5). SS is mixed because some structurally similar reduced Mo oxides have high computed hull energies yet are reported synthetically, indicating that structural similarity alone is not a reliable thermodynamic penalty; no useful negative signal strong enough to reduce F1 below 95.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-tube solid state

The severity score is reduced because analogous reduced Mo oxides require vacuum sealed tubes and long high-temperature treatments, e.g., SS2/SS3 at 1200 °C for 7 days and TS5 at 600/1000 °C for 3/7 days. The kinetic score is low because WO₂/MoO₂ are refractory oxides and W/Mo cation diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long annealing in SS2/SS3/TS5 and repeated firing in CS2. The uncertainty is whether WMo₃O₈ is reconstructively accessible from binary dioxides or whether it needs solution/flux mediation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, hydrothermal

The formal F2 score is high because the provided deduction scheme penalizes only >5 GPa high pressure, not 500 bar hydrothermal conditions; however, CS1 shows the actual route requires specialized high-pressure hydrothermal equipment. Kinetically, hydrothermal dissolution/reprecipitation should improve mass transport relative to dry oxide diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS1 demonstrates Mo/W oxide formation at only 573 K. The major uncertainty is phase control: CS1 explicitly reports a multiphasic sample, so WMo₃O₈ may appear only in a narrow redox/composition window.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, chemical vapor transport

F2 is penalized because CS3 and TS3 use sealed evacuated quartz ampoules and TeCl₄ transport agent, with CS3 noting purification and water removal. Kinetically, halide transport can deliver mobile species and improve crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], while TS3 shows V₄O₇ crystals grown after powder preparation and CS3 shows W-containing oxide transport at 1000–930 °C. Remaining uncertainty is whether W and Mo transport congruently; cation-ratio drift could produce MoO₂, WO₂, or other Magnéli-like oxides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, flux growth

F2 is moderate because SS1 uses evacuated double quartz tubes, 850 °C, and two-week dwell/cooling but no high pressure. F3 benefits from liquid flux transport, as SS1 successfully grows a reduced Mo oxide in Sn flux, but the target lacks Sn and Sn incorporation is a serious phase-selection risk. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for Sn-free WMo₃O₈ flux stability; therefore this route is treated as plausible but not preferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has very similar W–O and Mo–O bond lengths around 2.03–2.05 Å, so cation-size mismatch is low [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target description shows edge-sharing W/MoO₆ pentagonal pyramids but does not report simultaneous corner-, edge-, and face-sharing coexistence, so structural glass-forming frustration appears limited. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact unit-cell atom count, but the formula and site listing do not suggest a very large cell, so the amorphization risk is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

WMo₃O₈ is likely a near-ground-state or only weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.012 eV/atom, well within the range of experimentally accessible oxides supported by CS and TS analogues. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1, where Mo/W oxide chemistry is accessible hydrothermally, and from SS2/SS3/TS5, where reduced Mo oxide frameworks are accessible in sealed-tube ceramic reactions. The most critical enabling factor is precise oxygen-potential control to stabilize reduced W/Mo–O bonding rather than fully oxidized molybdates/tungstates. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: CS1 reports multiphasic hydrothermal products, CS2 requires repeated firing for single phase, and SS3 reports unsuccessful attempts for related ordered Mo oxides. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible, but likely requires systematic redox and stoichiometry screening.
