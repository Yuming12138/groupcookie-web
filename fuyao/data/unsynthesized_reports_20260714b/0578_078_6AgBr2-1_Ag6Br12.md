### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0080 eV/atom, which is inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS supports this because CuBr₂ and CdI₂ analogues are stable or nearly stable, with E_hull values from 0 to about 0.0084 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-5). TS strongly supports this because thermodynamic analogues have similarly low E_hull values around 0.0057–0.0139 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-4). SS is less decisive because some structurally similar halides have high calculated E_hull values, so structural similarity alone is not the main support (SS-1, SS-3).

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Condition severity is low because CS demonstrates room-temperature aqueous slow evaporation for CuBr₂ and CdI₂ crystals (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5), and SS demonstrates bromide-rich recrystallization from concentrated HBr without high pressure or high temperature (SS-1). The main severity penalty is chemical hazard from HBr/Br₂, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from the bromine-rich target stoichiometry. Kinetics are favorable because solution growth avoids long-range solid-state diffusion, but target ordering may be harder than simple CuBr₂/CdI₂ because the structure is triclinic P1 with many inequivalent Ag/Br sites and a distinct Br cluster.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The sealed-ampoule vapor route has moderate severity because TS-1 and SS-4 use evacuated ampoules, temperature gradients, and sometimes glovebox handling, while Br₂ volatility requires sealed containment by [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are plausible because TS-1 and SS-4 show bromide-rich cluster compounds can crystallize from vapor at only 423–453 K. The uncertainty is composition control: TS-1 reports byproducts and some metastable low-yield bromide phases, suggesting that precise halogen chemical potential may be critical.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
The solid-state route is more severe because it combines vacuum sealing, volatile Br₂, hazardous reagents, and multiple handling steps, analogous to SS-5 and TS-4 sealed-tube reactions. Kinetics are mixed: low-temperature halide reactions are possible, but SS-5 and TS-4 required four months and four weeks, respectively, indicating slow ordering in related systems. The target’s low-symmetry layered arrangement suggests solid-state reconstruction from AgBr may be sluggish unless fine powders, long anneals, or seeds are used.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation type, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered by [Reasonable Inference from the target formula]. The description does not clearly indicate a >40-atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity; therefore, evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient for those checks and they are treated as not triggered. The main crystallization risk is not amorphization but competing crystalline Ag–Br phases and bromine stoichiometry control.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgBr₂ is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0080 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues in TS occupy a similarly low-energy range. CS strongly supports mild crystallization routes for chemically related binary halides, especially CuBr₂ and CdI₂ grown by slow evaporation from solution. TS and SS support sealed-vessel alternatives for bromide-rich or halide-rich phases when volatile halogen chemical potential must be maintained. The most critical enabling factor is high bromide/bromine chemical potential during crystallization, which should stabilize the Br-rich AgBr₂ stoichiometry. The most critical limiting factor is competition with simpler AgBr or other bromine-rich molecular/polybromide species, requiring careful control of Br₂ concentration, light exposure, and crystallization rate.
