### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper boundary of the 90–100 scoring band, so a conservative score of 90 is assigned. CS/SS report an exact-composition KIO₂F₂ analogue with E_hull = 0.0, indicating that the composition is intrinsically stable or nearly stable. TS examples show experimentally synthesized iodate/oxyhalide materials with E_hull values of about 0.041–0.106 eV/atom, so the target’s small metastability is not prohibitive. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the target may be metastable relative to the known CS/SS KIO₂F₂ polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because CS/SS report crystal growth from saturated KIO₃ in 50% aqueous HF with no high temperature, high pressure, or inert atmosphere. The only major penalty is HF toxicity/corrosivity, which is directly present in CS/SS. TS gives insufficient evidence for this exact low-temperature HF crystallization route and is neutral, 50, for this route’s severity. The overall severity is therefore low to moderate.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CS/SS show that centimeter-scale transparent KIO₂F₂ crystals grow from solution, which implies favorable nucleation/growth kinetics and adequate mass transport. Because the target shares formula and space group with the CS/SS analogue, structural reconstruction is expected to be limited, although local coordination differences create uncertainty. TS gives insufficient route-specific kinetic evidence for HF cooling crystallization and is neutral, 50. The route scores very high kinetically.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CS/SS RbIO₂F₂ hydrothermal synthesis uses 230 °C, HF solution, an autoclave, and slow cooling, which are moderate rather than extreme conditions. TS hydrothermal iodates also use 200–230 °C in Teflon-lined autoclaves, supporting feasibility at similar temperatures. The penalties arise from sealed HF and toxic/corrosive reagents. The route is more severe than Route 1 but still practical in an equipped inorganic synthesis lab.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CS/SS show the Rb analogue forms as a single phase with high yield under hydrothermal conditions, suggesting favorable solution-mediated kinetics. TS hydrothermal iodates also crystallize under comparable temperatures/times, supporting adequate iodine-oxide rearrangement in water/autogenous pressure. The main uncertainty is cation-size effects: K⁺ may stabilize a different local coordination or polymorph compared with Rb⁺. Overall kinetics are strong.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The solid-state route is less directly supported: CS/SS KSeO₂F forms from SeO₂ and KF under Ar in sealed ampoules at 250 °C, but KIO₂F₂ would likely require an added fluorinating agent by [Reasonable Inference]. Dry atmosphere, sealed volatile HF/NH₄HF₂ chemistry, and multi-step handling create significant deductions. TS gives insufficient evidence for an anhydrous K–I–O–F solid-state route and is neutral, 50. Severity is therefore challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CS/SS KSeO₂F demonstrates that alkali oxyfluorides can form at low temperature by solid-state reaction, which supports moderate diffusion feasibility. However, transferring Se(IV) oxyfluoride chemistry to iodine(V) oxyfluoride requires more substantial chemical reconstruction and careful fluorination. The multi-day sintering precedent in CS/SS reduces the kinetic score relative to solution routes. Remaining uncertainty is high because no direct KIO₂F₂ solid-state synthesis is provided.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

SS reports CsOIF₄ recrystallization from CsI and IF₅ in acetonitrile, so the temperature/pressure demands are mild. However, IF₅ is reactive, volatile, and moisture-sensitive by physicochemical reasoning, and the route requires dry handling. CS gives insufficient evidence for IF₅/acetonitrile synthesis of KIO₂F₂ specifically and is neutral, 50; TS also provides insufficient evidence and is neutral, 50. The route is chemically hazardous but not thermally severe.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

SS supports rapid solution-phase formation/crystallization of an alkali iodine oxyfluoride/fluoride compound from IF₅ chemistry. Solution transport should be favorable, so diffusion is not limiting. The limiting kinetic/speciation issue is that SS targets CsOIF₄ rather than KIO₂F₂, so over-fluorinated iodine products may compete. This gives a good but not best kinetic score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ionic molecular/extended oxyfluoride with moderate structural complexity, and CS/SS exact analogue has a 24-atom Pca2₁ cell, below the >40-atom amorphization trigger. The target description indicates corner-sharing IO₃F₂ square pyramids rather than mixed edge/face/vertex networks, reducing glass-forming tendency. The K⁺ versus iodine(V) size/coordination contrast triggers one risk criterion by physicochemical reasoning. TS gives insufficient target-specific amorphization evidence and is neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a slightly metastable polymorph or coordination variant rather than a deep ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while the exact-composition CS/SS KIO₂F₂ analogue is reported at E_hull = 0.0. The CS/SS evidence is exceptionally strong for making the composition KIO₂F₂, especially by KIO₃ dissolution/crystallization in 50% HF. TS evidence supports the broader idea that iodine oxide/oxyhalide phases near this metastability range can be crystallized hydrothermally. The most important enabling factor is solution-mediated fluorination/crystallization in concentrated HF, which avoids slow solid-state diffusion and directly supplies the IOₓFᵧ chemistry. The main limiting factor is polymorph control: the known stable KIO₂F₂ analogue may crystallize preferentially instead of the target’s specific 6-coordinate K / IO₃F₂ square-pyramidal arrangement.
