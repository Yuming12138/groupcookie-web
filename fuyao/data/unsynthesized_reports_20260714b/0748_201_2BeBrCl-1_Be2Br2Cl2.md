### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0260 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score [Target]. TS analogues with similar low E_hull values are experimentally synthesized, including Ti(AlBr₄)₂ at 0.0234 eV/atom by CVT (TS1), Cu(AlCl₄)₂ at 0.0099 eV/atom by CVT (TS4), and SrThBr₆ at 0.0451 eV/atom by sealed-tube synthesis (TS3). CS evidence is supportive but less direct: TiOCl has E_hull ~0.033 and is synthesized by gas transport (CS2), while CoOBr at higher E_hull ~0.103 is also synthesized by sealed-tube reaction (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Br/Cl ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures and no high pressure, consistent with low-temperature halide CVT examples at 155–250 °C in TS1 and TS4. The deductions come from vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile halide sources, which are central to TS1 and TS4, plus beryllium toxicity and moisture sensitivity as a [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues show that sealed ampoules and inert handling are routine but nontrivial for halide frameworks (SS2, SS5, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS on Be-halide toxicity/volatility specifically, so CS is neutral 50 for that subfactor.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CVT/vapor equilibration should provide favorable mass transport because analogous halide frameworks crystallize through volatile Al₂Br₆/Al₂Cl₆ species at low temperature (TS1, TS4). The target’s 2D corner-sharing tetrahedral network requires assembly from binary halides, but analogous low-dimensional halide frameworks crystallize in sealed transport or ampoule conditions (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is absence of a direct BeBrCl analogue in CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction is scored moderately rather than maximally. CS provides only indirect support through TiOCl gas transport (CS2), while SS/TS provide stronger halide-specific transport evidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more operationally severe than Route 1 because it likely requires full or partial melting, slow cooling, and possibly quench/anneal optimization [Reasonable Inference]. Similar melt growth/fractional crystallization methods require sealed ampoules, inert atmospheres, quenching, slow cooling, or zone refinement in SS1, SS2, and SS4. The temperature remains moderate relative to ceramic solid-state synthesis, but volatile sealed halides and toxic Be compounds impose deductions [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for Be halide melt growth specifically and is neutral 50 for that route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt growth has good kinetics because the reacting halides are in a liquid or highly mobile phase, as shown by SS1, SS2, SS3, SS4, and SS5. However, SS4 demonstrates that quenching can stabilize a metastable framework and annealing can change phase identity, so cooling rate and phase selection are important. For BeBrCl, the desired ordered mixed-halide sheet may compete with BeCl₂/BeBr₂ solid solutions or separate binary phases [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are good but not as robust as a known same-structure melt system.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate temperature sealed annealing, similar to CoOBr at 320 °C in a sealed tube (CS1) and Li₂CrCl₄ at 400 °C for one week in evacuated Pyrex (TS5). Deductions arise from vacuum/sealed handling, volatile halides, likely glovebox operations, and possible repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference; supported by TS3 and TS5]. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions. The severity remains moderate-to-challenging because Be compounds add practical hazard not represented directly in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solid-state halide annealing is plausible because analogous binary-halide reactions work at 320–400 °C in CS1 and TS5. Nevertheless, TS3 and TS5 show that ordered halide/cation phases can require long annealing times of one to several weeks, indicating slower kinetics than melt or CVT routes. The target requires Br/Cl ordering around Be tetrahedra in a 2D sheet [Target], which may be difficult to equilibrate in a purely solid route [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, F3 is lower than for vapor or melt routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple composition with one cation and corner-sharing tetrahedra only [Target], so the checklist triggers none of the amorphization-risk criteria. Mixed edge/corner connectivity is common in several SS/TS analogues, such as SS1/SS2 and TS1/TS4, but the target itself is described only as corner-sharing BeBr₂Cl₂ tetrahedra [Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS directly quantifies amorphization of BeBrCl, so each source is neutral 50 for that evidence category. The final low-risk score is based on the target structural description rather than analogue statistics.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeBrCl is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its energy above hull is only 0.026 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from TS and SS analogues showing that low-dimensional halide frameworks with E_hull near zero can be synthesized by sealed-tube CVT, melt growth, or slow cooling at relatively low temperatures (TS1, TS4, SS1, SS2, SS4). CS support is weaker but still useful because chemically related 2D oxyhalides such as TiOCl and CoOBr are made by gas transport or sealed-tube reaction (CS1, CS2). The most important enabling factor is high halide mobility/volatility in sealed ampoules, which enables crystallization far below conventional ceramic temperatures (TS1, TS4, SS2). The most important limiting factor is practical control of toxic, moisture-sensitive, volatile Be halides and the risk of forming binary BeCl₂/BeBr₂ or disordered mixed-halide phases [Reasonable Inference].
