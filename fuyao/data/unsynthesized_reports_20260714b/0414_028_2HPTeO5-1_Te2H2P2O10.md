### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly indicates a predicted ground-state phase and warrants the maximum thermodynamic score. Many analogues in CS, SS, and TS also have E_hull ≈ 0 and reported syntheses, including CS1, CS3, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, and TS5. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or hydration/proton-position stability under laboratory conditions.

**F2 — Route 1 Hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal conditions of 160–250 °C are consistently reported across CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2, SS3, TS1, and TS2, so severity is low. No high pressure beyond autogenous hydrothermal pressure and no inert/reducing atmosphere are required in those sources. The only deduction is for toxic Te-containing precursors, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from tellurium chemistry.

**F3 — Route 1 Hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis gives high kinetic accessibility because acid solution chemistry activates phosphate and tellurium oxide precursors, as shown by CS4, SS2, and TS2. Ion diffusion limitations are minimal because framework assembly occurs from dissolved or partially dissolved species rather than dense solid-state diffusion. The uncertainty is the lack of a direct P–H–Te–O analogue in the provided set, so structural reconstruction is scored below ideal.

**F2 — Route 2 Slow Evaporation/Concentration.**  
This route is mild because CS5, TS3, and TS4 use ambient to 140 °C solution crystallization in air or simple desiccator conditions. It avoids autoclaves and controlled atmospheres, but still uses toxic Te compounds by [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS supports direct Te–P slow-evaporation synthesis, so confidence is lower despite mild conditions.

**F3 — Route 2 Slow Evaporation/Concentration.**  
Solution crystallization has favorable kinetics for protonated oxyanion salts, supported by CS5 and TS3. However, TS3 reports variable crystallization outcomes and mixture formation for some compositions, indicating that evaporation rate and acid ratio can strongly affect phase selection. Therefore, kinetics are good but phase-selective nucleation is less certain than in the hydrothermal route.

**F2 — Route 3 Flux/Sealed Growth.**  
CS2 gives a flux/sealed-ampoule precedent, but its chemical relevance is weak and its evidence appears internally inconsistent with the listed formula. A sealed/vacuum or volatile-component-controlled setup adds severity, and H/P precursors may release volatile species at elevated temperature by [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports this route, so it remains exploratory.

**F3 — Route 3 Flux/Sealed Growth.**  
Flux growth can reduce diffusion barriers, which is supported generally by CS2. The major kinetic and chemical problem is that a protonated target phase may dehydrate or reconstruct into anhydrous phosphate/tellurate phases at flux temperatures. Because SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-temperature stabilization of protonated P–Te oxide sheets, structural reconstruction is scored low.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target combines small tetrahedral P centers and larger TeO₅ square-pyramidal centers, triggering the ionic-radius/polyhedral-size mismatch check by physicochemical reasoning. The structure is corner-sharing only according to the target description, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve that factor, so it is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PHTeO₅ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS analogues strongly support solution-based synthesis of protonated oxyanion frameworks, especially hydrothermal synthesis of hydrogen phosphates and tellurium oxides. The most critical enabling factor is acidic hydrothermal chemistry, which can dissolve/activate TeO₂ and H₃PO₄ while preserving O–H groups and enabling corner-sharing framework assembly. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Te–O and P–O systems can form competing tellurates, phosphates, or hydrated acid salts depending on acidity, water activity, and cooling rate, as suggested by phase-control issues in CS4, TS2, and TS3. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with hydrothermal crystallization being the most credible route.
