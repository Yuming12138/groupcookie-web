### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0400 eV/atom, placing it in the highest prescribed stability bracket of ≤0.05 eV/atom. TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, and TS-5 show that materials with comparable E_hull values around 0.026–0.033 eV/atom have reported syntheses, supporting the idea that a 40 meV/atom phase is experimentally accessible. However, CS contains chemically related oxyhalides with higher E_hull values, including CdOCl at 0.321 eV/atom and CdOI at 0.295 eV/atom, so thermodynamic similarity is more encouraging than chemical-analogue hull data. Remaining uncertainty is that CoIF may be metastable against CoF₂/CoI₂ or other cobalt halides even though its hull distance is small.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses a sealed tube and controlled dry/vacuum atmosphere, analogous to CoOBr synthesis in a sealed tube at 320 °C (CS-3). Its temperature is mild compared with SS/TS solid-state analogues at 800 °C or 1000 °C (SS-1/SS-3; TS-1/TS-4), but iodide volatility and cobalt-halide toxicity require sealed handling. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for direct mixed fluoride/iodide cobalt halide formation, so the severity estimate relies mainly on CS-3 and chemical inference. No high pressure is required, which keeps the score from being low.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CoF₂/CoI₂ halide precursors should be more reactive than dense oxides **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS-3 demonstrates cobalt-halide-containing chemistry at only 320 °C. Diffusion may still be limiting because this is a solid-state route, and SS-2/SS-3 show repeated regrinding/heating cycles can be needed for layered phases. The target’s simple 2D corner-sharing tetrahedral topology reduces reconstruction burden, but no direct CS/SS/TS analogue has exactly CoIF, so the kinetics score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 has the mildest conditions because CS-1 and CS-4 demonstrate sealed hydrothermal crystallization of halide-containing materials at 210–250 °C. It avoids high temperature, high pressure above 5 GPa, and vacuum, but it still involves sealed autogenous pressure, toxic cobalt/fluoride salts, and volatile iodide/solvent components. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports solvothermal CoIF specifically; the route is mostly inferred from CS hydrothermal oxyhalide precedents. The main severity penalty is chemical handling rather than thermal or pressure demand.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution-mediated crystallization gives high mobility and high precursor activity, consistent with CS-1 and CS-4 producing crystals over several days at moderate temperatures. This route should bypass the slow bulk diffusion limitations observed in SS/TS solid-state syntheses that require long holds or repeated cycles (SS-2/SS-3; TS-2/TS-3). The uncertainty is chemical selectivity: CS-1/CS-4 form oxyhalides, and CS-3 shows cobalt hydroxide plus cobalt halide can form cobalt oxyhalide, so any water or hydroxide could divert the reaction away from oxygen-free CoIF. Therefore the kinetics score is high but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is more demanding because SS-5/TS-5 and SS-4 use sealed vacuum ampoules and temperature gradients, and SS-4 reports long CVT times and specialized two-zone furnace conditions. It does not require >1000 °C or high pressure, but it does require volatile iodine/halogen transport and controlled ampoule chemistry. CS-5 gives weak support for vapor methods, but its listed precursors appear inconsistent with CdOI, so it is not weighted strongly. The route is best regarded as a crystal-growth method after powder synthesis rather than the first screening route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Iodine vapor transport is supported by WOI₃ growth in a 440–400 °C gradient (SS-5/TS-5), and cobalt oxyhalide CVT is supported by SS-4. However, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows reliable fluorine vapor transport for CoIF, and simultaneous F/I stoichiometry control is likely difficult **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS-4 explicitly reports phase-control difficulty, off-stoichiometric optimization, and long growth times for related cobalt oxyhalides, which lowers the composition-control component. Kinetically, CVT may yield crystals but is less robust for phase discovery.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. Its described structure is simple, 2D, and corner-sharing, with no evidence for a large >40-atom cell or mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity. CS-3 and CS-4 show related 2D cobalt/copper oxyhalide sheets crystallize rather than amorphize, which supports low amorphization risk, although those are not exact CoIF analogues. Remaining uncertainty is that anhydrous mixed-halide ordering could be frustrated if F/I disorder occurs.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoIF is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.040 eV/atom, slightly above the convex hull. The analogue support is good but indirect: CS-3 provides the strongest chemical precedent for sealed cobalt halide chemistry, CS-1/CS-4 support low-temperature sealed-fluid crystallization, and SS-5/TS-5 support iodide vapor transport, but no source directly reports oxygen-free Co–I–F. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty combined with low-temperature routes that can preserve volatile iodide and allow layered crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is F/I stoichiometry control under anhydrous conditions, because water or hydroxide could favor cobalt oxyhalides like the CS analogues. Overall, synthesis is feasible, with solvothermal screening recommended first and sealed solid-state synthesis as the most chemically direct backup.
