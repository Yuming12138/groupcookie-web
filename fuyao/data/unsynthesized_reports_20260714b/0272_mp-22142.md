### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper boundary of the ≤0.05 eV/atom category, so a high but not perfect score of 90 is assigned. CS supports feasibility because chemically close GeS₂ and GeSe₂ polymorphs have E_hull values near 0.055 and 0.0125 eV/atom and reported syntheses (CS-1, CS-5). TS also supports synthesizability of metastable selenides around 0.054–0.083 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS for the exact binary SiSe₂ thermodynamic competition, so SS is treated as neutral for F1.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because the closest SiS₂ analogues require high pressure of 2.8–4.0 GPa and high temperature up to 1473 K, followed by quenching and decompression (CS-2/CS-3/CS-4). GeSe₂/GeS₂ analogues can require 3.0–7.7 GPa or up to 9 GPa, confirming that pressure tuning may be important (CS-1, CS-5; SS-4; TS-5). The score is not lower than 60 because the recommended starting window is 3–5 GPa, avoiding the >5 GPa penalty initially. Insufficient evidence from SS for a better low-pressure route to the exact target polymorph, so CS is weighted most heavily.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are moderately favorable under HP conditions because SiS₂ HP phases form in only 15 min at 1073–1473 K (CS-2/CS-3/CS-4). The target has the same Si-centered tetrahedral ribbon motif as the SiS₂ analogue, reducing the reconstruction penalty (CS-2). The main uncertainty is elemental Si reactivity, because Si is refractory and may retain an oxide skin [Reasonable Inference]. Using pre-reacted amorphous/glassy SiSe₂ is a reasonable way to improve kinetics, based on GeS₂/GeSe₂ glass starting materials in CS-1/TS-5.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high pressure and uses standard evacuated ampoule conditions similar to many SS/TS selenides: 550–800 °C for days in evacuated silica or vycor tubes (SS-1 to SS-5) and 600–950 °C in iodine-assisted or solid-state syntheses (TS-1). However, it still requires vacuum sealing, volatile Se containment, staged heating, and toxic Se handling [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore moderate rather than easy. Insufficient evidence from CS for ambient-pressure formation of the desired SiSe₂ polymorph lowers confidence.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct solid-state synthesis from Si and Se is kinetically limited by refractory Si and the need to build an ordered 1D edge-sharing tetrahedral network from elements [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues show that sealed-tube selenide reactions can work, but often require several days to weeks, e.g. 4–17 days in SS-2/SS-3/SS-5 and 72 h or longer in TS-1/TS-2. Molten or vapor-phase Se should help mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. The route may form amorphous SiSe₂ or another polymorph rather than the target I4/mcm phase because CS points to pressure sensitivity for close SiS₂ analogues.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT avoids high pressure and uses temperatures comparable to TS iodine-assisted selenide syntheses, usually 600–950 °C (TS-1, TS-2). The penalties arise from evacuated sealed tubes, volatile Se, iodine handling, and a multi-step protocol similar to the two-step powder formation plus iodine transport in TS-2. The method is less equipment-intensive than Route 1 but chemically more delicate than a simple solid-state reaction. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS that iodine CVT stabilizes the exact SiSe₂ I4/mcm polymorph.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Iodine-assisted transport improves kinetics by enabling vapor-mediated redistribution, which is directly supported by TS-1 and TS-2. This can bypass some limitations of Si solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, phase selection is uncertain because the closest structural SiS₂ analogue is obtained under high pressure in CS-2/CS-3/CS-4. Thus Route 3 is kinetically better than Route 2 but less phase-selective than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary with one cation species, so no large cation-radius mismatch is present. The structure is described as 1D edge-sharing SiSe₄ tetrahedral ribbons, not a complex mixed-polyhedral framework, which supports low amorphization risk. CS-2 gives a closely related simple SiS₂ ribbon structure, reinforcing this assessment. Remaining uncertainty is that Si–Se network formers can form glasses [Reasonable Inference from GeS₂/GeSe₂ glass use in CS-1/TS-5], but the specified target structure itself does not trigger the prescribed amorphization checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target SiSe₂ phase is likely a slightly metastable phase rather than a robust ground state because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the metastability threshold. CS gives the strongest support: the closest SiS₂ ribbon analogue is synthesized by high-pressure quench methods, and GeSe₂/GeS₂ polymorphs show similar chalcogenide networks can be pressure-stabilized. SS and TS support general selenide synthesis in evacuated ampoules and iodine-assisted transport, but they do not prove access to the exact binary I4/mcm SiSe₂ phase. The most critical enabling factor is pressure-assisted stabilization of the edge-sharing SiSe₄ ribbon topology. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against amorphous Si–Se, other SiSe₂ polymorphs, or Se-deficient products caused by Se volatility.
