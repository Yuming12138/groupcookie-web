### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0540 eV/atom, placing it just inside the 0.05–0.20 eV/atom bracket and only slightly above the commonly accessible metastability threshold. TS-1 is especially supportive because Cu₈SiSe₆ has nearly the same E_hull, ≈0.0545 eV/atom, and was synthesized in sealed quartz ampoules at 500 °C for 4 days. CS/SS data are also favorable because the closest BaCu₂Se₂ Pnma analogue is very near the hull, E_hull ≈0.004 eV/atom, and was synthesized by flux growth (CS-3/SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is that Sr substitution may shift relative polymorph stability; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an experimentally known SrCu₂Se₂ Pnma phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 Flux Growth

Route 1 uses only moderate temperature, 480 °C, directly supported by BaCu₂Se₂ flux growth (CS-2/CS-3/SS-1/TS-2). Deductions arise from sealed volatile Se/selenocyanate chemistry, multiple handling/heating/washing steps, and sensitive selenide/flux components (CS-2/CS-3/SS-1). No high pressure or >1000 °C heating is needed based on the closest analogues. The main uncertainty is whether Sr(CuSe)₂ shares the same flux solubility and crystallization window as BaCu₂Se₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 Flux Growth

Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because the closest BaCu₂Se₂ Pnma analogue formed at 480 °C, indicating liquid-mediated transport and low diffusion barriers (CS-3/SS-1). The structural reconstruction penalty is small because the target and orthorhombic BaCu₂Se₂ share the same Pnma framework and CuSe₄ tetrahedral connectivity (CS-3/SS-1). The score is reduced because BaCu₂Se₂ sources report α/β polymorph coexistence and product/washing sensitivity (CS-2/CS-3/SS-1/TS-2). Insufficient evidence from TS for the exact flux kinetics of Sr rather than Ba, so Sr-specific behavior remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2 Solid-State Sealed Tube

The solid-state route is more severe because it likely requires vacuum sealing, volatile Se containment, SrSe/Sr handling, and repeated grinding/annealing [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-4 and TS-1]. Similar solid-state selenides were made at 500–850 °C, avoiding >1000 °C penalties (CS-1/CS-4/CS-5/TS-1). CS-4 explicitly used vacuum, slow heating/cooling, regrinding, pelletization, and secondary sintering, supporting the multi-step deduction. Insufficient evidence from SS for direct solid-state synthesis of the Pnma BaCu₂Se₂ structure, so route transferability is less certain than flux.

#### F3 — Route 2 Solid-State Sealed Tube

Binary Cu₂Se-based reactions can work at 500 °C in sealed quartz ampoules, as shown by TS-1, and chemically similar selenides can form at 800–850 °C (CS-1/CS-4/CS-5). However, dense SrSe and Cu powders require solid-state diffusion, and CS-4’s need for regrinding and secondary sintering indicates kinetic limitations. The structural match to Pnma BaCu₂Se₂ is favorable (CS-3/SS-1), but the lack of a direct solid-state example for the same structure lowers the score. Remaining uncertainty is whether solid-state annealing will instead yield tetragonal ThCr₂Si₂-type SrCu₂Se₂ or binary impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 Melt / Bridgman

Melt growth is plausible because TS-5 reports BaFe₂Se₃ crystal growth from a melt of high-purity elements, and TS-4 reports vacuum-sealed high-temperature chalcogenide crystal formation with controlled cooling. Severity is increased by likely high temperature, volatile Se pressure, vacuum/sealed containment, and air-sensitive Sr [Reasonable Inference; TS-4 supports vacuum sealing and Ar handling]. No high-pressure equipment is indicated in TS-4 or TS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for melt growth of the exact Pnma A–Cu–Se phase makes this a less preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 3 Melt / Bridgman

Once molten, diffusion is favorable, so the kinetic score benefits from the liquid state, consistent with TS-5 melt growth. However, direct crystallization of the desired Pnma polymorph is uncertain because the closest BaCu₂Se₂ analogue was made by flux, not melt growth (CS-3/SS-1). Controlled cooling in TS-4 suggests a practical way to grow crystals, but BaCu₂Se₂ polymorph competition in CS-2/CS-3/SS-1/TS-2 lowers confidence. The main uncertainty is incongruent melting or loss of Se/Sr from the melt [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has large cation size contrast between Sr and Cu, and its structure includes mixed corner/edge/face-sharing Cu-centered polyhedra, both of which raise amorphization or disorder risk [Reasonable Inference; mixed connectivity explicitly appears in target and Ba analogue CS-3/SS-1]. The unit cell is likely not large, because the Pnma Ba analogue is Ba₄Cu₈Se₈ with 20 atoms per listed cell (CS-3/SS-1). Thus the risk is medium rather than high. Insufficient evidence from TS for amorphization behavior in SrCu₂Se₂ specifically.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(CuSe)₂ with E_hull = 0.054 eV/atom is likely a slightly metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply unstable compound. The CS/SS evidence is strong because the Pnma BaCu₂Se₂ analogue has nearly identical local coordination and was synthesized by flux growth (CS-3/SS-1). TS evidence supports that materials at similar E_hull values can be synthesized, especially Cu₈SiSe₆ at ≈0.0545 eV/atom by sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction (TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature flux transport, which can crystallize the Pnma framework without requiring slow bulk diffusion (CS-2/CS-3/SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among A–Cu–Se polymorphs and possible sensitivity during post-synthesis washing, both observed for BaCu₂Se₂ analogues (CS-2/CS-3/SS-1/TS-2).
