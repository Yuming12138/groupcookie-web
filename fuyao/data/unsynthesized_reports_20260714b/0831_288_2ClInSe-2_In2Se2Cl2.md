### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0210 eV/atom, which falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range; this is a target-provided value. Thermodynamic analogues with similar small positive hull energies, such as Li₂In₂GeSe₆ at 0.017 eV/atom and Ag₂In₂GeSe₆ at 0.018 eV/atom, were synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods (TS-1, TS-2). Cd₄GeSe₆ at ~0.022 eV/atom was also experimentally obtained, though with minor GeSe₂ impurity (TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that the hull does not guarantee the specific layered polymorph, but it strongly suggests the composition is not highly metastable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, close to the 400 °C GaTeCl synthesis (CS-2), so no high-temperature penalty was applied. The main severity comes from evacuated sealed ampoule operation and air-/moisture-sensitive chloride handling, both supported by GaCl₃/GaTeCl handling under inert gas and evacuated ampoules (CS-2). InTeBr also supports sealed halide-chalcogenide handling but indicates hygroscopicity and phase-growth care (CS-4/SS-4). TS gives **insufficient evidence from TS** for chloride-specific severity, so its route-severity signal is treated as neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because GaTeCl forms from a trihalide and sesquichalcogenide at only 400 °C over 7 days (CS-2), implying reactive halide-assisted transport rather than refractory diffusion. The target structure is extremely close to GaTeCl, both having MCh₃Cl tetrahedral sheets and single-bonded Cl (CS-2 and target description). The uncertainty is phase competition: CS-2 notes GaTeCl as an intermediate/hygroscopic phase, and CS-1 shows mixed halide products can be stoichiometry-sensitive. SS and TS provide general In–Se tetrahedral support but **insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS** for InSeCl chloride kinetics, neutral = 50 for that specific point.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not thermally severe because InTeBr melt growth occurred at 520 °C (CS-4/SS-4), far below 1000 °C. It still requires sealed/vacuum ampoule practice and controlled crystal-growth conditions, as supported by the Bridgman arrangement for InTeBr (CS-4/SS-4). Chloride volatility is likely more challenging than bromide volatility [Reasonable Inference], so sealed-system and stoichiometry penalties are retained. TS gives **insufficient evidence from TS** for Bridgman growth of In–Se–Cl specifically, neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt/Bridgman growth should have good mass transport because InTeBr crystals were obtained from the melt at 520 °C (CS-4/SS-4). Structurally similar In–Se tetrahedral compounds can also be grown from melts, though often at much higher temperatures and with more complex furnace programs (SS-1). The limitation is that InSeCl may not melt congruently, and InTeBr itself has incongruent melting behavior and hygroscopic handling concerns (CS-4/SS-4). Therefore kinetics are good, but phase retention during crystallization is less certain than for a simple anneal.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses low temperature, based on In–Se solvothermal synthesis at 185–200 °C (CS-3), so thermal severity is low. The deductions arise from autoclave/amine or NH₃ media, multiple processing steps, and chloride moisture sensitivity inferred from the GaCl₃ analogue (CS-2, CS-3). SS gives **insufficient evidence from SS** and TS gives **insufficient evidence from TS** for solvothermal InSeCl formation, so both are neutral = 50 for direct transferability. This route is experimentally attractive but less proven.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solvothermal chemistry can activate In and Se powders and produce indium selenide frameworks at moderate temperature (CS-3), giving good precursor-activity and diffusion scores. However, CS-3 reports solvent-dependent layered versus chain products and byproducts, which suggests phase selectivity could be difficult. There is no supplied analogue showing chloride-terminated InSeCl sheets by solvothermal methods; **insufficient evidence from SS/TS** for this pathway is neutral = 50. Thus the route is kinetically plausible but structurally uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered by the target description. The supplied structure is a simple 2D InSeCl sheet built from corner-sharing InSe₃Cl tetrahedra, with no edge/face-sharing coexistence stated, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered. Similar tetrahedral In–Se environments are common in CS-3, SS-1 to SS-3, and TS-1/TS-2, supporting crystallization rather than amorphization. The remaining uncertainty is the actual crystallographic cell size, but no evidence indicates >40 atoms.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InSeCl is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.021 eV/atom, and several TS analogues with comparable hull energies have been synthesized (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The strongest support comes from CS-2: GaTeCl is a very close III–chalcogen–chloride sheet analogue synthesized directly from MCl₃ + M₂Ch₃ at 400 °C. InTeBr provides a second indium halide-chalcogenide analogue accessible by melt/Bridgman growth (CS-4/SS-4). The most important enabling factor is the reactive, low-temperature metal-halide/chalcogenide route, which should avoid the very slow diffusion problems seen in some SS analogues. The main limiting factor is chloride volatility/moisture sensitivity and possible competition with binary In–Se or In–Cl phases.
