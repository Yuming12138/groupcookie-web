### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for a ground-state candidate and justifies a high score. CS contains stable layered binary analogues such as TiSe₂ and YI₃ with E_hull = 0 (CS-2, CS-5), while TS contains several stable selenide analogues with E_hull = 0 (TS-1 to TS-5). SS gives a mixed signal because the closest P4/nmm TmSe₂/LuSe₂ analogues are metastable at 0.084–0.118 eV/atom (SS-2, SS-4), but they are still experimentally synthesized, so this does not strongly penalize the target.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct sealed-ampoule route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, consistent with CS-5, SS-3 / TS-2, and TS-5, but requires vacuum/sealed ampoules due to volatile Se. Multi-step annealing/regrinding is likely because long anneals and reanneals are reported in CS-5 and SS-1 / TS-1. CS gives useful support for sealed elemental chalcogenide synthesis; SS/TS confirm similar ampoule conditions; no source removes the need for careful atmosphere control.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by elemental Y diffusion and reconstruction from elements, which is inferred from refractory metal behavior and long solid-state anneals in CS-5, SS-1 / TS-1, and TS-5. Se vapor/liquid transport may help at the proposed temperatures **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with successful sealed-tube selenide syntheses in CS-5 and SS-3 / TS-2. Remaining uncertainty is whether ambient-pressure Y–Se equilibration reaches the P4/nmm phase rather than a competing binary.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The HPHT route is penalized primarily for pressure because the directly relevant P4/nmm rare-earth selenides in SS-2 and SS-4 require 14–70 kbar. Temperature can be kept below the harshest end of the reported 400–2000 °C range **[Reasonable Inference]**, but a high-pressure press, capsule loading, and quenching are unavoidable. CS and TS provide insufficient direct high-pressure evidence for Y–Se and are treated as neutral, 50, for the pressure-specific assessment.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetically, this route benefits from the closest structural match: TmSe₂ and LuSe₂ in SS-2 and SS-4 share the P4/nmm 9-coordinate rare-earth selenide framework. HPHT should enhance reaction contact and enable dense phase formation **[Reasonable Inference]**, but SS-2 reports multiple phases and reaction-window mapping, so phase selection is not trivial. The route is therefore kinetically credible but operationally difficult.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperatures, similar to TS-4 at 700 °C and SS-3 / TS-2 at 600 °C, and avoids high pressure. It still requires evacuated ampoules, volatile Se or I₂ containment, flux drying, slow cooling, and inert handling, all supported by TS-4 and SS-3 / TS-2. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for halide-flux Y–Se synthesis and is neutral, 50, for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux or iodine-assisted transport improves mass transfer relative to dry powder reaction, supported by TS-4 for rare-earth chalcogenide platelets and SS-3 / TS-2 for iodine-assisted selenide crystal growth. This is the main reason Route 3 has the best kinetic score. The uncertainty is that the most structurally exact analogues in SS-2 / SS-4 were made by HPHT, not flux, so flux may yield a different Y–Se phase.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple binary cation sublattice, no large reported unit cell, and only edge/corner-sharing connectivity, so none of the three amorphization triggers is activated. SS-2 and SS-4 support crystallization of very similar P4/nmm rare-earth selenide frameworks. CS is insufficient for this specific amorphization factor and is neutral, 50; TS gives indirect support from crystalline layered selenides but also notes phase-control issues in TS-4 and TS-5.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YSe₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is strongest structurally from SS-2 and SS-4, where P4/nmm rare-earth selenide analogues with 9-coordinate rare-earth sites were synthesized, but those analogues required high pressure. CS and TS support the broader feasibility of sealed-tube and flux synthesis of layered selenides, especially from elements in evacuated ampoules. The most important enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability plus close P4/nmm rare-earth selenide structural precedent. The most important limiting factor is phase selection: ambient-pressure routes may form competing Y–Se binaries, while the most structurally transferable route requires specialized HPHT equipment.
