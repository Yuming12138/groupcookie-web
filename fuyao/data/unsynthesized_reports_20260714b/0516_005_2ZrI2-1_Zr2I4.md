### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability category. The closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue is the same ZrI₂ family with E_hull ≈ 0 and E_above_ground_state = 0.0 [CS1, SS1, TS1]. This strongly supports ground-state synthesizability, although DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on finite-temperature entropy effects, so that aspect is neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Chemical Vapor Transport

The severity is high because the directly reported ZrI₂ route requires sealed vacuum Ta tubes, a 750/850 °C gradient, 4 weeks, and drybox-type handling [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Kinetically, the route is favorable relative to ordinary solid-state diffusion because ZrI₄ enables vapor transport, but the long growth time shows slow nucleation/crystal growth [CS1]. This is the most reliable route because it is directly demonstrated for an essentially identical layered monoclinic ZrI₂ phase [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty concerns whether the target one-sheet setting corresponds exactly to the reported two-sheet polymorph or a closely related polytype [CS1 vs target description].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed Solid-State Comproportionation

The same ZrI₄ + Zr redox chemistry is supported by the exact ZrI₂ transport synthesis [CS1], and sealed high-temperature Ta reactions are common for related iodides [CS4, SS4, TS4]. However, without a gradient, phase separation and crystal growth may be less controlled than in CVT [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. Kinetic limitations arise from dense Zr metal and possible iodine-rich competing phases such as ZrI₃ [CS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates gradient-free ZrI₂ synthesis, so this route is less certain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Direct Elemental Iodination

Elemental iodination is supported for YI₃, Nb iodides, and LaI₃/LaI₂ precursor chemistry [CS2, CS3, CS5]. For ZrI₂, however, direct use of I₂ introduces more difficult stoichiometry and oxidation-state control than using preformed ZrI₄ + Zr [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. The staged heating is inferred from YI₃ ampoule synthesis and LaI₃/LaI₂ preparation [CS3, CS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows direct elemental synthesis of the target ZrI₂ polymorph, so the kinetics and phase purity are uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-Pressure Conversion

High pressure is demonstrated for structurally/thermodynamically similar NdI₂ at 20–40 kbar and 400–500 °C [SS3, TS3]. The raw F2 score is favorable only because the rubric penalizes pressures above 5 GPa, while the reference uses 2–4 GPa [SS3, TS3]. Expert judgment downgrades its practical attractiveness because exact ZrI₂ synthesis does not require pressure [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any pressure-stabilized ZrI₂ polymorph is needed for the target.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a simple binary layered compound with one cation and no reported mixed polyhedral connectivity [target structure; CS1, SS1, TS1]. The closest ZrI₂ analogue is crystalline and low-complexity, with Zr₄I₈ = 12 atoms in the reported cell, below the >40 atom amorphization trigger [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Thus amorphization risk is low. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS addresses actual melt-quench or thin-film amorphization behavior, so that empirical aspect is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target ZrI₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest ZrI₂ analogue has essentially zero hull energy [target; CS1, SS1, TS1]. The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually strong because the top analogue in all three categories is essentially the same ZrI₂ chemistry and layered monoclinic structure [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the ZrI₄/Zr chemical transport comproportionation chemistry, which provides both redox control and mass transport at 750–850 °C [CS1]. The most critical limiting factor is experimental handling: volatile/air-sensitive iodides, sealed Ta ampoules, long temperature-gradient growth, and possible competing zirconium iodides [CS1; CS3–CS5]. Overall, the compound is quite synthesizable in a well-equipped solid-state halide laboratory, but not straightforward in a standard oxide-synthesis setup.
