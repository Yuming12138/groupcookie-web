### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom gives a very high thermodynamic score by the required rubric. This is reinforced by multiple stable alkali/alkaline-earth or alkali/Mg phosphide analogues, including NaBaP, LiBaP, BaCuP, KMgP, and K–Na–M–P compounds at E_hull = 0 (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee an easily accessible kinetic pathway, as phase mixtures or byproducts are noted in flux and some alkali phosphide syntheses (CS-2, CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids external high pressure and can plausibly use the 650–800 °C regime based on CsMnP synthesis at 675–875 K (CS-5), though KMgP provides a higher-temperature precedent including 1150 °C and 800 °C annealing (SS-1/TS-1). The major severity penalties are sealed containment of volatile Cs/P and air-sensitive/toxic precursors, which are implied by Ar sealed bomb/ampoule practice in CsMnP and KMgP (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). The uncertainty is whether Mg incorporation in CsMgP will require the higher KMgP-style temperature, which would reduce the F2 score if >1000 °C becomes necessary (SS-1/TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The exact CsMnP structural analogue and Mg-bearing KMgP analogue strongly reduce the expected structural reconstruction barrier because the target combines Cs-layer topology from CsMnP and MgP₄ tetrahedra from KMgP (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). Cs and Mg are not refractory cations, and Cs is liquid during reaction [Reasonable Inference], so cation diffusion should be manageable. However, the long 5–8 day or several-day anneals in the analogues imply slow ordering or diffusion through solid phosphide products (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The binary precursor route likely operates at moderate temperatures near 550–700 °C, consistent with binary precursor syntheses at 870–950 K in CS-4 and SS-2/TS-2. It receives heavier penalties because evacuated/sealed conditions, prior Cs phosphide synthesis, and highly moisture-sensitive phosphides add operational complexity [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed handling in CS-4 and SS-2/TS-2]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct CsMgP formation from Cs–P/Mg–P binaries, so this route is less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Binary phosphides should reduce P volatilization and provide reactive M–P building blocks [Reasonable Inference], and related ternary pnictides can be formed from KP/InP-type precursors (SS-2/TS-2). Diffusion is still required to reorganize into the P4/nmm ternary lattice, and the route lacks the direct structural analogue support that Route 1 has from CsMnP/KMgP (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). Remaining uncertainty is significant because Cs phosphide stoichiometry and phase purity may be difficult to control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures, with LiBaP precedent at 900 °C for 120 h (CS-2). The severity comes from Nb tube welding, sealed vacuum/silica jacket handling, air-sensitive alkali reagents, and post-growth flux removal, all of which are described or implied in the CS-2 flux protocol. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for flux growth of the exact P4/nmm CsMgP phase, so this route is scored as operationally more complex.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

The molten flux should greatly improve mass transport and crystallization kinetics [Reasonable Inference], and CS-2 demonstrates single-crystal formation of LiBaP in Na/Sn or Li₃N fluxes. However, CS-2 also indicates that target compounds were observed during attempts to prepare other phases and that phase mixtures can occur, so phase selectivity and composition control are uncertain. For CsMgP, Mg solubility and Cs volatility in Sn/Cs flux are additional uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes, Cs and Mg, triggering the radius-mismatch check; this is also evident from the long Cs–P vs shorter Mg–P distances in the target and analogues (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). The unit cell is expected to be small for a P4/nmm 111-type layered phase, and analogous CsMnP/KMgP structures are simple layered materials rather than large-cell frameworks (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). The target has corner/edge-sharing MgP₄ tetrahedra but no stated face-sharing coexistence, so the polyhedral-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsMgP is likely a ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related alkali/alkaline-earth phosphides are also reported at or near hull (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The synthesis is well supported by the exact layered CsMnP structural analogue and the Mg-containing KMgP analogue, both prepared by sealed solid-state methods (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match: CsMgP essentially combines the Cs-layered P4/nmm motif of CsMnP with the MgP₄ tetrahedral network of KMgP (CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is handling and stoichiometry control of volatile/reactive Cs and P in sealed systems, as reflected by the sealed ampoule/bomb requirements across the related syntheses (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1/TS-1). Overall, direct sealed solid-state synthesis is the preferred route, with flux growth mainly reserved for single-crystal optimization.
