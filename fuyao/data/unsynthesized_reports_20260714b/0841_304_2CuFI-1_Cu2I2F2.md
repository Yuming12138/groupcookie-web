### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0240 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic score bracket and suggesting it is either stable or only weakly metastable. Thermodynamic analogues CuHgSCl, CuHgSBr, AgCuS, and CuHgSI all have E_hull ≈0.021–0.031 eV/atom and are experimentally accessible [TS1–TS4]. Structurally similar phases also include stable or near-stable examples such as CuBr₂ at E_hull = 0 and Hg₂OI at E_hull = 0 [SS2, SS5]. CS evidence is mixed because chemically similar oxyhalides include high-hull CdOCl/CdOI entries [CS1, CS3], so CS is not heavily weighted for F1 and is treated near neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, comparable to CuHgSI at 553 K and CuHgSCl at 573 K [TS4, SS1/TS1], so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The main severity penalties are the evacuated sealed tube, volatile iodine/halide control, and hazardous fluoride/iodine precursors [TS4, SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Equipment is not exotic, since analogues use evacuated Pyrex tubes [TS4, SS1/TS1], but fluoride compatibility may require a liner [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether CuIF can form without solution-mediated ion mobility.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary halide precursors should be moderately reactive, consistent with CuI/CuX use in sealed-tube analogues [TS4, SS1/TS1]. Kinetics benefit from low-temperature halide transport, since analogues form far below Cu’s melting point [TS4, SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. However, the target’s 2D CuIF sheet likely requires reconstruction from binary halides, and 2-week analogue reaction times imply slow ordering [TS4, SS1/TS1]. Thus kinetics are acceptable but not optimal.

#### F2 — Route 2

Hydrothermal synthesis is moderate in condition severity: CuOCl forms at 250 °C for 72 h [CS5], and Hg₂OI forms at 450 K hydrothermally [SS5]. The route requires sealed autoclaves and hazardous HF/HI chemistry, but not >5 GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperature [CS5, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. The process has several steps, similar to hydrothermal CuHgSBr growth involving ampoule sealing, reaction, cooling, washing, and crystal selection [SS3/TS2]. The main safety/severity uncertainty comes from fluoride acid handling rather than temperature.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution-mediated synthesis gives high kinetic accessibility because hydrothermal CuOCl and Hg₂OI single crystals form at low-to-moderate temperatures [CS5, SS5]. Concentrated HX media have been shown to transport halides effectively and yield high-quality CuHgSBr crystals [SS3/TS2]. Direct assembly of a 2D sheet is plausible because CuOCl is itself a layered Cu oxyhalide formed hydrothermally [CS5]. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates an anhydrous Cu–F–I binary sheet.

#### F2 — Route 3

Cooling crystallization is mild in temperature and pressure, supported by Ag₂OIF-related crystallization at about 323 K [SS4] and CuBr₂ slow evaporation at room temperature [SS2]. Penalties remain for HF/iodine hazards, potential sealed handling of volatile acids/iodine, and multiple solution-processing steps [SS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS do not provide direct evidence for this route, so their route-specific signal is neutral, source score 50. Severity is therefore moderate despite low temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Precursor mobility is excellent in solution, and AgF/AgI cooling crystallization demonstrates that mixed fluoride/iodide solution chemistry can produce crystalline materials [SS4]. CuBr₂ evaporation further supports the ability of simple Cu halides to crystallize under mild conditions [SS2]. However, structural reconstruction/nucleation confidence is low because SS4 involves Ag and oxygen/hydration, not an anhydrous CuIF phase. The route is kinetically attractive but phase-selectivity-limited.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation type, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Target structure]. The provided structure description indicates a relatively simple 2D CuIF sheet with one Cu, I, and F site, and no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell is given [Target structure]. Mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence is not specified for the target, unlike some analogues that explicitly mention edge/corner sharing [SS1, SS3], so the target is scored as low amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is that the full crystallographic cell size is not explicitly provided.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuIF is likely a **near-ground-state or weakly metastable** phase because its energy above hull is only 0.0240 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from thermodynamic analogues with similar low hull energies that have been synthesized by sealed-tube or hydrothermal methods [TS1–TS4], and from layered Cu oxyhalide hydrothermal chemistry [CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility of halide-rich sealed or solution media, which supports crystallization without high-temperature decomposition [CS5, SS3/TS2, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is Cu–iodide redox chemistry: Cu(II) in the presence of I⁻ can favor CuI and I₂ rather than a mixed Cu–F–I phase [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with hydrothermal/solvothermal crystallization offering the best balance of kinetic accessibility and phase control.
