### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the accessible/stable boundary and justifying a high score of 90. Stable P4/mbm rare-earth tetraborides such as TbB₄, HoB₄, LuB₄, PrB₄, and LaB₄ are reported with E_hull = 0 in the CS/SS data [CS1–CS5, SS3, SS5]. TS gives a mixed signal: similar-energy borides/borocarbides can be synthesized [TS1–TS5], but some require high pressure or show phase impurities [TS1, TS2], so the target should be considered slightly metastable rather than unequivocally ground-state.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Al flux growth

F2 is limited by the high temperature of ~1550 °C, multi-step growth/cooling, and air-sensitive rare-earth handling [CS2, CS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is required, and Ar is a standard inert atmosphere rather than vacuum or reactive gas [CS2, CS3, SS5]. F3 is favorable because molten Al provides liquid-phase transport and because the reported rare-earth tetraboride flux series explicitly includes ErB₄ [CS2, CS3, SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is mainly Al incorporation or flux-removal chemistry, which is not detailed in the provided sources.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Solid-state sintering

F2 is moderate-to-challenging because analogues require ~1400 °C and long annealing under Ar [CS1, CS4, SS3]. F3 is lower because direct Er+B reaction must overcome slow diffusion in refractory boron and possible formation of competing Er borides [CS1, CS4, TS2]. The structural match is good because HoB₄/LuB₄/TbB₄ share the tetraboride family [CS1, CS2, CS4, SS3, SS5], but the 100 h anneal in analogous solid-state syntheses indicates slow kinetics [CS1, CS4, SS3]. Uncertainty remains over whether pure ErB₄ or mixtures with ErB₂/ErB₆ would dominate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Chloride flux

F2 is moderate because the temperature is lower than Al flux, but sealed Ta capsule work, hygroscopic ErCl₃, and flux removal add practical complexity [SS2; Reasonable Inference]. F3 is reasonably favorable because Gd₂B₅ forms from a rare-earth chloride flux at ~1077 °C over 14 d [SS2]. However, structural transferability is weaker because SS2 is Gd₂B₅ rather than P4/mbm ErB₄. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for chloride-flux ErB₄ specifically and are therefore neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Oxide + B vacuum route

F2 is challenging because TS reports high vacuum and temperatures up to 2100 °C for rare-earth borides from M₂O₃ + B [TS2]. F3 is limited by the refractory/stable nature of Er₂O₃ and B and by oxygen-removal requirements [TS2]. TS also warns that phase control in Ho/Er borides can be difficult, including HoB₆ not being free of tetraboride and ErB₆ not being prepared cleanly [TS2]. CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for oxide-borothermic ErB₄ and are neutral for this specific route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

F4 is high because ErB₄ is a crystalline, chemically simple binary with only one cation and a known ordered rare-earth tetraboride structure type [CS2, CS4, SS3, SS5]. The conventional analogue cells are M₄B₁₆, i.e., 20 atoms, below the >40 atom amorphization flag [CS1–CS5, SS3, SS5]. The boron network is complex but well represented in stable rare-earth tetraborides, so it is not by itself a strong amorphization risk [CS1–CS5, SS3, SS5]. TS gives insufficient direct evidence on ErB₄ amorphization and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ErB₄ is likely a **near-ground-state or weakly metastable** phase because its E above hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and many chemically identical rare-earth tetraboride analogues are hull-stable [CS1–CS5, SS3, SS5]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS/SS Al-flux data, which explicitly includes ErB₄ among rare-earth tetraborides grown from molten aluminium flux [CS2, CS3, SS5]. The key enabling factor is liquid-flux transport, which overcomes slow B diffusion and allows crystallization of the P4/mbm tetraboride network [CS2, CS3, SS5]. The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control among possible Er–B binaries and avoidance of Al inclusions or residual flux [TS2; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, ErB₄ should be synthesizable with good probability, especially by Al flux growth.
