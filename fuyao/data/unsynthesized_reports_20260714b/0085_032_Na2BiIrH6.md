### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score cutoff given in the rubric, so it receives 90/100. TS provides useful support because several thermodynamically similar hydrides with E_hull values around 0.054–0.084 eV/atom are reported as synthesized phases, including NaBaPdH3, Mg2RuH4, Sr2PdH4, and Eu2PdH4 (TS1–TS4). CS also supports hydride viability because NaBaIrH6 and NaCaIrH6 are ground-state Ir hydrides with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, CS2). The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source contains Bi, so the target’s Bi–H/H–H motif remains unvalidated by direct analogue evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the mildest pressure class among the plausible routes, directly supported by 5 MPa H2/D2 Ir hydride synthesis in CS1/CS2/SS5. It is penalized for controlled H2 atmosphere, air-sensitive hydrides, stepwise processing, and likely need for sealed containment of Na/Bi **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for how Bi behaves at 400–510 °C in this hydride chemistry, so the Bi-related severity is uncertain. Overall, conditions are specialized but not extreme relative to high-pressure hydride synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Ir powder, although CS1/CS2 demonstrate that Ir–H frameworks can form at 400–510 °C. The target is lower-symmetry than cubic NaBaIrH6/NaCaIrH6 (CS1/CS2/SS5), implying more demanding cation/hydride ordering. SS/CS analogues indicate multiple attempts and phase optimization, supporting a moderate-to-low kinetics score. Bi precursor reactivity is not covered by CS/SS/TS, so this factor is scored conservatively.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses 50–150 bar H2, supported by TS1, SS2, and CS5. The temperature remains moderate, but high-pressure H2, air-sensitive precursors, multi-step handling, and volatile/segregating Na/Bi concerns **[Reasonable Inference]** make the route challenging. TS1 explicitly notes optimization and air/moisture sensitivity, supporting the severity penalty. There is insufficient Bi-specific evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

The higher hydrogen pressure should increase hydrogen chemical potential and improve hydride formation relative to Route 1, as seen for TS1/SS2/CS5. However, long anneals of 48 h to several days are commonly reported for these analogues (TS1, SS2), indicating slow kinetics. Ir diffusion and Bi incorporation remain limiting **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thus Route 2 slightly improves kinetics over Route 1 but does not remove the main ordering problem.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits the high-pressure H2/D2 conditions of CS5, where 100–150 bar and 500–530 °C were used with LiH/LiD flux. It is penalized for controlled H2 atmosphere, air-sensitive hydrides, multiple steps, and Bi/Na containment issues. Flux introduces additional handling and impurity risks, also consistent with impurity issues noted for flux-derived hydrides in CS5. No CS/SS/TS source confirms LiH flux compatibility with Bi hydrides.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux improves the kinetic score because CS5 demonstrates LiH/LiD-assisted formation of a complex 5d hydride at 500–530 °C. This route may better overcome Ir diffusion and low-symmetry ordering barriers **[Reasonable Inference]**. The penalty remains for possible Li-containing impurities and lack of direct Bi precedent, both of which could complicate phase isolation. Among practical routes, it gives the best balance of hydrogen chemical potential and mass transport.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is supported by SS3/SS4, where Na2PdH4 and K2PtH6 form at 1500–2500 bar H2/D2 and about 770–775 K. The rubric only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, so the formal F2 score is not extremely low, but chemically and practically this is a severe high-pressure hydrogen route. It is penalized for controlled H2, air-sensitive hydride precursors, and sealed-system volatility concerns. The major uncertainty is that CS Ir analogues form at lower pressure, so the benefit of kbar H2 for Na2IrBiH6 is unproven.

#### F3 — Route 4

Very high H2 pressure should favor hydride formation and may stabilize metastable hydride motifs **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS3/SS4 support direct synthesis of structurally related alkali noble-metal hydrides, but those systems do not contain Ir or Bi. The target’s P1 symmetry and mixed Na/Ir/Bi/H/H2 bonding make complete ordering more difficult than Na2PdH4 or K2PtH6. Kinetics are therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has strongly dissimilar cations and mixed bonding environments, which triggers a cation-size/bonding mismatch risk **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its formula unit is small, so the >40 atom-count trigger is not applied, although exact P1 cell multiplicity is not fully specified. Mixed polyhedral/connectivity risk is supported by the target’s multiple Na, Ir, Bi, and H coordination environments and by the prevalence of mixed face/edge/corner-sharing hydride polyhedra in CS3, SS1/SS2, and TS1–TS3. The resulting F4 score is medium because long-range ordering is plausible but not guaranteed.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2IrBiH6 is likely a borderline stable or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the common metastability threshold, while TS examples show related hydrides near 0.05–0.08 eV/atom can be synthesized (TS1–TS4). The CS and SS data strongly support formation of Na–Ir–H and Na–late-transition-metal hydride frameworks, especially through NaBaIrH6/NaCaIrH6 and Na2PtH4 analogues (CS1–CS3, SS1, SS5). The most important enabling factor is high hydrogen chemical potential under controlled H2/D2 pressure, which is common to successful analogues (CS1/CS2/CS5/SS2/TS1). The main limiting factor is the absence of direct Bi-containing precedent in CS/SS/TS, especially given the target’s unusual Bi–H and H–H bonding. A LiH/LiD-assisted high-pressure hydride flux route appears most practical because it combines enhanced hydrogen activity and improved mass transport, although phase purity will be difficult (CS5).
