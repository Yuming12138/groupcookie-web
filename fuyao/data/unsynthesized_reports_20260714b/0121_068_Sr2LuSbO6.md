### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high end of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” category and therefore justifying a high score of 90 [Target]. Thermodynamically similar Sr₂YSbO₆ and Ca₂LaSbO₆ have E above hull values near 0.05 eV/atom and are reported as synthesizable by solid-state routes [TS1, TS2]. Chemically and structurally close Sr₂DySbO₆, Sr₂HoSbO₆, Sr₂SmSbO₆, and Sr₂LaSbO₆ are on-hull in the provided data, which supports the stability of the Sr₂LnSbO₆ family [CS1–CS4, SS1, SS3, SS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target structure is triclinic P-1 while many analogues are monoclinic P2₁/c, so the exact distortion may be metastable relative to another polymorph [Target, CS1–CS4].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 1400 °C in air for 48 h, which is high-temperature but standard ceramic processing for Dy/Ho analogues [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS3]. The route does not require high pressure or controlled atmosphere, and the equipment is limited to agate mortar and Al₂O₃ crucibles [CS1, CS2]. Deductions come from T >1000 °C, repeated grinding/firing, and antimony oxide toxicity [CS1, CS2; toxicity Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Lu/Sb₂O₅ route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 severity except for general ambient-pressure solid-state comparability [TS1, TS2].

#### F3 — Route 1

Reaction kinetics are moderate because SrCO₃ and Lu₂O₃ are not high-reactivity precursors, and Lu₂O₃ is expected to be refractory [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The strongest kinetic support is that Dy/Ho analogues form at 1400 °C in 48 h with several intermediate grindings [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS3]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target belongs to the same corner-sharing ordered double-perovskite network as Sr₂DySbO₆/Sr₂HoSbO₆ [Target, CS1, CS2, SS1, SS3]. Remaining uncertainty is whether smaller Lu³⁺ increases octahedral tilting enough to slow ordering or favor a competing polymorph [Reasonable Inference from ionic-size trend; SS analogues support tolerance of tilted frameworks].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because the staged Sm/La route reaches 1770 K, approximately 1497 °C, and involves many separate 24 h heat treatments [CS3, CS4, SS4, TS2]. It remains ambient-pressure and air-based, so it avoids the major penalties associated with high pressure or reactive atmospheres [CS3, CS4, TS2]. Its condition score is reduced by high maximum temperature, multi-step processing, and antimony oxide toxicity [CS3, CS4, TS2; toxicity Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Lu-specific behavior in this staged program, so SS is supportive structurally but neutral for exact Lu thermal severity [SS4].

#### F3 — Route 2

The staged route improves diffusion and homogenization by repeated heating and regrinding, as used for Sr₂SmSbO₆/Sr₂LaSbO₆ and Ca/Sr analogues [CS3, CS4, TS2]. However, the very need for multiple firings indicates slow solid-state kinetics and phase-purity challenges [CS3, CS4, TS2]. A small Sm₂O₃ impurity was reported for Sr₂SmSbO₆, suggesting that complete reaction of rare-earth oxide can be difficult [CS3, SS4]. For Lu₂O₃, this risk may be equal or greater because Lu₂O₃ is refractory [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 can use lower nominal temperatures around 1090–1275 °C for Y analogues, but it often requires very long sintering or may require temperatures up to 1500 °C in the broad Sr₂BᴵᴵᴵSbO₆ series [SS2, SS5, TS1, CS5]. It uses air and ambient pressure, which is favorable [SS2, SS5, TS1, CS5]. Its score is reduced by long multi-step processing, possible Sb₂O₃ volatility, and precursor risks such as optional SrO air sensitivity and Sb₂O₃ toxicity [SS5, TS1; volatility/air sensitivity Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a sealed system is required, but the volatility risk is chemically plausible [CS5, SS2, SS5].

#### F3 — Route 3

The Sb₂O₃/SrO route may have somewhat higher precursor activity than SrCO₃/Sb₂O₅ routes, as SrO and Sb₂O₃ can react readily in ceramic mixtures [SS5, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. However, Sr₂YSbO₆ required 135 h sintering, showing slow ordering or diffusion even for a close small-trivalent-cation analogue [SS5, TS1]. The broad Sr₂BᴵᴵᴵSbO₆ ceramic family including Lu supports feasibility, but conditions vary widely from 1000–1500 °C and 2–10 days, implying nontrivial optimization [CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is Sb oxidation-state control because Sb₂O₃ must oxidize to Sb⁵⁺ in the final Sr₂LuSbO₆ composition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large A-site/B-site cation size contrast, which triggers C1, but this is normal for perovskites and is supported by many crystalline Sr₂LnSbO₆ analogues [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS4]. The target octahedra are corner-sharing rather than mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, which lowers amorphization risk [Target, CS1–CS4]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂LuSbO₆ is likely near the ground-state/metastability boundary rather than deeply metastable, because its E above hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from chemically and structurally close Sr₂DySbO₆, Sr₂HoSbO₆, Sr₂SmSbO₆, Sr₂LaSbO₆, and Sr₂YSbO₆ analogues, all of which are reported as ceramic/solid-state products in air [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5]. The most important enabling factor is the robust Sr₂LnSbO₆ ordered double-perovskite framework, which tolerates a range of Ln³⁺ sizes and octahedral tilts [CS1–CS4, SS1–SS4]. The most important limiting factor is solid-state kinetic sluggishness from refractory Lu₂O₃ and the need for B-site ordering between Lu³⁺ and Sb⁵⁺ [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limiting factor is possible antimony oxide volatility or oxidation-state drift at high temperature, especially for Sb₂O₃-based routes [SS2, SS5, TS1; Reasonable Inference].
