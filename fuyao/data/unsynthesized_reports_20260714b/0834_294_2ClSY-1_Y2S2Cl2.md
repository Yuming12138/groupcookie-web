### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

YSCl has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores at the top of the provided stability rubric. This is reinforced by DySBr, HoSI, and DySI analogues, all of which are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and closely related rare-earth sulfide-halide chemistry (CS5 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2; SS4 / TS4). The remaining uncertainty is experimental: DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee rapid formation or resistance to hydrolysis, and Y–Cl–S phase competition is not directly reported for YSCl.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, because the direct DySBr analogue used 750 °C and a standard tube furnace in a sealed evacuated ampoule (CS5 / SS1 / TS1). The score is reduced because vacuum sealing, volatile halogen management, and toxic Cl₂ handling are required; Br₂ condensation at −70 °C in the analogue signals that halogen volatility is a real control issue (CS5 / SS1 / TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Cl₂ reacts too aggressively with silica or overchlorinates Y to YCl₃-rich side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is favored because it closely matches the proven DySBr reaction and the target has the same Pmmn MSX framework type (SS1 / TS1). The use of Y metal and Y₂S₃ imposes diffusion limitations, but halogen-assisted transport should enhance reaction rates compared with purely refractory oxide routes [Reasonable Inference based on successful 750 °C DySBr synthesis, CS5 / SS1 / TS1]. The need for 5 d annealing indicates moderate rather than fast kinetics (CS5 / SS1 / TS1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures comparable to Y₂NCl₃ and HoSI analogues, but it requires sealed inert/vacuum conditions, air-sensitive YCl₃/Y metal, and likely long annealing (SS5 / TS5; SS2 / TS2). It is safer than Cl₂ in one sense but more procedurally demanding because anhydrous YCl₃ handling and possible precursor drying are critical (SS5 / TS5). The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports YSCl from YCl₃ + Y + S, so the CS signal for this exact route is insufficient and scored neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

The chloride salt may provide better local chloride mobility than Cl₂ dosing [Reasonable Inference], and YCl₃ is a proven precursor in yttrium chloride solid-state chemistry (SS5 / TS5). However, the target MSX framework must assemble from separate YCl₃, Y, and S reactants, so the structural reconstruction burden is higher than for the direct rare-earth sulfide/metal/halogen route. Long durations in analogues, including 10 d for HoSI and up to 16 d for Y₂NCl₃-related synthesis, support a moderate kinetic score rather than a high one (SS2 / TS2; SS5 / TS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT/recrystallization requires sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile transport agents, as iodine transport at around 1000 °C was used for GdPS/YPS single crystals (CS4). It is penalized because it is inherently multistep if preformed YSCl is needed, and transport agents such as I₂ or Cl₂ are volatile/corrosive (CS4; [Reasonable Inference]). The SS and TS sets do not provide direct CVT evidence for the Pmmn sulfide-halide analogues, so SS/TS are neutral, 50, for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT can yield good crystals in related rare-earth sulfide materials, as shown by iodine-transport growth of lath-shaped GdPS/YPS crystals around 1000 °C (CS4). However, composition control for YSCl may be difficult because transport chemistry can change halide chemical potential and possibly substitute I for Cl [Reasonable Inference]. Since the closest Pmmn MSX analogues were prepared by direct solid-state routes rather than CVT (SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2), Route 3 is best viewed as a crystal-growth/purification strategy after phase formation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger, and the structure is a relatively simple layered Pmmn MSX phase by comparison with DySBr and HoSI analogues (target; SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2). The target contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, but no face-sharing connectivity is reported, so the specified C3 trigger is not activated (target structure; SS1 / TS1). The uncertainty is small because the closest analogues are crystalline, phase-pure, and structurally simple (SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YSCl is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest rare-earth sulfide-halide analogues are also hull-stable (target; CS5 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2). The CS/SS/TS support is strong: DySBr is nearly isostructural to YSCl and was synthesized as a single-phase crystalline product by direct sealed-ampoule reaction (CS5 / SS1 / TS1). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a robust Pmmn layered MSX structure type across rare-earth sulfide halides, including DySBr, DySI, and HoSI (SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2; SS4 / TS4). The main limiting factor is not thermodynamics but experimental control of volatile/toxic chlorine and moisture-sensitive yttrium chloride/sulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by halogen/vacuum handling in CS5 / SS1 / TS1 and glovebox handling in SS5 / TS5]. Overall, YSCl should be experimentally accessible with conventional sealed-tube solid-state methods, with Route 1 the most direct and highest-confidence route.
