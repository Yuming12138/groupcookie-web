### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom directly places it at the highest stability tier. CS includes chemically similar chalcogenides with E_hull = 0 or near 0, SS includes low-hull Ag/Cu chalcogenide analogues, and TS includes multiple E_hull = 0 Au-containing phases. The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee that the specific Pbcm layered polymorph forms rapidly under laboratory kinetics.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule reaction

CS, SS, and TS all support sealed evacuated ampoules as standard for metal chalcogenides, so the condition severity is moderate rather than extreme. The main F2 penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, and Se toxicity. Kinetically, this route is slower because Au and Cu are dense metals, and SS/CS analogues often require long holds, slow cooling, or regrinding for full reaction.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary-selenide route

SS gives the strongest support for this route because AgCuS forms from Ag₂S + Cu₂S in evacuated ampoules. The binary-selenide route should improve kinetics relative to elements because Cu–Se bonding is pre-formed, but it adds steps and possible nonstoichiometry in Cu₂Se or Au–Se precursors. The uncertainty is that Au selenides are less robust and less commonly used than Ag₂S/Cu₂S analogues, so transferability is plausible but not proven.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux/self-flux route

TS and SS Au-chalcogenide flux examples support sealed-tube flux growth with controlled cooling, especially Au₄Tl₂S₃ and LiAuS. This route receives the best F3 because a Se-rich or chalcogenide flux can dissolve Au/Cu and lower diffusion barriers. Its limitation is stoichiometry: too much Se or reactive flux can stabilize competing Au–Se, Cu–Se, or flux-incorporated phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-pressure contingency route

CS provides the only direct support for high-pressure synthesis, with Ni₃Pb₂S₂ prepared at 40 kbar and 850 °C. Because the target is already on the hull and SS/TS analogues form at ambient sealed-tube pressure, high pressure is not the preferred route. It may help if competing phases persist, but the equipment burden and uncertain pressure dependence reduce its practical attractiveness.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a small ordered ternary chalcogenide sheet structure, and no CS/SS/TS analogue indicates glass formation as a dominant issue. One risk factor is the Au/Cu cation size and bonding mismatch, which can promote disorder if quenched too rapidly. Controlled cooling, as used in CS/SS/TS analogues, should mitigate amorphization or disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuCuSe is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS, SS, and TS datasets collectively support synthesis of related noble/coinage-metal chalcogenides by evacuated sealed-tube methods, with especially relevant evidence from SS Ag–Cu chalcogenides and TS Au chalcogenides. The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with accessible sealed-tube chalcogenide chemistry. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/stoichiometric control, especially Au dissolution and Se volatility. A Se-rich self-flux or mild chalcogenide flux is therefore the best first approach, followed by direct solid-state annealing as a simpler backup.
