### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the given target data. CS supports this because the closest CuBr₂ analogues are on or near the hull, with E_hull = 0 and 0.0084 eV/atom [CS1, CS2]. SS and TS also support feasibility because many structurally or thermodynamically similar halides lie on or close to the hull [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not specify the kinetic pathway or redox window needed for Cu₃Br₄ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 scores very high because CuBr₂ crystallizes by slow evaporation at room temperature with no special pressure or atmosphere reported [CS1, CS2, TS1]. The main operation is mild solution crystallization, and no high-temperature, high-pressure, or vacuum condition is required by the reference route [CS1, CS2]. The uncertainty is that Cu₃Br₄ requires Cu(I) stabilization, likely needing HBr/excess bromide or inert handling **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization provides high precursor activity and short ion-transport distances, as demonstrated by room-temperature CuBr₂ crystal growth [CS1, CS2, TS1]. Structural assembly is less certain because the target is not simply CuBr₂; it requires mixed-valence Cu ordering into a different Cu₃Br₄ sheet **[Reasonable Inference]**. CuBr₂ twinning issues [CS1, CS2] reduce the structural/crystal-quality component but do not prevent synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires vacuum sealing and long annealing, as in structurally similar CuHgSX materials made in evacuated Pyrex tubes at 553–573 K for 1–2 weeks [SS2, SS3]. Thermodynamic analogues also commonly use evacuated sealed ampoules [TS3, TS4, TS5]. The severity is moderate because temperatures are not extreme, but bromide volatility and sealed-tube pressure control are important **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Binary copper bromides are more reactive than refractory oxides, and halide analogues form at moderate temperatures in sealed tubes [SS2, SS3]. Diffusion is still slower than in solution, and SS/TS analogues often require one week to four months of annealing [SS2, SS3, TS3, TS4]. The target requires reconstructing binary CuBr/CuBr₂ into a distinct layered mixed-valence phase, so structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic term **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses sealed hydrothermal conditions, supported by CuHgSBr synthesis in concentrated HBr/HCl at 670 K [SS1] and by hydrothermal Ni₃S₄ synthesis at 120 °C [CS3]. It avoids vacuum and very high temperature, but it requires corrosive acid and a sealed pressure vessel **[Reasonable Inference]**. The score is therefore lower than open evaporation but better than CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Hydrothermal media give high ion mobility, and SS1 demonstrates that concentrated HBr/HCl can transport Cu and Br into crystalline halide-containing phases. Controlled cooling in SS1 gives crystals, suggesting solution-mediated growth can support ordered structures [SS1]. The uncertainty is whether the simpler binary Cu–Br system will select Cu₃Br₄ rather than CuBr or CuBr₂ under hydrothermal redox conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

CVT requires a sealed evacuated ampoule, volatile halide species, and a temperature gradient, matching the moderate-complexity procedure in TS2. TS2 also reports glovebox-like sensitivity and low yields for related halide CVT chemistry, increasing practical difficulty [TS2]. Therefore Route 4 is more severe than evaporation, hydrothermal synthesis, or simple sealed-tube annealing.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport can deliver material efficiently, and TS2 demonstrates bromide CVT crystal growth at about 360 °C for 5 days. However, TS2 reports low yields, unreacted components, and moisture/air sensitivity, indicating difficult phase control [TS2]. Cu₃Br₄ composition control is expected to be narrow because both Cu oxidation state and Br chemical potential must be maintained **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation element, Cu, so cation-size mismatch is not a likely amorphization driver **[Reasonable Inference]**. The described structure contains corner-sharing CuBr₄ tetrahedra and low-coordinate Br environments without reported edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met from the target description. CS/SS/TS do not directly discuss amorphization, so evidence from CS, SS, and TS is insufficient and treated as neutral source signal; the score is based on structural checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃Br₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CuBr₂ analogues that are chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close and crystallize under very mild slow-evaporation conditions [CS1, CS2, SS4, SS5, TS1]. The most important enabling factor is the softness and mobility of Cu–Br chemistry, which allows crystallization from solution or low-temperature sealed systems, as shown by CuBr₂ evaporation and halide sealed-tube analogues [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3]. The most important limiting factor is maintaining the mixed Cu(I)/Cu(II) stoichiometry needed for Cu₃Br₄ rather than forming CuBr or CuBr₂ **[Reasonable Inference]**. Aqueous/HBr slow evaporation is the best first attempt, while sealed-tube 2 CuBr + CuBr₂ annealing is the best backup if solution redox control fails **[Reasonable Inference]**.
