### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.0140 eV/atom places it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube methods, including AgCuS at 0.0306 eV/atom (SS2/TS1), TlCu₃Se₂ at 0.0199 eV/atom (SS5/TS2), and Tl₅Cu₁₄Se₁₀ at 0.0188 eV/atom (TS3). CS3 provides a direct Cu–Se stable compound, CuSe₂ with E_hull = 0.0, but it is Se-rich relative to Cu₂Se and therefore supports chemistry more than target phase identity. Remaining uncertainty is that 0.014 eV/atom may still correspond to a metastable polymorph rather than the ground-state Cu₂Se structure.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule direct reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the route requires vacuum sealing and volatile/toxic Se, which are explicitly part of Cu–Se ampoule synthesis in CS3 and related sealed-tube routes in SS2/TS1 and SS5/TS2. Temperatures are not severe because analogous Cu–Se and Ag–Cu–S reactions occur at 200–300 °C (CS3, SS2/TS1). Kinetics are limited by Cu metal diffusion and complete reconstruction from elements [Reasonable Inference], but chalcogen vapor/liquid transport likely accelerates reaction because similar phases form far below Cu’s melting point (CS3, SS2/TS1). The main uncertainty is whether low-temperature annealing yields the tetragonal 2D polymorph or a more common Cu₂₋ₓSe phase.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: hydrothermal / solvothermal

Condition severity is low because CS5 and SS1 demonstrate chalcogenide hydrothermal synthesis at 120–210 °C and 413 K, respectively, without high external pressure or high-temperature vacuum. The route still carries chemical hazard deductions because Se and reducing reagents are toxic/reactive [Reasonable Inference; Se use in CS5 and CS3]. Kinetics are favorable because soluble Cu and in situ selenide avoid long-range diffusion through dense Cu metal [Reasonable Inference], and CS5 specifically demonstrates 2D sheet-like selenide formation. Remaining uncertainty is high phase selectivity: TS provides insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Cu₂Se, so the TS contribution to this specific route is neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: mechanochemical alloying + annealing

This route has higher condition/process penalty because it combines inert milling, sealed annealing, volatile Se control, and multiple processing steps [Reasonable Inference; milling/annealing complexity supported by SS4/TS5]. Kinetically, milling should improve precursor activity and shorten diffusion lengths, consistent with mechanical alloying used to achieve high-purity Ag₃CuS₂ in SS4/TS5. However, structural ordering into the tetragonal 2D Cu₂Se phase likely still needs annealing and phase monitoring, as long anneals and phase-purity challenges are repeatedly reported in SS4/TS5 and SS5/TS2. The main uncertainty is whether milling promotes the desired crystalline phase or an amorphous/nanocrystalline Cu–Se mixture.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: thin-film co-deposition

The thin-film route has moderate severity due to vacuum processing and Se overpressure, but it avoids bulk diffusion and high pressure [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS, SS, and TS for vapor deposition of the target or analogues, so its evidence base is neutral relative to provided data. Kinetically, vapor deposition gives high precursor availability and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization and composition control are uncertain because Se is volatile and Cu₂Se stoichiometry is narrow [Reasonable Inference supported indirectly by phase/stoichiometry issues in CS3, SS2/TS1, SS5/TS2]. This route is best considered exploratory for thin films rather than primary bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Cu, so cation radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk [Target]. The described structure is simple, with one Cu₂Se sheet, 2-coordinate Cu, and 4-coordinate Se, rather than a large mixed-polyhedral framework [Target]. Some analogues have complex connectivity, such as Cu₉S₅ with multiple Cu sites and face/edge/corner sharing (CS1), but this is not structurally representative of the target. The main uncertainty is the exact unit-cell atom count, which is not explicitly provided by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state metastable polymorph rather than a strongly unstable phase, because its E_hull is only 0.0140 eV/atom [Target]. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through low-temperature chalcogenide sealed-tube and hydrothermal chemistry: direct Cu–Se reaction is demonstrated in CS3, hydrothermal selenide formation in CS5, and low-hull Cu/Ag/Tl chalcogenides in SS2/TS1 and SS5/TS2. The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of selenium under sealed or solution conditions, which allows chalcogenides to form at only 120–450 °C rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (CS3, CS5, SS1, SS2/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: related Cu/Ag/Tl chalcogenides often show impurities, homogeneity ranges, or phase mixtures (CS3, SS2/TS1, SS5/TS2, TS3). Low-temperature hydrothermal synthesis is therefore the best first attempt, while sealed-ampoule annealing is the most chemically direct backup.
