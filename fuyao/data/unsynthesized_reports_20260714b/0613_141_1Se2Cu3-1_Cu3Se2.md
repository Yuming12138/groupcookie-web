### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull of 0.036 eV/atom falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin, giving a high baseline score. TS is especially supportive because Cu3SbSe3 at 0.0346 eV/atom [TS1], TlCu4Se3 at 0.0293 eV/atom [TS2], AgCuS at 0.0306 eV/atom [TS3], and Cu8GeS6 at 0.0451 eV/atom [TS5] are all synthesized despite comparable metastability. CS and SS also support feasibility through stable CuSe [CS1/SS1] and CuSe2 [CS5], but they also imply possible competing binary Cu–Se phases. Remaining uncertainty is whether the specific P-6m2 2D Cu3Se2 polymorph is the experimentally accessible phase rather than another Cu3Se2 polymorph or a mixture **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because it uses ordinary evacuated silica/glass tube equipment as in CuSe [CS1/SS1] and CuSe2 [CS5], but it requires sealed-tube handling of volatile Se. CuSe synthesis uses a high but not >1000 °C temperature of 1170 K and long annealing [CS1/SS1], while CuSe2 can form at 200 °C but has phase/crystal-growth issues [CS5]. The main deductions are vacuum/sealed atmosphere, volatile Se, multi-step annealing, and Se hazard. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for elemental binary Cu3Se2 specifically, but TS supports similar evacuated ampoule processing for selenides [TS1, TS4].

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Cu + Se has a known reactivity mismatch: Se is reactive/volatile, while metallic Cu can be diffusion-limited **[Reasonable Inference]**. CuSe required slow heating, long high-temperature holds, repeated annealing, and a lower-temperature final anneal [CS1/SS1], which supports the moderate kinetics score. The target’s Cu–Se distances and low-coordinate motifs resemble CuSe’s trigonal-planar and tetrahedral Cu–Se environments [CS1/SS1], but the specific 2D sheet likely needs ordering not proven by CS/SS/TS. Thus kinetics are feasible but not fast.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has lower severity than Route 1 because it can operate at 350–500 °C, matching binary/solid-state chalcogenide syntheses [SS2, TS1, TS3]. Evacuated sealed ampoules are still required, as in Cu8SiSe6 [SS2], Cu3SbSe3 [TS1], and AgCuS [TS3]. The route avoids very high temperature and can avoid a multistep penalty if Cu2Se and CuSe are available. The remaining penalties are vacuum control, Se volatility, and Se-containing precursor hazard **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary selenide precursors should have better activity and shorter diffusion distances than elemental Cu + Se **[Reasonable Inference]**. This is supported by successful binary-precursor reactions such as Cu2Se + Sb2Se3 to Cu3SbSe3 [TS1] and Ag2S + Cu2S to AgCuS [TS3]. The stoichiometric identity Cu2Se + CuSe → Cu3Se2 is particularly attractive **[Reasonable Inference]**. The uncertainty is phase competition, because CuSe is stable [CS1/SS1] and CuSe2 is also stable [CS5], so the desired intermediate composition may require careful annealing.

#### F2 — Route 3

No CS/SS/TS source directly supports thin-film deposition, so this route is mostly a reasonable extrapolation. The severity is not high thermally because the proposed temperature is low, but high vacuum, Se volatility, and post-deposition annealing introduce penalties **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thin-film processing may enable metastable 2D phases, but this is not demonstrated by the provided analogues. Therefore the score is moderate-low despite mild temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Cu and Se can in principle be supplied by evaporation/sputtering **[Reasonable Inference]**, but Se vapor pressure makes composition control difficult. The crystallization pathway is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS example confirms direct thin-film crystallization of a related Cu–Se 2D phase. Composition control is likely narrow because deviations from Cu3Se2 can produce Cu2Se-, CuSe-, or CuSe2-like phases **[Reasonable Inference]**, with stable CuSe and CuSe2 explicitly represented in CS [CS1, CS5]. Thus Route 3 is viable mainly as a metastable-phase exploration route rather than the first choice.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its reported bonding is ordered low-coordinate Cu–Se with corner-sharing SeCu4 tetrahedra, not a highly connected mixed edge/face/vertex network. CS/SS analogues include more complex polyhedral sharing in some cases [SS2, SS5], but that complexity is not present in the target description. Remaining uncertainty is that a 2D metastable sheet may have stacking/disorder issues, but not necessarily amorphization **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu3Se2 with E_hull = 0.036 eV/atom is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a strongly unstable compound, because comparable thermodynamic analogues are experimentally synthesized [TS1–TS5]. The CS and SS data support the general Cu–Se chemistry strongly through successful sealed-ampoule syntheses of CuSe and CuSe2 [CS1/SS1, CS5], while TS supports the idea that phases ~0.03–0.05 eV/atom above hull can be isolated [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of chemically matched Cu–Se binary precursors, especially Cu2Se + CuSe, which exactly matches the Cu3Se2 stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]** and is consistent with binary chalcogenide precursor routes [SS2, TS1, TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with neighboring Cu–Se binaries, since stable CuSe [CS1/SS1] and CuSe2 [CS5] are documented. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, but phase-pure bulk isolation will likely require careful stoichiometry, sealed-ampoule control, and annealing optimization.
