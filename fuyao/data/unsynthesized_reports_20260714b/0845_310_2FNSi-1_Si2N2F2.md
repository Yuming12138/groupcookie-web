### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull, `E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom`, so the baseline thermodynamic score is very high [Target]. SS/TS analogues provide strong support because multiple Si–N/O tetrahedral frameworks are also ground-state or near-ground-state phases, including BaAl₂Si₃O₄N₄, BaSi₆N₈O, BaSi₂O₂N₂, and Ca₂Si₅N₈ [SS-2 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-4]. CS evidence is mixed: PNF₂ is stable on the hull [CS-1], but N₂F₂ and SnN₂F₂ are substantially above hull [CS-2, CS-3], so CS contributes moderate rather than decisive support.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for NH₃ atmosphere and toxic/volatile fluoride chemistry [CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The temperature is relatively mild because the closest ammonolysis analogue operates at 613 K and PNF₂ polymerization occurs at 350–360 °C [CS-3, CS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether silicon fluorides retain enough F while incorporating N into an ordered SiNF sheet [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Ammonolysis has favorable kinetics because gaseous NH₃ can react with fluorinated precursors at moderate temperature, as shown by the Sn–N–F analogue [CS-3]. The route avoids multi-cation diffusion, and the target has only Si as cation [Target]. The main kinetic risk is structural ordering into the 2D orthorhombic sheet rather than forming amorphous silicon nitride fluoride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids the very high temperatures used in SS/TS oxonitride analogues [SS-3 to SS-5, TS-2 to TS-4] and instead follows the lower-temperature sealed molecular precursor logic of PNF₂ [CS-1]. It is still penalized for sealed/vacuum handling, volatile fluorosilicon species, and toxic NH₃/HF-forming chemistry [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The route is less severe than high-temperature solid-state synthesis but more hazardous than ordinary oxide sintering [CS-1].

#### F3 — Route 2

This route has the best kinetic score because a molecular Si–N/F precursor should reduce the need for reconstructive diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 directly supports conversion of a molecular N/F precursor into an extended PNF₂ material at 350–360 °C, which is highly relevant chemically even though P replaces Si [CS-1]. The key uncertainty is whether the precursor condenses into the crystalline Pmn2₁ sheet rather than a glassy or polymeric Si–N–F phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits the harshness of solid-state Si nitride/oxonitride synthesis, where analogues require 1350–1630 °C under N₂ and often glovebox/radiofrequency furnace handling [SS-3 to SS-5, TS-2 to TS-4]. The proposed SiNF adaptation should lower the temperature to reduce F loss, but volatile fluorinating agents still require sealed or carefully controlled conditions [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore more conditionally severe than the molecular route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state kinetics are poor because α-Si₃N₄ and Si–N frameworks are refractory, and several analogues require long high-temperature treatments [SS-3 to SS-5, TS-2 to TS-4]. Si(NH)₂ can improve reactivity, as shown in BaSi₆N₈O and Ca₂Si₅N₈ syntheses [SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-4]. However, converting dense Si–N phases plus F into a layered SiNF sheet likely requires significant reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film deposition avoids high bulk synthesis temperatures [Reasonable Inference], but it requires plasma/vacuum processing and volatile/toxic gases [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for thin-film synthesis of these analogues, so CS/SS/TS are neutral, 50, for route validation. The route is conditionally feasible but experimentally uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor availability is reasonable because Si, N, and F can be supplied by standard thin-film sources [Reasonable Inference]. Crystallization and composition control are the weak points because no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct crystallization of SiNF films, and F-containing plasmas can etch or over-fluorinate Si–N networks [Reasonable Inference]. The route may be useful for kinetic trapping but not necessarily for bulk crystalline material [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, a small simple formula, and corner-sharing tetrahedra only, so none of the specified amorphization checks are triggered [Target]. SS/TS analogues demonstrate that ordered Si–N tetrahedral frameworks can crystallize, often as phase-pure products under optimized conditions [SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-4]. The remaining uncertainty is that Si–N–F molecular condensations may form amorphous polymeric products before crystallizing [CS-1; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SiNF is likely a true thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The structural evidence is strong: SS/TS analogues show stable Si–N tetrahedral networks with Si–N distances very close to the target’s 1.74 Å, and BaSi₂O₂N₂ contains directly analogous SiN₃O tetrahedra [SS-4, TS-3]. The chemical evidence is supportive but less direct: PNF₂ demonstrates N/F tetrahedral polymer chemistry under sealed moderate-temperature conditions [CS-1], while Sn–N–F ammonolysis demonstrates NH₃-based N/F formation [CS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is the local compatibility of SiN₃F tetrahedra with known Si–N framework chemistry [SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling fluorine volatility and avoiding amorphous silicon nitride fluoride or Si₃N₄/SiF₄ byproducts [CS-1, CS-3; Reasonable Inference].
