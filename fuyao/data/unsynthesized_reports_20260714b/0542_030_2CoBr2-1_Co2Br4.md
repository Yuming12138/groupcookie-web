### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0010 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin [Target data]. Thermodynamically similar bromide/halide references include several near-hull phases with E_hull near 0–0.016 eV/atom, showing that low-energy halide phases are synthetically accessible [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target’s specific Pmc2₁ tetrahedral polymorph may be nearly degenerate with other CoBr₂ polymorphs rather than uniquely stable [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The main reference FeBr₂ route uses moderate-temperature sublimation at 773 K under Ar, with no high pressure or complex equipment [CS1]. Related bromides can require sealed ampoules or Br₂ transport agents, which adds moderate hazard and atmosphere control if adopted [CS5, TS3]. Therefore the severity is moderate rather than easy, mainly due to moisture-sensitive/toxic bromide handling and possible sealed-system use [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport is kinetically favorable because FeBr₂ directly gives crystals by sublimation [CS1], and PdBr₂ gives crystals by CVT with Br₂ [CS5]. Using preformed CoBr₂ avoids long-range solid-state cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural: the target tetrahedral CoBr₄ sheet differs from FeBr₂ octahedral sheets and CuBr₂ square-planar/ribbon motifs [CS1–CS3].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct bromination routes require evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile Br₂, as shown by TlFeBr₄ and PdBr₂ references [SS2/TS2, CS5]. SS2/TS2 explicitly report ~200 bar pressure and pressure-compensated autoclave containment, indicating significant operational risk even though temperature is only 573 K [SS2/TS2]. Thus this route is synthetically plausible but condition-severity limited.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Bromination of metal is chemically active because Fe + Br₂ participates in the TlFeBr₄ synthesis [SS2/TS2]. However, forming crystalline CoBr₂ from elemental Co requires nucleation and reconstruction, and the phase selection may be complicated by local Br₂ pressure and temperature gradients [SS2/TS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. The kinetics are acceptable but less favorable than starting from preformed CoBr₂.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CuBr₂ crystallization by slow evaporation occurs at room temperature with basic equipment [CS2, CS3], and acid halide precipitation/recrystallization is also mild [SS3]. The route scores well on severity because it avoids high temperature and sealed Br₂ pressure [CS2, CS3]. The main penalties are corrosive HBr and cobalt salt toxicity [SS3; Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Solution growth has high precursor mobility, as demonstrated by immediate precipitation/recrystallization in acid halide systems [SS3] and slow-evaporation CuBr₂ crystal growth [CS2, CS3]. However, CS2/CS3 also report twinning in larger CuBr₂ crystals, implying possible crystal-quality issues. For CoBr₂ specifically, hydration/solvation competition makes formation of the anhydrous Pmc2₁ phase uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple binary compound with only one cation and corner-sharing tetrahedra, so the listed amorphization triggers are not activated [Target data]. Similar layered halides crystallize readily as single crystals by sublimation, evaporation, or CVT rather than forming amorphous products [CS1–CS3, CS5]. The only uncertainty is the exact unit-cell size for the Pmc2₁ model, but the formula and described sheet are not suggestive of a >40-atom complex framework [Target data; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target CoBr₂ phase is very likely thermodynamically accessible because its E_hull is only 0.0010 eV/atom [Target data]. It may nevertheless behave as a near-degenerate polymorph, because chemically close MBr₂ analogues FeBr₂ and CuBr₂ adopt different coordination motifs and symmetries [CS1–CS3]. The strongest synthesis support comes from FeBr₂ sublimation under Ar and PdBr₂ bromine-assisted CVT, which show that binary transition-metal bromides can be crystallized by vapor-phase methods [CS1, CS5]. The key enabling factor is the availability of a simple anhydrous binary precursor and vapor-transport chemistry [CS1, CS5]. The key limiting factor is polymorph control of the orthorhombic tetrahedral sheet versus more common octahedral or hydrated CoBr₂-related phases [CS1–CS3; Reasonable Inference].
