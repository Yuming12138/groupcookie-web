### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.009 eV/atom, which is well within the commonly synthesizable near-hull range; this directly gives a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues also cluster near the hull, including TiZrTe4 at 0.014 eV/atom (TS-1), PbHfS3 at 0.005 eV/atom (TS-2), and EuZrSe3 at 0.025 eV/atom (TS-5). Structurally similar ZrS2, ZrTe2, and HfTe2 are stable layered compounds (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3), supporting the physical plausibility of the octahedral sheet. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is a quaternary mixed-cation/mixed-anion ordered phase, and ordering entropies/competing binaries are not fully captured by E_hull alone [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses sealed silica/vacuum or dry Ar conditions analogous to direct ZrTe2 and TiZrTe4 synthesis (SS-2, TS-1), so it avoids high pressure but requires controlled atmosphere. Temperatures around 850–950 °C are moderate for chalcogenide solid-state synthesis and align with TS-1 and SS-2, but volatile S/Te require sealed tubes, as in CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, and TS-1. The main deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogens, multi-step regrinding/reheating, and handling of fine Hf/Zr/Te/S powders [Reasonable Inference]. CS data provide useful evidence for sealed-tube mixed chalcogenide synthesis but less direct structural transfer to this 1T target; structural evidence is stronger from SS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction from Hf and Zr metals is kinetically slow because both are refractory metals with stable oxide skins [Reasonable Inference], despite successful direct syntheses of Zr/Hf chalcogenides in SS-2 and mixed TiZrTe4 in TS-1. The repeated grinding and reheating used for TiZrTe4 (TS-1) and equilibrium cycling in Zr/Hf chalcogenides (SS-2) indicate that diffusion and homogenization are nontrivial. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target shares edge-sharing octahedral sheets with ZrS2, ZrTe2, and HfTe2 (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). Uncertainty remains in whether S/Te and Hf/Zr order into the predicted P3m1 arrangement rather than forming binary or disordered solid solutions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT uses no high pressure and moderate temperatures, consistent with ZrS2 iodine CVT at 900→700 °C (SS-1), HfTe2 iodine transport at 700 °C (SS-3), and TiS2-related iodine-assisted crystallization at 850–900 °C (TS-4). The severity score is lowered by evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile I2/S/Te, toxicity/corrosivity of iodine, and the multi-step prereaction plus transport procedure [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides strong evidence for this route because the closest structural analogues are grown by CVT; TS-4 also supports iodine-assisted crystallization of layered dichalcogenide motifs. CS provides only general sealed-chalcogenide support here, so CS is a weaker signal for CVT specifically.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT improves kinetics by moving material through volatile iodine-containing species, which is why it is used for ZrS2 and HfTe2 crystals (SS-1, SS-3). It also reduces dependence on bulk diffusion of refractory Hf/Zr, a major limitation in Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. The crystallization pathway is highly plausible because the target’s layered octahedral motif matches the structural family of CVT-grown ZrS2/HfTe2 (SS-1, SS-3). The major uncertainty is composition control: Hf, Zr, S, and Te may transport at different rates, potentially shifting the Hf:Zr or Te:S ratio [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses structurally matched binary dichalcogenide precursors, but still requires evacuated sealed silica and inert handling, consistent with SS-2 and TS-1. Temperatures can likely remain below 1000 °C because ZrS2, ZrTe2, and HfTe2 analogues form or transport between 700 and 900 °C (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). It is multi-step because the binary precursors must be prepared or verified, then mixed, annealed, and reannealed [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is required, consistent with all relevant SS and TS analogues.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Starting from layered binary dichalcogenides should lower structural reconstruction because ZrS2, ZrTe2, and HfTe2 already contain the relevant edge-sharing octahedral sheets (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). However, Hf/Zr and S/Te interdiffusion within layered solids can be slow, and TS-1/SS-2 show repeated grinding/heating is often needed for mixed or equilibrated chalcogenides. This route may be better than elements for phase topology but worse than CVT for crystal growth and homogenization [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether binary precursors interdiffuse completely before segregating into stable ZrS2/ZrTe2/HfTe2 domains [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has low amorphization tendency because Hf and Zr are chemically and size compatible in octahedral chalcogenide environments, as suggested by HfTe2/ZrTe2 analogues and mixed TiZrTe4 octahedral sheets (SS-2, SS-3, TS-1). The target is a simple layered edge-sharing octahedral structure rather than a large, mixed-connectivity framework, matching ZrS2/ZrTe2/HfTe2 topology (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). There is insufficient evidence from CS for amorphization tendency because the chemically similar examples include different frameworks and cations; CS is therefore treated as neutral for this subfactor. The residual risk is not amorphization but phase segregation or disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfZr3(TeS)4 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.009 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues with similar or higher hull energies have reported syntheses (TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The strongest support comes from structurally similar layered dichalcogenides: ZrS2, ZrTe2, and HfTe2 are established octahedral sheet compounds accessible by sealed-tube or CVT methods (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). The most important enabling factor is the excellent structural match to known 1T-type edge-sharing octahedral chalcogenides, combined with very similar Hf/Zr chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-1/SS-2/SS-3]. The most important limiting factor is composition control in a mixed Hf/Zr and mixed S/Te system, especially because volatile chalcogens and narrow phase windows are noted in related mixed chalcogenides (CS-1, CS-2) and repeated annealing is needed in mixed Ti/Zr telluride synthesis (TS-1). Iodine-assisted CVT is judged the best route because it is directly validated for the closest structural analogues and can overcome slow refractory-metal diffusion (SS-1, SS-3, TS-4).
