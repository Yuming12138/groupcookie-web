### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at **0.0000 eV/atom above hull**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase from the target data. Several similar halide/chalcogenide systems with E_hull = 0 are experimentally synthesized, including NbSe₂Cl₂ (CS-1), NbSeI₃ (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1), and TeI-related phases (SS-2 / TS-2). The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide the Ti–Se–I phase diagram, so competing TiSe₂/TiIₓ phases remain possible; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ti-specific phase competition, neutral 50.

#### F2: Route 1

The direct sealed-tube route inherits moderate-to-high severity from evacuated sealed silica tube syntheses of NbSeI₃ and Nb₄Se₄I₄ (CS-3, CS-5). Temperatures are not extreme by solid-state standards, but iodine/selenium volatility and sealed-tube pressure create operational difficulty **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main uncertainty is whether Ti reacts cleanly before forming stable binary TiSe₂ or volatile Ti iodides **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3: Route 1

Elemental routes are proven for Nb–Se–I analogues (CS-3, CS-5), but Ti metal diffusion and reaction can be slow because Ti is refractory **[Reasonable Inference]**. Long durations in CS-3 and CS-1 suggest slow ordering and transport in related chalcogenide halide systems. The score is lowered because the target 2D sheet must assemble from elements rather than from a structurally related precursor.

#### F2: Route 2

CVT uses lower temperatures than direct high-temperature synthesis, as shown by NbSe₂Cl₂ at 400–515 °C (CS-1) and CuBrSe₃ at 340→290 °C (SS-3 / TS-3). However, vacuum, sealed tubes, volatile halogens, and toxic/corrosive reagents still impose deductions. Phase control may be sensitive because CS-1 reports narrow windows and product contamination issues.

#### F3: Route 2

Vapor transport improves mass transport compared with static solid-state diffusion, as directly demonstrated by large single-crystal growth in CS-1. The target’s 2D nature aligns with the 2D NbSe₂Cl₂ analogue in CS-1, but the chemistry changes from Nb–Se–Cl to Ti–Se–I. The uncertainty is whether the volatile Ti–I species will transport the correct Ti:Se:I stoichiometry without forming TiI₄-rich deposits **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2: Route 3

The binary-precursor route operates near 530 °C in CS-4, avoiding extreme temperatures. It still requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and volatile/moisture-sensitive iodide chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. Severity is moderate rather than low because TiI₄/I₂ handling and ampoule pressure remain significant operational hazards.

#### F3: Route 3

This route has the best kinetics because a preformed TiSe₂ framework should reduce long-range Ti–Se diffusion and provide partial structural inheritance **[Reasonable Inference]**. The NbSe₂ + NbCl₄ precedent in CS-4 directly supports using a metal chalcogenide plus metal halide to access chalcogenide halides. The limiting uncertainty is that CS-4 reports poor reproducibility and narrow stability, so TiSe₂ may persist or over-iodination may occur.

#### F2: Route 4

Hydrothermal iodide synthesis occurs at only 195–280 °C for Te iodides (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5 / TS-2, TS-4), so thermal severity is low. The route still requires a sealed autoclave, concentrated HI, and corrosive/volatile iodine chemistry. There is insufficient evidence from CS for hydrothermal Ti–Se–I formation, so CS is treated as neutral 50 for this route.

#### F3: Route 4

Solution-mediated growth could reduce diffusion barriers, and structurally similar iodide frameworks form hydrothermally in SS-2/4/5 and TS-2/4. However, those examples are Te–I systems, not transition-metal selenoiodides, and Ti is prone to hydrolysis/oxide formation in aqueous media **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thus kinetics may be acceptable, but chemical selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern from the target formula. The formula unit is small, and the reported structure is an ordered 2D C2/m sheet, not a highly complex multi-cation network; analogous Nb(SBr)₂ is also a simple C2/m layered compound (CS-2). Exact unit-cell atom count and polyhedral sharing details are not fully resolved from CS/SS/TS, so the remaining uncertainty is treated as neutral 50 for those sources.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti(SeI)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom from the target data. The strongest support comes from chemically related Nb–Se–I and Nb–chalcogenide–halide syntheses in sealed tubes and CVT conditions (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5), with additional structural/thermodynamic support from iodide frameworks in SS/TS. The most critical enabling factor is halide-assisted transport or reactive iodide chemistry, which can overcome slow diffusion at moderate temperatures as seen in CS-1 and CS-4. The most critical limiting factor is phase control under volatile iodine chemical potential, since related Nb systems report phase-purity and reproducibility difficulties (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). Overall, the compound appears synthetically plausible, with the binary TiSe₂ + TiI₄/I₂ sealed-tube route being the most rational first attempt.
