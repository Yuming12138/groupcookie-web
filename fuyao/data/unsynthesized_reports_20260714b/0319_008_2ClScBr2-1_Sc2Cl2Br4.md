### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is high by the required rubric. Several close analogues are also on the hull: Sc₄NCl₆ in CS-3, NaScCl₄ in SS-5/TS-4, and the Hf bromide salts in SS-3/SS-4 and TS-2/TS-3. CS-2 gives a chemically similar but high-E_hull example, so it is treated as a weaker contradictory signal rather than dominant evidence. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Br/Cl ordering.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses sealed ampoules, controlled atmosphere, and air-sensitive halides, which are directly supported as necessary by CS-3, SS-5, and TS-4. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, unlike the high-pressure rare-earth chloride cases in SS-2/TS-1. The main severity comes from volatile/corrosive halides and moisture sensitivity, not from extreme temperature.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Solid-state halide reactions can be effective, as Hf bromide analogues form at 450 °C in sealed ampoules (SS-3/SS-4; TS-2/TS-3), but Sc-containing halide/nitride phases may need 850–950 °C and long durations (CS-3). The target shares octahedral Sc–halide motifs with NaScCl₄ (SS-5; TS-4), reducing structural reconstruction difficulty. Uncertainty remains because mixed Br/Cl order is not explicitly demonstrated in the references.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The melt/flux route has similar atmosphere and volatility penalties to Route 1, because NaScCl₄ and related halides require strict exclusion of moisture/oxygen (SS-5; TS-4). Temperatures are expected below 1000 °C, consistent with NaScCl₄/NaLuCl₄ melt growth at 630–903 °C (SS-5; TS-5). The method is not high pressure and is effectively one main thermal sequence.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The flux/melt route scores highest kinetically because molten halides allow fast ion and halide exchange, supported by NaScCl₄ melt growth (SS-5; TS-4) and NdBr₃ flux crystallization (CS-4). Slow cooling in SS-5/TS-5 indicates a practical pathway for crystal growth and ordering. The key uncertainty is whether ScBr₂Cl melts congruently or whether ScCl₃/ScBr₃ volatility creates composition drift.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Chemical vapor transport requires controlled sealed conditions, volatile transport species, and air-sensitive precursors, as shown by TbCl₃ transport with AlCl₃ (SS-1) and Sc halide/nitride vapor transport in Nb tubing (CS-3). This route adds complexity through temperature-gradient control and possible transport-agent optimization. High pressure is not required.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Vapor transport can grow halide crystals, as supported by SS-1 and CS-3, and volatile bromide chemistry is supported by SS-3/TS-2. However, maintaining the 2:1 Br:Cl ratio is likely harder in the gas phase because chloride and bromide transport equilibria may differ; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics for crystallization are acceptable, but composition control lowers F3.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is the most severe route because rare-earth chloride analogues require modified Belt apparatus conditions at 7.5–25 kbar and high temperatures (SS-2; TS-1). Even though the pressure may be below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, sealed capsules, air-sensitive halides, multi-step handling, and high temperature still impose large deductions. This route is less attractive because the target is already predicted as a ground-state phase.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
High pressure can help stabilize dense octahedral halide frameworks, as in TmCl₃ (SS-2; TS-1), and heating may improve diffusion. But the target is a layered AlCl₃-derived structure, so pressure could also favor a denser competing polymorph; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics may be reasonable, but phase selectivity is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only Sc as a cation, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger. The target is described as edge-sharing ScBr₄Cl₂ octahedral sheets, not a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data, so their signal for F4 is insufficient and neutral; the score is mainly based on the target’s simple chemistry and connectivity.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScBr₂Cl is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of moisture-sensitive scandium/rare-earth halides in sealed ampoules, melt growth, and vapor transport, with the strongest transferable evidence coming from NaScCl₄ melt growth and sealed bromide halide syntheses (SS-5; TS-4; SS-3/SS-4; TS-2/TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the use of an anhydrous molten or sealed halide environment, which promotes Br/Cl exchange and Sc–halide octahedral assembly. The main limiting factor is stoichiometry control of mixed chloride/bromide under volatile, moisture-sensitive conditions. Overall, synthesis is favorable but requires rigorous dry handling and careful thermal control.
