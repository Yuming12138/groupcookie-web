### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.028 eV/atom, which falls in the highest scoring ≤0.05 eV/atom range. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable small positive hull energies are documented, including Rb₂KInF₆ at 0.016 eV/atom [TS2], CsSrF₃ at 0.019 eV/atom [TS3], Cs₂KMnF₆ at 0.045 eV/atom [TS4], and K₂NaYF₆ at 0.032 eV/atom [TS5]. The exact-composition RbF high-pressure analogue is slightly higher at 0.0516 eV/atom but is experimentally accessible under pressure [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the tetragonal P4/mmm polymorph is recoverable at ambient pressure after synthesis.

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is favored because the exact RbF analogue is obtained by applying 9–15 kbar pressure to anhydrous RbF [CS1, SS1, TS1]. This is specialized but below the scoring threshold of >5 GPa, so the formal severity deduction is limited. The main practical penalty is moisture-sensitive handling of anhydrous RbF, inferred from strict dry fluoride handling in structurally similar synthesis [SS2]. Uncertainty remains because the reference route produces cubic Pm-3m RbF, not the target tetragonal phase [SS1].

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because Route 1 starts from RbF and requires only a polymorphic transformation, not multicomponent diffusion [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The known RbF transition occurs over a relatively accessible pressure window of 9–15 kbar [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The structural reconstruction score is reduced because the target is a two-dimensional P4/mmm sheet with RbF₅/RbF₆ units, whereas the reported high-pressure RbF analogue is cubic Pm-3m with eight-coordinate bcc-like bonding [SS1]. The unknown transition pathway to the layered target is the principal kinetic uncertainty.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures, consistent with sealed fluoride syntheses at 350–860 °C [SS2, TS3, TS5]. However, analogous syntheses require Ar atmospheres, sealed cells or ampoules, and moisture control [SS2, TS3, TS5]. These requirements produce deductions for controlled atmosphere, sealed handling, multi-step processing, and moisture-sensitive precursor handling. The route is less severe than high-temperature oxide synthesis but less directly targeted than Route 1.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Starting from RbF makes precursor activity high because the desired composition is already present [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Diffusion demands are low for a single-component salt, and fluoride analogues can crystallize at moderate temperatures [SS2, TS3, TS5]. The structural reconstruction score is modest because ambient sealed annealing is not the known driver for RbF polymorphism; pressure is the demonstrated driver in exact RbF [CS1, SS1, TS1]. A competing return to the ambient RbF polymorph is likely [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/SS1/TS1].

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Thin-film synthesis has no direct CS/SS/TS support; evidence is insufficient from CS, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS, so this route is speculative. The formal severity is lowered by vacuum processing, possible reactive fluorine use, multi-step deposition/anneal/capping, and volatile or air-sensitive alkali handling [Reasonable Inference]. The advantage is avoiding bulk equilibrium and potentially stabilizing a layered metastable phase [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is high because no analogue demonstrates RbF thin-film crystallization.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

Thin-film kinetics may benefit from short diffusion lengths and epitaxial templating [Reasonable Inference]. However, vapor availability and composition control are difficult because Rb-containing species can be volatile and fluorinating environments can be reactive [Reasonable Inference]. Direct crystallization of the P4/mmm RbF sheet phase is unverified; evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. Therefore Route 3 receives the lowest kinetic score despite being conceptually useful for metastable stabilization.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation size mismatch trigger [target]. The described crystallographic motif has two inequivalent Rb sites and two inequivalent F sites, with no evidence for a large >40-atom unit cell [target]. The connectivity includes corner and edge sharing, but not face sharing, so the strict mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not activated [target]. The remaining uncertainty is that a layered metastable phase can still disorder during decompression or thin-film deposition even when formal amorphization descriptors are favorable [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a metastable but low-energy RbF polymorph because its E_hull is 0.028 eV/atom rather than zero [target]. The strongest support comes from the exact-composition RbF analogue, which is synthesized as a high-pressure polymorph from anhydrous RbF at 9–15 kbar [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Structurally similar fluoride data support dry, sealed, inert processing of fluorides but do not directly prove access to the tetragonal P4/mmm RbF sheet structure [SS2, TS3, TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is the simple single-component chemistry: no cation ordering, redox, or anion incorporation reaction is required [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph selectivity, because the known RbF high-pressure product is cubic Pm-3m rather than the target tetragonal layered phase [SS1].
