### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. CS data supports this because chemically similar bromides are mostly at or near the hull, including CS4 at 0.0 eV/atom and CS1–CS3 below ~0.024 eV/atom; CS5 is less favorable at 0.0767 eV/atom but still moderately close. SS and TS data also support stability because SS2/TS1, SS5/TS2, TS3, TS4, and TS5 include multiple 0.0 eV/atom analogues. Remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic hull stability but whether the computed ground state is experimentally accessible without forming simpler MnBr₂/InBr₃ or oxidation-state variants.

#### F2 — Route 1: CVT / vapor-assisted crystallization

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures based on CS1, CS3, SS1, and SS2/TS1, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. It is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile halide transport species, multiple handling/heating steps, and toxic/air-sensitive bromide reagents, all supported by CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2/TS1, and CS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an exact MnInBr₆ CVT window, so the recommended 250–330 °C range is a [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is therefore moderate but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 1: CVT / vapor-assisted crystallization

The kinetics are favorable because volatile metal halides allow transport and nucleation at low temperatures, as shown by CS1, CS3, SS1, and SS2/TS1. However, the target is a 2D edge-sharing octahedral sheet, while many analogues are 0D or 1D clusters/ribbons, so structural reconstruction and cation ordering remain uncertain. CS/SS/TS provide useful evidence for halide mobility but insufficient direct evidence for Mn/In ordering in this exact AlCl₃-derived sheet. The final F3 is high-moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2: InBr₃ flux / reactive melt

Route 2 is less severe than Route 1 because it can be performed as a flux/melt growth rather than relying primarily on vapor transport, and CS4 directly supports a 475 °C InBr₃ melt synthesis with slow cooling. It still requires sealed or rigorously dry conditions because CS4 notes extreme air/humidity sensitivity, and bromide analogues in CS3/SS3 are moisture sensitive. The volatile/air-sensitive precursor penalty is justified by InBr₃ and optional Br₂ use, while no high-temperature or >5 GPa pressure penalties apply. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for an InBr₃ flux route specifically to MnInBr₆, so the adaptation is a [Reasonable Inference] from CS4.

#### F3 — Route 2: InBr₃ flux / reactive melt

The InBr₃ melt should strongly enhance mass transport and cation diffusion, following CS4 where elemental Zr is oxidized within InBr₃ melt to crystallize Zr(InBr₃)₂. This is the closest chemical analogue because the target also contains In–Br chemistry and another transition-metal bromide sublattice. Structural transfer is not exact because CS4 is not the same 2D edge-sharing Mn/In octahedral sheet, but it demonstrates that InBr₃-rich melts can produce metal–indium bromide phases. Remaining uncertainty is mainly Mn oxidation-state control.

#### F2 — Route 3: Direct bromination with pressure compensation

Route 3 is penalized for evacuated sealed ampoule handling, volatile Br₂, multiple pressure-rated steps, and toxic/corrosive reagents. These penalties are directly supported by SS5/TS2, where TlFeBr₄ required an evacuated ampoule and ~200 bar pressure compensation, and by CS5, where Br₂-containing sealed ampoules were placed in an autoclave. The synthesis temperature is only ~280–330 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists that direct bromination gives a layered Mn/In ordered phase rather than simpler bromides.

#### F3 — Route 3: Direct bromination with pressure compensation

Br₂ is reactive, but direct reaction from metals or binary bromides may suffer from passivation, local over-bromination, and slow ordering. SS5/TS2 demonstrates Fe/Tl bromide formation under pressure, but that analogue is not an InBr₃ flux process and does not prove easy Mn/In ordering. Compared with Route 2, diffusion and phase selection are less favorable because no liquid InBr₃ flux is necessarily present. Therefore F3 is moderate-low.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization/disorder trigger is the substantial Mn/In cation size mismatch in octahedral coordination, which can favor local strain or cation disorder; this is based on physicochemical ionic-radius reasoning and the target’s distinct MnBr₆/InBr₆ octahedra. The target connectivity is simple edge-sharing only, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not activated, despite some analogues showing mixed corner/edge or face-sharing motifs in CS1 and SS1. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not give sufficient direct evidence for target cell size, so that check is treated as neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnInBr₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues include multiple ground-state halides. The CS/SS/TS dataset strongly supports sealed halide ampoule chemistry as an enabling synthesis family, especially CVT and flux methods using volatile or molten metal bromides. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility of bromide/InBr₃ media, demonstrated by CVT and InBr₃ melt analogues in CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, and SS2/TS1. The most critical limiting factor is oxidation-state and stoichiometry control for Mn/In in a 2D ordered octahedral sheet, since several analogues report air sensitivity, phase mixtures, or special cooling/pressure requirements (CS4, CS5, SS2/TS1, SS5/TS2). Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with Route 2 giving the best balance of chemical transferability, kinetics, and manageable conditions.
