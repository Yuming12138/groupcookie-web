### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it lies on the calculated convex hull and is scored at 98/100. This is consistent with several close analogues also being on or near the hull: NbCrSe₅ is E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1), TaSe₃ is E_hull = 0.0 (SS4, TS3), and multiple NbSe₂ polymorphs are near-hull or on-hull (CS2–CS5, SS5, TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee a broad synthesis window, especially in volatile Se systems.

#### F2 — Route 1

Direct sealed-tube synthesis uses only moderate temperatures, with TaSe₃ synthesized at 800 °C for one week (SS4, TS3) and NbSe₂ analogues at 600–1100 °C (CS2, SS5). Deductions arise from the evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile/toxic Se precursor, both consistent with the sealed-tube practices in CS2, SS4, and TS3. Insufficient evidence from CS1 for direct selenide solid-state synthesis, so CS1 is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Nb metal, which is a dense precursor [Reasonable Inference], but the successful formation of NbSe₂ and TaSe₃ analogues from elements shows feasibility (CS2, SS4, TS3). Long reaction times of 7–15 days in NbCrSe₅ and TaSe₃ analogues indicate slow ordering and diffusion (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The main uncertainty is whether NbSe₃ forms directly or competes with NbSe₂ under Se-deficient conditions.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT/vapor transport remains moderate in severity because analogues use 650–950 °C gradients rather than extreme temperature or high pressure (CS4, TS4). The main penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Se/I₂, and toxic/corrosive transport chemistry [Reasonable Inference], consistent with iodine- or bromine-assisted CVT in CS4 and SS2/TS2. Insufficient evidence from solution chemistry CS1 for CVT, so CS1 is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by transporting Nb and Se through volatile halide/selenide species; CS4 explicitly describes transport reactions for layered Nb/Ta chalcogenides. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the closest TaSe₃ analogue has the same P2₁/m MSe₃ motif and forms under sealed/vapor-type conditions (SS4, TS3). The uncertainty is transport-agent optimization: too little I₂ gives poor transport, while too much may stabilize iodide-containing phases, as suggested by the existence of Ta–Se–I phases (TS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

The two-step feed + CVT route uses similar temperatures and sealed ampoules as Route 2, but receives an additional multi-step penalty. CS4 supports the general two-stage logic of preparing polycrystalline chalcogenide material followed by transport growth. The route is more operationally demanding than direct CVT but can improve purity and crystal size [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Using pre-reacted NbSe₃ feed should reduce the burden of forming the phase during final crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and vapor transport minimizes bulk diffusion limitations as supported by CS4 and TS4. The structural reconstruction score is high because final growth should be recrystallization rather than complete reaction from elements [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is that pre-reacted feed may contain NbSe₂ or Se-rich secondary phases, affecting transport selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Nb, so cation size mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The target contains edge/corner-sharing distorted Nb–Se polyhedra but no face-sharing is specified, and related NbCrSe₅/TaSe₃ structures crystallize successfully rather than amorphizing (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided by CS/SS/TS; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell trigger, so no penalty is applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbSe₃ is likely a ground-state or very robust equilibrium phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close structural/thermodynamic analogues such as TaSe₃ and NbCrSe₅ are also on the hull (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The analogue support is strong: Nb–Se chemistry is directly demonstrated in NbSe₂ syntheses (CS2–CS5, SS5, TS4), and the MSe₃ P2₁/m framework is directly represented by TaSe₃ (SS4, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is sealed-tube or CVT control of selenium chemical potential, which is common to the successful analogues (CS2, CS4, SS4, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control between NbSe₂ and NbSe₃, especially if Se is lost during heating [Reasonable Inference from NbSe₂ prevalence in CS2–CS5]. Overall, iodine-assisted CVT is the best route because it combines established Nb selenide transport chemistry with the crystallization behavior of the TaSe₃ analogue (CS4, SS4, TS3, TS4).
