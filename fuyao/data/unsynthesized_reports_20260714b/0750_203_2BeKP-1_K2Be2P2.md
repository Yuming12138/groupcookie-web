### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided thermodynamic data. Chemically similar analogues KMgP, K₂NiP₂, NaSrP, KLiS, and KMgAs are also all reported with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1 to CS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar references likewise have E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5), so CS/SS/TS consistently support stability rather than metastability. Remaining uncertainty is experimental: Be-containing K–Be–P phase equilibria are not directly represented in the references, so kinetic or volatility limitations may still prevent isolation.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a lower severity score because the best same-structure analogue KMgP uses 1150 °C followed by several days at 800 °C in Ta/Fe under Ar (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). The high temperature, multi-step dwell/anneal/cool sequence, and need to seal volatile K/P components justify deductions; similar high-temperature direct phosphide synthesis is also seen for KMgAs and Yb/Zn phosphides (CS-5, SS-4, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on whether Be specifically requires the 1150 °C step, so the high-temperature requirement may be conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from using reactive K and P, and from a direct same-structure analogue, KMgP (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). However, Be metal is expected to be less reactive and more diffusion-limited than Mg because of strong Be–O surface passivation and higher covalent bonding tendency [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in KMgP indicate that even chemically favorable P4/nmm alkali phosphides may need extended ordering time (CS-1). Thus, kinetics are feasible but not rapid.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C conditions because K₂LiAlP₂ and K₂NiP₂ form at 925–950 K in sealed ampoules (SS-3, TS-3, CS-2). The severity is dominated by sealed-tube/vacuum handling, air-sensitive K, volatile P/K species, and Be toxicity [Reasonable Inference for Be hazard]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Be₃P₂ specifically in this structure family, so the route is plausible but not proven.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary phosphide precursors can improve reaction homogeneity, as LiP/AlP and BaP/InP are used in related phosphide syntheses (SS-3, TS-3, SS-5, TS-5). The target still requires reconstructing into the P4/nmm KBeP sheet structure; the closest same-structure support comes from KMgP, which was not made from binary phosphides (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). Kinetics should be comparable to other sealed alkali phosphide syntheses, but Be–P precursor reactivity is uncertain because CS/SS/TS provide no direct Be analogue.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses lower temperatures, consistent with NaSrP flux growth at 600 °C and related 600–900 °C flux conditions (CS-3). However, it requires glovebox handling, sealed Nb/Ta ampoules, vacuum/silica jacketing, long holds, slow cooling, and flux removal, all of which increase operational severity (CS-3, SS-1, TS-1). The major route-specific uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not demonstrate KBeP solubility or stability in Sn/K flux.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux provides the best kinetic pathway because molten alkali/Sn media can accelerate mass transport relative to solid-state diffusion; Na/Sn flux was used for NaSrP-type phosphide crystals (CS-3). Slow cooling and long equilibration are established for structurally/thermodynamically similar alkali pnictides (SS-1, TS-1). The downside is phase control: CS-3 notes flux products and secondary phases, while SS-1/TS-1 report side phases and optimization needs. Therefore, kinetics for crystallization are good, but purity may be challenging.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large K/Be cation size mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]; related structures accommodate such mismatch by separating large alkali coordination from smaller tetrahedral cation sites, as in KMgP and K₂LiAlP₂ (CS-1, SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). The structure is simple and low-atom-count, and the described BeP₄ network involves corner/edge sharing but no face sharing, reducing glass-forming complexity relative to highly connected polyhedral networks. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for amorphous K–Be–P behavior specifically, so F4 is based mainly on structural heuristics.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KBeP is likely a true ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and all close CS/SS/TS analogues listed are also on the hull. The strongest support comes from KMgP, which is chemically and structurally very close: it is P4/nmm, Matlockite-type, and contains K–P coordination plus edge/corner-sharing MP₄ tetrahedra (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable layered alkali-metal phosphides with tetrahedral metal–P units under sealed solid-state or flux conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-3, TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is the combination of volatile/air-sensitive K and P with toxic Be handling, especially in sealed high-temperature systems [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed ampoule requirements in CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1]. Phase purity is also a concern because flux/slow-cooled pnictide references report side phases and optimization requirements (CS-3, SS-1, TS-1).
