### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Rh2Te has E_hull = 0.0420 eV/atom, placing it just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range. CS/SS/TS support the idea that Rh compounds with small positive hull energies can be synthesized, since Rh2As at 0.0806 eV/atom is reported by direct reaction [CS-3; SS-1; TS-1], and Rh3P2 at 0.0677 eV/atom is grown from Sn flux [TS-2]. The remaining uncertainty is that a small positive hull energy suggests possible metastability relative to competing Rh–Te phases rather than guaranteed ground-state formation [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated silica tubes and high-temperature furnace conditions, which are directly supported for Rh3Te4 and Rh2As analogues [CS-2; CS-3]. The main severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Te, multiple heat/grind steps, and Te toxicity precautions [CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives strong evidence; SS and TS also support sealed-tube Rh-pnictide routes [SS-1; TS-1], while they provide insufficient direct evidence specifically for Te volatility beyond CS-2, so SS/TS are treated as neutral on that subpoint.

#### F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state kinetics

Kinetics are limited by Rh’s refractory nature and the need to form a new ordered binary structure from elements [Reasonable Inference]. Rh3Te4 synthesis at 600–1000 °C shows Rh–Te reactions are feasible under sealed-tube conditions [CS-2], but intermediate grinding and multi-firing indicate nontrivial diffusion/phase-control barriers [CS-2]. Rh2As analogues required careful staged thermal treatment to avoid phase mixtures, supporting a lower structural-reconstruction score [CS-3; TS-1].

#### F2 — Route 2 NaCl/KCl flux severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C temperatures because Rh2Sb was grown in NaCl/KCl flux at 800 °C [CS-1; SS-3]. Severity remains moderate because the method requires evacuated sealed silica tubes, long annealing, flux removal, and volatile/toxic Te precautions [CS-1; CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for NaCl/KCl flux in tellurides, so TS is neutral for this route-specific flux choice.

#### F3 — Route 2 NaCl/KCl flux kinetics

Flux growth improves kinetic accessibility by increasing local transport and enabling crystal growth below Rh’s melting point [CS-1; SS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support is Rh2Sb, a Rh2X analogue, which crystallized from NaCl/KCl flux at 800 °C, directly matching the proposed stoichiometric motif [CS-1; SS-3]. The long 5-week dwell suggests ordering/dissolution kinetics may still be slow, so the route is not scored as easy [CS-1].

#### F2 — Route 3 Sn-flux severity

Sn flux is supported for Rh3P2 single crystals at 1423 K / about 1150 °C in sealed evacuated silica ampoules [TS-2]. Compared with NaCl/KCl flux, it is more severe because it may require >1000 °C and still uses a sealed system with volatile Te [TS-2; CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for Sn-flux Rh–Te synthesis, so their Sn-flux signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 Sn-flux kinetics

Sn flux can dissolve and transport Rh species, as shown by Rh3P2 crystal growth from Sn-rich melts [TS-2]. However, Sn is chemically active in Rh intermetallic systems, and Rh2CoSn synthesis shows that Rh–Sn-containing phases are accessible [TS-3], so composition and phase selectivity are uncertain. This gives good liquid-mediated diffusion but only moderate structural selectivity [TS-2; TS-3].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a binary ordered crystalline Rh–Te phase with no stated large unit cell, no strong cation-size mismatch, and no described coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral motifs [Target description; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for Rh2Te, so their direct evidence is insufficient and neutral, but the target’s structural checks give zero triggers. The uncertainty is that rapid quenching from melt or flux could trap disordered Rh–Te material, although this is not indicated by the provided analogues [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rh2Te is likely a **slightly metastable but experimentally accessible** phase because its E_hull is only 0.0420 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest support comes from chemically close Rh–Te and Rh2X analogues: Rh3Te4 is synthesized from elements in evacuated silica tubes [CS-2], and Rh2Sb is grown as Rh2X from NaCl/KCl flux [CS-1; SS-3]. The most critical enabling factor is flux-assisted transport, especially NaCl/KCl flux, which can overcome slow Rh diffusion at about 800 °C [CS-1; SS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control in the Rh–Te system, especially avoiding Te-rich phases such as Rh3Te4 [CS-2; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, Route 2 is the most plausible first attempt, with Route 1 as a practical powder backup.
