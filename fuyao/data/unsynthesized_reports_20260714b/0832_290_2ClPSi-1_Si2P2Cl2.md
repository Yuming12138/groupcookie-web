### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.025 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom range and justifying a high F1 score. TS provides strong supporting precedent because several thermodynamically similar chloride/layered compounds are near-stable, including TS-1 at 0.037 eV/atom and TS-3 at 0.0286 eV/atom. SS is also favorable because top Si–P framework analogues SS-1 to SS-3 are on the hull, and SS-4 is near the hull at 0.0072 eV/atom. CS is mixed: CS-2, CS-4, and CS-5 are stable or near-stable, but CS-1 is highly metastable at 2.22 eV/atom, so CS is not weighted as heavily for F1.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, guided by Si–P solid-state synthesis at ~897 °C in SS-1 and pnictide/chalcogenide sealed-tube reactions at 800–850 °C in CS-2/CS-5. The main severity penalties come from the need for evacuated ampoules, volatile P/chloride components, and toxic or moisture-sensitive chlorinating agents, consistent with SS-4, CS-2, and TS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an exact SiPCl direct reaction, so the route is penalized for uncertainty in precursor chemistry rather than for high temperature or high pressure.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because solid Si is refractory, and complete reconstruction from Si + P + Cl source into a 2D ordered SiPCl sheet is nontrivial. SS-1 demonstrates that Si–P tetrahedral frameworks can form by direct solid-state reaction, but CS-5 shows P-containing solid-state syntheses may require weeks and repeated treatments. Use of preformed SiP is a **[Reasonable Inference]** to improve precursor activity and reduce reconstruction, but this is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS for SiPCl.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids external high pressure and can likely operate below 1000 °C, consistent with SS-4 at 1200 K and TS-1 at 620–730 K. Severity arises from evacuated ampoules, halide transport agents, volatile P, and multi-step gradient growth; these are all supported by SS-4 and TS-1. CS-2 additionally supports the use of Cl₂/halogen chemistry in sealed tubes but also flags the need for careful atmosphere control.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT improves kinetics by using gas-phase transport and controlled crystallization, as demonstrated for Al–Si–P in SS-4. TS-1 supports that 2D chloride compounds can be obtained from chloride-containing sealed ampoules, which favors layered crystal growth. The uncertainty is that SiPCl may overchlorinate to molecular silicon chlorides, a risk inferred from TS-4, and exact Si:P:Cl stoichiometry control is not directly established.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is conditionally more severe because SS-2/SS-3 flux growth uses 1050 °C and very slow cooling, triggering the >1000 °C penalty. It also requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile red P, chloride additives, and post-growth flux separation, all supported by SS-2/SS-3 and SS-4. No high pressure is required, unlike TS-2, so that penalty does not apply.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth gives the best kinetic score because Sn flux can overcome solid-state diffusion limits, and SS-2/SS-3 directly demonstrate Si–P crystal growth from Sn flux. Slow cooling promotes ordering and crystal growth, also supported by SS-2/SS-3. The remaining limitation is chemical selectivity: adding a chloride source to Sn flux is a **[Reasonable Inference]**, not directly proven for SiPCl, and may generate competing Sn–P, Si–P, or Si–Cl species.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is structurally simple, with one SiPCl sheet and corner-sharing SiP₃Cl tetrahedra, so no mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing network is indicated by the target description. The P3m1 target description gives no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell, and there is no mixed-cation radius mismatch because the network contains only Si as the tetrahedral cation. SS analogues demonstrate many Si–P tetrahedral networks crystallize well, including SS-1 to SS-4, supporting a low amorphization tendency. Remaining uncertainty is that 2D chlorophosphide sheets may exfoliate or disorder if Cl/P volatility is not controlled; this concern is inferred from volatile chloride behavior in TS-4.

---

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SiPCl is likely a slightly metastable but experimentally accessible phase rather than a deep ground-state compound, because its E_hull is only 0.025 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS analogues showing robust Si–P tetrahedral frameworks at similar bond lengths and from TS analogues showing that low-dimensional chloride-containing compounds can be synthesized in sealed ampoules. The most critical enabling factor is controlled halide/vapor transport, because it can both introduce Cl and reduce the kinetic barrier for forming ordered 2D sheets, as suggested by SS-4 and TS-1. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of P and Cl chemical potentials: P loss would form Si-rich phases, while excess chloride could form volatile or molecular silicon chlorides, a risk inferred from TS-4. Overall, the target is feasible but will likely require sealed-ampoule or CVT optimization rather than simple open-tube heating.
