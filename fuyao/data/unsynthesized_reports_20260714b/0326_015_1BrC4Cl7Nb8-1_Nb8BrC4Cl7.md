### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 score range by the required rubric. This is reinforced by several structurally or thermodynamically related Nb carbides being on the hull, including Nb₂C/Nb₈C₄ and Nb₆C₅ (SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). CS also contains stable or near-stable layered Nb₂S₂C analogues, with E_hull ≈0.017–0.022 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2), supporting the idea that layered Nb–C–anion sheets can be low-energy. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic, not thermodynamic, because CS/SS/TS do not directly report Nb–C–Br–Cl synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 requires controlled vacuum or sealed ampoule conditions, which are supported by Nb₂S₂C topochemical work under vacuum/evacuated tubes (CS-1, CS-2) and Nb₂C high-vacuum synthesis (SS-5, TS-3). It also uses volatile, moisture-sensitive halogenating agents such as NbCl₅/NbBr₅, which is **[Reasonable Inference]** because direct Br/Cl Nb carbide sources are absent from CS/SS/TS. The route is multi-step because a Nb₂C scaffold may need to be prepared, then halogenated, then annealed/washed, paralleling the multi-step topochemical Nb₂S₂C route (CS-1, CS-2). Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact halogenation temperature, so the severity score is conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is structural retention: Nb₂S₂C topochemical extraction preserved the S–Nb–C–Nb–S framework (CS-1, CS-2), and Nb₂C provides the target-like CNb₆ carbide scaffold (SS-5, TS-3). The limiting factor is that Nb carbides are refractory and Nb–C diffusion is slow, as shown by 2000 °C or 2300 K carbide synthesis conditions (SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). Halogen diffusion in a layered material should be easier than reconstructing the carbide lattice, but that is **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 benefits from common solid-state ampoule precedent: chloride/chalcogenide materials were synthesized in evacuated silica ampoules at 850 °C for a week (CS-3), and sulfides in evacuated quartz tubes at 1000–1300 °C (TS-5). However, volatile Nb halides require sealed-system handling, and their corrosive/moisture-sensitive nature is **[Reasonable Inference]**. If Nb₂C forms in situ from Nb + C, the conditions may need to approach carbide synthesis temperatures from SS-5/TS-3/TS-4, making the route substantially harder than the nominal 700–950 °C program. Thus F2 is moderate, but with a hidden risk of needing higher temperature.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The kinetic score is low because Nb metal and graphite are refractory and poorly reactive compared with molecular or salt precursors; Nb₂C and Nb₆C₅ analogues require very high temperatures under vacuum (SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). Direct formation also demands ordering of C, Cl, and Br around Nb in a low-symmetry monoclinic 2D sheet, but no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates this exact reconstruction. The CS ampoule chloride examples support sealed reactions but involve more ionic chalcogenide/halide systems, not refractory Nb–C lattice formation (CS-3, CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is whether volatile niobium halides can transport Nb enough to bypass slow solid-state diffusion.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses evacuated ampoules and flux, directly supported by CS-5 where NaCl or excess MCl₃ flux enabled complete reaction and single crystals at 850 °C, and by CS-3 where slow-cooling ampoule synthesis at 850 °C produced chloride-containing crystals. It still requires controlled atmosphere and volatile/corrosive halide reagents, so deductions apply. It avoids the very high 2000 °C carbide sintering conditions if preformed Nb₂C is used, which is why F2 is better than Route 1 when the carbide scaffold is purchased or separately available. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Nb–C–Br–Cl flux chemistry specifically.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth improves precursor activity and mass transport; CS-5 explicitly states that NaCl or excess MCl₃ flux secures complete and fast reaction and single-crystalline products. Nb–C scaffold formation remains intrinsically slow, as shown by SS-5/TS-3/TS-4, but starting from Nb₂C and using molten halides should reduce the need for full reconstruction **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because the target preserves CNb₆ motifs seen in Nb₂C and Nb₂S₂C analogues (CS-1/CS-2, SS-5/TS-3), while adding ordered mixed Br/Cl layers not directly demonstrated in the references. Overall, this route balances transport and structural retention best.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Thin-film deposition avoids high bulk sintering temperatures, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any thin-film route, so all thin-film conditions are **[Reasonable Inference]**. Vacuum/plasma operation, toxic halogen feeds, and multi-step deposition/anneal/halogenation all increase severity. The absence of high pressure is favorable, but composition control in a mixed Br/Cl carbide film is nontrivial. The severity score is therefore moderate-low.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₈C₄BrCl₇ is likely a true thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from Nb–C frameworks: Nb₂C/Nb₈C₄ and Nb₆C₅ are experimentally accessible and have similar CNb₆ motifs and Nb–C bond lengths (SS-5, TS-3, TS-4), while Nb₂S₂C shows that layered Nb–C–anion sheets can survive topochemical chemistry (CS-1, CS-2). The most important enabling factor is using a pre-formed Nb₂C-like scaffold so that synthesis only needs halogen insertion/order rather than complete Nb–C reconstruction. The main limiting factor is mixed Br/Cl stoichiometry under volatile, corrosive halide chemical potentials, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct demonstration. The best-supported practical route is molten halide flux or transport-assisted growth using Nb₂C plus controlled NbCl₅/NbBr₅ or alkali halide flux.
