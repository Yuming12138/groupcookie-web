### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff, so the assigned F1 = 90 is conservative. CS and SS analogues are mostly reported with E_hull = 0.0 and have successful synthesis records (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). TS is particularly supportive because several experimentally synthesized analogues lie at similar E_hull values, e.g., U₂Co₃Al₉ at 0.04998 eV/atom and U₄Ni₅Al₁₈ at 0.0638 eV/atom (TS1/TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that Pd-specific competing phases are not listed in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting plus evacuated annealing requires controlled Ar/vacuum and multiple processing steps, as shown in CS1, CS3, SS5, TS1, and TS3. The temperature is not extreme because many analogues anneal between 600 and 900 °C (CS1/CS3/SS5/TS1/TS3). Ce air sensitivity is a [Reasonable Inference], supported indirectly by the inert-atmosphere handling of Ce analogues in SS2/SS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for severe volatile loss of Pd or Al under these exact conditions.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Elemental precursors lower kinetic activity relative to salt/sol-gel routes, and all relevant analogues use dense elements (CS1–CS5/SS1–SS5/TS1/TS3). Repeated remelting and long annealing are used for homogeneity in CS3, SS5, and TS3, indicating manageable but nontrivial kinetics. The target’s seven inequivalent Al sites and mixed connectivity increase ordering difficulty relative to simpler analogues in CS5/SS4.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Direct solid-state reaction has relatively simple processing, supported by CS5/SS4, but it uses temperatures above 1000 °C. It avoids vacuum and specialized crystal-growth equipment if done under Ar, as in CS5/SS4. The principal severity penalty beyond temperature is Ce handling [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether this route alone can yield single-phase Ce₂Pd₃Al₁₁.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The reaction benefits from molten Al and likely molten Ce at 1000–1100 °C [Reasonable Inference], improving diffusion despite solid Pd. CS5/SS4 show direct Al-rich intermetallic synthesis can be rapid, but those structures are simpler than the target. Therefore F3 remains moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Czochralski/Bridgman growth uses specialized melt-growth equipment, as demonstrated in CS4/SS1 and SS2. It is not high-pressure and is commonly performed under Ar, which reduces chemical severity. The multi-step nature and air-sensitive Ce handling justify deductions. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives the exact Ce–Pd–Al liquidus, so no >1000 °C penalty was applied with high confidence.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt growth improves kinetics by bypassing much of the solid-state diffusion barrier; this is supported by TbNiAl₄ and CeCuAl₃ growth examples in SS1/SS2. Slow pulling and post-annealing should help order the complex P4mm structure [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is segregation or incongruent melting, which is not resolved by CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
The Al-flux route is mostly a [Reasonable Inference], not directly reported in CS/SS/TS. It requires sealed ampoules, high temperature, decanting, and air-sensitive Ce handling, so F2 is lower. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents assigning low severity despite favorable chemistry.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Molten Al flux should strongly enhance mass transport and lower nucleation barriers [Reasonable Inference]. The target is Al-rich, so an Al-rich melt is chemically plausible, and CS/SS/TS broadly support melt-based synthesis of aluminides. However, no cited analogue confirms flux selection of the Ce₂Pd₃Al₁₁ structure, so phase selectivity remains uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has significant size mismatch among Ce, Pd, and Al [Reasonable Inference], and the target description explicitly includes mixed edge/corner/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity. These features increase structural frustration and may promote disorder or glassy byproducts if quenched too rapidly. The unit-cell atom count trigger is uncertain because Z is not specified; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and the target description prevents counting it.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ce₂Pd₃Al₁₁ is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible (TS1/TS3/TS4). The CS and SS datasets strongly support synthesis of Al-rich rare-earth/transition-metal intermetallics from elements by arc melting, direct heating, and melt growth (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). The most important enabling factor is the Al-rich metallic chemistry, which permits melt-mediated diffusion and slow ordering, as seen across CS/SS/TS aluminide examples. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target has many inequivalent sites and mixed polyhedral connectivity, so competing Ce–Pd–Al or Pd–Al/Al-rich phases may form unless stoichiometry and annealing are controlled. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires iterative optimization of annealing temperature, cooling rate, and composition.
