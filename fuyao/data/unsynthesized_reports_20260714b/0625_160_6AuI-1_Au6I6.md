### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E above hull is 0.0290 eV/atom, which is within the high-synthesizability window of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Chemically similar BiI phases have E_hull ≈0.027–0.028 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized in sealed ampoules (CS-1, CS-2), while AuBr analogues range from stable to mildly metastable and are experimentally accessible (CS-3, CS-4; SS-2; TS-2). Thermodynamic analogues also cluster near the hull, with E_hull values around 0.014–0.031 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy supports existence but does not guarantee the specific orthorhombic Pma2 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, consistent with BiI synthesis at 558 K in sealed Ar ampoules (CS-1, CS-2) and iodide analogues at 473–573 K in evacuated tubes (SS-3; TS-3, TS-4). The severity penalties arise from using volatile I₂ in a sealed controlled atmosphere; sealed ampoules are repeatedly required in CS/SS/TS iodide syntheses (CS-1, CS-2; SS-3; TS-3). The route is moderately severe because iodine pressure and corrosivity must be controlled. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Au + I₂ binary AuI formation specifically; TS is therefore treated as supportive but not decisive for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic limitation is Au metal reactivity: Bi reacts directly with I₂ in CS-1/CS-2, but AuBr analogues are instead made by decomposing Au₂Br₆, implying direct Au-halogen routes can be less straightforward for Au(I) halides (CS-3, CS-4). Diffusion is helped by volatile iodine and possible vapor transport, consistent with low-temperature halide syntheses in CS and TS (CS-1, CS-2; TS-3). Structural reconstruction from elements is complete, and phase control is likely difficult because BiI and AuBr analogues show polymorphism and sluggish transitions (CS-1–CS-4; TS-2). SS provides useful evidence for sealed-tube iodide reactions, but insufficient evidence from SS for direct binary AuI formation, so SS is supportive rather than primary.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses mild temperatures but controlled vacuum/dry N₂ and a chemically sensitive iodine-rich precursor, matching AuBr decomposition conditions in CS-3/CS-4, SS-2, and TS-2. The main severity is not temperature but multistep handling: precursor synthesis, decomposition, powdering, and annealing. AuBr analogues explicitly require long decomposition and periodic powdering (CS-3, CS-4; TS-2). Insufficient evidence from BiI CS entries for Au(III)-iodide decomposition specifically; the strongest evidence is the AuBr entries.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetically, a preformed Au–I precursor should be more reactive than elemental Au [Reasonable Inference], and this is strongly supported by AuBr formation from Au₂Br₆ (CS-3, CS-4; SS-2; TS-2). Low-temperature decomposition minimizes long-range diffusion, giving a high ion-diffusion score. The structural reconstruction score is limited because AuBr analogues show sluggish, nonreproducible polymorph transitions and difficult phase purity (CS-3, CS-4; TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether AuI₃/Au₂I₆-like precursors can be cleanly prepared and decomposed without Au metal or iodine-rich byproducts.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures and standard sealed-ampoule/CVT equipment, as seen for BiI gradients (CS-1, CS-2), BiBr CVT (CS-5), and Hg halide CVT in evacuated quartz ampoules (TS-5). The penalties come from sealed/vacuum operation, volatile I₂, and multistep pre-synthesis plus gradient crystallization. No high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is supported by any source. SS gives general sealed-tube halide support but insufficient direct evidence for CVT growth of binary AuI, so SS is neutral-to-supportive here.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport improves kinetics by moving material through volatile halide species rather than bulk diffusion; this is supported by BiBr CVT at 272→165 °C (CS-5) and Hg halide gas-phase growth (TS-5). Starting from preformed AuI should reduce reconstruction to recrystallization [Reasonable Inference]. However, target-polymorph selection remains the major risk because BiI and AuBr analogues show multiple modifications, metastability, and difficult reproducibility (CS-1–CS-4; TS-2). The route is therefore kinetically promising for crystals but not guaranteed for the Pma2 phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a simple binary AuI with one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered from the target description. The described structure has two inequivalent Au and two inequivalent I sites, with no evidence for a very large >40-atom unit cell or complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network. CS/SS/TS analogues do not provide target-specific amorphization evidence; for these checks, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral source score 50. Physicochemically, heavy-metal halides often crystallize readily at low temperature, but polymorphism rather than amorphization is the likely issue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target AuI phase is likely **metastable but accessible**, because its E above hull is only 0.0290 eV/atom and comparable low-hull halide analogues are experimentally synthesized (CS-1–CS-4; TS-1–TS-5). The strongest support comes from AuBr, which is chemically and structurally close and can be prepared by controlled decomposition of Au₂Br₆ (CS-3, CS-4; SS-2; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the use of iodine-rich, sealed, dry/vacuum conditions to provide mobile Au–I chemistry at low temperature, as supported by AuBr decomposition and BiI/BiBr ampoule methods (CS-1–CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase control: BiI and AuBr analogues both show multiple modifications, metastability, sluggish transitions, or reproducibility problems (CS-1–CS-4; TS-2). Thus, synthesis is feasible, but isolating the specific orthorhombic Pma2 layered polymorph will likely require careful iodine chemical potential, slow annealing, and repeated PXRD screening.
