### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.011 eV/atom, placing it in the high-stability range of the provided rubric. CS analogues TaOsTe₄, TaIrTe₄, TaRhTe₄, and Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆ are near or on the hull, with CS-1 to CS-5 reporting E_hull values from 0 to ~0.00005 eV/atom. SS and TS also include stable or near-stable layered Ta tellurides such as TaTe₂ and Ta₂Te₃ (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-4). The main uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly confirms the Ta–Sn–Te ternary.

#### F2 — Route 1, direct solid-state reaction

Direct sealed-tube synthesis requires vacuum and sealed ampoules, which are repeatedly used for Ta/Nb/transition-metal tellurides in CS-3, CS-5, TS-2, and TS-3. The maximum recommended temperature is near 1000 °C, matching CS-3/CS-5 and avoiding the >1000 °C penalty if controlled carefully. The route is penalized for vacuum and volatile Te in a sealed system, both supported by CS/SS/TS. The uncertainty is whether Sn volatility or melt formation makes the actual ampoule pressure more difficult than in the Pd/Ir/Co analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state reaction

Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta, consistent with the long reaction times in CS-5 and TS-3. Te and Sn can provide liquid/vapor-mediated transport by **[Reasonable Inference]**, but CS-5 still reports impurity phases in Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆, indicating phase competition. The target layered motif is related to Ta–Te sheets in SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-4, but forming ordered Ta₃SnTe₆ from elements likely requires substantial reconstruction. This justifies a relatively low F3 despite acceptable F2.

#### F2 — Route 2, CVT

CVT avoids >1000 °C conditions and is directly supported for TaTe₂ at 873/773 K in SS-1/TS-1. It is also supported for layered TaMTe₄ analogues in CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-5, though those entries contain a precursor-snippet inconsistency. Deductions arise from vacuum, volatile/corrosive transport agents, multi-step ampoule preparation, and toxic halogens. These penalties are supported by the I₂ handling and evacuated ampoule procedure in SS-1/TS-1.

#### F3 — Route 2, CVT

CVT has better kinetic prospects because transport through volatile halide/telluride species can bypass slow solid-state Ta diffusion, as evidenced by single-crystal TaTe₂ growth in SS-1/TS-1. Layered TaMTe₄ analogues are also associated with CVT in CS-1/CS-2/CS-4 and SS-3/SS-5. Composition control is the limiting kinetic/process factor because Sn transport is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS and because CVT phase control is sensitive to gradient and transport-agent loading. The assigned F3 is therefore good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3, Ta–Te precursor route

This route inherits the vacuum and sealed-crucible requirements reported for Ta₂Te₃ synthesis from TaTe₂ + Ta in SS-2/TS-4. It also includes a multi-step penalty because TaTe₂/Ta₂Te₃ must be prepared before reaction with Sn/Te. Some reported Ta₂Te₃ conditions reach above 1000 °C in SS-2/TS-4, adding severity. The benefit is chemical preorganization, but not lower condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3, Ta–Te precursor route

Using TaTe₂ or Ta₂Te₃ should improve precursor activity relative to elemental Ta because Ta–Te bonds are already present, supported by the TaTe₂-to-Ta₂Te₃ conversion in SS-2/TS-4. However, SS-2/TS-4 also show that interconversion among Ta tellurides still needs high temperatures and days of annealing. Sn insertion into a layered Ta–Te framework is a **[Reasonable Inference]** and not directly supported by CS/SS/TS. Thus the route has intermediate kinetics but high uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline layered telluride with defined Ta, Sn, and Te coordination environments and no explicit indication of very large unit cell size or mixed face/edge/vertex sharing in the target description. CS/SS/TS analogues are crystalline layered tellurides, including TaTe₂ and TaMTe₄ phases (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5, CS-1, CS-2), which suggests low intrinsic amorphization tendency. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Ta/Sn radius mismatch and target conventional cell size, so these checks are treated conservatively as non-triggered rather than strongly favorable. The main amorphization risk would be kinetic glass formation during rapid quench, but the analogues generally crystallize under slow or extended thermal treatment.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃SnTe₆ is likely a near-ground-state or very mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.011 eV/atom, within the commonly synthesizable range. CS, SS, and TS analogues strongly support the accessibility of layered Ta–Te frameworks by both sealed-tube solid-state reaction and CVT, especially TaTe₂, Ta₂Te₃, TaIrTe₄, TaOsTe₄, and Ta₄Pd₃Te₁₆. The most important enabling factor is the rich layered Ta–Te chemistry and the availability of halogen-assisted transport routes shown in SS-1/TS-1 and CS-1/CS-2/CS-4. The most important limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control involving Sn, because none of the provided CS/SS/TS analogues directly contains Sn. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with CVT recommended first for crystals and sealed-tube solid-state reaction as a complementary bulk route.
