### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0090 eV/atom, which falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range; this directly supports a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies, including VNbF6 at 0.00857 eV/atom and CaTiF5 at 0.0119 eV/atom, are experimentally synthesized (TS1 / TS3). Structurally similar FeNbF6 and Nb2O2F3 are also near the hull and synthesized (SS2 / SS3 / SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability of Nb3SF7 does not guarantee kinetic accessibility of the specific 2D sulfide-fluoride sheet; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct Nb–S–F phase evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from moderate temperatures because VNbF6 and FeNbF6 form at 520 °C and 500 °C, respectively, in sealed copper tubes under inert gas (CS3 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2). The main penalties are sealed handling of volatile/moisture-sensitive fluorides, toxic/air-sensitive precursors, and likely precursor preparation steps, all consistent with fluoride synthesis complications in CS1 and CS3. There is no evidence that high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures are needed from CS/SS/TS. Uncertainty arises because sulfur volatility and Nb sulfide reactivity are inferred rather than directly shown in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The fluorides in CS2, CS3, SS2, TS1, and TS2 show that Nb–F frameworks can form at moderate temperature, indicating reasonable fluoride-mediated kinetics. However, Nb metal and Nb sulfides are expected to be less reactive than soluble or molecular precursors [Reasonable Inference], and formation of a 2D Nb3SF7 sheet from binary fluorides/sulfides likely requires substantial reconstruction. Long reaction times of 2–4 days or more in CS3 / SS1 / TS1 and SS2 / TS2 support a moderate-to-low kinetic score. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for kinetics of sulfide-fluoride layer formation specifically.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Flux growth is experimentally supported for reduced Nb oxyfluorides in sealed welded Nb containers and evacuated fused silica jackets (SS3 / SS5 / TS5) and for NaTiF4 in chloride flux at 800 °C under Ar with slow cooling (SS4). The conditions are not ultra-high-temperature or high-pressure, but they require sealed systems, controlled atmosphere, multiple handling/separation steps, and moisture-sensitive fluorides. These requirements justify a lower F2 than direct solid-state synthesis. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for flux synthesis of Nb–S–F compounds, so the flux route carries additional uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux should improve reaction kinetics by increasing mass transport, supported by Nb oxyfluoride flux synthesis in SS3 / SS5 / TS5 and fluoride crystal growth in SS4. This gives Route 2 a higher F3 than Route 1 despite more severe handling. However, the structural transformation from flux precursors to a 2D Nb3SF7 sheet remains nontrivial, and slow programmed cooling in SS4 suggests ordering and phase selection can be important. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that sulfide and fluoride species remain compatible in the same flux without side reactions.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is based on sealed Ta-tube fluoride synthesis with slow heating, long hold, and slow cooling at 730 °C for NaZr2TiF11 (TS4), plus sealed-tube fluoride methods from CS1–CS3 and SS1–SS2. The temperature remains below 1000 °C and no external high pressure is required, but the method requires arc-welded metal tubes, controlled atmosphere, volatile fluoride containment, and multistep handling. TS4 explicitly reports difficult precursor preparation and impurity issues, supporting the severity penalty. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether such long high-temperature treatment preserves Nb3SF7 rather than decomposing it.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Long-duration sealed redox chemistry can access complex fluorides, as shown by TS4, but the long 3-week treatment indicates slow equilibration and difficult phase control. Nb metal/sulfide diffusion remains kinetically limiting [Reasonable Inference], and the target layered sulfide-fluoride structure differs from the mainly 3D fluoride analogues in CS/SS/TS. Volatile fluoride transport may help, but may also drive competing phases. Therefore, Route 3 has similar or slightly worse kinetics than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a single cation type, Nb, so the cation-radius-mismatch check is not triggered; this is based on the target composition, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient additional source-specific signal and are treated neutral. The formula contains 11 atoms, and the described P3m1 sheet does not indicate a very large unit cell, so the atom-count check is not triggered. The target description does not state coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is not clearly triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that 2D materials can form stacking disorder, but that is not the same as bulk amorphization and is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb3SF7 is likely a near-ground-state or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0090 eV/atom, comparable to synthesized thermodynamic analogues such as VNbF6 (TS1) and CaTiF5 (TS3). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the feasibility of sealed-tube Nb fluoride chemistry, especially at 500–600 °C under inert atmosphere (CS3 / SS1 / TS1; SS2 / TS2), but they do not directly demonstrate Nb–S–F layered sulfide-fluoride synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive, anhydrous Nb fluoride precursors and sealed-tube conditions that retain volatile fluorine species. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Nb oxidation state, sulfur activity, and fluorine chemical potential without forming competing NbFx or NbSx phases. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but experimentally nontrivial, with the best initial attempt being a sealed-tube solid-state redox reaction.
