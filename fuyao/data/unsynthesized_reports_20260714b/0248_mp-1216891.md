### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom lies at the boundary of the highest scoring thermodynamic category, so a score of 90 is appropriate rather than 95–100 [Target]. TS analogues show experimentally synthesized U-containing phases with comparable or higher hull energies, including USi₂ at 0.112 eV/atom and UCoSi at 0.090 eV/atom [TS-1, TS-3], while CS analogues include multiple stable U–T–Si phases [CS-1 to CS-5]. SS provides structural analogues with similar U–Si cuboctahedral motifs but insufficient evidence from SS alone to determine whether the exact trigonal U₂RuSi₃ ordering is a ground state. Remaining uncertainty is the possible competition from stable URu₂Si₂, USi₂, and other U–Ru–Si compositions [CS-5, SS-2/TS-1].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for very high local arc-melting temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, and uranium toxicity/reactivity [CS-5, SS-5/TS-3]. It avoids high pressure and can be performed with standard metallurgy equipment, as arc melting is repeatedly demonstrated for U–T–Si and U–Si analogues [CS-2, CS-5, SS-2/TS-1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for exotic pressure, reactive gases, or flux chemistry, so no such penalties were applied. The main uncertainty is whether direct elemental melting equilibrates the trigonal phase or freezes in competing binary/ternary phases.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental U/Ru/Si precursors are dense metallic or semimetallic solids, giving modest precursor activity [CS-2, CS-5]. Arc melting improves macroscopic mixing, but Ru is refractory and long annealing is often used for UTSi phases, indicating slow ordering kinetics [SS-5/TS-3]. Structural reconstruction is partially helped by similarities to USi₂ and UCoSi cuboctahedral environments [SS-2/TS-1, SS-5/TS-3]. The remaining uncertainty is the diffusion length needed for Ru/Si ordering on the trigonal sublattice.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the strongest chemical precedent but the most steps: U–silicide preparation, mixing with Ru/Si, melting/quenching, and annealing [CS-1/SS-3, CS-3/SS-1]. It requires controlled Ar and evacuated-tube treatment but no high pressure, matching the analogues [CS-1/SS-3]. Uranium-bearing precursors impose toxicity and air-sensitivity penalties [CS/SS/TS all U analogues]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that a volatile sealed precursor is required; the sealed tube is primarily for inert annealing.

#### F3 — Route 2

The U₃Si₂ or USi₂ intermediate is kinetically advantageous because part of the U–Si network is already formed [CS-1/SS-3, CS-3/SS-1]. USi₂ is structurally close to the target’s U(2)Si₁₂ cuboctahedra and has similar U–Si distances near 2.96–3.06 Å [SS-2/TS-1], which should reduce reconstruction demand. Ru diffusion and ternary ordering remain limiting because Ru is refractory and the target has ordered U/Ru/Si coordination not identical to USi₂ [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Overall this route receives the best F3 among the proposed bulk routes.

#### F2 — Route 3

Melt growth requires specialized tri-arc/induction/Czochralski equipment and controlled inert atmosphere, as shown for UIrSi₃ and U₃Ru₂NiAl₃ [CS-3/SS-1, SS-4/TS-2]. It is not high-pressure chemistry, but it is operationally demanding because of high-temperature molten U-containing alloys [CS-3/SS-1]. Multi-step feed preparation and post-annealing are likely needed [Reasonable Inference from CS-3/SS-1]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms congruent melting of U₂RuSi₃, lowering confidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Liquid-state growth reduces diffusion barriers and can yield high-quality crystals for U intermetallics [SS-4/TS-2]. However, composition control can be difficult because U–Ru–Si may have competing phases, and UIrSi₃ itself was described as a special case in its series [CS-3/SS-1]. Structural reconstruction is not the limiting issue once molten, but controlled crystallization into the desired trigonal phase is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore Route 3 is better treated as a follow-up crystal-growth method after Route 2 confirms the phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with compact U/Ru/Si coordination and no evidence of a large complex unit cell [Target]. Only the U–Ru size mismatch check is triggered by reasonable metallic-radius comparison [Reasonable Inference], while the target description shows edge and face sharing but not the full vertex/edge/face coexistence criterion [Target]. CS/SS/TS do not directly discuss amorphization for these materials, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an empirical amorphization tendency; a neutral-to-favorable score is justified by the many crystalline U–T–Si analogues [CS, SS]. Remaining uncertainty is whether rapid quenching from melt could trap disorder before annealing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

U₂RuSi₃ is likely a **low-energy metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase**, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom [Target]. The CS evidence is strong for U–Ru–Si chemistry, especially URu₂Si₂ and the U₃Si₂ + Si + transition-element route that explicitly includes Ru [CS-5, CS-1]. The SS evidence is also supportive because USi₂ and UCoSi contain closely related U-centered cuboctahedral and 9-coordinate Si/transition-metal environments [SS-2, SS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is using a U–silicide precursor to pre-form the U–Si framework and reduce diffusion/reconstruction demands [CS-1/SS-3, CS-3/SS-1]. The main limiting factor is competing phase formation among stable U–Si and U–Ru–Si phases, requiring long annealing and careful stoichiometry control [CS-5, SS-2/TS-1, SS-5/TS-3].
