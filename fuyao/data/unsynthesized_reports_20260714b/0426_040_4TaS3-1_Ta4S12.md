### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly indicates ground-state thermodynamic stability in the provided dataset [Target]. CS supports synthesizability of nearby Ta sulfides because TaS₂ polymorphs have E_hull = 0.0–0.016 eV/atom and are experimentally synthesized [CS-2, CS-3]. SS/TS also support stability because close structural/thermodynamic analogues such as Ta₂PdS₆ and NbS₃ are reported at E_hull = 0.0 [SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5]. Remaining uncertainty is experimental phase selection among multiple Ta–S phases, which CS explicitly flags as difficult near TaS₂ [CS-2].

#### F2 and F3 — Route 1, sealed-tube solid-state

F2 is moderate because the route uses standard evacuated silica/quartz tubes, as in TaS₂ and related sulfides [CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2], but sulfur volatility requires sealed operation [CS-2, SS-2]. F3 is low-to-moderate because Ta metal is refractory and solid-state diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with reports of long annealing, homogenization, and repeated treatments for Ta sulfides [CS-2, SS-1, TS-1]. The main uncertainty is whether equilibrium TaS₃ forms cleanly before decomposing or converting to TaS₂-like compositions under sulfur loss [Reasonable Inference based on CS-2].

#### F2 and F3 — Route 2, iodine CVT

F2 is lower than solid-state because I₂ adds toxicity/corrosivity and requires a sealed two-zone ampoule [CS-3]. F3 is better than ordinary solid-state because vapor transport reduces long-range solid diffusion and is proven for TaS₂ single crystals [CS-3] and related Ta sulfides with I₂ [SS-4, TS-4]. The uncertainty is that CVT conditions optimized for TaS₂ may be too hot or sulfur-poor for TaS₃, so the gradient and sulfur chemical potential need optimization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 and F3 — Route 3, high-pressure HPHT

F2 scores high under the provided rubric because the closest analogue uses only 700 °C and 2 GPa, below the >5 GPa penalty threshold [SS-5, TS-5]. Practically, the route is still challenging because the source requires a cubic anvil device, carbon heaters, BN sleeves, and pyrophyllite cubes [SS-5, TS-5]. F3 is mixed: elemental precursors are simple [SS-5, TS-5], but Ta diffusion at 700 °C is slow [Reasonable Inference]; however, the very close NbS₃ P2₁/m structural match strongly improves the structural reconstruction score [SS-5, TS-5].

#### F2 and F3 — Route 4, halide flux

F2 is moderate because flux growth can occur at ~630 °C but still uses evacuated sealed quartz tubes and volatile sulfur [SS-3, TS-3]. F3 is moderate because molten halides improve mass transport, as shown for AgTaS₃ growth [SS-3, TS-3] and TaS₂-related KCl flux chemistry [CS-4]. The main uncertainty is phase purity: AgCl/LiCl flux is proven for Ag-containing AgTaS₃ [SS-3, TS-3], but binary TaS₃ may incorporate Ag or alkali cations unless flux chemistry is carefully selected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ta, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk [Target]. The structure is moderately complex, with two Ta and six S inequivalent sites, but not clearly above the >40 atom threshold from the supplied description [Target]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no additional signal on exact target cell size. Mixed edge/corner-sharing connectivity is present in the target [Target], and related layered Ta sulfides also show connected polyhedra [SS-4, TS-4], so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned. Overall the crystallization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaS₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The analogues strongly support synthesis: chemically similar TaS₂ is made from Ta + S in evacuated tubes and by I₂-CVT [CS-2, CS-3], while the closest structural analogue NbS₃ in monoclinic P2₁/m is made by HPHT at 700 °C and 2 GPa [SS-5, TS-5]. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur chemical potential during reaction, because sulfur-rich TaS₃ must compete against TaS₂/Ta-rich sulfides [Reasonable Inference supported by Ta–S phase-control concerns in CS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity and kinetics: Ta is refractory, solid-state diffusion is slow, and related Ta sulfides require long anneals, transport growth, or high-pressure methods [CS-2, CS-3, SS-5, TS-5]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but likely requires sealed sulfur management or HPHT conditions rather than a simple open-tube heat treatment.
