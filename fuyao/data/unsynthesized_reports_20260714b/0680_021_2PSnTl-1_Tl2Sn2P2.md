### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0490 eV/atom, just inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability bin. Thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS4 have similar small positive hull energies of ~0.035–0.047 eV/atom and reported synthesis routes, supporting metastable but accessible formation. CS analogues include several E_hull = 0.0 Tl-containing compounds, suggesting Tl-containing heavy-element phases can be stable, but the exact TlSnP phase is not experimentally confirmed in the provided data. Remaining uncertainty is whether TlSnP competes strongly with binary Tl–P, Sn–P, or elemental phases near this composition; insufficient direct phase-diagram evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, consistent with SS1 and TS1/TS2 where related phases form at 600–700 °C. The main penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile/toxic components: SS1 and CS5 use sealed ampoules for P-containing reactions, and Tl toxicity is a chemical handling concern [Reasonable Inference]. The staged program is extrapolated from CS5, which used a 400 °C hold before 800 °C annealing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure, so that item is neutral/no deduction.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

SS1 gives the strongest kinetic support: NaSnP forms from elements at ~650 °C after only 1 h, showing Sn–P network formation is not intrinsically sluggish. CS5 shows phosphide formation by elemental reaction at 800 °C, but requires 100 h, indicating heavier-element phosphides can need longer equilibration. The target’s Sn–P distances are close to SS1 and SS4, but the target’s Pmn2₁ layered topology differs from NaSnP, so structural reconstruction is only partially favorable. Remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity versus binary Sn–P/Tl–P phases.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 also avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, because CS2/SS5 flux growth occurs at 350–425 °C and CS5 phosphide flux growth occurs at 800 °C. It still requires vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and handling of volatile/toxic Tl/P reagents; those features are directly or indirectly supported by CS2/SS5, CS5, and SS1. The multi-step nature—pre-reaction, dissolution, slow cooling, possible flux separation—matches the complexity of CS2/SS5 and CS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact TlSnP flux composition remains a key uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because liquid Tl/Sn-rich media should reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and CS2/SS5 demonstrate Tl–Sn chalcogenide single-crystal growth using slow-cooling sealed-ampoule flux/melt conditions. CS5 shows that a heavy-metal flux can enable crystallization of a phosphide at 800 °C. The slow-cooling approach is especially suitable for the target’s two-dimensional layered structure, but no CS/SS/TS source proves that TlSnP has a broad crystallization window. The major uncertainty is maintaining 1:1:1 incorporation from a Tl/Sn-rich melt.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses room-temperature Ar milling from TS3 plus a moderate anneal comparable to SS1, so temperature severity is low. It still needs controlled atmosphere and sealed annealing due to P and Tl volatility/toxicity, as implied by SS1/CS5 sealed phosphide procedures and TS3 inert milling. Multi-step processing adds a penalty. Insufficient direct CS/SS evidence for mechanochemical Tl–Sn–P synthesis makes this less certain than Routes 1–2.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

TS3 supports mechanochemical activation of elemental powders and phase formation in an In–Te analogue under Ar. Such milling should reduce diffusion length before annealing [Reasonable Inference], which is helpful for low-temperature formation of TlSnP. However, TS3 also reports multiple phases and minor oxide, showing mechanochemical routes can have phase-control issues. Because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates mechanochemical Tl–Sn–P phosphide formation, this route is plausible but not preferred.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner- and edge-sharing PTl₂Sn₃ trigonal bipyramids, which directly triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion; CS2/SS5 shows analogous mixed edge/corner-sharing chalcogenide polyhedra can still crystallize. A cation-radius mismatch between Tl and Sn is likely significant [Reasonable Inference], and the target uses different Tl and Sn coordination environments, adding disorder risk. There is insufficient evidence that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that criterion is not triggered. The resulting F4 = 60 reflects medium risk: crystallization should be possible, but disorder/competing phases are plausible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlSnP is best viewed as a near-hull metastable phase rather than an unambiguously stable ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0490 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: SS1 directly supports ternary Sn–P solid-state formation, CS2/SS5 supports Tl–Sn low-temperature flux crystallization, CS5 supports heavy-element phosphide flux growth, and TS1/TS2 show similar small-positive-hull heavy-element phases can be made. The most critical enabling factor is the low melting/reactive nature of Tl/Sn-containing systems, which should allow flux or melt-mediated diffusion at moderate temperatures. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under sealed-tube conditions because both Tl toxicity/volatility and P vapor pressure can drive off-stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, TlSnP appears moderately likely to be synthesizable, with flux/slow-cooling routes preferred over a simple one-step firing.
