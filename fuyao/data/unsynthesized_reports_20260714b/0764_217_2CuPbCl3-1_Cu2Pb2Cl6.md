### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0110 eV/atom, which falls well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues are similarly close to the hull, including CsBi2Br7 at 0.0104 eV/atom and Bi3(TeCl5)2 at 0.0285 eV/atom [TS-4; TS-1]. Structurally similar Pd(PbCl3)2 is reported at E_hull = 0 [SS-2], strengthening the case that Pb–transition-metal halide frameworks can be stable. Remaining uncertainty is that low hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility of the exact trigonal P-31m Cu–Pb sheet [Target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 uses conditions directly supported by Pb–transition-metal halide analogues: evacuated quartz ampoule, melting, quenching, and annealing at 300 °C [SS-2; TS-2]. F2 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic halides, and multiple steps, but not for high temperature or high pressure [SS-2; TS-2]. Kinetically, molten or partially molten chlorides should react faster than oxide solid-state systems [Reasonable Inference], but the target requires ordering into a 2D CuPbCl3 sheet with Cu–Cu contacts, which is not directly demonstrated in SS/TS [Target; insufficient evidence from SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by chemically similar Cu chloride melt growth: TlCuCl3 was grown by vertical Bridgman at 450 °C under Ar with controlled translation [CS-1]. Similar halide Bridgman growth in evacuated quartz is also reported for TlCoCl3 [CS-2]. F2 is moderate because of sealed/inert handling and toxic/air-sensitive chloride precursors, while F3 is favorable because melt growth removes most solid-state diffusion barriers [CS-1; CS-2]. The main uncertainty is whether CuPbCl3 crystallizes congruently or peritectically, since no CS/SS/TS source gives a Cu–Pb–Cl phase diagram [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Aqueous/hydrothermal crystallization is supported for some Pb and transition-metal chloride analogues: K–Pb chloride crystals form on slow cooling a boiling aqueous solution [SS-5], and Tl2PdCl4 forms hydrothermally at 150 °C for 2 d in sealed evacuated quartz [SS-3]. F2 is relatively mild due to low temperature, but sealed oxygen-limited conditions and toxic Pb compounds remain concerns [SS-3; SS-5]. F3 is mixed: ion transport in solution is favorable, but CuCl solubility and Cu(I) redox stability are problematic [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that an aqueous route can produce a Cu–Pb–Cl sheet with Cu–Cu bonding.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Flux synthesis is supported by low-temperature chloride molten salt growth, especially Na[AlCl4]/AlCl3 flux at 130 °C for structurally similar halide phases [SS-4]. Chemically similar TlGaCl4/TlAlCl4 form in sealed ampoules at only 150–200 °C [CS-4; CS-5]. F2 is penalized for dry inert handling, volatile/corrosive AlCl3 flux, and multiple operations [SS-4]. F3 is high because chloride fluxes provide rapid ion mobility, but the available flux analogues are largely 0D cluster compounds rather than 2D Cu–Pb sheets, leaving structural selectivity uncertain [SS-4; Target].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines small Cu⁺ coordination with larger Pb²⁺ octahedral coordination, which triggers the ionic-size mismatch check [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The structure is not described as having a very large unit cell, and the target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing [Target]. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization, so the score is mainly based on target structural descriptors and general crystallization chemistry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuPbCl3 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0110 eV/atom [Target]. The analogue support is reasonably strong: chemically similar Cu chlorides are melt-grown by Bridgman [CS-1], and structurally/thermodynamically similar Pb–transition-metal halides are made by sealed-ampoule melt–quench–anneal methods [SS-2; TS-2]. The most important enabling factor is the high mobility of chloride melts/fluxes, which should allow Cu/Pb ordering at moderate temperature [CS-1; SS-2; SS-4]. The most important limiting factor is Cu(I) stoichiometry/redox control, because the target likely requires Cu⁺ and contains a short Cu–Cu contact of 2.71 Å [Target; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthetically feasible, with the best route being controlled sealed-ampoule melt growth rather than open-atmosphere solid-state heating.
