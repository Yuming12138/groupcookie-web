### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target Ag2Se has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score [target]. Thermodynamic analogues include several on-hull compounds, such as Ag8SnSe6 and Ag2HgSI2, showing that Ag/chalcogen-containing compounds can be thermodynamically stable [TS1; TS5]. CS analogues include both stable and metastable compounds, so they support general feasibility but not exclusively ground-state behavior [CS2/CS3/CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: E_hull supports the composition/structure computationally, but synthesis may still produce another Ag2Se polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis

The condition severity is moderate because evacuated sealed-tube synthesis is repeatedly used for Ag-containing chalcogenides [CS5/SS3/TS1]. The main deductions come from vacuum sealing, Se volatility, multi-step heating/annealing, and Se toxicity [CS5/SS3/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not indicate that external high pressure is needed for Ag–Se binaries, so high-pressure severity is not applied [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure need]. Uncertainty is the exact temperature required to obtain the tetragonal polymorph rather than another Ag2Se phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis

Kinetics are acceptable because Ag has a relatively low melting point and Ag chalcogenides often form at moderate temperatures [Reasonable Inference; SS4; CS5/SS3/TS1]. However, starting from dense elemental Ag requires full chemical reconstruction, and analogues frequently use long anneals or controlled cooling [CS2/SS5; CS5/SS3/TS1]. Se can enhance reaction through vapor/liquid transport, but this also increases sealed-ampoule risk [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2: Pre-reacted Ag2Se plus ordering anneal

This route avoids very high temperature during the final phase-selection step, but still uses vacuum ampoules and multi-step processing, matching Ag3SI and Ag8SnSe6 protocols [CS2/SS5; CS5/SS3/TS1]. Volatile Se is less severe than in full melt growth if only a small Se buffer is used, but a sealed system remains prudent [Reasonable Inference]. Toxicity and stoichiometry control remain relevant because selenium-containing powders are used [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports this exact Ag2Se polymorph anneal, so the severity score retains uncertainty [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2: Pre-reacted Ag2Se plus ordering anneal

Kinetics are favorable because the chemical composition is already formed and the main task is ordering/polymorph selection [Reasonable Inference]. Ag3SI and Ag8SnSe6 show that long low-temperature anneals can stabilize ordered Ag-containing phases [CS2/SS5; CS5/SS3/TS1]. The limiting kinetic factor is possible slow ordering within a silver sublattice or transformation between Ag2Se polymorphs [Reasonable Inference]. This route scores higher than direct elements because it minimizes long-range interdiffusion.

#### F2 — Route 3: Melt growth / Bridgman crystallization

The melt route requires sealed ampoule handling, controlled thermal gradients, and Se volatility management, consistent with Ag3SI Bridgman growth in quartz ampoules [CS3/SS2]. It does not require external high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures under the proposed conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The CS/SS/TS analogues show melt growth can yield crystals but also mixed phases, requiring separation or post-anneal [CS3/SS2]. Severity is therefore moderate-to-challenging despite favorable thermodynamics.

#### F3 — Route 3: Melt growth / Bridgman crystallization

Reaction kinetics are the best among bulk routes because the liquid state removes most solid diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. Ag3SI single-crystal growth demonstrates transferability of sealed ampoule melt processing to Ag-containing chalcogenide/halide systems [CS3/SS2]. The structural reconstruction score is reduced because crystallization from melt may select the high-temperature Ag2Se polymorph unless the cooling/anneal schedule is tuned [Reasonable Inference]. Phase separation risk is supported by the Ag3SI report of mixed phases after melt growth [CS3/SS2].

#### F2 — Route 4: Supercritical ethylenediamine synthesis

Supercritical ethylenediamine routes operate at only 300–340 °C, but require autoclaves, sealed tubes, Ar counterpressure, and hazardous solvent handling [SS1/TS3/TS4]. The scoring rubric does not penalize 2400 psi as “high pressure” because it is far below 5 GPa, but operationally the route is still specialized [SS1/TS3/TS4]. Subcritical reactions failed for KAg3S2 analogues, showing that conditions are not freely interchangeable [SS1/TS4]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for binary Ag2Se solvothermal synthesis, so transferability is uncertain [insufficient evidence from CS].

#### F3 — Route 4: Supercritical ethylenediamine synthesis

Kinetics are strong because supercritical solvent and polyselenide chemistry provide high transport and reactivity at low temperature [TS3; SS1/TS4]. This route is promising for low-dimensional Ag chalcogenide frameworks, as structurally and thermodynamically similar analogues form under these conditions [SS1/TS3/TS4]. The score is reduced because the reported products are ternary alkali silver chalcogenides, not binary Ag2Se [TS3; SS1/TS4]. The main uncertainty is whether alkali/polyselenide chemistry can be excluded from the final binary phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 5: Thin-film deposition
