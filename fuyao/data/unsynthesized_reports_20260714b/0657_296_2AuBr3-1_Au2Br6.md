### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target data. CS strongly supports stable binary/molecular halides because IrBr3, AlI3, IrCl3, and OsCl4 are listed as E_hull = 0 (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). TS also supports stability because the closest thermodynamic analogues include hull-stable AuTeI, PdBr2, Pd–Te–Br, and Te/Ga/Al iodides (TS-1 to TS-5). SS is less consistent because some structurally similar materials have significant E_hull values, e.g., Sb2Br9 and CdCl4 (SS-1, SS-5), so SS provides only partial support.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure, unlike the high-temperature IrBr3 and PdBr2 examples that operate at 700–900 °C (CS-1, TS-2), because lower temperature is inferred for thermally sensitive AuBr3 **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main penalties are the sealed/vacuum or inert ampoule, volatile Br2, and toxic/corrosive bromine handling, all of which are supported as necessary for analogous halides in CS-1, CS-2, and TS-2. No ≥3-step process is required, although optional sublimation purification is analogous to BiBr3 and AlI3 workflows (CS-2, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is the exact temperature window before AuBr3 decomposes or reduces.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because Au metal is relatively inert, but Br2 is a highly reactive brominating agent; analogous direct halogenation reactions form IrBr3 and BiBr3 (CS-1, CS-2). The diffusion burden is reduced because Br2 vapor/liquid can react at surfaces and transport halide species, analogous to closed bromine transport in PdBr2 (TS-2). The main uncertainty is whether the Pc 1D AuBr3 ribbon nucleates directly or whether AuBr/Au side products form first **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not severe in temperature, because it is inferred to use low-temperature sublimation/CVT analogous to AlI3 at 100–150 °C (CS-3), not high-temperature PdBr2 CVT at 700 °C (TS-2). It still requires a sealed ampoule, Br2 vapor, and dry handling, consistent with TS-2, CS-3, TS-4, and TS-5. The route is operationally moderate because preformed AuBr3 removes the need for full chemical synthesis during the growth step. Remaining uncertainty is AuBr3 vapor stability during transport.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable because transport of preformed AuBr3 bypasses solid-state diffusion and only requires recrystallization. This is strongly analogous to PdBr2 CVT from powder with Br2 (TS-2), IrCl3 recrystallization/transport from powder (CS-4), and sublimation growth of AlI3-type halides (CS-3). Structural reconstruction is minimal, so this route is likely best for single-crystal quality if AuBr3 powder is available. The main uncertainty is controlling Br2 chemical potential to prevent decomposition or over-transport **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild temperature and no extreme pressure, consistent with hydrothermal AuTeI in hydroiodic acid (SS-2, TS-1) and HBr-based precipitation/recrystallization of bromide compounds (SS-1). It is penalized for volatile/toxic Br2 and corrosive HBr, and likely requires a sealed vessel. The rubric does not penalize moderate autogenous hydrothermal pressure, so F2 remains high. The main uncertainty is whether an anhydrous/neutral AuBr3 phase rather than solvated or ionic Au bromide forms.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution kinetics should be good because Au–Br species can dissolve, transport, and crystallize without bulk diffusion; this is consistent with solution precipitation in SS-1 and hydrothermal growth in SS-2/TS-1. Au oxidation/dissolution may be slow from metal, so HAuBr4 or preoxidized Au(III) bromide solution is recommended as a **[Reasonable Inference]**. Structural reconstruction is moderately favorable but less certain than Route 2 because solution chemistry may favor AuBr4− salts rather than neutral AuBr3. This uncertainty motivates the −3 expert adjustment.

The target has one cation and a simple binary composition, so the cation-radius-mismatch check is not triggered. The described structure is a 1D AuBr3 ribbon with terminal and bridging bromides, and there is no evidence for a >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity from the target description. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for amorphization tendencies in AuBr3, so they are treated as neutral for this factor. Physicochemically, low-dimensional molecular/chain halides generally crystallize readily by vapor or solution transport if decomposition is avoided **[Reasonable Inference]**.

AuBr3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom.
