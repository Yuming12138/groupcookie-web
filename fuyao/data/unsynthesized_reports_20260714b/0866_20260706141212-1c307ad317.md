Thermodynamic analogues include Nd(PO3)3, Pr(PO3)3, BaSiP4(HO7)2, AlPO4, and NaCa(PO3)3 at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), reinforcing that phosphate/metaphosphate frameworks are thermodynamically accessible. CS is slightly mixed because SiP2O7 and Cu3(P2O7)2 are above hull by ~0.06–0.09 eV/atom, but chemically close AlPO4 and GeP2O7 are stable (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee the cubic I-43d polymorph is the kinetically preferred product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Phosphoric-acid flux

The condition severity is low-to-moderate because SS/TS flux analogues form at 530 K–930 °C without high pressure or special atmosphere (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2). The main F2 deduction is multi-step handling: dissolution/mixing, heating/slow cooling, and washing, explicitly reflected in Gd/Pr flux procedures (SS1, SS3). Kinetically, the route is favorable because the liquid phosphate flux improves precursor activity and diffusion, as demonstrated by single-crystal growth of Gd, Nd, and Pr metaphosphates (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2). The uncertainty is whether small Al3+ will adopt the target octahedral framework rather than stable tetrahedral AlPO4 motifs (target structure; CS4–CS5; TS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Solid-state dehydration

The F2 score is high because Lu(PO3)3 and NaCa(PO3)3 analogues use ordinary furnace heating in air or unspecified standard atmospheres at ≤1000 °C (SS5; TS5). The multi-step deduction is justified by staged decomposition/heating in Lu and NaCa routes (SS5; TS5). Kinetics are less favorable than flux because solid-state formation from Al2O3/ammonium phosphate requires long-range reconstruction and Al2O3 can be refractory [Reasonable Inference]. A key uncertainty is phase competition with AlPO4, which is stable and readily crystallized hydrothermally (CS4–CS5; TS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal/solvothermal

The F2 score is high because Cu phosphate hydrothermal synthesis occurs at 152 °C for 24 h and AlPO4 hydrothermal growth is directly reported, with no high pressure beyond autogenous hydrothermal pressure (CS3–CS5; TS4). The multi-step deduction is for loading, heating, cooling, washing, and drying, consistent with CS3. Kinetics are strong because solution-mediated transport can overcome diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and CS3 shows phosphate formation from oxide plus H3PO4 at low temperature. The limiting uncertainty is structural: AlPO4 hydrothermal analogues favor AlO4 tetrahedra, whereas the target requires AlO6 octahedra (CS4–CS5; target structure).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-pressure/high-temperature

The F2 score is low because CS1 uses 16 GPa and 2000 °C, triggering high-temperature and high-pressure penalties. Kinetics and structural matching are nevertheless good because CS1 demonstrates small-cation SiO6 octahedra linked to PO4 tetrahedra, closely paralleling the target AlO6–PO4 framework (CS1; target structure). Water mineralizer and high temperature should accelerate reconstruction (CS1). The uncertainty is whether such severe conditions are necessary for Al(PO3)3 or whether they might instead produce another dense Al phosphate polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Chemical vapor transport

The F2 score is moderate-to-low because CS2 requires sealed ampoules, Cl2 transport, long duration, and multiple precursor-processing steps. The route has plausible kinetic advantages for crystal growth because GeP2O7 crystals were obtained in the cooler zone of a CVT gradient (CS2). However, transferability to Al is uncertain because no Al phosphate CVT example is provided, and Al chloride volatility may cause stoichiometry drift [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports CVT specifically, so SS and TS are neutral for this route-specific method signal.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target consists of corner-sharing AlO6 octahedra and PO4 tetrahedra, with no edge- or face-sharing described, which lowers topological frustration relative to mixed sharing networks (target structure). The large cation size/coordination contrast between Al3+ and P5+ and likely large cubic cell introduce medium amorphization/glass risk [Reasonable Inference]. Phosphate systems can form glasses under dehydrating conditions, so controlled cooling or seeding may be important [Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS do not directly report amorphization behavior for Al(PO3)3, so they provide insufficient direct evidence and are treated as neutral for this subfactor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Al(PO3)3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very low-risk phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar M(PO3)3 metaphosphates grown by phosphoric-acid flux and solid-state routes (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2, TS5). The most enabling factor is the phosphate-rich flux chemistry, which has repeatedly produced metaphosphate single crystals at accessible temperatures and ambient pressure (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: known AlPO4 analogues strongly favor tetrahedral AlO4 frameworks, whereas the target requires AlO6 octahedra (CS4–CS5; TS4; target structure). If ambient-pressure flux and hydrothermal routes fail, the high-pressure route is structurally well motivated by the SiO6 phosphate analogue but much more difficult (CS1).
