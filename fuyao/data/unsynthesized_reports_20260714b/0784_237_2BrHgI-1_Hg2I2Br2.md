### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0060 eV/atom, which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability range by the required rubric. Exact-composition HgBrI is experimentally reported and has similarly low E_hull ≈0.0105 eV/atom (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1), while 2D HgI₂ is nearly on the hull at ≈0.0006 eV/atom (SS-1; TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a different polymorph from the reported exact HgBrI, so thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee selective crystallization of Pmc2₁ sheets (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route is supported by exact HgBrI formation from iodine plus mercurous halides (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1). CS/SS/TS do not provide temperature, time, or atmosphere for that exact synthesis, so temperature and sealing requirements are inferred from iodine/Hg-halide volatility [Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS-1/SS-2/TS-1 for exact conditions]. The severity score is lowered mainly for recommended vacuum/sealed handling, volatile iodine, and mercury toxicity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be better than oxide solid-state reactions because Hg halides and iodine are reactive molecular/ionic solids [Reasonable Inference], and exact HgBrI formation is reported directly in the solid state (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1). The main kinetic/structural penalty is polymorphic reconstruction: the reported HgBrI is 0D linear molecular Cmc2₁, whereas the target is a 2D corner-sharing tetrahedral Pmc2₁ sheet (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1 versus target). This makes the route chemically direct but structurally uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2

Sealed ampoule heating at moderate temperature is strongly supported by Hg₃S₂I₂/Hg₃Se₂I₂ syntheses at 260 °C for 2–3 days and Hg–Se–I systems in evacuated ampoules at 820–960 K plus annealing (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; SS-3, SS-5; TS-2). No high pressure or >1000 °C conditions are needed in the analogues (CS-2 to CS-4; TS-2). Deductions arise from vacuum ampoules, volatile Hg halides, and mercury toxicity [Reasonable Inference; ampoule use supported by CS/SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2

The reaction kinetics are favorable because binary mercury halides are more mobile/reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and related Hg halide/chalcogenide compounds crystallize in sealed ampoules at 260 °C for 2–3 days (CS-3, CS-4; SS-3, SS-5). Structurally, 2D HgI₂ contains corner-sharing HgI₄ tetrahedra with Hg–I ≈2.83–2.86 Å, matching the target Hg–I distance of 2.83 Å (SS-1; TS-5). The uncertainty is whether Br/I ordering in HgI₂Br₂ tetrahedra produces the Pmc2₁ target rather than the known 0D HgBrI polymorph (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 3

The solution route is mild because structurally similar HgI₂ was crystallized simply by cooling a saturated 2-chloroethanol solution from boiling (SS-1; TS-5). There is no direct evidence from CS for solution-grown HgBrI, so the mixed-halide extension is an inference. Only toxicity receives a formal deduction; nonetheless, practical safety constraints remain significant because mercury halides and 2-chloroethanol are hazardous [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution crystallization gives high precursor activity and essentially eliminates solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and a 2D HgI₂ sheet polymorph crystallizes from solution (SS-1; TS-5). This is the best route for accessing a layered tetrahedral Hg-halide network because it can favor crystal growth at low temperature rather than thermally driving the system to another polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is polymorph control, since the orange HgI₂ form was metastable and transformed on touching or aging (SS-1; TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT/gas-phase growth is supported for Hg₃S₂ICl in evacuated quartz ampoules (SS-4; TS-4) and for BiSeI by chemical transport at 600 °C for 1 week (CS-5). The route does not require high pressure or extreme temperature based on analogues (CS-5; SS-4; TS-4), but vacuum ampoules and volatile toxic precursors lower F2. Evidence for exact HgBrI CVT is absent, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for optimal transport temperature of the target.

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor availability is plausible because Hg halide-containing materials can be grown from the gas phase in evacuated ampoules (SS-4; TS-4). The crystallization pathway is moderate because CVT can produce single crystals in analogues (CS-5; SS-4; TS-4), but no source demonstrates vapor growth of Pmc2₁ HgBrI. Composition control is the limiting kinetic factor because Br and I species may transport at different rates, potentially producing Br-rich/I-rich deposits or the 0D HgBrI polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hg, so the cation-size-mismatch amorphization trigger is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description specifies corner-sharing HgI₂Br₂ tetrahedra and does not describe edge- or face-sharing connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is absent. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, but the described simple Pmc2₁ sheet motif suggests a small cell [Reasonable Inference], so F4 is high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgBrI in the target Pmc2₁ layered form is likely a very low-energy metastable polymorph rather than an obviously unique ground-state phase, because its E_hull is only 0.0060 eV/atom while an exact-composition HgBrI polymorph is already known experimentally in a different 0D Cmc2₁ structure (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1). The CS and TS evidence strongly support the chemical existence of HgBrI, while the SS and TS evidence from 2D HgI₂ strongly supports the plausibility of a layered corner-sharing Hg-halide sheet (SS-1; TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and crystallizability of mercury halides, demonstrated by direct solid-state HgBrI formation (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1), solution-grown HgI₂ sheets (SS-1; TS-5), and sealed-ampoule Hg halide/chalcogenide syntheses (CS-2 to CS-4; SS-3 to SS-5; TS-2 to TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control: the system may crystallize as molecular HgBrI or segregate toward HgBr₂/HgI₂-related phases instead of the desired 2D Pmc2₁ sheet [Reasonable Inference; competing exact polymorph supported by CS-1/SS-2/TS-1]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but should be approached as polymorph-selective crystal growth rather than merely phase formation.

|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Mercury toxicity and containment | High | Use sealed ampoules or closed solution systems, secondary containment, small batches, and fume-hood/glovebox handling [Reasonable Inference]; evacuated ampoules are common in related syntheses (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4; SS-3, SS-4, SS-5; TS-2, TS-4). |
| 2 | All | Volatile halide/Hg-halide component loss | High | Use evacuated sealed ampoules for thermal routes, minimal headspace, and slight calibrated excess of volatile component [Reasonable Inference]; sealed ampoules are used for Hg halide/chalcogenide analogues (CS-2 to CS-4; SS-3 to SS-5; TS-2, TS-4). |
| 3 | All | Competing 0D molecular HgBrI polymorph | High | Favor low-temperature solution or slow crystallization to explore metastable layered growth; exact 0D HgBrI exists by solid-state route (CS-1; SS-2; TS-1), while 2D HgI₂ sheets crystallize from solution (SS-1; TS-5). |
| 4 | Route 2 / Route 4 | Br:I stoichiometry drift during annealing or vapor transport | Medium–High | Use 1:1 starting ratios, temperature gradients as small as possible, and post-synthesis compositional analysis [Reasonable Inference]; gas-phase ampoule growth is known for Hg₃S₂ICl but can be condition-sensitive (SS-4; TS-4). |
| 5 | Route 3 | Solution speciation may favor molecular complexes rather than sheet crystals | Medium | Screen solvent, concentration, cooling rate, and seed crystals from HgI₂-like layered phases [Reasonable Inference]; HgI₂ solution crystallization demonstrates solvent-mediated access to a 2D sheet but also metastability (SS-1; TS-5). |
| 6 | Route 1 | Incomplete solid-state reaction or iodine inhomogeneity | Medium | Fine grinding, repeated gentle regrinding under controlled conditions, and slow annealing [Reasonable Inference]; the source only reports solid-state formation without detailed conditions, so evidence is insufficient from CS-1/SS-2/TS-1 for optimized kinetics. |
| 7 | Route 4 | Crystal growth difficulty and halide fractionation in vapor phase | Medium | Use two-zone ampoule with low source temperature and small gradient; analyze source/residue to tune Br:I transport [Reasonable Inference]; evacuated quartz ampoule gas-phase growth is supported for Hg₃S₂ICl (SS-4; TS-4). |
| 8 | All | Light or mechanical sensitivity of Hg halide polymorphs | Medium | Store products in dark, minimize handling, use low-temperature XRD/Raman where possible [Reasonable Inference]; related Hg₃S₂I₂/Hg₃Se₂I₂ decompose under daylight (CS-3, CS-4; SS-3, SS-5), and orange HgI₂ transforms on touching/aging (SS-1; TS-5). |
