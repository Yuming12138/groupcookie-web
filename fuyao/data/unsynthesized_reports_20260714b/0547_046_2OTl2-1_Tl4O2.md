### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which gives a high intrinsic stability score by the required rubric. This is reinforced by the closest Tl₂O analogue, which has essentially zero hull energy and is present in all three similarity sets [CS1; SS1; TS1]. Remaining uncertainty is not whether Tl₂O is thermodynamically viable, but whether the specific trigonal P-3m1 stacking can be isolated rather than relaxing to the known monoclinic C2/m stacking [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because the closest Tl₂O synthesis requires 600 °C for 14 days under Ar and/or vacuum-compatible containment [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The temperature itself is not extreme, and no high pressure is required [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The difficulty comes from toxic Tl reagents, long sealed/inert processing, and sensitivity to oxidation/disproportionation [CS1; SS1; TS1].

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

The precursor set is only moderately active because Tl metal, Tl₂O₃, and Tl₂CO₃ are not as reactive as nitrate/acetate/sol-gel precursors [CS1; SS1; TS1]. Cation diffusion should be easier than in refractory multi-cation oxides because only Tl is present and structurally related Tl oxides are synthesized below 650 °C [CS1; SS1; TS1; SS2; TS2]. However, the reported 14-day anneal implies slow equilibration or ordering, so structural-reconstruction kinetics are not ideal [CS1; SS1; TS1].

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids full chemical formation and uses preformed Tl₂O, so the number of active reaction steps is lower [Reasonable Inference]. The 450–650 °C sealed Ag crucible conditions are directly supported for omega-derived Tl oxides [SS2; TS2], and 600 °C is supported for Tl₂O itself [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The route is still penalized for controlled inert/sealed handling and toxic/air-sensitive Tl₂O [CS1; SS1; TS1].

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Using preformed Tl₂O gives the correct composition and local Tl–O bonding from the start, which should reduce diffusion and reconstruction barriers [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Structurally, the target and known Tl₂O share 2D Tl₂O sheets, T-shaped Tl coordination, and edge-sharing OTl₆ octahedra [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether the P-3m1 stacking is accessible by annealing or whether it transforms to the more commonly observed C2/m stacking [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

The proposed thin-film route avoids high bulk temperatures and high pressure [Reasonable Inference]. However, it requires vacuum/controlled atmosphere, toxic Tl handling, and likely composition compensation because Tl can be volatile under deposition/annealing conditions [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS, insufficient evidence from SS, and insufficient evidence from TS for validated thin-film Tl₂O conditions.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

The thin-film route could in principle stabilize a 2D trigonal stacking by substrate effects [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates direct crystallization of Tl₂O films, so crystallization and composition control are uncertain [insufficient evidence from CS; insufficient evidence from SS; insufficient evidence from TS]. The narrow Tl/O chemical potential window inferred from the closest Tl₂O analogue’s oxidation/disproportionation sensitivity makes kinetics and stoichiometry control challenging [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger [target structure]. The P-3m1 Tl₂O cell is small and structurally simple, and the target description gives edge-sharing OTl₆ octahedra rather than mixed connectivity [target structure]. The main risk is not glass formation but competing crystalline Tl–O phases or stacking variants [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Tl₂O analogue is also essentially on the hull [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The CS, SS, and TS datasets strongly support synthesis of Tl₂O chemistry and very similar 2D omega-like Tl–O sheets, especially through sealed inert/vacuum solid-state processing [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of an almost identical experimentally synthesized Tl₂O analogue with matching local coordination and low thermodynamic energy [CS1; SS1; TS1]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: the desired trigonal P-3m1 stacking may compete with the known monoclinic C2/m Tl₂O stacking, and Tl₂O is sensitive to oxidation, disproportionation, and impurities [CS1; SS1; TS1]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but isolation of the exact trigonal polymorph will require careful atmosphere, stoichiometry, and thermal-history control.
