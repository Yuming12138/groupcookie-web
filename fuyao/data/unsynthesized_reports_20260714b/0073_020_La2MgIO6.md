### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the boundary of the highest stability scoring bin. TS analogues are especially relevant because TS1, TS3, TS4, and TS5 include synthesized materials at similar E_hull values of ~0.056–0.075 eV/atom, and TS2 includes a La–O–I phase at 0.098 eV/atom. CS and SS analogues are generally stable or near-stable, but they do not exactly match the iodine-containing Mg–La–O–I chemistry, so they support feasibility only indirectly.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because CS1 uses sealed ampoules under dynamic vacuum/inert Ar and long heating, and iodine-containing reagents are expected to require sealed conditions due to volatility [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids >1000 °C, which is favorable compared with CS3/CS4/TS3 high-temperature ceramic routes. Remaining uncertainty is high because no provided solid-state example directly synthesizes Mg–La–O–I.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate to poor because MgO and La₂O₃ are relatively refractory oxide precursors, and CS1 reports difficulty obtaining single-phase rare-earth oxyhalide/oxyanion samples. Flux assistance may improve transport, as in CS1, but the target’s P1 structure and iodine retention require substantial reconstruction and stoichiometry control. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for solid-state iodide/iodate formation, so it is not used to raise this kinetic score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from low temperature, directly consistent with TS2’s 453 K hydrothermal La–O–I synthesis. It is penalized for evacuated sealed ampoules, HI volatility/corrosivity, autogenous pressure, and multi-step pH-sensitive processing, all explicitly or implicitly supported by TS2. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for hydrothermal iodine chemistry and are treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated transport bypasses slow cation diffusion, and TS2 demonstrates La–O–I formation at low temperature. The main kinetic uncertainty is Mg incorporation, since TS2 contains La–I–O but no Mg, while CS2 only supports Mg–O coordination in a different oxide family. The structural reconstruction score is therefore reduced despite favorable precursor activity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the best condition-severity score because SS1–SS5 demonstrate complex lanthanide oxide growth at only 600–625 °C without high pressure. Deductions remain for corrosive hydroxide flux, sealed handling, iodine volatility, and post-reaction flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hydroxide flux iodine chemistry and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux kinetics are strong because SS1–SS5 show molten hydroxides can dissolve lanthanide oxides and crystallize complex Ln oxides rapidly relative to ceramic methods. TS2 provides the missing chemical evidence that La–O–I bonding is accessible, while CS2 supports Mg–O coordination compatibility. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: no provided flux-grown analogue contains Mg–La–I–O, and iodine redox/volatility may shift the product toward simpler La–I–O or MgO phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically and size-distinct Mg, La, and iodine cations, so cation-size mismatch is a concern [Reasonable Inference]. The target description explicitly includes mixed corner- and edge-sharing OLa₂MgI polyhedra, which increases structural complexity, although CS1, CS2, SS1, TS4, and TS5 show that mixed connectivity can still crystallize. The unit-cell atom count is not clearly >40 from the supplied target description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for this check, so it is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgLa₂O₆I is likely a low-energy metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, not clearly a deep ground state. The strongest support comes from TS2, which directly establishes La–O–I bonding, and from SS1–SS5, which show that complex lanthanide oxides can crystallize from hydroxide fluxes at moderate temperature. CS evidence supports general rare-earth oxyhalide/oxyanion synthesis and Mg–O coordination compatibility, but it does not directly prove Mg–La–I–O phase formation. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution/flux transport that preserves iodine while allowing La/Mg/O framework assembly. The most critical limiting factor is iodine stoichiometry/redox control, because iodine species are volatile/reactive and competing La–I–O phases are plausible.
