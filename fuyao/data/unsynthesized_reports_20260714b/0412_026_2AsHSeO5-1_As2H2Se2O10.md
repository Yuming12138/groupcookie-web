### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0230 eV/atom, which is within the common experimentally accessible near-hull range and receives a high score. TS analogues with similar E_hull values, including CuTlHSeO5 at ~0.019 eV/atom and VH2SeO5 at ~0.032 eV/atom, have reported syntheses [TS]. CS gives mixed support because one chemically similar entry is highly unstable while several others are stable [CS]. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not include hydration/proton ordering and solution speciation effects.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis is strongly supported by CS, SS, and TS analogues using SeO2, H2SeO4, water, and Teflon-lined autoclaves at 160–230 °C [CS/SS/TS]. Severity is moderate because autogenous pressure is not extreme, but SeO2 and arsenic reagents are toxic and volatile enough to require sealed handling [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics should be favorable because solution transport avoids refractory solid-state diffusion [CS/SS/TS]. The main uncertainty is whether the target’s one-dimensional As–Se ribbon nucleates instead of separate arsenate/selenite phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Oxyacid solution crystallization

This route is supported by CS examples where hydrogen selenates/sulfates crystallize from concentrated acid solutions at 120–140 °C or by cooling from hot concentrated acid [CS]. Its condition severity is moderate: temperature and pressure are mild, but concentrated selenic/arsenic media are hazardous [CS; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are excellent because the reagents are molecular/ionic in solution and crystallization is controlled by supersaturation rather than diffusion [CS]. The largest uncertainty is selenium oxidation state control, since the target appears to combine As(V)O4 with trigonal Se(IV)O3 units [Target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed-tube / flux

CS provides a generic precedent for sealed flux growth, but it is less chemically transferable to protonated AsHSeO5 [CS; insufficient evidence from SS/TS]. Severity is higher because the route combines toxic volatile Se/As species with a sealed vessel [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may be acceptable in a flux, but structural reconstruction and H retention are problematic [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best treated as a crystal-growth fallback, not the primary synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a low-dimensional crystalline ribbon, which can sometimes form well from solution because local AsO4 and SeO3 units preorganize in acidic media [Target; CS/SS/TS]. Only one amorphization trigger is clearly present: distinct As and Se coordination preferences/radii [Target; Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target cell contains >40 atoms, and the target description does not report edge/face sharing [Target]. Therefore, amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AsHSeO5 is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is only 0.0230 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support low-temperature solution-based synthesis of protonated selenium oxyanion materials, especially by hydrothermal and concentrated-acid crystallization routes. The most important enabling factor is the solution chemistry of As–O and Se–O polyhedra, which should allow crystallization without high-temperature solid-state diffusion. The main limiting factor is chemical speciation: the target appears to require As(V) tetrahedra and Se(IV)-like trigonal units in a protonated framework, so oxidizing conditions must not drive all selenium into Se(VI). A secondary limitation is safety and handling, since arsenic and selenium oxides/acids are toxic and potentially volatile.
