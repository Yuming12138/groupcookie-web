### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the 90–100 stability bin and justifies a score of 100 (Target). Thermodynamic analogues include stable CrBO₃ and NiCO₃ with E_hull = 0.0, supporting that trigonal oxoanion frameworks can be ground-state phases (TS-1, TS-2). However, SS includes high-pressure CO₂ network structures with E_hull ≈0.45–0.51 eV/atom, so structural similarity alone gives insufficient positive stability evidence and is treated cautiously (SS-1, SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate hydrothermal temperatures close to the 490 K carbonate analogue condition and standard PTFE-lined vessels, so no high-T or high-pressure penalty is applied (CS-1, TS-2). The sealed CO₂/carbonate system triggers the volatile/sealed-system deduction, and Be toxicity triggers the toxic-precursor deduction [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for this specific hydrothermal BeCO₃ route and is neutral for F2.

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal solution transport gives high precursor activity and avoids the slow solid-state diffusion expected for BeO [Reasonable Inference]. The NiCO₃ hydrothermal precedent at 490 K for 4 d shows carbonate crystallization can occur under moderate conditions (CS-1, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether Be²⁺ hydrolysis competes with direct BeCO₃ crystallization, which is not resolved by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is mild in temperature and pressure, consistent with CuCO₃ precipitation at 0–50 °C and Ni oxalate precipitation at 60 °C (CS-5, CS-3/SS-2). Deductions arise from the multi-step nature of precipitation/aging/washing/drying and the toxicity of Be salts [Reasonable Inference]. This route is operationally easier than high-pressure routes but less directly supported for an anhydrous crystalline BeCO₃ phase.

#### F3 — Route 2

Soluble Be salts and bicarbonate/carbonate should give very high precursor activity, analogous to soluble salt precipitation in CS-3 and CS-5 [Reasonable Inference]. Solution nucleation avoids solid-state diffusion, but structural ordering may be poorer than in hydrothermal growth, and CS-5 notes optimization is needed for carbonate particle morphology/phase control. The main uncertainty is formation of hydrated/basic beryllium carbonate rather than the target trigonal anhydrous phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses elevated CO₂ pressure and sealed capsules, directly supported by CuCO₃ synthesis at 20 kbar and 500 °C (CS-4). Since the proposed pressure is below 5 GPa, the rubric high-pressure penalty is not applied, but sealed volatile CO₂, multistep capsule handling, and toxic BeO are penalized (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). TS-3 supports that oxide/oxoanion phases can require p >25 kbar, but it also signals added difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid BeO carbonation is expected to have lower kinetic activity than solution routes because BeO is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. Water traces can promote carbonate formation, as reported for CuCO₃ under CO₂ pressure (CS-4), improving kinetics somewhat. Nevertheless, transformation from BeO to the target BeO₄–carbonate network is a substantial reconstructive process (Target; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 receives the largest severity penalty because SS-4 and SS-5 use 7.5 GPa multianvil synthesis, which exceeds the >5 GPa threshold. The route also requires sealed volatile CO₂/carbonate sources, multiple HPHT steps, rapid quenching/decompression, and toxic Be handling (SS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). It is therefore a backup route rather than the preferred synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can accelerate densification and stabilize frameworks, as shown by SS-4, SS-5, and TS-3. However, the route still relies on oxide/carbonate powder reaction, so precursor activity and diffusion are inferior to hydrothermal/precipitation routes [Reasonable Inference]. The structural analogy is only partial because SS high-pressure analogues are borates/oxide frameworks rather than Be carbonates.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cationic building units, BeO₄ tetrahedra and trigonal CO₃ groups, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination mismatch check [Reasonable Inference; Target]. The target description reports corner-sharing BeO₄ tetrahedra and carbonate linkages, with no edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not activated (Target). CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct unit-cell atom count for the target; based on the simple formula and provided structure description, the >40-atom trigger is not applied, but that remains a small uncertainty (Target).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target BeCO₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically and thermodynamically similar carbonate/oxoanion materials made hydrothermally or by precipitation under mild-to-moderate conditions (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, TS-2). Structurally similar data are mixed: SS-2 supports mild precipitation chemistry, but SS-1/SS-3 indicate that some tetrahedral CO₂-like networks require extreme pressure, so SS evidence is not uniformly favorable. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high CO₂/carbonate activity at low temperature to avoid decarbonation while allowing BeO₄–carbonate ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is Be chemistry: Be compounds are highly toxic, Be²⁺ hydrolyzes readily, and competing hydrated/basic beryllium carbonates may form [Reasonable Inference].
