### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability edge of the requested 90–100 bin. CS provides useful support because chemically similar Na chlorides with E_hull values near 0.013–0.054 eV/atom have reported syntheses, including CS-1, CS-2, and CS-5. SS also supports feasibility because structurally related chlorides include ground-state or near-ground-state materials such as Na3InCl6 and Li2ZnCl4 (SS-1, SS-4). TS is especially relevant because thermodynamic analogues around 0.039–0.075 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable (TS-1 to TS-5), though the target may be metastable rather than a strict ground state.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure; this is supported by TS-1 at 400 °C, TS-2 at 200 °C, and SS-4 at 403 °C. The main deductions come from sealed/vacuum or inert handling, which is directly supported by CS-1, CS-2, TS-1, TS-2, and SS-4. The volatile AlCl3 penalty is a [Reasonable Inference], but it is consistent with the frequent use of sealed ampoules for chloride syntheses in CS/SS/TS. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Na2LiAlCl6 decomposition pressure, so the severity score is conservative.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CS and TS show that binary chloride precursors can react at moderate temperatures in sealed environments, supporting a relatively good precursor-activity score (CS-1, TS-1, TS-2). Ion diffusion should be easier than in oxide ceramics because chloride salts can melt, soften, or form flux-like phases [Reasonable Inference], consistent with melt/anneal behavior in TS-2 and SS-4. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic term because the target has low-symmetry P1 ordering and mixed Li/Al polyhedra; long reaction times in TS-1, TS-2, and SS-4 indicate possible slow cation ordering. Insufficient evidence from CS for Li/Al ordering specifically requires a neutral-to-cautious treatment of this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains low temperature and ambient pressure, supported by melt growth in SS-1 and low-temperature sealed chloride annealing in TS-2 and SS-4. It still requires sealed ampoules, dry handling, and volatile/corrosive AlCl3 flux, giving the same principal deductions as Route 1. SS-1 confirms melt growth for a structurally similar Na trivalent-metal chloride, but insufficient evidence from SS gives no exact temperature for Na3InCl6, so the 180–320 °C target program is [Reasonable Inference]. TS contributes useful moisture-control evidence through Li6FeCl8 glovebox handling (TS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because a chloroaluminate melt should eliminate most solid-state diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 directly supports melt growth of a structurally related Na3MCl6 compound, while TS-2 supports low-temperature melt/anneal synthesis of a Li chloride. Structural ordering still may be nontrivial because SS-4 required long treatment and reported phase sensitivity between Li2ZnCl4 forms. CS offers insufficient direct evidence for Al-containing flux crystallization, so the score is high but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is mild in temperature, supported by room-temperature or ambient solution crystallization in TS-4 and TS-5. However, directly transferring aqueous routes to Na2LiAlCl6 is unsafe chemically because AlCl3 hydrolyzes and the target is anhydrous [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, inert/dry atmosphere and corrosive anhydrous media are required, supported indirectly by glovebox handling in TS-1/TS-2. SS-2 gives only weak signal because its reported solution route concerns a different hydrate/chloride system, so it is treated cautiously.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution routes have excellent transport and precursor activity, as TS-4 and TS-5 show double chlorides crystallizing from solution. The uncertainty is structural: TS-4/TS-5 solution products are not Li/Al mixed anhydrous chlorides, and hydration/solvation could dominate [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for solution synthesis of the target chemistry, and SS gives insufficient direct evidence for anhydrous Na–Li–Al chloride crystallization. Thus Route 3 is kinetically attractive but chemically less certain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target triggers the cation-radius-difference check because Na+, Li+, and Al3+ differ substantially in ionic size [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives a small P1 formula-unit description, not a >40 atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS changes this because F4 is target-structure based. The target reports corner-sharing LiCl4–AlCl5 connectivity but not coexisting edge and face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low-to-medium, mainly from multi-cation disorder rather than framework complexity.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2LiAlCl6 is likely a mildly metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the cutoff for high thermodynamic feasibility. CS, SS, and TS collectively support synthesis of related alkali chlorometallates by sealed-ampoule, melt, and solution methods, with the strongest practical match being sealed low-temperature melt/flux crystallization from chlorides. The most critical enabling factor is the probable formation of low-temperature chloride/chloroaluminate melts, which should overcome diffusion barriers and enable cation ordering [Reasonable Inference], consistent with melt-growth and melt/anneal analogues in SS-1, SS-4, and TS-2. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under volatile, moisture-sensitive AlCl3-containing conditions [Reasonable Inference], with general phase-control difficulties supported by TS-1 and SS-4. Overall, the material appears synthesizable with careful dry sealed-ampoule technique, but obtaining a phase-pure P1 polymorph may require slow cooling and compositional screening.
