### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s energy above hull is **0.037 eV/atom**, placing it within the ≤0.05 eV/atom range for high synthesizability. This is comparable to several successful analogues: CS2 has E_hull ≈0.029 eV/atom, TS2 ≈0.032 eV/atom, TS3 ≈0.039 eV/atom, and TS1 ≈0.050 eV/atom. The phase is therefore likely accessible as a near-ground-state or mildly metastable compound, but not guaranteed as a thermodynamic ground state. Remaining uncertainty comes from mixed-halide ordering and the absence of a direct synthesis report for this exact composition.

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity penalty because sealed evacuated ampoules and slow cooling are required, as shown for CS2, SS5, and TS1. The temperature is not severe—400–450 °C is supported by CS2/SS5/TS1—but volatile iodine/chloride/bromide species require sealing and careful pressure management. Toxic/air-sensitive halides such as InBr3/InCl3 are inferred from SS3/SS5/TS1 handling requirements. The uncertainty is whether sulfur incorporation requires higher temperature or longer annealing than the bromide-only analogues.

#### F3: Route 1

The kinetics are favorable relative to oxide solid-state synthesis because halide precursors are reactive and may partially melt, consistent with SS3 and SS5. Diffusion should be efficient because In and In halides form low-temperature melts/vapor species, and SS5/TS1 produce In-halide ribbons at 450 °C. The main kinetic barrier is structural/compositional ordering: the target combines In–S framework units with I, Br, and Cl, whereas SS/TS analogues are mostly simpler bromides. Thus, F3 is good but not excellent.

#### F2: Route 2

CVT requires vacuum-sealed tubes, volatile transport agents, and a thermal gradient, as shown in CS5 and TS2. Temperatures can be moderate if modeled on CS5, but the multi-step route—pre-reacted charge plus transport—is more complex than one-pot synthesis. I2 and AlCl3 are corrosive/volatile; TS2 specifically uses high vacuum with AlCl3, and CS5 uses iodine in a sealed tube. The key uncertainty is whether the target remains congruent during vapor transport.

#### F3: Route 2

Vapor transport is kinetically attractive because halide transport agents can move metal species efficiently, supported by CS5 and TS2. Crystal growth should be feasible because CS5 reports chalcoiodide single crystals and TS2 reports large In-containing chalcogenide crystals. However, composition control is the weak point: I, Br, Cl, and S may have different transport rates, making off-stoichiometric crystals likely [Reasonable Inference]. This lowers the F3 score despite good crystallization kinetics.

#### F2: Route 3

Ionothermal synthesis is mild in temperature, with CS1 demonstrating mixed chalcogenide-halide crystallization at 165 °C for 5 days. It still requires moisture-free handling because CS1 uses Lewis-acidic EMIMBr–AlCl3, and the inferred In halides/I2 are air- or moisture-sensitive. The sealed-vessel and washing steps add moderate procedural complexity. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for ionothermal synthesis of the target, so the route is scored with uncertainty despite low temperature.

#### F3: Route 3

The ionic liquid should provide high precursor mobility and chemical activation, which is why CS1 can form low-dimensional mixed chalcogenide-halide crystals at 165 °C. This minimizes solid-state diffusion limitations and may favor kinetically stabilized 1D ribbons. The structural reconstruction score is lower because the CS1 analogue is Bi/Se/Al-based and 2D, not In/S-based and 1D. The route is nevertheless the best balance of mild conditions and high transport kinetics.

#### F2: Route 4

The molten halide flux route has similar severity to Route 1 because it uses dry/vacuum sealed ampoules and volatile halide fluxes. SS3 supports dry Ar halide melts at 250–400 °C, while SS5/TS1 support evacuated ampoules at 450 °C. The route avoids high pressure and very high temperature but requires careful separation of crystals from corrosive halide flux. The uncertainty is flux compatibility with sulfide species.

#### F3: Route 4

Kinetics are strong because molten halides provide rapid ion transport, as shown by SS3, SS5, and TS1. Structurally, this route is well matched to the target’s low-dimensional In-halide motifs, and CS2/CS5 support the broader feasibility of chalcohalide frameworks. The limiting kinetic factor is achieving the exact S:I:Br:Cl ratio in a flux where halide activities may strongly bias the product. Overall, it is the strongest backup route if ionothermal synthesis fails.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, In, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered; this is a favorable factor not contradicted by CS/SS/TS. The formula unit has 20 atoms, and no evidence is provided that the primitive cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered. The structure contains corner- and edge-sharing but no face-sharing polyhedra, so C3 is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty comes from low-dimensionality and mixed halides, which may cause disorder, but the formal amorphization checks indicate low risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase**, since its E_hull is 0.037 eV/atom and successful analogues exist in the 0.03–0.05 eV/atom range (CS2, TS1–TS3). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: SS and TS strongly support low-dimensional In-halide ribbon formation in sealed halide media, while CS supports mixed chalcohalide chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and chemical activity of halide-rich melts or Lewis-acidic ionic liquids, supported by CS1, SS3, SS5, and TS1. The most critical limiting factor is precise mixed-anion stoichiometry, especially retaining the single Cl and Br while incorporating six I and four S. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible but will likely require screening of halide chemical potentials and slow crystallization conditions.
