### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, so it falls at the upper edge of the most favorable prompt-defined bin, giving **90/100**. TS provides useful support because several thermodynamically similar compounds with E_hull ≈ 0.04–0.10 eV/atom have reported syntheses, including Na3As [TS2], Li2HgGe [TS3], EuAg4In8 [TS4], and Ca3Hg [TS5]. CS and SS provide strong chemical/structural support through stable NaTl at E_hull = 0 [CS1, SS1], but insufficient evidence from CS/SS that Na3Tl4 itself is a ground-state phase rather than a shallow metastable polymorph/composition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the closest NaTl recipe uses 1100–1200 °C, evacuation to ~10⁻⁴ Torr, dry Ar, and extended annealing/stirring for Na compounds [CS1, SS1]. Na is explicitly handled with cleaning/storage to avoid air contact [CS1], and Tl introduces toxicity as a [Reasonable Inference] from its elemental use in NaTl and Tl intermetallics [CS1, SS3]. TS gives insufficient evidence for needing such high temperature in Na–Tl specifically, so the severity is driven mainly by CS/SS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from melt formation because NaTl and related intermetallics are prepared by direct melting/fusion [CS1, CS3, SS1]. The low melting points of Na and Tl imply rapid mass transport once molten [Reasonable Inference], so diffusion is favorable. The main kinetic uncertainty is ordering into the lower-symmetry R3m Na3Tl4 structure with multiple inequivalent Na/Tl sites, whereas NaTl is a simpler Fd-3m tetrahedral framework [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C operation by using the sealed Ta ampoule strategy demonstrated for Na6ZnSn2 at 773 K [SS2] and other Na intermetallics at 350–600 °C [SS4], while remaining consistent with sealed-container Na3Hg2 synthesis [CS3]. Deductions remain for vacuum/controlled sealing, volatile/air-sensitive Na, toxic Tl, and multiple operational steps. TS2 supports sealed-tube synthesis at 700 °C for a similarly metastable Na-rich phase, but insufficient evidence from TS for exact Na–Tl volatility control.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has favorable kinetics because elemental fusion in Ta ampoules is directly successful for several Na-rich intermetallics [SS2, SS4] and Na3Hg2 [CS3]. Slow cooling rates used in Na6ZnSn2 synthesis [SS2] and extended annealing in Na/Tl-related systems [CS1, SS3] support the idea that ordering can be improved without extreme temperatures. Remaining uncertainty is competing binary Na–Tl phase formation, since CS/SS provide only NaTl as the closest experimentally reported Na–Tl binary [CS1, SS1].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because it uses lower temperatures but requires a sealed Na-rich flux, inert/vacuum containment, and likely decanting or separation under inert conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Flux-growth complexity is supported by EuAg4In8, where flux removal required centrifugation and post-treatment [TS4], but that is not a Na–Tl system. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Na–Tl flux growth, making this route less certain despite similar severity deductions.

#### F3 — Route 3

A liquid Na-rich flux should provide excellent diffusion and high precursor activity [Reasonable Inference], and TS4 demonstrates that metallic flux growth can produce intermetallic single crystals under controlled cooling. SS evidence that Na intermetallics can require excess Na and careful cooling supports the plausibility of a Na-rich solution route [SS2, SS4, SS5]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: flux conditions may favor Na-rich competing phases or residual Na, and CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence for the Na3Tl4 primary crystallization field.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

F4 is scored high because the target is a binary metallic/intermetallic phase with no oxide/nitride network or high-temperature quench requirement, and CS/SS analogues crystallize readily as intermetallics by fusion [CS1, SS1, SS2]. The target has mixed corner/edge sharing but no described face-sharing, so the prompt’s strongest connectivity trigger is not met. The main uncertainty is the unknown unit-cell Z; however, insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests a >40-atom unit cell or high amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na3Tl4 is likely a shallow metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, not clearly unstable but not as secure as the stable NaTl analogue [CS1, SS1]. The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis from the elements because NaTl and multiple Na-rich intermetallics are made by direct melting/fusion in inert sealed systems [CS1, SS1, SS2, CS3]. TS data supports the broader idea that compounds around 0.04–0.10 eV/atom above hull can be experimentally isolated under controlled solid-state or flux conditions [TS2–TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is liquid-phase alloying of low-melting Na and Tl, which should greatly reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference, supported by fusion routes in CS1/SS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity/stoichiometry control in a volatile, air-sensitive, toxic Na–Tl sealed system, especially because the closest experimentally documented binary is NaTl rather than Na3Tl4 [CS1, SS1].
