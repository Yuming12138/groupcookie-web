### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored as a predicted ground-state phase. This is supported indirectly by stable oxygen-rich analogues: SrO2 has E_hull = 0.0 (CS4), BaO2 has E_hull = 0.0 (TS5), K3NO3 has E_hull = 0.0 (TS2), and K3TaO8 is close to stable at 0.022 eV/atom (SS5; TS3). CS1–CS3 have high hull energies and do not support thermodynamic stability for KO3 specifically; therefore, they provide insufficient evidence for this factor and are treated as neutral relative to the target hull.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable relative to hydrothermal CS1/CS2 and high-temperature solid-state CS3. However, it requires dry O3/O2, air/moisture-sensitive KO2, and likely sealed or controlled ozone handling, which is penalized by analogy to strict moisture/CO2 exclusion in TS2 and controlled O2/CO2-free peroxide preparation in CS4/TS5. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly reports KO3 ozonation, so the atmosphere severity is inferred.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetic prospects are reasonably good because KO2 and O3/O2 are highly reactive **[Reasonable Inference]**, and related peroxo/oxide salts form under mild oxidizing conditions (CS4; TS5; SS5/TS3). Diffusion distances should be short for a binary K–O system, but gas–solid conversion can be surface-limited. Structural reconstruction from superoxide/peroxide to ordered KO3 sheets is the main kinetic uncertainty because no direct analogue in CS/SS/TS confirms this transformation.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses very low temperatures, which is favorable and consistent with low-temperature crystallization in SS3, SS4, SS5/TS3, and TS4. The severity comes from liquid NH3/O3 handling, volatile toxic gases, and strict exclusion of H2O/CO2, with the latter supported by TS2 and CS4/TS5. CS/SS/TS do not directly establish liquid-ammonia KO3 chemistry, so this route carries extrapolation risk.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are scored highest because solution crystallization avoids long-range solid-state diffusion, and several structurally similar oxo/peroxo salts crystallize at 0–25 °C or by slow evaporation/interdiffusion (SS2, SS4, SS5/TS3, TS4). K3TaO8 single crystals from KOH/H2O2 at 0 °C specifically support low-temperature crystallization of a potassium peroxo salt (SS5; TS3). The uncertainty is that water-based analogues cannot be directly copied for hydrolysis-sensitive KO3, so an anhydrous solvent adaptation is required **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids the >700–1100 °C temperatures used in CS3 because KO3 is oxygen-rich and likely thermally sensitive **[Reasonable Inference]**. It still requires controlled dry O2/O3 atmosphere, sealed handling, and air-sensitive precursors, which are penalized based on TS2 and CS4/TS5. The lack of direct temperature data for KO3 is the largest uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state oxygenation has lower kinetic confidence than solution crystallization because low-temperature solid-state diffusion and oxygen ordering can be slow. CS3 shows that some solid-state oxide syntheses require high temperature and long times, while CS5 notes narrow phase windows for metastable flux products, suggesting bulk phase control may be difficult. However, the binary K–O composition is simpler than multication oxides, so the route is not kinetically impossible **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the ionic-radius mismatch criterion does not apply. The described structure contains edge-sharing KO6 units and edge/corner-sharing O-centered units, but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing; face-sharing appears in SrO2 (CS4) but not in the target description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell atom count, but the binary KO3 description with one K and two O sites suggests a low-complexity cell **[Reasonable Inference]**, giving low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KO3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase according to the provided E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, not merely a metastable phase. The best analogue support comes from oxygen-rich peroxide/peroxo compounds that are stable or near-stable and form under oxidizing, low-temperature, moisture-controlled conditions (CS4; TS5; SS5; TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high oxygen chemical potential supplied by O3/O2 while maintaining low temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**, because high-temperature oxide sintering analogues such as CS3 are poorly matched to an oxygen-rich ozonide. The most critical limiting factor is environmental sensitivity: related potassium oxidized salts require strict exclusion of moisture and CO2 (TS2), and alkaline earth peroxides require CO2-free/O2-controlled washing and drying (CS4; TS5). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but experimentally delicate, with Route 2 favored for crystal growth and Route 1 favored for simpler bulk preparation.
