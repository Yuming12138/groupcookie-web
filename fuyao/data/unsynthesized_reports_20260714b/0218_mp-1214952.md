### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold for the 90–100 score band, so a score of 90 is appropriate. CS provides moderate support because CdC₂O₄ is close to the hull at 0.0718 eV/atom (CS-5), and Zn/Ni oxalates are also near-hull at 0.059–0.076 eV/atom (CS-2, CS-4), although some Cu analogues are much higher in energy (CS-1, CS-3). TS provides strong thermodynamic-context support because the listed thermodynamic analogues cluster around 0.056–0.113 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). SS provides weaker support because several structural analogues are significantly higher above hull, so SS is treated as a neutral-to-weak signal for F1.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because the direct Cd oxalate analogue was synthesized at 423 K/150 °C in water in a Teflon-lined autoclave, with no special atmosphere or extreme pressure reported (CS-5). The only deduction is for Cd precursor toxicity, which is a physicochemical safety concern [Reasonable Inference] tied to the Cd salt used in CS-5. SS and TS give additional mild-solution precedent but do not improve the specific Cd–C–O hydrothermal evidence; insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS for exact target formation.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Hydrothermal Cd nitrate/oxalate chemistry should have high precursor activity because Cd(NO₃)₂·4H₂O and K₂C₂O₄ react in water to form CdC₂O₄ crystals (CS-5). Diffusion limitations are low because the reaction occurs in solution rather than through dense solids, and many SS/TS analogues crystallize from solution at room temperature to 50 °C (SS-3, TS-1, TS-3). The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction: the target’s discrete Cd–C/CO/O₂ clusters are not represented by CS-5’s Cd–O oxalate network, and there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that such rearrangement occurs.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains mild in temperature because CS-1 used ambient precipitation followed by 150 °C vacuum drying, and CS-3/CS-4 used 40–60 °C precipitation. Deductions are applied for vacuum/controlled drying, as explicitly used in CS-1 and CS-2, and for Cd salt toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide direct evidence for vacuum dehydration to the target, so their signal is neutral for this severity assessment.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Precursor activity is high because aqueous metal nitrate + oxalate/squarate precipitation is repeatedly successful in CS-1, CS-3, and CS-4. Solution precipitation minimizes ion diffusion barriers, giving a high diffusion score. The low structural-reconstruction score reflects that dehydration of a carbon-oxide salt can form anhydrous CuC₄O₄ (CS-1), but there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that it can produce the target’s Cd–C/CO/O₂ cluster arrangement rather than a conventional Cd oxalate phase like CS-5.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The base crystallization conditions are mild because SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, and TS-3 use room-temperature to 50 °C solution precipitation/diffusion. However, the proposed Cd–C/CO/O₂ adaptation likely requires toxic cyanide or acetylide chemistry, possible volatile CO, and controlled atmosphere/sealing, all of which are [Reasonable Inference] beyond the direct analogues. Therefore the F2 score is much lower than for Routes 1–2.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution diffusion is kinetically favorable for crystallization, as shown by SS-3 and TS-1. Linear M–C–N fragments in SS-2 to SS-5 support partial structural analogy to the target’s linear Cd–C units, but they do not address the target’s CO and O₂ clusters. Because no CS/SS/TS example combines Cd–C bonding with discrete CO/O₂ units, structural assembly is scored as difficult.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one metal cation, Cd, so cation-size mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell likely exceeds 40 atoms because the target description explicitly lists multiple CO, CdC₄, and O₂ clusters, triggering the large-cell check. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is not triggered because the target is described as zero-dimensional rather than a framework of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedra; CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for target amorphization behavior, so this is mainly inferred from the target structure.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a borderline stable or very low-energy metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS analogues strongly support the accessibility of Cd–C–O oxalate-type chemistry under mild hydrothermal conditions, especially CdC₂O₄ from Cd(NO₃)₂·4H₂O and K₂C₂O₄ (CS-5), but they do not directly support the target’s unusual Cd–C/CO/O₂ molecular clusters. SS and TS analogues support mild solution crystallization of linear carbon-ligand coordination solids, especially cyanide/cyanometallate systems (SS-2 to SS-5, TS-1, TS-3), but their chemistry differs substantially from oxygen-rich Cd carbonyl/peroxide clusters. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution or hydrothermal crystallization, which can preserve molecular carbon-oxide motifs and avoid high-temperature decomposition. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: conventional CdC₂O₄, hydrated oxalates, CdCO₃, or amorphous Cd carbon-oxide phases are more obvious products than the target’s discrete 0D Cd–C/CO/O₂ structure.
