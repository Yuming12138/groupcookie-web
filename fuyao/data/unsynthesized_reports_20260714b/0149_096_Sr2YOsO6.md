### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the threshold where the rubric assigns 90–100, so I assign 90 rather than a higher value. CS1/SS1 report the same composition Sr2YOsO6 as a stable E_hull = 0.0 monoclinic double perovskite, strongly supporting thermodynamic accessibility. TS1, TS4, and TS5 show that Re/Os oxide analogues with E_hull near 0.04–0.06 eV/atom can be synthesized, supporting the idea that the target triclinic variant may be accessible as a low-energy polymorph. Remaining uncertainty is whether the triclinic P-1 distortion is a true isolable polymorph or a slight relaxation/distortion of the known monoclinic phase.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled Ar/sealed-tube operation, volatile/toxic Os chemistry, and multi-step handling; all of these are explicitly present in CS1/SS1. It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which improves its severity score relative to high-temperature ceramic routes. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact Sr–Y–Os Ar sealed route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity. The main uncertainty is sealed-tube oxygen pressure during SrO2 decomposition.

#### F3 — Route 1

CS1/SS1 require about 100 h and intermittent regrinding, which indicates slow diffusion and ordering kinetics despite direct success. The use of SrO2 helps reactivity relative to SrCO3 [Reasonable Inference], but OsO2 and Y2O3 remain refractory oxide components. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the exact double-perovskite framework is formed in CS1/SS1. TS gives no direct kinetic evidence for Sr2YOsO6, so TS is neutral here.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits evacuated sealed-tube and redox-buffer complexity from SS2/SS4, including OsO2/Os handling and possible controlled oxidation. It has similar temperature severity to Route 1 if adapted near 900–960 °C, but oxygen fugacity control is more delicate than the Ar route. CS provides partial support through the same SrO2/OsO2/Y2O3 chemistry in CS1, while TS is insufficient for this specific Os-buffered Sr–Y route and is scored neutral in condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2

SS2 shows that OsO2/Os/BaO2 mixtures can form Ba2LnOsO6 in only 5 h at 1170 K, suggesting effective redox-assisted formation for some Os double perovskites. However, SS4 shows that Ca2ScOsO6 may require a two-step oxygen-control process, implying that kinetics and oxygen stoichiometry can be limiting. For Sr2YOsO6, the structural match is strong from CS1/SS1, but the exact vacuum-buffer recipe is inferred rather than demonstrated. TS gives insufficient evidence for this specific kinetic pathway.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses air and ambient pressure, which reduces atmosphere penalties, but it requires >1000 °C based on SS3 and CS2. SS3 uses 1400 °C with Os metal and 10% excess Os for Ba2YOsO6, so Os toxicity and volatility remain important. The route is penalized for multi-step precursor preparation because SS3 uses preformed Ba4Y2O7 and CS2 uses repeated regrinding in ceramic synthesis. CS supports high-temperature ceramic feasibility for related Sr/Ru double perovskites, but not exact Sr2YOsO6.

#### F3 — Route 3

High temperature improves reaction rate, as SS3 forms Ba2YOsO6 in 1.5 h, but Os metal and preformed alkaline-earth–Y oxides are relatively refractory [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 reports several days and frequent regrinding for related ceramic double perovskites, so diffusion and ordering can still be slow. SS3 also reports impurity phases by neutron diffraction, reducing the structural-reconstruction/phase-purity score. TS is insufficient for this exact high-temperature air route and is neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size/coordination differences among Sr, Y, and Os, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check; this is reflected in distinct Sr–O, Y–O, and Os–O distances in the target and CS1/SS1. The target description gives a corner-sharing double-perovskite network and does not show mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered. The atom-count check is not triggered because the provided formula unit is small and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a >40-atom cell. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr2YOsO6 is likely synthesizable, with the target triclinic P-1 form best viewed as a borderline-stable or low-energy metastable distortion because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom while the closely related monoclinic Sr2YOsO6 analogue is reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1/SS1). The CS and SS evidence are unusually strong because they include the same Sr–Y–Os–O composition and essentially the same ordered double-perovskite connectivity (CS1/SS1). TS evidence is supportive but less specific: several Re/Os oxide analogues near similar hull energies are experimentally accessible, but they do not directly prescribe the Sr2YOsO6 route (TS1/TS4/TS5). The key enabling factor is the demonstrated sealed-tube SrO2 + OsO2 + Y2O3 route that stabilizes Os in the desired oxide environment (CS1/SS1). The key limiting factor is osmium volatility/toxicity and oxygen fugacity control, which recur across Os perovskite syntheses (CS1, SS2, SS4, SS3). Overall, the target should be accessible as a bulk polycrystalline phase, though isolating the triclinic polymorph specifically may require careful cooling, annealing, or structural verification.
