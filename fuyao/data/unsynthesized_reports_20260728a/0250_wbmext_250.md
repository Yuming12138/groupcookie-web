### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuTe is best treated as a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and many CS/SS/TS analogues are on or near the hull (CS1–CS2, CS5, SS1–SS4/TS1–TS4). The analogue support is strongest for Au–Te local bonding and sealed-tube processing, especially CrAuTe₄ and AuTe₂ with AuTe₆-type motifs (SS4/TS4, SS5). The most important enabling factor is that Au–Te chemistry is repeatedly accessible by solid-state, Bridgman, hydrothermal, and halide-assisted routes (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS4, CS2–CS5). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: AuTe₂ and Au–Te–halides are strongly represented competitors, while binary AuTe itself is not experimentally identified in the provided data (SS1–SS3, SS5/TS1–TS3). Therefore, the best practical first attempt is a sealed-tube 1:1 Au:Te solid-state screen with careful Te volatility and annealing control (SS4/TS4, CS3–CS5).

| 1 | All | Volatile Te loss and Au:Te drift at elevated temperature | High | Use evacuated sealed ampoules, slow prereaction near Te melting, and only slight Te excess; sealed Te-containing syntheses support this control strategy (CS3, SS4/TS4). |
