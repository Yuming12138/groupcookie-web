### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
CeReN₃ is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a very high thermodynamic score under the required rubric; several reference nitrides are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 and experimentally synthesized. (CS1, CS5, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5) The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a theory-only WBM candidate with no experimental synthesis identified, so the hull value supports stability but not kinetic accessibility. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 1 Solid-State**  
The solid-state route uses high temperature, inert/purified nitrogen, CeN precursor preparation, pressing, and repeated firings, all of which are supported by Ce₂MnN₃ and X₂YN₃ analogue syntheses. (SS3, TS1, CS2, CS3, TS4, TS5) It avoids high pressure and volatile fluxes, so condition severity is better than sealed flux routes, but CeN handling and repeated cycles justify meaningful deductions. (SS3, TS1)

**F3 — Route 1 Solid-State**  
Kinetics are limited because CeN and Re metal are not highly reactive molecular precursors, and Re is expected to diffuse slowly as a refractory metal. [Reasonable Inference] Ce₂MnN₃ required multiple grinding/pressing cycles for full conversion, indicating that even less refractory Mn systems can have slow solid-state ordering. (SS3, TS1)

**F2 — Route 2 Li₃N Flux**  
Li₃N flux routes are directly supported for rare-earth/transition-metal nitrides and Ce₂CrN₃, but they require air-sensitive Li₃N, sealed tubes/ampoules, and inert handling. (CS1, CS5, TS3) The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed reactive flux, multi-step workup, and air-sensitive precursors. (CS1, CS5, TS3)

**F3 — Route 2 Li₃N Flux**  
Flux growth improves kinetics by increasing nitrogen activity and mass transport at 900–1030 °C, as shown by Ce₂CrN₃ and La₃Ta₂N₆/La₃Nb₂N₆ syntheses. (TS3, CS1, CS5) The main uncertainty is that ReN₆ octahedral R-3c CeReN₃ has no direct analogue in the provided data, so structural reconstruction is only moderately scored. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3 Na/Ga Flux**  
Na/Ga flux is supported by La₃Cr₂N₆ and rare-earth Ta/Nb nitride routes, but it uses air-sensitive/volatile Na, sealed Nb/Ta containers, slow cooling, and post-treatment. (SS5, TS2, CS1, CS5) This creates similar condition severity to Li₃N flux but with greater phase-control concerns. (SS5, TS2)

**F3 — Route 3 Na/Ga Flux**  
Metallic flux should improve transport for refractory Re compared with dry sintering, but La₃Cr₂N₆ was obtained only in small amounts with competing phases. [Reasonable Inference] The analogue evidence therefore supports possible crystallization but warns that composition and phase windows may be narrow. (SS5, TS2)

**F2 — Route 4 High-Pressure NH₃**  
The NH₃ autoclave route has lower temperature than solid-state or flux routes, but it requires high-pressure NH₃, LiNH₂/NH₃ handling, sealed pressure equipment, and multi-step precursor preparation. (SS2) CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for a Ce–Re ammonothermal route, so direct transferability from CS/TS is neutral.  

**F3 — Route 4 High-Pressure NH₃**  
High nitrogen chemical potential may help stabilize nitride products at lower temperature, as shown by Li₂Ta₃N₅ in NH₃ at 0.6 GPa. (SS2) However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/TS for Ce–Re transport, Re incorporation, or R-3c framework formation under these conditions, so kinetics are scored moderate/neutral. (SS2)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target’s corner-sharing ReN₆ network lowers amorphization risk relative to analogues with mixed edge/face/corner sharing, but the large Ce/Re coordination and size contrast triggers one risk check. [Reasonable Inference] SS/TS analogues show that complex rare-earth transition-metal nitrides can crystallize, but several require fluxes, repeated cycles, or careful phase control, so the risk is low–medium rather than low. (SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeReN₃ is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state candidate because the supplied hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple related nitrides in CS/SS/TS are also reported at or near hull stability. (CS1, CS5, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3) The analogue support is strongest for rare-earth/transition-metal nitride formation under Li₃N flux or solid-state nitrogen atmospheres, but direct Ce–Re experimental precedent is absent in the provided data. (CS1, CS5, SS3, TS1, TS3) The critical enabling factor is high nitrogen activity combined with enhanced cation transport, best provided by Li₃N flux. (CS1, CS5, TS3) The critical limiting factor is Re incorporation into an ordered ReN₆ octahedral network without forming competing CeN/Re nitride/flux-derived phases. [Reasonable Inference] Overall, the material appears **plausibly synthesizable but not routine**, with the best initial attempt being sealed Li₃N flux around 900–1050 °C. (CS1, CS5, TS3)
