CS/SS/TS provide strong analogue thermodynamic support but no direct target experiment.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for 1030 °C synthesis, sealed Ar ampoule handling, Li₃N reactive flux, multistep processing, and air-sensitive precursors. CS1/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS3. It avoids the 1600 °C penalty and avoids true high pressure, making it less severe than high-temperature Ga flux. CS1/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS3.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 benefits from a lower 900 °C synthesis temperature but still requires Ar handling, sealed Nb tubes, volatile Na/NaN₃ chemistry, slow cooling, and vacuum post-treatment. CS2; CS5; SS2; TS2. The main severity is chemical/containment risk rather than temperature. CS2; CS5; SS2; TS2.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is most temperature-severe because the Ga-flux analogue route reaches 1600 °C and requires Ar → N₂ switching. CS1/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS3. It is not high-pressure, but the 1600 °C condition and multistep gas program justify the lowest F2 among the flux routes. CS1/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS3.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 uses comparatively standard nitride sintering conditions, but it still requires controlled N₂, repeated grind/press cycles, and air-sensitive nitride precursors. TS4/TS5. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS supports direct solid-state I4/mmm Pr₃V₂N₆ formation, so those sources are neutral 50 for this route’s transferability. CS neutral 50; SS neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Li₃N flux gives good precursor activity and transport, and the isostructural Ln₃T₂N₆ analogues reduce structural-reconstruction risk. CS1/CS3; SS1/SS3; TS1/TS3.
