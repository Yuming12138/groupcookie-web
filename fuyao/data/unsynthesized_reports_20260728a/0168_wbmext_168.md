## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The TS analogues include stable alkali–Al–Se and related selenides with \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV atom⁻¹ [TS: mp-18217, mp-9682, mp-11121, mp-11116, mp-7117]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental accessibility because the candidate is explicitly theory-only and no experimental synthesis is identified [target validation data].

### F2 — Route 1

Direct elemental synthesis requires evacuated or sealed containment, and the target contains volatile/toxic Se and potentially volatile Rb under the proposed temperature range [SS: mp-18217, mp-9682; target safety note; Reasonable Inference]. The reaction temperature is moderate relative to high-temperature ceramic synthesis because the closest Na/K aluminum selenides are prepared at 1075 K, approximately 802 °C [SS: mp-18217, mp-9682]. The main uncertainty is whether direct elemental diffusion will produce the tetragonal RbAlSe₂ phase rather than binary Rb–Se or Al–Se phases [SS: mp-18217, mp-9682; Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2

Carbonate-assisted synthesis is supported by Rb₂CO₃-based preparation of Rb₂Ni₃Se₄ at 850 °C and by carbonate-based synthesis of related alkali chalcogenides at 750–900 °C [CS: mp-18691, mp-11695]. Dry Ar/N₂ is less operationally demanding than H₂ and avoids the additional flammability and redox-control concerns associated with hydrogen [CS: mp-18691, mp-11695]. The uncertainty is that carbonate chemistry has not been directly demonstrated for RbAlSe₂, and carbonate decomposition may broaden the phase assemblage [CS: mp-18691; Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3

Flux/fusion synthesis is supported by high-confidence reports of alkali chalcogenides prepared with excess alkali carbonate and chalcogen at 800–850 °C under Ar [CS: mp-1079718, mp-14336]. The route avoids high pressure and can improve contact and transport, but it requires sealed handling because of volatile selenium-containing species [CS: mp-1079718, mp-14336; target safety note]. The principal uncertainty is phase selectivity: the cited fluxes produce transition-metal or rare-earth selenides rather than the target Al-containing tetragonal phase [CS: mp-1079718, mp-14336; SS: mp-11121, mp-11116].

### F3 — Route 1

The direct elemental route receives a modest precursor-activity score because elemental powders generally provide less reactive contact than solution-derived or decomposable precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion is favorable enough at 800–850 °C because analogous alkali–Al–Se reactions occur near this temperature [SS: mp-18217, mp-9682]. Structural reconstruction is partial: AlSe₄ tetrahedra are common in the closest SS analogues, but the target’s exact Rb coordination and I4/mcm ordering are not directly reproduced [SS: mp-18217, mp-9682, mp-7117; target structure].

Rb₂CO₃ provides a decomposable alkali precursor, and the Rb₂CO₃/Ni/Se synthesis demonstrates that a Rb-containing selenide can form from a carbonate-assisted mixture at 850 °C [CS: mp-18691].
