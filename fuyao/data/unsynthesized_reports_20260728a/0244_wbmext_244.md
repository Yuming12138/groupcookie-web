# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is supplied with an energy above hull of 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ and a negative WBM leave-one-out hull improvement, indicating a favorable computed thermodynamic position in the target dataset. The chemical analogues Tl₂SnF₆ and Tl₂TiF₆ also have zero reported hull distance, while Ga-containing Na₂NiGaF₇ and LiInF₄ are likewise reported at or near the ground state (CS-1; SS-2; SS-4; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3). This supports a high F1 score, but the target remains theory-only, so computed stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or experimental phase purity (target validation).

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because its temperature is relatively mild and direct TlF-based fluoride formation is demonstrated at 300 °C (CS-1; SS-4). The deductions arise from the need for dry inert handling, sealed containment, multiple preparation operations, and thallium toxicity (CS-1; SS-3; SS-4). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is supported by CS/SS/TS.

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe operationally because analogous reactions use 650–850 °C, vacuum or sealed inert conditions, and controlled cooling (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). Regrinding and repeated heating may be needed, as shown especially for PbSnF₄, and the thallium precursor remains a major safety limitation (CS-3). The route does not require high pressure, so the high-pressure deduction is not applied.

## F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the same nominal F2 score as Route 2 because it requires sealed dry handling, extended thermal processing, and possible volatility control. Its temperature remains below 1000 °C based on melt-growth analogues at 790 °C and high-temperature LiInF₄ treatment (SS-1; SS-3; TS-2). The major uncertainty is whether TlGaF₄ melts congruently or instead decomposes or loses fluoride/Tl-containing species; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** directly resolves this behavior.

## F3 — Route 1

The direct solid-state route uses binary fluoride precursors, which are less chemically activated than nitrate, acetate, or sol-gel precursors, so precursor activity is assigned a moderate value (CS-1; SS-2; SS-4). Diffusion is favorable relative to conventional refractory oxide synthesis because TlF-based reactions occur at 300 °C and GaF₆ frameworks form in fluoride systems below 850 °C (CS-1; SS-2; SS-4). Structural reconstruction is only partial because the target combines a two-coordinate Tl sublattice with a corner-sharing GaF₆ network, whereas the closest Tl analogues contain isolated TiF₆/SnF₆ units (CS-1; SS-4; target structure).

## F3 — Route 2

The high-temperature sintering route improves diffusion, supported by successful synthesis of related fluoride frameworks at 650–850 °C (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). However, PbSnF₄ is explicitly described as difficult to obtain reproducibly and requiring repeated processing, which lowers the structural-reconstruction and kinetic scores (CS-3). The GaF₆ network analogy is favorable, but the coordination mismatch between Tl in the target and Na in the closest Ga analogue introduces uncertainty (SS-2; TS-1; target structure).

## F3 — Route 3

The melt-assisted route receives the highest F3 score because a liquid or partially liquid phase should greatly enhance mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and RbIn₃F₁₀ was obtained by melting binary fluorides followed by slow cooling (SS-1). Slow cooling is also directly used for LiInF₄ crystallization and should favor long-range ordering (SS-3; TS-2). The score is limited because no CS/SS/TS record demonstrates congruent melting or direct crystallization of TlGaF₄; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for the target’s liquidus and decomposition behavior.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a relatively simple reported connectivity: corner-sharing GaF₆ octahedra with a separate low-coordinate Tl environment, and no supplied description of face-sharing units or simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing. This favors crystallization over amorphization [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The main risk is the strong difference between the two-coordinate Tl site and six-coordinate Ga site, which could make cation ordering and nucleation sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no direct target unit-cell atom count, so that criterion is neutral at 50 because there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a computed ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied energy above hull is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, although this remains theoretical rather than experimentally established (target validation). CS and TS provide particularly strong chemical support through stable Tl₂SnF₆/Tl₂TiF₆ fluoride chemistry and Ga-containing fluoride frameworks with GaF₆ octahedra (CS-1; SS-4; TS-1). The most enabling factor is the compatibility of binary-fluoride solid-state chemistry with the target’s GaF₆ framework (CS-1; SS-2; SS-4). The most limiting factor is not thermodynamic stability but kinetic and compositional control: the target combines a low-coordinate Tl site with an extended tilted GaF₆ network, and no supplied source reports its experimental formation or melting behavior (target validation; SS-2; TS-1). A sealed, dry, melt-assisted or low-temperature solid-state screen is therefore justified.
