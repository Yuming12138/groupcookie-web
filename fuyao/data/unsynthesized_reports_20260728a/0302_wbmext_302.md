[CS-2; CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2; TS-4; TS-5] The main uncertainty is that the target is a theory-only WBM candidate with no identified experimental synthesis. [Target]

[CS-5; SS-1; TS-1] It is not penalized for high pressure because analogues are ambient-pressure syntheses. **F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetics benefit from melt-assisted homogenization, which is important for refractory Nb/Ru systems. [SS-1; CS-5; Reasonable Inference] Structural reconstruction is favorable because Pnma analogues have the same high-coordinate 1:1:1 topology as the target. [CS-5; SS-5; TS-4] Remaining uncertainty comes from possible competition between Pnma, Ima2, and hexagonal ternary germanide polymorphs. [SS-2; SS-3; TS-2; TS-3; TS-5]

[CS-2; TS-4] **Insufficient evidence from SS** for this route is treated as neutral **50**.

Powder sintering is kinetically weaker because Nb and Ru are refractory elemental powders with slow diffusion. NbRuGe is likely a **ground-state or very near-ground-state phase** because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and the thermodynamic analogues are mostly hull-stable. [Target; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5] The analogue support is strong but not perfect: exact Pnma 1:1:1 analogues support the target topology, while several closest Ru–Ge analogues adopt Ima2 or P-62m structures, indicating possible polymorph competition. [CS-5; SS-2; SS-3; SS-5; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4; TS-5] The most critical enabling factor is melt-assisted homogenization followed by long annealing, which directly addresses refractory Nb/Ru kinetics. [SS-1; CS-5; TS-1] The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing ternary intermetallic structures and the absence of a reported experimental NbRuGe synthesis. [Target; SS-2; SS-3; TS-2; TS-3] Overall, the material is **credible and moderately accessible**, with Route 1 recommended as the first synthesis attempt. [CS-5; SS-1; SS-5; TS-1; TS-4]
