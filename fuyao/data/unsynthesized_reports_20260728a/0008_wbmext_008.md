### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported on the hull at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the hull-based score is high. (Target data) The close thermodynamic and structural analogues include on-hull CaRhO3, Ba2Hg3Pd7O14, Ca2Pt3O8, and SrFeO3, while CaPtO3 is only ~0.011 eV/atom above hull, supporting oxide phase stability in this chemical neighborhood. (CS #3; CS #5; SS #1; SS #2; SS #3; TS #1; TS #2; TS #3) Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because the target is theory-only and no CS/SS/TS row reports synthesized CaPdO3 itself. (Target data; insufficient direct synthesis evidence from CS/SS/TS)

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 scores best because both the closest structural analogue CaPtO3 and the closest Ca-based Rh analogue require high pressure plus oxidizer, directly matching the target’s Cmcm edge/corner-sharing octahedral framework. (SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1) The condition score is penalized for controlled oxygen potential and multi-step high-pressure handling, but not for >1000 °C in the preferred CaPtO3-like temperature window. (SS #1; [Reasonable Inference]) Kinetics are favorable relative to other routes because the structure is already demonstrated in CaPtO3/CaRhO3 analogues, although oxide diffusion and Pd oxidation still impose uncertainty. (SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is chemically plausible because high-pressure oxygen stabilizes Pd oxide phases, as shown by Ba2Hg3Pd7O14, and oxygen annealing stabilizes oxidized perovskite SrFeO3. (SS #3; TS #2; CS #5) Its score is lower because high-O2-only data do not demonstrate the target Cmcm CaPdO3 topology, and long annealing times in the Pd oxide analogue imply slow kinetics. (SS #3; TS #2; insufficient evidence from CS for exact Cmcm Ca–Pd–O) This route is best viewed as a secondary screen after Route 1 or as a precursor-optimization path. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 benefits from active hydrothermal precursor chemistry, because Ca2Pt3O8 forms from CaPt(OH)6 under 800 K/170 MPa hydrothermal conditions. (TS #3) It is penalized for structural reconstruction because TS #3 forms Ca2Pt3O8 rather than ABO3 Cmcm and reports impurity/crystallinity issues. (TS #3) There is insufficient evidence from CS and SS for hydrothermal CaPdO3 synthesis, so those source families are treated as neutral 50 for this route’s transferability. (insufficient evidence from CS/SS; TS #3)

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Flux synthesis is plausible for oxide crystal growth because YFeO3 and Ca2Mn3O8 are made via PbO/CaCl2 flux methods. (CS #1; TS #4) It is penalized because no flux analogue demonstrates high-valent CaPdO3 or the Cmcm CaPtO3/CaRhO3-like topology. (CS #1; TS #4; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for exact flux transfer) The route may help crystal growth if the phase already forms, but it is unlikely to be the primary stabilization method without oxidizing or pressure assistance. (CS #1; TS #4; SS #1; [Reasonable Inference])

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the ionic-radius mismatch check is triggered, because Ca is a large 8-coordinate A-site cation while Pd is an octahedral B-site cation. (Target data; SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1; [Reasonable Inference]) The target and closest analogues are not large-cell complex structures and show edge/corner-sharing rather than vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. (Target data; SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1) The residual amorphization risk is therefore low-to-medium, mainly from cation size contrast and high-pressure quench conditions. ([Reasonable Inference])

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaPdO3 is likely a DFT ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several close analogues are on or near the hull. (Target data; CS #3; SS #1; SS #2; TS #1; TS #2) The synthesis evidence is strongest from structurally similar CaPtO3 and CaRhO3, both of which use high-pressure oxidizing conditions to stabilize CaBO3 frameworks with edge/corner-sharing BO6 octahedra. (SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1) The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential, because Pt/Rh analogues use KClO4 and Pd oxide analogues require high O2 pressure. (SS #1; CS #3; SS #3; TS #1; TS #2) The most critical limiting factor is the lack of direct experimental CaPdO3 synthesis and the likely need to stabilize Pd4+ in a dense Cmcm oxide framework. (Target data; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the material is feasible but facility-dependent, with the best probability under oxidizing multi-anvil conditions. (SS #1; CS #3; SS #2; TS #1; [Reasonable Inference])
