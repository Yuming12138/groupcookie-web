### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported at E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, and multiple nearest analogues are also on-hull or near-on-hull, including CdPdF6, CdPtF6, RbRuF6, CsRuF6, and CuMoF6. **(Target; CS, SS, TS)** The score is high but not 100 because the target remains theory-only with no experimental synthesis identified. **(Target; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and multi-GPa pressure, but it requires elemental F2 and a staged fluorination/tempering process. **(CS, SS, TS)** The major severity penalty comes from F2/Cd hazard and multi-step handling rather than thermal extremes. **(CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F3 — Route 1.** Precursor activity is favorable because CdPdF6-type and Ru fluoride analogues form under direct F2 fluorination. **(CS, SS, TS)** Kinetics remain only moderate because Ru is refractory and complete ordering into Cd/Ru octahedral sites may require regrinding or repeated fluorination. **(CS; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 is more severe because close Pt/Cd analogues use F2/Ar autoclave conditions, sealed Pt/Au tubes, long anneals, and slow cooling. **(CS, SS, TS)** It is not penalized as >5 GPa pressure because the cited F2 autoclave pressure is hundreds of atm, not multi-GPa. **(CS; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F3 — Route 2.** High F2 chemical potential improves fluorination and may help reach ordered CdRuF6, but weeks-long annealing in CdPtF6-type evidence implies slow crystallization/order formation. **(SS, TS)** This route is therefore kinetically credible but operationally heavy. **(SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 avoids flowing F2 during the final anneal, but it requires sealed dry handling, volatile/reactive fluorides, and likely prior RuF4 preparation. **(CS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** Insufficient evidence from **SS** for this route; SS source score = **50**. **(SS)**

**F3 — Route 3.** Sealed-tube ABF6 analogues support eventual formation of corner-sharing fluoride frameworks, but reported durations of days to weeks imply slow diffusion and ordering. **(CS, TS)** The absence of a direct Cd–Ru sealed-tube analogue keeps the kinetics score lower. **(CS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** One amorphization trigger is assigned because Cd and Ru have substantially different effective octahedral sizes, reflected by much longer Cd–F than Ru–F bonds in the target and close Cd–Pd/Pt analogues. **(Target; CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** The framework is otherwise simple corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, not mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, so amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. **(Target; CS, SS, TS)**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CdRuF6 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic analogues are also on-hull or nearly on-hull. **(Target; TS)** The synthesis evidence is fairly strong because CdPdF6/CdPtF6 support the Cd–MF6 R-3 framework, while RbRuF6/CsRuF6 support formation of RuF6 octahedra under F2 fluorination. **(CS, SS, TS)** The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential from F2, which is common to the strongest direct analogues. **(CS, SS, TS)** The most critical limiting factor is practical: hazardous F2/Cd chemistry plus Ru oxidation-state and phase-purity control. **(CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with Route 1 preferred and Route 2 as a more forcing fallback. **(CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])**
