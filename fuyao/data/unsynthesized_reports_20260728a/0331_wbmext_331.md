### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom from the provided target data, placing it in the highest stability bin. Similar rare-earth intermetallics in CS/SS/TS are also frequently at E_hull = 0, including Gd2Pd2Cd, GdGa, Gd2In, Gd2MgGe2, and YIn. [CS-1; CS-3; SS-1; SS-2; SS-3; SS-5; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4] Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not prove experimental phase selection or ordering. [CS-2; SS-4]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc melting plus annealing is standard but not trivial because it requires inert atmosphere, remelting, sealed annealing, and rare-earth handling. [CS-2; SS-4; TS-4] No high-pressure condition is supported, and no sustained >1400 °C anneal is required by the analogues. [CS-2; SS-4; TS-4] The major severity penalty is operational rather than exotic-equipment severity. [CS-2; SS-4]

**F3 — Route 1**  
The molten alloy step improves kinetics and homogeneity, which is why Route 1 scores higher than sealed solid-state sintering. [CS-2; SS-4; TS-4] Long annealing in Dy–Ag–Sn and LuAgSn indicates that ordering and phase purity still require time. [CS-2; SS-4] The closest conflict is that CS-2 reports impurity/narrow-homogeneity sensitivity, while SS-4 reports X-ray-pure LuAgSn after anneal; I weight CS-2 strongly for composition risk because it is chemically closest to Gd–Ag–Sn. [CS-2; SS-4]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Sealed Ta-tube induction melting uses higher controlled-temperature processing and sealed containment, so its condition severity is greater than open arc melting followed by ampoule anneal. [CS-1; CS-3; SS-2; TS-2] The 1670 K CS route is just below 1400 °C, so the additional >1400 °C penalty is not applied. [CS-1; CS-3] Volatile-element retention is a real concern in the Cd analogues and is extrapolated cautiously to Sn. [CS-1; CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Induction melting provides good kinetic access through a molten state, but the best CS induction examples are RE–Pd–Cd rather than RE–Ag–Sn. [CS-1; CS-3; CS-4; CS-5] SS/TS direct reactions show Gd intermetallics can be formed in sealed Ta/quartz systems. [SS-2; TS-2] The structural-ordering uncertainty for full-Heusler Gd2AgSn remains unresolved by CS/SS/TS. [CS-2; SS-5; TS-4]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Solid-state sintering requires sealed ampoules, inert handling, high temperature, and likely multiple thermal/mechanical steps. [SS-3; TS-3] It avoids arc equipment but does not avoid controlled atmosphere or air-sensitive Gd. [SS-3; TS-3; [Reasonable Inference]] CS provides insufficient evidence for this exact sintering route and is neutral 50 for route-specific condition transfer.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Dense metallic precursors and ordered Heusler formation make kinetics slower than a melt route. [SS-3; TS-3; [Reasonable Inference]] SS-3 explicitly shows that volatility and secondary phases can require precursor optimization, which lowers the kinetics score. [SS-3; TS-3] A transient Sn-rich liquid may help diffusion, but that benefit is extrapolated rather than directly shown. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target’s simple Heusler/bcc-like coordination lowers amorphization risk relative to large mixed-polyhedra structures. [SS-5; TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]] One size-mismatch trigger is retained because Gd is much larger than Ag/Sn by metallic-radius reasoning. [Reasonable Inference] CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct ionic-radius evidence, so that subcheck uses neutral source evidence and an explicit extrapolation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Gd2AgSn is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and many nearby rare-earth intermetallic analogues are also on the hull. [CS-1; CS-3; SS-1; SS-2; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3] The synthesis evidence is strongest for bulk metallurgical preparation, especially arc melting plus annealing, because Dy–Ag–Sn and LuAgSn analogues use the same rare-earth/Ag/Sn chemical family. [CS-2; SS-4] The most important enabling factor is molten-state homogenization followed by long annealing to develop the ordered intermetallic phase. [CS-2; SS-4; TS-4] The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Gd–Ag–Sn ternary system, since CS-2 reports impurity sensitivity and narrow homogeneity behavior in related Dy–Ag–Sn samples. [CS-2] Overall, the material is experimentally plausible but should be approached as a phase-purity optimization problem rather than a one-shot synthesis. [CS-2; SS-4; [Reasonable Inference]]
