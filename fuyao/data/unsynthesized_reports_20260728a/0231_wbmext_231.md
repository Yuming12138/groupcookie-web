### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:**  
The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin by the required rubric [Target data]. This is reinforced by stable same-prototype Cu3VSe4 [CS3], stable Ni3S4 [SS2, TS1], and multiple stable thermodynamic-neighbor sulfides [TS1–TS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and the source dataset does not identify an experimental synthesis [Target data].

**F2 — Route 1:**  
Direct sealed-tube synthesis avoids >1000 °C and high pressure by following 500–650 °C long-anneal analogues [CS1, CS3]. The main penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur containment, and multi-step grind/anneal/regrind workflows [CS1, CS3, SS1]. Toxicity is treated as normal inorganic powder risk rather than a special route penalty [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1:**  
Elemental precursors are kinetically slower than salts or sulfides, but direct elemental sulvanite synthesis is demonstrated for Cu3VSe4 [CS3]. Long annealing in CS1 and CS3 indicates slow diffusion/order development, so F3 is moderate rather than high [CS1, CS3]. Same-prototype structural transferability offsets some kinetic penalty [CS3].

**F2 — Route 2:**  
Binary-sulfide precursor annealing still requires vacuum or sulfur-buffered conditions and volatile sulfur control [CS5, TS5]. It is multi-step because V2S3/Ni sulfides may need preparation before final reaction [CS2, TS5]. It avoids high pressure and can plausibly remain below 1000 °C [CS5, TS5].

**F3 — Route 2:**  
Using V/Ni sulfides should improve precursor activity because M–S bonds are already present [CS5, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion and cation ordering remain nontrivial, but sulfide-to-sulfide conversion should be easier than direct reaction of dense metals [Reasonable Inference supported by CS5, TS5]. This gives the best balance of kinetics and control among the routes.

**F2 — Route 3:**  
CVT requires evacuated ampoules, halogen transport agents, and a temperature gradient [CS2, CS4, TS3]. These conditions are standard but chemically more severe due to volatile/toxic halogens and multistep handling [CS2, CS4]. High pressure is not required [CS2, CS4, TS3].

**F3 — Route 3:**  
Vapor transport can overcome slow solid diffusion and is proven for V–Cu–S and Ta–Ni–S sulfides [CS2, TS3]. However, V–Cu–S CVT reports competing sulfide phases, so composition and phase selectivity are uncertain [CS2]. CVT is therefore attractive for crystals but not necessarily the easiest first synthesis route.

**F2 — Route 4:**  
Hydrothermal synthesis is mild in temperature and uses standard autoclaves for Ni3S4 and FeS analogues [SS2, TS1, TS4]. The added sulfurization anneal introduces controlled atmosphere, sulfur volatility, and multistep penalties [TS5, CS3; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient hydrothermal signal for V–Ni–S sulvanite and is treated as neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 4:**  
Soluble salts and Na2S provide excellent precursor activity, as shown for Ni3S4 and FeS [SS2, TS1, TS4]. The weak point is structural reconstruction: no provided source demonstrates hydrothermal crystallization of a ternary sulvanite V–Ni–S phase [SS2, TS1, TS4]. The route is best viewed as a reactive precursor strategy, not a confirmed direct crystallization path [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:**  
The structure is compact and tetrahedral, and same-prototype CS3 shows an ordered sulvanite framework rather than a large complex cell [CS3]. Edge and corner sharing are present, but face-sharing is not reported for the target or CS3, reducing amorphization risk [CS3; Target data]. The main F4 penalty is the inferred cation-radius/valence mismatch between V and Ni, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct radius data and the trigger is based on [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VNi3S4 is likely a DFT ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and thermodynamic-neighbor sulfides include several stable compounds [Target data, TS1–TS5]. The strongest synthesis support is the exact sulvanite analogue Cu3VSe4 made by long sealed-tube reaction, plus Ni–S analogues showing compatible tetrahedral Ni–S bond lengths [CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1]. The most enabling factor is the combination of zero hull energy and a known sulvanite chalcogenide topology [Target data, CS3]. The main limiting factor is cation chemistry: replacing Cu-like monovalent tetrahedral sites with Ni is chemically less standard and may compete with stable Ni3S4 or spinel-type sulfides [CS3, SS2, TS1, CS4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful sulfur chemical potential and repeated annealing rather than a quick one-step reaction [CS1, CS3, TS5].
