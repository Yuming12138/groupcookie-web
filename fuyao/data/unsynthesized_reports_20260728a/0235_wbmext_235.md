### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
MgIrF6 is a DFT ground-state candidate with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest rubric band. (Target) CS, SS, and TS analogues are also mostly on the hull, including same-family MgPdF6 and MgPbF6, which supports thermodynamic plausibility. (CS1; TS5; SS1; SS2) The uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because the target is theory-only and the source dataset does not identify a synthesis. (Target)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, because closest MgPbF6/MgPdF6 analogues form under F2 at roughly 100–500°C. (CS1; TS5) The severity score is reduced mainly for F2 atmosphere, multi-step fluorination, and toxic/reactive reagents. (CS1; TS5; SS1) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Ir precursor behavior, so that precursor-specific sub-signal is neutral 50 and treated as **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to other routes because same-topology MgPbF6 and MgPdF6 form in hours under F2. (CS1; TS5) The main kinetic penalty is Ir cation ordering and possible dense Ir precursor reactivity, which is **[Reasonable Inference]** because CS/SS/TS contain no Ir analogue. (CS1; TS5) The structural-reconstruction score is high because the target and MgPbF6/MgPdF6 share R-3 corner-sharing MgF6/MF6 networks. (Target; CS1; TS5)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is harsher because Pt analogues require staged fluorination, 350–400 atm F2, and optional sealed Au-tube annealing. (CS2; CS4) The rubric does not apply the >5 GPa deduction, but reactive atmosphere, sealed processing, multi-step treatment, and F2 toxicity all apply. (CS2; CS4) The uncertainty is whether Ir truly needs high F2 pressure, which is **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly shown. (CS2; CS4)

**F3 — Route 2**  
High F2 pressure improves fluorination activity, but the multi-day treatments in CoPtF6/NiPtF6 analogues imply slower ordering than Route 1. (CS2; CS4) The R-3 Pt fluoride frameworks are structurally relevant, but they do not contain Mg or Ir, so transferability is partial. (CS2; CS4; Target) SS/TS provide general high-pressure fluoride support through VF4 but not target-specific ternary Mg–Ir kinetics. (SS5; TS4)

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 uses moderate temperature but requires sealed inert handling and likely air-sensitive IrF4, so condition severity remains high. (SS4; TS3; CS3) Sealed-tube analogues show that volatile/reactive fluorides can require containment and long annealing. (SS4; TS3) Direct IrF4 evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral 50 for precursor specificity.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Kinetics are the weakest because binary fluorides such as MgF2 are relatively unreactive and CuMoF6 required weeks in a sealed tube. (SS4; TS3) Structural reconstruction from MgF2 + IrF4 into R-3 MgIrF6 is plausible but not directly demonstrated. (Target; SS4; TS3) Competing binary fluoride impurities are a meaningful risk because CuMoF6 analogue reports impurity/phase-control difficulty. (SS4; TS3)

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 has moderate nominal temperature but uses sealed F2/Xe, Au tubes, and long heating, so the hazard/complexity remains high. (CS3) SS/TS do not directly validate MgIrF6 CVT, so CVT-specific support from SS/TS is insufficient and scored neutral 50. (SS5; TS4) The route is better viewed as a crystal-growth/annealing route after powder synthesis. (CS3)

**F3 — Route 4**  
Vapor transport can reduce diffusion limitations, but CS3 still requires 30–35 days and careful thermal control. (CS3) The target-specific transport chemistry is uncertain because MgIrF6 volatility and Ir oxidation state stability are not shown in CS/SS/TS. (CS3; Target) The kinetics score is therefore moderate rather than high. (CS3)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a simple corner-sharing octahedral network and no edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered. (Target; CS1; TS5) Mg–F and Ir–F bond lengths are close, and the same Mg–M–F6 topology is experimentally represented by MgPbF6 and MgPdF6 analogues, so cation-size frustration appears low. (Target; CS1; TS5) SS analogues are less decisive for F4 because several ranked SS entries have different A-site coordination or dimensionality. (SS1; SS5)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgIrF6 is likely a DFT ground-state phase rather than a high-energy metastable phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. (Target) The best synthesis support comes from MgPbF6 and MgPdF6, which share the R-3 MgF6/MF6 corner-sharing framework and are made by direct F2 fluorination. (CS1; TS5) The most important enabling factor is structural transferability from known Mg–M hexafluoride analogues at moderate temperature. (CS1; TS5; Target) The most important limiting factor is Ir-specific fluorine chemistry, because no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates Mg–Ir–F synthesis or Ir precursor selection. (CS; SS; TS) Overall, the material is **moderately-to-highly synthesizable** for a fluorine-capable inorganic laboratory, but not routine because F2 handling and Ir oxidation/volatility control dominate the risk. (CS1; TS5; CS2; CS4)
