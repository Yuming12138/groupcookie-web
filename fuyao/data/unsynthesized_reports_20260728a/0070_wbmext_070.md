### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull at 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high [Target]. SS and TS analogues reinforce this because the closest Ge-containing oxide analogues are stable or nearly stable, including multiple E_hull = 0.0 examples [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5]. CS is supportive but mixed, since CS-1/CS-2/CS-4 are stable or near-stable whereas CS-3 and CS-5 are high above hull [CS-1–CS-5]. The main uncertainty is that the target is WBM theory-only and not experimentally identified in the provided dataset [Target].

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Solid-State / Ceramic**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because SS/TS analogues use air-fired oxide synthesis up to 1330 °C without high pressure or inert/reducing atmosphere [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1, TS-5]. The kinetic score is moderate-high because GeO₂ is a stable oxide precursor and the binary composition avoids multication diffusion, but polymorph conversion still requires Ge–O network rearrangement [SS-1–SS-5; Target; Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for this route and is scored neutral 50 for solid-state transferability [CS-1–CS-5].

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Flux / HT Solution Growth**  
Route 2 has moderate severity because it uses high temperature near 1080 °C but ambient air pressure and standard Pt-crucible oxide-growth equipment [TS-3]. The kinetic score is the highest because a molten flux should improve precursor activity and ion transport relative to dry solid-state diffusion [TS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The residual uncertainty is phase selectivity, because TS-3 demonstrates Ca–Ge oxide flux growth rather than binary β-cristobalite GeO₂ [TS-3; Target].

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal**  
Route 3 has the mildest thermal severity because CS/TS hydrothermal analogues crystallize oxides/arsenates at 100–360 °C in sealed vessels [CS-1; CS-2; TS-4]. Its kinetic score is limited by structural transferability, because the hydrothermal analogues are arsenic oxides or calcium arsenate and do not prove β-cristobalite GeO₂ selection [CS-1; CS-2; TS-4]. SS gives insufficient hydrothermal evidence and is scored neutral 50 for Route 3 [SS-1–SS-5].

**Route 4 F2/F3 — High Pressure**  
Route 4 is strongly penalized because CS-5 requires ultra-high pressure and laser heating, and the route is experimentally specialized [CS-5]. Its kinetics and transferability are weak because CS-5 produces dense octahedral pyrite-type SiO₂, whereas the target is a corner-sharing tetrahedral GeO₂ framework [CS-5; Target]. SS and TS provide insufficient high-pressure evidence and are scored neutral 50 for this route [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
F4 is low-risk because the target is a simple binary GeO₂ with one cation species and corner-sharing GeO₄ tetrahedra only [Target]. SS/TS analogues support that GeO₄ tetrahedral networks with Ge–O distances near 1.73–1.80 Å are synthetically accessible [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although the provided WBM status is theory-only rather than experimentally validated [Target]. SS and TS provide strong support for GeO₄-based oxide synthesis because several structurally/thermodynamically similar germanates are made by air-fired ceramic or flux-like routes and are at or near the hull [SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5]. CS gives weaker and more mixed support because it includes hydrothermal oxides and high-pressure SiO₂/Sb₂O₅ cases that are chemically relevant but structurally less direct [CS-1–CS-5]. The key enabling factor is the simple binary GeO₂ composition with a Ge–O distance matching known GeO₄ tetrahedral analogues [Target; SS-1–SS-5]. The key limiting factor is polymorph control: the route must crystallize or retain β-cristobalite GeO₂ rather than forming amorphous GeO₂ or another GeO₂ polymorph [Target; Reasonable Inference].
