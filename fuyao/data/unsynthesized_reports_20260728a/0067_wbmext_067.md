### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin, and the closest K₂TeO₃/Cs₂TeO₃ analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 (Target data; CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The high F1 score is reinforced by multiple listed thermodynamic analogues at E_hull = 0.0, but no CS/SS/TS source confirms experimental Rb₂TeO₃, so experimental confirmation is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because the closest tellurite analogues form at ~700–830 °C in gold vessels under Ar/anhydrous conditions (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The main severity deductions are for controlled dry atmosphere and tellurium/alkali handling; no high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is supported by CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has similar moderate temperatures but greater handling severity because it uses Rb₂O, and related Rb₂O syntheses require exclusion of air and controlled heating/cooling (SS5, TS5). The melt route is strongly supported by K₂TeO₃ and Cs₂TeO₃, but precursor sensitivity makes it less practical than carbonate synthesis (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has mild temperature and no >5 GPa pressure requirement because CS5 uses 493 K hydrothermal conditions in a Teflon-lined vessel (CS5). However, SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for hydrothermal Krennerite tellurite synthesis and are neutral 50, so the lower condition severity does not translate into high confidence.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Carbonate + TeO₂ kinetics are reasonable because Cs₂CO₃ + TeO₂ directly forms Cs₂TeO₃ and K₂TeO₃ confirms the same tellurite structure family is accessible (CS2, SS1, TS1; CS1, SS2, TS2). The target should require only moderate reconstruction because K₂TeO₃ is isostructural and Cs₂TeO₃ is very close, but ≥15 h heating suggests ordering/phase completion is not instantaneous (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2).

**F3 — Route 2**  
Melt growth gives the best kinetic pathway because liquid or self-flux transport bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion barrier, as shown by K₂TeO₃ and Cs₂TeO₃ slow-cooling/melt protocols (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The kinetic score is high, but practical precursor sensitivity from Rb₂O keeps Route 2 from being the best overall route (SS5, TS5).

**F3 — Route 3**  
Hydrothermal transport can be kinetically favorable in general, and CS5 supports oxyanion crystallization at 493 K (CS5). The structural-reconstruction score is low because no CS/SS/TS source shows hydrothermal formation of the Krennerite-derived alkali tellurite topology, and SS/TS hydrothermal evidence is insufficient and neutral 50.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is applied: Rb⁺ and Te⁴⁺ are very different cations by radius/charge [Reasonable Inference], while the closest K/Cs tellurites show that such cation contrast can still crystallize in ordered phases (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The unit cell and connectivity are not flagged as high-complexity because the analogues are small Krennerite-derived structures without simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2).
