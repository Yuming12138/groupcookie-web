### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s zero hull energy places it in the 90–100 bracket, and this is strongly reinforced by on-hull LaAsTe and YAsSe/REAsSe analogues. (CS1, TS1–TS5) SS gives weaker but nonzero support because related Y/Ln tellurides exist but are higher-energy and high-pressure-stabilized. (SS1–SS5) Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because the provided target is theory-only while CS/TS analogues are experimentally synthesized. (CS1, TS1, TS2)

**F2 — Route 1 Flux Severity**  
Flux growth avoids >1000 °C and avoids external high pressure, which lowers severity. (CS1, CS5, TS2) The score is reduced for vacuum/Ar handling, sealed volatile As/Te chemistry, multi-step cooling/washing, and toxic arsenic. (CS1, CS5, TS1, TS2) SS is insufficient for judging this route because it only reports high-pressure binary tellurides, so SS is neutral = 50 for Route 1 transferability. (insufficient SS)

**F3 — Route 1 Flux Kinetics**  
The molten alkali-halide flux should improve mass transport relative to pellet diffusion, and TiAsTe/LnAsTe flux analogues formed successfully. (CS1, CS5, TS2) The Pnma LaAsTe analogue gives very favorable structural reconstruction because the target appears isostructural after replacing La with Y. (CS1, TS2; [Reasonable Inference]) The uncertainty is that Y was not listed in the reported LnAsTe flux series, so ionic-size transferability must be tested experimentally. (CS1, TS2)

**F2 — Route 2 Solid-State Severity**  
Solid-state ampoule synthesis uses moderate temperatures and no external pressure, but it requires sealed ampoules, repeated heating/grinding, and arsenic-containing precursors. (TS1, TS3–TS5, CS4) The severity score matches Route 1 because both routes share sealed volatile/toxic arsenide chemistry. (CS1, TS1, CS4) SS is insufficient for ambient ternary solid-state scoring because its analogues are high-pressure binary tellurides, so SS is neutral = 50 for this route. (insufficient SS)

**F3 — Route 2 Solid-State Kinetics**  
YAsSe strongly supports structural plausibility, but TS analogues require several-day reheating and show stoichiometry sensitivity from arsenic loss. (TS1, TS3–TS5) Elemental Y/rare-earth metal diffusion in pellets is slower than flux-mediated transport, so the kinetics score is lower than Route 1. (TS1, TS3–TS5; [Reasonable Inference]) CS2 also warns that direct solid-state telluride analogues can suffer phase-control issues and binary byproducts. (CS2)

**F2 — Route 3 High-Pressure Severity**  
The high-pressure route is penalized heavily because SS analogues require up to 100 kbar and up to 1200 °C. (SS1–SS5) SS does not specify atmosphere, so atmosphere severity is treated as insufficient evidence from SS and neutral = 50 rather than an added deduction. (insufficient SS) Volatile As/Te containment and toxic precursor handling remain limiting by [Reasonable Inference] from arsenide/telluride chemistry. (CS1, TS1, SS1–SS5)

**F3 — Route 3 High-Pressure Kinetics**  
High temperature and pressure can help densify Y–Te coordination motifs, but SS analogues are not exact ternary Pnma YAsTe analogues. (SS1–SS5) SS reports narrow P–T windows, impurities, and instability, which lowers the structural-reconstruction and phase-control portions of F3. (SS1–SS5) CS/TS do not indicate that high pressure is needed for LnAsTe/YAsSe-type phases, so CS/TS are neutral = 50 for high-pressure necessity. (insufficient CS/TS)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation type, so cation-radius disorder is not a concern. (CS1, TS1, TS2) The isostructural LaAsTe analogue has a small La4Te4As4 cell, so YAsTe is unlikely to exceed the >40-atom amorphization threshold by [Reasonable Inference]. (CS1, TS2) Edge/corner-sharing motifs are present in analogues, but face-sharing coexistence is not reported, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. (CS1, TS2, SS1–SS5)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YAsTe is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target hull energy is zero and multiple chemically/thermodynamically similar Pnma analogues are also on the hull. (CS1, TS1–TS5) The strongest synthesis support comes from LaAsTe flux growth and YAsSe sealed-ampoule synthesis, which together cover both the exact arsenide telluride structure family and the same Y–As–chalcogen chemistry. (CS1, TS1, TS2) The most critical enabling factor is the Pnma LnAsCh structural family with 9-coordinate rare-earth/Y environments and pnictogen–pnictogen bonding motifs. (CS1, TS1, TS2) The most critical limiting factor is volatile/toxic arsenic control and possible stoichiometry drift, since TS analogues report As deficits and repeated processing. (TS1, TS3–TS5) The main evidence conflict is that SS analogues suggest high-pressure stabilization for related Y/Ln tellurides, but CS/TS ternary arsenide-chalcogenide analogues are more chemically specific and therefore weighted more heavily for the target. (SS1–SS5 vs. CS1, TS1, TS2)
