## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

Structurally and chemically related MgPdF₆, CdPtF₆, PtPbF₆, and CaPdF₆ also have reported hull distances of 0.0 eV/atom [SS, TS]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target has no reported experimental synthesis and the supplied WBM candidate is theory-only [CS, SS, TS].

### F2 — Route 1

Direct fluorination is supported by MgPbF₆ and MgPdF₆ procedures using F₂ streams and multiple heating stages [CS, SS, TS]. F₂ is highly reactive and requires specialized containment, while the procedures may involve prefluorination, main fluorination, tempering, and controlled cooling [CS, SS, TS]. No deduction is applied for very high temperature or >5 GPa pressure because the analogue conditions do not reach those thresholds [CS, SS, TS]. The main uncertainty is the absence of an exact MgPtF₆ experimental recipe [CS, SS, TS].

### F3 — Route 1

The direct route benefits from reactive salt, oxide, carbonate, or acetate precursors and intimate grinding [CS, SS, TS]. Nevertheless, Pt-containing fluoride formation may require extensive chemical reconstruction and solid-state diffusion, particularly if Pt chloride precursors are used [CS, SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. The repeated formation of R-3 MgMF₆ analogues raises the structural-ordering component of the score [SS, TS]. The unspecified reaction temperature for the MgPdF₆ family limits confidence in the kinetic estimate [SS, TS].

### F2 — Route 2

The high-pressure route requires F₂ at 350–400 atm, prefluorination, prolonged autoclave operation, and often sealed Pt or Au tube annealing [CS, SS, TS]. These conditions justify deductions for reactive atmosphere, volatile/reactive gas containment, multistep processing, and toxic or air-sensitive materials [CS, SS, TS]. The stated pressure is only approximately 0.04 GPa, so the rubric’s >5 GPa deduction does not apply [CS]. The route is operationally more demanding than ambient direct fluorination even though both receive a similar rubric score under the specified deductions [CS, SS, TS].

### F3 — Route 2

High-pressure Pt fluoride analogues demonstrate that Pt-containing fluoride phases can be generated through prefluorination and extended thermal treatment [CS, SS, TS]. Pressure and prolonged annealing should improve fluorine transport and facilitate ordering, but this is an extrapolation from related compositions and is therefore a **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route receives a higher F3 score than Route 1 because the analogue procedures explicitly use extended annealing and slow cooling to obtain ordered phases [CS, SS, TS]. Direct evidence for the specific Mg/Pt combination remains absent [CS, SS, TS].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target consists of MgF₆ and PtF₆ octahedra connected through corners, with no reported mixture of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra [Target data; SS]. Comparable MgPdF₆ and Pt-containing structures are crystalline and retain related octahedral frameworks [SS, TS]. Unit-cell atom count is not reported in CS, SS, or TS, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2 and it is scored neutral. Overall, the topology suggests low amorphization tendency, although fluorine reactivity and phase competition can still prevent crystallization [CS, SS, TS].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgPtF₆ is likely a calculated ground-state phase rather than an obviously metastable compound because its supplied energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target data]. The analogue set strongly supports its chemical and structural plausibility: MgPdF₆ and MgPbF₆ reproduce the MgMF₆ corner-sharing framework, while CoPtF₆, NiPtF₆, and CdPtF₆ demonstrate the accessibility of Pt-containing hexafluoride networks [CS, SS, TS]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural and thermodynamic continuity across the MgMF₆ and MPtF₆ analogue families [CS, SS, TS]. The most critical limiting factor is controlled fluorination of Pt under F₂ without forming competing Pt fluoride, Mg fluoride, or partially fluorinated phases [CS, SS, TS]. Accordingly, the target is judged experimentally plausible but technically challenging, with Route 2 offering the strongest kinetic precedent [CS, SS, TS].
