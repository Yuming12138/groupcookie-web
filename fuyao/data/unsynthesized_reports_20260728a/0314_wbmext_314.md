## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The supplied target calculation gives \(E_\text{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom and a negative formation energy, supporting a ground-state assignment in the supplied dataset. Thermodynamic analogues Pd₂Si and Ni₂Si have \(E_\text{hull}=0\), and CuZrSi/NbNiSi are also reported at or essentially at the ground state (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2; CS-2, CS-4). CS is somewhat mixed because FeZnSb has a much larger reported hull value, so CS alone is not decisive for exact target stability. **Insufficient evidence from CS alone for the exact target; the supplied target calculation and SS/TS binary analogues justify F1 = 100.**

### F2: Route 1

Arc melting is supported by FeZnSb, CuSiTi, and GdNi₈Si₃ synthesis records (CS-1, CS-5; SS-5). The main penalties are the anticipated high melting temperature, controlled atmosphere, and multistep remelting/annealing sequence. The exact liquidus and optimum annealing temperature for SiNiPd are not supplied, so the temperature penalty is partly [Reasonable Inference].

### F2: Route 2

Sintering near 1000 °C is directly supported by CuZrSi and NbNiSi, while solid-state reaction is supported by Ni₂Si (CS-2, CS-4; SS-2). This route avoids high pressure and can use standard furnace equipment, but vacuum operation is recommended only by analogy to Ni-rich systems rather than by direct target evidence (SS-3, SS-4). Its score is therefore relatively favorable for equipment and pressure but reduced for atmosphere control and the possibility of incomplete reaction.

### F2: Route 3

Levitated-melt growth is directly demonstrated for Pd₂Si (SS-1; TS-1). It avoids high pressure and can provide excellent mixing, but requires controlled high-temperature melt handling and multiple crystallization operations. The absence of numerical target-specific growth conditions creates uncertainty, so no additional severe-temperature penalty beyond the conservative \(T>1000\) °C deduction was applied.

### F3: Route 1

The molten state increases precursor activity and mass transport relative to direct powder sintering, consistent with arc- and induction-melting precedents (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-5). However, the target structure is not directly reproduced by the cited arc-melted materials, and related ternaries may require prolonged annealing for ordering or phase purification (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-5). This supports a moderate-to-good F3 score rather than a very high one.

### F3: Route 2

Elemental powders have lower effective precursor activity than solution-derived precursors, and diffusion among dense metallic particles can be slow [Reasonable Inference]. Ni₂Si and NbNiSi establish that Ni–Si-containing intermetallics can form by solid-state processing (CS-4; SS-2), but the target’s Matlockite-type arrangement requires substantial structural reorganization relative to the cited Pnma and Cotunnite analogues. Long annealing or repeated processing reported for related systems further lowers the kinetic score (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-3, SS-4).

### F3: Route 3

Levitated melting provides the highest chemical homogeneity and diffusion rate among the proposed routes, directly supported by Pd₂Si melt growth (SS-1; TS-1). The limiting factor is nucleation selectivity: the melt may crystallize Pd₂Si or Ni₂Si before the ternary phase, since both binary compounds are thermodynamically stable in the supplied records (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). Therefore, the route has strong mixing kinetics but only moderate confidence in selective target crystallization.

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target contains mixed face- and corner-sharing polyhedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion; a similar mixture of face, edge, and corner sharing is described for FeZnSb (CS-1). The unit-cell atom count is not supplied, and conventional cation-radius comparison is not reliable for this metallic intermetallic. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is therefore assigned to C1 and C2, with neutral scoring for those checks. The resulting F4 score is 80/100.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a calculated ground-state phase rather than a deliberately metastable candidate because its supplied physical energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the associated formation energy is negative. The analogues provide strong support for synthesis: Pd₂Si is experimentally melt-grown, Ni₂Si is formed by solid-state reaction, and several chemically similar ternaries are prepared by arc melting or sintering (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of liquid-phase mixing through arc melting, which should reduce Ni/Pd/Si diffusion limitations. The most critical limiting factor is selective phase formation because stable Pd–Si and Ni–Si binaries may compete with the ternary target [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, arc melting followed by extended homogenization annealing is the most defensible initial route, with a predicted synthesizability of approximately 76/100.
