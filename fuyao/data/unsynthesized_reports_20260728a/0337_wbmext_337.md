### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin [Target data]. CS/SS/TS analogues are also mostly on or near the hull, including HoCoSn, CeNiSn, CeCuSn, Ce–Ru–In, and CeRhSn entries, so the analogue thermodynamic signal is strongly favorable [CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3]. The uncertainty is that the target is a theory-only WBM relaxed candidate and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for direct experimental synthesis of monoclinic C2/m CeCoSn, neutral 50 for exact-target experimental validation [Target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting plus annealing is standard for the closest RCoSn analogue and several Ce–Sn intermetallic analogues, so the route severity is mainly equipment/atmosphere/long-anneal rather than exotic pressure [CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1]. Deductions come from transient very high-temperature melting, sealed Sn-containing annealing, multi-step remelting/annealing, and Ce/Co handling risk [CS-2, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]]. The main uncertainty is whether vacuum annealing is required or Ar-backfilled sealed annealing is sufficient, since both styles appear among analogues [CS-2, CS-5, SS-3, TS-3].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are moderate because dense elemental metals are low-activity precursors, but arc melting produces an alloyed button and RCoSn analogues become single-phase after annealing [CS-2]. Long annealing in structurally similar Ce–Ru–In systems shows that ordering can be slow, especially for low-symmetry C2/m variants [SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4]. The key uncertainty is whether CeCoSn prefers the direct RCoSn-type Pnma analogue or the WBM monoclinic C2/m target [CS-2, SS-4, TS-4; Target data].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed Ta/high-frequency route avoids high pressure and is directly reported for CeRhSn, but still requires high-temperature sealed-tube handling [SS-3, TS-3]. The route earns deductions for high-temperature reaction, sealed Sn containment, multi-step processing, and Ce/Co handling risk [SS-3, TS-3; [Reasonable Inference]]. CS gives insufficient direct signal for high-frequency Ta-tube synthesis and is neutral 50 for this route-specific method, although CS supports the broader arc-melting chemistry [CS-2, CS-5].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
High-frequency melting should improve homogenization versus simple solid-state reaction, and the CeRhSn analogue shows phase formation by this method [SS-3, TS-3]. The kinetic score is held down because CeRhSn is hexagonal and Rh-based, not Co-based monoclinic C2/m CeCoSn [SS-3, TS-3; Target data]. The main uncertainty is whether Co fully reacts at the analogue temperature window without separate arc premelting [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
HPHT is clearly harsher because CeNiSn analogue synthesis uses 7.5–11.5 GPa and 1100–1200 °C [CS-3]. The route is penalized for high pressure, high temperature, multi-step pre-alloy/milling/HPHT/quench operations, and Ce/Co handling [CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeCoSn is likely to be a thermodynamic or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable [Target data, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1]. The structural support is moderate because C2/m Ce–Ru–In analogues show similar low-symmetry Ce–transition-metal–p-block motifs, but they also warn that pure-phase formation may require long annealing and careful control [SS-4, TS-4]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a proven arc-melt/anneal workflow for closely related stannide intermetallics [CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is polymorph/competing-phase control, especially because the direct RCoSn analogue is Pnma while the target is monoclinic C2/m and theory-only in the provided validation [CS-2, SS-4, TS-4; Target data].
