### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s provided E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin. The closest YHSe analogue and several REHSe/hydride analogues also have E_hull = 0.0, so CS/SS/TS all support high thermodynamic plausibility (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-4 / SS-3 / TS-3).

**F2 — Route 1.** Flux growth requires sealed Ta/silica containment, dried salt flux, hydride handling, and volatile chalcogen retention, so the route is not “easy” despite moderate temperatures (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-4 / SS-3 / TS-3). No high pressure or >1000 °C step is supported, which prevents a lower severity score (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1).

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are favorable relative to direct sintering because NaCl/CsCl flux enables complete reactions and single crystals in REHSe analogues (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-4 / SS-3 / TS-3). The main uncertainty is whether Te preserves the same phase window as Se [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Direct sealed solid-state reaction avoids flux workup but still requires sealed ampoules, hydride handling, and chalcogen retention (CS-1 / CS-5 / SS-2 / TS-2). The lack of a flux means the route is conditionally severe from a phase-control standpoint [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** Direct solid-state kinetics are weaker because the closest YHSe evidence specifically uses NaCl flux, and hydrogen-free conditions can lead to NaCl-type YSe rather than the hydride phase (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). Hydride halide analogues confirm sealed high-T reactions are feasible, but they do not prove rapid Y–Te–H ordering (CS-1 / CS-5 / SS-2 / TS-2).

**F2 — Route 3.** Mechanochemical activation has lower thermal severity but still needs controlled Ar/H₂ atmosphere and likely sealed post-annealing (TS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS and insufficient evidence from SS are treated as neutral 50 signals for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.** TS-5 supports rare-earth hydride mechanochemistry, but it also reports low yield and impurity formation, so kinetic confidence is limited. No CS or SS source directly supports mechanochemical synthesis of a P-6m2 Y–chalcogen hydride, so the route remains exploratory.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The simple single-cation 1:1:1 structure lowers amorphization risk by avoiding cation size mismatch and large unit-cell complexity (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). Mixed face/edge/corner-sharing polyhedra trigger one amorphization-risk check, giving a low–medium risk score rather than a maximum score (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1; CS-4 / SS-3 / TS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YHTe is likely a DFT ground-state candidate because the provided target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues YHSe and HoHSe are also hull-stable (TS-1 / TS-3). The strongest synthesis support comes from YHSe, which shares the Y–H trigonal planar coordination and P-6m2 framework with the target (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is sealed alkali-halide flux growth, which promotes complete REHSe reactions and single-crystal formation at 700–850 °C (CS-3 / CS-4 / SS-3 / TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is H/chalcogen stoichiometry control, because YHSe formation is hydrogen-sensitive and hydrogen-free conditions give a competing NaCl-type YSe phase (CS-3 / SS-1 / TS-1). Te volatility relative to Se is an additional concern [Reasonable Inference].
