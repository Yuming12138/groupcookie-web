(CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5) Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because the target is identified as theory-only rather than experimentally synthesized. (Target data)

**F2 — Route 1.** Sn-flux growth avoids >1000 °C processing but requires vacuum ampoules, volatile P containment, multistep growth/removal, and controlled handling. (CS1, CS5; SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3) The deductions are therefore moderate rather than severe, because no high pressure or ultra-high temperature is required. (CS1, CS5; SS2, SS4; TS3)

**F2 — Route 2.** The binary-phosphide route is penalized because it likely needs higher temperatures, vacuum sealing, P control, and repeated grind/anneal cycles. (SS2, SS4; TS3; [Reasonable Inference]) Insufficient evidence from CS exists for this non-flux route, so CS is neutral = 50 for Route 2 condition evidence. (insufficient evidence from CS)

(SS2, SS4; TS3; [Reasonable Inference]) This route remains plausible but is less supported than Sn flux because most close analogues rely on flux-mediated transport. (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5)

**F3 — Route 3.** Liquid Ga/Bi flux should improve mass transport, but the structural transfer is weaker because Ga-flux evidence is for Ca–Ga–P chemistry and Bi-flux evidence is only indirectly pnictide-related. (CS2; SS4; [Reasonable Inference]) The route is useful as a contingency if Sn flux fails, but not as the primary recommended route. (CS2; SS4; [Reasonable Inference])

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target triggers mixed-connectivity risk because the structure contains face-, edge-, and corner-sharing tetrahedral connectivity, matching several I4/mmm analogues. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc(FeP)₂ is most likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many thermodynamic analogues are also at E_hull = 0. (Target data; TS1–TS5) The analogue support is strong for flux synthesis because CS/SS/TS repeatedly show ternary phosphides grown from elemental components in Sn flux under evacuated sealed-tube conditions. (CS1, CS4, CS5; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS3) The most critical enabling factor is liquid-flux transport, which compensates for slow Sc/Fe diffusion and supports crystallization of 122-like phosphide frameworks. (CS1, CS4; SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3) The most critical limiting factor is phase competition in Fe-rich phosphide systems, because related sources report Fe₂P/Fe₃P or other impurity issues despite successful growth. (CS5; SS5; TS4) Overall, the best assessment is moderately high synthesizability, with Sn flux as the preferred first attempt. (CS1, CS4; SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3)
