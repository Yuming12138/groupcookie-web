### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull at **0.0000 eV/atom**, which places it in the 90–100 range. [Target] TS analogues are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Ta₃Ru₅B₂ and TaRuSi, so thermodynamic similarity strongly supports stability. [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5] The main uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and not identified as experimentally synthesized in the supplied dataset. [Target]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc-melting is experimentally supported for the closest Ta–Ru analogues, but it imposes high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere penalties. [SS-1, TS-1, SS-2, TS-2] Repeated remelting and annealing make the process multi-step, while no high-pressure condition is indicated by CS/SS/TS. [SS-1, TS-1, SS-2, TS-2] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a need for toxic or air-sensitive chemical precursors, so that penalty is not applied. [SS-1, SS-2]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Elemental Ta/Ru precursors are less reactive than salts or molecular precursors, but arc-melting supplies a liquid-state homogenization pathway. [SS-1, TS-1, SS-2, TS-2] The target’s bcc-like Ta–Ru coordination is structurally compatible with several bcc/distorted-bcc analogues. [Target, SS-3, TS-3, SS-4, TS-4] The remaining kinetic risk is incomplete melting or composition drift, which is suggested by Ta-rich and arc-melt mass-loss problems in related systems. [CS-1, CS-2]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Powder metallurgy avoids the very high instantaneous temperatures of arc-melting, but it still requires >1000 °C annealing and controlled atmosphere. [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2] It is multi-step because grinding, pressing, annealing, and likely regrinding are needed. [CS-3, SS-2, TS-2, Reasonable Inference] No high-pressure requirement is supported by CS/SS/TS, so high-pressure scoring remains zero deduction. [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state kinetics are the main weakness because Ta/Ru are refractory elemental metals and analogues require long anneals. [CS-3, SS-2, TS-2, Reasonable Inference] The structural target is not overly complex, but forming an ordered 1:1 intermetallic from powders likely needs repeated annealing. [Target, SS-3, TS-3, SS-4, TS-4, Reasonable Inference] This route is therefore feasible but less attractive than arc-melting. [CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target is a small binary intermetallic with bcc/distorted-bcc coordination, so it does not trigger the large-cell or mixed-connectivity amorphization checks. [Target] CS/SS/TS provide useful structural analogues but do not directly provide Ta/Ru ionic radii or the exact target atom count, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS applies to those exact F4 sub-checks and neutral source score = 50 for C1/C2 source-specific evidence. [SS-3, TS-3, SS-4, TS-4] The resulting amorphization risk is low, though thin-film or rapid-quench synthesis could still produce disorder. [Reasonable Inference]
