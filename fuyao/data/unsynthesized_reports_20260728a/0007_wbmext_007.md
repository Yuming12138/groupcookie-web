### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is calculated at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. [Target] The closest structural/thermodynamic analogues NaBiO₃ and MgBi₂O₆ are also hull-stable, and several chemically similar oxides are hull-stable as well. [SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5] The remaining uncertainty is experimental, because the target is reported as theory-only and not identified as synthesized in the dataset. [Target]

**F2/F3 Route 1.** Hydrothermal MgBi₂O₆ provides the best condition match: 200 °C, 24 h, Teflon autoclave, and NaBiO₃·nH₂O precursor. [SS2, TS2] The kinetic score is high because the route uses a preformed Bi(V) oxide precursor and solution-mediated transport rather than long solid-state diffusion. [SS2, TS2] The main uncertainty is whether larger Sr²⁺ can be incorporated without changing to a different Sr–Bi–O topology. [SS2, CS2; Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 Route 2.** The ion-exchange route has very mild conditions because AgBiO₃ forms from NaBiO₃·nH₂O at 70 °C for 1 day. [SS1, SS3, TS1] Its kinetic penalty comes from the likely need to exchange two Na⁺ per Sr²⁺ and reconstruct from an ilmenite-like Na/AgBiO₃ framework toward a hydrophilite SrBi₂O₆ framework. [SS1, SS3, TS1, SS2; Reasonable Inference] Insufficient evidence from CS for this specific exchange route is treated as neutral at 50. [CS]

**F2/F3 Route 3.** Oxygen-high-pressure synthesis is supported by SrPbO₃ at 450 °C and >3500 atm O₂, so it is credible for high oxygen fugacity stabilization. [TS3] The route is penalized because high-pO₂ equipment and controlled cooling are specialized, and Pb⁴⁺ perovskite chemistry is only partially transferable to Bi⁵⁺ hydrophilite chemistry. [TS3, SS2, TS2] Insufficient evidence from SS for high-O₂ SrBi₂O₆ synthesis is treated as neutral at 50 for this route-specific factor. [SS]

**F2/F3 Route 4.** Ambient solid-state synthesis is supported for SrBi₂O₄ from SrCO₃ and Bi₂O₃ at 750 °C for four days, but that phase is oxygen-poorer than SrBi₂O₆. [CS2] This conflicts with the MgBi₂O₆ evidence that pentavalent bismuth hydrophilite phases decompose above ~380 °C, so Route 4 is scored lower despite simple equipment. [CS2, SS2, TS2] Regrinding and long firing are common in related solid-state oxides, which lowers the kinetic score. [CS3, CS5]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrBi₂O₆ is best viewed as a **computed ground-state candidate** because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but experimental realization may still be kinetically constrained because pentavalent bismuth oxides are reported as difficult and thermally narrow. [Target, SS2, TS2] The strongest support comes from MgBi₂O₆, a hydrophilite-derived ABi₂O₆ analogue synthesized hydrothermally from NaBiO₃·nH₂O. [SS2, TS2] The most critical enabling factor is using a preformed Bi(V) precursor under low-temperature hydrothermal conditions to avoid high-temperature reduction/decomposition. [SS2, TS2] The most critical limiting factor is competition with lower-oxygen Sr–Bi–O phases such as SrBi₂O₄ under conventional solid-state conditions. [CS2, SS2, TS2] Overall, the material is promising but should be pursued first by soft-chemical hydrothermal synthesis rather than high-temperature ceramic firing. [SS2, TS2, CS2]
