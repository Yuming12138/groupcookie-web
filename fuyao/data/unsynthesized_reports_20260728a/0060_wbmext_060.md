### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Ba(AsO₃)₂ is calculated exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic score band [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues include multiple near-hull or hull-stable arsenates/oxides, especially SrAs₂O₆ and Cd(AsO₃)₂ with matching hydrophilite/P-31m-type connectivity [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The main uncertainty is that the target is theory-only in the provided WBM dataset and lacks experimental synthesis confirmation [Target].

**F2 — Route 1 Solid-State Severity**  
The closest SrAs₂O₆ analogue forms at only 700 °C in air, avoiding high pressure and controlled atmospheres [CS3, SS1, TS2]. The deductions come from multi-step grinding/heating, arsenic toxicity, and likely As₂O₅ volatility during calcination [CS3, SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Ba compound’s arsenic-loss tolerance, so that risk is treated conservatively.

**F3 — Route 1 Solid-State Kinetics**  
Kinetics are favorable because SrAs₂O₆ adopts the same octahedral framework after 36 h at 700 °C [CS3, SS1, TS2]. The carbonate route is less reactive than nitrate/solution routes, but the identical Sr analogue and related hydrophilite oxides support manageable reconstruction [SS1, SS3, TS2, TS3]. The key uncertainty is Ba’s larger A-site size and whether it stabilizes competing Ba arsenates [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 Hydrothermal Severity**  
Hydrothermal routes operate at low temperatures of ~190–250 °C and autogenous pressure, so they avoid severe thermal and GPa-pressure penalties [CS1, SS2, TS1, TS5]. Deductions remain for multi-step autoclave handling and toxic arsenic precursors [CS1, TS5; Target safety note]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves exact Ba(AsO₃)₂ growth, but the condition envelope is mild.

**F3 — Route 2 Hydrothermal Kinetics**  
Aqueous arsenate chemistry and soluble/reactive Ba sources should improve precursor activity and reduce diffusion constraints [CS1, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Ba/Sr/Cd/Hg arsenates form hydrothermally under similar autogenous conditions, and hydrophilite-type Cd(AsO₃)₂ is structurally close [TS5, SS4, TS4]. The uncertainty is whether hydrothermal speciation selects the AsO₆ metarsenate framework instead of tetrahedral arsenate phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 Solution-Derived Calcination Severity**  
The nitrate/acid analogue route supports ambient-pressure air calcination with staged heating [CS2]. Severity is increased by drying/pre-calcination/final calcination steps and arsenic toxicity/volatility [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS exists for Ba(AsO₃)₂ by nitrate or sol-gel processing, so this route is extrapolated.

**F3 — Route 3 Solution-Derived Kinetics**  
Nitrate or solution-derived precursors should improve cation homogeneity and shorten diffusion paths compared with dry BaCO₃/As₂O₅ powders [CS2; Reasonable Inference]. The target framework is supported by SrAs₂O₆ and Cd(AsO₃)₂ analogues, which increases structural-confidence scoring [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The uncertainty is whether nitrate decomposition and arsenic volatilization disturb the Ba:As ratio [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 CVT Severity**  
CVT is the most severe plausible route because arsenate(V) examples require evacuated quartz ampoules, TeCl₄, 6–20 d reaction times, and ~950–1080 °C gradients [CS5]. The method adds sealed-system, controlled-atmosphere, volatile/corrosive transport-agent, and toxic-precursor penalties [CS5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports CVT for Ba(AsO₃)₂ specifically.

**F3 — Route 4 CVT Kinetics**  
Transport-mediated growth improves mobility and can yield crystals rather than powders [CS5]. However, the demonstrated CVT products are REAsO₄ phases, not hydrophilite Ba(AsO₃)₂, so structural reconstruction and composition control are uncertain [CS5]. This route is best treated as a crystal-growth contingency after powder synthesis succeeds [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has simple P-31m connectivity with only corner and edge sharing, and no face sharing is reported [Target]. One amorphization trigger is assigned for the large Ba–As cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but the existence of ordered SrAs₂O₆ and Cd(AsO₃)₂ hydrophilite analogues reduces concern [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the larger Ba site destabilizes long-range ordering or instead expands the lattice cleanly [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba(AsO₃)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar arsenate oxides are also on or near the hull [Target, CS1–CS3, SS1, TS1–TS3]. The strongest synthesis support comes from SrAs₂O₆, which has the same P-31m octahedral AsO₆ framework and was made by a simple 700 °C solid-state route [CS3, SS1, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated stability of the hydrophilite-type AAs₂O₆ framework with octahedral AsO₆ connectivity [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The most critical limiting factor is arsenic precursor toxicity/volatility and possible competition with other Ba arsenate phases during synthesis [CS1, CS3, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, hydrothermal synthesis is the best first attempt because it combines Ba-containing arsenate precedent with mild temperatures and reduced arsenic-loss risk [CS1, TS5].
