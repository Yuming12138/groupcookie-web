### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, placing it in the highest thermodynamic score bin (Target). The top chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar analogues are also reported with **E_hull = 0.0**, which supports the plausibility of stable neighboring alkali oxide phases (CS#1–5; SS#1–5; TS#1–5). The main uncertainty is that the target is a WBM theory-only candidate with no identified experimental synthesis (Target).

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids >1000°C and high pressure, but it needs dry inert sealed-tube handling and air-sensitive Cs oxide chemistry (CS#2; SS#2–5; TS#2–5). Its kinetics are the best-supported because structurally similar Ti/Zr oxides form under comparable sealed alkali oxide conditions, though long anneals show ordering is slow (CS#2; SS#2–5; TS#2–5). Insufficient evidence from CS#1 for this Ti solid-state route is neutral 50 because CS#1 is an aqueous chromate example, not a titanate framework route.

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has easier conditions because Cs₂CO₃/TiO₂ ceramic processing can use air or dry O₂ and avoids highly reactive Cs₂O₂/CsO₂ [Reasonable Inference], with carbonate heat-treatment precedent from CS#3. Its kinetic score is lower because carbonate + TiO₂ must reconstruct into the target titanate, and CS#3 itself required repeated cycles for high purity (CS#3). Insufficient carbonate-titanate evidence from SS and TS is neutral 50 for this route-specific chemistry.

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 benefits from highly reactive peroxide/superoxide precursors, and peroxide routes are experimentally demonstrated for alkali ruthenates (CS#4; CS#5). Its severity is high because dry oxidizer handling, sealed ampoules, oxygen activity, and long anneals are required (CS#4; CS#5). Transfer to Ti is plausible but not direct, so structural reconstruction is penalized relative to Route 1 (SS#2–4; TS#2–4; [Reasonable Inference]).
