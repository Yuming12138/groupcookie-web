### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s supplied E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom places it in the 90–100 bin, so 95/100 is assigned. TS provides strong supporting signal because all five TS analogues are E_hull = 0.0, while CS and SS include near-hull sulfides and pyrite-like analogues with low or zero ground-state offsets (TS#1–#5, CS#1–#5, SS#1–#5). Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than computational because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports MgS2 synthesis (insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct MgS2 experimental validation).

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** Route 1 is condition-severe because high pressure above 5 GPa is required, and high-pressure pyrite sulfide analogues use 60–89 kbar conditions (SS#1, SS#2). Its kinetics score is high because it directly targets the same pyrite-like Pa-3 topology represented by CdS2 and ZnS2 (SS#1, SS#2). TS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure pyrite MgS2 and is treated as neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Route 2 has milder equipment demands than Route 1 but still requires evacuated ampoules, sulfur pressure control, and air-sensitive handling (TS#2–#4; [Reasonable Inference]). Its kinetics are penalized because TS ampoule analogues are not pyrite MS2 phases, while closest pyrite MS2 analogues are high-pressure products (TS#1–#5, SS#1–#2). CS provides insufficient evidence for ambient-pressure pyrite MgS2 formation and is treated as neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** CVT is plausible for crystal growth because TiS2 and a Cu–Bi sulfide were grown by sealed-tube transport with gradients (CS#1, SS#4). Its F3 is lower because neither analogue demonstrates pyrite MgS2 transport, and Mg–iodide side chemistry could disrupt Mg:S stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for CVT and is treated as neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.** Thin-film sulfurization has manageable temperature severity but poor evidence support because no CS/SS/TS source reports thin-film MgS2 or pyrite MS2 deposition (insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS). Its F3 is low because Mg and sulfur volatility complicate vapor availability and composition control [Reasonable Inference]. This route is retained only as an exploratory metastability screen [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target is a simple binary MS2 pyrite-like material with one cation, compact formula-unit count, and corner-sharing pyrite connectivity, so no amorphization checks are triggered (SS#1, SS#2, CS#4; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and CS provide useful structural analogues, while TS is not pyrite-specific and is neutral 50 for F4 (SS#1–#2, CS#4; insufficient evidence from TS). The main uncertainty is not glass formation but whether MgS2 can be trapped as the ordered pyrite phase [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgS2 is most likely a computational ground-state or near-ground-state candidate because the supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues are thermodynamically stable sulfides (TS#1–#5). The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally similar pyrite-like CdS2 and ZnS2, both of which use high-pressure synthesis and share the Pa-3 MS2 motif (SS#1, SS#2). The key enabling factor is the close structural match to known pyrite disulfide frameworks with S–S dumbbell chemistry (SS#1, SS#2; [Reasonable Inference]). The main limiting factor is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates MgS2 synthesis, and the best structural analogues require multi-GPa pressure (SS#1, SS#2; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct MgS2 synthesis). Overall, MgS2 is **moderately likely synthesizable but experimentally challenging**, with high-pressure sulfurization as the preferred first attempt (SS#1, SS#2; [Reasonable Inference]).
