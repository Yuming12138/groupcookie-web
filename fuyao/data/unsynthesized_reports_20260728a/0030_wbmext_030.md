### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic baseline is strong [Target]. CS analogues are all listed at E_hull = 0.0, and the most relevant SS/TS analogue Zr4Pd2O is also E_hull = 0.0 [CS#1–5, SS#1, TS#1]. The score is held at 95 rather than 100 because the target is theory-only and one structurally similar SS entry, Zr4Ni2O, has a high listed E_hull despite experimental synthesis evidence [Target, SS#3].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 loses severity points for >1000 °C and >1400 °C melting, multistep remelting, and Zr handling, but avoids high pressure, vacuum, and volatile sealed precursors [CS#1, SS#1, TS#1]. Its kinetics are the best because melt processing bypasses much of the refractory solid-state diffusion problem and the structural match is strong through CS#1 and SS/TS#1 [CS#1, SS#1, TS#1].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is more severe because it combines arc melting with vacuum/sealed annealing and long heat treatment, consistent with Zr3O and Zr4Ni2O analogues [SS#2, TS#2, SS#3]. Kinetics are moderate because pre-melting improves cation mixing, but long anneals imply slow ordering and oxygen equilibration [SS#2, TS#2, SS#3].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has moderate condition severity but weaker kinetics because dense Zr/Pt/ZrO2 powders are low-activity and analogues often need high temperatures, long times, or repeated cycles [CS#2–5, SS#5, TS#4, TS#5].
