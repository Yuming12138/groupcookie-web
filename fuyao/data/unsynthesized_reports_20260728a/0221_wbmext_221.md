### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamics:** The target is predicted exactly on the hull, and the closest CaPdF6 analogue plus Cs/RbRuF6 analogues are also on the hull [Target data, CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental: the target is a theory-only WBM candidate with no reported synthesis in the supplied target data [Target data]. CS2 and CS4 have higher hull energies and are less direct, so they are down-weighted rather than used as negative proof [CS2, CS4].

**Route 1 F2/F3:** F2 fluorination is condition-severe because elemental fluorine is hazardous, but temperatures are low and no high pressure is needed [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Kinetics are favorable because the exact CaPdF6 topology forms by direct fluorination and Ru fluoride analogues form under F2, although Ru diffusion/volatility remains a constraint [CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. CS3 and CS5 synthesis snippets are unrelated oxide syntheses, so their synthesis-route signal is **insufficient evidence from CS**, neutral **50** [CS3, CS5].

**Route 2 F2/F3:** Sealed binary-fluoride annealing has moderate thermal severity but needs closed corrosion-resistant containment, fluorine-potential control, and likely RuF4 or AgF2/F2 buffering [SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because CaF2 and Ru fluorides require solid-state reconstruction and longer holds, as analogues use 20 h to 14 d treatments [SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3]. CS provides only partial support for this route through CaPdF6 topology, not sealed-binary-fluoride conditions [CS1].

**Route 3 F2/F3:** Hydrothermal synthesis has low temperature but uses toxic HF/NH4HF2 in sealed autoclaves [CS2, TS5]. Solution transport helps kinetics, but no hydrothermal CaRuF6 or RuF6 analogue is supplied, and TS5 reports low-yield crystals while CS2 notes polymorph/phase-control issues [CS2, TS5]. Structural-similarity evidence gives **insufficient evidence from SS** for this hydrothermal route, neutral **50**.

**Route 4 F2/F3:** Flux growth can improve crystallization and transport, and FeZrF6 is explicitly described as flux-grown [CS4]. However, CS4 lacks temperature/time detail, and Rb/Cs fluoride environments may stabilize competing RbRuF6/CsRuF6 phases rather than CaRuF6 [CS4, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. TS provides **insufficient explicit flux-growth evidence**, neutral **50** for flux-specific scoring.

**F4 — Amorphization:** One amorphization trigger is assigned because Ca2+ and Ru4+ differ substantially in effective octahedral size, reflected by target Ca–F 2.28 Å vs Ru–F 1.95 Å [Target data, Reasonable Inference]. The target is not penalized for large cell size or mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity because the provided description is a simple corner-sharing octahedral ABF6 network [Target data, CS1]. SS/TS Ru analogues are less direct for F4 because their alkali-site polyhedral connectivity differs from the target, so SS/TS are neutral **50** for direct amorphization evidence [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaRuF6 is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state candidate** rather than a purely metastable phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several close fluoride analogues are also on the hull [Target data, CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The synthesis case is strongest for direct F2 fluorination because CS1 matches the CaM F6 R-3 octahedral framework and SS/TS Ru analogues demonstrate RuF6 chemistry under F2 [CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4]. The key enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, which can convert Ca/Ru salts into a fully fluorinated Ru(IV)-fluoride network [CS1, SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. The key limiting factor is controlling Ru oxidation/volatility and avoiding competing Ru fluoride or alkali ruthenium fluoride phases [SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite plausible but requires a specialized fluorination setup rather than routine oxide-style solid-state synthesis [CS1, SS1, TS1].
