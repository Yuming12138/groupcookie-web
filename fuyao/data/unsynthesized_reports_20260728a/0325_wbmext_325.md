### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom from the supplied target data, and the closest thermodynamic analogues Li2PdGa, Li2PdAl, and LiPd2Tl are also on the hull (TS1, TS3, TS4). Chemically similar Li2CdPb, Li2PtIn, and Li2AuSn are likewise reported at E_hull = 0.0, supporting a high thermodynamic score (CS1, CS2, CS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is WBM theory-only, so CS/SS/TS provide analogue stability rather than direct experimental confirmation of Li2TlPd.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because relevant analogues synthesize at 650–900 °C under Ar in sealed ampoules/crucibles (CS2, SS1, SS3, TS4). Deductions come from sealed handling and toxic/air-sensitive elemental precursors, especially Li and Tl by analogy to LiPd2Tl and Li-rich intermetallic handling (TS4, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Li2TlPd requires the long 4-week LiPd2Tl-style anneal or shorter Li2PdAl/Ga-style holds (SS1, SS3, TS4).

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because same-prototype Li2PdGa/Li2PdAl reduce structural reconstruction risk, but Pd incorporation may be slow and TS4 reports minor unreacted Pd after long heating (SS1, SS3, TS4). The low synthesis temperatures relative to Pd’s melting point imply Li/Tl-assisted transport rather than full solid Pd diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS2, SS1, TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is phase competition in the Li–Pd–Tl ternary because only LiPd2Tl, not Li2TlPd, is experimentally represented in TS.

**F2 — Route 2**  
The melt/flux route uses temperatures below 1000 °C, but it incurs larger severity deductions because SS5-style Li-rich growth uses welded Ta tubes, evacuated quartz jackets, staged heating, slow cooling, and long annealing (SS5). This route also inherits Li/Tl volatility and toxicity concerns [Reasonable Inference from TS4, SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS for a true Li/Tl flux route to this prototype is scored neutral = 50 for that specific route-transfer signal.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Liquid Li/Tl-rich media should improve transport and Pd dissolution [Reasonable Inference], and Li-rich intermetallics are demonstrably accessible from high-temperature elemental melts (SS5, TS5). However, SS5 reports peritectic/incongruent behavior and persistent impurity phases, so structural and compositional control are weaker than for direct same-prototype solid-state analogues. The main uncertainty is whether Li2TlPd crystallizes congruently from a Li/Tl-rich melt or prefers competing Li–Tl, Li–Pd, or LiPd2Tl phases.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target-like F-43m analogues are simple ordered intermetallics, so large-cell amorphization risk is low for C2 (CS2, SS1, SS3). Mixed corner/edge/face-sharing connectivity is present in target-like Li–Pd intermetallic descriptions, so C3 is triggered and raises disorder/amorphization risk (SS1, SS3). Direct ionic-radius data are insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral = 50, but C1 is triggered by [Reasonable Inference] from Li/Pd/Tl size mismatch.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li2TlPd is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and multiple close analogues are also on the hull (CS1–CS3, TS1, TS3, TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from same-prototype Li2PdGa/Li2PdAl and exact ternary LiPd2Tl, all made by elemental high-temperature routes under Ar (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3, TS4). The key enabling factor is that Li–Pd–main-group F-43m phases are already experimentally accessible using standard sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis (CS2, SS1, SS3). The key limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially unreacted Pd and long equilibration seen in LiPd2Tl (TS4). A secondary limitation is thallium toxicity and sealed inert handling, which makes the synthesis operationally hazardous [Reasonable Inference from TS4 and target safety note].
