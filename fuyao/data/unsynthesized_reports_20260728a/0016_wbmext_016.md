### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. (Target data) The closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue Cs2TeO3 also has E_hull = 0.0, and several related Cs oxoanion analogues are also hull-stable. (CS1; SS1; TS1; CS2; SS2; SS3; TS2; TS3) The remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and lacks reported experimental synthesis in the provided data. (Target data)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C, high pressure, and reactive atmospheres, matching the moderate solid-state conditions used for Cs2TeO3 and related Cs oxides. (CS1; CS2; CS5; SS1; TS1) Deductions come from the likely need for SeO2 volatility control, multi-step grinding/annealing, and selenium toxicity. ([Reasonable Inference]; Target safety note) The main uncertainty is whether SeO2 loss shifts stoichiometry before Cs2SeO3 fully forms. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable because the closest analogue is isostructural Cs2TeO3, and carbonate/oxide routes repeatedly form cesium oxoanion phases. (CS1; CS2; CS5; SS1; TS1) Structural reconstruction is limited relative to unrelated solids because Cs2TeO3 already has P321 CsO6/TeO3 topology analogous to the target CsO6/SeO3 topology. (CS1; SS1; TS1; [Reasonable Inference]) The uncertainty is phase competition with Cs2SeO4 or Se-deficient products if oxygen potential or Se vapor pressure is not controlled. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is penalized for sealed/vacuum or Ar ampoule handling, volatile Se-containing charge, and multi-step slow-cooling crystal growth. (CS1; SS1; TS1; SS5; TS5; [Reasonable Inference]) It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, consistent with the Cs2TeO3 melt/slow-cooling precedent.
