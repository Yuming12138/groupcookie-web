### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is scored high because the provided target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest TS analogues SrN₂, CaN₂, and BaN₂ are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. (TS#1–3) The strongest structural support comes from SS#1 and SS#2, where SrN₂/CaN₂ share the I4/mmm diazenide motif with short N–N bonding. (SS#1, SS#2) The main uncertainty is that CS#1 gives a chemically exact EuN₂-like entry with high E_hull and an NH₃ reaction snippet that does not isolate EuN₂, so CS gives a mixed thermodynamic/chemical signal rather than a clean confirmation. (CS#1)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 loses points mainly for >5 GPa pressure and hazardous azide/moisture-sensitive handling, because the closest diazenide analogues use 3–12 GPa multianvil HP/HT decomposition of azides. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3) It avoids high-temperature penalties because the relevant Ca/Sr/Ba diazenides form at 450–800 °C. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3) Remaining uncertainty is the availability and safe decomposition behavior of Eu(N₃)₂, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from alkaline-earth azides rather than directly shown. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3)

**F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has the best kinetics because azides are reactive nitrogen-rich precursors and close analogues form in short 15–20 min holds. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3) Structural reconstruction is favorable because I4/mmm SrN₂/CaN₂ are close structural matches to target EuN₂. (SS#1, SS#2; TS#1, TS#2) The uncertainty is Eu redox/stoichiometry control, because CS#1 shows Eu/NH₃ chemistry can instead form EuN and Eu amides. (CS#1)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperature, but it incurs NH₃ atmosphere, sealed volatile-reactant, multi-step, and air-sensitive/toxic handling penalties. (CS#1, CS#3, CS#4) CS evidence indicates demanding NH₃ pressures from hundreds to thousands of bar and long growth times in some cases. (CS#1, CS#3) Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports a direct ammonothermal EuN₂ route, so SS and TS are neutral for this route. (SS: insufficient; TS: insufficient)

**F3 — Route 2**  
NH₃ fluid chemistry can aid transport, but CS#3 indicates BaN₂ crystal growth may require 3–4 months, lowering the kinetic score. (CS#3) CS#1 also shows competing EuN and Eu amide products under Eu/K/NH₃ conditions, which reduces structural/selectivity confidence for EuN₂. (CS#1) The route remains plausible only as a [Reasonable Inference] combining Eu/NH₃ reactivity with BaN₂ ammonothermal diazenide evidence. (CS#1, CS#3)

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized heavily for >1400 °C operation because the Eu-containing sealed-ampoule analogue LiEu₄(BN₂)₃ is reported at 1950 K. (SS#4; TS#4) It also requires sealed inert/vacuum containment and air-sensitive nitride/Li reagents. (SS#4; TS#4, TS#5) CS provides insufficient evidence for this sealed high-temperature EuN₂ route, so CS is neutral. (CS: insufficient)

**F3 — Route 3**  
High temperature should improve diffusion, but the structural pathway is weak because the relevant Eu analogue is a ternary cubic boronitride rather than binary I4/mmm EuN₂. (SS#4; TS#4) EuN is a sensible Eu–N precursor because it appears in the LiEu₄(BN₂)₃ synthesis, but conversion of EuN into diazenide EuN₂ is a [Reasonable Inference]. (SS#4; TS#4) The main uncertainty is whether N–N diazenide units survive or form under very high sealed-ampoule temperatures. (SS#4; TS#4)

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperature because Eu₂SiN₃ flux growth is reported at 900 °C. (TS#5) It is still penalized for Ar/sealed crucible operation, volatile/reactive Li/LiN₃ chemistry, multiple handling steps, and hazardous air-sensitive precursors. (TS#5) CS provides insufficient evidence for Li-flux EuN₂ formation, so CS is neutral for this route. (CS: insufficient)

**F3 — Route 4**  
Flux chemistry improves mass transport and crystallization kinetics, as shown by Eu₂SiN₃ slow-cooling growth. (TS#5) The kinetic score is limited because the flux analogue is a ternary Eu–Si nitride rather than a binary diazenide, and SS#4/TS#4 show Eu can enter more complex Li–Eu–B–N frameworks. (TS#5; SS#4; TS#4) The main uncertainty is binary phase selectivity versus Li-containing ternary nitride formation. (TS#5; SS#4; TS#4)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
F4 is high because the closest SS/TS analogues are simple single-cation binary diazenides, which do not indicate cation-size disorder or large mixed-cation complexity. (SS#1, SS#2; TS#1, TS#2) The target-like analogues show simple N–N diazenide units and cation coordination rather than extensive mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral networks. (SS#1, SS#2; TS#1, TS#2) The only uncertainty is exact target unit-cell size, which is not explicitly provided by CS/SS/TS, so the low-amorphization assignment partly relies on [Reasonable Inference] from the simple I4/mmm analogues. (SS#1, SS#2; TS#1, TS#2)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

EuN₂ should be treated as a plausible ground-state candidate rather than a purely kinetic metastable target because the supplied target hull value is 0.0 eV/atom and the closest TS analogues SrN₂/CaN₂/BaN₂ are also hull-stable. (TS#1–3) The strongest synthesis support is structural: SrN₂ and CaN₂ share the I4/mmm diazenide framework and have very high structural/thermodynamic similarity to the target. (SS#1, SS#2; TS#1, TS#2) The most critical enabling factor is controlled high-pressure azide decomposition, which directly stabilizes alkaline-earth diazenides with N–N units. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3) The most critical limiting factor is Eu-specific phase selectivity, because CS#1 shows Eu/NH₃ chemistry can form EuN and Eu amides rather than EuN₂. (CS#1) Overall, synthesis is challenging but credible, with the multianvil azide route the most defensible first attempt. (SS#1–3; TS#1–3; CS#1)
