### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is supplied at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and close analogues in CS/SS/TS are also stable or near-stable, including HfRuP, HfOsP, ZrRhP, NbRhP, and ScIrP [CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4]. The score is high because thermodynamics does not appear to be the limiting factor, but the target is still theory-only and lacks direct experimental confirmation [CS, SS, TS].

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Cu/CuP flux.** F2 is penalized for high temperature, evacuated sealing, volatile P, and multiple processing steps, all present in the flux analogue [CS1, SS2, TS2]. F3 is comparatively strong because the HfRuP analogue has the same P-62m topology and closely matching coordination, so the required structural rearrangement is small [CS1, SS2, TS2]. The main uncertainty is that the recorded precursor text references ZrRu while the analogue formula is HfRuP, so HfRh master-alloy transfer is a **[Reasonable Inference]** [CS1, SS2, TS2].

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Bi flux.** F2 is similar to Route 1 because ScIrP uses evacuated silica, red P, 1370 K, long annealing, and controlled slow cooling [SS5, TS4]. F3 is slightly lower than Route 1 because the structure is the same P-62m type, but the cation pair Sc/Ir is less chemically direct than Hf/Ru to Hf/Rh [SS5, TS4]. CS gives insufficient evidence for Bi-flux synthesis and is therefore neutral 50 for this route [CS].

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Arc melting plus annealing.** F2 is lower because arc melting introduces very high local temperatures, Ar control, sealed annealing, and P-loss management [CS5, SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3]. F3 is lower because dense refractory elements have slow ordering kinetics and the closest Rh-containing bulk analogues are Pnma rather than P-62m [SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3]. This route is useful for bulk screening but is less selective for the target hexagonal phase [SS1, TS1].

**Route 4 F2/F3 — Two-step phosphidation.** F2 is penalized for prealloying, evacuated sealed-tube phosphidation, P volatility, and multi-step processing [TS5]. F3 is the weakest because the supporting analogue Ce(RhP)₂ is tetragonal and compositionally different, so the pathway to P-62m HfRhP is indirect **[Reasonable Inference]** [TS5]. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral 50 [CS, SS].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The small cell size of the analogues reduces amorphization risk, while likely Hf/Rh size mismatch and mixed face/edge/corner-sharing connectivity increase it [CS1, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4]. The crystalline synthesis of multiple related phosphides indicates that amorphization is not prohibitive, but mixed connectivity and cation-size contrast justify a medium-risk score **[Reasonable Inference]** [CS1, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfRhP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several close analogues are also hull-stable [CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2]. The strongest synthesis support comes from same-prototype hexagonal P-62m HfRuP and ScIrP flux-grown analogues [CS1, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4]. The most important enabling factor is liquid-flux transport, which can overcome slow Hf/Rh diffusion while preserving the small P-62m ternary phosphide framework **[Reasonable Inference]** [CS1, SS2, TS2]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition with orthorhombic Rh-containing MTP analogues, which are repeatedly reported for ZrRhP/TaRhP/NbRhP under arc/anneal conditions [SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with Cu/CuP flux growth preferred over direct arc melting because it best matches the target prototype [CS1, SS2, TS2].
