## SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target ScRhP is predicted to be thermodynamically stable at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull and has an orthorhombic Pnma structure with the same 11-coordinate Sc–metal–P topology reported for ScCoP, ZrRhP, and ScPtP. **[Target record; CS-1; CS-4; SS-1]** The strongest transferability comes from ZrRhP because it contains Rh and P and crystallizes in Pnma with closely matching Rh–P distances of 2.42–2.54 Å versus 2.41–2.52 Å for the target. **[Target record; CS-4]**

## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied calculated energy above hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category under the required rubric. **[Target record]** The negative raw normalized value was clipped to zero because physical distance above the convex hull cannot be negative. **[Target record]** Stable analogues ScCoP, ZrRhP, ScPtP, and ScNiP also have reported zero hull distances, providing convergent support for the chemical system. **[CS-1; CS-4; SS-1; SS-4; TS-1; TS-4]** The main uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and has not been experimentally validated. **[Target record]**

### F2 — Route 1

Sn-flux synthesis is moderately severe because it requires evacuated containment, phosphorus management, prolonged heating, slow cooling, and flux removal. **[CS-1; SS-3]** No high pressure or temperature above approximately 1000 °C is required. **[CS-1; SS-1]** The score remains uncertain because ScRhP-specific flux composition and phase-purity data are absent; therefore the exact F2 estimate is an extrapolation. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting plus annealing receives the lowest F2 score because it combines controlled Ar/vacuum conditions, high-temperature melting, sealed phosphorus-containing annealing, and multiple processing steps. **[CS-4; SS-4]** The route is nevertheless experimentally credible because ZrRhP was made by essentially this sequence. **[CS-4]** The deduction for air-sensitive precursors is conservative because the supplied records do not explicitly quantify handling hazards for Sc or P. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F2 — Route 3

Direct sealed-tube reaction avoids arc-furnace hardware and temperatures above 1000 °C, but it still requires a controlled evacuated tube and phosphorus retention. **[SS-2; SS-5]** Intermediate grinding and reheating are likely necessary because Sc and Rh are refractory and diffusion is limited in a dry powder compact. **[Reasonable Inference]** The precise temperature window for ScRhP is not provided, so there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for a target-specific optimum.

### F2 — Route 4

Pb flux provides a practical low-pressure route near 1000 °C and has been used for the closest Sc–metal–P structural analogue, ScPtP. **[SS-1; TS-1]** The score is reduced by sealed handling, phosphorus volatility, toxic Pb handling, and chemical flux removal. **[SS-1; Reasonable Inference]** The principal uncertainty is whether Pb will promote or suppress Rh-containing competing phases; there is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Sc–Rh–P/Pb compatibility.

### F3 — Route 1

The Sn flux improves precursor contact and mass transport, which is supported by successful ScCoP flux growth at 850 °C. **[CS-1]** Structural reconstruction is scored highly because ScCoP and ZrRhP share the target’s Pnma coordination topology. **[Target record; CS-1; CS-4]** The remaining kinetic risk is the refractory Rh component and possible formation of binary Rh phosphides. **[CS-4; Reasonable Inference]**

### F3 — Route 2

Arc melting can overcome initial solid–solid contact limitations, and ZrRhP demonstrates that an Rh-containing phosphide can be obtained through this route. **[CS-4]** However, ScNiP data show that high-temperature annealing may be needed for adequate ordering or crystal quality. **[SS-4]** Since this route lacks a persistent liquid flux during annealing, diffusion and phase homogenization remain more difficult than in the flux routes. **[SS-4; Reasonable Inference]**

### F3 — Route 3

Direct solid-state synthesis is kinetically less favorable because it relies primarily on diffusion between elemental Sc, Rh, and P or between a pre-alloy and P. **[SS-2; CS-4; Reasonable Inference]** The structural similarity to ZrRhP improves the reconstruction score, but the absence of a flux and the need for repeated grinding reduce the overall F3 value. **[CS-4; SS-2; SS-5]** There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that ScRhP forms cleanly in a single sealed-tube cycle.

### F3 — Route 4

Pb flux has a strong structural precedent from ScPtP and should provide a liquid transport mechanism that lowers the effective diffusion barrier. **[SS-1; TS-1]** The risk is that Pb flux growth may favor crystals only within a narrow compositional window; ScPtP powder preparations contained impurity phases even though single crystals were obtained. **[SS-1]** Thus, this route is attractive for crystal growth but less certain for phase-pure bulk powder.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a relatively simple 1:1:1 ordered ternary with a well-defined 3D periodic structure and close ordered analogues. **[Target record; CS-1; CS-4; SS-1]** The conservative C1 trigger reflects the size mismatch between Sc and Rh, but this is only an approximate metallic/coordination-radius inference rather than a directly reported ionic-radius analysis. **[Reasonable Inference]** There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to confirm the full mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing criterion for ScRhP, so C3 is not counted.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScRhP is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase rather than an intrinsically metastable phase because its calculated physical energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. **[Target record]** CS, SS, and TS data provide strong support through ScCoP, ZrRhP, ScPtP, and ScNiP, which reproduce either the Pnma topology, the Rh–P chemistry, or both. **[CS-1; CS-4; SS-1; SS-4; TS-1; TS-3]** The most important enabling factor is the existence of a robust Sc–transition-metal–P structural family with the same high-coordinate framework. **[CS-1; CS-4; SS-1; SS-3]** The most important limitation is phase competition and phosphorus control, particularly during flux removal or high-temperature treatment. **[CS-1; CS-4; SS-1; SS-4]** Overall, experimental synthesis is plausible, with Sn or Pb flux growth preferred for initial screening and arc melting/annealing preferred for bulk confirmation.

## SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:-:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Phosphorus volatilization and loss of target stoichiometry | High | Use red P excess, evacuated sealed ampoules, and/or sealed quartz annealing. Excess P and sealed tubes are directly supported by ZrRhP and Nd(RhP)₂ procedures. **[CS-4; SS-2]** |
| 2 | All | Competing binary or ternary phosphide formation | High | Maintain accurate Sc:Rh:P ratios, use repeated homogenization, and inspect products by powder XRD. ScCoP and ScPtP reports identify impurity phases and composition-sensitive phase formation. **[CS-1; SS-1]** |
| 3 | All | Slow reaction kinetics from refractory Rh | Medium–High | Use a liquid Sn/Pb flux, arc remelting, fine powders, or pre-synthesized ScP/pre-alloyed ScRh. **[CS-1; CS-4; SS-4; Reasonable Inference]** |
| 4 | Route 1 | Incomplete removal of Sn flux | Medium | Optimize cold dilute-acid or other chemically compatible flux-removal treatment and verify that ScRhP is not attacked. ScCoP uses acid treatment after Sn flux growth. **[CS-1]** |
| 5 | Route 2 | Phosphorus loss during arc melting | High | Use pressed pellets, excess P, rapid controlled melting, and sealed post-annealing. Excess P and sealed annealing are supported by ZrRhP. **[CS-4]** |
| 6 | Route 2 | Need for specialized arc-melting and induction/vacuum equipment | Medium | Use the direct sealed-tube or Sn-flux route for initial feasibility if arc-melting infrastructure is unavailable. **[CS-4; SS-4]** |
| 7 | Route 3 | Slow ordering and incomplete conversion after one heating cycle | Medium–High | Apply intermediate grinding, repelletizing, and repeated annealing; prolonged heating with intermediate grinding is used for related RE–Pt–P materials. **[SS-5]** |
| 8 | Route 4 | Pb toxicity and chemical waste from flux removal | Medium–High | Use appropriate containment and waste procedures; reserve Pb flux primarily for crystal-growth screening. The need for Pb-flux removal is documented for ScPtP. **[SS-1]** |
| 9 | Routes 1 and 4 | Narrow crystal-growth/phase-purity window | High | Perform a small matrix varying flux ratio, P excess, maximum temperature, and cooling rate. ScPtP achieved clean crystals only under specific conditions and showed impurities in powder preparations. **[SS-1]** |
| 10 | All | Lack of experimental precedent for the exact ScRhP composition | Medium | Start with multiple parallel routes and confirm phase identity by powder XRD, composition analysis, and, if possible, single-crystal diffraction. The supplied target record explicitly identifies ScRhP as theory-only. **[Target record; Reasonable Inference]**
