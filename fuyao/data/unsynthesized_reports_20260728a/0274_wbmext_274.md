# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an exact physical energy above hull of 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, with a negative formation energy of −0.408610 eV atom⁻¹ [Target validation]. The normalized raw value was clipped to zero because the physical distance above the hull cannot be negative, so the zero value should be interpreted as thermodynamic stability within the dataset rather than as proof of experimental accessibility [Target validation]. The candidate is nevertheless theory-only, and no experimental synthesis has been identified [Target validation], which is why the score is 95 rather than 100.

## Route 1 — F2

Route 1 uses a relatively simple melt-and-cool sequence consistent with Ba–Tl, Ba–Hg, and Ca–Tl alloy preparation [CS, SS, TS]. The principal deductions arise from the need to contain potentially volatile Tl and to handle toxic Tl and air-sensitive Ba [Target validation; SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure, hydrogen, ammonia, or extreme temperature above 1400 °C is required [CS, SS, TS].

## Route 1 — F3

Melting provides efficient liquid-phase mixing, and related Ba–Tl and Ba–Hg syntheses demonstrate that such temperatures are experimentally accessible [CS, SS, TS]. However, the target’s orthorhombic Cmcm structure is not the same as the cubic Tetraauricupride structures of CaTl, SrTl, and YbTl or the hexagonal Tl₂Ba structure [CS, SS, TS]. Therefore, diffusion is favorable but structural ordering and phase selection remain only moderately favorable.

## Route 2 — F2

The sealed-ampoule route uses moderate annealing temperatures, but it requires more operations than direct melting: arc melting, sealing, annealing, controlled cooling, and possibly repeated heat treatment [SS, TS]. The use of sealed refractory-metal ampoules is well supported for Ba–In and rare-earth–Tl intermetallics [CS, SS, TS]. Toxic Tl and air-sensitive Ba remain important safety and handling penalties [Target validation; SS, TS].

## Route 2 — F3

The initial melting step should provide good elemental mixing, so bulk diffusion is not expected to be the dominant limitation [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. In contrast, the long thermal schedules used for Ba₉In₄—5 days at 650 °C followed by very slow cooling and 10 days of annealing—show that Ba-rich intermetallic ordering can be kinetically demanding [SS, TS]. The target’s structure differs from the major structural analogues, so a moderate structural-reconstruction score is appropriate [CS, SS, TS].

## F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has unique-prototype status, three-dimensional periodic connectivity, and a relatively simple binary composition [Target validation]. Its described coordination is predominantly eight-coordinate and body-centered-cubic-like rather than a complex mixture of polyhedral connectivities [Target structure]. The only provisional trigger is a possible Ba/Tl size mismatch, but formal ionic-radius analysis is poorly defined for this intermetallic [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference].

BaTl is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied physical energy above hull is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ and its formation energy is strongly negative [Target validation]. The CS, SS, and TS analogues provide substantial support for elemental synthesis under inert atmosphere using melting, annealing, and controlled cooling, particularly the Ba–Tl and related Ba-rich intermetallic examples [CS, SS, TS].
