### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is given as E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and every listed CS/SS/TS analogue also has E_hull = 0.0, so thermodynamic feasibility is strongly supported. (CS1–5; SS1–5; TS1–5) The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS validate nearby compounds rather than experimental NdAuPb, so experimental confirmation of the target phase is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated as an uncertainty rather than a hull penalty.

**F2 — Route 1.** Pb-flux growth avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires evacuated sealing, slow cooling, annealing, centrifugation, Pb handling, and air-sensitive lanthanide handling. (CS1–3; SS3–4; TS3–4) These conditions are standard for intermetallic flux growth but not simple, giving Route 1 a moderate severity score. (CS1–3; SS3–4; TS3–4)

**F2 — Route 2.** Arc melting under purified Ar plus sealed annealing is directly supported for NdAuSn, but adapting it to Pb introduces Pb-loss and sealed-anneal concerns. (CS5; SS1; TS1) The route is penalized more heavily than flux growth because arc melting is harsher and composition control for Pb is less forgiving **[Reasonable Inference]**. (CS5; SS1; TS1)

**F2 — Route 3.** Sealed induction/solid-state annealing benefits from lower nominal annealing temperatures around 800 °C, as shown for RAuGe analogues. (SS2, SS5; TS2, TS5) It still requires inert/sealed handling, long annealing, and Pb containment, while CS gives insufficient direct induction-route evidence and is treated as neutral for this specific route. (SS2, SS5; TS2, TS5)

**F3 — Route 1.** Pb flux should improve mass transport and nucleation because close analogues are grown as single crystals from Pb flux. (CS1–3; SS3–4; TS3–4) The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: the closest flux-grown analogues are cubic I-43d Ln₃Au₃Bi₄ phases, not half-Heusler NdAuPb, so structural reconstruction evidence is incomplete. (CS2; SS3; TS3)

**F3 — Route 2.** Arc melting gives rapid liquid-state mixing, and NdAuSn forms after 800 °C annealing for 4 d. (CS5; SS1; TS1) However, dense elemental precursors, possible Pb evaporation, and a reported minor impurity phase in NdAuSn lower the kinetics/phase-control score. (CS5; SS1; TS1)

**F3 — Route 3.** Induction-melted RAuGe analogues are reported as generally homogeneous after long 800 °C anneals, supporting feasible bulk diffusion. (SS2, SS5; TS2, TS5) The limiting uncertainty is that Pb-containing half-Heusler ordering is not directly demonstrated, so the structural pathway remains extrapolative. (SS2, SS5; TS2, TS5)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target is a small-cell ordered intermetallic rather than a large, complex framework, and structurally/thermodynamically similar RAuX analogues crystallize readily by melt/anneal or flux routes. (SS1–5; TS1–5) One size-mismatch trigger is assigned by **[Reasonable Inference]**, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius and glass-forming data, so amorphization risk is judged low–medium rather than negligible. (CS1–5; SS1–5; TS1–5)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NdAuPb is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the CS/SS/TS analogue set is dominated by on-hull intermetallics. (CS1–5; SS1–5; TS1–5) The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong chemically because Nd–Au–heavy-p-block analogues are experimentally accessed by Pb flux and arc/anneal routes, but exact half-Heusler NdAuPb is not directly demonstrated. (CS2, CS5; SS1, SS3; TS1, TS3) The most important enabling factor is the availability of a Pb-rich liquid medium that can act as both reactant and self-flux for slow crystal growth. (CS1–3; SS3–4; TS3–4) The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity, because the closest analogues favor I-43d or P6₃mc structures rather than the target F-43m half-Heusler. (CS2, CS5; SS1, SS3; TS1, TS3) Overall, the material is **moderately likely to be synthesizable**, with Pb-flux growth the best first attempt. (CS1–3; SS3–4; TS3–4)
