### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the supplied target data, and the closest analogue Ba(BeN)₂ is also a computed ground-state phase with E_hull = 0.0. (CS1, SS1, TS1) Similar thermodynamic analogues LiBeN and several Li–Ca–Al/Si nitrides also have E_hull ≈ 0, supporting nitride ground-state accessibility. (SS2, TS2, TS3–TS5) Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only WBM and no experimental synthesis is reported in the supplied target data; CS/SS/TS do not provide direct experimental Yb(BeN)₂ validation. (CS1, SS1, TS1)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized heavily because the best exact analogue Ba(BeN)₂ required high temperature, N₂/sealed Ta processing, two heating steps, intermediate grinding, and air/moisture-sensitive nitride precursors. (CS1, SS1, TS1) The score is not lower because the route avoids ultra-high pressure and uses established solid-state nitride equipment rather than autoclaves or diamond-anvil conditions. (CS1, SS1, TS1) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Yb precursor stability window, so that uncertainty is treated conservatively. (CS1, SS1, TS1)

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetic strength of Route 1 is the near-exact structural transfer from Ba(BeN)₂ to Yb(BeN)₂, which reduces reconstructive burden. (CS1, SS1, TS1) The kinetic limitation is diffusion through solid binary nitrides, evidenced by the two-step Ba(BeN)₂ synthesis and the need for regrinding. (CS1, SS1, TS1) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on Yb diffusion or Yb nitride reactivity in this exact structure, so the kinetics score remains moderate rather than high. (CS1, SS1, TS1)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 benefits from the simpler sealed-ampoule LiBeN route, which used Li₃N + Be at 1275 K and only a post-treatment vacuum step. (SS2, TS2) It is still penalized for Be toxicity, air-sensitive/reactive Yb/Be nitride handling, and sealed-system requirements. (SS2, TS2) Insufficient evidence from CS exists for a Yb analogue made from metal + nitride in a sealed steel ampoule, so the route is less certain than Route 1. (CS; SS2, TS2)

**F3 — Route 2**  
LiBeN confirms that Be metal can form trigonal-planar Be–N frameworks under sealed high-temperature conditions. (SS2, TS2) However, LiBeN is monoclinic rather than I4/mcm, so achieving the target topology likely requires more reconstruction than the Ba(BeN)₂-based route. (SS2, TS2, CS1/SS1/TS1) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Yb metal incorporation into this exact Be nitride network. (CS1, SS2, TS2)

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux-assisted growth has a better condition score because related nitrides form at 900–950 °C, below the direct Ba(BeN)₂ sintering temperature. (SS3, SS5, TS3–TS5) The route remains challenging because it requires sealed Ta ampoules, inert glovebox handling, Li flux, and toxic Be-containing precursors. (SS3, SS5, TS3–TS5; CS1/SS1/TS1) Insufficient direct evidence from CS exists for flux synthesis of Ba(BeN)₂ or Yb(BeN)₂, so the route is an extrapolation. (CS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux should improve kinetics by enhancing mass transport and lowering effective diffusion barriers, consistent with flux/metathesis analogues producing related nitrides at 900–950 °C. (SS3, SS5, TS3–TS5) The weakness is phase selectivity: several flux/metathesis analogues report heterogeneous products, side phases, or single-crystal isolation rather than clean bulk phase formation. (SS5, TS4) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists that Li flux is compatible with Yb(BeN)₂ without Li incorporation. (SS3, SS5, TS3–TS5)

**F2 — Route 4**  
NH₃/autoclave nitride routes avoid >1000 °C temperatures but require high NH₃ pressure, reactive atmosphere, specialized autoclaves, and long dwell times. (CS2, CS5) These conditions are substantially more severe operationally than sealed Ta solid-state work despite the lower temperature.
