**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
High-O₂ cold-seal synthesis is justified because PtO₂/Pt₃O₄ analogues require high oxygen pressure to stabilize oxidized Pt phases. **[SS1, TS1, TS3]** The route is penalized for controlled high-pressure oxygen handling and because binary Pt oxide references do not directly template HgPt₃O₆. **[SS1, TS1, TS3, SS2]** The uncertainty is whether high pO₂ improves target formation or instead stabilizes competing PtO₂/Pt₃O₄ phases. **[TS3; Reasonable Inference]**

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Multi-anvil synthesis is supported by high-pressure Tl–Pt, PtO₂, Ca–Cu–Pt–O, and Bi–Pd–O analogues. **[CS5, SS4, SS5, TS5]** Its severity is high because analogous syntheses use 6–17 GPa or 12 GPa and specialized capsules/apparatus. **[SS4, SS5, TS5]** Kinetics can be acceptable at high temperature, but phase selectivity is risky because high-pressure Pt oxide polymorphs and impurity phases are common. **[SS5, TS3]**

**F2/F3 — Route 5**  
Hydrothermal synthesis has mild temperature severity because CS4 demonstrates 240 °C pyrochlore formation in alkaline oxidizing solution. **[CS4]** Kinetic mixing may be favorable, but the structural evidence is weak because the hydrothermal analogue is Bi₂Ir₂O₇ rather than HgPt₃O₆. **[CS4, SS2]** There is insufficient evidence from **SS** and insufficient evidence from **TS** for hydrothermal HgPt₃O₆ crystallization, so those source signals are treated as neutral 50. **[CS4]**

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the cation-size mismatch check is triggered because Hg–O distances are much longer than Pt–O distances in the target, implying a large A-site/B-site size contrast. **[Target data, SS2, TS2; Reasonable Inference]** The >40 atom-cell trigger is not supported by CS/SS/TS, and the vertex+edge+face-sharing trigger is not met because the closest Cmmm analogue reports corner/edge sharing rather than face sharing. **[SS2, TS2, SS4]** The remaining uncertainty is that Hg substitution may introduce local disorder not captured by the analogue descriptions. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg(PtO₂)₃ is likely a **DFT ground-state candidate** because its reported hull energy is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic/structural analogue MnPt₃O₆ is also on the hull. **[Target data, SS2, TS2]** The strongest synthesis support comes from the exact Cmmm MPt₃O₆ analogue, which reproduces the target’s square-planar Pt, octahedral Pt, and 8-coordinate A-site topology. **[SS2, TS2]** Chemical analogues show that Pb–Pt–O and Tl–Pt–O oxides can be synthesized, but their structures and conditions vary from ambient solid-state to high pressure. **[CS1, CS3, CS5]** The most critical enabling factor is use of β-PtO₂/Pt redox buffering to form the mixed Pt coordination network. **[SS2, TS2; Reasonable Inference]** The most critical limiting factor is safe retention of Hg during high-temperature synthesis while avoiding PtO₂/Pt₃O₄ competing phases. **[TS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]**
