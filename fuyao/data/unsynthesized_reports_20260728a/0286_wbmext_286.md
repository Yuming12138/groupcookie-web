### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom in the supplied target record, so it falls in the 90–100 band. CS supports this because most chemically similar entries are on the hull, with only Cr2Ta modestly above hull at 0.0745 eV/atom (CS-1–CS-5). SS and TS are especially supportive because every top listed structural/thermodynamic analogue has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and CS/SS/TS do not provide exact VRe experimental confirmation.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** Arc melting has moderate severity because analogues require >1000 °C treatment, inert atmospheres, repeated processing, and sometimes quenching (CS-1; CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). Kinetics are acceptable relative to other routes because liquid arc melting mitigates refractory solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the closest VTc analogue has the same Pm-3m Tetraauricupride structure (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether Re substitution creates slower ordering or competing Re-rich phases, as Re-bearing analogues show high-temperature/long-anneal demands (SS-4; TS-4).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Powder metallurgy has similar condition severity because it uses controlled atmosphere, high-temperature sintering, pressing, and multiple processing steps (CS-3; SS-2; TS-2; SS-3; TS-3). Its kinetics score is lower because V and Re must interdiffuse as refractory elemental powders without a liquid-mixing step [Reasonable Inference], and related sintered systems report phase-control challenges (CS-3; CS-4). This route is plausible but less favored than arc melting because CS/SS/TS evidence supports sintering generally, not exact B2 VRe formation.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** Directional solidification is supported directly only by CS-2 for a Tetraauricupride analogue, while SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral 50 for directional growth. The route is severe because V–Re melt growth likely needs >1400 °C and controlled atmosphere [Reasonable Inference], consistent with difficult Re-bearing melt processing (SS-4; TS-4). Kinetics may be better than pure sintering due to liquid growth, but crystal growth and stoichiometry control remain uncertain because no V–Re directional-growth analogue is provided.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target is a simple binary Pm-3m Tetraauricupride phase with two crystallographic sites and body-centered 8-fold coordination (Target record; CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). C2 and C3 are therefore not triggered, and C1 is also not triggered by small V/Re metallic-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], although CS/SS/TS do not directly report ionic radii and are neutral 50 for C1. The amorphization risk is low because the closest analogues are crystalline intermetallics rather than glass-forming complex networks (CS-1; CS-5; SS-1; TS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VRe is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state ordered intermetallic because the supplied target hull value is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly hull-stable (Target record; CS-1–CS-5; SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from VTc, which has the same V-containing Pm-3m Tetraauricupride structure and is experimentally tied to arc melting/annealing/quenching conditions (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the simple B2/Tetraauricupride topology, which minimizes reconstruction and amorphization risk (CS-1; CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is refractory-metal homogenization and ordering involving Re, because Re-bearing analogues require specialized arc furnaces, purified atmospheres, high temperatures, or long anneals (SS-4; TS-4; SS-5; TS-5). Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with arc melting plus annealing as the best first attempt, but exact experimental confirmation remains absent from CS/SS/TS.
