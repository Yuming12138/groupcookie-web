## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV atom⁻¹, which supports a calculated ground-state assignment rather than a thermodynamically elevated metastable phase (target thermodynamic record). The closest HfCoGe and TaCoGe analogues also have zero reported hull energy, and TiRuGe is only 0.00216 eV atom⁻¹ above hull (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). Experimental uncertainty remains because the target is identified as a theory-only WBM candidate without source-dataset evidence of synthesis (target validation).

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for the controlled evacuated atmosphere and the possibility of Ge loss in a sealed high-temperature system [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure, temperatures substantially above 1000 °C, and a formally multi-step reaction, consistent with the relatively simple sintering procedures reported for HfCoGe and TaCoGe (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). CS-1 further supports evacuated silica processing for a related Nb-containing ternary compound.

### F3 — Route 1

The elemental powders provide lower chemical activation than nitrates, acetates, or sol–gel precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Nb is refractory, so solid-state diffusion is a likely kinetic limitation [Reasonable Inference], although the formation of Pnma HfCoGe and TaCoGe near 1000 °C suggests that the reaction is kinetically accessible in this structural family (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). Structural reconstruction receives a high score because the closest analogues share the Pnma structure and closely related coordination environments (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2).

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 requires a controlled Ar or vacuum environment and at least one additional regrinding/annealing cycle, so deductions are applied for atmosphere control, possible Ge loss, and the explicitly multi-step procedure [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature remains near the approximately 1000 °C range used for the closest Pnma analogues (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a separate toxicity or air-sensitivity deduction specific to the precursor set.

### F3 — Route 2

Repeated grinding increases contact area and reduces diffusion lengths, which should improve completion of the reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, the route still relies on refractory elemental Nb and therefore retains a solid-state diffusion limitation [Reasonable Inference]. The Pnma structural analogy strongly supports the final reconstruction step (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2).

### F2 — Route 3

Arc melting requires local temperatures above 1000 °C and controlled Ar, followed by vacuum or sealed-tube annealing, leading to deductions for temperature and atmosphere control (CS-4, CS-5; SS-1, SS-4; TS-3, TS-4). Ge-loss risk during melting is an extrapolated concern rather than a directly reported NbCoGe observation [Reasonable Inference]. The route does not require high pressure.

### F3 — Route 3

Arc melting can improve precursor mixing through partial liquid processing, but Nb's refractory character may prevent complete homogenization [Reasonable Inference]. Related ternary materials have been arc-melted and then annealed, demonstrating that this general kinetic strategy is viable (CS-4, CS-5). The structural reconstruction score is lower than for Route 1 because the closest Pnma HfCoGe and TaCoGe examples specifically report sintering rather than arc melting (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2).

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

CS/SS/TS do not provide the unit-cell atom count, cation ionic-radius comparison, or explicit vertex/edge/face-sharing classification required by the three F4 checks. Therefore, there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**, and each source is treated as neutral with a score of 50. The target's three-dimensional connectivity and unique prototype are useful qualitative structural information, but they do not justify changing the prescribed neutral score (target validation).

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbCoGe is likely to be a calculated ground-state phase because its supplied hull distance is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, although the target remains experimentally unvalidated (target thermodynamic record; target validation). The synthesis evidence is particularly favorable because HfCoGe and TaCoGe share the Pnma structure type and closely related coordination topology, and both are reported to form readily by sintering near 1000 °C (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). The most important enabling factor is the strong structural and thermodynamic analogy to these Pnma Co–Ge compounds (SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2). The principal limitation is likely kinetic and compositional control involving refractory Nb and possible Ge loss during high-temperature processing [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, direct sealed-tube solid-state sintering is the most defensible first experiment.
