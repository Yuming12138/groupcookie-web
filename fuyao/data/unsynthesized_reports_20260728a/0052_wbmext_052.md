### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which places K5BiO5 in the highest stability bin; close CS/SS/TS analogues also include many E_hull = 0 phases such as K3BiO4, K3BiO3, K4PbO4, Rb3BiO3, and K-rich tellurates (CS1–CS4; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic: the target is theory-only and no synthesis is reported in the supplied validation data [Reasonable Inference from CS/SS/TS analogue stability].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires controlled Ar handling, sealed vessels, and air-sensitive oxidizers such as K2O2/KO2 (CS2, CS4). The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy, with the main penalty from sealed alkali-peroxide chemistry rather than temperature (CS2, CS4).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
K2O2/KO2 are highly reactive precursors, and AO2 + Bi directly forms K3BiO4 at 700 °C, supporting good precursor activity (CS2). Kinetics are limited by the need to form a new K-rich Bi(V) framework that is not identical to K3BiO4 or K3BiO3, so structural reconstruction is only moderate (CS2, CS3; SS5).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses the validated K2O + Bi2O3 chemistry for K3BiO3, but adding K2O2 and sealed flux conditions increases handling difficulty (CS3, CS4; SS2, SS3). The penalties come from Ar/sealed-vessel operation, air-sensitive alkali oxides, and multi-step processing (CS3; SS2; TS2).

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The K2O/K2O2/Bi2O3 mixture should be chemically active, and sealed alkali-oxide analogues crystallize at accessible temperatures, supporting favorable ion mobility (CS3, CS4; SS2, SS3). The uncertainty is whether Bi2O3 can be cleanly oxidized to the K5BiO5 Bi(V) phase without trapping K3BiO3 or K3BiO4 impurities [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS3].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is conditionally mild in temperature but operationally challenging because it is explicitly stepwise and may require long O2 or peroxide oxidation in sealed Ag vessels (CS1, CS3). The route inherits the 100-day oxidation precedent from K4PbO4, so severity is driven by time and redox control rather than temperature (CS1; SS1; TS1).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Starting from K3BiO3 gives a chemically related K–Bi–O precursor, which improves reconstruction relative to starting from Bi metal/Bi2O3 (CS3; SS5; TS5). However, the K4PbO3 → K4PbO4 analogue shows that oxidative ordering can be very slow, so kinetics remain only moderate (CS1; SS1; TS1).

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 matches the long sealed alkali-oxide flux conditions used for structurally similar K-rich oxides, so it is experimentally plausible but demanding (SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3). Its difficulty comes from sealed alkali-oxide flux handling, long anneals, and post-growth separation under dry conditions (SS2, SS3; CS5).

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Flux-like alkali oxide media should improve diffusion, and SS/TS analogues form crystals at 700–720 °C over weeks (SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3). The lower structural-reconstruction score reflects the absence of a direct K–Bi–O flux analogue and the reported phase-control difficulty for related K-rich oxides (SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The main amorphization risk is the large size/coordination contrast between K and high-valent Bi/Pb/Te sites, reflected by much longer alkali–O than Bi/Pb/Te–O distances in analogues (CS2, CS3; SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5). Mixed corner/edge-sharing networks are common in the analogues, but face-sharing is not reported, and the target atom count check is not confirmed by CS/SS/TS, so the risk is low–medium rather than high (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K5BiO5 is likely a DFT ground-state candidate because the supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close CS/SS/TS analogues are also on the hull (CS1–CS4; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strongest chemically through K3BiO4 from AO2 + Bi and K3BiO3 from K2O + Bi2O3, while structural/thermodynamic analogues support sealed alkali-oxide synthesis at 550–720 °C (CS2, CS3; SS2, SS3, SS5; TS2, TS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is a reactive potassium oxidizer such as K2O2 or KO2 that simultaneously supplies K and oxidizing equivalents for Bi(V) formation [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control in an air-sensitive, K-rich, high-oxygen-potential composition space where analogues often require sealed vessels and long anneals (CS1, CS4; SS2, SS3). Overall, the target appears moderately synthesizable, with the best chance coming from direct K2O2/Bi or KO2/Bi sealed solid-state screening rather than conventional oxide-only sintering (CS2, CS4).
