## SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target is a Ca–Ru–Si intermetallic with the Matlockite-type tetragonal \(P4/nmm\) structure. The closest direct structural precedent is CaCoSi, which has the same Matlockite structure and was made by short high-temperature solid-state reaction in argon [CS]. Ru–Si–rare-earth analogues demonstrate that Ru–Si intermetallics can be formed by arc melting followed by annealing under inert atmosphere [SS/TS]. Ca–Pd–Si and related Ca–Pd–Si phases further support melt-growth processing of alkaline-earth transition-metal silicides [CS].

## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target record reports \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom and a negative WBM leave-one-out hull improvement, which strongly supports thermodynamic stability in the supplied computational dataset [target validation record]. The chemical analogues also show favorable calculated stability: Ca–Pd–Si and Sr–Pd–Si entries are at 0 eV/atom, while CaCoSi is only 0.00897 eV/atom above the ground state [CS]. The SS and TS analogues all report 0 eV/atom [SS/TS]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is explicitly theory-only and has no supplied experimental synthesis record [target validation record].

### F2: Route 1

The sealed-ampoule route requires temperatures around 1100–1200 °C, multiple preparation steps, and air-free Ca handling [CS/SS/TS]. These factors reduce the condition-severity score, while the absence of high pressure prevents a larger deduction [SS/TS]. The exact target temperature and whether a Ca excess is necessary are not experimentally established [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### F2: Route 2

Arc melting is a standard route for Ru–Si intermetallics and related transition-metal silicides, but it requires repeated remelting and subsequent annealing for homogeneity or ordering [CS/SS/TS]. Ca introduces air-sensitivity and possible high-temperature mass-loss concerns [CS/SS/TS; mass-loss interpretation is a Reasonable Inference]. No supplied source establishes the minimum number of remelts or the optimal annealing temperature for CaRuSi [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

### F2: Route 3

The induction-melt route receives the highest F2 score because the closest Ca–Pd–Si melt-growth examples use approximately 900 °C rather than temperatures above 1000 °C, with argon and no high pressure [CS]. The score is reduced for the likely need to control Ca loss and for the multi-step melt/anneal process [CS; Ca-loss interpretation is a Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a target-specific CaRuSi liquidus or crystallization temperature.

### F3: Route 1

The route uses dense elemental precursors, including refractory Ru, so solid-state diffusion is intrinsically limiting [SS/TS; kinetic interpretation is a Reasonable Inference]. However, the identical Matlockite structure of CaCoSi means that the structural reconstruction burden is low [CS]. Pre-melting or grinding the Ru–Si portion and using a molten or partially molten reaction zone should improve kinetics [CS/SS/TS; process recommendation is a Reasonable Inference].

### F3: Route 2

Arc melting improves chemical mixing and mass transport, and the successful preparation of Ru–Si analogues supports this kinetic strategy [SS/TS]. Nevertheless, the relevant arc-melting examples generally produce different stoichiometries or structures, so a long anneal may be required to obtain ordered CaRuSi Matlockite rather than a metastable alloy or binary mixture [CS/SS/TS; metastable-alloy concern is a Reasonable Inference]. Repeated remelting is supported by the Ru–Si analogue procedures [SS/TS].

### F3: Route 3

Induction melting provides the strongest liquid-phase mass transport among the proposed bulk routes, and Ca–Pd–Si synthesis explicitly combines reaction, induction melting, and prolonged annealing [CS]. Controlled cooling is also supported by the SrPdSi melt-growth precedent [CS]. The target-specific crystallization pathway remains unknown, so the structural-ordering component is lower than the CaCoSi direct-reaction precedent [CS; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct CaRuSi crystallization].

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has a compact three-element intermetallic structure in \(P4/nmm\), and the supplied description does not indicate a large unit cell or obviously mixed polyhedral-sharing network [target structure description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact unit-cell and sharing metrics]. A provisional cation-size mismatch trigger is assigned because Ca occupies a large 9-coordinate site while Ru and Si form substantially different local environments [target structure description; size interpretation is a Reasonable Inference]. Since only one of three checks is triggered provisionally, the amorphization risk is assessed as low–medium.
