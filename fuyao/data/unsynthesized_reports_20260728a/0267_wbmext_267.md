### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and the thermodynamic-neighbor set contains multiple ground-state or near-ground-state analogues, including HfRuAs, ZrRhP, HfRuP, and HfRhSi. (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5) Chemically and structurally similar analogues also include HfRuAs at 0.0–0.0047 eV/atom and HfRhGe near the hull, supporting a high F1 score. (CS1, CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, SS3) The uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and no CS/SS/TS entry proves experimental HfRhAs synthesis. (CS, SS, TS)

**F2 — Route 1.** Arc/solid-state synthesis is strongly evidenced for Pnma and arsenide analogues, but it requires high-temperature arc melting, controlled Ar/vacuum or sealed-tube processing, long anneals, and toxic volatile As handling. (SS4, TS2, TS5, CS4, SS5) These penalties produce a low condition-severity score despite good analogue support. [Reasonable Inference] (CS4, SS5)

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are limited by dense refractory Hf/Rh precursors and long solid-state diffusion distances, as reflected by multi-day to multi-week anneals in analogues. (CS4, SS5, TS5, CS5) Structural reconstruction is favorable because Pnma ZrRhP and HfRhSi closely match the target framework, but polymorph control remains uncertain. (SS4, TS2, TS5, CS1)

**F2 — Route 2.** Flux growth avoids direct refractory-metal melting above 1400 °C, but it still requires high-temperature sealed quartz operation, volatile As control, multi-step flux removal, and toxic/corrosive handling. [Reasonable Inference] (CS2, SS1, TS1) Therefore, Route 2 has better severity than Route 1 but remains moderate-to-challenging. (CS2, SS1, TS1)

**F3 — Route 2.** The Cu flux should improve precursor activity and mass transport relative to dry powders, consistent with successful flux growth of close HfRuAs/HfRuP-type analogues. [Reasonable Inference] (CS2, CS3, SS1, TS1, TS4) The key uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the target Pnma HfRhAs rather than a hexagonal or competing arsenide phase. (SS1, SS2, SS4, TS2)

**F2 — Route 3.** Induction/melt homogenization is supported by HfRhGe, but applying it to an arsenide adds sealed As handling and likely high-temperature refractory-metal processing. (CS1, SS3, CS4, SS5) TS gives insufficient route-specific induction-melt evidence and is treated as neutral 50 for this route-transferability factor. (TS)

**F3 — Route 3.** Melt homogenization helps Hf/Rh mixing, but the HfRhGe analogue is hexagonal and phase-control issues are noted, so structural transfer to Pnma HfRhAs is weaker than Route 1 or Route 2. (CS1, SS3) The As substitution introduces volatility constraints not present in Ge melt growth. [Reasonable Inference] (CS4, SS5)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target’s likely small Pnma cell is supported by Pnma Zr4P4Rh4 and Hf4Si4Rh4 analogues, so the >40-atom trigger is not met. (SS4, TS2, TS5) A Hf/Rh size mismatch likely triggers one amorphization-risk point, but extensive crystalline analogues suggest the framework tolerates the mismatch. [Reasonable Inference] (CS1, SS4, TS5) Mixed polyhedral sharing is proven for hexagonal HfRuAs analogues but not for target-like Pnma entries, so C3 is not triggered and CS/SS/TS evidence is neutral for that specific check.
