### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the thermodynamic bin. Exact structural/thermodynamic analogues Sr₂ZnN₂ and Ca₂ZnN₂ are also hull-stable, and chemically related nitride/rare-earth compounds include stable or near-stable examples. Remaining uncertainty comes from the target being theory-only with no reported experimental synthesis in the provided record. **(SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, CS1, CS4, CS5)**

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has harsh handling but mild thermal conditions: the closest analogue uses 300–750°C, Ar sealing, Nb/quartz containment, slow cooling, and NH₃ washing, so F2 is limited by controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile/reactive components, and multi-step workup. Kinetically, Route 1 scores highest because molten Na flux and NaN₃ lower diffusion barriers and directly produced Sr₂ZnN₂, the same M₂ZnN₂ structural family. The main uncertainty is whether Yb enters as a stable divalent-like cation without forming binary Yb nitrides or metallic Yb. **(SS1, TS1; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is operationally simpler than flux growth because Ca₂ZnN₂ forms from Ca₃N₂ + Zn at 680°C under N₂, and ternary nitrides such as BaCeN₂/SrHfN₂ form by binary-nitride solid-state routes. Its F3 is lower than Route 1 because solid-state diffusion and precursor preparation are slower, and long holds or residual impurities are reported in chemically similar nitride syntheses. The main uncertainty is the availability and phase purity of a suitable Yb nitride precursor. **(SS2, TS2, CS1, CS5; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is less directly supported because the analogues show direct N₂ reactions for other Ca/Sr nitride systems, not the exact Yb–Zn–N composition. F2 is penalized for higher possible temperature, controlled N₂, Zn volatility, and air-sensitive metals. F3 is moderate because reactive metals can nitride, but the target framework must nucleate from elements and may compete with binary Yb nitride formation. **(TS5, SS3, TS3; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is weakly supported by high-pressure rare-earth oxychalcogenide synthesis at 10 kbar and 1600°C, but exact Zn nitride analogues are ambient-pressure phases. F2 is penalized by very high temperature, capsule/pressure operations, and Zn retention risk; F3 is limited by short dwell time and lack of a pressure-synthesized M₂ZnN₂ analogue. This route is best treated as an exploratory fallback, not the primary route. **(CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is clearly active: large Yb–Zn cation size/coordination mismatch. The exact M₂ZnN₂ analogues are simple ordered crystalline structures with edge/corner sharing rather than highly complex mixed face/edge/vertex connectivity, so the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. Uncertainty remains because the target is a theory-only Yb analogue and Yb valence disorder could broaden the phase field. **(SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2; [Reasonable Inference])**
