### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic score band (Target data). Close CdPtF₆, CdPdF₆, ZnPdF₆, RbRuF₆, and CoPtF₆ analogues are also on or essentially on the hull, which reinforces that this fluoride framework is thermodynamically plausible (CS1, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is WBM theory-only and not experimentally validated in the supplied data (Target data).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for F₂ atmosphere, multistep staged fluorination, and toxic/reactive materials, all of which are directly present in the direct F₂ analogues (CS2, SS4, SS5, TS3, TS4). It avoids sealed autoclaves and long noble-metal tube anneals, so its severity is lower than pressure or AHF routes (CS1, CS4, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an exact CdIrF₆ direct route is handled through the Ir-substitution extrapolation rather than an additional numerical penalty.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Direct fluorination has good precursor activity because acetate/chloride/oxide mixtures react under F₂ in CdPdF₆, ZnPdF₆, NiPbF₆, and RbRuF₆ analogues (CS2, SS3, SS4, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS4). Kinetics are limited by Ir’s refractory character [Reasonable Inference], but F₂ chemistry enables analogue synthesis at only 350–500 °C (CS2, SS3, TS2). Structural reconstruction is favorable because CdPtF₆ and CdPdF₆ already adopt the target-like R-3 corner-sharing octahedral framework (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Pressure fluorination is more severe because the analogue route uses high-pressure F₂, staged prefluorination, and sealed noble-metal annealing (CS1, TS5). The CS/SS/TS method labels sometimes call these “high-pressure,” but the reported pressures are gas autoclave pressures rather than >5 GPa, so the rubric’s >5 GPa deduction is not applied (CS1, SS1, TS1, TS5). The route remains challenging due to F₂ pressure, equipment, and long treatment times (TS5).

**F3 — Route 2**  
Pressure F₂ improves conversion and oxidation potential, which is helpful if Ir(IV) formation is sluggish [Reasonable Inference], and Pt analogues show successful high-pressure fluorination (TS5). Ion diffusion remains moderate because long multiday treatment and possible sealed-tube annealing are needed (CS1, TS5). Structural kinetics are favorable because CdPtF₆ is the closest same-framework analogue (CS1, SS1, TS1).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Sealed noble-metal tube annealing is penalized for controlled F₂/Ar handling, sealed containment, multistep processing, and hazardous fluorine chemistry (CS1, CS3, CS5). Temperatures are not extreme by ceramic standards, but durations of weeks and noble-metal tubes make the route operationally challenging (CS1, CS5). This is best viewed as a crystal-growth route rather than the first attempt at bulk synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Prefluorinated feed and fluorine-assisted transport can help crystallization [Reasonable Inference], but long anneals in CdPtF₆ and Au fluoride analogues indicate slow ordering kinetics (CS1, CS5). Structural reconstruction is good when starting from pre-made CdIrF₆ or a CdPtF₆-like feed, but the route is less efficient for initial phase discovery (CS1, SS1, TS1). Au fluoride CVT examples support sealed-tube fluoride handling but are less structurally transferable to CdIrF₆ (CS3, CS5).

**F2 — Route 4**  
AHF solvothermal synthesis is penalized for sealed autoclave operation, anhydrous HF, controlled Ar handling, and hazardous/toxic precursors (CS4). The low 300 °C temperature reduces thermal severity, but HF compatibility and safety dominate the difficulty (CS4). Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for AHF synthesis of this framework is treated as neutral 50 source signal.

**F3 — Route 4**  
AHF may improve fluoride mobility and allow lower-temperature crystallization [Reasonable Inference], but CS4 is a different AuF₄-based structure and was obtained accidentally from NiF₂ contamination. No SS/TS analogue demonstrates AHF formation of an Upper Bainite-derived Cd–M–F₆ framework, so route transferability is uncertain and SS/TS are neutral 50. This makes Route 4 plausible but not preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The only clear trigger is cation size mismatch, because Cd²⁺ is much larger than octahedral Ir⁴⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. However, same-family CdPtF₆ and CdPdF₆ tolerate Cd–M octahedral size mismatch in the R-3 framework, lowering amorphization concern (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The target and closest analogues are corner-sharing octahedral networks without mixed edge/face connectivity, so structural frustration is limited (Target data, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CdIrF₆ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple Cd/Pd/Pt/Ru/Pt fluoride analogues are also hull-stable (Target data, CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). The analogue support is strong: CdPtF₆ and CdPdF₆ match the same Cd–M–F₆ R-3 corner-sharing octahedral framework, making the structural transfer to Ir plausible (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, because all close synthetic analogues require F₂ fluorination or strongly fluorinating conditions (CS1, CS2, SS4, TS3, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is operational control of F₂/HF chemistry and Ir oxidation/volatility, especially avoiding competing iridium fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is credible but experimentally demanding, with direct F₂ fluorination the best first route.
