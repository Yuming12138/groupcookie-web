### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported at **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, which places it in the highest stability bin (Target). The closest ScNiP analogue is also on the hull, and several Ni–As thermodynamic analogues are reported at or near zero hull energy, supporting high thermodynamic plausibility (SS #2; TS #1; TS #2; TS #3; TS #4). Remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic, because the target is theory-only and not identified as synthesized in the source dataset (Target).

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized for high-temperature arc-melting/annealing, volatile As containment, multistep ScAs preparation + melting + annealing, and toxic arsenide handling (SS #2; TS #1; SS #1; TS #4). It avoids high pressure and can use Ar rather than reactive gas, matching multiple arc-melted intermetallic analogues (CS #2; CS #3; SS #2). The severity is therefore moderate rather than extreme.

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 has a lower peak temperature window, because rare-earth nickel arsenides are commonly annealed at 660–1000 °C, but it requires evacuated sealed tubes, long annealing, regrinding/pressing, and arsenic handling (SS #1; SS #3; TS #4). The route is not high pressure and uses standard sealed-tube equipment, so the main penalties are atmosphere, volatility, and procedure length (SS #1; TS #4).

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 benefits from lower temperature flux conditions near 1050 K and no high pressure, but it still requires sealed As handling, multistep flux processing, and toxic precursor precautions (SS #5; TS #3). **Insufficient evidence from CS** exists for flux synthesis, so CS is neutral-scored **50** for this route signal; SS/TS carry the flux evidence (SS #5; TS #3).

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are helped by arc-melting/remelting and by the same-structure ScNiP analogue, which reduces structural reconstruction uncertainty (CS #2; SS #2; TS #1). The main kinetic limitation is precursor activity because ScAs and Ni are dense solids before melting [Reasonable Inference] (SS #2; TS #1). Overall F3 is solid but not near-perfect because As containment and post-anneal ordering remain necessary [Reasonable Inference] (SS #1; TS #4).

**F3 — Route 2.** Solid-state kinetics are weaker because sealed-tube arsenide analogues require one to two weeks and sometimes grinding/pressing/reannealing, indicating slow diffusion (SS #1; SS #3; TS #4). Structural selectivity is also less certain because the strongest Ni–As sealed-tube analogues are tetragonal 122 phases rather than ScNiAs Pnma (SS #1; TS #2; TS #4).

**F3 — Route 3.** Sn flux should improve transport and lower diffusion barriers, but the structure-selection evidence is weak because the flux analogues are mainly 122-type pnictides rather than the target 1:1:1 Pnma phase [Reasonable Inference] (SS #5; TS #3). The supplied flux evidence is internally cautious for arsenides/antimonides, so structural reconstruction was strongly down-weighted (SS #5; TS #3).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target is a small ordered intermetallic/pnictide prototype, and the closest Pnma analogue has only a small 1:1:1 cell, so large-cell amorphization risk is low (SS #2; TS #1; Target). The cation-size check is conservatively triggered by [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii, so those sources are neutral **50** for C1. Mixed polyhedral sharing is not triggered for the target because that motif is not reported in the target description and appears only in a different hexagonal ScCuSi analogue (Target; CS #5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScNiAs is best interpreted as a likely **DFT ground-state candidate** rather than a clearly metastable phase, because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and a negative formation energy in the provided target data (Target). The synthesis case is strongest through the **ScNiP** analogue, which shares the Pnma 1:1:1 topology and is synthesized by arc-melting plus annealing (SS #2; TS #1). CS analogues further support arc-melted 1:1:1 Sc/Y–transition-metal–p-block phases, although most do not contain As (CS #2; CS #3; CS #4). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a same-structure pnictide analogue, ScNiP, that is experimentally synthesized and hull-stable (SS #2; TS #1). The most important limiting factor is arsenic control: toxic/volatile As chemistry requires sealed handling and creates risk of competing Ni–As or Sc–As phases [Reasonable Inference] (SS #1; TS #4).
