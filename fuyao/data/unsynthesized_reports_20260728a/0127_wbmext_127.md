### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom, so the score is placed near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. (Target data) Thermodynamic analogues are also stable or nearly stable, including Ce₂SeN₂, ThNCl, ThNBr, and Ce₂NCl₃. (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5) The uncertainty is that the target is theory-only WBM and not identified as experimentally synthesized in the provided target data. (Target data)

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 gets the highest combined score because exact matlockite ThNCl is synthesized by ammonolysis under moderate high-temperature NH₃ conditions. (SS2, TS2) The main kinetic advantage is direct formation of an MNCl framework rather than reconstructing from refractory binaries. (SS2, TS2, SS3) The remaining uncertainty is Ce-specific: CeNCl likely needs Ce(IV), while Ce₂NCl₃ shows Ce–N–Cl chemistry can instead stabilize a Ce(III)-rich chloride nitride. (TS5)

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is well supported for Ce–N–Cl access because Ce₂NCl₃ is produced from Ce/CeCl₃/NaN₃/NaCl flux at 850 °C for 7 days. (TS5) Flux improves kinetics and crystallinity, but the demonstrated product is not CeNCl, so phase selectivity and Ce oxidation state lower the structural-reconstruction score. (TS5, SS2, TS2)

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is supported only indirectly by sealed rare-earth chloride/chalcogenide synthesis in evacuated silica ampoules. (CS4) It is penalized because nitride–halide solid-state metathesis from dense powders likely requires slower diffusion and repeated annealing. (CS4) SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact sealed solid-state route to CeNCl, so SS and TS are neutral 50 for this route-specific factor. (SS2, TS2)

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is plausible for crystal growth because iodine CVT yields single crystals of chemically similar lanthanide phosphide sulfides near 1000 °C. (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5) It is not the best discovery route because no SS/TS record shows direct CVT formation of CeNCl, and halide exchange or iodine incorporation is possible by **[Reasonable Inference]**. (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5)

**F2/F3 — Route 5**  
Route 5 is supported by ZrNCl high-pressure reactive flux processing at 3 GPa and 900 °C. (SS4, TS3) It is penalized kinetically because the analogue is R-3m ZrNCl rather than P4/nmm CeNCl, and because multianvil treatment is more of a polymorph/phase-control fallback than a chemically direct route. (SS4, TS3, SS2, TS2)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
CeNCl has only one cation species, so cation-size disorder is not expected to drive amorphization. (Target data) The matlockite analogues use compact MNX-type cells and edge/corner-sharing tetrahedral motifs without reported face-sharing coexistence, supporting a low amorphization risk. (SS2, TS2, SS5, TS4) CS has insufficient target-specific structural signal for C1–C3, so CS is treated neutral 50 for F4. (CS1–CS5)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeNCl is predicted as a likely ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and TS analogues are also at or near the hull. (Target data, TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5) The strongest synthesis support comes from exact matlockite ThNCl/ThNBr structural analogues made by ammonolysis and from Ce–N framework analogues with similar Ce–N distances. (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5) The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated MNCl matlockite topology under ammonolysis conditions. (SS2, TS2, SS3) The most critical limiting factor is Ce oxidation-state control, because the closest Ce–N–Cl flux analogue forms Ce₂NCl₃ rather than CeNCl. (TS5) Overall, CeNCl appears synthetically plausible but not trivial, with best prospects under dry ammonolysis or carefully oxidizing chloride/azide flux conditions. (SS2, TS2, TS5)
