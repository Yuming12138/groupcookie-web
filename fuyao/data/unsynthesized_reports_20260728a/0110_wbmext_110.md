## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The supplied target validation gives \(E_\text{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom and identifies a unique three-dimensional relaxed prototype, supporting a thermodynamically stable candidate. CS/SS/TS do not independently report the target’s hull value, so there is **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** for the exact target thermodynamic score; those sources are neutral for that exact value. Nevertheless, CS contains several \(R_3\)InN compounds at zero hull energy, and SS/TS report Sr\(_3\)BiN at zero hull energy, providing favorable chemical and structural precedent (CS; SS; TS).

### F2: Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because CS requires a 1220 K, one-week anneal, evacuated ampoule containment, arc melting, and air-sensitive binary precursors (CS). The temperature is approximately 947°C, so the \(>1000^\circ\mathrm{C}\) penalty does not apply under the supplied rubric. The multi-step preparation and precursor sensitivity justify the deductions (CS).

### F2: Route 2

Route 2 is less severe because SS/TS demonstrate formation of an \(A_3\)BiN phase from an elemental melt bead and N\(_2\) at ambient pressure near 1070 K (SS; TS). The route has only two main operations and avoids prior synthesis of YbN and Yb\(_2\)Bi. The remaining penalty reflects the likely air sensitivity of Yb and the need for inert handling [Reasonable Inference].

### F3: Route 1

The binary-nitride/intermetallic route has lower precursor activity because it begins with preformed dense binary solids rather than activated salts or solution-derived powders [Reasonable Inference]. Its diffusion score is improved by the intermetallic fusion step, and CS demonstrates that the same broad \(R_3\)XN perovskite framework can be obtained by this method (CS). However, CS explicitly notes repeated annealing and nitrogen-composition variation for some members, reducing the structural-reconstruction score (CS).

### F3: Route 2

Route 2 benefits kinetically from the formation of a homogenized Yb–Bi melt bead before nitrogen incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS directly demonstrate that this type of melt-bead/nitrogen sequence can produce single-phase cubic \(A_3\)BiN powders at moderate temperature (SS; TS). The structural score is high because the reported Sr\(_3\)BiN product has the same Pm\(\bar{3}\)m topology as the target, although Yb-specific reaction kinetics remain unconfirmed (SS; TS; CS).

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target and closest analogues are compact cubic Pm\(\bar{3}\)m structures with regular coordination and untilted corner-sharing octahedral networks (CS; SS; TS). No source indicates a large unit cell or a strongly mixed edge/corner/face-sharing framework for the target. Because CS/SS/TS do not provide a definitive oxidation-state-dependent ionic-radius analysis for Yb versus Bi, the C1 test is neutral due to **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The supplied target validation places Yb\(_3\)BiN on the calculated convex hull, so it is more consistent with a ground-state phase than a deliberately metastable phase. CS supports the Yb-centered cubic nitride-perovskite chemistry through Yb\(_3\)SnO and multiple rare-earth \(R_3\)InN analogues, while SS/TS provide direct structural and synthetic precedent for cubic Sr\(_3\)BiN (CS; SS; TS). The most important enabling factor is the combination of a highly favorable calculated stability and a recurring Pm\(\bar{3}\)m \(R_3\)XN structural motif (CS; SS; TS). The main limitation is that no supplied source directly reports experimental synthesis of Yb\(_3\)BiN, so Yb-specific nitrogen uptake, competing Yb–Bi phases, and phase-purity behavior remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 is preferred because it combines the direct Bi–N precedent from SS/TS with the Yb-based perovskite precedent from CS.
