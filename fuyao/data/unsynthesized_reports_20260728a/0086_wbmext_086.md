### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 phases such as Ti2AlN, Ti2GaN, V3GaN, and Hf3AlN (CS5; SS2; TS1; TS2; TS5). Exact-composition cubic Ti3AlN is only 0.0297 eV/atom above hull, still in the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score bin (CS1; SS1). The uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because no CS/SS/TS entry reports the exact orthorhombic Ti3AlN polymorph as synthesized (CS1; SS1; CS5; TS5).

**Route 1 F2/F3 — High-Temperature Solid-State.**  
The route is penalized for >1000 °C synthesis, controlled vacuum/Ar atmospheres, Al volatility risk, and multiple processing steps, all of which are supported by Ti–Al–N analogue procedures (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS2; TS1). Kinetics are moderate because TiN/AlN are refractory, but Al-containing master alloys and long high-temperature anneals improve reaction completion [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS3]. The major uncertainty is polymorph selectivity, because exact Ti3AlN evidence is cubic while the target is orthorhombic Cmcm (CS1; SS1; CS5; TS5).

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Sealed Ampoule.**  
This route has lower severity than Route 1 because the closest ampoule analogues operate at 500–1100 °C without high pressure, but sealed-tube control and long annealing remain necessary (CS2; CS4; CS5; SS3; SS4; TS2; TS3). Kinetics are only moderate because analogous routes require hundreds of hours, but structural transfer is strong from Cmcm V3GaN and Hf3AlN-type evidence (CS5; TS5). The main uncertainty is whether Ti/Al specifically adopts the Cmcm distortion rather than the known cubic Ti3AlN polymorph (CS1; SS1; CS5; TS5).

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Melt-Assisted Growth.**  
This route has the harshest condition score because melt/arc processing likely exceeds 1400 °C and requires controlled Ar/vacuum environments [Reasonable Inference from TS5/SS5/TS4]. Its kinetics score is better because melt-assisted mixing can reduce diffusion limitations, and a Cmcm M3AlN analogue was made by melting under Ar and slow cooling (TS5). The uncertainty is chemical transfer, because the closest melt-growth analogue is Hf/Zr-based rather than Ti-based, while CS provides only partial support through Ti–Al master-alloy arc melting (TS5; CS1; CS3).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target-like Cmcm analogues are compact M3AN-type structures with edge/corner-sharing octahedra, which does not suggest a large-cell amorphization penalty (CS5; TS5). Cation-size mismatch is treated as one amorphization trigger by [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii directly, so that specific source signal is insufficient and neutral 50. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because the structure is crystalline, small, and repeatedly represented by ordered analogues in CS/SS/TS (CS5; TS5; CS1; SS2; TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti3AlN is likely a computed ground-state candidate because the supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and thermodynamically similar analogues are stable or near-stable (CS1; CS5; TS1; TS2; TS5). The synthesis evidence is strong chemically because Ti3AlN and Ti2AlN are both supported in Ti–Al–N solid-state datasets, but structurally incomplete because the exact Ti3AlN reference is cubic rather than orthorhombic Cmcm (CS1; SS1; CS3; SS2; TS1). The strongest enabling factor is the robust Ti–N/M–N octahedral nitride framework seen across Ti3AlN, Ti2AlN, V3GaN, and Hf3AlN analogues (CS1; CS3; CS5; SS1; SS2; TS5). The strongest limiting factor is polymorph and competing-phase control, especially against cubic Ti3AlN and Ti2AlN/H-phase products (CS1; SS1; CS3; SS2; TS1). The best first experiment is a sealed-ampoule nitride-transfer screen near 950–1100 °C with long annealing, because this route best balances Cmcm structural transfer and manageable synthesis severity (CS5; CS2; CS4; TS2; TS3).
