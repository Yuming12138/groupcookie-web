# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a physical \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category specified by the scoring rubric. Closely related Ba₂RuH₆, Ba₂MgH₆, Sr₂FeH₆, and Ca₂RuH₆ also have reported \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\), providing consistent chemical-space support. **[CS; SS; TS]** The remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and has no supplied experimental synthesis validation. **[CS; SS; TS]**

## F2: Route 1

Route 1 is penalized primarily for the required H₂ atmosphere and air-sensitive hydride precursor. The temperature and pressure are demanding but substantially less severe than the >1500 bar conditions used for K₂PtH₆ and Rb₂PtH₆. **[CS; SS; TS]** The direct one-reaction format keeps the procedural penalty limited, although prolonged reaction times and phase control may reduce practical reproducibility. **[CS; TS]**

## F2: Route 2

Route 2 uses a much higher hydrogen pressure than the closest Ba₂RuH₆ precedent, so it receives a lower condition-severity score despite its moderate temperature. High-pressure Pt hydride synthesis confirms that this pressure regime can stabilize ternary transition-metal hydrides, but it does not directly establish Ba₂CoH₆ formation. **[CS]** The main uncertainty is whether the additional pressure improves Co incorporation enough to justify the substantially greater equipment and safety burden. **[CS; SS; TS]**

## F2: Route 3

The flux route receives penalties for H₂ pressure, air-sensitive hydrides, and the additional flux-management step. The BaMg₂RuH₈ precedent indicates that LiH/LiD-containing flux chemistry can operate near 500–530 °C under 100–150 bar hydrogen/deuterium. **[SS; TS]** However, the reference product has a different structure and impurity phases were reported, so transferability to phase-pure Ba₂CoH₆ is less certain. **[SS; TS]**

## F3: Route 1

The direct route benefits strongly from structural continuity: Ba₂RuH₆ has the same Fm-3m framework and closely related Ba–H geometry, while Sr₂FeH₆ has a very similar transition-metal hydride octahedron. **[CS; SS; TS]** Diffusion remains the kinetic limitation because the reaction uses solid BaH₂ and Co rather than solution-derived or highly reactive precursors. **[CS; SS; TS] [Reasonable Inference]**

## F3: Route 2

The high-pressure route has essentially the same cation-diffusion and structural-reconstruction scores as Route 1 because the starting solid combination and target framework are unchanged. The higher H₂ pressure may improve hydrogen uptake and suppress hydrogen deficiency, but this benefit is an extrapolation rather than a direct observation for Co. **[CS; SS; TS] [Reasonable Inference]**

## F3: Route 3

The flux route may enhance mass transport and contact between BaH₂ and Co, based on the successful Ba-containing flux synthesis. **[SS; TS]** Its structural-reconstruction score is lower because BaMg₂RuH₈ is tetragonal, contains multiple inequivalent hydrogen sites, and was associated with impurity phases. **[SS; TS]** Direct evidence for LiH-assisted synthesis of a cubic Ba₂CoH₆ phase is absent; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the optimal flux composition and cleanup procedure.

## F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target is a compact, highly symmetric cubic Fm-3m structure with a single hydrogen site and a well-defined BaH₁₂/CoH₆ framework. **[Target structural description; CS; SS; TS]** The supplied records do not give sufficient information to evaluate the ionic-radius-difference and unit-cell-size checks, so those checks were scored neutral rather than assumed. **[CS; SS; TS]** The target description reports corner and face sharing but not the complete vertex–edge–face combination required to trigger C3.
