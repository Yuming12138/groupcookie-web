## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied energy above hull of 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, and the chemically identical MoB₂ analogue also has \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0\) eV atom⁻¹. **(CS: mp-2331)** The same MoB₂ analogue is also the top SS and TS match, with both similarity values approximately 0.9994 and zero reported hull distance. **(SS, TS: mp-2331)** This strongly supports a thermodynamically favorable MoB₂ composition, although it does not by itself prove that the exact P6₃/mmc polymorph is experimentally accessible.

### F2 — Route 1

The Al-flux route requires 1500 °C, controlled Ar, precise atomic ratios, controlled cooling, and flux removal. **(CS, SS, TS: mp-2331)** These factors justify deductions for high temperature and multi-step processing, but not for high pressure, volatile precursors, or toxic reagents based on the supplied evidence. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for exact oxygen tolerance or residual-Al contamination under modified conditions.

### F2 — Route 2

Direct solid-state synthesis is more severe because it requires temperatures near 1400–1600 °C, vacuum or another controlled atmosphere, extensive grinding/pressing, and prolonged annealing. **(SS, TS: mp-1890, mp-1019317)** Related solid-state boride studies also report long reaction times and difficulty separating multiple boride phases, supporting the multi-step and phase-control deductions. **(SS, TS: mp-1019317, mp-1890)**

### F2 — Route 3

Reactive melting uses high temperature and controlled atmosphere, and a practical protocol may require repeated melting followed by annealing. **(SS, TS: mp-1890, mp-1087505)** The deduction for controlled atmosphere is especially relevant if H₂ is used, whereas Ar-based arc melting would be less chemically reactive. **(SS, TS: mp-1890, mp-1087505)** Exact melting behavior of the Mo–B composition and the minimum number of remelts are **insufficiently evidenced by CS/SS/TS**.

### F3 — Route 1

The flux route receives the highest kinetics score because Al provides a liquid transport medium at the reported synthesis temperature, reducing the diffusion limitation associated with refractory Mo. **(CS, SS, TS: mp-2331)** Structural reconstruction is also favorable because the direct MoB₂ analogue is chemically identical and nearly perfectly similar by all three retrieval metrics. **(CS, SS, TS: mp-2331)** The principal uncertainty is polymorphism: the analogue is described as R-3m, whereas the target is P6₃/mmc.

### F3 — Route 2

The elemental solid-state route has low precursor activity and very difficult diffusion because Mo is a refractory metal and no flux is present. **[Reasonable Inference]** based on the use of Mo powder in high-temperature MoB synthesis and the long-duration vacuum sintering required for related borides **(SS, TS: mp-1890, mp-1019317)** The structural score is intermediate because TcB₂ shares P6₃/mmc symmetry, while MoB₂ and MoB analogues demonstrate that Mo–B chemistry supports several competing structures. **(SS, TS: mp-1019317, mp-1890, mp-2331)**

### F3 — Route 3

Melting or induction processing improves mixing and mass transport compared with static sintering, and Mo–B boride formation under 1500–1700 °C treatment is documented. **(SS, TS: mp-1890)** However, related arc-melting systems can retain substantial impurity phases or require repeated melting and annealing, indicating imperfect phase selectivity. **(SS, TS: mp-1087505, mp-1019317)** Exact kinetics for formation of P6₃/mmc MoB₂ from a melt are **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS**.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The single-cation composition does not create a cation-radius mismatch trigger, so C1 is not expected to apply. **[Reasonable Inference]** The supplied CS/SS/TS records do not provide enough information to determine the target unit-cell atom count or explicitly classify all vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra; therefore C2 and C3 are neutral rather than assumed positive. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** supports a more specific F4 score.
