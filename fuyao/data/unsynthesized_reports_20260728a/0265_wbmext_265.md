### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin, and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also at or near the hull (Target; CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). This supports a likely ground-state or near-ground-state phase, but the target is still theory-only with no experimental synthesis identified in the supplied data (Target). Remaining uncertainty is whether finite-temperature entropy, off-stoichiometry, or competing ternaries alter the 0 K hull prediction; CS/SS/TS do not directly resolve that for ScPtBi.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc melting is condition-severe because it requires very high local temperature and inert handling, but it avoids high pressure and is standard for Pt–Sc/Sn and Pt–rare-earth intermetallic analogues (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS2; TS1; TS5). Bi sealed containment is penalized as [Reasonable Inference] because direct Bi volatility evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. The resulting F2 = 60 reflects moderate practical difficulty, not an exotic synthesis barrier.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state reaction because arc melting bypasses long-range diffusion through dense Pt/Sc powders, and analogous Pt–Sn/ScPtSn compounds are homogenized after melting (SS1–SS2; TS1; TS5). Structural ordering is supported by same half-Heusler ScAuSn and Bi half-Heuslers, but competing hexagonal Pt–Sn analogues create some structural uncertainty (CS1; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2; TS4–TS5). This gives Route 1 the highest F3 score.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Induction melting in Ta vessels is directly supported for Bi half-Heusler analogues, with 500 °C anneals under Ar (SS3; TS2; TS4). Severity is increased by inferred high melt temperature for Pt/Sc substitution and inferred Bi containment needs, while high pressure and reactive gas are absent (SS3; TS2; TS4). CS gives insufficient induction-route evidence and is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor.

**F3 — Route 2**  
The structural match is excellent because YPdBi/HoPdBi are cubic F-43m half-Heuslers, but the chemical match is weaker because Pd/Y/Ho differ from Pt/Sc (SS3; TS2; TS4). Low-temperature annealing after melting is encouraging, though Sc/Pt homogenization is expected to be slower [Reasonable Inference]. The precursor ambiguity in SS/TS metadata for Bi versus antimony adds uncertainty to precursor transfer.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Solid-state sintering avoids extreme melt temperatures but requires sealed handling, likely >1000 °C excursions, and multiple grind/anneal operations (CS1; SS4–SS5; TS3). The route is not high-pressure and does not require H₂/NH₃/vacuum under the scoring definition (CS1; SS4–SS5; TS3). Bi containment and Sc air sensitivity remain [Reasonable Inference] penalties.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Kinetics are the weakest because Sc/Pt are dense or refractory elemental precursors, and solid-state diffusion below melting is slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports the same half-Heusler structure by solid-state sintering, but SS4–SS5/TS3 require long anneals and grinding, indicating kinetic limitations. This route is feasible but less robust than melt-based routes.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target and closest half-Heusler analogues are small, ordered intermetallic structures rather than large, compositionally complex frameworks (Target; CS1; SS3; TS2; TS4). The source data do not provide ionic radii, so C1 has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is treated neutrally at the source level; the final check uses a [Reasonable Inference] that size mismatch is not clearly above threshold. Face/corner sharing appears, but the required vertex+edge+face coexistence is not shown, so amorphization risk is low (Target; CS1; SS3; TS2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScPtBi is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many thermodynamic analogues are also at the hull (Target; TS1–TS5). The synthesis case is well supported by analogues: CS1 supports the same half-Heusler structure with Sc chemistry, SS3/TS2/TS4 support Bi half-Heusler formation, and SS2 supports Sc–Pt–Sn intermetallic processing (CS1; SS2–SS3; TS2; TS4). The most critical enabling factor is bulk melting followed by annealing, which should overcome refractory Sc/Pt diffusion barriers and promote half-Heusler ordering (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS2; TS1; TS5). The most critical limiting factor is Bi stoichiometry control during high-temperature processing, because CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify Bi loss and sealed containment is therefore a [Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limiting factor is competing structure formation, since ScPtSn/LuPtSn-type analogues appear in hexagonal P-62m rather than cubic half-Heusler structures (SS1–SS2; TS1; TS5).
