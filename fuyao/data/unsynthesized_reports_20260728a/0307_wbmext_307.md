### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 scoring band by the required rubric. TS strongly supports this with NbRuSb at E_hull = 0.0 in TS1 and several other thermodynamic analogues at E_hull = 0.0 in TS2–TS5. CS is mixed because CS2 and CS5 are on-hull, but CS1 is far above hull at 0.484 eV/atom; therefore CS does not independently prove universal stability. Remaining uncertainty is that WBM/DFT ground-state status does not guarantee experimental accessibility or absence of low-temperature competing ordered variants.  

**F2 — Route 1: Arc-Melting + Annealing**  
Severity is moderate because CS1/CS2/CS5 and SS5/TS3 require inert arc melting, repeated remelting or annealing, and controlled Ar/vacuum conditions. The route earns deductions for controlled atmosphere, multi-step processing, and volatile Sb handling [Reasonable Inference] based on sealed pnictide synthesis in SS1/TS1. No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates high pressure or >1400 °C requirements, so those penalties are not applied. Uncertainty remains because no supplied analogue is exactly Nb–Os–Sb made by arc melting.  

**F2 — Route 2: Sealed-Tube Solid-State Sintering**  
Severity is slightly better than arc melting because SS1/TS1 directly demonstrates half-Heusler NbRuSb from elemental powders in sealed silica tubes at 1000 °C for 24 h. The sealed/vacuum requirement, Sb volatility [Reasonable Inference], and multiple solid-state steps reduce the score. SS2/SS3 show Os pnictides can require longer sintering and annealing, which increases operational burden. Insufficient evidence from CS on direct NbOsSb sealed-tube behavior is treated as neutral rather than strongly positive.  

**F2 — Route 3: Flux Growth**  
Flux growth has moderate-to-high severity because CS3/CS4/TS5 require evacuated quartz capsules, high temperatures, controlled cooling, and flux removal. It avoids direct prolonged solid diffusion but adds flux composition and separation variables. Exact temperature is not specified in CS3/CS4/TS5, so the >1000 °C deduction is partial. Insufficient evidence from SS for antimonide half-Heusler flux growth is treated as neutral for route-specific severity.  

**F3 — Route 1: Arc-Melting + Annealing**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Nb/Os metals [Reasonable Inference], but arc melting in CS1/CS2/CS5 and SS5/TS3 improves precursor mixing and shortens diffusion distances. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target matches the half-Heusler topology of NbRuSb in SS1/TS1 and HfRhSb in CS1. Repeated remelting/annealing in CS2/SS5/TS3 indicates homogenization is still nontrivial. Overall kinetics are moderate.  

**F3 — Route 2: Sealed-Tube Solid-State Sintering**  
The solid-state route has the weakest kinetics because elemental Nb/Os powders are expected to diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference], and SS2/SS3 Os pnictides use long multi-step sintering/annealing. The strongest positive is structural: SS1/TS1 is the same Nb–Sb half-Heusler framework with Ru replacing Os. The route may work if powders are very fine and repeated regrinding is used, but kinetics and ordering are likely rate-limiting. Insufficient evidence from CS on exact Nb–Os–Sb diffusion is treated neutrally.  

**F3 — Route 3: Flux Growth**  
Flux growth gets the best F3 among the routes because CS3/CS4/TS5 show cooled flux melts can produce crystalline transition-metal pnictides, which should reduce long-range solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is structural transferability: those flux examples are mostly P/As systems and not direct half-Heusler antimonides. SS1/TS1 supports the desired half-Heusler endpoint, but not the flux pathway itself. Composition control in flux is uncertain and may be narrow for a 1:1:1 ordered phase [Reasonable Inference].  

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The small half-Heusler cell lowers amorphization risk because SS1/TS1 reports the same simple F-43m half-Heusler topology for NbRuSb. Mixed connectivity is present in the target description and in several CS/SS/TS analogues, including CS3/CS4/SS2/SS3/SS5/TS3/TS5, which increases structural complexity. The Nb/Os size and bonding mismatch is flagged as [Reasonable Inference] because direct ionic-radius evidence is not provided by CS/SS/TS. The resulting risk is medium, not high, because the closest analogue SS1/TS1 is an ordered crystalline half-Heusler.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbOsSb is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic analogue NbRuSb is also on-hull in TS1. The CS/SS/TS analogue support is strong for the half-Heusler structure because NbRuSb in SS1/TS1 has the same F-43m framework and very similar Nb–Ru/Sb coordination to the target Nb–Os/Sb environment. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of experimentally synthesized 1:1:1 transition-metal antimonide half-Heuslers by sealed-tube or arc/anneal routes, especially SS1/TS1 and CS1. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control involving refractory Os/Nb plus volatile Sb under high-temperature sealed or inert conditions [Reasonable Inference], with SS2/SS3 showing Os pnictides can require multi-step sintering. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but not trivial, with flux growth or arc-melt/anneal preferred over purely dry solid-state reaction.
