### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 scoring band. (Target record) This is reinforced by multiple on-hull Cu–chalcogenide analogues, especially sulvanite Cu3VSe4 and chemically close TiCu4S4. (CS1; CS2; SS4; TS3) The main uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and no experimental CrCu3S4 synthesis is reported in the supplied evidence. (Target record; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for experimental CrCu3S4)

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, matching the moderate-temperature sealed-tube analogues. (CS1; CS2; CS5; TS3) The main penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile sulfur handling. (CS1; CS2; SS2; TS2) The route is not penalized as inherently multi-step because Cu3VSe4 is reported as a direct elemental reaction with long anneal and slow cooling. (CS2; SS4; TS3)

**F3 — Route 1.** Direct elemental powders are proven for analogues but are kinetically slow, especially with refractory Cr as the target transition metal. (CS1; CS2; CS5; [Reasonable Inference]) The strong structural match to sulvanite Cu3VSe4 raises the reconstruction score, while the 6-week to 2-month anneals lower the kinetics score. (CS1; CS2; SS4; TS3) The remaining uncertainty is whether Cr reaches the necessary oxidation/coordination state without forming binary chromium sulfides. ([Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS)

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 uses standard furnace/ampoule conditions but adds regrinding and reannealing, so it carries a multi-step deduction. (CS5; SS5; TS4) Vacuum or sulfur atmosphere and sulfur volatility add the same atmosphere and sealed-system penalties as Route 1. (CS1; TS5; SS2; TS2) Exact Cr2S3 precursor behavior is not established by CS/SS/TS, so that precursor-specific evidence is neutral 50. (insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS)

**F3 — Route 2.** Binary sulfide precursors should improve reaction activity because metal–sulfur bonds are preformed, and TS5 supports use of pre-synthesized copper vanadium sulfide in related chemistry. (TS5; [Reasonable Inference]) Regrinding/reannealing analogues show that cation diffusion and ordering can still be slow. (CS5; SS5; TS4) The uncertainty is whether sulfur can oxidize/rearrange Cr2S3 into the nominal CrCu3S4 sulvanite phase rather than leaving Cr-rich sulfides. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux growth benefits from lower temperature, because NaCu4S4 was made at 500 °C. (SS1; TS1) The route is penalized for vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multi-step flux removal, and air-/moisture-sensitive sulfide fluxes. (SS1; TS1; TS5) CS provides insufficient flux-specific evidence and is neutral 50 for this route. (insufficient evidence from CS)

**F3 — Route 3.** Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because a sulfur-rich flux can improve mass transport and crystal growth at lower temperature. (SS1; TS1) The structural score is moderated because the NaCu4S4 flux analogue is not sulvanite CrCu3S4 and may compete through alkali/Ba-containing Cu–S phases. (SS1; TS1; [Reasonable Inference]) The largest uncertainty is Cr solubility and Cr incorporation into the target phase. ([Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** Only one amorphization trigger is assigned because the nominal Cu+/Cr5+ cation contrast is large, while the sulvanite cell is simple and lacks face-sharing in the target description. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CrCu3S4 is best interpreted as a **computational ground-state candidate** because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but experimental ground-state status remains unproven because the supplied dataset identifies it as theory-only. (Target record) The synthesis case is strongly supported by the exact sulvanite analogue Cu3VSe4 and by several Cu–chalcogenide analogues synthesized in sealed tubes. (CS2; SS4; TS3; CS1; CS5) The most important enabling factor is the proven Cu3MCh4 tetrahedral sulvanite framework under moderate-temperature evacuated ampoule conditions. (CS2; SS4; TS3) The most important limiting factor is likely stabilization of nominal high-valent Cr in a sulfide environment without forming competing Cr–S or Cu–S phases. ([Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for CrCu3S4 experimental behavior) Overall, the target is likely synthesizable but will require long annealing, sulfur chemical-potential control, and careful impurity screening. (CS1; CS2; CS5; SS2; TS2; TS3)
