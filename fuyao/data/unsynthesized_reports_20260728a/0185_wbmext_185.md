### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported as E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the provided target data, and the closest CS/SS/TS analogues include multiple zero-hull Sr/Ba/Ca/Mg/K CrF6 phases [CS #1–#5, SS #1/#3/#4/#5, TS #1–#5]. The SS Mn analogue Li2MnF6 is also close to the hull at ~0.0336 eV/atom, supporting high thermodynamic plausibility for Mn–F octahedral chemistry [SS #2]. Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide direct experimental synthesis evidence for SrMnF6 itself, so direct-target experimental proof is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids very high temperature and >5 GPa pressure because the closest analogues are made at 300 °C and up to 300 atm [CS #1/#5, SS #1/#3, TS #1/#2]. The score is reduced mainly for F2/Ar handling, sealed Monell autoclave operation, multi-step gas loading/heating/post-handling, and fluorine toxicity [CS #1/#5, SS #1/#3/#5, TS #1/#2/#4]. The remaining uncertainty is Mn-specific redox control, since the cited direct analogues use Cr rather than Mn [CS #1/#5, SS #3, TS #2; [Reasonable Inference]].

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are favorable relative to ordinary solid-state synthesis because the CrF6 analogues form at only 300 °C under aggressive fluorination [CS #1/#5, SS #1/#3, TS #1/#2]. Structural reconstruction is especially favorable because SrCrF6 is nearly isostructural with the target SrMnF6 framework [CS #1, SS #3, TS #2]. The uncertainty is whether Mn(IV) fluoride volatility/decomposition behaves as controllably as Cr fluoride chemistry [CS #1/#5, SS #5, TS #4; [Reasonable Inference]].

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 has mild temperature and pressure because K/Rb CrF6 photochemistry occurs at ambient temperature with F2/HF and UV [CS #3/#4, TS #5]. The score remains low because anhydrous HF, F2, sealed fluoropolymer hardware, long UV irradiation, and volatile removal create substantial operational severity [CS #3/#4, TS #5]. Insufficient evidence from SS for this photochemical route is treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2.** HF/F2/UV chemistry provides high precursor activity and avoids bulk diffusion, as shown by ambient-temperature ACrF6 synthesis [CS #3/#4, TS #5]. The structural score is lower because the analogues are alkali CrF6 compounds, not alkaline-earth MnF6 compounds [CS #3/#4, TS #5]. The main uncertainty is whether Sr2+ solubility and Mn(IV) stabilization in HF give the same framework rather than soluble complexes or side fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 has the mildest physical conditions because SS #2 reports aqueous Mn fluoride synthesis at room temperature or boiling under air [SS #2]. The score is reduced for the likely need to use or prepare K2MnF6/(NH4)2MnF6 and to handle HF/NH4F-rich media [SS #2; [Reasonable Inference]]. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this solution-metathesis route and are treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3.** Solution precipitation should minimize diffusion limitations, and SS #2 supports formation of MnF6-containing fluoride solids from solution [SS #2]. The structural-reconstruction score is low because Li2MnF6 is not the same R-3m SrF12/MnF6 framework, and solution precipitation may favor hydrated or simpler Sr/Mn fluoride phases [SS #2; [Reasonable Inference]]. The key uncertainty is whether a solution route can generate the dense SrF12 cuboctahedral topology rather than a different MnF6 salt [SS #2; [Reasonable Inference]].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target framework has large A-site/B-site size contrast by analogy to SrCrF6, where Sr–F distances are far longer than Cr–F distances, so C1 is triggered [CS #1, SS #3, TS #2; [Reasonable Inference]]. Mixed corner/edge/face-sharing connectivity is present in the closest R-3m analogues, so C3 is triggered [CS #1/#5, SS #1/#3, TS #1/#2]. C2 is not triggered because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient explicit evidence for a >40-atom unit cell and the closest R-3m descriptions are simple-site frameworks [CS #1, SS #3, TS #2; neutral treatment for insufficient explicit atom-count evidence].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrMnF6 is likely thermodynamically accessible because the target is reported at zero hull and multiple chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar fluoride analogues are at or near zero hull [CS #1–#5, SS #1–#5, TS #1–#5]. The strongest synthesis support comes from SrCrF6, which is the direct Sr-containing R-3m analogue with matching SrF12/MF6 topology and 300 °C F2/Ar autoclave synthesis conditions [CS #1, SS #3, TS #2]. The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential under controlled autoclave conditions, which should stabilize a high-valent MnF6 octahedral network by analogy to CrF6 phases [CS #1/#5, SS #1/#3, TS #1/#2; Mn extension is [Reasonable Inference]]. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity under hazardous high-valent fluoride chemistry, because analogues report volatility, thermal decomposition, chromium losses, and difficulty obtaining pure samples [CS #1/#5, SS #5, TS #1/#2/#4]. Direct experimental proof for SrMnF6 is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the material should be treated as a plausible but not routine synthesis target.
