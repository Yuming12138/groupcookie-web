### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin; the closest thermodynamic analogues K2NiF6, K2NaCuF6, Cs3NiF7, and SrNiF6 also sit on the hull. [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5] The exact Cu analogue Cs2CuF6 is only slightly metastable at 0.0086 eV/atom, which reinforces high thermodynamic plausibility. [SS1, TS2] Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic because the supplied target record is theory-only and does not identify experimental synthesis. [TS; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 1:** The direct fluorination route avoids extreme temperature and multi-GPa pressure, as K2NiF6 was fluorinated at 275 °C. [CS1, TS1] The score is reduced because F2 is a reactive/toxic atmosphere and gas handling requires specialized equipment. [CS1, SS1] The main uncertainty is whether Cu can be oxidized to the target Cu(IV) phase under the milder K2NiF6-like conditions. [SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 1:** KCl/NiCl2 fluorination shows good low-temperature kinetics for a same-framework K2MF6 analogue. [CS1, SS2, TS1] However, the Cu case is extrapolated, and the exact Cu analogue required pressure-autoclave F2 conditions, so structural-transfer kinetics are penalized. [SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference] Phase impurities seen in related fluorination chemistry add uncertainty. [CS1]

**F2 — Route 2:** The autoclave route has no >1000 °C or >5 GPa penalty, but it uses F2/Ar gas, sealed pressure equipment, CuF3 preparation, and long annealing. [SS1, TS2] These factors create a high condition-severity burden even though the thermal temperature is moderate. [SS1, TS2] The score therefore reflects chemical hazard and process complexity more than heat/pressure extremity. [SS1, TS2]

**F3 — Route 2:** This route has the strongest kinetics/structure basis because Cs2CuF6 is an exact A2CuF6 Cu analogue with Fm-3m symmetry. [SS1, TS2] CuF3 + F2 is chemically well matched to high-valent Cu fluoride formation, but the 5-week anneal implies slow crystal growth or ordering. [SS1, TS2] The main uncertainty is whether smaller K stabilizes the same CuF6 network as effectively as Cs. [SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3:** Binary-fluoride bomb routes use 400–885 °C and controlled Ar/vacuum or sealed conditions, so temperature is manageable but atmosphere/sealing complexity is significant. [CS5, SS4, TS3] Toxic fluorides and the likely need for F2/CuF3 oxidant reduce the condition-severity score. [SS1, TS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a purely non-oxidizing binary route to Cu(IV), so that variant is treated as neutral/50 and not recommended. [CS5, SS4, TS3]

**F3 — Route 3:** KF/CuF2 solid-state chemistry is demonstrated, but it tends to form K3Cu2F7, KCuF3, K2CuF4, or K2NaCuF6-like compositions rather than K2CuF6. [CS5, SS4, TS3] Long 17-day anneals and impurity removal indicate slow kinetics and difficult phase selection. [CS5] The route is plausible only with added oxidation, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** from the CuF3/F2 Cs2CuF6 route. [SS1, TS2]

**F2 — Route 4:** Flux growth uses moderate temperatures of 300–500 °C but still requires dry Ar and diluted F2, so atmosphere and toxic-gas deductions apply. [SS5, TS4] The route also likely needs intermediate preparation or staged handling, creating multi-step complexity. [SS5, TS4; Reasonable Inference] No CS/SS/TS source directly proves KF-flux growth of K2CuF6, so direct target transfer has uncertainty. [SS5, TS4]

**F3 — Route 4:** KF-rich flux should improve ionic mobility relative to dry solid-state diffusion, as inferred from A3MF7 fluoride flux/tempering chemistry. [SS5, TS4; Reasonable Inference] The penalty is structural selectivity because the demonstrated flux analogue produces A3MF7-type phases, not K2CuF6. [SS5, TS4] Phase control is also difficult in related fluoride flux systems due to moisture sensitivity and container/impurity issues. [SS5, TS4]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** The target framework is simple and highly ordered by analogy to Fm-3m K2NiF6 and Cs2CuF6, which lowers amorphization risk. [CS1, SS1, SS2] One trigger is retained for large A-site/B-site cation size mismatch, but this mismatch is structurally accommodated by cuboctahedral K/Cs sites and octahedral Cu/Ni sites. [CS1, SS1; Reasonable Inference] Uncertainty remains because the target itself is theory-only and lacks experimental disorder data. [TS; Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2CuF6 is predicted as a likely ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple close thermodynamic analogues are on or near the hull. [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5] The strongest synthesis support comes from Cs2CuF6, which has the same A2CuF6 Fm-3m Cu-fluoride framework, and from K2NiF6, which has the same K2MF6 Fm-3m topology. [SS1, TS2, CS1, SS2, TS1] The most critical enabling factor is access to strongly oxidizing fluorination chemistry that can stabilize high-valent Cu in CuF6 octahedra. [SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is phase purity, because Cs2CuF6 and K–Cu–F analogues report difficult pure-phase formation or competing phases. [SS1, TS2, CS5, SS4] Overall, the material is synthesizable in principle but best treated as a specialized high-valent fluoride target rather than a routine solid-state ceramic. [CS1, SS1, TS2; Reasonable Inference]
