## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target is supplied with an energy above hull of 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, and the source validation states that its physical hull distance is clipped to zero from a slightly negative raw normalized value. **(Target validation)** TS analogues such as Zr₃Cu₄Si₆, HfCuSi, and NbSi₂ are also reported at or very near zero hull energy, indicating that related Nb/Cu/Si and transition-metal silicide chemistries can produce stable or nearly stable compounds. **(TS)** The remaining uncertainty is that the target stability is computational rather than experimentally confirmed. **(Target validation)**

### Route 1 — F2

Route 1 requires a temperature near 1200 °C and an evacuated sealed ampoule, matching the reported NbSi₂ and Zr–Cu–Si processing conditions. **(SS, TS, CS)** The vacuum deduction reflects atmosphere control rather than severe pressure; no direct evidence indicates that Nb, Cu, or Si requires sealing because of volatility at this temperature. **(CS, SS, TS)** The principal limitation is the refractory nature of Nb and the need for solid-state interdiffusion. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### Route 1 — F3

The route uses elemental powders, which have lower effective reaction activity than molecular or nanoscale precursors. **[Reasonable Inference]** NbSi₂ synthesis at 1200 °C demonstrates that Nb–Si diffusion is feasible below the melting point of Nb, but the target adds Cu and requires ordering into a more complex P4/nmm structure. **(SS, TS)** The long reaction times reported for related intermetallics support a moderate kinetic penalty. **(CS, SS, TS)**

### Route 2 — F2

Arc melting followed by annealing is documented for several structurally related Cu–transition-metal–Si phases, including Zr₂CuSi₄, Hf₂CuSi₄, HfCuSi, and TiCuSi. **(SS, TS)** The route is penalized for high local melting temperatures, repeated processing, and the need for controlled Ar handling. **(SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS for an external high-pressure requirement or a volatile target component. **(CS, SS, TS)**

### Route 2 — F3

Arc melting improves precursor mixing and provides a liquid-assisted homogenization pathway, which is advantageous for a refractory Nb-containing system. **(SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** The strongest structural evidence is HfCuSiAs, which has the same P4/nmm space group and closely analogous eight-coordinate Hf–Si/As and Cu–anion connectivity. **(TS)** Transfer from Hf/As to Nb/Si is chemically plausible but not experimentally demonstrated, so the score remains moderate rather than high. **(TS; [Reasonable Inference])**

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

C1 cannot be scored from the provided similarity data because ionic radii and formal oxidation states are not supplied; this factor is therefore neutral. **(CS, SS, TS)** C2 also lacks sufficient evidence because the target unit-cell atom count is not provided. **(CS, SS, TS)** C3 is supported because the target includes mixed face- and corner-sharing Cu-centered polyhedra, a connectivity pattern also present in related Cu–transition-metal–Si structures. **(Target structure; CS, SS)** The resulting one-trigger assessment gives an F4 score of 80. **(CS, SS)**

The target is computationally predicted to lie on the convex hull, so it is more consistent with a ground-state or highly stable phase than with an intrinsically metastable phase, although no experimental synthesis is identified. **(Target validation)** CS, SS, and TS provide strong support from stable Cu–transition-metal–Si compounds, NbSi₂, and especially the P4/nmm HfCuSiAs analogue. **(CS, SS, TS)** The most important enabling factor is the close structural and chemical analogy to experimentally accessible Cu–transition-metal silicides and P4/nmm pnictide-derived compounds. **(SS, TS)** The main limiting factor is kinetic and phase-selectivity control: refractory Nb must be homogenized with Cu and Si while avoiding competing Nb–Si or Cu–Si phases. **(CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])** Overall, arc melting followed by prolonged argon annealing is the preferred first experimental route. **(SS, TS)**
