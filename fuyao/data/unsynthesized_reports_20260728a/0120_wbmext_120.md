### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
EuMnN3 is predicted exactly on the hull at 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic baseline is excellent [Target]. Most CS/SS/TS analogues are also on or very near the hull, which supports the plausibility of stable nitride chemistry in related chemical and structural spaces [CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is explicitly theory-only and not experimentally validated [Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 Solid-State Nitridation**  
The condition score is moderate because the route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but requires controlled N2, glovebox handling, and repeated processing [CS4, SS4, SS5, TS5]. Kinetics are the main limitation because Ce2MnN3 required multiple grinding/reaction cycles and the target has a unique prototype rather than a directly copied structure [CS4, Target]. Insufficient evidence from CS3 for dense nitride solid-state transfer is treated as neutral 50 for this route [CS3].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Li3N Flux**  
The condition score is lower than direct sintering because sealed ampoules, Li3N flux, and air-sensitive precursors are required [CS5, CS2]. The kinetic score is the best because Li3N flux has demonstrated recrystallization of binary rare-earth/transition-metal nitrides into ternary crystals [CS5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Li3N flux details is treated as neutral 50, but CS evidence is directly relevant [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5, CS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Na/Ga Flux**  
The condition score is limited by sealed reactive Na/Ga/NaN3 flux handling, even though the temperature is only ~900 °C [CS1]. Kinetics benefit from flux transport, but CS1 reports small product amounts and competing La–Cr–N/LaN phases, so structural/phase selection is risky [CS1]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Na/Ga flux is treated as neutral 50 [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 4 High-Pressure Azide**  
The condition score is lowest because SS/TS analogues require 3–12 GPa, hazardous azides, and specialized multianvil equipment [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. Kinetics are mixed: azides provide high nitrogen activity, but converting to an ordered ternary Eu–Mn phase is a major reconstruction with no direct Eu–Mn analogue [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3, CS1–CS5]. This route is justified mainly by the target’s short minimum distance and SS/TS diazenide similarity, so it remains exploratory [Target, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the cation-size/coordination mismatch check is triggered because Eu is high-coordinate while Mn is tetrahedral in the target [Target]. The unit-cell-size trigger is not supported by the target description, and mixed vertex+edge+face connectivity is not reported for EuMnN3 even though some CS analogues have mixed sharing [Target, CS4, CS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the target unit-cell atom count is treated as neutral 50 for that specific check [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

EuMnN3 is best viewed as a **DFT ground-state candidate** rather than a confirmed experimental phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom but the supplied record says it is theory-only [Target]. The synthesis support is fairly strong from chemical analogues: Ce2MnN3 and Ce2CrN3 show rare-earth transition-metal nitrides can be made near 900 °C by solid-state or Li3N flux routes [CS4, CS5]. The most critical enabling factor is high nitrogen activity under inert/sealed conditions, especially via Li3N flux, which improves transport and nitride formation [CS5, CS2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because CS1 and CS4 report small yields, competing phases, repeated cycles, and sensitivity to reaction conditions [CS1, CS4]. Overall, the target is **moderately synthesizable**, with Li3N flux as the best first experimental route [CS5, CS2, Target].
