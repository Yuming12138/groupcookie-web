### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is on the computed hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin [Target]. TS analogues are also stable or nearly stable, with several E_hull = 0.0 entries [TS1/TS2/TS4] and low positive entries near 0.016–0.018 eV/atom [TS3/TS5]. CS and SS independently show low-hull boride analogues, so no CS/SS/TS thermodynamic source is neutral here [CS1/CS3/CS5/SS1/SS2/SS3/SS4/SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and not experimentally validated in CS/SS/TS [Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 HPHT**  
The HPHT severity score is penalized by 1800–1900 °C, sealed high-T handling, and multistep capsule processing, but the pressure is 3.0 GPa and therefore below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty [CS1/SS2/TS3]. Kinetics are better than ambient solid-state synthesis because the same Mn–B analogue forms after only 10–30 min at HPHT conditions [CS1/SS2/TS3]. The main uncertainty is exact-prototype transfer, because Mn₃B₄ is not the same Mn₂B Fddd phase [CS1/SS2/TS3; Target].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Solid-State**  
Solid-state severity is high because close analogues use vacuum/Ar control, 1200–1500 °C temperatures, and sometimes very long annealing schedules [CS3/TS4]. Kinetics are limited by elemental powder reaction and boride ordering, as CS3 and TS4 both imply high-temperature and/or long-duration processing [CS3/TS4]. The route is plausible because Mo₂B is Khatyrkite-structured, but it is less chemically direct than Mn–B HPHT [CS3 vs. CS1].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Al Flux**  
Flux severity is moderate because the route avoids pressure but still uses >1000 °C, Ar atmosphere, flux removal, and multistep workup [SS1/TS1]. Kinetics are favorable because molten Al provides a liquid transport pathway, but structural selectivity is uncertain because Al-containing Mn–Al–B phases and impurities are directly indicated by the analogue evidence [SS1/TS1]. **Insufficient evidence from CS** exists for flux growth specifically, so CS is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 Arc-Melting / Anneal**  
Arc processing is severe because it requires very high temperature, Ar/vacuum control, repeated melting, and annealing [CS2/CS4/TS4]. Kinetics benefit from melting, but composition control is a major uncertainty because exact Mn₂B is not reported and long anneals are needed for related ternary borides [TS4]. **Insufficient evidence from SS** exists for arc-melt synthesis specifically, so SS is neutral 50 for this route-specific factor.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger C1 [Target; Reasonable Inference]. C2 is tentatively triggered because the Fddd setting with multiple inequivalent sites likely gives a large conventional cell, but CS/SS/TS do not directly provide target atom count and are neutral 50 for this check [Target; Reasonable Inference]. C3 is not triggered because the target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, although several structurally similar ternary analogues do show mixed polyhedral sharing [SS3/SS4/TS2/TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂B is likely a **DFT ground-state candidate** because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues are also stable or near-stable [Target; TS1/TS2/TS3/TS4/TS5]. The analogue support is meaningful but not definitive: CS/SS/TS include Mn–B HPHT synthesis, Khatyrkite-type Mo₂B solid-state synthesis, and Mn–Al–B flux growth, but none reports exact Mn₂B Fddd synthesis [CS1/CS3/SS1/SS2/TS1/TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the combination of on-hull thermodynamics and close Mn–B local bonding similarity to known Mn–B / Mn–B-containing borides [Target; CS1/SS1/SS2/TS3]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity under harsh conditions, especially competition with other Mn–B or Mn–Al–B borides and Mn loss at high temperature [CS1/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the best route is HPHT direct reaction because it is the most chemically direct and already demonstrated for a nearby Mn–B phase [CS1/SS2/TS3].
