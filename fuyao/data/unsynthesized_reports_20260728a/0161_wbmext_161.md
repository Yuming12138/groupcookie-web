# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a calculated physical E_hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category under the supplied scoring rubric (target validation). RbCrF₆ and RbRuF₆ also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS), and several thermodynamic analogues including Na₂GeF₆, BaGeF₆, and BaSiF₆ have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS). The main uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee favorable reaction kinetics or successful isolation of the desired polymorph.

## F2 — Route 1

The photochemical route operates at ambient temperature, avoiding severe thermal penalties (CS). However, F₂ and anhydrous HF require controlled atmospheres and specialized handling, while the reaction lasts 10–20 days and requires UV irradiation (CS). The resulting score is therefore limited by operational severity rather than thermodynamic instability.

## F2 — Route 2

The direct fluorination route is thermally moderate at approximately 350 °C, but it still requires elemental F₂ and likely a sealed fluorination apparatus (CS). Volatile phosphorus halides/fluorides and possible multi-step precursor preparation add penalties [Reasonable Inference]. This route is operationally simpler than the photochemical route but chemically less directly matched to the target.

## F2 — Route 3

The solution route uses mild temperatures and ambient pressure, consistent with slow-evaporation fluoride syntheses reported for BaSiF₆, BaGeF₆, and related materials (SS; TS). Its score is reduced by the need for dry handling and potentially volatile liquid SO₂ or moisture-sensitive PF₆⁻ chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of a direct RbPF₆ crystallization precedent creates additional uncertainty.

## F3 — Route 1

The strongest kinetic evidence comes from RbCrF₆, which forms the same overall structure through a liquid HF/photochemical fluorination process at ambient temperature (CS). The liquid medium reduces diffusion limitations, and the exact Rb-based structural analogue supports a high structural-reconstruction score (CS). The uncertainty is whether P(V) fluoride formation proceeds with comparable kinetics to chromium fluoride formation; this is [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 2

RbRuF₆ demonstrates that an Rb-containing MF₆ phase can form by direct fluorination near 350 °C (CS). The same family of cuboctahedral and octahedral connectivity gives this route a high structural-reconstruction score (CS). Nevertheless, phosphorus-containing volatile intermediates could reduce precursor utilization and cause competing phases, so the precursor-activity score is lower [Reasonable Inference].

## F3 — Route 3

Slow evaporation and diffusion routes produce crystalline fluoride materials under mild conditions (SS; TS). These methods support the feasibility of obtaining ordered fluoride crystals but do not specifically demonstrate RbPF₆ or the target R-3 structure. Consequently, solution kinetics are favorable while structural selectivity is scored conservatively.

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains one cation species, so a cation-radius mismatch trigger is not applicable (target structure). The unit-cell atom count is not supplied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is therefore assigned neutral treatment for that criterion. Mixed corner, edge, and face connectivity is present, giving one definite trigger, but the same connectivity is observed in the highly similar RbCrF₆ and RbRuF₆ structures (CS), resulting in a low–medium rather than high amorphization risk.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbPF₆ is most likely a calculated ground-state phase rather than a metastable material because its supplied physical E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and chemically related fluorides are also calculated at or near the hull (target validation; CS; TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from RbCrF₆, which reproduces the target’s R-3 framework and was obtained by photochemical fluorination in anhydrous HF (CS). The principal enabling factor is the strong structural-family continuity among RbMF₆ compounds, especially the matching RbF₁₂/PF₆-type connectivity (CS). The principal limitation is the absence of a direct experimental RbPF₆ synthesis record and the likely volatility and moisture sensitivity of phosphorus fluoride intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible and worth experimental testing, with Route 1 providing the best evidence-based starting point.
