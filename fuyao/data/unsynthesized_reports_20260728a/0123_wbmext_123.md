### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest SS/TS M2NBr/M2NX analogues are also on-hull, supporting a high thermodynamic score. [SS][TS] CS also contains on-hull rare-earth nitride and Eu2PBr analogues, so CS provides positive but somewhat less direct thermodynamic support. [CS] Remaining uncertainty is that WBM status is theory-only, so experimental stability against unlisted Yb–N–Br polymorphs or off-stoichiometric phases is not proven. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions if modeled after Sr2NBr/Sr2NCl and Eu2PBr temperatures, but it still requires sealed Ar handling and air-sensitive nitride/halide precursors. [SS][TS][CS] The severity score is limited mainly by precursor preparation, sealed containers, and possible bromide/Yb volatility. [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference] CS/SS/TS do not directly report Yb2NBr, so the Yb-specific volatility/redox penalty is an extrapolation. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: Sr2NBr and Ca2NBr already form the same Caswellsilverite framework. [SS][TS] The kinetic penalty comes from solid-state diffusion over 5-day firings and reported minor impurity phases in alkaline-earth analogues. [SS][TS] CS Eu2PBr supports the divalent rare-earth halide-pnictide framework but uses P rather than N, so nitrogen incorporation remains uncertain. [CS]

**F2 — Route 2.**  
CS azide/flux routes operate at manageable 800–900 °C, but they require evacuated or sealed ampoules, hazardous NaN3/CsN3, and long heat treatments. [CS] SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for azide-flux synthesis and are scored neutral for this route-specific factor. [SS: insufficient evidence][TS: insufficient evidence] The score is therefore moderate rather than high despite favorable temperatures. [CS]

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux and azide chemistry should improve precursor activity and nitrogen delivery, as CS examples form rare-earth nitride-containing crystals from Ln metal, azide, halide, and alkali-halide flux. [CS] The weakness is structural transferability: most CS azide/flux examples are nitride chalcogenides or bismuth nitrides rather than M2NBr Caswellsilverite phases. [CS] SS/TS support the target structure but not the azide flux mechanism, so their route-specific signal is insufficient and neutral. [SS: insufficient evidence][TS: insufficient evidence]

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 keeps temperatures in the same 750–900 °C band as SS/TS/CS analogues, but it still uses sealed inert handling, hygroscopic flux, and air-sensitive Yb reagents. [SS][TS][CS] Its condition severity is not lower than Route 1 because flux separation and slow cooling add procedural complexity. [CS][Reasonable Inference] No high pressure is needed, which strongly helps feasibility. [SS][TS]

**F3 — Route 3.**  
This route has the best kinetic score because it combines the correct binary nitride–bromide chemistry from SS/TS with flux-assisted transport from CS. [SS][TS][CS][Reasonable Inference] The main uncertainty is that no source directly reports flux growth of Yb2NBr or a lanthanide M2NBr nitride bromide, so the improvement is extrapolated. [Reasonable Inference] Slow cooling should help ordering/crystallinity but may also amplify competing phase growth. [CS][SS][Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target is a simple single-cation Caswellsilverite ternary, and the closest SS/TS analogues show ordered octahedral frameworks rather than large, disordered, multi-cation cells. [SS][TS] Edge/corner sharing is present, but the full vertex + edge + face-sharing coexistence trigger is not met for the target-like analogues. [SS][TS][CS] CS contains one less-relevant face-sharing Pr–Bi–N example, but SS/TS are more structurally direct and therefore dominate the F4 assessment. [CS][SS][TS]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Yb2NBr is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target hull energy is zero and the closest SS/TS M2NBr analogues are on-hull. [SS][TS] The synthesis case is strong structurally because Sr2NBr and Ca2NBr share the same Caswellsilverite R-3m framework, while Eu2PBr supports divalent rare-earth bromide-pnictide Caswellsilverite chemistry. [SS][TS][CS] The key enabling factor is the existence of multiple sealed-ampoule nitride halide routes at 750–900 °C. [SS][TS][CS] The key limiting factor is Yb redox/stoichiometry control, because the target requires Yb(II)-like charge balance while rare-earth nitride/halide chemistry can access competing Yb(III)-rich products. [Reasonable Inference based on CS/SS/TS] Overall, the material is realistically synthesizable but likely requires careful sealed-ampoule, anhydrous, reducing-condition optimization. [CS][SS][TS][Reasonable Inference]
