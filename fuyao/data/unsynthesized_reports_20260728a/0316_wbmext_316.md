# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is supplied with \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom, a negative formation energy, and a reported WBM leave-one-out hull improvement of −0.036 eV/atom, supporting a thermodynamically favorable calculated phase (target validation data). CS-1, SS-1, SS-2, and TS-1/2 also show stable Ta-containing carbide references with hull values at or near zero, providing chemical precedent for stable Ta–C frameworks (CS-1; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an independent exact-hull calculation of Ta₆C₅**, so the numerical score relies primarily on the supplied target metadata.

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because the demonstrated TaC route operates at 1350 °C and requires Ar with reduced oxygen partial pressure (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The proposed oxide route adds multiple operations, including reduction, high-temperature reaction, and likely grinding, which lowers the score **[Reasonable Inference]**. It avoids high pressure and avoids intrinsically volatile or radioactive precursors, so the severity is lower than for vacuum or molten-metal alternatives **[Reasonable Inference]**.

## F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized for prolonged high-temperature treatment and controlled atmosphere requirements **[Reasonable Inference]**. The Ta₂S₂C analogue required five days of sintering and annealing, supporting the expectation that direct Ta/carbon diffusion may be slow (SS-5; TS-4). The route does not require high pressure, but repeated grinding and annealing are likely necessary for adequate reaction completion **[Reasonable Inference]**.

## F2 — Route 3

The molten-metal route is based on Ta₃AlC₂ formation at 1400–1500 °C for 50 h under Ar, followed by flux removal (SS-2; TS-2). Its main severity sources are the high temperature, long duration, controlled atmosphere, and chemical post-treatment (SS-2; TS-2). Additional uncertainty arises because Al/Sn may stabilize competing ternary phases rather than the binary Ta₆C₅ target (SS-2; SS-4; TS-2; TS-5).

## F3 — Route 1

The oxide/carbon reaction has moderate precursor activity because Ta₂O₅ and carbon are refractory solids, but the use of finely divided carbon can improve contact **[Reasonable Inference]**. TaC formation at 1350 °C confirms that Ta–C reaction is kinetically accessible below the Ta melting temperature (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The target nevertheless requires ordering from a simple cubic TaC-like local environment into a monoclinic structure with two Ta sites and three C sites, reducing the structural-reconstruction score **[Reasonable Inference]**.

## F3 — Route 2

Direct Ta plus carbon has the lowest precursor-activity score because both principal reactants are refractory solids **[Reasonable Inference]**. The prolonged treatment used for Ta₂S₂C supports a low-diffusion concern for related Ta carbide chemistry (SS-5; TS-4). Although TaC provides local Ta–C structural precedent, the target’s multiple inequivalent sites and monoclinic symmetry imply substantial ordering requirements (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1).

## F3 — Route 3

A liquid Al/Sn medium can improve mass transport and precursor contact, as demonstrated in Ta–Al–C molten-metal synthesis (SS-2; TS-2). This gives Route 3 a higher kinetic score than direct Ta/carbon mixing **[Reasonable Inference]**. However, the known products are ternary MAX/H-phase compounds, so the flux may promote a chemically different structural family rather than the binary target (SS-2; SS-4; TS-2; TS-5).

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only Ta as a cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its structure contains edge- and corner-sharing polyhedra but no reported face-sharing units, so the mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered (target structural description). **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the unit-cell atom count**, so that check is treated as neutral rather than as a positive amorphization trigger.

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₆C₅ is most plausibly a calculated ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied physical energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although this has not been experimentally validated (target validation data). The TaC references provide strong chemical and local-bonding support, while Ta-rich carbide analogues show that complex Ta–C frameworks can be accessed by high-temperature synthesis (CS-1; SS-1; SS-2; SS-5; TS-1; TS-2; TS-4). The most important enabling factor is the strong, thermodynamically favorable Ta–C chemistry combined with demonstrated carbide formation under reducing conditions (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The main limiting factor is selective phase control: TaC and other Ta-containing carbides or ternary phases may form more readily than the ordered monoclinic Ta₆C₅ structure **[Reasonable Inference]**. Route 1 is therefore recommended as the initial experimental strategy, followed by Route 3 only if direct oxide/carbon processing gives persistent TaC-rich products.
