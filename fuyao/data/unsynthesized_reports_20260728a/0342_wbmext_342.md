### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin (Target record). The top chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar analogues all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, giving consistent support rather than contradictory signals (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic: the target record identifies SmAuPb as a theory-only WBM candidate without identified experimental synthesis (Target record).

**F2 — Route 1 Pb Flux**  
The Pb-flux route avoids temperatures above 1000 °C but requires vacuum sealing, slow cooling, annealing, and centrifugation, which produces a moderate condition penalty (CS1–CS5). Pb toxicity and sealed-system handling add practical severity **[Reasonable Inference]**; the target safety note explicitly flags lead handling. SS/TS do not give direct Pb-flux processing evidence, so **insufficient evidence from SS/TS; neutral 50 for route-specific F2 signal**, though they still support structural plausibility (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5).

**F3 — Route 1 Pb Flux**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to a dry solid-state route because molten Pb flux should improve dissolution and transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, and the CS analogues are grown successfully from lead flux (CS1–CS5). Structural reconstruction is not perfectly direct because CS flux analogues are Ln3Au3Bi4 rather than half-Heusler SmAuPb, but half-Heusler REAuSn analogues reduce the structural risk (CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether Pb self-flux stabilizes stoichiometric SmAuPb rather than Pb-rich or Sm–Au competing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 2 Arc-Melting**  
Arc melting is strongly supported for REAuSn half-Heuslers but likely exposes Pb to high local temperatures, which increases condition severity **[Reasonable Inference]** (CS4, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The route also requires purified Ar, repeated melting, and sealed annealing, matching the analogue workflows (CS4, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The uncertainty is that no analogue route explicitly treats Pb-containing SmAuPb under arc conditions, so Pb-loss deductions are extrapolated **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 2 Arc-Melting**  
Arc melting improves homogenization and bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion barrier, as analogue syntheses re-melt buttons for homogeneity (SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2). The same half-Heusler F-43m topology appears in ErAuSn, LuAuSn, and ScAuSn, supporting structural assembly after melting/annealing (CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). However, some related samples show minor unidentified phases, so phase purity is not assumed trivial (CS4, SS3, TS3).

**F2 — Route 3 Solid-State / Induction Anneal**  
This route uses lower nominal temperatures, commonly 700–800 °C in analogues, but still requires Ar or sealed containment and long annealing (SS4–SS5, TS4–TS5). Pb volatility/toxicity and Sm handling add severity **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS does not directly support this processing mode for SmAuPb, so **insufficient evidence from CS; neutral 50 for route-specific F2 signal**.

**F3 — Route 3 Solid-State / Induction Anneal**  
The lower-temperature route may suffer slower Sm/Au ordering because dense elemental metals are less reactive than solution precursors **[Reasonable Inference]**. SmAuGe and ScAuSn analogues demonstrate that ordered RE–Au–p-block intermetallics can form by induction/solid-state annealing, but they are not direct Pb analogues (SS4–SS5, TS4–TS5). The uncertainty is whether Pb’s transient liquid behavior helps ordering enough to offset lower-temperature diffusion limits **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target is a small, ordered half-Heusler-type intermetallic rather than a large complex unit cell, lowering amorphization risk (CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5; Target record). One size-mismatch trigger is assigned from Sm/Au/Pb radius differences **[Reasonable Inference]**, but CS/SS/TS do not provide explicit radii, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the C1 radii check; neutral 50 for that source-specific signal**. The target reports face- and corner-sharing connectivity but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the strongest topology-driven amorphization penalty is not triggered (Target record).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SmAuPb is most likely a **DFT ground-state candidate** because the target record gives E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, but it remains experimentally unvalidated in the supplied data (Target record). The analogue support is fairly strong: CS provides direct Pb-flux chemistry for Ln–Au–Bi phases, while CS/SS/TS provide multiple REAuSn half-Heusler examples with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS5, CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the combination of known RE–Au–p-block intermetallic formation and a Pb flux that can also act as the target Pb source **[Reasonable Inference]** (CS1–CS5). The most critical limiting factor is Pb stoichiometry control, because Pb can behave simultaneously as reagent, flux, and possible volatile/toxic component **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with the Pb self-flux route giving the best balance of thermodynamic stability, mass transport, and structural transfer.
