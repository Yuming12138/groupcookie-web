[Target validation] This supports a high thermodynamic score and suggests that the phase is more likely to be a ground-state candidate than a deliberately trapped metastable phase. [Reasonable Inference] The remaining uncertainty is that the stability is computational and the source dataset identifies no experimental synthesis. [Target validation]

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses temperatures comparable to reported sealed-tube fluoride reactions and avoids high pressure or highly reactive gases. [SS-3; TS-1; TS-5] The main penalties are assigned for the expected volatility and moisture sensitivity of GeF₄, for which the supplied analogue records provide no direct handling evidence. [Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for GeF₄-specific behavior] The route is therefore moderately severe rather than intrinsically extreme.

## F2 — Route 2

Flux growth is supported by CaAlF₅ synthesis using a NaF/CaF₂/AlF₃/chloride flux under Ar and controlled cooling. [CS-5] It requires additional flux preparation, slow cooling, crystal separation, and corrosion-resistant containment, while GeF₄ introduces a volatility concern. [CS-5; Reasonable Inference] These factors justify a lower F2 score than direct sealed-tube sintering.

## F2 — Route 3

Direct fluorination has a relevant CaPdF₆ precedent at 100 °C using diluted F₂, followed by tempering and F₂-stream cooling. [CS-1] The low temperature is favorable, but F₂ is toxic and highly reactive, and the required fluorination apparatus is more specialized than a conventional sealed-tube furnace. [CS-1; Reasonable Inference] The absence of direct Ca–Ge fluorination data increases uncertainty. [insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Ca–Ge direct fluorination]

## F3 — Route 1

CaF₂ is a stable binary fluoride, while GeF₄ is molecular and chemically active; this gives moderate precursor activity but creates a retention problem.
