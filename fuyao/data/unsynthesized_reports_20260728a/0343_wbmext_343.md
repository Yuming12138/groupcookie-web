### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has supplied E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range by the required rubric; TS also shows several nearest thermodynamic analogues at E_hull = 0.0 [TS1–TS5]. CS/SS evidence is supportive but not uniform, because Sm/Gd P4/mbm analogues are on-hull while several P4₂/mmc rare-earth analogues are ~0.13–0.16 eV/atom above hull [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because EuB₂C₂ is theory-only in the supplied target description and Eu phase formation is problematic in analogue data [CS3, SS2, SS4, TS2].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc-melting is penalized for high fusion temperature, controlled Ar atmosphere, sealed annealing, multiple steps, and Eu volatility [CS2, CS3, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS3]. The route is chemically relevant because Sm/Gd RB₂C₂ analogues were made this way, but the Eu-specific volatility warning keeps the condition-severity score low [CS3, SS2, SS4, TS2].

**F3 — Route 1**  
The liquid/fused arc-melt stage improves kinetic access compared with a purely solid-state powder reaction [CS2, CS3, SS2, TS2, TS3]. The structural target is close to Sm/Gd RB₂C₂ analogues, but EuB₂C₂/YbB₂C₂ failure or purity difficulty lowers the reconstruction score [CS3, SS2, SS4, TS2].

**F2 — Route 2**  
The sealed-tube route is less severe because the exact I4/mcm CaB₂C₂ analogue forms at 1050 °C rather than 1900 °C [SS1, TS1]. Deductions still apply for controlled atmosphere, sealed-tube handling, multiple processing steps, and Eu volatility/air sensitivity by [Reasonable Inference] from Eu/Yb evidence [SS1, TS1, CS3, SS2, TS2].

**F3 — Route 2**  
The exact CaB₂C₂ structural match gives this route the best reconstruction score, and the 1050 °C/48 h synthesis suggests the framework can crystallize without extreme sintering [SS1, TS1]. The kinetic uncertainty is that Ca is not Eu, and Eu volatility/phase instability may narrow the usable processing window [CS3, SS2, TS2].

**F2 — Route 3**  
The boride/oxide/graphite route is strongly penalized for 1900 °C vacuum processing and multi-step boride pre-synthesis [CS1, CS4, CS5, SS3]. Its route severity is partly offset by stable oxide/graphite precursors, but Eu loss at 1900 °C remains a [Reasonable Inference] from Eu/Yb volatility evidence [CS3, SS2, TS2].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Rare-earth RB₂C₂ phases are demonstrated by this method, so the structural endpoint is plausible [CS1, CS4, CS5, SS3]. However, oxide/boride/graphite precursors require major reconstruction and high-temperature diffusion, so the kinetic score is lower than the sealed-tube elemental route [CS1, CS4, CS5, SS3].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target and close analogues are single-cation crystalline borocarbides, so no cation-size-mismatch trigger is assigned [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS3]. The reported M₂B₄C₄-type analogue formulas and successful crystalline products argue against a large-cell amorphization tendency, but exact polyhedral-sharing evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and is treated as neutral 50 for that sub-check [SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3].

Eu(BC)₂ is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues are also on-hull [TS1–TS5].
