### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted exactly on the hull, so the primary thermodynamic score is near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom band. (Target data) The analogue set strongly supports this because most CS/SS/TS entries are also at E_hull = 0 or very close to it. (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5) Remaining uncertainty is experimental: the target is theory-only WBM and not reported as synthesized in the provided dataset. (Target data)

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids high temperature but requires glovebox work, high-vacuum ampoule sealing, volatile selenium chemistry, and multi-step flux handling. (SS1, TS1) Kinetics are favorable relative to bulk solid-state synthesis because the closest analogue forms at 350 °C and already contains NbSe4 tetrahedra, but Ag removal and Cs:Nb:Se stoichiometry tuning are nontrivial. [Reasonable Inference] (SS1, TS1, Target data) CS has insufficient direct signal for Nb-polyselenide flux and is treated as neutral 50 for this route-specific evidence.

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 uses standard sealed-ampoule solid-state conditions, but long anneals, protective-gas grinding, and volatile/air-sensitive Cs–Se reagents justify condition deductions. (CS1, SS4, TS3) Kinetics are slower because the analogue requires weeks to months and intermediate grinding, implying diffusion and ordering limitations. (CS1, SS4, TS3) The route is chemically plausible but less structurally direct than Route 1 because NbSe4 framework formation must occur from binary precursors. [Reasonable Inference] (SS1, TS1)

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 benefits from direct oxide/carbonate selenization precedent in alkali selenides, but it uses a reactive Se-loaded H2 stream and volatile/toxic selenium species. (CS3, CS4, CS5) Kinetics are limited by the inferred need to convert refractory Nb2O5 into a fully selenized Nb(V) tetrahedral phase. [Reasonable Inference] (CS3, CS5) SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Nb oxide-to-Cs3NbSe4 conversion by this route, so both are neutral 50 for that route-specific factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is mild in temperature because the solvothermal analogue runs at 170 °C, but sealed organic solvents, toxic amines/thiophenol, and inferred moisture-sensitive Nb(V) reagents add practical severity. (CS2) Kinetic transport may be better in solution, but crystallizing a 3D cubic Cs–Nb–Se framework from solvothermal media lacks direct support. [Reasonable Inference] (CS2) SS and TS provide insufficient solvothermal NbSe4-framework evidence and are neutral 50 for this route.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The large Cs/Nb cation-size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. [Reasonable Inference] The target’s reported connectivity includes edge and corner sharing but not the full vertex + edge + face coexistence criterion, so C3 is not triggered. (Target data) CS/SS/TS analogues show complex chalcogenide connectivity, but they do not provide direct target unit-cell atom-count evidence, so that sub-check uses neutral 50 source treatment. (CS1, SS1, TS1)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs3NbSe4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple close analogues are also on the hull. (Target data, CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5) The strongest synthesis support comes from Cs2NbAgSe4, which directly demonstrates Cs–Nb–Se chemistry with NbSe4 tetrahedra and nearly identical Nb–Se bond length. (SS1, TS1) The most critical enabling factor is the transferable NbSe4 tetrahedral motif under low-temperature polyselenide flux conditions. (SS1, TS1, Target data) The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Ag-free Cs–Nb–Se system, because the closest analogue notes that some related compositions could not be prepared under the same flux conditions. (SS1, TS1) Overall, the target is **moderately likely synthesizable**, with Route 1 preferred for discovery-scale screening. (SS1, TS1)
