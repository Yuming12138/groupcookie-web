### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
BaVF6 is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 thermodynamic score bracket. [Target] Close fluoride analogues BaCrF6, BaCrF5, BaVOF4, and VF3 are also on or near the hull, supporting a stable fluoride chemical space. [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4] Remaining uncertainty is experimental, because the target is theory-only in the supplied dataset. [Target]

**Route 1 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
The F₂ autoclave route has the strongest structure-transfer evidence because BaCrF6 is a direct AMF6 analogue with BaF12/MF6 connectivity matching BaVF6. [CS1, SS5, TS3, Target] The severity score is low because F₂, 300 atm pressure, Monell autoclaves, and volatile/high-valent fluoride phase-control problems are explicitly reported. [CS1, CS5, SS5, TS3] Kinetics are favorable relative to ordinary solid-state synthesis because reactive F₂ enables formation at 300 °C, but phase purity and fluoride loss remain limiting. [CS1, CS5, Reasonable Inference]

**Route 2 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
The sealed-tube route balances manageable conditions with strong fluoride precedent, because phase-pure BaCrF5 forms from BaF2 + CrF3 in Pt ampoules at 750 °C for 65 h. [CS2, SS2, TS2] The condition penalty comes from vacuum/sealed tubes and likely volatile/moisture-sensitive V fluorides, not from extreme temperature or pressure. [CS2, SS2, TS2, TS4, Reasonable Inference] Kinetics are moderate because BaF2 is refractory, but starting from fluorides reduces chemical reconstruction relative to oxide fluorination. [CS2, SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]

**Route 3 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
HF hydrothermal synthesis has mild temperatures and proven Ba–V–F crystallization through BaVOF4 and VF3, so precursor transport is likely good. [SS1, TS1, TS4] The major kinetic/phase penalty is that the demonstrated Ba–V product is an oxyfluoride, and BaVOF4 was obtained only as very few crystals. [SS1, TS1] Insufficient evidence from CS for hydrothermal Ba–V fluoride synthesis is treated neutral at 50, and oxygen-free BaVF6 formation by this route remains extrapolated. [Reasonable Inference]

**Route 4 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
Photochemical F₂/HF synthesis is supported for RbCrF6 at ambient temperature and 3 bar F₂, so diffusion barriers are low. [CS4] The severity is still substantial because anhydrous HF, F₂, UV irradiation, and long 10–20 day reactions are required. [CS4] Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for photochemical synthesis is treated neutral at 50, and transfer from alkali RbCrF6 to alkaline-earth BaVF6 is indirect. [CS4, Target, Reasonable Inference]

**Route 5 — F2/F3 Rationale**  
Melt/flux growth is supported by BaCrF5 single-crystal growth after heating to 1150 °C and slow cooling, with CrF2 used to depress melting. [CS2, SS2, TS2] The route scores poorly on severity because high temperature, sealed ampoules, and volatile V fluoride control are likely problematic. [CS2, SS2, TS2, TS4, Reasonable Inference] SS4 reports melt-grown BaCoF4 but lacks synthesis conditions, so SS4 is neutral 50 for condition scoring. [SS4]

**F4 — Amorphization Rationale**  
BaVF6 has a large cation-size mismatch between 12-coordinate Ba and octahedral V, which triggers C1 by [Reasonable Inference]. [Target] Mixed corner/edge/face sharing triggers C3, but this same topology exists in crystalline BaCrF6, so the amorphization risk is medium rather than prohibitive. [Target, CS1, SS5, TS3] C2 is not triggered because exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS and the described target/AMF6 analogues appear compact. [Target, CS1, SS5, TS3, Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaVF6 is most likely a DFT ground-state candidate rather than a high-energy metastable phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. [Target] The strongest synthesis support comes from BaCrF6, which has essentially the same BaF12/MF6 framework and is experimentally accessible, though under demanding F₂ autoclave conditions. [CS1, SS5, TS3] The most enabling factor is the existence of stable, crystalline Ba–transition-metal fluoride analogues with similar polyhedral topology. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS5, TS2, TS3] The most limiting factor is control of V(IV) fluoride stoichiometry under dry, sealed, fluorinating conditions, because related high-valent fluoride systems show volatility and phase-purity challenges. [CS1, CS5, SS5, TS3, Reasonable Inference] Overall, BaVF6 is plausible but not routine, with the sealed-tube fluoride route recommended as the first experimental screen. [CS2, SS2, TS2, Target, Reasonable Inference]
