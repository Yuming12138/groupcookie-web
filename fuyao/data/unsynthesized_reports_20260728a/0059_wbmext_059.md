### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the top of the provided stability scoring range, and many close CS/SS/TS analogues are also at or near the hull. [CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5] The main uncertainty is that the WBM target is theory-only and the provided validation does not identify experimental synthesis of this exact relaxed prototype. [CS3, TS5]

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Precipitation-Dehydration.**  
Severity is moderate because the route uses ≤340 °C and ambient pressure, but it is multi-step and uses toxic/corrosive H2SeO4. [CS1, CS2] Kinetics are favorable because the cations are introduced through aqueous acid-base chemistry rather than refractory solid-state diffusion, but dehydration can be phase-sensitive and hygroscopic analogues are reported. [CS1, CS2, CS5]

**Route 2 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal / Sealed Ampoule.**  
Severity is higher than Route 1 because sealed hydrothermal or ampoule operation with selenic acid/selenium oxide introduces pressure and containment risk, even though temperatures remain moderate. [SS2, SS3, SS4, TS5] Kinetics and structural transferability are excellent because hydrothermal solution transport is effective and PdSeO4 is an exact C2/c square-planar MSeO4 analogue. [SS2, SS3, SS4, TS5]

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Slow Evaporation.**  
Severity is moderate because equipment is simple and pressure is ambient, but concentrated H2SeO4 and multi-step isolation/dehydration are still required. [SS1, SS5, CS5] Kinetics are good for solution crystallization, but the route is vulnerable to hydrate retention and hygroscopic anhydrous-product handling. [SS1, SS5, CS5]

**Route 4 F2/F3 — High Pressure.**  
The rubric does not penalize 4 GPa as “>5 GPa,” but practical difficulty is high because specialized high-pressure apparatus is required. [CS3] Kinetics are less favorable than solution routes because the route starts from an anhydrous powder and pressure may drive a different polymorph rather than the target C2/c structure. [CS3; Reasonable Inference]

**Route 5 F2/F3 — CVT.**  
Severity is high because Cl2 transport uses a reactive sealed atmosphere, and the precedent is only ZnSO4 CVT rather than CuSeO4 CVT. [CS4] Kinetics and composition control are uncertain because selenium oxosalts may decompose or redistribute Se/O/Cl species at transport temperatures. [CS4; Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target has one radius/coordination mismatch trigger because square-planar Cu2+ and tetrahedral Se6+ are chemically dissimilar, but the framework is small and lacks evidence for complex mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity. [TS5; Reasonable Inference] The exact PdSeO4 analogue supports a crystalline square-planar/tetrahedral topology, reducing amorphization concern. [TS5]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuSeO4 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and many close selenate analogues are also stable or nearly stable. [CS1, CS3, SS1, SS2, TS5] The synthesis evidence is strong at the composition and topology levels: CS analogues show divalent-metal selenates can be precipitated/dehydrated, and TS5 shows an exact C2/c square-planar MSeO4 analogue. [CS1, CS2, CS3, TS5] The most critical enabling factor is solution/acid-mediated Se(VI) chemistry, which avoids refractory diffusion and enables low-temperature formation. [CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3] The most critical limiting factor is controlling hydration, hygroscopicity, and selenium volatility/toxicity during conversion to the anhydrous phase. [CS5, SS1, TS1] Overall, the target is **highly synthesizable as a powder** and **moderately challenging as a phase-pure single crystal**. [CS1, CS2, CS5, TS5]
