The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Ti₂BRh₆ and ZrRh₃B analogues are also E_hull = 0.0, supporting a high thermodynamic score (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). HfRh₃B and YRh₃B are also on-hull analogues, reinforcing that Rh-rich early-transition-metal borides can be thermodynamically stable (SS3; SS5; TS3; TS5). The main uncertainty is that the target itself is theory-only, so experimental phase competition is not fully resolved by CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 loses points for arc-melting severity because complete melting of refractory Zr/Rh/B is required [Reasonable Inference], but it avoids high pressure, sealed volatile reagents, and complex atmosphere beyond Ar (CS1; SS2; TS2). This is the least severe proven analogue route because Ti₂BRh₆ was synthesized directly from elements under Ar with repeated remelting (CS1; SS2; TS2).

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Zr/Rh/B are refractory and not highly reactive [Reasonable Inference], but the liquid arc-melt route bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion barrier (CS1; SS2; TS2). Structural reconstruction is favorable because Ti₂BRh₆ has the same Fm-3m M₂BRh₆ topology, making the Ti→Zr substitution a relatively conservative structural change [Reasonable Inference] (CS1; SS2; TS2).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is more severe because Zr/Hf Rh–B analogues use vacuum sintering, arc melting, crushing/recompaction, and 1100 °C homogenization (CS3; SS1; SS3; TS1; TS3). Powder handling and vacuum processing add difficulty relative to Route 1 [Reasonable Inference], though the equipment remains standard for refractory intermetallics (CS3; SS1; SS3; TS1; TS3).

**F3 — Route 2**  
The added homogenization improves phase equilibration but signals slower ordering kinetics than direct melt crystallization (CS3; SS1; SS3; TS1; TS3). ZrRh₃B/HfRh₃B/YRh₃B provide relevant Zr/Hf/Y–Rh–B motifs, but they are Pm-3m perovskites rather than the exact Fm-3m M₂BRh₆ target, lowering the structural-transfer score (CS3; SS1; SS3; SS5; TS1; TS3; TS5).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized for arc melting, sealed/vacuum annealing, and multi-step long-duration treatment (CS2; CS4; CS5). The long annealing times in related intermetallics imply significant operational burden even though no high pressure is required (CS2; CS4; CS5).

**F3 — Route 3**  
Long anneals of 15–30 days, 600 h, or three months indicate slow ordering or crystal-growth kinetics in related Rh-rich intermetallics (CS2; CS4; CS5). The chemistry is less directly matched to Zr₂BRh₆ than Ti₂BRh₆ or ZrRh₃B, so this route is better viewed as a refinement/single-crystal route than the first synthesis attempt (CS1; CS2; CS3; CS4; CS5).

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is severe because SS4/TS4 require 950–1550 °C, ultra-pure H₂, long reaction times, and complex purification equipment (SS4; TS4). Transfer to Zr–B–Rh likely requires metallothermic adaptation, which adds air-sensitive reagents and cleanup steps [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4**  
The kinetic score is low because PaRh₃ lacks B and does not prove direct Zr₂BRh₆ framework formation (SS4; TS4). Reduction-alloying must both reduce precursors and reconstruct the ternary boride network, which is a larger kinetic barrier than melting elemental Zr/Rh/B [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 5**  
The nominal condition severity is relatively mild because sputtering can be done below bulk melting temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source reports a thin-film route, so the evidence basis is neutral and unsupported by direct analogue synthesis.

**F3 — Route 5**  
Thin-film kinetics are uncertain because no analogue confirms direct crystallization of this Fm-3m boride in a film (insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS). Composition control is likely limiting because Zr₂BRh₆ is Rh-rich and contains light B, which usually requires careful sputter calibration [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The exact Fm-3m Ti₂BRh₆ analogue reports an ordered cuboctahedral/octahedral network with corner and face sharing, which supports relatively low amorphization risk (CS1; SS2; TS2). Structurally similar perovskite ZrRh₃B/HfRh₃B/YRh₃B entries include more mixed edge/face/corner connectivity, but those are not the exact target prototype, so they are weighted less for F4 (CS3; SS1; SS3; SS5; TS1; TS3; TS5). The remaining risk is the Zr/Rh size mismatch and possible disorder during rapid melt cooling [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₂BRh₆ is likely a DFT ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest Ti₂BRh₆/ZrRh₃B analogues are also on the hull (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The synthesis evidence is strong because Ti₂BRh₆ is an exact M₂BRh₆ Fm-3m analogue made by simple elemental arc melting, and Zr-containing Rh–B phases are also arc-melt accessible (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). The main enabling factor is the proven stability and synthesis of the same Rh₆B octahedral / M-Rh cuboctahedral framework in Ti₂BRh₆ (CS1; SS2; TS2). The main limiting factor is phase competition with other stable Zr–Rh–B phases such as ZrRh₃B, which may require tight stoichiometry and repeated homogenization (CS3; SS1; TS1). Overall, the target is a strong bulk arc-melting candidate, but experimental confirmation is still uncertain because the provided target record is theory-only and no CS/SS/TS source reports synthesized Zr₂BRh₆ directly.
