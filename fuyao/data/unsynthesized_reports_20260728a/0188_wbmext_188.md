### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Na2HfSe3 is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the target record, and the closest Na2ZrSe3 analogue is also E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. (SS1, TS1) All listed CS analogues are also E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so the chemical family is not thermodynamically implausible. (CS1–CS5) Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only WBM and no experimental synthesis is identified in the supplied data. (Target record; insufficient experimental validation from CS/SS/TS for Na2HfSe3 itself)

**F2 — Route 1**  
The direct route is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Se, air-sensitive Na2Se, and multiple handling/heating steps. (SS1, TS1) It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because Na2ZrSe3 forms at 800 °C in a sealed quartz ampoule. (SS1, TS1) The main uncertainty is whether Hf reacts as readily as Zr under the same conditions. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are helped by structural identity between Na2ZrSe3 and the target Na2HfSe3 framework. (SS1, TS1) Kinetics are limited by using Hf metal powder and by possible heterogeneous products, since the Na2ZrSe3 product was reported as crystals in a metallic matrix. (SS1, TS1; Hf limitation is [Reasonable Inference]) The route is therefore plausible but may require regrinding or flux assistance. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth has the same severity deductions as the direct route because it uses sealed/vacuum or Nb ampoules, volatile selenium, air-sensitive Na–Se reagents, and multi-step loading/heating/washing. (CS1, CS2) It remains below 1000 °C in the cited NaErSe2 and NaYbSe2 cases, avoiding the highest-temperature penalties. (CS1, CS2) The uncertain factor is whether a Na–Se or NaCl flux dissolves and transports Hf efficiently. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 2**  
Flux growth improves kinetic access by providing liquid or molten transport and slow crystallization, as shown for NaYbSe2 and NaErSe2. (CS1, CS2) Its structural match is weaker than Na2ZrSe3 because CS analogues are NaLnSe2 rather than Na2MSe3. (CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1) The route receives the best score because transport advantages likely offset the stoichiometric uncertainty. ([Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3**  
The extended annealing route requires staged heating, long holds, grinding, resealing, and inert/vacuum handling. (CS3, CS4) It does not require high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C, so its severity is operational rather than extreme. (CS3, CS4) The long duration makes it less attractive than Route 1 or Route 2. (CS3, CS4)

**F3 — Route 3**  
Long annealing and repeated grinding in CS3/CS4 indicate slow ordering and diffusion for bulk Caswellsilverite-type selenides. (CS3, CS4) Hf is expected to worsen diffusion relative to some lanthanides because it is refractory. ([Reasonable Inference]) This route is useful as a fallback for phase equilibration but not the fastest discovery route. (CS3, CS4; [Reasonable Inference])

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has mixed edge- and corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, matching the Na2ZrSe3 analogue, which raises reconstruction complexity but also shows crystalline ordering is achievable. (SS1, TS1) The Na/Hf cation size mismatch is a risk for disorder or glassy intermediates. ([Reasonable Inference]) Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so that sub-check has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is treated neutrally. (CS, SS, TS)
