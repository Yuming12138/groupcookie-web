### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the top thermodynamic score bin; this is reinforced by multiple stable or near-stable analogues in SS/TS. (Target data; SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1–TS5) CS evidence is mixed because CS1 lists E_hull = 0.545 eV/atom while also reporting high-confidence synthesis and E_above_ground_state = 0.0; I treat that conflict as a phase-control warning rather than a direct penalty to the target’s hull score. (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5)

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C processing, vacuum/Ar atmosphere, and multi-step arc/remelt/anneal handling. (CS1, CS2, SS1) It avoids high pressure and avoids volatile/toxic precursors if Zr pieces and ZrO2 are used. (CS2, CS4; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are moderate because Zr/ZrO2 are refractory, but arc melting provides a liquid homogenization step and analogues were successfully synthesized by similar bulk metallurgy. (CS1, CS2, CS4) Structural reconstruction is not minimal, but OZr6 octahedra and Zr–M bonding motifs transfer well from Zr3O and Zr4M2O analogues. (SS1, SS3, TS1)

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 is more severe because true melt growth of a Zr-rich charge may require very high temperature and controlled Ar. (SS4, TS3; [Reasonable Inference]) Its advantage is procedural simplicity and strong structural precedent from phase-pure Cmcm Zr3AlN. (SS4, TS3)

**F3 — Route 2.** The same Cmcm topology in Zr3AlN gives the highest structural-reconstruction score among routes. (SS4, TS3) The remaining kinetic uncertainty is oxygen incorporation, because SS4/TS3 prove Al/N chemistry rather than Cu/O chemistry. (SS4, TS3; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 has substantial severity from sealed-tube oxidizer handling, vacuum conditions, multi-step prealloy/oxidation/annealing, and possible gas-pressure risk. (CS1, SS3) **Insufficient evidence from TS** for perchlorate-assisted oxygen insertion; TS route-specific signal is treated as neutral **50**. (TS1–TS5)

**F3 — Route 3.** Prealloying improves cation homogeneity and NaClO4 provides reactive oxygen, which supports a good precursor-activity score. (CS1, SS3) The uncertainty is whether oxygen insertion into a Zr–Cu alloy will form the Cmcm target rather than ZrO2 plus Zr–Cu intermetallics. (CS1, CS4; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 4.** Flux growth uses moderate-high temperature, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and decant/removal steps, giving moderate condition severity. (SS5, TS4) **Insufficient evidence from CS** for flux growth of Zr–Cu–O; CS route signal is neutral **50**. (CS1–CS5)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr3CuO is likely a **thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase** because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close SS/TS analogues are also on or near the hull. (Target data; SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1–TS5) The strongest synthesis support comes from Zr-rich intermetallic oxides made by arc melting/annealing and from the same-structure Cmcm Zr3AlN melt-growth analogue. (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4, TS3) The most important enabling factor is that oxygen sits in Zr6 octahedral environments that recur across the analogues. (CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3) The main limiting factor is phase control: close analogues report residual oxides/intermetallic impurities and difficult purity windows. (CS1, CS2, CS4) Overall, the material is **plausibly synthesizable**, with bulk arc melting plus controlled annealing as the best first attempt. (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4)
