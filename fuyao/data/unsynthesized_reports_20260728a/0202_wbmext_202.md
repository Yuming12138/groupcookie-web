### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s reported E_hull = 0 places it in the highest stability category, and TS analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 fluorides such as CrCdF6, CrHgF6, CrF5, MgCrF6, and KCrF6. [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5] Chemically similar MnCdF5 phases are nearly on the hull at ~0.004–0.005 eV/atom, supporting the stability of the Mn–Cd–F chemical space even though they are not the same stoichiometry. [CS1, CS2] Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and no CS/SS/TS entry reports experimental MnCdF6 itself. [CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is less severe than F2/HF methods because Mn–Cd fluoride analogues use 640 °C dry-Ar thermal treatment and FeNbF6 uses 500 °C sealed copper tubes under Ar, with no high gas pressure or GPa pressure. [CS1, CS2, CS5] The score is reduced because dry/inert fluoride handling, sealed or noble-metal containment, long dwell time, and toxic Cd are required. [CS1, CS2, CS5] Insufficient evidence from SS for Mn–Cd sealed-tube synthesis specifically, so SS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity at 50. [SS1–SS5]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Binary fluorides provide moderate precursor activity because MnCdF5 and FeNbF6 are formed from fluoride precursors without oxide ceramic temperatures. [CS1, CS2, CS5] Kinetic risk remains because the target R-3 CdF6/MnF6 network differs from the MnCdF5 CdF7 mixed-connectivity analogue, implying partial reconstruction and possible competing MnCdF5 formation. [CS1, CS2] Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Mn–Cd sealed fluoride kinetics, so TS is neutral for Route 1 kinetics at 50. [TS1–TS5]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has low thermal severity because the closest R-3 Cd analogue CrCdF6 is made at up to 300 °C, but it is penalized for F2/Ar, high gas pressure up to 300 atm, Monell autoclaves, and toxic/reactive precursor handling. [SS1, TS1] This route is challenging rather than prohibitive because the scoring rubric penalizes >5 GPa pressure, and the analogue uses high gas pressure rather than multi-GPa pressure. [SS1, TS1] CS supports the same CrCdF6 route as chemically similar rank 3, so CS provides useful support for the condition severity. [CS3]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because the structurally closest analogue CrCdF6 has the same R-3 Cd-containing corner-sharing octahedral network, similar Cd–F and transition-metal–F distances, and similar 150° F bridge geometry. [CS3, SS1, TS1] F2/Ar fluorination likely provides high anion activity and enables low-temperature formation over 2–3 d, which reduces diffusion burden compared with ceramic routes. [SS1, TS1, TS4] Remaining uncertainty is Mn oxidation-state compatibility, because the closest demonstrated analogue uses Cr rather than Mn. [CS3, SS1, TS1]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized for F2 atmosphere, Monel autoclave operation, passivation, dry-box handling, and volatile removal steps shown in CrF5 synthesis. [SS5, TS3] It avoids >1000 °C and avoids >5 GPa pressure, so thermal and mechanical severity are moderate relative to high-temperature/high-pressure routes. [SS5, TS3] Insufficient evidence from CS for direct F2 autoclave synthesis of Mn–Cd fluorides, so CS is neutral for Route 3 condition severity at 50. [CS1–CS5]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Direct fluorination has high precursor activity because elemental F2 converts chromium powder to CrF5 at 260–300 °C. [SS5, TS3] Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor because CrF4/CrF5 analogues are one-dimensional Cr fluoride frameworks, not the target 3D Mn/Cd R-3 network. [SS4, SS5, TS3] Remaining uncertainty is whether Mn and Cd cations order into the target corner-sharing framework rather than forming binary fluorides or MnCdF5-like phases. [CS1, CS2, SS5]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target-like R-3 CrCdF6 analogue has simple corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk relative to MnCdF5 analogues with corner/edge sharing and CdF7 polyhedra. [CS1, CS2, CS3, SS1, TS1] One risk trigger is retained because the target has substantially different Mn–F and Cd–F bond lengths, and the closest Cd analogues also show distinct transition-metal–F and Cd–F distances. [CS3, SS1, TS1] Exact target unit-cell atom count is not established by CS/SS/TS, so C2 is treated conservatively as not triggered but uncertain. [CS3, SS1, TS1]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCdF6 is most likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0 and multiple thermodynamic analogues in the fluoride family also sit on the hull. [TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5] The strongest support comes from CrCdF6, which matches the R-3 space group, CdF6/transition-metal-F6 corner-sharing topology, similar Cd–F distances, and similar bent fluoride bridges. [CS3, SS1, TS1] The most critical enabling factor is access to sufficiently high fluorine chemical potential, because the closest R-3 analogues are made by F2/Ar fluorination in Monell autoclaves. [SS1, TS1, TS4] The most critical limiting factor is practical synthesis severity and phase control, because F2/HF/high-pressure fluoride routes require specialized equipment and analogues report volatility/decomposition and narrow phase windows. [SS1, SS3, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4] A lower-risk sealed-fluoride route is chemically plausible from MnCdF5 precedents, but it may favor MnCdF5 or binary fluorides instead of the higher-fluorine MnCdF6 phase. [CS1, CS2]
