### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeMoN₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable nitrides. (Target data; CS1; CS2; CS3; CS5; SS1; SS3; SS4; TS1; TS2; TS3) The strongest synthesis support comes from Ce₂CrN₃ and Ce₂MnN₃, which show that Ce–transition-metal nitrides can be made by Li₃N flux or N₂ sintering. (CS3; CS5; SS1; SS4; TS1; TS3) The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high nitrogen chemical potential while using a flux or reactive nitride precursor to overcome Mo diffusion limits. [Reasonable Inference] (CS3; CS2; TS5) The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because related flux and solid-state nitrides show competing phases, repeated annealing, or nonstoichiometry. (CS1; CS5; SS1; TS1; TS5) Overall, the target is experimentally plausible but should be approached as a careful phase-screening problem rather than a guaranteed one-shot synthesis. [Reasonable Inference] (CS1; CS3; CS5; TS1; TS5)
