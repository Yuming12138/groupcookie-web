### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is on the calculated hull at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and every listed chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar analogue also has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [Target, CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. This strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility, especially because the TS set includes high-similarity YIrGa / REIrGa and REIrIn₂-type compounds [TS1–TS5]. The main uncertainty is that the target is still theory-only with no reported experimental synthesis in the provided validation [Target].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc melting requires very high local temperatures, inert Ar, and repeated melting for homogeneity [CS1–CS4]. No high pressure is required, and the atmosphere is standard Ar rather than reactive H₂/NH₃/vacuum [CS1–CS4]. The severity is moderate because the process is equipment-intensive but well established for these intermetallic analogues [CS1–CS4].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Elemental Y/Ir/In precursors are straightforward, but Ir is refractory and elemental precursors are kinetically less active than molecular precursors [CS5, Reasonable Inference]. Arc melting improves mixing by forming a molten alloy, and close analogues including YPtIn and YAuSn are made this way [CS1, CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is whether YIrIn forms directly or requires annealing to order into the P-62m phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
The sealed Ta route uses 1420–1470 K reaction temperatures and annealing under Ar, which triggers the >1000 °C deduction but not the >1400 °C deduction in °C units [CS5, SS3–SS5]. Sealed containment adds complexity because In-containing REIrIn reactions are performed in Ta tubes [CS5]. No high pressure is reported, keeping the severity moderate [CS5, SS3–SS5].

**F3 — Route 2**  
This is the best kinetic match because REIrIn analogues are synthesized directly from rare-earth metal, Ir powder, and In in sealed Ta tubes [CS5]. The same P-62m CeIrIn structure strongly lowers reconstruction burden for YIrIn [CS5]. The uncertainty is phase competition with REIrIn₂-type compositions, which are also accessible in the same chemical family [SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5].

**F2 — Route 3**  
The In-flux route likely uses high temperature, sealed containment, and multiple steps including slow cooling and decanting [CS1, Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and reactive gas, consistent with related Ar-based indide syntheses [CS1, CS5]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct flux evidence for this route and are neutral 50 for route-specific condition support.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Liquid In should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction [CS1, Reasonable Inference]. However, excess In creates a compositional bias toward In-rich REIrIn₂-type phases, which are well represented among structural and thermodynamic analogues [SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5]. This makes the route attractive for crystal growth but less reliable for first-pass phase-pure bulk YIrIn [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target is a small-cell intermetallic with close crystalline analogues, including P-62m YPtIn and CeIrIn, which argues against high amorphization tendency [CS1, CS5]. A metal-size mismatch trigger is retained as a low–medium risk [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data and are neutral 50 for direct amorphization evidence. The final F4 score is therefore favorable but not maximum.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YIrIn is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable [Target, TS1–TS5]. The analogue support is strong: YPtIn and CeIrIn reproduce the same P-62m rare-earth–transition-metal–indide framework, while YIrGa/YIrSn-type materials support Y–Ir–p-block chemistry [CS1, CS5, SS1, TS1, CS2]. The key enabling factor is the existence of experimentally synthesized REIrIn / YPtIn analogues using simple elemental precursors under Ar [CS1, CS5]. The main limiting factor is kinetic and phase control around refractory Ir and possible competition with REIrIn₂-type indides [SS3–SS5, TS3–TS5, Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears **reasonably likely to be synthesizable**, with sealed Ta-tube induction melting as the most defensible first route [CS5].
