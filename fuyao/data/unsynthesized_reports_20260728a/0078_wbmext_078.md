### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** Ti₂SnN has target E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic score band, and the closest Ti₂GaN/Ti₂InN/Ti₂AlN H-phase analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is explicitly theory-only, so the score is slightly below perfect despite strong thermodynamic and analogue support [target statement; CS/SS/TS analogues].

**Route 1 F2/F3:** Route 1 has mild temperature severity because close analogues form at 500–900 °C in quartz ampoules without high pressure or explicitly reactive atmosphere [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2]. Kinetics are only moderate because TiN/Ti diffusion is slow and reported holds are 500–900 h, but the exact H-phase structural match strongly reduces reconstruction risk [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2].

**Route 2 F2/F3:** Route 2 is penalized for higher temperature, vacuum/Ar processing, and multiple steps because Ti₂AlN analogues used master alloys, pulverization, cold pressing, and sintering under vacuum/Ar or in sealed tubes [CS5, SS3, TS3]. Kinetically, pre-alloying should improve cation mixing [Reasonable Inference], but high-temperature equilibrium and long annealing still imply moderate ordering difficulty [CS5, SS3, TS3].

**Route 3 F2/F3:** Route 3 is more severe because arc melting requires controlled Ar atmosphere, very high transient temperature, and post-annealing [CS4, CS5, SS3, TS3]. Kinetic mixing is improved by melting/pre-alloying [Reasonable Inference], but direct evidence for arc-melt synthesis of H-phase Ti₂SnN-like nitrides is insufficient from CS4 and weaker than the sealed-ampoule H-phase analogues [CS4 vs. CS1/CS2/SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2].

**Route 4 F2/F3:** Route 4 is exploratory because TS5 supports sealed-tube transport only for Ti₂P, while CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for nitride flux/CVT synthesis and are neutral 50 for this route [TS5; insufficient evidence from CS/SS]. The flux concept may help transport Sn [Reasonable Inference], but TiN incorporation and phase purity remain uncertain, especially because TS5 reports impurity and crystal-quality challenges [TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** The target is a small, periodic H-phase cell rather than a large complex unit cell, and the closest H-phase analogues are crystalline, experimentally synthesized phases [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. Direct ionic-radius evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral for C1, but analogous Al/Ga/In A-site tolerance and matching Ti–Sn/Ti–In bond length argue against high amorphization risk [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3; Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂SnN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and its closest H-phase analogues are also reported as hull-stable [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The analogue support is strong: Ti₂InN and Ti₂GaN match the target H-phase P6₃/mmc topology, Ti coordination, and Ti–N/Ti–A bond ranges very closely [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2]. The most important enabling factor is the existence of multiple experimentally synthesized Ti₂A N H-phase analogues using simple sealed-ampoule solid-state routes [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The main limiting factor is kinetics and phase purity, because analogue syntheses require hundreds of hours and some related systems show residual mononitride or narrow phase-stability windows [CS1, CS3, CS5, SS3, TS3]. A secondary limiting factor is Sn retention at long annealing times, which is a [Reasonable Inference] based on Sn volatility/melting behavior rather than direct CS/SS/TS reporting.
