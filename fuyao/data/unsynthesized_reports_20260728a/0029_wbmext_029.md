### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted on the hull, and the closest K3TaO8 analogue is also near-hull at 0.022 eV/atom, which strongly supports the high F1 score (SS1, TS2). Chemically similar titanates/oxometallates are mostly low-hull, including Rb2Ti6O13 and Na2Ti6O13 at E_hull = 0.0, which supports chemical-space stability (CS1, CS4). TS also contains several E_hull = 0 oxygen-rich analogues, supporting thermodynamic plausibility (TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic because the target is theory-only in the provided target data.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 receives a high F2 score because the closest analogue K3TaO8 crystallizes at 0 °C from KOH/H2O2 with no high pressure or furnace step (SS1, TS2). The only deduction is for practical multi-step handling, because fresh hydrous Ti precursor preparation, crystallization, isolation, and drying are likely needed [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS2]. CS gives insufficient direct low-temperature K3TiO8 peroxide evidence and is neutral 50 for this exact route basis.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization avoids solid-state diffusion and because the K3TaO8 analogue already has the same peroxide framework (SS1, TS2). The score is reduced because Ti-for-Ta substitution changes the accessible redox chemistry, and no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates an experimental K3TiO8 phase [Reasonable Inference]. K2FeO4 low-temperature crystallization supports handling of high-valent K oxometallates but also shows instability concerns (CS3, TS4).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 avoids high temperatures and high pressure by adapting K–Ti hydrothermal chemistry from CS5 to a lower-temperature peroxide-compatible regime [Reasonable Inference from CS5 and SS1/TS2]. It receives only a multi-step deduction because autoclave hydrothermal pressure is not >5 GPa and no controlled H2/NH3/vacuum atmosphere is required by the analogue data (CS5). SS/TS provide insufficient direct hydrothermal K–Ti evidence and are neutral 50 for hydrothermal transfer, although they support the peroxide motif (SS1, TS2).

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are moderate because concentrated KOH can activate Ti and form K–Ti oxides hydrothermally (CS5). The score is lower than Route 1 because CS5 forms a different potassium titanate framework, so structural reconstruction toward K3TiO8 peroxide remains uncertain (CS5, SS1, TS2). Competing K titanate formation is a realistic concern [Reasonable Inference from CS5].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized for sealed dry oxidizing conditions, reactive peroxide/superoxide precursors, and multi-step sealed-tube handling, consistent with demanding sealed oxometallate syntheses (TS3, TS5). It avoids the high-temperature deductions because the proposed peroxide-preserving version is kept below 300 °C [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS2]. SS gives insufficient evidence for sealed flux synthesis of this prototype and is neutral 50 for that route.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Kinetics are the weakest because sealed oxometallate analogues require very long dwell times and report phase-selection challenges (TS3, TS5). TiO2 is a less reactive solid precursor than solution Ti species, so structural reconstruction into K3TiO8 is more difficult [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS4 and TS3/TS5]. This route is mainly exploratory rather than preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target-like K3TaO8 structure is relatively ordered and not a large-complexity framework, supporting no C2 trigger by analogy (SS1, TS2). The corner/edge-sharing peroxide framework lacks the vertex+edge+face coexistence needed for C3, based on the K3TaO8 structural description (SS1, TS2). The only trigger is large K/Ti cation size mismatch, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because CS/SS/TS do not report ionic radii numerically.

**Conflict Handling**  
CS ceramic titanate analogues show high-temperature oxide routes, while SS/TS show the direct peroxide K3TaO8 analogue forms by mild KOH/H2O2 crystallization (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, TS2). I weight SS1/TS2 more heavily because the target contains O–O peroxide bonds and the same K3MO8 structural prototype, whereas high-temperature CS ceramic routes would likely favor non-peroxide titanates [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS2 and CS1/CS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K3TiO8 is likely a computed ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several thermodynamic analogues are also low-hull or on-hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support is the isostructural K3TaO8 peroxide analogue, which crystallizes directly from KOH/H2O2 at 0 °C (SS1, TS2). Chemical support for K–Ti oxide formation exists from hydrothermal potassium titanate synthesis, but that source does not prove the exact peroxide prototype (CS5). The most important enabling factor is low-temperature alkaline peroxide crystallization, which can preserve O–O units and avoid refractory solid-state diffusion (SS1, TS2). The most important limiting factor is the unusual Ti–peroxide redox requirement and lack of direct experimental K3TiO8 precedent in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS2 and CS3/TS4 instability evidence].
