### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom, and the closest CS/SS/TS analogues include several E_hull = 0.0 Pnma RENiSn phases, so the stability score is near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5) Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only, and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct experimental GdNiSn validation; direct-target synthesis evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient and neutral at 50.

**F2 — Route 1.** The HP route is heavily penalized because close analogues require 7.5–11.5 GPa, ~1100–1200 °C, precursor preparation, and controlled processing. (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS4) This severity is partly justified by the strong structural transferability to GdNiSn, but synthesis accessibility is low because specialized multianvil equipment and narrow phase windows are reported. (CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1)

**F2 — Route 2.** The direct melt/anneal route avoids GPa pressure, but arc/induction melting, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, sealing, and long anneals still produce substantial deductions. (CS2, CS3, TS5) SS gives insufficient direct evidence for this route and is neutral at 50, because the structurally similar set mainly supports HP or flux routes rather than direct Gd-like melt growth. (SS1, SS2, SS3)

**F2 — Route 3.** The Sn-flux route earns the best F2 because SS2 uses only ~970 K and ambient pressure, but deductions remain for Ar/vacuum sealing, multistep processing, Sn-flux removal, and air-sensitive Gd handling by **[Reasonable Inference]**. (SS2) CS and TS give insufficient direct flux evidence and are neutral at 50 for Route 3.

**F3 — Route 1.** HP conversion benefits from strong structural continuity among Pnma Tb/Dy/Nd analogues, but dense pre-alloyed intermetallic precursors and no explicit flux transport limit kinetic ease. (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS4) Phase-control difficulty is supported by reports of metastable HP phases, narrow windows, and impurities. (CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1)

**F3 — Route 2.** Direct melting improves mixing and diffusion relative to ordinary solid-state reaction, which supports a moderate F3 despite dense elemental precursors. (CS2, CS3, TS5) The main kinetic uncertainty is whether GdNiSn crystallizes into the desired Pnma phase rather than a competing normal-pressure polymorph or secondary phase. (CS1, CS3, CS4, TS5)

**F3 — Route 3.** Sn flux provides the strongest kinetic benefit because liquid-mediated transport at ~970 K can promote crystallization below the melting points of Gd/Ni, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** based on SS2. (SS2) Structural reconstruction risk is moderate because ScNiSn matches Pnma topology, but Sc→Gd transfer is chemically less direct than Tb/Dy/Nd→Gd transfer. (SS2; CS1, CS4, SS3, TS1, TS2)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The Pnma RENiSn analogues have compact 12-atom conventional cells, so the >40 atom trigger is not met. (CS1, CS3, CS4, SS1, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS5) A size-mismatch trigger is assigned by **[Reasonable Inference]** from RE–Ni versus Ni–Sn bonding differences, while mixed polyhedral connectivity has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is neutral at 50. (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2)
