### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin by the provided rubric (target data). CS supports this because BiVO4/BiNbO4/InTaO4 analogues are mostly on or near the hull, with E_hull values from 0.0 to 0.0194 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). TS strongly supports stability because TlPO4 and several thermodynamic analogues are at or very near 0 eV/atom (TS1–TS4). SS is positive but less clean because some structurally similar phases are synthesized despite E_hull values up to ~0.14 eV/atom, so SS stability evidence is useful but mixed (SS1–SS5).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature because the key analogues use 280 °C and 350–400 °C, with autogenous rather than extreme pressure (SS3, TS2). The only major deduction is toxic Tl handling from the target safety note. Insufficient evidence from CS for a hydrothermal route is treated as neutral = 50, but SS3 and TS2 provide strong route-specific support.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Hydrothermal synthesis gives high precursor activity and avoids slow bulk diffusion, as shown by solution-based hydrothermal oxometallate syntheses (SS1–SS3, TS2). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target combines the TlO6–XO4 Cmcm motif of TlPO4 and the VO4–MO6 Cmcm motif of FeVO4 (TS2, SS3). Remaining uncertainty is Tl(III) redox and pH stability in vanadate solution [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth is not extremely high temperature in the preferred 750–900 °C window, based on CS3, SS4, SS5, and TS1. Deductions arise from volatile-component control, multiple flux-growth operations, and Tl toxicity; V/Mo oxide evaporation in SS4 is the strongest direct warning signal. CS/SS/TS all provide useful flux-route evidence, although none is direct TlVO4 flux synthesis.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Flux improves kinetics through liquid-mediated transport, supported by successful flux growth of BiNbO4, FeVMoO7, and KFe(MoO4)2 (CS3, SS4, SS5, TS1). The structural match is moderate rather than high because exact TlVO4 topology is supported by TS2/SS3, but those exact analogues are not flux-grown. Composition control is the main kinetic uncertainty due to Tl and V oxide volatility [Reasonable Inference; SS4].

**F2 — Route 3**  
The adapted solid-state route avoids >1000 °C by using Tl/V oxide reactivity and sealed conditions [Reasonable Inference], even though CS4 shows that some ceramic analogues require 1150 °C. Deductions come from volatile sealed-system needs, repeated processing, and Tl toxicity (CS4, TS3; target safety note). Insufficient solid-state evidence from SS is neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Solid-state kinetics are slower because binary oxides must reconstruct into the Cmcm oxovanadate framework [Reasonable Inference; TS2, SS3]. TS3 and CS4 show bulk oxide/salt routes are feasible for related oxometallates, but they do not eliminate diffusion and regrinding risk. V2O5 may help local transport, but that is an inference rather than direct CS/SS/TS proof [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
CS1–CS2 support melt growth for BiVO4, but they do not give a TlVO4 liquidus or demonstrate Tl-containing melt growth. The route receives deductions for likely high melt temperature, volatility containment, feed preparation, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference; CS1–CS2; target safety note]. Insufficient melt-growth evidence from SS and TS is neutral = 50 for both sources.

**F3 — Route 4**  
If TlVO4 melts congruently, melt growth would have excellent ion mobility, as inferred from BiVO4 Czochralski growth (CS1, CS2). The weak point is structural/crystallization certainty because no direct TlVO4, TlPO4, or FeVO4 melt-growth analogue is provided (insufficient evidence from SS/TS). This route is therefore better as a crystal-growth follow-up after Route 1 establishes phase existence.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is counted: large cation-size/coordination contrast between TlO6 and VO4 units [Reasonable Inference from target structure and TS2]. The cell/topology is not highly complex because the target has a simple Cmcm ABO4-type framework analogous to TlPO4 and FeVO4 (TS2, SS3). Edge and corner sharing coexist, but the required vertex + edge + face-sharing coexistence is not present in the target description, TS2, or SS3.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlVO4 is likely a computational ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although it remains theory-only with no experimental synthesis identified in the source dataset (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from the exact Tl-containing Cmcm TlPO4 analogue and the exact VO4-based Cmcm FeVO4 analogue, both of which were synthesized hydrothermally (TS2, SS3). Chemical analogues such as BiVO4 support stable AV O4 chemistry but are less structurally transferable because BiVO4 uses Bi coordination environments different from TlO6 in the target (CS1, CS2; TS2). The key enabling factor is the simple, low-energy Cmcm tetrahedra–octahedra framework shared with known compounds (TS2, SS3). The key limiting factor is practical handling of toxic/volatile Tl chemistry and maintaining Tl(III)/V(V) stoichiometry during synthesis [Reasonable Inference; target safety note, SS4].
