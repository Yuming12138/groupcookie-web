### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is supplied with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 thermodynamic score band. (target data) Thermodynamic analogues in CS, SS, and TS are also reported at E_hull = 0.0, so the similarity evidence is consistent with stable rather than strongly metastable chemistry. (CS, SS, TS) Remaining uncertainty is that the WBM candidate is theory-only, so experimental entropy, iodine fugacity, and competing finite-temperature phases are not fully captured. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 1 Hydrothermal Iodide Growth**  
This route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but it requires sealed hydrothermal iodine chemistry and hazardous iodide/acid handling. (SS, TS) The main severity penalty comes from volatile iodine in a sealed system and the long, controlled equilibration reported for Pt iodide analogues. (SS, TS) Insufficient evidence from CS for exact PtTeI hydrothermal temperature/time, so CS contributes route plausibility but not a precise severity score. (CS)

**F3 — Route 1 Hydrothermal Iodide Growth**  
PtI₃/PtI₂ analogues demonstrate that Pt–I frameworks can crystallize hydrothermally at 290–420 °C, which supports favorable kinetic transport compared with refractory solid-state Pt diffusion. (SS, TS) Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target mixes Pt–Te and Pt–I bonds in PtTe₄I₂ octahedra rather than forming a pure PtIₓ network. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Remaining uncertainty is whether Te incorporation competes with Pt iodide phase formation under iodine-rich conditions. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2 Sealed-Tube Solid-State / Transport**  
The sealed-tube route uses standard vacuum/Ar ampoule synthesis and moderate temperatures based on Pd–Te–Br and Pt–Te analogues. (CS, SS, TS) Severity is reduced by absence of external high pressure, but penalties apply for vacuum/Ar control, iodine volatility, likely multistep annealing, and hazardous iodine/tellurium handling. (CS, SS, TS) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an experimentally reported Pt–Te–I sealed-tube recipe, so the route remains extrapolated. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 2 Sealed-Tube Solid-State / Transport**  
Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because Pt is dense/refractory, but halide transport and preformed Pt iodides may enhance reaction activity. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) CrAuTe₄ and Pt₂TlTe₃ demonstrate that Pt/late-metal tellurides can form in sealed high-temperature reactions, but repeated anneals and slow cooling indicate nontrivial ordering kinetics. (SS, TS) The uncertainty is whether iodine remains in the target stoichiometry rather than forming separate PtI₂/PtI₃ phases. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3 Hydroiodic-Acid Hydrothermal from Elements**  
This route has relatively mild nominal temperature and one-pot execution because AuTeI is reported from elements in hydroiodic acid. (CS) Penalties remain for sealed iodine/acid handling and toxicity/corrosivity. (CS; [Reasonable Inference]) Insufficient evidence from CS for exact AuTeI temperature/time, and insufficient evidence from SS/TS that elemental Pt reacts readily under identical HI conditions. (CS, SS, TS)

**F3 — Route 3 Hydroiodic-Acid Hydrothermal from Elements**  
CS gives direct evidence that a chemically similar tellurium iodide can form hydrothermally from elements in HI. (CS) SS/TS add that Pt iodide hydrothermal systems exist but have narrow stability regions, so kinetics are plausible but phase-selectivity is uncertain. (SS, TS) The main uncertainty is whether Pt dissolves/iodinates sufficiently to form PtTeI rather than remaining Pt metal or forming PtIₓ. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 4 Melt / Bridgman-Type Crystal Growth**  
Melt/Bridgman processing is supported by InTeBr and high-temperature telluride analogues, but iodine volatility makes the route substantially harsher for PtTeI. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Vacuum/Ar sealing, internal iodine pressure, and likely pre-reaction/slow-cooling steps drive the severity penalties. (CS, SS, TS) However, target PtTeI likely has competing iodine-loss and PtIₓ formation risks at elevated temperature. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Structural reconstruction from a melt is less controlled than hydrothermal growth because no direct Pt–Te–I melt analogue is supplied. (CS, SS, TS)
