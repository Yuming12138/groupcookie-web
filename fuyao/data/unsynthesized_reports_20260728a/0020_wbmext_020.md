### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
SrPtO3 receives a high thermodynamic score because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the formation energy is strongly negative [Target]. Stable or near-stable analogues include CaPtO3 [CS1/SS1/TS3], CaRhO3 [SS3/TS2], and Ca2Pt3O8 [SS2/TS1], so CS/SS/TS all provide useful thermodynamic support. The main uncertainty is that the target is a WBM theory-only candidate with no experimental synthesis in the supplied dataset [Target].

**F2 — Route 1**  
The closest Pt analogue CaPtO3 requires high-pressure equipment but only 800 °C and 30 min, so the rubric penalizes multi-step handling and hazardous oxidizer/air-sensitive precursor use rather than temperature [CS1/SS1/TS3; Reasonable Inference]. The 4 GPa primary pressure does not trigger the >5 GPa deduction, although CaRhO3 shows that 6 GPa may be needed for related post-perovskite oxides [SS3–4/TS2]. Volatile precursor evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS; neutral source score = 50.

**F2 — Route 2**  
The ambient ceramic route has moderate conditions because BaPbO3 analogues form at 800–950 °C in air/O2 without high pressure [CS4–5]. It is penalized for repeated firings/grinding and for PtO2/SrO handling risks [CS4–5; CS1/SS1/TS3; Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for ambient-pressure SrPtO3 formation; neutral source score = 50 for each.

**F2 — Route 3**  
The hydrothermal route avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa because Ca2Pt3O8 forms at 800 K and 170 MPa [SS2/TS1]. It is still penalized for precursor synthesis, sealed-tube hydrothermal handling, washing, and Pt chloride/KOH hazards [SS2/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient hydrothermal-route evidence; neutral source score = 50.

**F2 — Route 4**  
The high-pO2 route avoids multi-GPa pressure but uses controlled oxygen pressure up to 3500 atm and specialized cold-seal equipment [TS4]. It is penalized for controlled reactive atmosphere, multi-step pressure-bomb handling, and oxidizing/toxic precursor risks [TS4; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient high-pO2 cold-seal evidence; neutral source score = 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to other bulk routes because the strongest analogue CaPtO3 forms in the same Cmcm PtO6 framework after only 30 min at high pressure [CS1/SS1/TS3]. The score is not higher because SrO/PtO2 are still bulk oxide precursors and Pt diffusion is expected to be sluggish [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is the strongest kinetic advantage because the target and CaPtO3 share the same key topology [Target; CS1/SS1/TS3].

The structural score is low because Ca2Pt3O8 is not SrPtO3 and the reported product has low crystallinity/Pt impurities [SS2/TS1]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPtO3 is best interpreted as a DFT ground-state candidate because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, although it remains experimentally unverified in the source dataset [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CaPtO3, which is chemically and structurally very close and was synthesized by high-pressure oxidizing solid-state quenching [CS1/SS1/TS3]. Hydrothermal and high-pO2 routes support Pt4+ oxide chemistry but have weaker topology transferability to SrPtO3 [SS2/TS1; TS4]. The key enabling factor is maintaining Pt in an oxidized octahedral network under pressure/oxygen-buffered conditions [CS1/SS1/TS3]. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: related post-perovskite oxides often require specialized high-pressure equipment, quenching, and oxidizer control [CS1/SS1/TS3; SS3–4/TS2].
