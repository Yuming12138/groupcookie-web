### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull is supplied as 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. CS/SS/TS support this directionally because TbAg, Nd₂Ag₄, Pr₂Ag₄, DyAg₂, and other rare-earth–Ag analogues are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for experimental LuAg itself, so the direct synthesis validation component remains uncertain and neutral.

**F2 — Route 1, Arc Melting + Annealing**  
CS/SS/TS show rare-earth–Ag alloys synthesized under purified/high-purity Ar using arc/electric-arc melting, with annealing under vacuum or controlled atmospheres. The main severity deductions are controlled atmosphere, specialized arc melting, air-sensitive rare-earth handling, and multistep annealing/remelting. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that LuAg needs extreme pressure or >1400 °C sustained holds, so those deductions are not applied.

**F3 — Route 1, Arc Melting + Annealing**  
Kinetics are favorable because molten alloying bypasses much of the slow solid-state diffusion, and CS/SS/TS demonstrate rare-earth–Ag intermetallics by arc melting. Structural reconstruction is favorable because TbAg in CS/SS/TS has the same Tetraauricupride/Pm-3m motif as target LuAg. The uncertainty is phase purity, because CS Eu–Ag and Tb-related examples report impurity or repeated processing in some cases.

**F2 — Route 2, Sealed-Ta / Sealed-Tube Solid-State Reaction**  
CS/TS support sealed evacuated/Ar container synthesis, including quartz capsules and Ta containers, so the route is plausible but requires controlled atmosphere and multiple heat treatments. The route avoids high pressure and probably avoids volatile-precursor penalties for Lu/Ag, but it uses air-sensitive rare-earth metal and may exceed 1000 °C. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for an exact LuAg solid-state temperature, so the temperature program is an extrapolation.

**F3 — Route 2, Sealed-Ta / Sealed-Tube Solid-State Reaction**  
Dense metal powders/turnings are kinetically slower than molten arc-melted buttons, and CS reports grinding/reheating until suitable quality for related compounds. The same Pm-3m analogue TbAg improves the structural-reconstruction score, but long diffusion lengths and surface oxide on Lu can slow ordering. Remaining uncertainty is whether LuAg forms congruently or requires a narrow annealing window; CS/SS/TS do not directly answer this.

**F2 — Route 3, Induction Melting in Ta Crucible + Long Anneal**  
TS demonstrates induction heating of rare-earth–Ag-containing intermetallics to ~1200 °C followed by long annealing under Ar in Ta crucibles. This route gets deductions for >1000 °C, controlled atmosphere, multistep processing, and rare-earth air sensitivity. Insufficient evidence from TS that binary LuAg requires ternary-style 10–12 day anneals, so that duration is conservative.

**F3 — Route 3, Induction Melting in Ta Crucible + Long Anneal**  
Induction melting plus long annealing should improve homogeneity and ordering, and TS supports this approach for rare-earth–Ag-containing intermetallics. The target’s simple Pm-3m two-sublattice structure should be easier to order than more complex ternary TS phases. The limiting uncertainty is container compatibility and whether long high-T treatment promotes competing Lu–Ag phases; CS/SS/TS provide only analogue-level evidence.

**F2 — Route 4, Co-Sputtering / Thin-Film Annealing**  
This route has lower thermal severity but is based on [Reasonable Inference], not CS/SS/TS precedent. Controlled vacuum/Ar processing and multistep deposition/annealing are required, and Lu target oxidation is a likely handling issue. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for thin-film Lu–Ag or rare-earth–Ag deposition, so its score is less reliable despite mild temperatures.

**F3 — Route 4, Co-Sputtering / Thin-Film Annealing**  
Sputter targets for metals are plausible, giving good precursor availability by [Reasonable Inference]. Crystallization and composition control are uncertain because CS/SS/TS contain no thin-film examples and only support bulk metallurgical routes. The route may be useful for screening, but it is not the recommended primary synthesis pathway.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a simple cubic Pm-3m 1:1 structure, and the closest TbAg analogue in CS/SS/TS has the same Tetraauricupride structure and simple 8-coordinate rare-earth–Ag bonding. CS/SS/TS do not show a large-cell or mixed-polyhedral topology for the target-like phase, so amorphization tendency is low. Exact radius-difference evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated conservatively rather than used as a strong positive claim.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LuAg is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and CS/SS/TS contain multiple rare-earth–Ag analogues with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. The strongest analogue is TbAg, which has the same Tetraauricupride/Pm-3m structure and similar rare-earth–Ag bonding geometry in CS/SS/TS. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of directly comparable rare-earth–Ag intermetallic synthesis routes by arc melting and annealing in CS/SS/TS. The most critical limiting factor is phase-purity control, because CS examples report repeated grinding/reheating or impurity phases for some related rare-earth intermetallics. Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible, with bulk arc melting plus annealing as the best-supported route.
