### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
KPtO₂ is predicted on the convex hull in the provided target data, and the closest K–Pt–O analogue K₃(PtO₂)₂ is also on-hull in CS/SS/TS evidence (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). Additional alkali Pt/Pd oxide analogues, including Na₂PtO₂ and K₃Pd₂O₄, are also on-hull, so the thermodynamic family signal is strong (TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed 0 K stability does not guarantee an accessible oxygen chemical potential at synthesis temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because the closest analogues form at 500–700 °C in sealed or controlled atmospheres (SS-1, TS-1, SS-2, TS-2, TS-5). The main deductions are controlled atmosphere, sealed alkali-oxide handling, and KO₂ air sensitivity (SS-2, TS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). CS provides structural support through K₃(PtO₂)₂ but insufficient direct evidence for KO₂/Pt reagent selection, so CS is neutral = 50 for that sub-factor.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Peroxide/superoxide precursors improve precursor activity, as shown by KO₂/PdO and Na₂O₂/Pt analogues (SS-2, TS-2, TS-5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because K₃(PtO₂)₂ already contains Cmcm K–Pt–O square-planar PtO₄ units similar to the target (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). Kinetic uncertainty remains because Pt metal can be sluggish and phase purity issues are reported for related capsule syntheses (SS-5, TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperature and no high pressure, consistent with sealed alkali oxide syntheses at 350–700 °C (CS-4, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1). It is penalized for air-sensitive K₂O/KOₓ, sealed/vacuum or Ar handling, and likely precursor preparation steps (CS-4, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Its severity is higher than Route 1 because dry K₂O/PtO₂ equilibration is less chemically self-oxidizing than KO₂/Pt [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2**  
The structural match is good because K₃(PtO₂)₂ is a close K–Pt–O analogue (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because K₂O/PtO₂ is a more conventional solid-state oxide route and may require long annealing to equilibrate [Reasonable Inference from CS-4, CS-5, SS-1]. The main uncertainty is whether PtO₂ buffers the correct Pt oxidation state rather than producing K₃(PtO₂)₂ or residual Pt/PtO₂ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3**  
The KOH flux route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C because the KOH-flux analogue uses 750 °C at ambient pressure (SS-4). It is penalized for flux handling, slow cooling/washing, and likely need for controlled oxidizing conditions or KO₂/KNO₃ additive for Pt³⁺ control [Reasonable Inference from SS-4, SS-2, TS-2]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct KOH-flux evidence for KPtO₂, so both are neutral = 50 for direct flux transfer.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux growth improves precursor activity and cation transport because molten KOH dissolves and transports components, as demonstrated for K–Pd–O crystal growth (SS-4). Structural reconstruction is the weak point because SS-4 is a different K–Ln–Pd–O topology, not the target Cmcm KPtO₂ framework. The route is useful for crystal growth screening, but phase selectivity is less predictable than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Dry-O₂ synthesis uses conventional furnace temperatures and no high pressure, consistent with the 800–1000 °C CS evidence (CS-1, CS-3). It is penalized for controlled dry O₂ and likely repeated annealing because related CS evidence notes phase-purity difficulty (CS-1, CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for open dry-O₂ KPtO₂ synthesis, so SS = 50 and TS = 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
The RbAuO₂ analogue is topologically close to the target and supports square-planar noble-metal oxide formation under dry O₂ (CS-1). However, dry ceramic routes have lower precursor activity than KO₂/peroxide or flux methods, and high temperature may promote K loss [Reasonable Inference]. The CS precursor snippets are partially inconsistent with the listed Au oxide formulas, so I weight CS-1/CS-3 more for topology and oxidizing-condition signal than for exact precursor recipes.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing connectivity, which triggers C3 and is mirrored by similar mixed-connectivity descriptions in RbAuO₂ and K₃(PtO₂)₂ analogues (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The large K/Pt cation-size mismatch triggers C1 by standard ionic-size reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. Exact target unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so C2 uses neutral source handling and is not triggered based on the small-formula Cmcm analogue pattern [Reasonable Inference].
