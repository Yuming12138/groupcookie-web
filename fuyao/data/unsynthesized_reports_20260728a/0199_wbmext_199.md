# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied physical energy above hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category. **[Target/WBM validation]** The CS analogues CaTbF6, SmZrF7, and PrZr2F11 are all within approximately 0.05 eV/atom of the hull, while SS/TS ScF3 analogues are essentially on the hull. **[CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, SS-1–SS-4, TS-1–TS-4]** The remaining uncertainty is that the target value is a computational prediction and the supplied WBM record explicitly identifies the material as theory-only rather than experimentally synthesized. **[Target/WBM validation]**

## F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, and its direct-mixing procedure is simple according to the Zr-fluoride precedents. **[CS-1, CS-4, SS-5]** The deductions arise from the likely need to contain and protect anhydrous HfF4 during heating, although target-specific volatility and air-sensitivity data are not supplied. **[Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]** The route is therefore moderately severe rather than intrinsically difficult.

## F3 — Route 1

Binary fluoride powders have lower precursor activity than solution-derived salts, and solid-state diffusion involving Hf is expected to be slow. **[Reasonable Inference; CS-1, CS-4, SS-5]** The score is partly rescued by the structural precedent: CaTbF6 uses CaF2 and a tetravalent fluoride, while ScF3-related compounds demonstrate that corner-sharing fluoride octahedral frameworks are chemically accessible. **[CS-2, SS-1–SS-4]** No source reports the exact CaHfF6 ordering pathway, so the structural-reconstruction score remains intermediate.

## F2 — Route 2

Fluorine-assisted sintering uses only moderate temperatures, but pure fluorine is a strongly reactive and hazardous atmosphere. **[CS-2, SS-1, SS-4]** CaTbF6 also requires initial heating, grinding, and annealing, which justifies the multi-step deduction. **[CS-2]** There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for whether fluorine is necessary for CaHfF6, so the route should be treated as an alternative rather than the default.

## F3 — Route 2

The fluorine atmosphere may improve fluoride retention and facilitate reaction at temperatures below those required for conventional diffusion, but no direct flux or liquid-phase mechanism is reported. **[CS-2, SS-1, SS-4; Reasonable Inference]** The use of binary fluorides still limits precursor activity, and the target must order Ca and Hf over a highly symmetric framework. **[Target structure; Reasonable Inference]** Consequently, the kinetics score is only slightly higher than for Route 1.

## F2 — Route 3

The hydrothermal/solvothermal route uses substantially lower temperatures than the solid-state routes and does not require an external reactive gas. **[CS-5, SS-2, SS-3]** It nevertheless requires an autoclave, slow cooling, and several workup steps, and the proposed Hf(IV) precursor may be moisture-reactive. **[CS-5; SS-2, SS-3; Reasonable Inference]** The exact precursor compatibility and hazard profile are not established by the supplied sources.

## F3 — Route 3

Soluble nitrate/halide precursors plus NH4F should provide high chemical activity and solution-mediated cation transport. **[SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2; Reasonable Inference]** Related ScF3 and LiCeF5 crystals demonstrate that fluoride phases can crystallize in sealed aqueous or solvothermal systems. **[CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2]** However, no source demonstrates formation of CaHfF6 specifically, and Hf hydrolysis could redirect the reaction toward HfO2, hafnium oxyfluoride, or hydrated fluoride phases. **[Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target phase]**

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple three-dimensional corner-sharing octahedral network, with no reported mixture of edge-, face-, and vertex-sharing polyhedra. **[Target structure]** The Ca/Hf size and charge mismatch may create ordering strain, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned. **[Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact ionic-radius criterion]** The unit-cell atom count is not given, so CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for C2 and it is scored neutrally rather than treated as a trigger.
