### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom places LaPbAu in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. TS gives strong support because all top thermodynamic analogues listed are E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and CS is also supportive because the closest chemically similar analogues are mostly E_hull = 0.0 with one still-low 0.043 eV/atom case [CS][TS]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than computational: the prompt identifies LaPbAu as a theory-only WBM candidate, while CS/SS/TS analogues show nearby chemistries are experimentally accessible but not the exact half-Heusler phase [CS][SS][TS].

**F2 — Route 1, Pb Self-Flux Growth**  
The route is penalized mainly for vacuum sealing, Pb-containing sealed flux, multi-step cooling/annealing/centrifuging, and toxic/air-sensitive materials [CS][SS][TS]. It avoids high pressure and does not exceed the analogue-supported 1000 °C threshold [CS][SS][TS]. The severity is moderate because these operations are standard for flux-grown intermetallics, but Pb handling and ampoule control are nontrivial [CS][SS][TS][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1, Pb Self-Flux Growth**  
Kinetics are favorable because molten Pb flux enables liquid-mediated diffusion and crystal growth during slow cooling [CS][SS][TS]. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: the analogues are La/Ce/Nd/Pr–Au–Bi or related lanthanide–Au–p-block compounds, not exact F-43m LaPbAu half-Heusler analogues [CS][SS][TS]. This justifies a good but not near-perfect F3 score [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2, Arc Melting + Annealing**  
Arc melting is supported for LaAuSn / HoAuSn-type analogues, but it likely exceeds 1000 °C transiently and requires Ar plus sealed annealing [CS][SS][TS][Reasonable Inference]. Pb volatility adds a sealed-system and stoichiometry penalty, and La/Pb hazards add precursor-severity penalties [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains plausible because arc melting plus annealing is a standard intermetallic synthesis method in the analogues [CS][SS][TS].

**F3 — Route 2, Arc Melting + Annealing**  
Initial reaction kinetics benefit from complete melting, but ordering into a specific ternary phase requires annealing at 800–850 °C for 48 h to 4 d in analogues [CS][SS][TS]. Phase control is weaker than in flux growth because related arc-melted samples showed impurity or foreign phases [CS][SS][TS]. The lack of an exact half-Heusler analogue lowers the structural-reconstruction score [CS][SS][TS].

**F2 — Route 3, Induction Melt / Bridgman-Type Growth**  
This route requires controlled Ar containment, likely above-1000 °C melt homogenization, and sealed handling to retain Pb [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for this specific melt-growth route and is treated as neutral = 50 for that route. The method is more severe than self-flux because melt containment and composition control are harder [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3, Induction Melt / Bridgman-Type Growth**  
Liquid melt growth gives reasonable diffusion kinetics, and LaAuGe analogues were made by induction melting followed by 800 °C annealing [SS][TS]. However, the LaAuGe evidence also notes heterogeneity in some samples, which lowers confidence in phase-pure growth [SS][TS]. The route is kinetically plausible but less robust than Pb self-flux [SS][TS][Reasonable Inference].
