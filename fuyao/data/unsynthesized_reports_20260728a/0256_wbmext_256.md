### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target has supplied E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic/structural analogues NbRuSb and TiPtSn are also on the hull [TS1, TS3, SS1, SS3]. Chemically similar TiRhGe is also on the hull, while TiSnSb and TiPdGe variants are near-hull to stable, supporting a stable Ti/Rh/Sb-like intermetallic chemistry [CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that the supplied target record is theory-only and not experimentally validated, so the score is high but not 100 [SS1, TS1].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed-tube route uses standard solid-state operations at 1000 °C and no high pressure, which keeps severity moderate [SS1, TS1]. The main penalties are evacuated ampoule use, Sb retention, and Sb/Ti handling hazards [SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact solid-state half-Heusler route, so CS is neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Reaction kinetics are limited by dense refractory Ti/Rh metals and solid-state diffusion [SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. The same F-43m half-Heusler structure in NbRuSb strongly reduces the structural-reconstruction penalty [SS1, TS1]. The remaining uncertainty is whether TiRhSb orders cleanly without forming Ti–Sb, Rh–Sb, or Ti–Rh binaries [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Arc melting plus vacuum annealing is experimentally common for half-Heuslers and Rh-containing intermetallics, but it imposes high-temperature, controlled-atmosphere, and Sb-retention penalties [SS3, TS3, SS4, TS4]. CS arc-melted TiPdGe analogues confirm bulk intermetallic access but also show high-temperature processing windows [CS4, CS5]. The route is credible but operationally harsher than sealed-tube sintering [SS3, TS3].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Arc melting improves homogenization and partially bypasses solid-state diffusion limits [SS3, TS3, Reasonable Inference]. TiPtSn gives strong structural support because it is the same F-43m half-Heusler type [SS3, TS3]. Long annealing times in analogues indicate ordering and phase equilibration are still nontrivial [SS3, TS3].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Induction melt growth is supported by TiRhGe, but adapting Ge chemistry to Sb introduces added volatility/containment concerns [CS1, SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. The reported 950 °C anneal is moderate, but the melting step is high-severity for Ti/Rh-containing charges [CS1, SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore less attractive than direct sealed-tube synthesis for first-pass polycrystalline screening [SS1, TS1].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt processing helps Ti/Rh homogenization, and TiRhGe proves compatible Ti–Rh–main-group bonding [CS1, SS2, TS2]. However, TiRhGe is orthorhombic Ima2 rather than half-Heusler F-43m, so structural transferability is weaker [CS1, SS2, TS2]. The main uncertainty is whether melt growth selects the half-Heusler polymorph rather than a denser TiRhX-type variant [CS1, SS2, TS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Flux growth has the mildest temperature severity because TiSnSb was made at 500 °C [CS2]. The penalties are evacuated-tube handling, multiple processing steps, and acid/flux removal hazards [CS2]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for flux growth of a TiRhSb half-Heusler, so SS = 50 and TS = 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Flux should improve transport relative to dry powders, and CS2 confirms Ti–Sb reactivity in Sn flux [CS2]. The major kinetic/phase issue is structural mismatch: the demonstrated flux product is TiSnSb, not F-43m TiRhSb [CS2]. Sn incorporation or stabilization of Ti–Sn–Sb phases is the main uncertainty [CS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The best structural analogues are simple 1:1:1 half-Heuslers with regular 10-coordinate pnictogen environments, not large complex unit cells [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. The reported connectivity in analogues involves face- and corner-sharing motifs, not the full vertex/edge/face coexistence trigger [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct amorphization assessment, so CS is neutral = 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiRhSb is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state candidate because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and the strongest TS analogues are also hull-stable [TS1, TS3]. The synthesis case is strongest structurally: NbRuSb and TiPtSn are exact F-43m half-Heusler analogues with similar coordination environments [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a directly transferable sealed-tube antimonide half-Heusler route at 1000 °C [SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity, because chemically similar TiRhGe and TiSnSb adopt non-half-Heusler structures, showing that Ti/Rh/Sb-like chemistry may access competing ternary motifs [CS1, CS2]. Overall, TiRhSb is a credible synthesis target, with the best first experiment being sealed-tube solid-state sintering followed by XRD-guided reannealing [SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference].
