### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 bracket and receives a high score [Target]. CS analogues are mostly on hull, while SS/TS analogues are also on or near hull, supporting stability in this fluoride chemistry space [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]. The uncertainty is that the WBM candidate is theory-only and no experimental TlInF₄ synthesis is reported in the supplied validation [Target].

**F2 — Route 1:** Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and avoids high pressure, which keeps severity moderate [CS1, CS5, SS3, TS3]. Deductions come from Ar/dry sealed handling, multistep pellet/anneal processing, and toxic/air-sensitive Tl-fluoride handling [Target, CS1, CS5]. Volatility is not directly proven for Route 1, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is treated as neutral 50 and not deducted in the base score.

**F2 — Route 2:** Route 2 stays below 1000 °C but uses melt/slow-cooling in sealed Pt containment [SS1, TS1]. The higher temperature makes Tl/F loss more plausible, so volatility and sealed-system deductions are applied as [Reasonable Inference] from the TlF-containing target chemistry and sealed-tube analogue practice [SS1, TS1, Target, Reasonable Inference]. CS provides **insufficient evidence from CS** for melt growth and is neutral 50 for this route-specific condition.

**F2 — Route 3:** Route 3 uses controlled vacuum/sealed-ampoule high-temperature sintering, which is more severe than Route 1 but still below 1000 °C [CS2, SS5, TS5]. Deductions reflect vacuum/sealed containment, multistep processing, and likely Tl/F stoichiometry risk [CS2, CS3, SS5, TS5, Target, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4:** Route 4 avoids >1000 °C and avoids >5 GPa, but F₂ pressure fluorination and Au/Pt-tube annealing are operationally severe [SS4, TS4]. The route receives major deductions for reactive F₂ atmosphere, multistep fluorination/anneal protocol, and toxic/reactive materials [SS4, TS4, Target]. It is not favored because no CS/SS/TS entry shows that TlInF₄ specifically needs F₂ chemistry [SS4, TS4, Target].

**F3 — Route 1:** Kinetics are favorable for a solid-state fluoride because closely related Tl fluorides form at 300 °C over 4 days and Tl-containing framework fluorides form at 550 °C over 3 days [CS1, CS5, SS3, TS3]. The structural penalty remains because CS Tl₂TiF₆/Tl₂SnF₆ are 0D, while TlInF₄ is a 3D InF₆ framework [CS1, CS5, Target]. SS/TS In-fluoride frameworks reduce that penalty but do not eliminate it [SS1, TS1].

**F3 — Route 2:** Melt growth should give better cation transport than solid-state diffusion, and RbIn₃F₁₀ is directly crystallized from a fluoride melt [SS1, TS1]. The uncertainty is whether TlInF₄ remains compositionally stable during high-temperature melt growth [Target, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3:** Higher-temperature annealing improves diffusion and is proven for several complex fluorides [CS2, SS5, TS5]. However, long anneals and repeated processing in analogues indicate slower ordering and phase-control challenges [CS3, SS5, TS5].

**F3 — Route 4:** F₂ fluorination provides high chemical activity and can access fluoride phases [SS4, TS4]. The kinetics score is limited because the route may over-fluorinate or bypass the desired Tl(I)–In(III) framework, and no direct TlInF₄ F₂ analogue is supplied [Target, SS4, TS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** One amorphization trigger is assigned because the target combines a large Tl site with smaller octahedral In–F bonding, as reflected by much longer Tl–F than In–F distances [Target, Reasonable Inference]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlInF₄ is likely a computational ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and the leave-one-out hull improvement is favorable [Target]. The analogue support is good but not perfect: CS strongly supports Tl-fluoride solid-state chemistry, while SS/TS support In–F framework formation and near-hull thermodynamics [CS1, CS5, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. The key enabling factor is the simple binary-fluoride precursor set TlF + InF₃, which maps directly onto the target stoichiometry [CS1, CS5, SS1, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is practical control of toxic, moisture-sensitive, sealed thallium fluoride chemistry without a direct experimental TlInF₄ precedent [Target, CS1, CS5]. Overall, the material is **plausibly synthesizable** by careful sealed-ampoule solid-state chemistry, but it should be treated as a moderate-risk fluoride synthesis rather than a routine ceramic reaction [CS1, CS5, SS3, TS3, Target].
