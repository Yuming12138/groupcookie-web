### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
HfCdF6 has a target E_hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin [Target data]. CS, SS, and TS all provide positive support because their analogues are on or near the hull: CS1–CS5 are ≤0.026 eV/atom, SS1–SS5 are ≤0.028 eV/atom, and TS1–TS5 are 0.0 eV/atom. The remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic, because the target is theory-only in the supplied dataset [Target data].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is moderate in condition severity because its closest Cd–Zr/Hf analogues use sealed Pt tubes at only 550–600 °C for 3 days rather than extreme temperature or high pressure (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4). The deductions come from dry controlled sealing, likely fluoride volatility/moisture sensitivity, and Cd toxicity [CS1, SS2, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signal for this route, so no source is neutral for Route 1.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Binary fluorides are viable because multiple analogues are made from dried or stoichiometric fluorides, including CdF2 and HfF4/ZrF4 systems (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS4). Kinetics are not trivial because Hf-containing cation diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference], but fluoride media and sealed-tube conditions allow reaction far below the elemental cation melting point in the analogues (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target is a simple corner-sharing ABF6 framework, but it must still assemble from binary fluorides [CS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is penalized for reactive F2 atmosphere, repeated heating, and hazardous Cd/F2 handling (CS4). The temperature itself is not extreme because CS4 uses 450–750 °C. Insufficient evidence from SS for Route 2; source score neutral 50. Insufficient evidence from TS for Route 2; source score neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2**  
F2 flow should improve fluorination activity and suppress oxygen contamination [Reasonable Inference from CS4]. However, CS4 is a Tb–Cd fluoride rather than an HfCdF6 R-3 ABF6 analogue, so structural transfer is partial rather than direct. Kinetic uncertainty remains because the route may be unnecessarily oxidizing for stable Hf4+/Cd2+ chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS4].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 avoids very high temperature but uses pressure fluorination and specialized Monel/autoclave hardware, which creates high operational severity (CS5, CS2). The rubric does not apply the >5 GPa deduction because CS5/CS2 pressures are below that threshold. Insufficient evidence from SS for Route 3; source score neutral 50. Insufficient evidence from TS for Route 3; source score neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 3**  
The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: CS5 is an exact R-3 ABF6 corner-sharing octahedral analogue. The weakness is chemical transferability because Cr/Hg fluorides can have different redox/volatility behavior from Hf/Cd fluorides [Reasonable Inference from CS5]. This route is best viewed as an escalation if the sealed-tube route fails.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is severe because CS3 uses F2/Xe, sealed Au tubes, long duration, and careful thermal control. The CS3 temperature details are internally broad/mixed, so the route is treated as exploratory rather than primary [CS3]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Route 4; source score neutral 50. Insufficient evidence from TS for Route 4; source score neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Vapor transport may help single-crystal growth, as CS3 reports fluorinating sealed-tube growth of chemically similar fluorides. The main uncertainty is composition control because no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms matched transport of Hf and Cd into HfCdF6 [CS3; Reasonable Inference]. This makes Route 4 less suitable for first synthesis but plausible after powder-phase identification.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has one clear amorphization risk: Hf–F and Cd–F distances differ substantially, and Cd–Zr/Hf analogues show consistently longer Cd–F than Zr/Hf–F bonds (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4). The target’s connectivity is favorable because it is corner-sharing octahedra only, matching the simple R-3 ABF6 analogue in CS5 rather than the mixed edge/corner networks in SS2/SS5/TS2/TS4. Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is scored neutral 50 for that subcheck.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfCdF6 is likely a DFT ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the CS/SS/TS analogues are overwhelmingly on or near the hull (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The best synthesis support comes from sealed-tube Cd–Zr/Hf fluoride analogues using CdF2 and ZrF4/HfF4 at 550–600 °C for 3 days (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4). The key enabling factor is the simple charge-balanced binary-fluoride reaction HfF4 + CdF2 → HfCdF6 [Reasonable Inference from SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4]. The main limiting factors are lack of reported experimental synthesis for the target, cadmium toxicity, moisture-sensitive fluoride handling, and possible fluoride volatility in open systems [Target data; CS1, SS2, SS5]. Overall, the target is **moderately high** in synthesizability, with Route 1 recommended first and F2-based routes reserved for phase-purity or crystal-growth escalation.
