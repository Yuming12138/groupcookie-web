### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin, and CS1–CS4 contain multiple E_hull = 0 Caswellsilverite Li/Na lanthanide chalcogenides. SS1 is near-hull at 0.0032 eV/atom for LiTiS2, and TS1–TS5 mostly show E_hull = 0 binary sulfides, so all three evidence classes support thermodynamic accessibility. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not include LiHfS2 itself or Hf-containing LiMS2, so the exact Hf redox chemistry is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The elemental sealed-tube route is condition-severe because CS1–CS4 require evacuated tubes, dry-box handling, staged heating, grinding, resealing, and long 900 °C anneals. Kinetics are only moderate because Hf powder is refractory [Reasonable Inference], even though CS1–CS4 show that elemental Li/Na + metal + chalcogen routes can form LiMS2-type products. TS gives insufficient route-specific signal for elemental LiHfS2 synthesis and is treated neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has the strongest transferability because SS1 directly uses Li2S + metal + sulfur to make LiTiS2, while CS1–CS4 confirm related LiMS2 structures. The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multistep processing, and air-sensitive Li2S [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are better than Route 1 because Li2S supplies a preformed Li–S network, but Hf diffusion and possible competing Hf sulfides remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CS5 supports H2/H2S sulfurization at 900 °C, which lowers the nominal temperature burden but introduces toxic/reactive gas handling. Kinetics are weaker because HfO2 or Hf chloride conversion to a layered LiHfS2 sulfide requires substantial chemical reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for H2S/halide LiHfS2 synthesis and are treated neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
SS3, SS5, and TS2 support sulfide CVT with I2 near 950–1000 °C, so the route is plausible for crystal growth. The route is not ideal for first synthesis because no CS/SS/TS example shows Li-containing ternary CVT stoichiometry control, and Li/I/S volatility may shift composition [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for CVT and is treated neutral 50.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target’s mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity triggers one amorphization-risk check, while CS1–CS4 and SS1 mostly show simpler edge/corner-sharing LiMS2 frameworks. The small formula and high-symmetry site description argue against a large-cell amorphization penalty [Reasonable Inference], and the Li/Hf cation-size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% [Reasonable Inference]. TS binary rock-salt references do not meaningfully address mixed Li/Hf polyhedral connectivity, so TS is neutral 50 for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiHfS2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate rather than a merely metastable phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest CS/TS analogues are largely on-hull. The CS evidence strongly supports LiMS2/Caswellsilverite-type sulfide formation, and SS1 gives the most relevant structural precedent through LiTiS2. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of established sealed-tube LiMS2 sulfide routes using either elements or Li2S, supported by CS1–CS4 and SS1. The most critical limiting factor is Hf chemistry: no CS/SS/TS analogue contains Hf, and the target may require reduced Hf in a sulfide lattice rather than the more common HfS2-like chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but not routine, with Route 2 offering the best balance of structural transferability and manageable conditions.
