**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for >1000°C synthesis, Ar/vacuum control, Be volatility, multiple powder-processing steps, and Be toxicity. **(SS1, TS2, TS4, CS4, SS4, TS3; [Reasonable Inference])** It avoids high pressure and can be kept at or below ~1400°C in the recommended adaptation, so it avoids the >1400°C deduction. **(SS1, TS2; [Reasonable Inference])**

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental powders and refractory Ir limit reaction rate, but beryllide analogues form at 900–1600°C and the same F-43m Be5Co framework strongly supports structural accessibility. **(SS1, TS1, TS2, TS4; [Reasonable Inference])** The main uncertainty is whether Ir diffuses fast enough without full melting. **([Reasonable Inference])**

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 receives heavy severity deductions because arc melting involves very high local temperature, controlled He/Ar atmosphere, repeated remelting, Be toxicity, and likely Be loss. **(CS1, CS2, CS3, SS2, CS4, SS4, TS3; [Reasonable Inference])** It avoids high-pressure equipment and is directly supported by multiple Be-rich melt syntheses. **(CS1, CS2, CS3, SS2, TS5)**

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are the best among the routes because molten processing improves contact and homogenization, and repeated fusions are explicitly used for Be-rich beryllides. **(CS1, CS2, CS3)** The structural uncertainty is moderate because the exact F-43m analogue is Be5Co, while the melt-grown precious-metal analogues are mostly tetragonal Be12M phases. **(SS1, TS1, CS2, CS3, SS2)**

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth has moderate severity because it likely needs >1000°C, sealed/inert handling, flux separation, and Be toxicity controls. **(CS4, SS4, TS3; [Reasonable Inference])** Exact temperature and atmosphere are not provided for the UBe13 flux precedent, so those source-specific signals are treated as neutral 50 where unspecified. **(insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact flux temperature/atmosphere)**

Flux improves mass transport and can grow Be-rich crystals, but UBe13 is structurally different from Be5Ir and no Be–Ir flux chemistry is given. **(CS4, SS4, TS3; SS1)** The largest uncertainty is Ir solubility and whether Al stabilizes unwanted ternary or residual phases. **([Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ir–Al–Be flux behavior)**

HPHT has a relatively better rubric F2 because the cited pressure is ~1.2 GPa, below the >5 GPa penalty, and the reported temperature is 1400°C rather than >1400°C. **(CS5; [Reasonable Inference])** Practical difficulty remains high because the route needs specialized Belt-type equipment and Ta capsules. **(CS5)**

Kinetic confidence is low because the only direct high-pressure analogue is Mg3Ir, not a Be–Ir beryllide or F-43m Be5 phase. **(CS5; SS1, TS1)** This route is therefore exploratory rather than preferred despite acceptable formal severity scoring. **(CS5; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for high-pressure Be–Ir synthesis)**
