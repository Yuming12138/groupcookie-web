### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest K/Na/Sr/Pb antimonate analogues in SS/TS are also on the hull, so the thermodynamic score is high. [SS1; SS2; SS3; SS5; TS1; TS2; TS3; TS5] CS gives useful but mixed thermodynamic context because KSbO3 is on-hull while BaSiO3 high-pressure analogues have high E_hull; the BaSiO3 entries are less transferable due to different chemistry. [CS1; CS3; CS4]

**F2 — Route 1.** Hydrothermal synthesis is low-temperature and avoids high-pressure DAC conditions, so severity is low-to-moderate. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2] The main deduction is toxic Sb precursor handling, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from use of Sb oxides in antimonate analogues. [SS3; SS4; SS5; TS3; TS4; TS5]

**F3 — Route 1.** Solution-mediated hydrothermal transport gives favorable kinetics relative to dry solid-state diffusion. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2] The closest structural analogues are R-3 KSbO3/NaSbO3, but the sources also describe ilmenite phases as metastable or non-conventional, so phase-window uncertainty remains. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2]

**F2 — Route 2.** Ion exchange avoids extreme temperature/pressure but adds multi-step processing because a Na/K parent phase must first be prepared. [CS1; SS2; TS2] Toxic Sb oxide handling remains a route-independent deduction by [Reasonable Inference]. [SS3; SS4; SS5; TS3; TS4; TS5]

**F3 — Route 2.** Topochemical exchange is kinetically attractive because it preserves the SbO6 framework, but Rb+ exchange through a dense ilmenite lattice may be incomplete. [SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2; Reasonable Inference] The Na/K analogues support the structural concept, but CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for Rb exchange itself, so that source-specific proof is neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux growth avoids DAC-level severity and can operate below 1000 °C, but it requires a covered or controlled crucible, multiple steps, and toxic Sb precursors. [SS4; TS4; Reasonable Inference] Slow cooling and quenching are directly supported for K–Zn–Sb–O crystal growth. [SS4; TS4]

**F3 — Route 3.** A molten Rb-rich flux should improve ion mobility and crystallization relative to dry powders. [SS4; TS4; Reasonable Inference] However, direct RbSbO3 flux evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and is therefore neutral 50 for target-specific flux precedent.

**F2 — Route 4.** Conventional solid-state synthesis uses standard furnace equipment and air in several Sb oxide analogues. [SS3; SS4; SS5; TS3; TS4; TS5] Its severity increases because long holds, regrinding, and Rb volatility control are likely needed. [SS5; TS5; Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 4.** Solid-state kinetics are slower because carbonate/oxide reactions require diffusion and repeated grinding. [SS3; SS5; TS3; TS5] The closest R-3 alkali antimonates are hydrothermal rather than conventional firing, so solid-state transferability is weaker than Route 1. [SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** One amorphization trigger is counted because the alkali/Sb size mismatch is large, as reflected by much longer alkali–O than Sb–O bonds in close analogues. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2] The structure is otherwise relatively simple and the closest KSbO3 analogue emphasizes edge-sharing SbO6 rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, though NaSbO3 provides a conflicting connectivity description. [SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbSbO3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest antimonate analogues are also on-hull. [SS1; SS2; SS3; SS5; TS1; TS2; TS3; TS5] The strongest synthesis support comes from KSbO3 and NaSbO3, which share the trigonal R-3 ilmenite-type alkali–Sb–O framework and are linked to hydrothermal/ion-exchange synthesis. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2] The key enabling factor is structural transferability from KSbO3, because K and Rb occupy analogous alkali sites and Sb remains octahedral. [CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference] The key limiting factor is the absence of direct RbSbO3 experimental evidence in CS/SS/TS, plus expected Rb stoichiometry and Sb oxidation-state control. [SS3; TS3; Reasonable Inference] Overall, the best practical attempt is hydrothermal crystallization with Rb excess and oxidizing Sb(V) conditions. [CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2]
