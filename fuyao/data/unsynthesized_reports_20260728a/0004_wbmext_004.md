### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 band and is scored 98 [Target data]. CS support is mostly favorable because four CS analogues are ≤0.05 eV/atom, although CS2 at 0.241 eV/atom is a cautionary outlier [CS1–CS5]. SS and TS support is strong because the structural and thermodynamic analogue sets are predominantly on-hull or near-hull [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 Hydrothermal.** Hydrothermal conditions are mild in SS1/SS4/TS2/TS5 and avoid high temperature, high pressure, and reactive gas penalties, with the main severity penalty being arsenic toxicity [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS5]. Kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated transport bypasses refractory oxide diffusion, but SS4’s failed LiInAs2O7 attempt lowers structural-transfer confidence for In-containing arsenates [SS4]. CS1/CS3 hydrothermal precursor details are inconsistent with their Cd arsenate formulas, so those CS precursor details are insufficient evidence and neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Flux.** Flux growth has moderate severity because it requires high-temperature handling, slow cooling, flux removal, and arsenic volatility control, even though most preferred arsenate flux examples stay below 1000 °C [SS2/TS1, TS4, CS5]. Kinetics benefit from molten flux transport, and TS3 is especially relevant because it crystallizes an InO6–tetrahedral oxide framework from flux [TS3]. Phase selectivity is the limiting kinetic term because SS2/TS1 report minor GaAsO4 impurity and alkali fluxes can stabilize competing arsenates [SS2/TS1; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Diffusion.** Diffusion synthesis has low thermal severity but CS2 uses an Ar atmosphere and arsenate salts, so F2 is penalized for controlled atmosphere and toxicity [CS2]. F3 is limited mainly by structural reconstruction because CS2 supports low-temperature arsenate crystals but does not prove an anhydrous InO6–AsO4 framework [CS2]. SS and TS provide insufficient diffusion-route evidence and are scored neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 HPHT.** HPHT is severe because CS4 requires 1573 K and 5.2 GPa, and target adaptation would need sealed containment for arsenic retention [CS4; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate because pressure/temperature can force dense In–O frameworks, but stable oxide precursors and short dwell times limit reaction completeness [CS4]. SS and TS provide insufficient HPHT arsenate evidence and are scored neutral 50 for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 5 Gas–Solid.** Gas–solid oxidation has moderate condition severity due to reactive Cl2/O2 atmosphere and toxic gas/arsenide handling [SS5]. Kinetics are weak because SS5 reports low yield, secondary phases, and poor reproducibility even for GaAsO4, implying difficult reconstruction and phase control for InAsO4 [SS5]. CS and TS provide insufficient gas–solid route evidence and are scored neutral 50 for this route.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has a large formal cation size/coordination contrast between octahedral In and tetrahedral As, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The atom-count trigger is uncertain because CS/SS/TS do not directly provide the target cell atom count, so that source contribution is neutral 50. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InAsO4 is best treated as a predicted ground-state phase because the supplied target record gives E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues are also largely on-hull or near-hull [Target data; TS1–TS5]. The analogue support is strongest for hydrothermal and flux synthesis because multiple arsenate frameworks with octahedral metal centers and AsO4 groups crystallize by those routes [SS1, SS2/TS1, SS4, TS2, TS4]. The most important enabling factor is solution- or flux-mediated transport, which avoids long-range refractory oxide diffusion and is repeatedly successful in arsenate analogues [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS3]. The most important limiting factor is In-specific phase selectivity because SS4 reports unsuccessful LiInAs2O7 attempts, and no CS/SS/TS source directly validates InAsO4 synthesis [SS4; CS/SS/TS absence]. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with hydrothermal synthesis the preferred first route and flux growth the best crystal-growth fallback [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS3].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy (from CS/SS/TS) |
|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | Arsenic toxicity and hazardous arsenate/arsenic oxide handling | High | Use closed autoclaves or contained crucibles, small batches, and arsenic waste capture; arsenate precursors are used throughout SS/TS analogues [SS1, SS4, TS2, TS5]. |
| 2 | All | Competing arsenate / diarsenate phase formation | High | Keep In:As near 1:1, screen pH and As excess, and verify by powder XRD; K/Sr/Cd/Ga diarsenates appear as stable analogue families [SS2/TS1, TS2]. |
| 3 | Route 1 | In2O3 dissolution and In(III) hydrolysis mismatch | Medium | Use fine In2O3 or soluble In salt, acidic pH ~1–1.5, and pre-dissolution before hydrothermal treatment [SS1, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow ordering/crystal growth in hydrothermal conditions | Medium | Use 4–10 d dwell and slow furnace cooling, matching hydrothermal arsenate analogue practice [SS1, SS4, TS2, CS1/CS3]. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Arsenic loss at high flux temperature | High | Prefer lower-temperature arsenate/borate fluxes, covered Pt crucibles, or sealed secondary containment; flux analogues operate from ~500–950 °C [SS2/TS1, TS4, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Alkali flux incorporation or competing K/Na arsenates | Medium | Compare B2O3 vs alkali arsenate fluxes, limit alkali activity, and leach flux thoroughly; KGaAs2O7 and NaInGe2O6 show alkali-containing phases are readily stabilized [SS2/TS1, TS3]. |
| 7 | Route 2 | Crystal growth impurity / phase purity control | Medium | Use controlled slow cooling and post-growth hot-water flux dissolution; SS2/TS1 report minor GaAsO4 impurity in a flux-grown analogue [SS2/TS1]. |
| 8 | Route 4 | HPHT apparatus burden and arsenic capsule pressure | High | Use pre-reacted precursor, short dwell, noble-metal capsule, and quench under pressure; CS4 demonstrates 5.2 GPa / 1573 K HPHT feasibility for In oxide frameworks [CS4]. |
| 9 | Route 5 | Reactive Cl2/O2 atmosphere sensitivity and corrosion | High | Use controlled vapor-pressure/gas-handling equipment and low sample loading; SS5 identifies Cl2/O2 gas-solid synthesis as possible but difficult [SS5]. |
| 10 | Route 5 | Poor reproducibility and secondary phases | High | Treat as exploratory only after hydrothermal/flux failure; SS5 reports low yield, secondary phases, and poor reproducibility for GaAsO4 [SS5]. |
