### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted on the convex hull with **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, which strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability (**Target**). Similar phases Zr3AlN, Zr2InN, and Hf3AlN are also hull-stable at **E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom** (**CS1/SS1/TS1; CS5/SS2/TS2; SS3/TS3**). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is WBM theory-only with no experimental synthesis reported (**Target**).

**F2 — Route 1**  
The melt route is directly supported by Zr3AlN synthesis under pure Ar in a high-frequency furnace with slow cooling (**CS1/SS1/TS1**). Severity is reduced by the simple workflow but penalized for high inferred temperature, controlled Ar, and In volatility **[Reasonable Inference]**. Exact melt temperature is insufficient from **CS1/SS1/TS1**, so temperature deductions rely on the physical implication of melting **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: Zr3AlN and the target share the same Cmcm framework and NZr6 octahedral motif (**Target; CS1/SS1/TS1**). Melt processing should improve diffusion relative to solid-state sintering **[Reasonable Inference]**. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether ZrN and In remain homogeneous before In loss or secondary phase formation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Sealed-ampoule annealing has moderate condition severity because the exact Zr–In–N analogue forms at 500–900 °C without high pressure (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). Deductions come from sealed/controlled atmosphere and In retention requirements **[Reasonable Inference]**. The source reports long times but not extreme equipment, which supports a moderate rather than severe score (**CS5/SS2/TS2**).

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are limited because Zr/ZrN are refractory and Zr2InN required 750–850 h anneals (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). The chemistry is favorable because Zr2InN demonstrates Zr–In–N formation with NZr6 octahedra (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). The structural uncertainty is polymorph selection: the analogue is hexagonal H-phase, while the target is orthorhombic Cmcm (**Target; CS5/SS2/TS2**).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Arc-melting is well precedented for Zr-rich analogues and uses Ar/vacuum control (**CS4/SS4/SS5/TS4/TS5**). It is penalized heavily because arc-melting implies very high temperatures and the route adds post-annealing/repeated processing (**CS4/SS4/TS4**). In volatility is a target-specific risk **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Arc-melting improves homogenization and can produce ordered Zr-rich crystalline products after annealing (**SS4/TS4; SS5/TS5**). However, Zr4Pd2O showed impurity phases even after repeated remelting, indicating phase-purity risk in related Zr-rich melts (**CS4**). No source directly demonstrates arc-melted Zr–In–N Cmcm formation, so that aspect is neutral-to-moderate rather than strong evidence from **CS/SS/TS**.

**F2 — Route 4**  
The In-flux route avoids >1000 °C processing by using the same moderate ampoule window as Zr2InN (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). It is penalized for sealed containment, volatile In, and multi-step flux removal **[Reasonable Inference]**. Flux growth itself has insufficient direct evidence from **CS/SS/TS**, so unsupported flux-specific source evidence is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Liquid In could improve precursor transport and reduce diffusion limitations **[Reasonable Inference]**. Zr2InN supports Zr–In–N chemistry at moderate temperature, but not flux crystallization of the Cmcm target (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). The structural pathway therefore remains uncertain and is scored near neutral for flux-specific evidence.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has mixed edge/corner-sharing NZr6 octahedra, which triggers one amorphization-risk check (**Target**). Closest analogues with the same or similar octahedral connectivity are crystalline and hull-stable, which mitigates the risk (**CS1/SS1/TS1; CS5/SS2/TS2**). Cell-size and cation-size checks do not trigger based on analogue cell scale and expected Zr–In size mismatch **[Reasonable Inference]**.

Zr3InN is likely a **ground-state or very near-ground-state crystalline phase** because the supplied target hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom (**Target**). The analogue support is strong: Zr3AlN has the same Cmcm structure and was synthesized by melt growth (**CS1/SS1/TS1**), while Zr2InN proves accessible Zr–In–N chemistry in sealed ampoules (**CS5/SS2/TS2**). The most critical enabling factor is the robust NZr6 octahedral framework shared across the target and close analogues (**Target; CS1/SS1/TS1; CS5/SS2/TS2**). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against Zr2InN/ZrN impurities and retention of In at elevated temperature, because Zr2InN showed mononitride impurity and long annealing requirements (**CS5/SS2/TS2**) and volatile p-block compensation is needed in related Zr systems (**SS5/TS5**). Overall, the target is **moderately likely synthesizable**, with sealed-ampoule annealing the safest first attempt (**CS5/SS2/TS2**).
