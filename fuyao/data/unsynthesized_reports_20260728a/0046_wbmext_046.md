### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is high by the requested rubric; TS supports carbonate ground-state plausibility through CdCO3, Ag2CO3, and NaHCO3 analogues with E_hull = 0 (TS1–TS3). CS is supportive but not uniform because Hg(CO2)2 is synthetically accessible despite E_hull ≈0.252 eV/atom, while Pb carbonate, CdCO3, and Ag2CO3 analogues are stable or low-energy (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS provide **insufficient direct evidence** for experimental HgCO3, so exact-target validation remains neutral 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** Route 1 has mild condition severity because CS1/SS1 show room-temperature Hg precipitation and TS3 shows ambient bicarbonate crystallization; the only formal F2 deduction is toxic Hg precursor use (CS1, SS1). Kinetics are favorable because soluble nitrate/carbonate precursors react through solution, but structural confidence is reduced because the exact calcite HgCO3 phase is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS5, TS2, TS3].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Route 2 is more severe because hydrothermal carbonate analogues require sealed autoclaves, multistep heating/cooling, and 150–230 °C holds (CS2, CS3, SS3, TS4/TS5). Kinetics remain good because hydrothermal transport aids crystallization, but CS2 reports multiple phases and TS4/TS5 mention subtle hydrothermal control, so phase selectivity is the main uncertainty.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** Route 3 has the best structural basis because CdCO3 is calcite R-3c with MO6 octahedra and carbonate groups, matching the target prototype most closely (CS4, SS2, TS1). Its penalties come from sealed CO₂/high-pressure processing, multistep capsule work, and Hg toxicity; the rubric does not deduct for 1 GPa because the high-pressure threshold is >5 GPa, but practical difficulty remains high (CS4, SS2, TS1).

**F2/F3 — Route 4.** Route 4 is mild thermally because CS5/TS2/TS3 use ambient slow evaporation, but ammoniacal chemistry triggers a severity deduction and may complicate Hg speciation (CS5, SS5, TS2). Kinetics are favorable in solution, but SS5 notes rough crystal morphology and no source proves HgCO3 crystal growth, so this is better as a polishing/crystal-growth route after Route 1 identifies the phase (SS5).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target-like calcite prototype is compact, single-cation, and dominated by corner-sharing octahedral carbonate connectivity, giving zero F4 triggers (CS4, SS2, TS1). Mixed edge/face/vertex networks in complex Na–Ln carbonates are present in TS4/TS5, but those are not the target prototype and are therefore not weighted heavily for HgCO3.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgCO3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state candidate because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic calcite carbonate analogue CdCO3 is also stable (TS1). The analogue support is strong for carbonate chemistry and the calcite prototype, but indirect for HgCO3 specifically because CS/SS/TS provide **insufficient direct experimental HgCO3 evidence** and are neutral 50 for exact-target proof. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution mobility, since Hg(II) precipitation and carbonate/evaporation analogues proceed under mild conditions (CS1, CS5, TS2, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase control under Hg chemistry: hydrolysis/basic carbonate formation, toxic Hg handling, and possible Hg loss are not resolved by the analogue set [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS2, CS4/SS2/TS1]. The recommended path is Route 1 first, with Route 3 as a structurally rigorous fallback if ambient precipitation fails to yield calcite HgCO3.
