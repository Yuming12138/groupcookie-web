**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because Hf₂N₂S is reported at 750 °C in vacuum-sealed quartz. (CS1, CS2, SS1) The deductions come from vacuum sealing, multi-step precursor/reaction/post-wash handling, and air/moisture-sensitive sulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. (CS1, CS2)

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable for a Hf compound because nitride-halide + sulfide metathesis forms Hf₂N₂S at only 750 °C. (CS1, CS2, TS1) The limitation is that this route most directly supports the composition and local coordination, not the exact P6₃/mmc polymorph. (SS1, SS2)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still uses sealed-tube vacuum metathesis below 1000 °C. (SS3, SS4, TS2) The main penalties are controlled atmosphere, multiple processing steps, and sulfide/nitride-halide handling. (SS3, SS4, CS1)

**F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 receives the highest kinetic score because P6₃/mmc Zr₂N₂S is directly synthesized by anion metathesis, while Hf₂N₂S chemistry is independently validated. (SS3, TS2, CS1) The uncertainty is that HfNCl-to-Hf₂N₂S P6₃/mmc transfer is an extrapolation, not a directly reported target synthesis. (SS3, CS1, [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth stays below 1000 °C but requires evacuated tubes, long heating, and volatile chalcogen management. (SS5, TS3) Insufficient evidence from CS for the flux route is treated as neutral 50, so Route 3 is supported mainly by SS/TS rather than CS.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux transport should improve crystallization relative to dry ceramic sintering, as shown by the Zr₂N₂Se flux analogue. (SS5, TS3) The kinetic penalty is HfN’s refractory nature and the need to substitute both Hf for Zr and S for Se [Reasonable Inference]. (SS5, TS3)

**F2 — Route 4**  
High-temperature sintering is the harshest route because the closest ceramic analogue uses 1500–1700 °C under N₂. (CS3) Insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for direct high-temperature Hf₂N₂S sintering are each neutral 50 signals, so Route 4 is treated as a fallback rather than a preferred route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Bulk diffusion through HfN/HfS-type refractory solids is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. (CS3, CS5) The route is chemically plausible because Th₂N₂S forms from ThS/ThN/N₂, but it provides weak control over the exact Hf₂N₂S polymorph. (CS3, CS1, CS2)
