# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at exactly 0.0000 eV/atom above hull, with a negative raw normalized value clipped to zero because physical hull distance cannot be negative [Target record]. This supports a ground-state or near-ground-state assignment and gives F1 = 100. The limitation is that the candidate is theory-only in the supplied validation, so thermodynamic stability does not guarantee that the phase will nucleate preferentially or be recoverable experimentally [Target record]. The zero-hull values of the closest CS/SS/TS analogues provide additional support for a stable intermetallic family [CS, SS, TS].

## F2: Route 1

Route 1 uses inert Ar and moderate-to-high temperatures similar to the NaAuSn synthesis, but it requires sealed containment because Na is volatile and air-sensitive [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. The staged heating and annealing sequence is more complex than a single heating step and reduces the score by an additional deduction [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure or reactive-gas penalty is supported by CS/SS/TS, so those terms remain neutral [CS, SS, TS].

## F3: Route 1

The route begins with dense elemental metals, giving moderate-to-low precursor activity [CS, SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion is helped by the low-melting Na/Sn components and by the precedent that NaAuSn forms under high-temperature treatment below or near the melting point of its more refractory constituents [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction should be relatively easy because NaAuSn has the same Pnma topology and essentially the same coordination pattern as the target [SS, TS], giving Route 1 a relatively high F3.

## F2: Route 2

High-frequency melting avoids some solid-state diffusion limitations but likely requires a temperature above 1000 °C for complete Pd homogenization [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. The route remains penalized by Na volatility and by the need for a post-melt anneal if ordered Pnma material is desired [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. The direct Pd-containing melting precedents demonstrate practical feasibility, but they do not specifically establish Na retention during melting [SS, TS].

## F3: Route 2

High-frequency melting improves mass transport and reaction kinetics, as demonstrated for SrPdIn₂ and CaPdIn₂ [SS, TS]. However, a melt route has a less direct structural pathway than a solid-state reaction patterned on NaAuSn, so structural reconstruction is scored lower [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]. The principal uncertainty is whether the target is stable during the high-temperature melt or whether Na loss and competing binaries dominate [SS, TS; Reasonable Inference].

## F4: Amorphization Tendency

The Na-to-Pd/Sn size difference is a plausible source of local strain and is treated as one trigger [Reasonable Inference]. Nevertheless, the existence of NaAuSn with the same Pnma topology shows that the size mismatch does not prevent formation of an ordered Na–Sn–late-transition-metal framework [SS, TS]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to determine whether the target unit cell contains more than 40 atoms or whether it has mixed vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra; both checks are therefore scored neutral at 50 [CS, SS, TS].

NaSnPd is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target record reports \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV/atom, although the supplied validation identifies it as theory-only [Target record]. A sealed, staged solid-state reaction is therefore preferred over open high-frequency melting.
