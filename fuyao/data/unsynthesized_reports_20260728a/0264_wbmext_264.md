### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the provided target data, which places it in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin. (Target data) The strongest analogue, Na3Pt4Ge4, is also E_hull = 0.0 and appears simultaneously as a chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar reference. (CS2/SS1/TS1) The main uncertainty is that the target is theory-only in the supplied target data, so the 96 score is high but not 100. (Target data)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for Ar/sealed handling, Na volatility, multi-step heating, and Na air sensitivity. (CS2/SS1/TS1) It avoids >1000 °C conditions if the Na3Pt4Ge4 thermal envelope is followed closely, which keeps severity moderate rather than severe. (CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F3 — Route 1**  
The exact I-43m Na3Pt4Ge4 analogue strongly supports structural kinetics and phase accessibility. (CS2/SS1/TS1) Pt and Si remain slow-diffusing refractory components, but excess molten/vapor Na and a 600 °C temper likely assist reaction completion. [Reasonable Inference] (CS2/SS1/TS1; CS1)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 receives a lower severity score because 1100–1200 °C exceeds the 1000 °C threshold and still requires sealed Ar/Ta handling for volatile Na. (CS1; CS2/SS1/TS1) The route is feasible but more hazardous and mechanically demanding than Route 1. (CS1; CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F3 — Route 2**  
The high-temperature Si-containing I-43m Ca3Ir4Si4 route supports formation of the Si framework at 1200 °C over 5 days. (CS1) The uncertainty is that replacing Ca/Ir with Na/Pt changes volatility and bonding, so the exact Na3Pt4Ge4 route is weighted more heavily. (CS1; CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 avoids >1000 °C, but NaN3 introduces toxic/explosive precursor risk and sealed-ampoule gas-evolution risk. (CS4) SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for an azide route specifically, so their azide-route signal is neutral at 50. (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5)

**F3 — Route 3**  
NaN3 can supply reactive sodium, but the NaAuGe precedent has a different structure and reports impurity formation. (CS4) The route is therefore plausible but less kinetically reliable for the I-43m Na3Pt4Si4 target than Route 1. (CS4; CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is penalized for high-temperature sealed handling, Na volatility, multi-step homogenization/annealing, and Na air sensitivity. (SS3/TS3; CS2/SS1/TS1) Open arc melting is not directly transferable because Na retention would be poor, so sealed-capsule adaptation is required. [Reasonable Inference] (SS4/TS4; CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F3 — Route 4**  
Induction/arc routes support Pt–tetrel intermetallic crystallization and homogenization, but the structurally similar melt-grown analogues are not the exact I-43m Na3Pt4Si4 framework. (SS3/TS3; SS4/TS4; SS5) The kinetic benefit of melting is partly offset by the need to keep Na sealed and stoichiometric. [Reasonable Inference] (CS2/SS1/TS1)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target’s compact intermetallic analogue family and multiple crystalline references argue against strong amorphization risk. (CS2/SS1/TS1; CS1) C1 is triggered by large Na-vs-framework size mismatch as a [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient explicit ionic-radius data and are neutral at 50 for that specific check. C2 and C3 are not clearly triggered because the closest I-43m analogues are compact ordered crystalline phases without reported mixed polyhedral sharing. (CS2/SS1/TS1)
