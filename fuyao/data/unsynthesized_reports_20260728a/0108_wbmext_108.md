### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic score bin. Chemically similar La5B2N6 and Pr3B2N4 are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1, CS-4), and structurally/thermodynamically similar NaSr4(BN2)3, LiSr4(BN2)3, and LiEu4(BN2)3 are likewise on the hull (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that WBM identifies the target as theory-only and not experimentally validated, so kinetic or competing-phase issues may still prevent synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state.**  
The condition severity is moderate-to-high because close analogues require sealed ampoules, Ar/vacuum handling, and 900–1375 K or 900–1100 °C anneals over days (CS-1; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). Kinetics are only moderate because BN and binary nitrides are relatively refractory, and SS-1/TS-1 require long 5-day reactions, while CS-4 shows rare-earth boron nitrides may need repeated grinding and 100 h anneals. Insufficient evidence from CS for exact Yb3BN3 phase selectivity; neutral score = 50 for Yb-specific phase selectivity from CS.

**F2/F3 — Route 2: Li3N/Li flux.**  
The route still requires sealed ampoules, reactive Li/Li3N, and strict inert handling, as seen in CS-2 and CS-5. Its kinetic score is higher because Li flux/Li3N flux syntheses operate at 900–1030 °C with 24–60 h holds and slow cooling, suggesting enhanced mass transport relative to purely solid-state diffusion (CS-2, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS for Li-flux synthesis of the exact Yb3BN3 phase; neutral score = 50 for exact compositional transferability from SS.

**F2/F3 — Route 3: Rare-earth metal + BN.**  
CS-4 directly supports rare-earth metal filings + BN as a route to rare-earth boron nitrides, but it requires 1200 °C prereaction and 1400 °C/100 h treatment. For Yb, this route is more problematic because Yb volatility and divalency control are extrapolated risks [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for metal + BN synthesis of Yb3BN3 specifically; neutral score = 50 for exact Yb phase formation from TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 4: HTHP nitride–BN sintering.**  
SS-4/TS-4 support high-pressure/high-temperature nitride–BN sintering for related boron nitrides and note specialized toroid-type equipment, Mo/Ta ampoules, and phase-control challenges. Kinetics may improve under pressure due to better contact and suppressed volatility [Reasonable Inference], but equipment severity and phase-window uncertainty remain substantial. CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis of chemically exact Yb3BN3; neutral score = 50 for CS on this route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a compact formula and is described as a 3D periodic crystalline network, which argues against a high amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference]. Related linear-BN boron nitrides crystallize successfully in well-ordered structures in SS/TS analogues (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2), but some analogues show mixed sharing motifs and phase-purity challenges (SS-4, SS-5; TS-4). The main amorphization concern is large size/coordination mismatch between Yb and B [Reasonable Inference], while the small cell and absence of face-sharing in the target reduce the risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Yb3BN3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar nitrides are also on the hull (CS-1, CS-4; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The analogue support is fairly strong for boron nitride frameworks with linear B–N units, because SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-2 have B–N distances of ~1.34–1.35 Å, matching the target’s 1.35 Å linear B–N motif. The most critical enabling factor is use of sealed Ta/Nb ampoule nitride chemistry, especially Li3N/Li flux, which improves nitrogen activity and transport under air-free conditions (CS-2, CS-5; TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is control of Yb oxidation state/volatility and avoidance of Li-containing or YbN/BN residual competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but not routine, with the flux route offering the best balance between kinetics and phase assembly.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Atmosphere sensitivity of nitrides, Li3N, rare-earth metal precursors | High | Use Ar glovebox and sealed Ta/Nb ampoules, as in CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, and TS-2. |
| 2 | All | Yb volatility and oxidation-state control | High | Use sealed refractory-metal ampoules; include slight Yb excess or reducing Li/Yb-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference], analogous to sealed ampoule and flux practice in CS-2/CS-5/TS-5. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Slow solid-state diffusion through refractory BN/Yb nitride mixture | Medium–High | Use fine grinding, pelletizing, long anneals of 3–5 days, and possible regrinding; supported by SS-1/TS-1 and CS-4. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Li incorporation or formation of Li–Yb–B–N competing phases | Medium | Optimize Li flux amount and remove flux after slow cooling; Li-containing boron nitrides are known in SS-2/TS-2/TS-5, so Li stoichiometry must be controlled. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Reactive Li/Li3N/LiN3 handling and pressure buildup | High | Prefer Li3N over LiN3 initially; if LiN3 is used, use small quantities and robust Ta/Nb sealed ampoules as in CS-2 [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Route 3 | Incomplete reaction of rare-earth metal + BN and long homogenization time | High | Follow CS-4 style prereaction, crushing, recompaction, and long final anneal; cap temperature or seal ampoule for Yb [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 7 | Route 4 | Specialized high-pressure equipment and narrow phase window | High | Use pressure only as a screening/stabilization route; SS-4/TS-4 show HTHP methods can access related boron nitrides but also indicate phase-control difficulty. |
| 8 | All | Competing phases such as YbN, BN, YbB6/Yb borides, or Li boron nitrides | Medium | Use phase-pure dried precursors, stoichiometric control, and iterative PXRD-guided annealing; phase-purity challenges are reported for related nitrides in CS-3, CS-4, SS-3, SS-4, and SS-5. |
