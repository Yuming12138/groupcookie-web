### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, so the base thermodynamic score belongs in the 90–100 bin. TS gives the strongest corroboration because all listed thermodynamic analogues have E_hull = 0.0, while CS and SS are supportive but mixed because some analogues are metastable at 0.106–0.484 eV/atom. (Target data; CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5)

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is severe because it uses arc melting, purified Ar/vacuum annealing, multiple remelts, and volatile/toxic Sb handling. The same severity is nevertheless standard for the closest half-Heusler and Ta intermetallic precedents. (CS1, CS4, SS2, TS1; Sb/Os hazards = [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are acceptable because melt homogenization and repeated remelting reduce the diffusion problem that would otherwise be severe for Ta/Os. The strongest kinetic/structural support is CS1, because it is the same half-Heusler structure type, while SS/TS Ta intermetallics support remelting/annealing practice. (CS1, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS2)

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 avoids full bulk melt processing during the main reaction but still requires a controlled sealed atmosphere, long sintering, multiple handling steps, and volatile Sb containment. The master-alloy route is directly supported for Os pnictides, but those analogues are not cubic half-Heuslers. (CS2, SS1, SS5)

**F3 — Route 2.** Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because refractory Ta–Os diffusion through a powder compact is likely slow, even with a master alloy. The Os–As analogues show that the approach can work, but they require long sintering and sometimes high-temperature annealing. (CS2, SS1, SS5; refractory diffusion = [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 is severe because it requires high-temperature melt handling plus vacuum annealing, and Sb loss must be controlled. CS3 supports the melt/anneal concept for an Os ternary germanide, but SS and TS provide insufficient direct melt-growth evidence and are therefore neutral for this route. (CS3; insufficient evidence from SS/TS)

**F3 — Route 3.** Melt growth improves transport relative to solid-state sintering, but the structural transferability is only moderate because CS3 is not a half-Heusler and not Sb-based. The unknown congruent melting behavior of TaOsSb is a key uncertainty. (CS3; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 4.** Route 4 lowers the need for full refractory-metal melting, but it still requires high temperature, evacuated sealing, flux handling, and toxic/volatile Sb management. CS5 supports pnictide flux growth, while SS and TS provide insufficient direct flux-growth evidence and are neutral. (CS5; insufficient evidence from SS/TS)

**F3 — Route 4.** Flux can improve mass transport and crystal growth, but Ta/Os solubility and half-Heusler ordering in Sb-rich flux are unverified. Therefore, the route is plausible but less evidence-backed than arc melting. (CS5; [Reasonable Inference])

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target is a compact cubic half-Heusler, and the closest CS half-Heusler analogue is experimentally crystalline, supporting low amorphization risk. Strict ionic-radius evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, but no large-cell or fully mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity trigger is evident from the target description. (CS1; target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for strict ionic-radius check)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaOsSb is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the TS analogues are also listed as hull-stable. (Target data; TS1–TS5) The synthesis evidence is strongest for an arc-melting/annealing route because the closest chemical analogue HfRhSb is a half-Heusler made by arc melting, and Ta-rich intermetallic analogues are routinely made by arc melting plus annealing. (CS1, CS4, SS2, TS1) The most important enabling factor is the simple half-Heusler F-43m structure, which has a direct Sb-containing analogue in CS1. (CS1) The main limiting factor is simultaneous control of refractory Ta/Os homogenization and volatile/toxic Sb stoichiometry. (CS2, SS1, SS5; volatility/toxicity = [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the material appears meaningfully synthesizable but not trivial, with the best probability coming from metallurgical arc melting followed by a long anneal. (CS1, CS4, SS3, TS2)
