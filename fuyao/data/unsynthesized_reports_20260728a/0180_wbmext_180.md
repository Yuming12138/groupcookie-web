### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Target CaS3 is reported on the convex hull at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high F1 score (Target data). TS is strongly supportive because all five TS analogues listed are at E_hull = 0.0, while SS is also supportive because KAuS5, Tl2S5, and CsCuS4 are at 0.0 and SrS2 is only 0.023 eV/atom above hull (TS/SS). CS is contradictory because CaP3 and K2S5 are stable, whereas NaCl3 and NaN3 analogues are significantly above hull, so CS is not used as the dominant thermodynamic signal (CS).

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and avoids high pressure, which keeps condition severity moderate [Reasonable Inference; CS/SS/TS]. Its penalties come from sealed vacuum/inert handling, volatile sulfur, and air/moisture-sensitive Ca sulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference; SS/TS]. The lack of direct experimental CaS3 synthesis remains the main uncertainty (Target data).

**F3 — Route 1**  
CaS + sulfur should be moderately reactive because sulfur can provide vapor/liquid transport, but converting CaS to CaS3 requires formation of S–S bonds and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Similar S–S distances in K2S5, KAuS5, Tl2S5, CsCuS4, SrS2, and ZnS2 support the polysulfide motif (CS/SS/TS). The uncertainty is whether CaS3 orders cleanly rather than forming CaS/CaS2 or amorphous calcium polysulfide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperatures similar to KAuS5, but it requires inert/vacuum handling, sulfur containment, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive alkali polysulfides (SS/TS). The route is kinetically attractive but operationally more complex than direct sealed-tube synthesis (SS/TS; Reasonable Inference). CS provides useful polysulfide solution precedent through K2S5, but it does not directly resolve Ca incorporation into flux-grown CaS3 (CS; Reasonable Inference).

**F3 — Route 2**  
Molten polychalcogenide fluxes provide high precursor activity and fast transport, as shown by KAuS5 formation at 250–350 °C (SS/TS). Structural transfer is plausible because sulfur-rich fluxes stabilize S–S bonded networks, but the Ca-only target could be contaminated by K/Na flux cations [Reasonable Inference]. This gives high kinetics but moderate phase-purity uncertainty (SS/TS).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Solvothermal/precipitation conditions are mild in temperature and pressure, as shown by CsCuS4 at 423 K for 24 h and K2S5 solution crystallization (SS/TS/CS). Penalties arise from inert handling, multistep polysulfide preparation, and sulfide/H₂S hazards [Reasonable Inference; CS/TS]. The supplied data do not directly show anhydrous calcium polysulfide crystallization from solution, so transferability is uncertain [insufficient direct CaS3 evidence from CS/SS/TS; neutral 50].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Solution chemistry gives excellent ionic mobility and high precursor activity [Reasonable Inference; SS/TS/CS]. However, crystallizing an anhydrous 3D CaS3 phase from solution is less supported than crystallizing multication CsCuS4 or K2S5, and hydrated/oxidized calcium products are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. This weak structural-reconstruction confidence lowers F3 despite mild conditions.

**F2 — Route 4**  
High-pressure sulfurization is strongly supported for SrS2 and ZnS2 analogues, but it carries large equipment and pressure penalties (SS). The proposed 2–6 GPa window is based on SrS2 at 20 kbar and ZnS2 at 60–89 kbar, while TS provides insufficient high-pressure signal for this route and is treated as neutral 50 (SS; insufficient evidence from TS). Sulfur volatility and capsule handling add further severity [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 4**  
High pressure can retain sulfur and promote dense S–S bonded sulfide phases, as shown for SrS2 and ZnS2 (SS). Kinetics should be acceptable at 700–900 °C, but CaS3 requires a different sulfur stoichiometry and structure than SrS2, so structural transfer is incomplete [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best viewed as a fallback if ambient-pressure routes fail [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a meaningful amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied, and CS/SS/TS do not provide that target-specific metric, so that subcheck is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50. Mixed edge/corner-sharing connectivity raises some glass/defect risk, but lack of explicit face-sharing and the existence of crystalline mixed-connectivity analogues keep the risk low–medium (SS/TS; Reasonable Inference).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaS3 is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state candidate because the reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but it remains theory-only with no experimental synthesis identified in the supplied target validation (Target data). The strongest support comes from SS/TS polysulfide analogues that are stable and contain S–S bonds near 2.04–2.12 Å, matching the target’s ~2.08 Å S–S bond length (SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is sulfur-rich chemistry: multiple analogues show that S–S bonded polychalcogenide frameworks can be crystallized by sealed-tube, flux, solution, or high-pressure methods (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase control under sulfur vapor or polysulfide conditions, because analogues report minor secondary phases, air sensitivity, long anneals, or narrow high-pressure windows (CS/SS/TS). Overall, CaS3 appears experimentally plausible but will likely require careful sulfur chemical potential control and inert sealed handling [Reasonable Inference].
