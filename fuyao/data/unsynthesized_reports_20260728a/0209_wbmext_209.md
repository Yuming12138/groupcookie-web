### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li2HfF6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close CS/SS/TS fluoride analogues are also at or near the hull (Target data; CS1–CS4; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from hydrothermal HfF4 + alkali fluoride chemistry and LiF-based hydrothermal fluoride crystallization (CS1; CS3; SS1; TS1). The most critical enabling factor is fluoride mineralizer/solution transport, which should help assemble the Li–Hf–F network without requiring extreme solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS1 and CS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among alkali hafnium fluorides, since close Hf-fluoride analogues form Na5Hf2F13 and RbHfF5 rather than the exact Li2HfF6 target (CS1; SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2). Overall, the target is highly plausible but still needs experimental optimization because the supplied dataset identifies it as theory-only with no known synthesis (Target data).
