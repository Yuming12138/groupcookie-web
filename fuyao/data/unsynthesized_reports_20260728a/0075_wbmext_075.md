### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest same-reduced-formula Cs₂TeO₃ analogue is also reported with E_hull = 0.0, placing the material in the 90–100 band. [Target; CS1; SS1; TS1] I assign 98 rather than 100 because the WBM target is theory-only and the source dataset does not identify an experimental synthesis of this exact relaxed P-3 prototype. [Target] Remaining uncertainty is mainly polymorph-level rather than decomposition-level because the closest analogues are thermodynamically stable. [CS1; SS1; TS1]

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, using standard furnace/Au boat conditions and common Cs₂CO₃ + TeO₂ precursors. [CS1; SS1; TS1] Deductions come from the required Ar atmosphere and the practical toxicity of TeO₂ by [Reasonable Inference]. [CS1; CS2] No sealed volatile-precursor deduction is applied because the direct analogue used gold boats rather than sealed ampoules. [CS1]

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because Cs₂CO₃ + TeO₂ directly form the same reduced composition and the analogue is Krennerite-derived. [CS1; SS1; TS1] The ≥15 h hold implies some ordering/diffusion requirement, so structural reconstruction is scored high but not perfect. [CS1] The main uncertainty is whether the target P-3 polymorph forms instead of the reported P321 analogue. [CS1; Target]

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Melt growth stays below 1000 °C and uses no high pressure, but it requires purified Ar/anhydrous conditions and slow cooling. [CS1; SS3; TS3] A multi-step deduction is applied because melt homogenization, slow cooling, and crystal recovery are more demanding than powder sintering. [CS1; SS3; Reasonable Inference] Air-sensitive precursor risk applies if Cs₂O is selected instead of Cs₂CO₃. [SS3; SS4; TS4]

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The melt route receives the best kinetics score because liquid/flux-like transport greatly reduces diffusion barriers. [CS1; SS3; TS3; Reasonable Inference] Structural transferability is strong because K₂TeO₃ is explicitly P-3 Krennerite-derived and Cs₂TeO₃ crystals are obtained by slow cooling. [SS3; CS1] The uncertainty is composition loss or polymorph selection during melt cooling. [CS1; SS3; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The low-temperature route has mild temperature severity but requires controlled atmosphere and regrinding. [CS2] The major conflict is that the documented low-temperature route uses O₂ to make Cs₂TeO₄, while the target Cs₂TeO₃ is lower oxygen stoichiometry. [CS2; CS1] Therefore, SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence for this exact low-T inert adaptation and are treated as neutral 50 for that route signal. [SS; TS]

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Precursor reactivity is proven by Cs₂TeO₄ formation from the same Cs₂CO₃ + TeO₂ pair, but 72 h plus regrinding indicates slower kinetics. [CS2] Structural reconstruction is only moderate because the proven low-T product is Cs₂TeO₄ rather than Cs₂TeO₃. [CS2; CS1] The route is best viewed as a screening route, not the primary synthesis. [CS2; Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 avoids high temperature and high pressure, but it uses air-sensitive Cs oxides and sealed/dry handling. [SS4; TS4; CS3; SS2; TS2] The severity score is reduced for Ar/sealed conditions, precursor sensitivity, and multistep handling. [SS4; TS4] CS provides only indirect chemical evidence through Cs–Sb–O entries, so Te-specific route evidence from CS is partial rather than direct. [CS3; CS4]

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Reactive alkali oxides give high precursor activity and similar compounds form at moderate temperatures. [SS4; TS4; CS3; SS2; TS2] Structural reconstruction is limited because the strongest examples are Bi/Sb analogues rather than tellurites, and impurities are reported for Cs₃SbO₃. [CS3; SS2; TS2] This route is plausible but lower priority than direct Cs₂CO₃ + TeO₂. [CS1; SS4; Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target has a large effective Cs/Te cation size contrast inferred from much longer Cs–O than Te–O bonds, triggering C1. [Target; CS1; SS1; TS1; Reasonable Inference] No CS/SS/TS entry shows a >40-atom cell or explicit coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing networks, so C2 and C3 are not triggered and source-specific evidence is neutral where absent. [CS1; SS1; TS1] The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because multiple crystalline analogues are experimentally accessible. [CS1; SS1; TS1]
