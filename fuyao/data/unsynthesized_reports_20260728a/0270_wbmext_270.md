## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied \(E_\text{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability category of the requested rubric. **[Target data]** The chemical analogues UIr₃, WIr₃, HfPt₃, and TaPd₂ also have reported \(E_\text{hull}=0\), while Ta₉Co₂S₆ is only 0.005 eV/atom above hull. **[CS1; CS2; CS4; CS5; SS3; SS5; TS4; TS5]** The principal uncertainty is that the target stability is computational and the supplied validation identifies it as theory-only rather than experimentally synthesized. **[Target data]**

### F2 — Route 1

Arc melting is performed at high temperature in the relevant CS, SS, and TS examples, commonly with subsequent annealing or homogenization. **[CS1–CS3; CS5; SS3; SS5; TS4; TS5]** The route is penalized for temperatures above 1400 °C and for its multistep processing, but not for high pressure, volatile target components, or a reactive atmosphere. **[CS; SS; TS]** The uncertainty is the absence of a directly reported Ta₃Pt experimental recipe. **[Target data]**

### F2 — Route 2

Vacuum sintering and post-annealing are supported by HfPt₃ and TaPd₂, with temperatures around 1100–1600 °C and vacuum conditions. **[CS4; CS5]** High vacuum and the need for pressing, sintering, possible regrinding, and annealing make this route more operationally demanding than a one-pass reaction. **[CS4; CS5; SS1; TS1]** The uncertainty is whether solid-state diffusion is sufficient to produce the ordered Pm-3n phase without a liquid or partial-melt step. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F2 — Route 3

A sealed Ta capsule is directly used for a Ta-containing high-temperature material in SS2/TS2. The target does not contain sulfur or another obviously volatile component, so sealing is not required for volatile-anion retention, but it can improve atmospheric control. **[SS2; TS2; Reasonable Inference]** The route is therefore scored as severe because of high temperature, vacuum/sealed processing, and additional capsule-handling steps. **[SS2; TS2]**

### F3 — Route 1

Ta and Pt are dense refractory elemental precursors, so their precursor activity is lower than that of nitrates, acetates, or oxide nanopowders. **[Reasonable Inference]** Arc melting creates a liquid state that should accelerate mixing, and related intermetallics are obtained by arc melting, but the high melting point of Ta and the need for homogenization reduce the kinetic score. **[CS1–CS3; CS5; SS3; SS5; TS4; TS5]** The target’s exact ordering pathway remains uncertain because none of the cited analogues has the same Ta₃Pt composition and Pm-3n structure. **[CS; SS; TS]**

### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state Ta–Pt interdiffusion is expected to be slow because both components are refractory metals, and no flux-mediated liquid mechanism is established for this route. **[Reasonable Inference]** CS4 demonstrates that Pt-containing refractory intermetallics can be formed by vacuum sintering, but it also reports multiphase products and additional annealing for phase purification. **[CS4]** SS1/TS1 support cold pressing and vacuum annealing of Ta-containing powders but do not directly establish rapid formation of Ta₃Pt. **[SS1; TS1]**

### F3 — Route 3

The sealed capsule allows a prolonged high-temperature reaction and may reduce environmental contamination, but it does not intrinsically eliminate the refractory-metal diffusion problem. **[Reasonable Inference]** SS2/TS2 show that Ta-containing materials can be prepared in sealed Ta capsules at high temperature, although their sulfur-containing chemistry is not directly transferable to Ta₃Pt. **[SS2; TS2]** Consequently, this route receives a slightly better kinetic score than conventional sintering but remains below arc melting. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has mixed face- and edge-sharing PtTa₁₂ cuboctahedral connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity check is triggered. **[Target data]** Ionic-radius mismatch is not directly applicable to this metallic intermetallic, and the unit-cell atom count is not supplied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is therefore available for those two checks, and both are treated as neutral. **[CS; SS; TS]** The resulting F4 score indicates low–medium amorphization risk rather than high risk. **[Target data; CS; SS; TS]**
