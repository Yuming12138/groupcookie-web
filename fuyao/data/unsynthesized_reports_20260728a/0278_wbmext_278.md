### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom in the supplied target record, which places it in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin. The thermodynamic analogue set is strongly supportive because all five TS examples are listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and several CS examples are also at or essentially at hull stability (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5; TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target is WBM theory-only and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct experimental TiIrSb synthesis evidence, so direct target precedent is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and treated as neutral direct-precedent signal = 50.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids a melt step above 1400°C and follows a 950–1000°C sealed-tube antimonide protocol, which reduces severity relative to arc/induction melting (SS5, TS5). Deductions remain for vacuum/sealed-tube handling, Sb volatility, multi-step grinding/pelletizing/sealing, and toxic/air-sensitive precursor handling (SS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). The key uncertainty is whether Ti–Ir diffusion is fast enough in 24–72 h, since related Ir systems often use longer anneals (SS2, TS2; SS4, TS4).

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are mixed: elemental powders are not highly reactive, but the Sb-containing half-Heusler analogue is directly synthesized from elemental mixtures in sealed tubes (SS5, TS5). The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: NbRuSb has the same F-43m half-Heusler motif, lowering the reconstruction penalty (SS5, TS5). The main uncertainty is refractory Ir/Ti homogenization, which may require regrinding or a Ti–Ir master alloy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Arc melting is well supported for equiatomic Ir intermetallic analogues, but it triggers the high-temperature deductions because Ti/Ir melting requires very high local temperature [Reasonable Inference] (CS3; SS2, TS2; SS4, TS4). Controlled Ar/vacuum annealing is also required, and Sb volatility adds sealed-containment risk (SS5, TS5). The severity score is therefore lower even though the method is experimentally common.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Arc melting improves precursor contact and bulk homogenization, which is valuable for refractory Ti/Ir chemistry (CS3; SS2, TS2). However, long anneals in analogous Ir systems show that ordering and phase equilibration can still be slow (SS2, TS2; SS4, TS4). Structural uncertainty remains because several chemically close Ti–Ir–Ge/Si analogues prefer Pnma rather than F-43m (CS1; SS4, TS4).

**F2 — Route 3**  
Induction levitation plus vacuum anneal is directly supported for TiIrGe/ZrOsGe-type analogues, but it still requires high-temperature melting, purified Ar, and vacuum annealing (CS1, CS5; SS1, TS1). Sb volatility is not directly tested in the induction-melt analogues, so the sealed-containment requirement is a [Reasonable Inference] supported indirectly by Sb half-Heusler sealed-tube routes (SS5, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for induction growth of TiIrSb specifically, so target-specific route confidence is neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 3**  
The melt step should improve reactivity and diffusion relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], and the 950°C/1–2 week anneal is well precedented for TiIrGe-type compounds (CS1; SS1, TS1). The structural penalty is larger than Route 1 because TiIrGe and TiIrSi are orthorhombic Pnma, not F-43m half-Heusler (CS1; SS4, TS4). The route is plausible but less phase-selective than the direct antimonide half-Heusler precedent.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The best structural evidence points to low amorphization risk because the target is a small 1:1:1 half-Heusler and NbRuSb is an experimentally supported F-43m analogue with the same face/corner-sharing coordination motif (SS5, TS5). The mixed-connectivity check is not triggered because edge-sharing coexistence is not stated for the target-like analogue (SS5, TS5). CS gives insufficient direct evidence for target amorphization behavior because its closest Ti–Ir analogues are mostly Pnma germanides/silicides rather than F-43m antimonides, so CS is neutral = 50 for this sub-factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiIrSb is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target record gives E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, and the TS analogue set is dominated by on-hull intermetallics (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is favorable but indirect: the strongest support comes from NbRuSb, which is the same half-Heusler F-43m structure and is made from elemental metals plus Sb in evacuated silica tubes (SS5, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a direct sealed-tube antimonide half-Heusler route at ~1000°C (SS5, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/stoichiometric control for refractory Ti–Ir chemistry combined with volatile Sb, which is inferred from long anneals in Ir analogues and sealed-tube Sb handling requirements (SS2, TS2; SS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, TiIrSb is a credible synthesis target, with the best first attempt being sealed-tube solid-state synthesis followed by phase-check-driven regrinding/annealing.
