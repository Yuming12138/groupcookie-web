### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
CeIN is supplied as E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. [Target] CS, SS, and TS analogues are also overwhelmingly at or near E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so none of the three evidence classes is thermodynamically discouraging. [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5] Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and not experimentally validated. [Target]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 receives a moderate severity score because the temperature window is not extreme, but NH₃ atmosphere, moisture exclusion, and multi-step precursor handling are required. [SS-3, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5] CS gives insufficient evidence for ammonolysis and is treated as neutral 50 for this route. The main uncertainty is whether a practical Ce(IV) iodide precursor can survive ammonolysis without redox decomposition. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable because exact ThNI-type nitride iodides form in hours at 650–850 °C, implying an effective chemical conversion pathway rather than only slow bulk diffusion. [SS-3, TS-3] The target and ThNI share the P4/nmm layered nitride iodide framework, so structural reconstruction is expected to be limited. [SS-3, TS-3] The uncertainty is chemical rather than structural: Ce iodide oxidation state control may be narrower than Th iodide chemistry. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile I₂, toxic/air-sensitive precursors, and likely multi-step annealing. [CS-3, CS-4, CS-5] SS and TS do not directly support CeN + I₂ solid-state conversion and are scored neutral 50 for this route. The route is operationally simple but chemically harsh due to iodine vapor and nitride reactivity mismatch. [CS-4; Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics are only moderate because CS analogues require long high-temperature anneals of several days to 10 days. [CS-3, CS-4, CS-5] Reconstructing CeN plus iodine into layered CeIN is likely slower than forming sulfide iodides from elemental sulfur and lanthanide metal. [Reasonable Inference] The route is plausible but risks residual CeN, CeI₃, or iodine-deficient products. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has significant handling severity because it uses azides, glovebox loading, refractory metal sealing, vacuum containment, and long controlled thermal treatment. [SS-1, TS-1] CS does not provide useful azide-flux evidence and is scored neutral 50 for this route. The temperature itself is moderate at 900 °C, so the penalty is driven mainly by containment and precursor hazards. [SS-1, TS-1]

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux/azide chemistry is kinetically attractive because Ce₂SeN₂ forms with Ce–N tetrahedra and similar Ce–N distances under flux conditions. [SS-1, TS-1] Slow cooling and a 120 h hold show that crystal growth is feasible but not fast. [SS-1, TS-1] The structural match is incomplete because Ce₂SeN₂ is not the same P4/nmm CeNI framework. [SS-1, TS-1]

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 has moderate-to-high severity due to iodine vapor transport, sealed-tube handling, and likely need for pre-reacted feed. [CS-2; Reasonable Inference] SS and TS provide insufficient direct CVT evidence for Ce–N–I and are scored neutral 50. The temperature is not beyond the 1000 °C penalty threshold, but composition control is demanding. [CS-2; Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 4**  
CVT kinetics are weakly supported because iodine transport is shown for a chemically similar rare-earth system, but not for Ce–N–I. [CS-2] The likely need to first synthesize CeIN feed lowers the route’s value as a primary synthesis pathway. [Reasonable Inference] Nitrogen retention and iodine volatility are the largest kinetic/composition uncertainties. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has a simple single-cation P4/nmm layered framework, so cation-radius disorder and large-cell complexity are not expected to drive amorphization. [Target, SS-3, TS-3] The reported connectivity is edge/corner sharing without face-sharing coexistence, which keeps the amorphization trigger count at zero. [Target, SS-3, TS-3] Remaining uncertainty is that WBM reports 3D periodic connectivity while the local description emphasizes layered sheets, but neither description implies amorphous-prone complexity. [Target]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeIN is likely a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and the closest CS/SS/TS analogues are also mostly on the hull. [Target, CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5] The strongest synthetic support comes from ThNI, which has the same P4/nmm nitride iodide structure and is made by ammonolysis. [SS-3, TS-3] The most critical enabling factor is the exact structural precedent for a layered MNI Matlockite phase. [SS-3, TS-3] The most critical limiting factor is Ce oxidation-state and iodide compatibility, because CeIN formally resembles a Ce(IV) nitride iodide and Ce(IV)–iodide chemistry may be redox-sensitive. [Reasonable Inference] Overall, the target is promising but should be screened under tightly controlled dry NH₃/iodide chemical potentials rather than treated as a routine sealed-ampoule product. [SS-3, TS-3, CS-4; Reasonable Inference]
