### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s reported E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom places it in the 90–100 range, and this is reinforced by Be5Co at ~0.002 eV/atom and Be17Nb2/Zr2Be17 at 0.0 eV/atom in similar Be-rich intermetallic spaces (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1; SS#3; SS#5; TS#2; TS#3). CS also contains Be12Pd/Ag/Pt/Au analogues with higher hull energies of ~0.079–0.119 eV/atom, so CS support is positive but slightly mixed (CS#2–#5). Remaining uncertainty is experimental: the target is theory-only, and no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports Be5Rh synthesis.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C, possible >1400 °C operation, Ar control, regrind/reheat, and Be toxicity (CS#1; SS#5; TS#3; TS#4). It is still preferred because it uses standard solid-state operations already demonstrated for the closest Be5Co analogue (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact Be5Rh dwell time means the 12–24 h hold is [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Rh is expected to diffuse slowly, but the Be-rich composition and high-temperature pellet route are strongly supported by Be5Co and related beryllides (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1; SS#3; TS#2). The structural reconstruction score is high because Be5Co has the same cubic F-43m Be5M framework (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The main uncertainty is whether Rh stabilizes the same ordered site occupancy without forming competing Rh–Be phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 is harsher because arc melting requires high temperature, He/Ar control, repeated remelting, and Be volatility/toxicity management (CS#2–#5; SS#2; SS#4; TS#5). The method is directly demonstrated for precious-metal beryllides, but those analogues report phase-purity and homogeneity difficulties (CS#2–#5). Insufficient evidence from TS for arc-melting Be5Rh specifically is neutral 50 for route specificity.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Liquid-state homogenization improves diffusion relative to ordinary solid-state sintering, which is supported by repeated melting routes for Be12Pd/Ag/Pt/Au (CS#2–#5; SS#2; SS#4). Structural transfer is only moderate because the arc-melt analogues are mainly Be12M tetragonal phases, not cubic Be5M (CS#2–#5; SS#2; SS#4). The largest uncertainty is whether rapid solidification from melt favors Be5Rh or competing Be–Rh compounds [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized for 1200–1450 °C operation, vacuum, Be-loss risk, and Be toxicity (SS#3; TS#2; TS#5). It avoids repeated grinding if the induction reaction is complete, so it receives no multi-step penalty based on the Be17Nb2 single-step precedent (SS#3; TS#2). Insufficient evidence from CS for vacuum induction synthesis is neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 3**  
The Be17Nb2 precedent shows Be-rich intermetallics can form rapidly under vacuum induction despite refractory metal chemistry (SS#3; TS#2). Kinetics are still limited by elemental Rh diffusion and the lack of a same-structure vacuum-synthesized Be5M analogue [Reasonable Inference]. Structural support is moderate because Nb/Zr Be-rich frameworks are similar but not F-43m Be5M (SS#3; SS#5; TS#2; TS#3).

**F2 — Route 4**  
Flux growth reduces peak temperature relative to arc melting but adds sealed containment, slow cooling, flux separation, and Be toxicity penalties [Reasonable Inference] (TS#5). TS supports Al flux only for UBe13, not Be5Rh, so CS and SS are insufficient for this route and scored neutral 50. The route remains plausible but experimentally less direct than Route 1.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Al flux can improve mobility and crystallinity, as shown for UBe13 crystals from slow cooling (TS#5). However, the structural reconstruction score is limited because no CS/SS/TS entry demonstrates flux growth of the Be5M F-43m prototype; CS and SS are neutral 50 for route-specific transfer. Al incorporation and Be5Rh composition control remain major uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Two triggers are applied: significant Be/Rh size mismatch by metallic-radius reasoning and mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing by analogy to Be5Co (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1; [Reasonable Inference]). The unit-cell-size trigger is not applied because the Be5Co F-43m analogue suggests a relatively compact prototype, but CS/SS/TS do not explicitly provide the target atom count, so that sub-check includes uncertainty (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The final F4 = 60 reflects medium amorphization/ordering risk, partly offset by many crystalline Be-rich analogues in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Be5Rh is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom and close Be-rich analogues also show near-zero hull energies (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1; SS#3; TS#2). The strongest synthesis support is the Be5Co analogue, which has the same cubic F-43m Be5M structural motif and was experimentally accessible by solid-state sintering under Ar (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a close Be5M prototype with very high chemical, structural, and thermodynamic similarity (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in Be-rich intermetallic chemistry, because several analogues report secondary phases, inhomogeneity, or repeated processing needs (CS#1; CS#2–#5; SS#5; TS#3). A secondary limiting factor is safe handling of Be powder/fumes, which materially complicates all routes (TS#5; [Reasonable Inference]).
