### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

**Source-use note:** CS1–CS3 and CS5 are the strongest chemical-route evidence because they describe rare-earth–Mg–Pd/Pt intermetallics made from elements in sealed Ta tubes under Ar; CS4 is internally mismatched because its MBE details describe Ga/As/Si rather than Mg–Ce–Pt, so **insufficient evidence from CS4** for MgYIr synthesis and it is treated as neutral support = 50. SS and TS strongly support Ir-containing intermetallic formation by arc melting / sealed ampoule routes, but **insufficient evidence from SS/TS** for Mg volatility control specifically, so Mg handling is inferred from CS and [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest CS analogues are all on-hull, which supports a high thermodynamic score. (Target record; CS1–CS5) SS/TS also support stability because most Ir-containing analogues are on-hull and SS1 remains within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score band. (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5) Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic because the target is a theory-only WBM candidate without reported synthesis in the provided data. (Target record)

**F2 — Route 1:** Route 1 is penalized for >1000 °C operation, sealed Ar/Ta handling, Mg volatility, multistep processing, and air-sensitive Mg/Y handling. (CS1–CS5; [Reasonable Inference]) It is not penalized for >1400 °C or high pressure because the cited Mg–RE–Pd/Pt syntheses use ~1200 °C / 1500 K-class conditions and no high-pressure apparatus. (CS1–CS5)

**F3 — Route 1:** Kinetics are moderate because elemental metals are dense precursors and Ir is expected to diffuse more slowly than Pd/Pt. (CS1–CS5; SS2, TS1; [Reasonable Inference]) The structural-reconstruction score is high because MgErPd and GdPdMg share the target’s P-62m equiatomic topology and MgT4 / RE–T coordination environment. (CS2, CS3)

**F2 — Route 2:** Route 2 receives the harshest condition score because arc melting implies very high local temperature and still requires a second sealed Mg anneal. (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4; CS1–CS3) The benefit is that Y–Ir bonding is directly supported by arc-melted Y–Ir analogues. (SS2, TS1)

**F3 — Route 2:** Arc melting improves Y–Ir homogenization and reduces the Ir diffusion barrier, which improves kinetics relative to direct solid-state reaction. (SS2, TS1; [Reasonable Inference]) The route loses points because adding Mg after pre-alloying creates a separate stoichiometry-control and diffusion problem. (CS1–CS3; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3:** Route 3 avoids arc-melting extremes but still uses >1000 °C sealed ampoule conditions, Mg containment, and long multistep annealing. (SS3, TS2; CS2, CS3) No high-pressure penalty is applied because the analogues use sealed ampoules/tubes rather than >5 GPa methods. (SS3, TS2; CS2, CS3)

**F3 — Route 3:** Direct solid-state kinetics are the weakest among bulk routes because dense Ir/Y/Mg precursors must interdiffuse over long distances. (SS3, TS2; [Reasonable Inference]) The route is still plausible because exact Mg–RE–Pd analogues and Ir ampoule analogues form under long anneals. (CS2, CS3; SS3, TS2)

**F2/F3 — Route 4:** Thin-film conditions avoid high bulk synthesis temperature, but high vacuum/Ar plasma, Mg volatility, capping, and composition calibration still make the route challenging. (CS4; [Reasonable Inference]) Because CS4’s MBE evidence is chemically unrelated and SS/TS provide no thin-film MgYIr analogue, the crystallization and composition-control evidence is weak and treated largely as neutral/extrapolated. (CS4 insufficient; SS/TS insufficient)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** The radius-mismatch check is triggered by [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius data and are neutral for that sub-check. The compact P-62m MgRE-Pd analogues and the absence of described face-sharing reduce amorphization concern, so only one of three checks is triggered. (CS2, CS3)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgYIr is most likely a **thermodynamic ground-state candidate** rather than a strongly metastable target because the provided target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the CS/TS analogues are also on-hull. (Target record; CS1–CS5; TS1–TS5) The strongest synthesis support comes from MgErPd and GdPdMg, which match the target’s P-62m equiatomic Mg–RE–T framework and local tetrahedral/9-coordinate motifs. (CS2, CS3) The most critical enabling factor is sealed Ta-tube inert-atmosphere synthesis, which directly addresses Mg retention and rare-earth oxidation while matching the successful analogue protocols. (CS1–CS5) The most critical limiting factor is replacing Pd/Pt with refractory Ir, which likely slows diffusion and may require longer annealing or pre-alloying. (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS4; [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the material is **moderately likely to be synthesizable** as a bulk intermetallic, with Route 1 preferred. (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5)
