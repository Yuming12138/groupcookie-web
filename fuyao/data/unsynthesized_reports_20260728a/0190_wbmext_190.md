### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Ti2CSe is predicted on the hull at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which supports a high thermodynamic score. (Target data) CS analogues are also essentially stable with E_hull values of 0–0.0006 eV/atom, and SS/TS analogues are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. (CS, SS, TS) Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and not experimentally validated in the provided data. (Target data)

**F2 — Route 1 Condition Severity**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C synthesis because H-phase Ti–C analogues are reported at 500–900 °C or ~850 °C in sealed tubes/ampoules. (CS, SS, TS) Deductions are driven by the need for controlled sealed-tube conditions, Se volatility, multi-step prereaction/anneal/regrind handling, and Se/Ti powder safety. (SS, TS; Target data; [Reasonable Inference]) CS/SS/TS give useful condition signals for this route, so no source is neutral for the main condition window. (CS, SS, TS)

**F3 — Route 1 Reaction Kinetics**  
The main kinetic penalty is solid-state diffusion through TiC/Ti powders, while the main kinetic advantage is that H-phase analogues form at temperatures far below refractory-metal melting points, suggesting soft A-site transport can assist phase formation. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Long 500–900 h anneals in H-phase analogues indicate slow ordering and justify a mid-range F3 score. (CS, SS, TS) The uncertainty is whether Se behaves like the Ga/Pb/Tl analogue species or instead forms competing Ti–Se binaries. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2 Condition Severity**  
CS supports arc-melting plus annealing for chemically similar carbides, but arc temperatures and multiple processing steps impose large deductions. (CS) SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for arc-melting condition severity and are scored neutral 50 for the arc step, although they support the later H-phase annealing concept. (SS, TS) Se volatility makes direct arc use of the complete Ti–C–Se charge risky. [Reasonable Inference]

**F3 — Route 2 Reaction Kinetics**  
Arc-melting can improve Ti–C homogenization and reduce diffusion length, which supports a better kinetics score than direct powder reaction for the refractory sublattice. (CS; [Reasonable Inference]) However, CS validates Sc–Cr–C carbides rather than Se-containing Ti phases, while SS/TS provide structural but not arc-kinetic evidence. (CS, SS, TS) The largest uncertainty is maintaining Se stoichiometry after high-temperature pre-alloying. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 3 Condition Severity**  
The topochemical analogue uses Ti2AlC at 1500 °C under Ar, but the proposed Se-exchange route must be lowered to 700–900 °C to avoid Se loss. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) The severity score is penalized by sealed-vapor conditions, volatile/toxic Se, and likely repeated exchange cycles. [Reasonable Inference] CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for actual Se exchange and are neutral 50 for that mechanism. (CS, SS, TS)

**F3 — Route 3 Reaction Kinetics**  
A Ti2AlC or Ti2GaC precursor could preserve Ti–C slabs and reduce reconstruction, which is favorable. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) The analogue topochemical Ti5Al2C3 route produced a phase mixture, so phase-pure transformation is not assured. (CS, SS, TS) The main uncertainty is whether Al/Ga can be replaced by Se without decomposing the Ti–C framework. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has only one metal cation, so cation-radius disorder is not expected to drive amorphization; CS/SS/TS do not provide direct radius data, so that direct sub-check has neutral source signal 50. (Target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]) The H-phase analogues are small-cell layered structures, so the >40 atom cell trigger is unlikely. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) Mixed corner/edge/face-sharing connectivity is present in the target and related analogues, giving one amorphization trigger and a low–medium risk score. (Target data; CS, SS, TS)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2CSe is likely a predicted ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, but it remains experimentally unconfirmed in the supplied data. (Target data) The analogue support is good because chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar H-phase Ti–C compounds such as Ti2GaC, Ti2PbC, and Ti2TlC are reported as synthesizable and on-hull. (CS, SS, TS) The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ti–C H-phase framework, since multiple analogues retain Ti–C octahedral layers with similar Ti–C bond lengths and stable hull energies. (CS, SS, TS; Target data) The most critical limiting factor is Se control, because CS/SS/TS do not provide direct Ti–C–Se synthesis precedent and Se volatility/toxicity must be managed by sealed-ampoule chemistry. (insufficient direct CS/SS/TS Ti–C–Se evidence; Target data; [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the material is **moderately synthesizable**, with the best chance via long-duration sealed solid-state reaction rather than open high-temperature melting. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])
