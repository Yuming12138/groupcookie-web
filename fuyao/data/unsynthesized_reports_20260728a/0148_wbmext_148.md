## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

DyMoN₃ receives a high thermodynamic score because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and most chemical/structural/thermodynamic analogues are also on or near the hull. [CS-1–CS-5; SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5] The strongest source signal is TS because all five TS analogues have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. [TS-1–TS-5] The main uncertainty is that SS includes a structurally similar W₂N₃ outlier at 0.340 eV/atom, so structural similarity alone is not sufficient proof of synthesizability. [SS-4]

### F2 — Condition Severity

Route 1 scores moderately because Li₃N flux avoids >1400 °C conditions but still requires >1000 °C, sealed ampoules, air-free handling, and multi-step flux processing. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; TS-5] Route 2 scores slightly lower because it uses hazardous Na/NaN₃, sealed Nb tubes, slow cooling, and vacuum Na removal, although its 900 °C temperature is milder. [CS-2; SS-1; TS-1] Route 3 has similar condition severity to Route 1 but worse kinetics because it relies on long solid-state nitride anneals. [SS-3, SS-5; TS-3, TS-4] Route 4 is most severe because the only supporting route requires multianvil high-pressure synthesis at ~5 GPa and high temperature. [SS-4]

### F3 — Reaction Kinetics

Route 1 has the best kinetics because molten/ionic Li₃N flux improves transport and nitrogen activity, while Mo refractory diffusion remains a penalty [Reasonable Inference]. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5] Route 2 benefits from reactive Na/Ga/NaN₃ flux, but the La₃Cr₂N₆ analogue’s low yield and competing phases indicate a narrow kinetic/phase window. [CS-2; SS-1; TS-1] Route 3 is kinetically weak because binary-nitride solid-state reactions require long anneals, repeated processing, and show impurity problems in SS/TS analogues. [SS-3, SS-5; TS-3, TS-4, TS-5] Route 4 may accelerate dense nitride formation under pressure, but insufficient evidence from CS and TS supports rare-earth molybdenum nitride formation by this path, so that transferability remains neutral/uncertain. [SS-4]

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The main amorphization risk is cation size/coordination mismatch, because Dy-like large cations and transition-metal nitride polyhedra show very different bond-length scales in the analogues; applying this to Dy/Mo is [Reasonable Inference]. [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes the target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is neutral 50 and not triggered. The strict C3 face-sharing trigger is not applied because target-style corner/edge connectivity is complex but the evidence provided does not require vertex + edge + face coexist for DyMoN₃. [CS-2; SS-1; TS-1] The resulting low–medium risk is consistent with the fact that several analogues crystallize despite distorted polyhedra and mixed connectivity. [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3]

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

DyMoN₃ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state DFT phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues are all zero-hull phases. [TS-1–TS-5] The analogue support is strongest for flux synthesis because rare-earth/transition-metal nitrides are repeatedly synthesized by Li₃N, Ga, and Na/Ga flux routes. [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5] The most important enabling factor is a reactive nitride flux that supplies nitrogen activity and accelerates cation transport below refractory-metal melting temperatures. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5] The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity, because La₃Cr₂N₆ forms only in small amounts with competing phases and Nb/Ta analogues show nonstoichiometry or reproducibility sensitivity. [CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-1; TS-1] Overall, the material is **plausibly synthesizable but experimentally challenging**, with the best first attempt being Li₃N-flux growth in sealed Ta/Nb ampoules. [CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-2; TS-2]
