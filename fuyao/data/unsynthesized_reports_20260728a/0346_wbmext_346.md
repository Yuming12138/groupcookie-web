### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 band, and CS/SS/TS analogues mostly report E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. (CS, SS, TS; target-supplied data) The one CS near-hull CeNiSn entry at 0.0488 eV/atom still remains within the most favorable rubric bin. (CS) Remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic, because CS/SS/TS do not report direct CeFeSn synthesis. (CS, SS, TS)

**F2 — Route 1.** Direct arc melting is well supported by CS/SS/TS, but the route requires controlled Ar/vacuum handling, Sn retention, repeated remelting, and long annealing. (CS, SS, TS) The main severity deductions come from controlled atmosphere, sealed Sn handling, multistep processing, and Ce air sensitivity. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 2.** Master-alloy synthesis keeps ambient pressure and sub-1000 °C sustained annealing while adding preparative steps. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]) The score is higher than Route 1 because master alloys should reduce reactivity mismatch without using HPHT, but it still requires vacuum/gettered annealing and Sn control. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3.** Flux-assisted synthesis is gentler in temperature but less directly supported, because insufficient evidence from CS, insufficient evidence from SS, and insufficient evidence from TS exists for actual Sn-flux growth in the listed analogues; direct source signal is neutral 50. The condition score therefore relies on [Reasonable Inference] from Sn-containing intermetallic chemistry and sealed-ampoule practice. (CS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 4.** HPHT has direct CS precedent but is severe due to 7.5–11.5 GPa and 1100–1200 °C treatment. (CS) SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for HPHT transferability and are neutral 50 for this route. The route is best treated as a backup if ambient-pressure routes produce competing phases. (CS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1.** Dense elemental metals imply moderate-low precursor activity, but arc melting and long annealing improve mixing. (CS, SS, TS) Structural reconstruction is moderate because SS/TS contain related Ce intermetallic motifs, yet no exact CeFeSn analogue is given. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 2.** Master alloys improve precursor contact relative to direct elemental buttons, which should help Fe incorporation. (CS; [Reasonable Inference]) Long homogenization is strongly supported by SS/TS Ce intermetallic analogues, but the exact monoclinic CeFeSn prototype remains unvalidated experimentally. (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 3.** A Sn-rich liquid medium should accelerate diffusion and crystal growth, but no direct CS/SS/TS flux synthesis is reported. (CS; [Reasonable Inference]) Phase selectivity is the main kinetic uncertainty because CS reports CeCuSn phase sensitivity. (CS)

**F3 — Route 4.** HPHT improves diffusion through high temperature and pressure, but CS also shows phase-control complexity in high-pressure rare-earth stannides. (CS) Because SS/TS do not provide HPHT examples, their HPHT kinetic signal is insufficient and neutral 50. (SS, TS)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** One amorphization trigger is assigned for likely Ce–Fe/Sn size mismatch, but this is a [Reasonable Inference] because CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii. The unit-cell atom count and full vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence are not established for the target from CS/SS/TS, so those checks are treated as not triggered with neutral 50 source signal. (CS, SS, TS) The crystalline success of many CS/SS/TS analogues argues against severe amorphization risk under annealed bulk conditions. (CS, SS, TS)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CeFeSn is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state candidate because the target is supplied with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and CS/SS/TS contain many on-hull Ce/rare-earth intermetallic analogues. (CS, SS, TS; target-supplied data) The analogue support for synthesis is strongest for ambient-pressure arc melting followed by long annealing, since that route recurs across CS, SS, and TS. (CS, SS, TS) The most critical enabling factor is that rare-earth–transition-metal–p-block intermetallics tolerate similar coordination environments and can be homogenized by melt/anneal processing. (CS, SS, TS) The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because CS reports polymorph sensitivity in CeCuSn and SS/TS report long anneals or difficulty obtaining pure phases in Ce–Ru–In/Al systems. (CS, SS, TS) Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable but should be approached as a phase-purity optimization problem rather than a simple one-shot reaction. (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference])
