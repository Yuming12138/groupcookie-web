**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids >1000°C and high pressure because close analogues use 823–1150 K sealed reactions rather than high-pressure methods [CS1; CS3; SS1/TS1; SS4/TS4]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaZnSb is most likely a DFT ground-state candidate rather than a high-energy metastable phase because the target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom** and the TS analogue set is hull-stable [Target; TS1–TS5]. The strongest synthesis support is the chemically close **Pnma NaCdAs** analogue, plus multiple Na-containing pnictides made by sealed elemental reactions [CS1; CS3; SS4/TS4]. The critical enabling factor is sealed-tube inert-atmosphere chemistry, which repeatedly stabilizes alkali-metal pnictides from elemental precursors [CS1; CS3; SS1/TS1]. The critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under volatile and air-sensitive alkali-metal conditions, especially because related Zn-pnictide systems show impurities, poor crystallinity, or Na-analogue difficulty [SS2/TS2; SS5/TS5]. Overall, NaZnSb should be considered **reasonably synthesizable**, with direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction as the first route to screen [CS1; CS3; SS1/TS1].
