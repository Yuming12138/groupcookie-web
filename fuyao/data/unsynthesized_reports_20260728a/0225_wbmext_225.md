### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range by the requested rubric. [Target data] The closest analogue Cu3VSe4 is also E_hull = 0.0 in CS/SS/TS, which supports thermodynamic plausibility for a Cu3MSe4 sulvanite framework. [CS1, SS1, TS1] Remaining uncertainty is that the WBM target is theory-only and not experimentally validated in the provided data. [Target data]

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions because Cu3VSe4 was annealed at 873 K, but it requires vacuum sealing and selenium containment. [CS1, SS1, TS1] The long 6-week duration raises practical difficulty but is not itself a listed F2 deduction except insofar as it indicates controlled processing. [CS1, SS1, TS1] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available on Ti-specific ampoule compatibility, so that uncertainty is treated as a [Reasonable Inference] rather than an extra formal deduction.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The same sulvanite P-43m analogue gives strong structural-kinetic support because only V is replaced by Ti in the Cu3MSe4 framework. [CS1, SS1, TS1] The main kinetic penalty is use of elemental powders and long annealing, which indicates slow diffusion/ordering. [CS1, SS1, TS1] Insufficient direct Ti diffusion evidence from CS/SS/TS remains, so Ti-related kinetic risk is neutral-to-moderate rather than severe.

**F2 — Route 2**  
The melt–anneal route is penalized by high temperature, vacuum sealing, selenium volatility, and multiple thermal steps, all seen across CuFeSe2, CuSe, and Cu5Sn2Se7 analogues. [CS2, SS2, TS2, CS5, SS4, TS4] The route is still conventional because the equipment is quartz/fused-quartz ampoules and furnaces rather than high-pressure devices. [CS2, SS2, TS2, CS5, SS4, TS4] The uncertainty is whether TiCu3Se4 survives a melt-like regime without decomposing into binaries, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence.

**F3 — Route 2**  
High-temperature processing can improve diffusion and reaction completion compared with low-temperature dry mixing. [CS2, SS2, TS2, CS5, SS4, TS4] However, these analogues are not the exact sulvanite structure, and CuFeSe2 reports impurity indications while Cu5Sn2Se7 notes phase/crystal-quality challenges. [CS2, CS5, SS4, TS4] Therefore, kinetics are acceptable but phase selectivity is less favorable than Route 1. [CS1/SS1/TS1 vs. CS2/CS5/SS4/TS4]

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux growth avoids >1000 °C but requires reactive flux preparation, inert handling, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and specialized glovebox/furnace workflow. [SS3, TS3] These requirements justify a challenging practical rating despite moderate peak temperature. [SS3, TS3] Insufficient evidence from CS is available for this route, so CS is neutral for Route 3 scoring.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux chemistry likely improves transport because KCu2NbSe4 uses reactive polyselenide flux and slow cooling to access unusual Cu–M–Se structures. [SS3, TS3] The structural match is partial because KCu2NbSe4 contains K and an orthorhombic framework rather than the target alkali-free cubic sulvanite. [SS3, TS3] This gives strong kinetic but moderate phase-selectivity confidence. [SS3, TS3]

**F2 — Route 4**  
Mechanochemical synthesis has moderate condition severity because the Cu2GeSe3 analogue uses N2 milling and only 600 °C post-heating. [CS3] It still requires controlled atmosphere and selenium handling. [CS3] Insufficient evidence from SS/TS is available for mechanochemical sulvanite synthesis, so SS/TS are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Ball milling improves precursor activity and can shorten diffusion paths, as shown for Cu2GeSe3. [CS3] Structural transferability is weaker because the analogue is not the same sulvanite P-43m phase and involves Ge rather than Ti. [CS3] The likely need for longer annealing or repeated cycles for TiCu3Se4 is a [Reasonable Inference] based on the absence of direct Ti mechanochemical evidence.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The small sulvanite-type tetrahedral framework argues against high amorphization risk, unlike large-cell complex quaternaries. [CS1, SS1, TS1] One risk trigger is mixed edge- and corner-sharing tetrahedra, which are explicitly present in the closest analogue and target-like description. [CS1, SS1, TS1] Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for measured glass-forming tendency, so F4 remains an inferred structural-risk score.
