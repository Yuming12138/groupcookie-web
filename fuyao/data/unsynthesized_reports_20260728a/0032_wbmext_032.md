### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₃O is likely a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the top thermodynamic analogue Zr3O is also near-hull (Target data; TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the Zr→Hf chemical transferability within group-IV refractory suboxides [Reasonable Inference], supported by Zr3O and Hf-containing Hf₃AlN/Hf-rich analogues (CS2; SS1; SS5; TS1; TS4). The most critical limiting factor is oxygen stoichiometry control, because too much oxygen favors stable higher oxides while too little oxygen leaves Hf metal [Reasonable Inference], and Zr-rich oxide analogues report phase impurities and repeated remelting needs (SS2; SS3; TS2). Overall, the material looks synthesizable, with best odds via bulk arc melting plus long anneal rather than direct solid-state diffusion or electrochemical routes (CS2; SS1; TS1).
