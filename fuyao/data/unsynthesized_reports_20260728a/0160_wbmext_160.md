### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has supplied E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin. (Target data) The chemical analogues include several stable or near-stable K/Rb transition-metal sulfides, and the structural/thermodynamic analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0. (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5) Remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic, because the target is reported as a theory-only WBM candidate with no source-dataset synthesis record. (Target data)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C operation and avoids high pressure, but it requires a sealed/vacuum or inert ampoule and air-sensitive sulfide precursors. (CS-2, SS-4/TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]) The severity score is therefore reduced mainly for controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur/potassium sulfide handling, and air sensitivity. (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 1**  
Binary sulfides should be more reactive than dense oxide/metal feeds, and K₂S/S chemistry is already demonstrated in a K-containing sulfide system. (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]) The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction because CS/SS/TS analogues share K–S and tetrahedral sulfide motifs but do not report the exact I4/mcm KAlS₂ prototype. (CS-1, SS-4/TS-4, Target data)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperatures but adds vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/polysulfide pressure, glovebox handling, and flux-removal steps. (CS-2) This gives a lower severity score than Route 1 even though the peak temperature is similar. (CS-2)

**F3 — Route 2**  
The K₂S₃ flux route has the best transport kinetics because CS-2 demonstrates K₂S/S flux growth at 750 °C for 6 days. (CS-2) The uncertainty is phase selectivity, since CS-2 explicitly notes difficulty preparing single-phase powder for the analogue. (CS-2)

**F2 — Route 3**  
Direct elemental synthesis is supported by K–Ga–S ampoule synthesis, but it requires volatile/reactive K and S in a sealed ampoule. (SS-4/TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]) The absence of high pressure or >1000 °C operation keeps severity moderate rather than extreme. (SS-4/TS-4)

**F3 — Route 3**  
Elemental K/S reactivity helps initiation, but Al metal passivation can hinder complete sulfidation. [Reasonable Inference] The structural pathway requires complete formation from elements, and there is insufficient exact-prototype evidence from CS/SS/TS, so this route is kinetically less attractive than sulfide or flux routes. (SS-4/TS-4; insufficient exact-prototype evidence from CS/SS/TS, neutral 50)

**F2 — Route 4**  
The H₂S route has direct KMS₂ precedent from KFeS₂ at 1000 K for 4 h. (CS-1) Its severity is limited by toxic/reactive H₂S handling, controlled gas flow, and post-reaction handling under inert conditions. (CS-1)

**F3 — Route 4**  
H₂S provides high sulfur chemical activity, and CS-1 shows fast KFeS₂ formation under these conditions. (CS-1) The transfer to Al is uncertain because Al sulfidation and oxide-passivation behavior differ from Fe sponge chemistry. (CS-1; [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 5**  
The hydrothermal route uses only 450–500 °C but requires kbar autoclave conditions, sulfur-rich mineralizer chemistry, and air/moisture-sensitive sulfide feeds. (CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]) The rubric does not penalize 2.7–3.2 kbar as >5 GPa high pressure, but practical equipment difficulty remains significant. (CS-3)

**F3 — Route 5**  
Mineralizer transport can reduce diffusion barriers, as shown for hydrothermal KFe₂S₃. (CS-3) The main uncertainty is chemical compatibility because Al sulfides can hydrolyze and no SS/TS source gives direct hydrothermal K–Al–S evidence. (CS-3; insufficient evidence from SS/TS, neutral 50; [Reasonable Inference])
