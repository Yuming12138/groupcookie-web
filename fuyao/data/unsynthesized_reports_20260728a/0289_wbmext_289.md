### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin [Target Data]. This is strongly supported by P-62m LuIrSn, TmIrSn, HoIrSn, and ErIrSn all being on-hull in SS/TS, and by chemically similar P-62m ScPtSn being near-hull at 0.0066 eV/atom [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. The main uncertainty is that CS2 gives a high-hull cubic ScPtSn entry, but that entry is a different F-43m polymorph and is weighted less for the P-62m target [CS2; Target Data].

**F2 — Condition Severity**  
Route 1 is penalized for arc melting, evacuated capsule homogenization, and Sn volatility risk, but these conditions are standard for the closest P-62m analogues [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. Route 2 has milder furnace temperatures and no sealed-vacuum requirement in the source reports, but it still requires staged heating and likely brief >1000 °C treatment for Sc compounds [CS2; CS3]. Route 3 is more severe because SS4/TS4 use 1900 K sealed Nb-tube processing, while Route 4 is multi-step because CS5 uses binary alloying, pressing, arc melting, and annealing [SS4; TS4; CS5].

**F3 — Reaction Kinetics**  
Route 1 scores best kinetically because liquid arc melting plus 900 °C homogenization already works for the closest ScPtSn and RIrSn analogues [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. Route 2 scores lower because solid-state diffusion of Ir is expected to be slow and the CS evidence points to half-Heusler products rather than P-62m products [CS2; CS3; Reasonable Inference]. Route 3 has good melt kinetics but uncertain structural selectivity because YIrSn is Pnma, and Route 4 improves precursor contact through prealloying but lacks direct P-62m ScIrSn evidence [SS4; TS4; CS5; Target Data].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target does not appear to have a large unit cell because close P-62m analogues are 3:3:3 formulations, so C2 is not triggered [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. C1 is triggered only by [Reasonable Inference] from Sc versus Ir/Sn size contrast, because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient quantitative ionic-radius evidence and are neutral at 50 for direct radius scoring. C3 is not triggered because the closest P-62m descriptions do not report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity; CS2/CS3 do report such sharing, but those are different half-Heusler structures [CS1–CS3; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScIrSn is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest RIrSn P-62m analogues are also on-hull [Target Data; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. The analogue support is strong because CS1 confirms the same P-62m framework with Sc and Sn, while SS/TS confirm the same P-62m framework with Ir and Sn [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. The most important enabling factor is melt-assisted alloying followed by evacuated 900 °C homogenization, which repeatedly succeeds for the closest analogues [CS1; SS1–SS3; SS5; TS1–TS3; TS5]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity, because related Sc–M–Sn and RIrSn compositions can also form half-Heusler or Pnma polymorphs [CS2; CS3; SS4; TS4]. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not report an experimental ScIrSn synthesis directly, so exact phase-purity conditions remain extrapolated [CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5; Target Data].
