## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied physical \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV/atom and a negative formation energy, which strongly favors thermodynamic accessibility. **[Target validation]** CS analogues also include low-hull Sc chalcogenides, especially Sc\(_4\)Cd\(_2\)Se\(_8\) at 0.0067 eV/atom and Sc\(_4\)Mn\(_2\)S\(_8\) at 0 eV/atom. [CS-1, CS-4] SS and TS provide additional support for stable Sc–chalcogen frameworks through low-hull ScS, YS, and related rare-earth chalcogenides. [SS-1, TS-1, TS-2] The remaining uncertainty is that the target has not been experimentally synthesized. **[Target validation]**

### F2 — Route 1

Direct elemental synthesis is penalized by the need for an evacuated sealed system, the volatility of As and Se, and toxic precursor handling. [Target validation, CS-1, CS-5] Temperatures near or above 1000 °C are consistent with related Sc chalcogenide syntheses. [CS-1, CS-2, SS-2] The nominal process is otherwise relatively simple, so no multi-step deduction is applied.

### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Sc is a refractory component, so solid-state diffusion is expected to be slow; related Sc sulfide and selenide reactions use high temperatures and long holds. [CS-1, CS-2, CS-5] Structural reconstruction is only partial because Sc-centered chalcogen octahedra are common in the analogues, but the target combines As and Se in a different tetragonal network. [SS-1, CS-5] Direct evidence for formation of Sc\(_4\)AsSe\(_3\) by this route is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

### F2 — Route 2

The flux route operates below 1000 °C based on the 850 °C Eu–Sc–Se precedent, avoiding the highest temperature penalty. [CS-5] It still requires vacuum sealing because both Se and the proposed As component are volatile, and the precursors are toxic. [Target validation, CS-5] The method is nominally one reaction step, although flux separation may add practical workup.

### F3 — Route 2

CsBr flux provides the strongest kinetic rationale because it enabled Eu–Sc–Se crystallization at 850 °C for 14 days. [CS-5] This suggests enhanced mass transport and crystal growth relative to a purely dry elemental reaction, although the exact transport chemistry of As is unknown. The mixed-anion target structure is only partially supported by Sc–Se octahedral analogues, so the reconstruction component remains moderate. [CS-5, SS-1]

### F2 — Route 3

CVT requires a controlled evacuated tube and a reactive iodine transport agent, and the proposed process includes source preparation plus transport. [SS-1, CS-4] These requirements increase operational complexity and introduce the risk of As/Se fractionation. **[Reasonable Inference]** The temperature is comparable to established Sc chalcogenide CVT conditions. [SS-1, TS-1]

### F3 — Route 3

I\(_2\)-assisted CVT has strong precedent for transporting Sc chalcogenides and improving crystal quality. [SS-1, CS-4] However, the target contains two anions with potentially different transport equilibria, and no direct As–Se CVT analogue is supplied. [CS/SS/TS insufficient evidence for mixed As/Se transport] Thus, transport kinetics are favorable but composition control is a major uncertainty.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target’s extended corner- and edge-sharing octahedral network is structurally ordered and should support crystalline phase formation. **[Target structure record]** The cation-radius mismatch criterion is not applicable to the single-cation target, while unit-cell atom count is unavailable from CS/SS/TS and is therefore neutral. The mixed connectivity and mixed As/Se sublattice could still produce slow ordering or competing local arrangements. **[Reasonable Inference]**

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most plausibly a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied physical energy above hull is 0 eV/atom and its formation energy is strongly negative. **[Target validation]** CS, SS, and TS analogues provide good support for forming Sc–chalcogen octahedral frameworks by evacuated solid-state, flux, and CVT methods, although none directly validates the mixed As/Se composition. [CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1] The most critical enabling factor is the calculated thermodynamic stability combined with the demonstrated ability of CsBr flux to crystallize Sc–Se frameworks at moderate temperature. [Target validation, CS-5] The most critical limitation is volatile, toxic As/Se chemistry and the possibility of competing Sc–As, Sc–Se, As–Se, or ternary phases. **[Reasonable Inference]** Overall, CsBr-flux synthesis is the best initial route, while direct solid-state synthesis is the simplest backup and CVT is better suited to crystal growth or purification.
