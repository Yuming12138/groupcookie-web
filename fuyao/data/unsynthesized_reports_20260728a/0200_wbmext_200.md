### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket, and TS analogues include several zero-hull chalcogenides [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5]. CS analogues also include multiple zero-hull rare-earth compounds, but they are chemically indirect because they are mainly halide/intermetallic systems rather than Y–Si–Se phases [CS-1, CS-2, CS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental: the WBM candidate is theory-only, so thermodynamic stability does not guarantee an accessible kinetic pathway [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 avoids >1000 °C processing by following 700–950 °C rare-earth/actinide selenide precedents, but it still requires evacuated sealed handling and Se volatility control [TS-3, TS-4]. The multistep prereaction/regrind/reanneal deduction is justified by repeated heating and grinding used in YSe/YTe-type solid-state syntheses [TS-4]. SS-2 and SS-4 provide insufficient evidence for this anhydrous high-T route and are neutral 50 because their hydrothermal organotin chemistry is mismatched [SS-2, SS-4].

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 has similar sealed-vessel and volatile-Se penalties, but it remains below 1000 °C based on CsBr and NaBr flux precedents [CS-3, CS-5]. Flux growth adds operational steps such as flux loading, slow cooling, and separation, so the multistep deduction applies [CS-5]. TS provides insufficient direct halide-flux evidence for Y–Si–Se, so TS is neutral 50 for the flux-specific factor, while CS is weighted most strongly [CS-3, CS-5].

**F2 — Route 3.** CVT operates below 1000 °C in the Dy₃Se₄ analogue, but it requires sealed evacuated tubes and a volatile transport agent [SS-3, SS-5]. The route is penalized for multistep pre-reaction and transport growth, and for likely halogen/Se hazards [SS-3, SS-5; Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient CVT-specific evidence and is neutral 50 for this route [insufficient evidence from CS].

**F2 — Route 4.** Melt/anneal is the harshest route because TS analogues reach 1700 °C and require refractory/vacuum containment [TS-1, TS-2]. The route also inherits sealed-Se, multistep, and toxic/reactive precursor deductions [TS-3, TS-4, CS-1]. It is retained only as a fallback equilibration route because high T may destabilize Se stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.** Direct powders give only moderate precursor activity because Si and Y are refractory relative to the target anneal range [TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion and structural ordering are limiting because the supplied analogues are mostly binary chalcogenides or different rare-earth halides, not direct Y₄SiSe₃ prototypes [TS-4, CS-1, CS-2]. Long anneals in CS analogues support the deduction for slow ordering [CS-1, CS-2, CS-4].

**F3 — Route 2.** Flux improves precursor activity and ion transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, as demonstrated by CsBr-flux EuSc₂Se₄ and NaBr-flux rare-earth crystal growth [CS-3, CS-5]. Structural reconstruction remains moderate because no direct target prototype has been synthesized in the supplied evidence [Reasonable Inference]. This balance makes Route 2 the best kinetic route despite the same F2 severity as Route 1 [CS-3, CS-5].

**F3 — Route 3.** CVT can bypass bulk diffusion and grow crystals under gradients, supported by Dy₃Se₄ and LaSe-containing transport examples [SS-3, SS-5]. However, Y/Si/Se transport stoichiometry is unproven and may fractionate, so precursor activity and structural reconstruction are only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS are insufficient for direct CVT transfer and are neutral 50 for this route-specific kinetic support [insufficient evidence from CS/TS].

**F3 — Route 4.** High T improves diffusion and may equilibrate refractory Si-containing mixtures, which is why melt/anneal remains plausible [TS-1, TS-2; Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic benefit is offset by likely Se loss and possible incongruent decomposition during melt-like processing [TS-3, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. No direct CS/SS/TS example shows Y–Si–Se melt crystallization, so that part is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The target has a compact, ordered octahedral network with corner and edge sharing but no stated face-sharing in the target description, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed face/edge/corner analogues [CS-5, SS-2, SS-3; Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly supplied by CS/SS/TS, so those sources are neutral 50 for the exact C2 check [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The main uncertainty is whether Si should be treated as a second cation for radius-mismatch scoring; under the Y-rich silicide-selenide interpretation, it is not counted as a conventional cation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y₄SiSe₃ is most likely a **thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because its target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom and TS analogues include several zero-hull chalcogenides [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5]. The synthesis support is moderate-to-strong for rare-earth selenide chemistry but indirect for the exact ternary Y–Si–Se prototype, because CS and TS analogues do not report this composition or structure [CS-3, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is sealed flux growth, which combines rare-earth selenide compatibility with improved diffusion/crystal growth below 1000 °C [CS-3, CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, especially Se volatility and possible Y–Se/Y–Si binary formation [TS-3, TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is feasible but not routine, with the best practical path being a CsBr/NaBr flux screen followed by solid-state backup anneals [CS-3, CS-5, TS-4].
