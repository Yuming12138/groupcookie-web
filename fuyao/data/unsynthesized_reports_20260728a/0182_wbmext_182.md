### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
SrPdF6 is scored very high because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest CaPdF6 analogue is also on the hull in CS/SS/TS [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. MgPdF6 and Rb2PdF6 provide additional stable Pd–F thermodynamic analogues, supporting the broader Pd fluoride stability field [SS-4/TS-3; SS-5/TS-4]. The remaining uncertainty is experimental, not thermodynamic, because the target is identified as theory-only rather than experimentally synthesized in the supplied validation data [CS/SS/TS analogues only indirectly support this limitation].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because the exact CaPdF6 analogue is made at ~100 °C under F2 [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The score is reduced mainly for elemental F2, multi-step fluorination/tempering/cooling, and moisture-sensitive fluoride handling [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. This is the best practical route if a dedicated fluorination setup is available [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1].

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are favorable because the reported CaPdF6 synthesis occurs at low temperature over only hours, implying high chemical activity of the precursor/F2 combination [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. Structural reconstruction is minimal because the analogue has the same R-3 PdF6 corner-sharing topology [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The main uncertainty is whether the larger Sr2+ ion creates a competing Sr fluoride or altered coordination phase [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/SS-1/TS-1].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has similar F2 hazards but higher precursor complexity because a dry Sr chloropalladate precursor must be prepared before fluorination [SS-5/TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. Its temperature is still moderate, since Rb/Cs chloropalladates fluorinate at 200–300 °C [SS-5/TS-4]. CS provides insufficient evidence for this route and is treated as neutral 50 for route-specific support.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Kinetics should be reasonable because preformed [PdCl6]2− complexes are chemically close to [PdF6]2− products [SS-5/TS-4; Reasonable Inference]. The structural penalty comes from the fact that the demonstrated Rb2PdF6 structure is not the SrPdF6 R-3 framework [SS-5/TS-4]. This route is attractive for stoichiometry control but less structurally proven than Route 1 [SS-5/TS-4; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is penalized for pressurized F2, sealed autoclave containment, long reaction times, and specialized Monel equipment [CS-4; TS-5]. It does not incur the >5 GPa deduction because the supplied pressures are autoclave-scale rather than multi-GPa [CS-4; TS-5; Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure Sr/Pd fluorination and is neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Pressurized F2 can enhance oxidation/fluorination, but the 2–10 day dwell times in analogues indicate slower kinetics than Route 1 [CS-4; TS-5]. CaCrF6 provides a corner-sharing AF6/MF6 comparison, but the transition metal differs and the phase window is reported as difficult [TS-5]. Route 3 is best viewed as a fallback for phase stabilization rather than first-pass synthesis [CS-4; TS-5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is mild in temperature and pressure because BaSiF6/BaGeF6 crystallize by slow evaporation [CS-2; CS-5]. The severity score is reduced for HF/Pd fluorometallate toxicity and solution-processing steps [CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for Pd-based solution crystallization and are neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Solution diffusion is favorable, but the key kinetic and structural uncertainty is generation and preservation of Pd(IV) [PdF6]2− in solution [CS-2/CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. The demonstrated solution analogues involve Si/Ge hexafluorometallates and R-3m structures, not target-like R-3 Pd frameworks [CS-2; CS-5]. Therefore, this route is exploratory despite mild apparent conditions [CS-2/CS-5].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has one amorphization trigger from large A-site/Pd-site size mismatch, inferred from the much longer A–F vs Pd–F distances in close analogues and ionic-radius reasoning [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1; Reasonable Inference]. The exact R-3 analogues use corner-sharing octahedra rather than mixed corner/edge/face-sharing networks, lowering amorphization risk [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1; SS-4/TS-3]. Explicit unit-cell atom count is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so C2 is treated neutrally at the source level [CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPdF6 is likely a true or near-ground-state phase because the target hull energy is zero and the closest Pd fluoride analogues are also stable on the hull [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1; SS-4/TS-3]. The synthesis case is strongly supported by CaPdF6, which shares the same R-3 Upper-Bainite-derived framework and was experimentally prepared by direct F2 fluorination [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The most critical enabling factor is access to a low-temperature oxidative fluorination pathway that can form Pd(IV)–F octahedra without requiring refractory diffusion [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is safe, dry, well-controlled elemental fluorine handling and prevention of hydrolysis or incomplete fluorination [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1; SS-5/TS-4]. Overall, SrPdF6 should be synthesizable in a specialized fluorine laboratory, with Route 1 clearly preferred over pressure or solution alternatives [CS-1/SS-1/TS-1].
