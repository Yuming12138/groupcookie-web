### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 band by the required rubric. TS strongly supports thermodynamic plausibility because the top thermodynamic analogues are on-hull, including EuPdH₃, Mg₂RuH₆, Mg₂CoH₅, Li₄RhH₄, and LiPdH. (TS) CS is mixed because it includes both on-hull Mg₂OsH₆/SrMgNiH₄ and high-hull SrH₃Al, so CS is supportive but less decisive. (CS)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C synthesis, but it requires high-pressure H₂ and reactive Mg handling. (CS, SS, TS) The severity is therefore moderate, matching the autoclave conditions used for Mg₂RuH₄, Mg₂RuH₆, Mg₂CoH₅, and Mg₂OsH₆. (CS, SS, TS)

**F3 — Route 1**  
The key kinetic limitation is Rh incorporation because dense late-transition-metal powders react slowly [Reasonable Inference], but Mg–Ru/Mg–Co/Mg–Os analogues show that H₂ autoclave sintering can overcome similar barriers. (CS, SS, TS) Structural reconstruction is nontrivial because the target is P4/mmm and not identical to Mg₂RuH₆/Mg₂OsH₆, but Mg–H and transition-metal–H bond lengths are closely matched. (CS, SS, TS)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 reduces the need to create Mg–H bonds from elemental Mg because MgH₂ is already present, but MgH₂ requires dry/inert handling and H₂ retention. (CS, TS) The conditions are moderate because analogues use 340–600 °C and 10–40 bar H₂ rather than GPa pressure. (CS, TS)

**F3 — Route 2**  
MgH₂ improves precursor activity, and Mg₂OsH₆ directly validates MgH₂ + noble/refractory metal hydride synthesis. (CS) Rh–H formation is supported by Li₄RhH₄, but the MgRhH₂ framework still requires cation/H rearrangement, so kinetics are only moderate. (TS, [Reasonable Inference])

**F2 — Route 3**  
Route 3 is condition-severe because it likely requires alloy preparation under high-temperature inert/sealed conditions followed by separate hydrogenation. (CS, SS, TS, [Reasonable Inference]) Mg volatility and multi-step processing lower the score, even though intermetallic hydrogenation itself is well represented by EuPdH₃ and Mg–RE–Ni hydrides. (CS, SS, TS)

**F3 — Route 3**  
If a Mg–Rh alloy can be made, cation mixing before hydrogenation should improve reaction kinetics [Reasonable Inference]. However, no direct Mg–Rh parent alloy route is supplied, so CS gives insufficient direct Mg–Rh evidence and is treated as neutral 50 for that sub-claim, while SS/TS provide the stronger intermetallic-hydrogenation signal. (SS, TS)

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is heavily penalized because GPa synthesis and internal hydrogen-source containment are intrinsically demanding. (CS) SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for a multi-anvil Mg/Rh route, so they are neutral 50 for this route’s condition-transferability assessment.

**F3 — Route 4**  
High pressure can raise hydrogen chemical potential and access hydrides, as shown in SrH₃Al-type high-pressure synthesis. (CS) The kinetic score remains moderate-low because the high-pressure analogue is not Mg/Rh/Rh-square-planar, and SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this route, scored neutral 50.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target has mixed face/edge/corner-sharing HMg₄Rh₂ octahedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity risk and is consistent with multiple analogous hydrides showing mixed polyhedral sharing. (CS, SS, TS) The cation-size trigger is counted as a [Reasonable Inference] because Mg and Rh have substantially different bonding environments, but direct ionic radii are not reported in CS/SS/TS and are neutral 50 for that specific check. The cell-size trigger is not counted because the target formula is simple and structurally analogous simple P4/mmm hydrides exist. (TS)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgRhH₂ is likely a DFT ground-state or near-ground-state hydride because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues are predominantly on-hull. (TS) The synthesis evidence is fairly strong because CS/SS/TS repeatedly show Mg–transition-metal hydrides forming under H₂ autoclave conditions, and TS includes a direct Rh–H square-planar analogue in Li₄RhH₄. (CS, SS, TS) The most critical enabling factor is high hydrogen chemical potential at moderate temperature, which is common to Mg₂RuH₄, Mg₂RuH₆, Mg₂OsH₆, Mg₂CoH₅, and LiPdH. (CS, SS, TS) The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: analogues report residual educts, incomplete reaction, impurity phases, or purification needs. (CS, SS, TS) Overall, MgRhH₂ is plausible but should be treated as a moderate-difficulty high-pressure hydrogenation target rather than an easy ambient-pressure solid-state compound. (CS, SS, TS)
