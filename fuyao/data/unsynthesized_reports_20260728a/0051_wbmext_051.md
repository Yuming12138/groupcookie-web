### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has reported E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the 90–100 stability band. [Target data] Close analogues including Cr₂TeO₆, VSbO₄, EuCrTeO₆, AgSbO₃, and LiSb₃O₈ also have E_hull = 0 or near-zero values, supporting thermodynamic plausibility. [CS4, CS5, SS1, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5] The uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and no CS/SS/TS source reports experimental MnSbO₄ synthesis. [Target data; insufficient direct synthesis evidence from CS/SS/TS = neutral 50]

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 scores moderately because ceramic analogues use standard furnaces, oxides, and ambient pressure, but repeated grinding/firing and Sb volatility control are expected. [CS4, CS5, SS1, TS5] Kinetics are credible because hydrophilite-derived Cr₂TeO₆ and VSbO₄ are structurally close, but oxide diffusion and ordering require thermal cycling. [CS4, CS5, SS2, TS2] The main uncertainty is whether Mn³⁺/Sb⁵⁺ remains stable under the same air-firing window. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has lower F2 because controlled pO₂ or sealed-tube operation adds atmosphere and volatility penalties. [CS1, CS5] Kinetics remain similar to Route 1 because the same hydrophilite framework is targeted, but redox control can either enable the phase or create competing Mn/Sb oxides. [CS5; Reasonable Inference] The route is most useful as a diagnostic alternative if air/O₂ firing fails. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has the best condition/kinetic balance because solution-derived precursors should reduce diffusion length and allow lower final crystallization temperatures. [Reasonable Inference] Its penalty comes from the multi-step gel/decomposition process and from MnAsO₄ evidence showing decomposition can leave impurity phases. [CS3] Because CS/SS/TS do not directly validate citrate-gel MnSbO₄, direct route evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutral 50 in the expert adjustment. [CS3, TS5; Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Route 4 scores lowest because EuSbO₄ CVT requires high temperature, evacuated ampoules, TeCl₄, and 14–21 days. [SS4, TS4] Vapor transport is credible for Sb oxides but not directly validated for MnSbO₄, so composition control and Mn transport are uncertain. [SS4, TS4; Reasonable Inference] This route is better suited to single-crystal growth after powder synthesis than to first-pass phase discovery. [Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target-like analogues are compact octahedral frameworks and do not show the high-complexity or face-sharing features that would strongly favor amorphization. [CS4, CS5, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2] The radius-mismatch check is favorable by octahedral Mn³⁺/Sb⁵⁺ estimates, but CS/SS/TS do not supply ionic radii directly, so that sub-evidence is a [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is cation ordering, since VSbO₄ and EuCrTeO₆ analogues mention superstructure/order sensitivity. [CS5, SS1, TS1]

MnSbO₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable. [Target data; CS4, CS5, SS1, TS1, TS5] The analogue support is strong structurally because Cr₂TeO₆ and VSbO₄ are hydrophilite-derived octahedral frameworks with similar corner/edge-sharing motifs. [CS5, CS1; Reasonable Inference] Overall, MnSbO₄ appears moderately to highly synthesizable, with reactive-precursor calcination as the best first attempt and direct ceramic firing as the strongest evidence-backed fallback.
