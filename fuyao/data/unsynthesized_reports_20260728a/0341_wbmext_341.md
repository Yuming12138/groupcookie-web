**F2 — Route 1**  
The direct solid-state route is relatively mild because CS analogues form at 800–900 °C without reported high pressure. **(CS-1–5)** The score is reduced for sealed/controlled atmosphere and toxic/volatile Tl handling, which are inferred from SS/TS sealed-tube practice and the target thallium safety note. **(SS-2/4; TS-1/3/5; Target; [Reasonable Inference])** Remaining uncertainty is the missing CS atmosphere detail, so **insufficient evidence from CS** for atmosphere, neutral 50. **(CS-1–5)**

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics are limited by elemental Tm and graphite, while liquid Tl may improve local reaction contact. **(CS-1–5; [Reasonable Inference])** Long CS anneals and reported heterogeneity suggest slow ordering and phase-purity challenges. **(CS-1–5)** The H-phase target requires reconstruction into a different prototype than the CS perovskites, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. **(CS-1; Target)**

**F2 — Route 2**  
Arc-melting plus evacuated-tube annealing has strong SS/TS precedent but carries high severity because it requires controlled Ar, sealing, multiple steps, and likely high fusion temperature. **(SS-2/4; TS-1/3/5; [Reasonable Inference])** Tl toxicity and volatility make this route harsher than the direct sealed-ampoule route. **(Target; [Reasonable Inference])** CS gives **insufficient evidence from CS** for arc-melting, neutral 50. **(CS-1–5)**

**F3 — Route 2**  
Arc-melting improves homogenization and cation mixing relative to unmelted powders. **(SS-2/4; TS-1/3/5)** The required 900 °C/21-day anneal indicates that equilibrium and carbon ordering can still be slow. **(SS-2/4; TS-1/3/5)** Structural transfer is only partial because Sc3TlC is a Tl carbide analogue but not the target H-phase. **(SS-4; TS-3; Target)**

Flux growth is penalized for >1000 °C operation, sealed ampoules, flux-removal steps, and toxic/air-sensitive materials. **(SS-1/5; TS-4; Target; [Reasonable Inference])** It avoids high pressure and may reduce diffusion barriers, but operational complexity remains high. **(SS-1; TS-4)** CS provides **insufficient evidence from CS** for flux growth, neutral 50. **(CS-1–5)**

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux improves kinetics by providing liquid transport and enhanced precursor contact. **(SS-1/5; TS-4; [Reasonable Inference])** However, SS reports at least one flux-grown related carbide as a minority phase, indicating uncertain phase selectivity. **(SS-1)** The flux route therefore scores best kinetically but not decisively enough to offset its severity. **(SS-1/5; TS-4)**
