# SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

The target is a tetragonal I-4 MnIn₂Se₄ phase with MnSe₄ and InSe₄ corner-sharing tetrahedra, and the supplied physical energy above hull is 0.000 eV/atom. The closest chemical analogue is MnIn₂Se₄ in the trigonal R3m structure, which has been grown by CVT; the closest structural and thermodynamic analogues are ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄, both tetragonal I-4 compounds made by prolonged vacuum solid-state sintering. **[CS1; SS1, SS4; TS1, TS2]**

A source conflict exists: CS1 supports the Mn–In–Se chemistry and CVT synthesis but reports a different R3m polymorph, whereas SS1/SS4 and TS1/TS2 support the I-4 tetrahedral topology using chemically substituted analogues. Therefore, SS/TS are weighted more heavily for structural transferability, and CS1 is weighted more heavily for the Mn–In–Se reaction chemistry and transport method.

# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The supplied target value is 0.0000 eV/atom above hull, and the source-paper validation states that the physical value was clipped to zero from a negative normalized raw value. **[Target/source-paper validation]** This places the target in the highest thermodynamic-stability category, consistent with ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄, which also have reported E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. **[CS4; TS1, TS2]** The residual uncertainty concerns whether the calculated I-4 polymorph is experimentally accessible without conversion to the known R3m MnIn₂Se₄ structure. **[CS1; SS1]**

## F2 — Route 1: CVT

CVT receives a moderate F2 score because the exact MnIn₂Se₄ precedent uses 840–820 °C and a standard silica-tube transport configuration, but requires approximately 10⁻⁶ mbar vacuum, AlCl₃, and a temperature gradient. **[CS1]** Selenium volatility/toxicity and AlCl₃ moisture sensitivity further increase handling severity. **[CS1; source-paper safety note]** The route is operationally demanding but does not require high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C. **[CS1]**

## F2 — Route 2: Vacuum Solid-State Sintering

The ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄ analogues demonstrate that I-4 indium selenides can be produced in evacuated quartz vessels near 950 °C. **[SS4; TS1, TS2]** However, the reported 4–5 week tempering period, pellet preparation, and careful heating make this a demanding route. **[CS4; SS4; TS1, TS2]** Selenium volatility and the need for sealed vacuum operation justify additional deductions. **[CS4; source-paper safety note]**

## F2 — Route 3: Melt-and-Anneal

The MnGa₂Se₄ precedent requires melting at 1150 °C, annealing near 600 °C, vacuum sealing, and slow cooling. **[CS3; SS5]** This provides a chemically reasonable route but has greater thermal severity than CVT or 950 °C sintering and still requires control of volatile Se. **[CS3; source-paper safety note]** The route is therefore scored lowest in F2 among the three routes. **[CS3; SS5]**

## F3 — Route 1: CVT

The exact-composition MnIn₂Se₄ CVT synthesis is the strongest kinetic evidence because it directly demonstrates formation and crystal growth from Mn, In, and Se. **[CS1]** Vapor transport reduces the dependence on slow solid-state cation diffusion, while I-4 Zn/Hg analogues support the plausibility of the tetrahedral framework. **[CS1; SS4; TS1, TS2]** The main uncertainty is polymorph selection because the exact-composition precedent crystallizes in R3m rather than I-4. **[CS1; SS1]**

## F3 — Route 2: Vacuum Solid-State Sintering

This route benefits from exact structural analogues: ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄ both possess the target I-4 connectivity, including corner-sharing MSe₄ and InSe₄ tetrahedra. **[SS4; TS1, TS2]** Its kinetic score is reduced because the analogue syntheses require 4–5 weeks at 950 °C, indicating slow diffusion and ordering. **[CS4; SS4; TS1, TS2]** The route should be more reliable for bulk phase formation than for rapid crystal growth. **[Reasonable Inference]**

## F3 — Route 3: Melt-and-Anneal

Melting should enhance homogenization by providing a liquid-mediated pathway, as demonstrated for MnGa₂Se₄ and related Ag/Ga/In chalcogenides. **[CS3; SS5; TS4, TS5]** However, the analogues do not directly establish the I-4 MnIn₂Se₄ ordering pattern; MnGa₂Se₄ is I-42m and the Ag-based materials are chalcopyrite-related. **[CS3; SS5; TS4, TS5]** Therefore, the kinetic benefit of melting is offset by uncertainty in final polymorph selection. **[Reasonable Inference]**

## F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target structure is a compact three-dimensional network of corner-sharing MnSe₄ and InSe₄ tetrahedra, with no reported edge- or face-sharing polyhedra. **[Target structure]** The I-4 ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄ analogues show similarly simple corner-sharing tetrahedral connectivity, supporting a low amorphization tendency. **[SS4; TS1, TS2]** Exact unit-cell atom count and quantitative ionic-radius differences are not supplied by CS/SS/TS; these checks therefore have insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS, although no structural warning signal is apparent. **[Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]**

# SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is most likely a calculated ground-state phase rather than an established metastable compound because its supplied physical E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and its prototype is reported as unique, although it remains theory-only in the supplied validation. **[Target/source-paper validation]** The analogues provide strong support: exact-composition MnIn₂Se₄ has been made by CVT, while ZnIn₂Se₄ and HgIn₂Se₄ demonstrate the same I-4 tetrahedral topology. **[CS1; SS4; TS1, TS2]** The most important enabling factor is the combination of zero calculated hull distance and a structurally coherent corner-sharing tetrahedral framework. **[Target/source-paper validation; SS4; TS1, TS2]** The principal limitation is polymorph competition between the known trigonal R3m MnIn₂Se₄ structure and the proposed tetragonal I-4 structure. **[CS1; SS1]** CVT is consequently the best initial route, with prolonged vacuum sintering as the strongest bulk-phase alternative. **[CS1; SS4; TS1, TS2]**
