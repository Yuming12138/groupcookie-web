## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied E_hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the highest stability score under the requested scale. **[Target data]** The analogue families also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS, SS, and TS, with Sc₂RuSi₂ only slightly above hull at 0.0071 eV/atom. **[CS, SS, TS]** The principal uncertainty is that the target value is a computational prediction and not experimental confirmation of phase formation. **[Target validation]**

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses a high-temperature arc-melting operation, purified Ar, repeated remelting, and Ca handling. **[CS, SS, TS]** The analogues show that these conditions are practical for Ru–Si intermetallics, but the rubric deductions reflect the high thermal severity, multistep homogenization, and inferred Ca air sensitivity/volatility. **[CS, SS, TS; Reasonable Inference]** Exact arc temperature and Ca mass loss are not provided, so the F2 score has moderate uncertainty.

### F3 — Route 1

The main kinetic limitation is the refractory nature of Ru and the need to homogenize a three-component metallic charge; repeated remelting is explicitly used for related compounds. **[CS, SS, TS]** Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target and the closest analogues share the I4/mmm ThCr₂Si₂-type framework and similar Ru–Si bond lengths. **[CS, SS, TS]** The score remains moderate rather than high because precursor melting and solidification segregation can still limit phase purity. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 adds evacuated or sealed-ampoule annealing to arc melting. Long anneals in evacuated quartz tubes are documented for RERu₂Si₂ compounds, and welded Nb/Ta ampoules are documented for the CaRh₂Si₂-related synthesis. **[CS, SS, TS]** The additional atmosphere control, containment, and extended process justify a lower F2 than direct arc melting. **[CS, SS, TS]**

### F3 — Route 2

Annealing improves solid-state diffusion and ordering, and related compounds have been annealed from approximately 500 °C for 600 h to 870 K for two weeks. **[SS, TS]** The same structure type substantially lowers the expected reconstruction barrier. **[CS, SS, TS]** However, prolonged annealing can increase Ca loss or ampoule-reaction risk, and the optimum duration for the target is unknown. **[Reasonable Inference]**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a compact, periodic I4/mmm structure with a simple connectivity pattern and close structural analogues that are routinely obtained as crystalline phases. **[Target data; CS, SS, TS]** The supplied information does not establish a formal ionic-radius mismatch or a unit-cell atom count above 40, so those checks are not triggered; this is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those specific criteria. **[CS, SS, TS]** Consequently, the amorphization risk is assessed as low.

Ca(RuSi)₂ is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar compounds are also predominantly on-hull. **[Target data; CS, SS, TS]** The analogue support is unusually strong: CaRh₂Si₂ provides the closest alkaline-earth-site precedent, while multiple rare-earth Ru₂Si₂ compounds confirm the target framework and Ru–Si bonding motif. **[CS, SS, TS]** The most important enabling factor is the robust I4/mmm ThCr₂Si₂-type structural family, which is repeatedly accessible by elemental arc melting. **[CS, SS, TS]** The main limiting factor is likely control of Ca during high-temperature processing, together with suppression of competing binary silicides; both concerns are partly extrapolated from elemental chemistry and partly supported by impurity problems in related systems. **[CS; Reasonable Inference]** Overall, the target is a credible synthesis candidate, with direct arc melting the first route to test.
