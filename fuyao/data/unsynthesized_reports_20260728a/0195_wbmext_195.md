## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV atom⁻¹, placing it in the highest stability category. This is supported by the chemically and structurally closest Cu₃VSe₄ analogue, which also has \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) in CS-1 and TS-3. The remaining uncertainty is that the target is a theory-only WBM candidate and lacks direct experimental confirmation in the supplied CS/SS/TS records; therefore, the score is high but not exactly 100 [Reasonable Inference].

### F2: Route 1

The direct elemental route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, but it requires vacuum sealing because of selenium volatility and uses a long anneal modeled on Cu₃VSe₄ (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). Vacuum control, selenium containment, and toxic-material handling justify the deductions. The exact optimum temperature for MnCu₃Se₄ is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

### F3: Route 1

Elemental Mn and Cu powders are relatively low-activity solid-state reagents compared with solution-derived or mechanochemically activated precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The six-week anneal required for Cu₃VSe₄ indicates that diffusion and cation ordering can be slow even when the target topology is structurally favorable (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). The same sulvanite framework raises the structural-reconstruction score, but Mn-for-V substitution may alter the ordering kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

### F2: Route 2

The mechanochemical route uses controlled N₂ and selenium-containing powders, so atmosphere and toxicity deductions remain applicable (CS-5; CS-1; TS-1). It avoids extreme temperature and pressure, and only two principal operations—milling and post-heating—are required in the precedent (CS-5). The target-specific need for sealed milling or an excess of Se is not established experimentally and is therefore [Reasonable Inference].

### F3: Route 2

Ball milling increases precursor contact and defect density, and the related Cu–Ge–Se system produced single-phase material after a short 600 °C post-heating treatment (CS-5). This gives Route 2 the strongest kinetic score among the proposed routes. However, the mechanochemical precedent has an enargite-like Cu₂GeSe₃ structure rather than the cubic sulvanite structure, so the structural-reconstruction score is lower than for direct Cu₃VSe₄ substitution (CS-5; SS-5).

### F2: Route 3

Binary precursors permit lower-temperature reaction, as shown for Cu₃SbSe₃ made from Cu₂Se and Sb₂Se₃ in evacuated ampoules (SS-4). However, preparing and verifying three binary precursors adds operational complexity, while vacuum sealing and selenium containment remain necessary (SS-4; SS-1). The additional multi-stage preparation is partly extrapolated from the binary-precursor concept [Reasonable Inference].

### F3: Route 3

Binary precursors should shorten the chemical reaction pathway relative to separate elemental Mn, Cu, and Se, but the data do not directly demonstrate formation of MnCu₃Se₄ from MnSe, Cu₂Se, and CuSe [Reasonable Inference]. The related Cu–Sb–Se system confirms that binary chalcogenides can react to form ternary selenides under moderate-temperature annealing (SS-4). Final cation ordering into the sulvanite structure may still require prolonged annealing [Reasonable Inference].

### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target/analogue framework contains both edge- and corner-sharing tetrahedra, providing one supported structural-complexity trigger (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). Exact primitive-cell atom count and oxidation-state-dependent ionic-radius differences are insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, so they were not treated as confirmed triggers. The resulting score indicates low–medium amorphization or disorder risk rather than a strong glass-forming tendency.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCu₃Se₄ is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹ and the closest Cu₃VSe₄ sulvanite analogue is also on the hull (CS-1; TS-3). The analogue evidence is unusually strong structurally: Cu₃VSe₄ has the same cubic \(P\bar{4}3m\) sulvanite framework and comparable tetrahedral bonding (CS-1; SS-5). The most critical enabling factor is this close structure/chemistry match combined with favorable predicted thermodynamics. The strongest limiting factor is kinetic: direct elemental synthesis of the analogue requires six weeks of annealing and slow cooling, indicating potentially slow cation ordering (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3). Mechanochemical activation is therefore the preferred first experiment because it may reduce diffusion limitations, although target-specific phase formation remains experimentally unverified (CS-5).
