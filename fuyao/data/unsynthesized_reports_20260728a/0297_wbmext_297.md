# SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

## F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has a supplied E_hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability category under the requested rubric (Target record). Multiple chemically or structurally related phases also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Na₂AgSb and Na₂ZnGe (SS-1; SS-4; TS-1; TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not establish an experimentally accessible reaction pathway or exclude kinetic competition.

## F2: Route 1

Route 1 receives 80/100 because Na₂AgSb and Na₃As demonstrate direct sealed solid-state reactions near 700 °C under Ar (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1). The deductions reflect sodium volatility and the air sensitivity of Na/P handling, while no extreme pressure or very high temperature is required (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1). The exact Na₂AgP phase-purity window remains unknown because the target has no reported experimental synthesis (Target record).

## F2: Route 2

Route 2 receives 70/100 because it requires a preformed binary phosphide or an additional precursor-preparation step, and Na₃P/P handling is strongly air-sensitive (TS-5). KLi₂As demonstrates that binary pnictide precursors can permit lower-temperature reaction than elemental synthesis (CS-1). However, direct transfer from arsenides to Na–Ag–P is an extrapolation and therefore carries greater uncertainty ([Reasonable Inference]; CS-1; TS-5).

## F2: Route 3

Route 3 receives 70/100 because Na-flux synthesis requires excess volatile Na, a sealed container, slow cooling, and possibly a separate Na-removal step (CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). These demands are offset by the fact that no high pressure or temperature above 1000 °C is intrinsically required in the proposed adaptation. Residual Na and flux-derived competing phases remain important practical risks (CS-5; SS-4; TS-3).

## F3: Route 1

The direct elemental route has moderate precursor activity because Ag and red P are relatively dense or refractory solids, although Na₂AgSb and Na₃As show that elemental reaction is viable under sealed conditions (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1). Ion transport is favorable because the highest relevant cation melting point is below 1000 °C and Na can provide mobile transport at reaction temperature; the latter is [Reasonable Inference] supported by Na-flux precedent (CS-5; SS-4). Structural reconstruction is only moderate because the target is not experimentally demonstrated, despite close Cmcm and Na/Ag/pnictide analogues (SS-1; SS-4).

## F3: Route 2

The binary-phosphide route is kinetically favored over a completely elemental route because Na₃P supplies precombined Na–P bonding and is used in related phosphide syntheses (TS-5). The use of binary pnictides to enable lower-temperature synthesis is also supported by KLi₂As (CS-1). Nevertheless, the exact reaction between Na₃P, Ag, and P has not been reported in the supplied data, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity remain uncertain ([Reasonable Inference]; CS-1; TS-5).

## F3: Route 3

Route 3 obtains the highest F3 score because Na₂ZnGe has both the Cmcm structure and a closely analogous coordination/connectivity description to the target (SS-4; TS-3). Its successful use of excess Na flux and prolonged cooling provides a direct kinetic model for improving diffusion and ordering (CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). The score is not higher because Na₂ZnGe is a Zn/Ge analogue rather than the exact Ag/P composition, and the target's phase window is unknown.

## F4: Amorphization Tendency

One trigger is assigned for the likely size mismatch between Na and Ag, but this is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than a value directly supplied by CS/SS/TS. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** is available for the target's exact unit-cell atom count and for whether its polyhedral network contains simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity; those source components are therefore neutral at 50. The reported 3D periodic connectivity and close Cmcm analogues support a low-to-medium rather than high amorphization risk (Target record; SS-1; SS-4).
