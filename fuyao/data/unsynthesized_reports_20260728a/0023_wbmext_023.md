### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target receives 96/100 because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and its physical hull distance is clipped to zero [Target]. This is reinforced by chemically similar and thermodynamically similar stable antimonate/oxide analogues, including BiSbO4, Hg2Sb2O7, LiSb3O8, Sb2O5, and LiSbO3 [CS1, CS2, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2, TS5]. The main uncertainty is that the target remains a theory-only WBM candidate rather than an experimentally validated phase [Target].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C because the closest analogues use 600–900 °C air firing [CS1, SS1, TS1]. Deductions come from multi-step regrinding, high-temperature Tl containment, and thallium toxicity [CS1, SS1, Target, Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful route evidence, so no neutral source score is needed for Route 1 [CS1, SS1, TS1].

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetics are moderate-good because oxide precursors are less reactive than solution precursors but are proven in closely related antimonate solid-state syntheses [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The strongest kinetic support is the near-isostructural BiSbO4 route, but the need for 96 h high-temperature annealing and regrinding implies nontrivial ordering kinetics [CS1]. Remaining uncertainty is Tl-specific volatility and oxidation behavior under the BiSbO4-like schedule [Target, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**  
Route 2 has low thermal severity but high atmosphere severity because it relies on O2 or oxygen pressure, and Sb2O5 evidence explicitly points to high oxygen pressure for stabilization [CS2, SS3, TS2]. It also retains toxicity and possible sealed-system penalties because Tl reagents are involved [Target, Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful oxygen-chemistry signals for this route [CS2, SS3, TS2].

**F3 — Route 2**  
The route benefits from oxidation-state-matched Sb2O5 chemistry, but the low-temperature Hg2Sb2O7 analogue required 14 days, implying slow kinetics [CS2]. Structural transferability is weaker because the Hg analogue is pyrochlore-like rather than monoclinic C2/c TlSbO4 [CS2, Target]. The route is therefore better as an oxidation-control backup than as the default synthesis [CS2, SS3, TS2, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3**  
Flux/transport growth avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but sealed ampoules, vacuum/control atmosphere, pre-synthesis, and Tl containment create substantial condition severity [SS2, CS4, Target]. Insufficient evidence from TS for a flux or CVT route is noted, so TS is neutral 50 for Route 3 route-selection evidence [TS]. The method is more appropriate for single crystals than first-pass phase discovery [SS2, CS4, Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3**  
Flux growth receives the best F3 because liquid-mediated transport improves cation mobility compared with dry oxide diffusion [SS2, Reasonable Inference]. The score is capped because the antimonate flux analogue reports twinned crystals and because no TlSbO4-specific flux chemistry is reported [SS2, Target]. CS supports sealed transport feasibility through a chemically similar Hg–As–O ampoule route, but the chemistry is not identical [CS4].

**F2 — Route 4**  
Route 4 is experimentally mild at the solution stage, as TlAsO4 and BiAsO4 analogues form by slow evaporation or precipitation/digestion [CS3, CS5]. The route still carries deductions for toxic Tl precursors, multi-step processing, and likely oxidizing calcination [Target, CS2, Reasonable Inference]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlSbO4 is likely a computational ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but it is not yet experimentally validated [Target]. The strongest analogue support is BiSbO4, which has the same monoclinic C2/c space group and was synthesized by a conventional oxide solid-state route [CS1]. Additional support comes from stable Li–Sb–O and Sb2O5-related octahedral oxide frameworks in SS/TS data [SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2]. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural/chemical match to BiSbO4 and the repeated success of air-fired antimonate solid-state reactions [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is thallium handling and possible Tl loss or redox mismatch during high-temperature oxidation [Target, CS2, SS3, TS2, Reasonable Inference].
