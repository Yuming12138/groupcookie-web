### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

High-temperature oxide/flux routes like Ba2TiO4 at 1340 °C are **not carried forward as primary routes** because the closest structural analogue, K3TaO8, is a peroxide/peroxo compound crystallized from KOH/H2O2 at 0 °C, while the target contains a short O–O contact of 1.47 Å; this exclusion is a **[Reasonable Inference]** from the K3TaO8 and alkaline-earth peroxide analogues. ### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which falls in the 90–100 range and supports a high F1 score. [Target data] The strongest analogues also show favorable thermodynamics: K3TaO8 is only 0.022 eV/atom above hull, while Cs2RuO4, SrO2, and BaO2 are on hull. [CS4; SS1; TS2; CS5; SS4; TS4; SS2; SS3; TS1; TS3] Remaining uncertainty is that the target is theory-only and lacks experimental synthesis validation. [Target data]

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Route 1 scores highly on F2 because the K3TaO8 analogue uses mild alkaline peroxide crystallization at 0 °C with no sealed tube, high pressure, or high-temperature furnace. [CS4; SS1; TS2] Route 1 also scores highly on F3 because fresh hydrous oxide and solution-phase peroxide chemistry should reduce diffusion barriers, but the structural-reconstruction score is reduced because Zr does not map as cleanly onto Ta(V) peroxo chemistry. [CS4; SS1; TS2; Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Route 2 scores lower on F2 because Cs2FeO4 and Cs2RuO4 analogues require sealed Au/Pd tubes, dry atmosphere control, reactive cesium peroxide/superoxide precursors, and long thermal schedules. [CS3; CS5; SS4; TS4] Route 2 scores lower on F3 because solid-state conversion involving ZrO2-like sources is expected to have slow diffusion and substantial framework reconstruction, consistent with long 31–62 day Ru analogue syntheses. [CS5; SS4; SS5; TS4; TS5; Reasonable Inference]

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Route 3 has mild condition severity because BaO2/SrO2 peroxide precipitation occurs at room temperature under O2 rather than at high pressure or high temperature. [SS2; SS3; TS1; TS3] Its kinetics are favorable for precursor mixing but less favorable for crystallinity because the peroxide references warn of hydration/decomposition and phase-purity control issues. [SS2; SS3; TS1; TS3]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only one amorphization trigger is assigned: large cation size/coordination mismatch between Cs and Zr, although K3TaO8 demonstrates that the topology tolerates a large alkali/high-valent-cation contrast. [CS4; SS1; TS2; Reasonable Inference] The explicit unit-cell atom count has **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** and is scored neutral 50 for that sub-check, while face-sharing is not assigned because the target-like K3TaO8 topology reports corner and edge sharing but not face sharing. [CS4; SS1; TS2]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs3ZrO8 is likely a **DFT ground-state candidate** because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but it should be treated experimentally as a chemically demanding peroxide/peroxo phase rather than a routine oxide. [Target data] The analogue support is strongest from K3TaO8 because it matches the I-42m structure type and O–O peroxide motif, while BaO2/SrO2 support low-temperature peroxide handling and Cs2FeO4/Cs2RuO4 support cesium high-oxygen-potential solid-state chemistry. [CS4; SS1; TS2; SS2; SS3; TS1; TS3; CS3; CS5; SS4; TS4] The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature alkaline peroxide crystallization, which avoids destroying O–O bonds and bypasses slow Zr diffusion. [CS4; SS1; TS2; Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is the unusual formal redox requirement for Cs3ZrO8 relative to chemically conventional Ta(V) in K3TaO8. [CS4; SS1; TS2; Reasonable Inference] Overall, the material is synthesizeable enough to justify an experimental attempt, but the likely success window is narrow and strongly dependent on peroxide/oxygen chemical potential control. [SS2; SS3; TS1; TS3; Reasonable Inference]
