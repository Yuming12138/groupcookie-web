### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target is reported at E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, and the top TS analogues are all hull-stable with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [TS1–TS5]. CS also gives a strong signal because Zr3AlN, Zr4Pd2O, Zr9(NiP2)2, and Zr5Sn3N are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS1/CS3/CS4/CS5]. The uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, because the target is a theory-only WBM candidate with no reported synthesis in the provided record.

**F2 — Route 1.** Route 1 is penalized for high-temperature melting, pure-Ar control, multiple processing steps, and Co/Zr powder hazards [CS1/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. It is not penalized for high pressure or volatile sealed precursors because the Zr3AlN melt route used ambient-pressure Ar and the recommended ZrN/Zr/Co set avoids volatile N sources [CS1/SS1/TS1; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is the actual liquidus/complete-dissolution condition for Zr3CoN, which is not provided directly by CS/SS/TS.

**F3 — Route 1.** The same Cmcm prototype in Zr3AlN gives a high structural-reconstruction score [CS1/SS1/TS1]. Melt processing improves diffusion relative to solid-state sintering, and this is directly supported by successful melt growth of the closest analogue [CS1/SS1/TS1]. The kinetic uncertainty is incomplete ZrN dissolution or segregation during cooling, which is a [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Route 2 avoids >1400 °C processing if held in the 1200–1350 °C nitride-sintering window, but it still requires sealed/vacuum handling, multi-step grinding/pelletizing, and powder hazards [CS5/SS3/TS3; Reasonable Inference]. The source signal is strong for sealed nitride synthesis because Zr5Sn3N and Zr2InN-type phases use long sealed or vacuum treatments [CS5/SS3/TS3]. The limiting uncertainty is whether Co incorporation into the Cmcm target proceeds before competing ZrN or Zr–Co phases persist.

**F3 — Route 2.** Solid-state kinetics are the weakest because the analogous nitride routes require 3–14 days or hundreds of hours [CS5/SS3/TS3]. Zr2InN showing mononitride impurity lowers the phase-purity expectation for a purely solid-state route [SS3/TS3]. The score remains nonzero because Zr nitride frameworks do form by reactive powder sintering in related systems [CS5/SS3/TS3].

**F2 — Route 3.** Route 3 is severe because close Zr–TM analogues use arc-melting plus 1400–1450 °C annealing under Ar/dynamic vacuum [SS2/SS4/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5]. It is not high-pressure, but it is multi-step and atmosphere-controlled [SS4/SS5/TS4/TS5]. The uncertainty is nitrogen retention during arc melting, which is not directly resolved by the provided analogues and is therefore [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.** Arc-melting helps cation mixing and high-temperature annealing supports ordering, as shown in Zr–Ni/Zr–Co pnictide analogues [SS2/SS4/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5]. The structural score is lower than Route 1 because those analogues are not the same Cmcm nitride prototype [SS2/SS4/SS5/TS2/TS4/TS5]. The route remains plausible because Zr–Co coordination is experimentally represented in Zr6CoAs2 [SS5/TS5].

**F4 — Amorphization.** The target has only one triggered amorphization check, the Zr/Co size mismatch by [Reasonable Inference]. The same-prototype analogue is a small crystalline Cmcm cell and uses edge/corner NZr6 octahedra, supporting a low–medium amorphization risk [CS1/SS1/TS1]. Direct amorphization data are absent, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for experimentally observed amorphous behavior and the direct-source signal is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr3CoN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and the top thermodynamic analogues are hull-stable [TS1–TS5]. The synthesis case is strongest for melt growth because Zr3AlN is the same Cmcm nitride framework and was made by melting compacted powders under Ar followed by slow cooling [CS1/SS1/TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a nearly exact structural analogue with phase-pure melt synthesis [CS1/SS1/TS1]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling nitrogen retention and avoiding residual ZrN or Zr–Co competing phases, which is inferred from impurity/phase-control issues in related nitride and intermetallic analogues [CS3/CS5/SS3/TS3; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is **moderately to highly synthesizable** but best attempted with high-temperature inert-atmosphere melt processing rather than low-temperature solid-state sintering [CS1/SS4/SS5/TS4/TS5].
