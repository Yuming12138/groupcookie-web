### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target is predicted on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which supports a high thermodynamic score [Target]. TS analogues are also on-hull or nearly on-hull, strengthening the thermodynamic plausibility of borate-like frameworks in this stability class [TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5]. The uncertainty is that the target is explicitly theory-only and not experimentally validated in the supplied dataset [Target].

**F2 — Route 1 Solid-State Severity**  
SS-5 and TS-4 show that anhydrous borates can form at 810–900 °C under ambient pressure, so no high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is applied [SS-5, TS-4]. Deductions are applied for multi-step heating/regrinding, volatility control, and toxic Sb precursor handling [TS-4, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for ambient-pressure solid-state B–Sb–O synthesis; CS source-score = 50.

**F3 — Route 1 Solid-State Kinetics**  
H3BO3/B2O3 sources are chemically active in SS-5 and TS-4, but the target still requires cation ordering and framework reconstruction from oxide precursors [SS-5, TS-4, Target]. The long holds and repeated thermal steps in analogues support only moderate kinetics [SS-5, TS-4]. The main uncertainty is whether Sb(III) can be incorporated before volatilization or oxidation competes [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 Flux Severity**  
The flux route avoids GPa pressure and stays below the >1000 °C penalty threshold while using borate-rich media inspired by CS-3 [CS-3]. Deductions remain for toxic Sb2O3, flux/mass-loss control, and multi-step grow/cool/wash handling [CS-3, Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct B2O3 self-flux growth; TS source-score = 50 for this specific route factor.

**F3 — Route 2 Flux Kinetics**  
Flux conditions improve precursor contact and ion transport relative to dry solid-state synthesis, consistent with CS-3’s boric-acid flux precedent [CS-3, Reasonable Inference]. The target’s unique prototype lowers the structural-reconstruction score because no analogue is the same B–Sb–O structure [Target]. Overall kinetics are rated strongest among routes because liquid-assisted growth mitigates the main diffusion bottleneck [CS-3, SS-5, Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 Hydrothermal Severity**  
CS-5 and SS/TS hydrothermal analogues use autoclave or sealed-ampoule conditions rather than >5 GPa multianvil pressure, so the condition-severity score is high [CS-5, SS-2, TS-2, TS-5]. **F3 — Route 3 Hydrothermal Kinetics**  
Hydrothermal media should improve transport, and several SS/TS analogues crystallize borate frameworks hydrothermally [SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5]. However, many closest analogues contain H/OH, while B3SbO6 is anhydrous, so the structural-reconstruction and phase-selectivity score is low [SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2, Target]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B3SbO6 is best interpreted as a **predicted ground-state candidate** because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but it remains experimentally unvalidated in the source dataset [Target]. The CS evidence is split: chemically similar Sn/Zn/As borates often require HPHT, while CS-3 and CS-5 show lower-temperature flux or hydrothermal access to related borate/oxoanion systems [CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5]. The SS/TS evidence strongly supports that borate frameworks can crystallize by hydrothermal or solid-state routes, but many closest structural/thermodynamic analogues are hydrated or contain large alkaline-earth/lead cations rather than Sb [SS-1, SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-4]. The most critical enabling factor is the zero hull energy combined with borate flux chemistry that can overcome slow diffusion [Target, CS-3, SS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Sb(III) volatility/oxidation and borate glass or hydrated competing phases could prevent isolation of the anhydrous B3SbO6 phase [SS-1, TS-1, Reasonable Inference].
