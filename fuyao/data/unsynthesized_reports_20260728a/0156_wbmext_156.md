## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from moderate-temperature sealed chloride precedents at 250–550 °C and from phase-pure ampoule syntheses. **CS4, SS1/TS1, TS5**  
Its severity is reduced relative to DAC synthesis, but Cl₂ and sealed-vessel handling still impose reactive-atmosphere, volatility, and toxicity penalties. **CS1, CS2**

### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is the most chemically direct analogue for a binary hyperchloride because NaCl₃ was made only under 10–80 GPa with excess chlorine. **CS1**  
The F2 score is low because high pressure, laser heating, excess Cl₂, and multi-step cell operation are severe. **CS1**  
The kinetic score is moderate because high pressure and heating can access unusual bonding, but no SS/TS source directly validates high-pressure Ca–Cl transport, so SS and TS are neutral for this route. **CS1; insufficient evidence from SS/TS = neutral 50**

### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by dry-Cl₂ FeCl₃ CVT and several temperature-gradient chloride ampoule syntheses. **CS2, SS2–SS5, TS2–TS3**  
The main condition penalties are controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile chlorine, and air-sensitive handling. **CS2, SS2, SS3**  
The main kinetic uncertainty is Ca transport, because the analogues involve more volatile Fe, Te, W, Nb, Zr, or Hf chloride species than CaCl₂. **CS2, SS2–SS5, [Reasonable Inference]**

### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has the best kinetic rationale because molten AlCl₃/GaCl₃ fluxes enable low-temperature chloride mobility. **CS3, CS5, TS5**

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency
