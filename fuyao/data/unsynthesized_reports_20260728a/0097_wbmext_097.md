Remaining uncertainty is experimental: the target is theory-only, so the 98 score leaves room for DFT and finite-temperature phase-boundary error [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1:** Route 1 is penalized for controlled N₂ atmosphere, multi-step grinding/pelletizing/annealing, and likely air-sensitive rare-earth nitride handling, all consistent with nitride analogue workflows (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). **F3 — Route 1:** Kinetics are favorable relative to other routes because the exact P4₂/mmc CaNiN analogue minimizes structural reconstruction (SS1, TS1). The main kinetic limitation is solid-state diffusion of Ni/Yb-bearing species and the need for long anneals/regrinding, consistent with SS1/TS1 and SS3/TS3. The high score is justified by analogues forming Ni–N frameworks at 750–800 °C rather than requiring ultra-high temperature (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3).

**F2 — Route 2:** Route 2 requires sealed controlled atmosphere and likely air-sensitive nitride/Li-containing precursors, matching the closed Ni-tube and nitride precursor evidence (SS2, TS2). It is more severe than Route 1 because stoichiometry depends on a wall reaction and possible Li-containing transport chemistry [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2]. **F3 — Route 2:** SS2/TS2 demonstrate that Ni tube walls can form linear Ni–N nitrides at 800 °C, which is a strong kinetic signal for Ni–N bond formation. **F2 — Route 3:** NH₃ ammonolysis avoids very high temperature and high pressure, but it requires reactive NH₃ and repeated sintering/regrinding as in SS4/TS4. YbNiN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the exact CaNiN structural analogue is also on hull (SS1, TS1). CS analogues support rare-earth/Ni/pnictide bulk reactivity but conflict with SS/TS in anion chemistry because they are phosphides, so SS/TS are weighted more heavily for the nitride target (CS2–CS5 vs SS1/TS1).
