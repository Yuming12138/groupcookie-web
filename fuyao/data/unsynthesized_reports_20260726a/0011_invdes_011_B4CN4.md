TS analogues are favorable because TS1–TS5 all report E_hull = 0.0, while CS is mixed because CS1/CS3/CS4 are stable or near-stable but CS5 is high-hull and CS2 is metastable. The 60/100 score reflects neutral missing target-hull evidence plus favorable but indirect analogue stability **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 requires >1000 °C and controlled sealed/inert processing, supported by TS1 and TS5. It avoids high pressure and volatile molecular precursors, but it needs multiple preparation/annealing steps **[Reasonable Inference]**. The resulting 65/100 score reflects moderate condition severity rather than extreme equipment demand.

The target ribbon requires complete reconstruction from BN + C rather than topotactic conversion **[Reasonable Inference]**. Regrinding and long dwell mitigate but do not eliminate the slow-ordering risk.

Composition control is a major penalty because CS2 shows product distributions can depend strongly on precursor ratio and temperature.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B4CN4 should be treated as an unproven metastable or near-metastable candidate rather than a confirmed ground-state phase because no target E_hull or phonon validation is reported (Target data). The analogue support is moderate: SS1 supports robust B–N frameworks, TS1/TS3/TS5 support stable B–C borocarbide bonding, and CS/TS examples show such chemistries can be synthesized under controlled high-temperature conditions. The key enabling factor is the chemical compatibility of short B–N and B–C covalent bonding motifs, supported by SS1 and TS1/TS3/TS5. The key limiting factor is phase selectivity: CS2, CS3, CS5, and TS3 all show that related main-group or boride/carbonitride syntheses can have narrow temperature, pressure, atmosphere, or stoichiometry windows. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires screening rather than a single-shot preparation **[Reasonable Inference]**.
