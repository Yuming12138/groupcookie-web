### SECTION 1 — Possible Synthesis Routes

Citation shorthand: CS1 = BN shock route; CS2 = ammonothermal Si–N route; CS3–CS5 = high-pressure Si–P/Si–S routes; SS1 = structurally similar BN shock route; SS2 = MgNB9 high-gas-pressure B–N–B network; TS1 = MgNB9 thermodynamic analogue; TS3 = high-pressure MgB2C2; TS5 = MgB12C2 flux route.

| Precursors | Graphitic/h-BN plus amorphous B, targeted as 4 BN + 1 B → B5N4; Cu powder matrix can be used as a shock medium as in the BN analogue [CS1; SS1; Reasonable Inference]. |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has a negative predicted formation energy, but no candidate-specific E_hull or phonon validation is reported, so it cannot be treated as a confirmed ground-state phase [Target evidence]. Thermodynamic analogues are encouraging because MgNB9, CaB2C2, GdB2C2, and MgB12C2 are reported at or near 0 eV/atom, but chemically similar entries include both moderately metastable BN and a high-hull Si–N analogue [CS1; CS2; TS1; TS2; TS4; TS5]. The score is therefore 60 rather than ≥65 because direct target hull evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS [insufficient direct E_hull evidence from CS/SS/TS].

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Shock compression has direct B–N chemical precedent, but the 550 kbar pressure and multi-step recovery impose high severity [CS1; SS1]. Kinetics are weak for ordered B5N4 because shock events are brief and the analogue only demonstrates BN polymorph conversion, not B-rich B/N ordering [CS1; SS1; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Static HP–HT has strong precedent for covalent framework transformations, but the route is penalized by high temperature, >5 GPa pressure, and multi-step capsule/quench handling [CS3; CS4; CS5; TS3]. Kinetics are better than shock but still limited by refractory B/BN diffusion and the need to form a specific B-rich 2D topology [SS2; TS3; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Ammonothermal synthesis has lower temperature than HP–HT and a clear nitridation analogue, but NH3 pressure, sealed autoclaves, and amide mineralizers add severity [CS2]. Kinetic transport can be good in mineralized NH3, yet structural transferability is uncertain because the analogue is Si–N rather than B-rich B–N, and SS/TS provide insufficient ammonothermal support [CS2; insufficient evidence from SS/TS].

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
The flux route avoids >5 GPa pressure while retaining high-temperature mobility, which gives the best weighted balance among evidence-supported routes [SS2; TS1; TS5]. Kinetics are favored because Mg/metal fluxes can dissolve and transport boron, but structural control is uncertain because known close analogues are ternary Mg–B–N or Mg–B–C phases rather than binary B5N4 [SS2; TS1; TS5; Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 5**  
Thin-film deposition has lower nominal severity because it avoids bulk high pressure, but CS/SS/TS provide no direct deposition analogue and are therefore neutral evidence sources for this route [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. Kinetics are scored conservatively because crystallization and 5:4 B:N composition control are both unproven [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The formal F4 triggers are not supported: there is no multi-cation radius mismatch, no evidence for a >40-atom target cell, and no demonstrated mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network in the target description [Target evidence; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. The low-trigger score does not mean synthesis is easy; it only means the specified amorphization heuristics are not strongly activated [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B5N4 is more likely an unverified metastable or near-metastable phase than a confirmed ground-state material because the target lacks a reported E_hull and phonon validation despite having negative predicted formation energy [Target evidence; Reasonable Inference]. CS supports B–N chemical accessibility through shock-transformed BN, while SS/TS support B-rich covalent networks through MgNB9 and related boride/carbide analogues [CS1; SS2; TS1; TS5]. The most critical enabling factor is enhanced atomic mobility under flux or high-pressure/high-temperature conditions, because dry B/BN solid-state diffusion is likely slow [TS3; TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, especially avoiding stable BN, elemental B, or Mg–B–N ternary phases [CS1; SS2; TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but challenging, with a best estimated synthesizability of **63/100** [CS/SS/TS synthesis evidence; Reasonable Inference].
