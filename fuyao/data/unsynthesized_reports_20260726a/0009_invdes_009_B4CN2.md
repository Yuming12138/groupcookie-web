## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
Thermodynamic analogues are favorable because all TS entries are reported at E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, while CS analogues are mostly near-hull with only CS5 as high as 0.103 eV/atom. (TS1–TS5; CS1–CS5) Structural analogues are less uniformly stable, with SS1/SS2/SS4/SS5 around 0.095–0.134 eV/atom, so the target is not treated as a proven ground state. (SS1–SS5) The target’s own convex-hull energy is not reported, so F1 is capped in the 65–85 band rather than scored as a confirmed ≤0.05 eV/atom phase. (Target note; TS1–TS5)

**F2 — Route 1**  
Direct solid-state synthesis inherits high temperature and multi-step penalties because analogous B–C phases require 1050–1950 °C and sealed/inert handling. (CS2; SS3; TS1) It avoids high-pressure and volatile-flux penalties, but the lack of a reactive nitrogen source beyond BN makes the route kinetically severe. [Reasonable Inference] (SS1; TS5)

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetic score is low because B, C, and BN powders require substantial reconstruction into a new 1D ribbon, and no same-structure analogue is provided. (SS1; SS3; TS1–TS3) Long anneals in borocarbide analogues support slow ordering and diffusion as a major limitation. (TS1; TS3)

**F2 — Route 2**  
Flux growth is penalized for high temperature, volatile/reactive Mg-rich flux, and multi-step growth/leaching. (CS3; TS5) It avoids >5 GPa pressure and has the best bulk-crystal precedent among the proposed routes. (CS3)

**F3 — Route 2**  
Flux improves precursor activity and diffusion relative to dry solid-state synthesis, which is why its F3 exceeds Route 1. (CS3) The score is still capped because MgB₁₂C₂ and MgNB₉ are not same-structure B₄CN₂ analogues, and flux inclusion/competing phases remain likely. (CS3; TS5)

**F2 — Route 3**  
HPHT is heavily penalized by the >5 GPa pressure term and high temperature. (SS1; TS5) The route is chemically relevant to B–N reconstruction but operationally the most demanding. (SS1)

**F3 — Route 3**  
Pressure can assist covalent-network rearrangement, but SS1 shows BN conversion under extreme shock rather than controlled formation of a ternary B–C–N ribbon. (SS1) The target’s 1D structure may not be favored under pressure, so the reconstruction score remains modest. [Reasonable Inference] (SS1; TS5)

**F2 — Route 4**  
Thin-film deposition scores highest on condition severity because it avoids >1000 °C bulk heating and high pressure while using only controlled Ar/N₂ vacuum. (CS5) The main severity penalty is the reactive/controlled N₂ atmosphere. (CS5)

**F3 — Route 4**  
Reactive sputtering provides accessible vapor/target sources, but CS5 does not validate crystalline B₄CN₂ ordering. (CS5) Composition control is the main kinetic risk because exact B:C:N = 4:1:2 must be maintained while incorporating reactive nitrogen. [Reasonable Inference] (CS5)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The formal C1–C3 rubric gives zero clear triggers because the target is a small, metal-free covalent formula and the provided target topology does not explicitly show mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. [Reasonable Inference] The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct amorphization metric for B₄CN₂, so CS/SS/TS are neutral for F4-specific evidence.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B₄CN₂ should be treated as **unconfirmed ground-state and possibly metastable**, because the target E_hull is not reported even though thermodynamic analogues are favorable. (TS1–TS5) The strongest support comes from known stable or near-stable B–C/B–N-containing analogues with comparable B–C and B–N bond lengths. (CS2; SS1; TS4; TS5) The most critical enabling factor is access to strong covalent B–C/B–N bonding under oxygen-free, high-activity conditions. (CS2; SS1; TS5) The most critical limiting factor is structural specificity: the CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly 3D borocarbides, BN, or metal-containing phases rather than a metal-free monoclinic 1D B₄CN₂ ribbon. (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5) If thin films are acceptable, reactive sputtering is the best exploratory route; if bulk crystalline material is required, metallic flux growth is the stronger practical route. (CS5; CS3; TS5)
