## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability
The target lacks a reported convex-hull energy, and the placeholder 0.000000 eV/atom must not be treated as a real hull value [TGT]. The negative predicted formation energy supports chemical plausibility, but it does not prove ground-state stability or dynamic stability [TGT]. TS analogues are favorable because the top thermodynamic neighbors are hull-stable and include Re–N chemistry, while SS azide-like analogues show that N-rich structures can be metastable [TS1–TS5, SS1–SS5]. The resulting F1 score is moderate rather than high because candidate-specific hull and phonon validation are insufficient from CS, SS, and TS [CS, SS, TS].

### F2 — Route 1
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because metathesis analogues operate at 450–650 °C in sealed ampoules [CS5, TS1–TS4]. The severity deductions come from Ar/vacuum handling, sealed volatile halide chemistry, multiple handling/heating steps, and air-sensitive reagents [CS5, TS1–TS4, Reasonable Inference]. SS gives insufficient route-condition evidence for Ti/Re metathesis and is neutral 50 for this route’s condition severity [SS1–SS5].

### F3 — Route 1
Halide/Li₂CN₂ metathesis gives high precursor activity, as shown by multiple carbodiimide syntheses [CS5, TS1–TS4]. Diffusion remains a concern because Ti/Re are refractory and must form an ordered mixed-cation P1 framework [TGT, Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is partial-to-large because known analogues retain C–N carbodiimide motifs, while the target has metal-bound carbon and short N–N units [TGT, CS5, TS1–TS4, SS1–SS5].

### F2 — Route 2
Route 2 has moderate condition severity because TS5 demonstrates Re nitridation at 850–900 °C under Ar/N₂ without high pressure [TS5]. It earns deductions for controlled atmosphere and multistep heating/cooling, but not for sealed volatile precursors if Ti/Re/C solid feedstocks are used [TS5, Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient direct-nitridation evidence for Ti₂ReCN₇ and are neutral 50 for this route’s condition severity [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5].

### F3 — Route 2
Direct nitridation has poor kinetics because refractory Re/Ti and carbon-rich solids are low-activity precursors [TS5, Reasonable Inference]. TS5 still supports the existence of ReN₄ tetrahedra under N₂, but the 60 h reaction and specialized crucible indicate slow ordering [TS5]. Complete reconstruction into Ti₂ReCN₇ is required, so F3 is the lowest among the routes [TGT, TS5, Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 3
Route 3 uses moderate temperatures because nitrido chloride and Re thermolysis analogues operate at 250–700 °C [CS2, CS3]. Deductions arise from vacuum/H₂/NH₃ control, volatile halides, gas-evolving multistep decomposition, and hazardous halide/ammonia chemistry [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference]. TS gives insufficient ammonolysis-specific evidence and is neutral 50 for this route [TS1–TS5].

### F3 — Route 3
Molecular or halide precursors are more reactive than refractory powders, so precursor activity is moderate-to-good [CS2, CS3]. The major kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction, because CS3 thermolysis reports phase variability and the target requires an ordered Ti/Re/C/N framework [CS3, TGT, Reasonable Inference]. This route may be useful for making reactive intermediates, but not necessarily for direct single-phase Ti₂ReCN₇ formation [CS2, CS3, Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 4
Route 4 has the same sealed-system and precursor-handling penalties as Route 1, plus long flux growth time [CS4, TS1]. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C because flux analogues use 500–700 °C and slow cooling [CS4, TS1]. SS gives insufficient evidence for Ti/Re flux chemistry and is neutral 50 for this route’s condition severity [SS1–SS5].

### F3 — Route 4
Flux improves kinetics by increasing ionic transport and supporting slow crystallization, as shown by halide flux growth and KCl/LiCl-assisted metathesis analogues [CS4, TS1]. Ti/Re ordering is still difficult because no provided analogue has the same mixed Ti/Re/C/N topology [TGT, CS4, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. Route 4 receives the best F3 among bulk routes because it balances reactive metathesis with enhanced diffusion and crystal growth [CS4, TS1, Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency
The target triggers one amorphization check because Ti and Re likely have a >15% effective radius mismatch depending on oxidation/coordination state [TGT, Reasonable Inference]. The target does not trigger the >40 atom-count check or the mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing check based on the provided P1 description and corner-sharing tetrahedral motif [TGT]. F4 remains relatively favorable because the structure is not a very large-cell, highly mixed-connectivity framework [TGT].

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂ReCN₇ should be treated as an **unverified metastable or near-stable candidate**, not a confirmed ground-state phase, because no candidate-specific E_hull or phonon validation is reported [TGT]. The strongest positive evidence is that TS analogues include hull-stable carbodiimides and a ReN₄-containing nitridorhenate, which supports the feasibility of C/N and Re–N bonding motifs [TS1–TS5]. The strongest structural caution is that SS analogues with short N–N units are often azide-rich and can be metastable or hazardous, which only partially transfers to Ti/Re/C/N chemistry [SS1–SS5, TGT]. The key enabling factor is a reactive halide/Li₂CN₂/chloride-flux environment that can lower diffusion barriers and promote crystallization [CS4, CS5, TS1–TS4]. The key limiting factor is simultaneous control of Ti/Re redox, carbon activity, high nitrogen chemical potential, and low-symmetry cation ordering [TGT, TS5, Reasonable Inference].

## SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| 1 | All | Unknown target hull stability and competing TiN/ReN/TiC/Re–C phases | High | Screen multiple temperatures and verify by PXRD/SCXRD; do not assume the placeholder hull is real [TGT, TS5, Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | Routes 1/3/4 | Volatile or moisture-sensitive halide loss during heating | Med–High | Use dry ampoules, staged heating, and sealed systems as in halide metathesis/ammonolysis analogues [CS2, CS5, TS1–TS4]. |
| 3 | Route 2 | Slow Ti/Re diffusion and ordering from refractory precursors | High | Use fine powders, repeated grinding, long holds, and Ta crucibles as suggested by direct nitridorhenate synthesis [TS5, Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Routes 1/4 | Stoichiometry control for N-rich Ti₂ReCN₇ | High | Use slight excess reactive N source and chloride flux; avoid large azide charges because SS azides are hazardous [TGT, SS2–SS5, TS1, Reasonable Inference]. |
| 5 | Route 3 | Gas evolution and phase variability during thermolysis | High | Use small charges, slow ramps, and controlled H₂/NH₃/N₂ conditions; CS3 shows thermolysis can yield phase variations [CS3, Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Routes 1/4 | Crystal growth difficulty for low-symmetry P1 target | Medium | Use slow cooling around 2–5 °C/h and excess flux, following flux-growth precedent [CS4, TS1]. |
| 7 | All | Precursor reactivity mismatch between C source, N source, Ti, and Re | Med–High | Compare Li₂CN₂, Li₃N, and graphite/TiC feedstocks in small screening matrices; this is extrapolated from metathesis and direct nitridation analogues [CS5, TS1–TS5, Reasonable Inference]. |
