Eu₃(CN₄)₂ is best viewed as a plausible metastable-to-near-ground-state candidate rather than a confirmed ground state, because the target lacks a reported convex-hull energy or phonon validation even though its predicted formation energy is negative (Target). The analogue base is reasonably strong: RE carbodiimides and LiRE(CN₂)₂ phases are made by closely related halide/cyanamide metathesis routes, and many thermodynamic analogues are on-hull or near-hull (CS1, CS2, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is reactive salt-metathesis chemistry using Li₂(CN₂), which lowers diffusion barriers and provides transferable RE–N/C–N bonding motifs (CS1, CS2, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is the target’s unusual N-rich CN₄/N–N chemistry, which is not directly demonstrated in the closest rare-earth carbodiimide analogues (Target, CS1, CS2). Overall, the material is moderately synthesizable, with Route 1 offering the best balance of chemical transferability and manageable conditions (CS1, CS2, TS3).
