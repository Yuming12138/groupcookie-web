### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target has a negative predicted formation energy, but no reported convex-hull energy, so it cannot be called a ground-state compound from the target data alone. [Target data] CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly on-hull or near-hull, while CS-5 is a conflicting high-hull boron-rich carbide signal. [CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5] The resulting **75/100** reflects favorable analogue thermodynamics but a real uncertainty around B–C–N competing phases. [CS/SS/TS, Target data]

**Route 1 F2/F3 — Flux growth.** The route is penalized for >1000 °C, possible >1400 °C screening, and multi-step ampoule/flux workup. [CS-1, CS-2, TS-2, TS-3] Its kinetics are the best among bulk routes because flux analogues demonstrate liquid-mediated formation of B-rich carbide crystals. [CS-1, CS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3] The main uncertainty is that the target is metal-free, while the closest flux analogues include Mg or Li-containing phases. [CS-1, CS-2, Target data]

**Route 2 F2/F3 — HPHT.** HPHT has strong structural precedent in SS/TS but carries a major >5 GPa penalty and requires specialized capsule/quench operations. [SS-1, SS-5, TS-1] Kinetically, pressure should help access dense/tetrahedral B–N and B–C motifs, but precursor diffusion remains solid-state limited. [SS-3, SS-5; Reasonable Inference] **Insufficient evidence from CS** for HPHT transferability was treated neutral **50**.

**Route 3 F2/F3 — Direct solid state.** Ambient-pressure solid-state synthesis is equipment-simple relative to HPHT but requires very high temperature because B/C/BN precursors are refractory. [SS-2, SS-3] The F3 score is low because forming the ordered Pmm2 B5CN2 network from separate B, C, and BN solids likely requires extensive bond reconstruction.
