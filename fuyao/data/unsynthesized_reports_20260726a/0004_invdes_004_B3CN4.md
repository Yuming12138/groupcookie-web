### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target has negative predicted formation energy, but no reported convex-hull energy or phonon validation, so it cannot be treated as a confirmed ground-state material. [Target] TS analogues are favorable because the top thermodynamic neighbors are E_hull = 0, but they include La/B/N, borates, carbodiimides, and borocarbides rather than metal-free B3CN4. [TS1–TS5] CS and SS analogues show that related B/C/N or B/N phases can be made, but their hull values are mixed and often metastable, supporting a capped F1 of 60. [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]

**Route 1 F2/F3.** Severity is moderate because the route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, but it requires controlled atmosphere, sealed or nitrogen-rich processing, multiple steps, and potentially sensitive C–N precursors. [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5] Kinetics are only moderate because flux/metathesis helps transport, while BN/B-derived networks remain refractory and the target requires a specific P1 B–C–N sheet plus N2 cluster. [TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5, Target; Reasonable Inference] CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact flux conversion route and are treated as neutral 50 for route-specific signal. [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5]

**Route 2 F2/F3.** HPHT has the harshest condition burden because the relevant analogues use GPa pressure or shock compression. [CS3, SS4] Kinetics may improve if a pre-homogenized amorphous B–C–N precursor is used, but diffusion and ordering in refractory B/C/N networks remain difficult. [CS3, SS4, TS5; Reasonable Inference] TS gives insufficient direct high-pressure signal for B3CN4 and is treated as neutral 50 for this route. [TS1–TS5]

**Route 3 F2/F3.** Thin-film deposition scores best on F2 because it avoids bulk high pressure and very high temperature while using controllable Ar/N2 plasma processing. [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS3, SS5] The limiting kinetic step is crystallization from an amorphous or polymeric B–C–N intermediate into the exact P1 target rather than forming a generic B–C–N solid. [Target; Reasonable Inference] The route is therefore kinetically plausible but structurally uncertain. [CS1, CS2, CS5, SS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.** The rubric checks do not trigger strongly because the target is a small covalent B/C/N framework, not a large multi-cation ionic structure. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B3CN4 should be treated as a **likely metastable or unvalidated phase**, not a confirmed ground-state compound, because no target convex-hull or phonon validation is reported despite a negative predicted formation energy. [Target] The CS/SS/TS analogues support the broader feasibility of B–N, B–C, and C–N chemistry, but none directly demonstrate metal-free crystalline B3CN4 with a 2D P1 sheet and an N2 cluster. [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5, Target] The most important enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature or non-equilibrium pathways—especially reactive thin-film deposition or molecular precursor processing—that can bypass bulk equilibrium. [CS4, CS1, CS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference] The most important limiting factor is phase specificity: nitrogen retention and ordering into the exact B3CN4 sheet/N2-cluster motif are not validated by the references. [Target; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS = neutral 50] Overall feasibility is **moderate**, with the best route being thin-film exploration followed by careful structural validation. [CS4; Reasonable Inference]
