### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has a negative predicted formation energy, but no candidate-specific convex-hull or phonon validation, so it cannot be treated as a confirmed ground-state phase. [Target] CS evidence is mixed because synthesized analogues span near-hull BN/SiS2 phases and a higher-hull Si–N analogue, while SS/TS analogues include many E_hull = 0 materials but with extra elements or different anion chemistry. [CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5] The score is therefore above neutral but below the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability band. [Target, CS1–CS5, TS1–TS5]

**F2/F3 — Route 1 Flux-Assisted Nitride Growth**  
F2 is low-moderate because flux growth requires >1000 °C, sealed/inert handling, multiple steps, and likely air-sensitive N-rich additives. [SS3, TS3, Reasonable Inference] F3 is moderate because B–N local bonding transfers well from LaBN2, but dissolving refractory BN and incorporating a discrete N2 cluster remain kinetically difficult. [SS3, TS3, Target] CS gives only partial support through BN/HP nitride behavior and is insufficient for direct CaCl2-flux B3N5 synthesis, so that CS route-transfer signal is neutral 50. [CS1–CS5]

**F2/F3 — Route 2 Ammonothermal / Supercritical NH3 Nitridation**  
F2 is limited by 6 kbar NH3, toxic/corrosive ammonia, and air-sensitive amide mineralizers, although temperature is only 600 °C. [CS1] F3 is moderate because ammonothermal transport can activate nitridation, but there is no direct evidence for ordering a B3N5 sheet-plus-N2 motif under these conditions. [CS1, Target, Reasonable Inference] SS/TS provide insufficient ammonothermal evidence for B3N5 and are neutral 50 for this route. [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]

**F2/F3 — Route 3 HPHT / Shock Route**  
F2 is the most severe because the route uses >5 GPa pressure, possible N-rich sealed capsules, and specialized recovery steps. [CS2, CS3–CS5] F3 is weak-to-moderate because pressure can transform BN, but CS2 shows formation of w-BN rather than nitrogen-rich B3N5, so competing BN polymorphs are a major kinetic sink. [CS2, Target] SS/TS provide insufficient HPHT-specific support for B3N5 and are neutral 50 for this route. [SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5]

**F2/F3 — Route 4 Solid-State Metathesis**

[SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2, Reasonable Inference] F3 is the best among routes because reactive halide–nitride chemistry avoids slow diffusion through dense BN, although complete reconstruction to B3N5 remains uncertain. [SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2, Target, Reasonable Inference] CS gives insufficient direct evidence for B-halide SSM to B3N5 and is neutral 50 for that route-specific claim. [CS1–CS5]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B3N5 is more likely a **metastable or unconfirmed near-stable phase** than a proven ground-state phase because the target has no reported E_hull or phonon validation despite a negative predicted formation energy. [Target] The strongest support comes from B–N structural analogues in LaBN2 and from low-temperature SSM analogues that demonstrate nitrogen-rich framework formation under sealed conditions. [SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3] The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high nitrogen chemical potential while forming B–N bonds before the system relaxes to ordinary BN polymorphs. [Target, CS1–CS2, SS1–SS3, Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: CS2 shows BN readily forms other polymorphs under forcing conditions, while no source directly validates the target’s discrete N2-cluster motif. [CS2, Target] Overall, synthesis is plausible but experimentally nontrivial, with Route 4 offering the best balance of reactivity and manageable conditions. [SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2, Reasonable Inference]
