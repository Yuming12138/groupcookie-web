### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:**  
The target lacks a reported E_hull, so the placeholder 0.000 eV/atom is not used as evidence. CS analogues are mostly on or very near the hull, including CS1 at 0.038 eV/atom and CS2–CS4 at 0 eV/atom; TS analogues are also near-hull, with TS1 at 0.0206 eV/atom and TS2–TS5 at 0 eV/atom. SS evidence is supportive but less uniform because SS2 is 0.1479 eV/atom while other SS entries are near-hull. The F1 score is therefore moderate-good rather than high because candidate-specific hull and phonon data are missing.

**F2/F3 — Route 1:**  
The Li₃N flux route has moderate condition severity because CS4 uses 1030°C and sealed ampoules, but not high pressure. Its kinetics are better than direct sintering because flux can enhance transport, as shown by CS4, and slow cooling is supported by CS2. The main uncertainty is that Re and C–N incorporation are [Reasonable Inference], not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 2:**  
NH₃ ammonolysis is conditionally moderate because SS2 demonstrates 800°C dry-NH₃ nitridation at ambient pressure. Kinetics are weaker because SS2 reports impurity phases and sensitivity to time, temperature gradients, and compaction. Carbon incorporation and Eu/Re redox control are [Reasonable Inference] and remain uncertain.

**F2/F3 — Route 3:**  
Direct solid-state synthesis is severe because SS3 and SS5 require 1200–1600°C or 1400°C/100 h conditions. Kinetics are poor because dense refractory Re-containing mixtures are expected to diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference], and SS5 required repeated processing. This route is plausible but likely less efficient than flux synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 4:**  
High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported for N–N-containing diazenides by TS4/TS5 and for nitride polymorph stabilization by TS1/SS1. Its condition severity is high because TS4/TS5 require multianvil pressures up to 12 GPa and hazardous azide handling. Kinetically, azides are reactive, but assembling a quaternary Eu–Re–C–N phase remains a major [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 5:**  
Topochemical insertion has mild temperatures because CS1 uses 450–600°C, but the route depends on an unavailable Re–C–N host. The low F3 score reflects insufficient host evidence from CS/SS/TS, treated neutral 50, then penalized for route-specific uncertainty. It is useful as an exploratory route only after a suitable framework precursor is found.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:**  
The target likely has a significant cation-size/bonding mismatch between Eu and Re [Reasonable Inference], which triggers C1. The atom count does not trigger C2 because the described P1 cell contains 13 atoms, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific cell-count evidence. Mixed connectivity is likely because structurally similar nitrides show edge/corner/face-sharing frameworks in SS1 and SS3, but applying that fully to the target is [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Eu₂Re₃CN₇ is best treated as a **potentially metastable or near-hull phase**, not a confirmed ground-state compound, because the target lacks candidate-specific E_hull and phonon validation. CS and TS analogues strongly support that chemically/thermodynamically similar nitrides can be synthesized near the hull, while SS2 and CS3 show that impurity formation and phase control can still be difficult. The most critical enabling factor is use of a reactive flux or reactive nitrogen source, especially Li₃N flux as in CS4. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Eu/Re/C/N stoichiometry and ordering in a low-symmetry nitride-carbonitride framework, which is not directly demonstrated by any CS/SS/TS analogue. Overall, synthesis is plausible but experimentally challenging, with Route 1 the most practical starting point.
