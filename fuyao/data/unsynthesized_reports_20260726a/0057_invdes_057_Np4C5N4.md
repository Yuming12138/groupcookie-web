### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has no reported convex-hull energy, so it cannot be scored as a confirmed ground-state phase; the negative predicted formation energy supports chemical plausibility but not stability against Np carbides, nitrides, graphite, or C–N phases. TS analogues are mostly reported at E_hull = 0, and the closest Np–C structural analogue is near-hull at 0.042 eV/atom, which supports a moderate stability score (SS1, TS1–TS5). CS evidence is mixed because some chemically similar carbides are stable or near-stable while CS5 is high above hull, so exact target stability remains uncertain (CS1–CS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact Np–C–N convex hull is scored neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 1**  
Arc melting is severe because it requires >1400°C, controlled inert atmosphere, multiple remelts/anneal, and actinide handling, all supported by CS1, CS5, SS1, SS3, TS1, and TS2. No high pressure is needed, which avoids the largest severity penalty (CS1, TS1). Nitrogen retention is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS and is treated as neutral 50 with [Reasonable Inference] mitigation.

**F3 — Route 1**  
Kinetics benefit from melt-phase mixing, as actinide carbide analogues form by arc melting and annealing (CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1). The limitation is structural ordering of C/N into the P1 target, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence and are neutral 50. The resulting F3 is moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 2**  
Conventional sintering needs high temperatures, inert atmosphere, multiple grinding/annealing steps, and actinide nitride/carbide handling, matching the severity profile in CS2, CS3, SS2, TS4, and TS5. It avoids extreme pressure and can avoid volatile N precursors if NpN is used [Reasonable Inference]. Direct NpN + graphite reaction evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state kinetics are the weakest because NpN and graphite are expected to be refractory/diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], and analogous solid-state carbide routes require long high-T treatments (CS2, CS3, SS2, TS4). Complete reconstruction into an ordered carbonitride is not shown by the references. This route is plausible but slow.

**F2 — Route 3**  
Pressure-assisted sintering has lower severity because the proposed maximum temperature stays below 1400°C while retaining inert handling and multi-step processing [Reasonable Inference from TS4]. TS4 directly supports 300 MPa compaction and Ar sintering, but direct SPS/hot-press synthesis is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50. The route remains penalized for controlled atmosphere and actinide precursor handling.

**F3 — Route 3**  
Pressure-assisted contact should improve powder reaction kinetics relative to Route 2 [Reasonable Inference], and TS4 supports compacted carbide pellets before high-T reaction. The route still requires C/N ordering with no direct Np–C–N analogue, so structural reconstruction is only moderate. This is why Route 3 is best overall but not high-confidence.

**F2 — Route 4**  
Melt growth is severe because CS4 uses ~1500°C, and nitrogen retention likely requires sealed or buffered conditions [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct melt-growth evidence for this target and are neutral 50. The route is mainly useful for crystals, not as the safest first synthesis attempt.

**F3 — Route 4**  
Liquid-phase diffusion is favorable by analogy to CS4, but crystallizing a low-symmetry Np–C–N phase from a melt with controlled N content is risky [Reasonable Inference]. Direct nitrogen-bearing melt evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50. Kinetics are acceptable, but phase selection is uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target does not trigger the three specified amorphization checks: it has one cation species, likely 13 atoms per formula cell, and no reported face-sharing coexistence in the target description. CS1 shows even more complex edge/corner/face-sharing carbide networks can crystallize, which supports low amorphization risk by analogy. Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide target-specific glass-forming or disorder data, so direct source signal is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Np₄C₅N₄ is best interpreted as a plausibly metastable or near-stable phase rather than a confirmed ground state, because its E_hull is not reported and the negative formation energy is not a convex-hull validation. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support high-temperature synthesis of actinide carbide frameworks by arc melting, sintering, compaction, and melt processing, especially Np₂C₃-like and U/Pu carbide systems (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS4). The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for crystalline actinide–carbon networks with similar Np/U/Pu–C distances and C–C units (SS1, SS2, TS2). The main limiting factor is the absence of direct Np–C–N synthesis evidence, especially nitrogen retention and C/N ordering in a low-symmetry P1 phase; this is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral 50. Overall, the target is synthetically plausible but likely requires careful inert/pressure-assisted processing and iterative phase-purity optimization.
