### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has a predicted formation energy of −0.369 eV/atom, which supports chemical plausibility, but it has no reported candidate-specific E_hull or phonon validation [Target]. TS analogues are all listed at E_hull = 0 and therefore strongly support nearby stable cyanamide/carbodiimide chemistry (TS1–TS5). CS is mixed because chemically similar actinide carbides include both hull-stable entries and a high-E_hull UFeC2 entry, while SS analogues show experimentally made C–N frameworks despite moderate listed hull values (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). The final F1 score is conservative because the target cannot be assigned to the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin without an actual convex-hull calculation [Target].

**F2 — Route 1**  
Route 1 benefits from room-temperature precipitation and simple vacuum drying demonstrated for Mn/Co dicyanamides (SS1, SS3). The deductions come from vacuum processing, multi-step dehydration/annealing, likely sealed handling for C/N retention, and Np radiochemical sensitivity [SS1/SS3; Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this aqueous route and are treated as neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1**  
The kinetic advantage is high precursor activity because Na[N(CN)2] reacts readily with aqueous metal salts in SS analogues (SS1, SS3). The limiting kinetic factor is structural reconstruction, because SS analogues are M(C2N3)2 frameworks whereas the target is metal-rich Np3(C2N3)2 with direct Np–C bonds [SS1/SS3; Target; Reasonable Inference]. This route may be more useful for making an Np–dicyanamide precursor than for directly crystallizing the final target [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a plausible but unvalidated metastable or near-ground-state candidate, because its formation energy is negative but no candidate-specific convex-hull or phonon calculation is reported [Target]. The strongest synthesis support comes from TS cyanamide/carbodiimide metathesis analogues, while SS dicyanamide analogues support the existence of related C2N3 coordination frameworks (TS1–TS3; SS1/SS3). CS analogues support actinide–carbon bonding and high-temperature actinide carbide synthesis, but they do not directly support preserving a C2N3 framework in Np3(C2N3)2 (CS1/CS4/CS5; Reasonable Inference). The key enabling factor is sealed, moderate-temperature C–N metathesis/flux chemistry that can provide C/N mobility without fully decomposing the C2N3 motif (TS1–TS3). The key limiting factor is the absence of any exact Np3(C2N3)2 analogue, combined with Np redox/oxygen sensitivity and likely competition from Np carbides, nitrides, and simpler cyanamides [Target; CS4/CS5; TS4/TS5; Reasonable Inference].
