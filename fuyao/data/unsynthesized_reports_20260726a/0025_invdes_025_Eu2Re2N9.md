### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability:** The direct target convex-hull energy is unavailable, so the target cannot be assigned a true ground-state score from the given hull table. TS analogues are favorable because TS1–TS5 all report E_hull = 0.0, but CS and SS are mixed because CS3/CS4/SS1/SS3 include high-hull or metastable N-rich analogues. The resulting 60/100 reflects positive formation-energy/analogue support but high uncertainty due to missing target-specific hull and phonons.

**F2/F3 — Route 1:** Flux growth is supported by CS5 and nitride sintering by TS2, but the high-temperature sealed nitride environment creates strong severity deductions. Kinetics are weak-to-moderate because Li₃N/Ga flux can improve transport, while Re remains refractory and the target framework requires substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2:** Route 2 has the best balance because TS1/TS5/SS4 show 450–650 °C metathesis/flux chemistry under sealed inert conditions. Its F3 is the highest because halides and active nitrogen donors should react faster than dense metals, while molten LiCl/KCl reduces diffusion limits; the main uncertainty is whether the all-nitride Eu–Re–N framework forms instead of simpler EuN/ReNₓ phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3:** High-pressure azide decomposition is strongly supported for N–N-containing diazenides by TS3/TS4, but it receives a severe F2 penalty from >5 GPa operation. Its kinetics benefit from energetic azide precursors, yet Re diffusion and mixed Eu/Re ordering during short high-pressure dwell times remain limiting [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4:** Ammonothermal synthesis is directly relevant to Eu–N chemistry through CS4 and indirectly relevant to 5d nitridation through TS2. It is penalized by NH₃ pressure and uncertain Re activation, because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates ammonothermal Eu–Re nitride formation.

**F2/F3 — Route 5:** Solution azide crystallization is mild and well supported for N-rich azides in CS3/SS1/SS3/SS5. It is not recommended as the main route because no CS/SS/TS source shows that such solution chemistry can build an extended Eu–Re nitride framework, so its structural-reconstruction score is very low.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency:** The largest amorphization risk is the large Eu/Re cation mismatch and mixed coordination environments, which is [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure and related mixed-cation nitride analogues in CS5/TS2. The atom-count and full vertex+edge+face-sharing criteria are not triggered, so the F4 score remains moderately favorable rather than high-risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Eu₂Re₂N₉ is more likely a metastable or narrowly stabilized phase than a confidently established ground-state phase because the target lacks a reported E_hull, even though TS analogues are ground-state-like with E_hull = 0.0. The CS/SS/TS evidence supports pieces of the chemistry: Eu–N formation from CS4, rare-earth/transition-metal nitride flux chemistry from CS5, metathesis routes from TS1/TS5/SS4, and N–N-rich motifs from CS3/SS1/SS3/TS3/TS4. The most important enabling factor is a high-activity nitrogen source in a molten-salt or flux medium, which can reduce diffusion barriers and preserve nitrogen-rich bonding [Reasonable Inference] from TS1/TS5/SS4/CS5. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Re nitridation, Eu valence, and N–N retention without forming EuN/EuN₂/ReNₓ or azide side phases [Reasonable Inference] from CS4/TS2/TS3/TS4. Overall, the recommended Route 2 gives a plausible but challenging synthesis path, not a high-confidence routine synthesis.

|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | No target-specific E_hull or phonon validation | High | Treat first experiments as screening and validate by PXRD plus composition; TS stability analogues help but do not prove the target phase. |
| 2 | All | Competing EuN, EuN₂, ReNₓ, or salt/azide phases | High | Use staged heating, small stoichiometry screens, and repeated annealing/flux optimization as in TS2/CS5/TS5. |
| 3 | All | Re diffusion and nitridation kinetics | High | Use halide metathesis or molten-salt/flux transport rather than dense Re-only reactions, based on SS4/TS5/CS5. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Re chloride volatility or ampoule side reactions | Med–High | Use sealed metal-lined ampoules, staged ramps, and excess nitrogen donor [Reasonable Inference] from TS1/SS4. |
| 5 | Route 1 / Route 4 | Eu air sensitivity and Eu valence control | High | Load in glovebox and maintain controlled nitrogen/NH₃ activity, following Eu/NH₃ handling in CS4. |
| 6 | Route 3 / Route 5 | Energetic azide or HN₃ safety | High | Use micro-scale handling, light/heat control, and containment strategies consistent with CS3/SS1/TS3/TS4. |
| 7 | All | Nitrogen loss or N–N bond destruction at high temperature | High | Favor sealed high-N-activity routes and lower-temperature metathesis before >1000 °C flux growth, based on TS1/TS5/SS4 versus CS5. |
| 8 | All | Crystal growth difficulty for low-symmetry P1 phase | Medium | Use slow cooling or recrystallization/flux growth strategies; CS5 and SS5 show slow crystallization improves structural-quality products. |
