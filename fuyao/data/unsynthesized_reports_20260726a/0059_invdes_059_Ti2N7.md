### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.** The target has no reported E_hull, and the 0.000 eV/atom value is explicitly only a similarity-search placeholder [Target]. The negative ML formation energy of −0.322 eV/atom is encouraging but not equivalent to convex-hull or phonon stability [Target]. TS analogues are mostly on-hull nitrides/diazenides, but CS includes high-E_hull N-rich azides, so the thermodynamic signal is mixed and only moderately favorable [TS-1–TS-5, CS-1, CS-2].

**Route 1 F2/F3.** Route 1 is condition-severe because the best analogues require multianvil pressures of 3–12 GPa and hazardous azide handling [SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2]. Its kinetics score is highest because azide decomposition directly supplies N–N-rich chemistry, and the target N–N distances closely match diazenide/azide analogues [Target, SS-1, TS-1, CS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is Ti-specific: no CS/SS/TS entry reports Ti₂N₇ or a titanium polynitride made by this exact route.

**Route 2 F2/F3.** The sealed-autoclave route has milder pressure than Route 1 and is supported by MgMoN₂ formation from Mg/Mo/NaN₃ at 700 °C [TS-4]. Its kinetics are capped because Ti₂N₇ requires N–N retention and very high nitrogen content, while TS-4 demonstrates a ternary nitride rather than a Ti polynitride [TS-4, Target]. **Insufficient evidence from SS** supports this autoclave route to Ti₂N₇, so SS route support is neutral.

**Route 3 F2/F3.** Flux routes are supported for structurally similar FeNCN/CoCN₂ and chemically similar MnP₄ polymorphs, indicating improved crystallization and diffusion [SS-3, SS-5, CS-5]. The route remains speculative for Ti₂N₇ because the analogues contain NCN or P networks, not a Ti₂N₇ N–N polynitride framework [Target, SS-3, CS-5]. **Insufficient evidence from TS** supports flux growth of Ti₂N₇, so TS route support is neutral.

**Route 4 F2/F3.** CS provides strong evidence that N-rich azide compounds can crystallize by liquid-ammonia or diffusion routes under mild temperatures [CS-1, CS-2]. Transferability is weak because Ti₂N₇ is an extended Ti–N framework, while the CS analogues are Mg/Cu azide-type materials [Target, CS-1, CS-2]. **Insufficient evidence from SS and TS** supports solution-grown Ti₂N₇, so those route signals are neutral.

**Route 5 F2/F3.** Ammonolysis is experimentally supported for nitrides such as MnMoN₂ and FeWN₂ under flowing NH₃ at 625–700 °C [TS-5]. Its structural-reconstruction score is low because NH₃ analogues do not demonstrate preservation or formation of N–N polynitride units [TS-5, Target]. **Insufficient evidence from CS and SS** supports NH₃-only synthesis of Ti₂N₇, so those route signals are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂N₇ is best treated as a **possibly metastable or pressure-stabilized N-rich titanium polynitride**, not a confirmed ground-state phase, because no candidate-specific E_hull or phonon validation is reported [Target]. The strongest support comes from SS/TS diazenide analogues with short N–N bonds made by high-pressure azide decomposition, which closely matches the target’s 1.19–1.30 Å N–N motif [Target, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2]. CS supports that very N-rich azide-like solids can be isolated, but CS also shows hazardous handling and difficult phase control for N-rich materials [CS-1, CS-2]. The key enabling factor is **high nitrogen chemical potential under pressure**, which should suppress N₂ loss and stabilize N–N units [SS-1, TS-1, Reasonable Inference]. The key limiting factor is the absence of direct Ti₂N₇ thermodynamic validation and the likely competition with simpler Ti–N products or nitrogen loss [Target, Reasonable Inference].
