### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target has negative predicted formation energy, which supports exothermic compound formation but does not prove ground-state stability because no target E_hull or phonons are reported (Target data). CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly stable or near-stable, including several E_hull = 0 entries and CS examples at 0.008–0.174 eV/atom, which supports a moderate stability prior (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is high because CS/SS/TS do not report the actual VRu2 convex-hull position, so direct target-hull evidence is insufficient from CS, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Arc-melting has severe high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere requirements, but these are standard across the closest V-containing and Ru-containing intermetallic analogues (CS1, CS5, SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4). Kinetics are moderate because liquid melting and repeated remelting improve mixing, while long anneals remain necessary for ordering and phase purity (CS1, CS5, SS2, TS2). The best transferability comes from Ru-containing structural analogues and the close Nb–Ru bcc-like local environment in SS4/TS4 (SS4, TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Powder/vacuum annealing avoids the most extreme arc-melting step but still requires controlled atmosphere, multiple processing steps, and long high-temperature anneals (CS5, SS3, TS3). Kinetics are poor because refractory metal powders have low activity and V/Ru diffusion is expected to be slow without a liquid-mixing step ([Reasonable Inference]; CS5, SS3, TS3). This route is therefore useful mainly as a secondary ordering or phase-screening route rather than the primary synthesis route ([Reasonable Inference]).

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Melt-spinning is supported only by CS2, and SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this route, so SS and TS are neutral route-transfer signals at 50 (CS2; insufficient evidence from SS/TS). The route could help if VRu2 is metastable, but CS2 also warns that rapid quenching can yield amorphous ribbons over limited composition windows (CS2). The main uncertainty is whether annealing can crystallize the desired I4/mmm VRu2 phase rather than a competing V–Ru phase or amorphous/crystalline mixture (Target data; [Reasonable Inference]).

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Flux growth has a real SS/TS precedent, but it is chemically distant because the example is a technetium phosphide rather than a binary V–Ru intermetallic (SS5, TS5). Flux improves transport but introduces sealed-tube, volatile iodine, and flux-inclusion risks (SS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). This route is best viewed as exploratory single-crystal growth after bulk phase existence is confirmed by Route 1 ([Reasonable Inference]).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
The target appears structurally simple, with small VRu2 stoichiometry and no reported mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network in the target description, so amorphization risk is scored low (Target data). CS2 shows that melt-spinning related alloys can become amorphous, but that is route-specific rather than a target-structure trigger (CS2). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target ionic radii and exact target cell atom count, so those source-specific signals are neutral at 50 where not supplemented by target data ([Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VRu2 should be treated as a plausible but unconfirmed ground-state candidate: its predicted formation energy is negative, but no candidate-specific convex-hull or phonon evidence is reported (Target data). The analogue set strongly supports bulk metallurgical synthesis of related refractory and Ru-containing intermetallics by arc-melting plus annealing, especially Route 1 (CS1, CS5, SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4). The most important enabling factor is the availability of direct elemental alloying routes with repeated remelting and gettered inert atmospheres, which are repeatedly successful in CS/SS/TS analogues (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS2, TS2). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: no exact VRu2 I4/mmm analogue or target E_hull is provided, so competing V–Ru phases could be favored (Target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target hull). Overall, the material is moderately synthesizable, with best odds from arc-melting followed by systematic annealing and XRD phase screening (CS1, CS5, SS2, TS2; [Reasonable Inference]).
