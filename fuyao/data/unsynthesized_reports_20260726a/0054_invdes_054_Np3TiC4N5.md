### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability**  
The target lacks reported E_hull, so the 0.000 eV/atom placeholder must not be used as stability evidence; the negative predicted formation energy only supports stability relative to elements, not against competing phases. TS analogues are hull-stable, SS analogues are mostly near-hull but include a high-energy UC₂ entry, and CS analogues are mixed but include stable carbide/nitride-related materials. (CS, SS, TS)

**F2/F3 — Route 1**  
Arc melting has strong transferability because SS/TS actinide–transition-metal carbides are made by arc melting and annealing under Ar, but the route is severe because it requires high temperature, inert atmosphere, multi-step processing, and actinide handling. Kinetics are helped by melt formation, but N retention and crystallization of a P1 Np–Ti carbonitride remain extrapolated. (SS, TS; **[Reasonable Inference]**)

**F2/F3 — Route 2**  
Solid-state sintering is well supported by CS/SS/TS high-temperature carbide examples, including Ta₂S₂C, UC/UC₂-derived carbides, and U₂RuC₂-type sintering. Its kinetic score is low because refractory binary carbides/nitrides diffuse slowly and repeated grinding/annealing is likely required. (CS, SS, TS)

**F2/F3 — Route 3**  
Metathesis has the lowest thermal severity and highest precursor activity because CS directly shows RECl₃ + Li₂CN₂/Li₃N reactions in sealed Ta ampoules at ~800 °C. Its major uncertainty is structural transfer: CS makes chloride carbodiimide nitrides, while SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for metathesis crystallizing dense Np–Ti carbonitrides. (CS; insufficient evidence from SS/TS)

**F2/F3 — Route 4**  
Carbothermic carbonitridation is chemically plausible because SS/TS show actinide/rare-earth carbides can be made from oxides and carbon at high temperature. It scores poorly on severity and reconstruction because oxygen removal, nitrogen incorporation, and quaternary phase ordering must all occur in one route. (SS, TS; **[Reasonable Inference]**)

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency**  
Only the cation-size mismatch trigger is assigned because Np/Ti size mismatch is expected to exceed 15%, while the target formula count is not >40 and face-sharing is not reported. SS/TS analogues show large actinide/transition-metal size mismatch can still crystallize, so the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. (SS, TS; **[Reasonable Inference]**)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Np₃TiC₄N₅ should be treated as an unvalidated near-ground-state or metastable candidate because its E_hull is not reported, even though the predicted formation energy is negative. TS and SS analogues support the broader feasibility of actinide/transition-metal carbide frameworks, while CS provides the strongest precedent for reactive C/N precursor chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is access to high-reactivity C/N chemistry through Li₂CN₂/Li₃N or preformed nitride/carbonitride powders. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Np radiological handling, N/C stoichiometry, and competing actinide carbide/nitride/oxide phases. Overall, synthesis appears **moderately feasible but experimentally demanding**. (CS, SS, TS; **[Reasonable Inference]**)
