### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. Close SS/TS analogues reinforce this: Hg3S2I2 is on the hull (SS-3, TS-1), Hg–S–Br/Cl is nearly on the hull at 0.00145 eV/atom (SS-1, TS-2), and Hg3S2ICl is low at 0.0139 eV/atom (SS-2). CS is less decisive because chemically similar Hg compounds span from stable/near-stable to highly metastable, so CS is treated as mixed rather than overriding the exact target hull.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because the closest Hg3S2I2 analogue forms at 260 °C in evacuated sealed ampoules (SS-3, TS-1). The deductions come from vacuum/sealed-tube handling and Hg toxicity, both consistent with Hg–S–halide syntheses using evacuated ampoules (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2). The main uncertainty is that HgF2 may be more corrosive and less structurally tolerant than heavier Hg halides, which is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly proven by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is favorable because Hg3S2I2 forms from HgS + HgI2 at only 260 °C in 2–3 days (SS-3, TS-1), and the target preserves very similar Hg–S coordination distances to the iodide/chloride/bromide analogues (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). The precursor activity is not maximal because HgS is a binary sulfide solid, but Hg-halide mobility should help reaction progress [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is the F-for-I structural contraction and whether Hg3S2F2 nucleates before binary HgS/HgF2 segregation.

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT is experimentally proven for Hg–S–Br/Cl frameworks in evacuated quartz ampoules at 500–540 °C with controlled gradients and repeated distillation (SS-1, TS-2). This route receives larger severity deductions because it requires vacuum, volatile mercury halides, a multi-step gradient protocol, and toxic Hg precursors (SS-1, SS-2, TS-2). The absence of any fluoride CVT example in CS/SS/TS makes the severity effectively higher than for heavier halides.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor growth can produce high-quality related Hg–S–halide crystals, as shown for Hg–S–Br/Cl and Hg3S2ICl (SS-1, SS-2, TS-2). However, SS-1 reports polymorphous modifications and statistical halogen occupation, indicating that composition and phase control can be delicate. Fluoride volatility/reactivity and possible ampoule attack are inferred uncertainties, so the kinetic/composition score is only moderate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal synthesis avoids vacuum and very high temperature; CuHgSBr forms at 670 K in sealed hydrothermal conditions (SS-4), and Bi2O2S forms at 673 K and 98 MPa (CS-3). The route still has deductions for sealed volatile/corrosive fluoride media, multiple processing steps, and Hg/HF hazards [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for hydrothermal Hg3S2F2, so the route is plausible but not primary.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal halide media can strongly improve transport, as CuHgSBr forms quantitatively with no observed side products from HgS in concentrated HBr (SS-4). This supports good precursor activity and diffusion relative to dry solid-state synthesis. The structural reconstruction score is low because CuHgSBr is not an exact Hg3S2X2 analogue, and no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates an anhydrous Hg–S–F framework from solution.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Hg, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description suggests a relatively ordered crystalline framework with a limited number of inequivalent sites, and no provided evidence indicates a >40-atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity. CS/SS/TS analogues show crystalline Hg–S–halide frameworks can be obtained by sealed-tube or CVT routes (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3), supporting low amorphization risk, though they do not directly prove the fluoride analogue.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg3(SF)2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS: Hg3S2I2 has the same Hg3S2X2 stoichiometry, very similar Hg–S distances, and is synthesized directly from HgS + HgI2 at 260 °C in sealed ampoules (SS-3, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the established stability and low-temperature formation of Hg–S–halide frameworks in sealed systems (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is that the provided data contain no demonstrated fluoride analogue, so Hg–F bonding, HgF2 volatility/corrosion, and F stoichiometry control remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite promising, with Route 1 being the most direct and experimentally defensible approach.
