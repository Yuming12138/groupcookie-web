### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

NaCoS2 has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the target is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase by the supplied target data. CS supports that chemically related Na–transition-metal sulfides such as Na2Mn2S3 can be on hull, while NaVS2 analogues are only modestly above hull but still experimentally prepared (CS). TS strongly supports thermodynamic viability because several similar sulfides are E_hull = 0, including KCu2AsS3, NaCu4S4, KCr3S5, LiCrS2, and TlCr3S5 (TS). SS is mixed because some structurally similar Ag/Cu–Cr sulfides are high above hull, so SS is not uniformly supportive for thermodynamics (SS).

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct elements

The direct route inherits moderate severity from NaVS2, which required evacuated quartz tubes, slow prereaction near 200 °C, 600 °C for 4 days, and quenching (CS). The main penalties are sealed/vacuum atmosphere, volatile Na/S containment, multistep heating, and air-sensitive Na metal [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for elemental Na–Co–S synthesis, and TS only indirectly supports evacuated elemental ampoule reactions through Tl–Cr–S (TS). Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether Co forms the desired NaCoS2 phase rather than binary cobalt sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct elements

Kinetics are moderate because elemental Co is less reactive than sulfide or flux precursors [Reasonable Inference], although NaVS2 demonstrates that elemental Na/M/S reactions can form layered NaMS2 at only 600 °C (CS). Diffusion is helped by molten/volatile sulfur and sodium species during sealed-tube heating [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the low synthesis temperature relative to refractory transition metals in NaVS2 (CS). Structural reconstruction is favorable because NaVS2 has NaS6 and VS6 octahedra similar to NaCoS2 (CS), but stacking symmetry differs and Co redox behavior is not directly tested [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux growth

Flux growth requires controlled atmosphere, sealed-tube volatile sulfur handling, slow cooling, and often inert-condition washing, as shown for Na2Mn2S3 and NaCu4S4 (CS, SS, TS). It avoids >1000 °C temperatures and high pressure, which keeps severity moderate rather than severe (CS, SS, TS). The route still receives penalties for multistep processing and air-sensitive sulfide fluxes, especially Na2S/BaS [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS all give useful route evidence here.

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux growth

Flux chemistry gives the best kinetics because Na2S/S or Na2CO3/S melts provide high chemical activity and short diffusion lengths, as demonstrated by NaCu4S4 at 500 °C and Na2Mn2S3 at 870 K (CS, SS, TS). Slow cooling supports crystal nucleation and ordering, which is directly reported for NaCu4S4 and Na2Mn2S3 (CS, SS, TS). The main kinetic uncertainty is phase selectivity: NaCu4S4 required a specific mixed flux, and Na2Mn2S3 had MnS contamination concerns (CS, SS, TS). Thus F3 is high but not near-perfect.

#### F2 — Route 3: Binary sulfide solid-state

Binary sulfide reactions in evacuated ampoules are directly supported for CuCrS2/AgCrS2 analogues (SS) and alkali layered LiTiS2 from Li2S/Ti/S (CS). Severity is moderate because temperatures are typically 750–1000 °C and vacuum sealing is required, but no high pressure is used (CS, SS). The route is penalized for sealed volatile sulfide conditions, possible regrinding/reannealing, and air-sensitive Na2S [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for NaCoS2 binary sulfide synthesis, although it supports sulfide stability generally (TS).

#### F3 — Route 3: Binary sulfide solid-state

Binary sulfide precursors should be more kinetically active than elemental Co because the sulfide framework is partly preformed [Reasonable Inference], and SS confirms binary chalcogenides can yield layered Cr sulfides (SS). However, LiTiS2 required long heating and additional H2S treatment to improve purity, showing that layered alkali transition-metal sulfides can have slow ordering and impurity persistence (CS). Structural reconstruction is moderate because NaCoS2 is a simple layered sulfide, but the exact P-3m1 ordering may require annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is the Co sulfide phase diagram and sodium activity control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4: CS2 sulfurization / CVT-like

The CS2 route is more severe because LiCrS2 synthesis used 800–1100 °C in Ar saturated with CS2 and slow cooling (TS). It is penalized for T >1000 °C if the crystal-growth condition is used, reactive gas flow, volatile/toxic CS2, and multistep gas-flow operation (TS, [Reasonable Inference]). CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for CS2 sulfurization of NaCoS2 specifically. The route is plausible but less attractive than flux due to safety and contamination concerns (TS).

#### F3 — Route 4: CS2 sulfurization / CVT-like

CS2 is an effective sulfurizing/transport agent for LiCrS2 from oxide/carbonate precursors (TS), giving a reasonable kinetic pathway for sulfide formation. The structural analogue LiCrS2 is P-3m1 and contains octahedral LiS6/CrS6 layers similar to the target’s NaS6/CoS6 layers (TS, target structure). However, conversion from oxide/carbonate to NaCoS2 requires major reconstruction and could suffer oxygen or carbon contamination, which was noted as an issue in LiCrS2 synthesis (TS). Co- and Na-specific volatility under CS2 flow is not directly evidenced, so uncertainty remains high [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple NaCoS2 formula and small P-3m1 layered structure, so the unit-cell-size amorphization check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The Na–Co ionic radius mismatch is large enough to trigger one risk factor [Reasonable Inference], but the structure has chemically distinct Na and Co sites, which helps accommodate size mismatch (target structure). The target description reports edge-sharing CoS6 octahedra but does not establish mixed vertex/edge/face sharing coexistence, so CS/SS/TS are insufficient to trigger C3 for the target. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaCoS2 is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: CS provides close NaMS2 and Na–Mn–S chemical analogues, SS/TS provide Na sulfide flux and layered chalcogenide precedents, and TS supports thermodynamic stability across related sulfides (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature Na polysulfide/sulfide fluxes, which can overcome diffusion limits and tune sulfur chemical potential (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against competing Co sulfides and Na-rich sulfides, because related Na sulfide systems show contamination or flux specificity (CS, SS, TS). Overall, the material appears synthesizable with moderate difficulty, with flux growth as the best initial experimental route.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile Na/S loss and ampoule pressure buildup | High | Use sealed evacuated tubes with slow prereaction heating near 200 °C, as done for NaVS2 (CS); avoid overfilling and use staged ramps [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | All | Competing binary cobalt sulfides or Na sulfides | High | Use sulfur/Na chemical-potential control via flux or small excess Na/S; phase-control issues in Na2Mn2S3 and NaVS2 show the need for optimized heat treatment (CS). |
| 3 | Route 2 | Flux specificity and product separation | Medium | Use slow cooling and controlled flux ratios; NaCu4S4 required a specific Na/Ba/S flux and washing under N2, indicating ratio and workup control are important (SS, TS). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Rapid exothermic Na/S reaction during heating | High | Adopt the NaVS2 staged heating protocol: slow heating to ~200 °C before high-temperature anneal (CS). |
| 5 | Route 3 | Slow ordering and incomplete reaction from binary sulfides | Medium | Use pelletizing, regrinding, and repeated annealing; prolonged anneals are used for LiTiS2 and structurally similar chalcogenides (CS, SS). |
| 6 | Route 4 | CS2 toxicity, flammability, and carbon contamination | High | Prefer Route 2 unless CS2 is necessary; if used, apply controlled Ar/CS2 flow and monitor carbon, as LiCrS2 synthesis reported carbon contamination concerns (TS). |
| 7 | All | Stoichiometry sensitivity of layered alkali transition-metal sulfides | Medium | Use sealed tubes, slight Na/S excess screening, and compare quenched versus slow-cooled products; NaVS2 showed sodium-content sensitivity and phase changes (CS). |
| 8 | Route 2 | Crystal growth difficulty | Medium | Use slow cooling rates of ~4 °C h⁻¹, directly supported by NaCu4S4 flux growth (SS, TS), and very slow cooling from melt as used for Na2Mn2S3 (CS). |
