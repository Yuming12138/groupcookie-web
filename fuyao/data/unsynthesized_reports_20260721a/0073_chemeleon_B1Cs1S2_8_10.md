### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the computed convex hull. Thermodynamic analogues RbBS₂, KBS₂, Cs₃BS₃, CsLi₂BS₃, and LiBa(BS₂)₃ also lie at or near the hull, supporting stability in related alkali thioborate chemistry (TS1–TS5). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase selectivity, especially because thioborate syntheses are reported to be moisture- and container-sensitive (CS1, SS1, TS1).

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because close thioborate analogues are made at 400–800 °C or 650–720 °C (CS1, SS3, TS3). Severity deductions come from controlled dry/inert or low-pressure sealed conditions, volatile sulfur in sealed tubes, multiple operational steps, and air-sensitive alkali sulfides (CS1, SS3, TS3). CS/SS/TS evidence is consistent for these penalties; no major contradiction is present.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Metal sulfides + amorphous B + S are known working precursors for thioborates, supporting moderate precursor activity (CS1, SS1, TS1). Kinetics remain limited by refractory boron and the need to form a specific monoclinic CsBS₂ framework rather than the trigonal R-3c Rb/K/NaBS₂ structure (SS1, SS2, SS5). Slow or programmed heating/cooling reported for analogues indicates nontrivial ordering kinetics (CS1, SS2, TS1).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses moderate temperatures but still requires sealed evacuated/carbon-coated tubes and sulfur/chalcogen vapor management, as seen in CsBSe₃ and Cs thioborate syntheses (CS2, SS3, TS3). Flux/slow-cooling introduces additional steps, so the severity remains comparable to Route 1 (CS2, CS3). The uncertainty is that an actual Cs–B–S self-flux window is not directly supplied by CS/SS/TS.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
A sulfide/sulfur-rich melt should improve transport and crystallization relative to dry powder diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and slow-cooled CsBSe₃ plus KP₂Te flux growth support this general chalcogenide strategy (CS2, CS3). The route scores best kinetically because liquid/flux-assisted transport can help incorporate B and grow ordered crystals. However, SS gives insufficient direct evidence for a CsBS₂ flux route, so this kinetic advantage is less certain than the solid-state route.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Direct fusion can be performed below 1000 °C by analogy to CsFeS₂ fusion and thioborate temperature windows (CS4, CS1). It has fewer formal synthesis steps but uses more hazardous elemental Cs and volatile sulfur, requiring sealed and dry/inert conditions (CS4, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Evidence from CS4/CS5 is chemically less specific to boron thioborates than CS1/SS1/TS1.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Direct elemental synthesis has poorer kinetics because refractory B must react with molten/reactive Cs–S mixtures to form a specific thioborate framework [Reasonable Inference]. Although direct elemental or fusion routes are demonstrated for RbSbS₂ and CsFeS₂ (CS4, CS5), closest thioborate analogues preferentially use metal sulfides, B, and S (CS1, SS1, TS1). Therefore, structural reconstruction is expected to be more difficult and competing binary/ternary sulfides more likely [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines very large Cs coordination with small trigonal-planar B–S units, so the cation-size/coordination mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference supported by analogue structures in CS1/SS1/TS1]. However, many crystalline alkali thioborates and Cs-containing thioborates exist, showing that this chemistry can crystallize under controlled conditions (CS1, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS4). Unit-cell size and exact mixed polyhedral sharing for the target are insufficiently evidenced from CS/SS/TS, so those checks are treated neutrally rather than penalized.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsBS₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several close alkali thioborate analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis case is strong because RbBS₂ is simultaneously chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically closest, and K/Na ABS₂ plus Cs₃BS₃/CsLi₂BS₃ provide additional structural and chemical precedent (CS1, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated formation of trigonal-planar BS₃ thioborate networks from alkali sulfides, amorphous B, and S in sealed carbon-compatible containers (CS1, SS3, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase/control sensitivity: thioborates are reported to be moisture-sensitive, reactive with containers, and difficult to obtain as pure well-defined phases (CS1, SS1, TS1). Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires rigorous sealed-tube technique and careful sulfur chemical-potential control.
