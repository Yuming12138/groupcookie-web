### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic stability category by the provided rubric. Chemically and structurally similar analogues are also predominantly hull-stable: KAsSe₂, K₂TeSe₃, Cs(SbSe₂)₂, RbSbS₂, and Rb₄PbP₂Se₈ all list E_hull = 0.0 in CS/SS/TS. The only slightly off-hull CS analogue is RbMnSe₂ at 0.024 eV/atom, still below 0.05 eV/atom (CS3). Remaining uncertainty is computational: a zero hull energy strongly supports equilibrium synthesizability, but it does not guarantee the C2/c polymorph is the experimentally selected low-temperature product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has low temperature severity because the closest solvothermal analogues form at 115–160 °C rather than high temperature (CS1, SS4, TS4). The score is reduced because sealed ampoules, inert/vacuum handling, long duration, and toxic Sb₂Se₃ are involved (CS1, SS4, TS4; toxicity is [Reasonable Inference]). CS/SS/TS provide no evidence for high pressure or >1000 °C requirements, so those penalties are not applied. The main uncertainty is whether RbSbSe₂ forms as the desired 1:1:2 phase rather than a related Rb–Sb–Se phase such as Cs(SbSe₂)₂-type chemistry (SS4, TS4).

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because carbonate/chalcogenide solvothermal reactions form related alkali pnictogen selenides at low temperature over 7–10 days (CS1, SS4, TS4). Solution-mediated transport reduces diffusion limitations compared with solid-state elemental synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because Sb₂Se₃ must reorganize, but Sb–Se see-saw coordination similar to the target appears in Cs(SbSe₂)₂ (SS4, TS4). The uncertainty is phase selectivity, since solvothermal chalcogenide systems can yield multiple products as seen in Cs–Te–Se chemistry (CS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids extreme pressure and likely avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, since related sealed-tube alkali chalcogenides are made at 850–1000 °C or 900 °C (CS3, CS4, SS2, TS2). Penalties arise from evacuated sealed tubes, Se volatility, multistep prereaction/annealing, and use of air-sensitive Rb metal [Reasonable Inference; evacuated/sealed methods in CS3/CS4]. CS/SS/TS do not show a direct RbSbSe₂ solid-state recipe, so the score retains uncertainty. The method is chemically credible but operationally more severe than the solvothermal route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental precursors are less kinetically active than solvothermal precursors, so precursor activity is moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion is helped by low-melting Rb and Se and by possible transient liquid/vapor transport in a sealed tube [Reasonable Inference], consistent with elemental alkali chalcogenide synthesis in evacuated ampoules (CS4, SS2, TS2). Structural reconstruction is complete, but direct elemental synthesis is supported for RbSbS₂ (SS5, TS5). The uncertainty is whether the C2/c RbSbSe₂ structure orders cleanly during cooling or competes with other Rb–Sb–Se phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures around 450–500 °C in the Rb₄PbP₂Se₈ analogue (SS3, TS3), so no high-temperature penalty applies. The route is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Se flux, multistep heating/slow cooling/flux removal, and air-sensitive Rb₂Se [Reasonable Inference; SS3/TS3 vacuum tube and flux procedure]. CS/SS/TS give insufficient evidence for direct RbSbSe₂ flux formation, so transferability is inferential. The method is attractive for crystals but less straightforward than carbonate solvothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are favorable because A₂Se + excess Se flux successfully produces Rb-containing selenides at 500 °C with slow cooling (SS3, TS3). Flux transport reduces cation diffusion barriers and promotes crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are good but not as predictably phase-selective as the best solvothermal route.
