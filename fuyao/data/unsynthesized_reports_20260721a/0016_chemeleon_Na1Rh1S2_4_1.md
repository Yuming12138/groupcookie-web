### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. The high score is further supported by multiple stable sulfide analogues at E_hull = 0.0, including NaInS₂ (CS1), NaNbS₂ (CS4), CuRh₂S₄ (SS1, TS1), NiRh₂S₄ (SS3, TS2), and KPt₂S₃ (SS4, TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field, especially for volatile Na/S systems [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, elemental sealed-tube solid-state.**  
Condition severity is moderate because analogous Rh sulfides form in evacuated quartz tubes at ~800 °C (SS1, TS1), and alkali transition-metal sulfides also form in evacuated tubes near 800 °C (CS4). Kinetics are less favorable because Rh metal is refractory and the target layered Caswellsilverite structure differs from the spinel Rh sulfide analogues (SS1–SS3), requiring substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes direct elemental synthesis of NaRhS₂ itself, so the route is plausible but not optimal.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, sulfide-precursor sealed-tube route.**  
Condition severity is moderate because Na₂S-based sealed-tube synthesis at 680 °C for a week is demonstrated for Na₄Sn₃S₈ (CS5), and Na/intercalated transition-metal sulfides are made in evacuated tubes at ~800 °C (CS4). Kinetics improve because Na₂S and Rh sulfides should shorten sulfidation pathways relative to Rh metal + S [Reasonable Inference], and RhS₆ local coordination is common in Rh sulfide analogues (SS1–SS3). The main uncertainty is Rh oxidation-state control in NaRhS₂, since CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct Rh³⁺ layered sodium rhodium sulfide precedent.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux route.**  
The flux route has harsher handling penalties because CS1 uses high-temperature flux conditions and TS4/TS5 use toxic H₂S streams for alkali sulfide formation. Its kinetic score is highest because the same Caswellsilverite R-3m structure is reported for NaInS₂ synthesized by flux methods (CS1), and fluxes provide liquid-phase mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Rh enters the same layered NaMS₂ structure instead of forming competing Rh sulfides/spinels, since the structurally similar Rh examples in SS/TS are mostly spinels (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS2).

**F2/F3 — Route 4, Bridgman/melt growth.**  
Bridgman growth is supported for LiGaSe₂-type chalcogenides with controlled translation and annealing (CS3), but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows congruent melting or Bridgman growth for NaRhS₂. The route has significant severity because Na/S volatility requires sealed ampoules and stoichiometry compensation [Reasonable Inference]. It is better considered a crystal-growth follow-up after powder synthesis rather than the primary discovery route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Only one amorphization trigger is assigned: the Na/Rh cation size mismatch is large, as reflected in target Na–S 2.89 Å versus Rh–S 2.42 Å distances and analogous Na–S/In–S separation in NaInS₂ (CS1). The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms for the simple R-3m NaRhS₂ formula [Reasonable Inference], and the target reports corner + edge sharing but not face-sharing, so mixed vertex/edge/face coexist is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that layered alkali sulfides can show stacking disorder even if they do not amorphize [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaRhS₂ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, and several related sulfides are also computed stable (CS1, CS4, SS1, TS1). The best synthesis support comes from the same-structure Caswellsilverite NaInS₂ flux analogue (CS1), while Rh–S octahedral bonding is supported by Rh sulfide spinels with Rh–S distances near the target value (SS1–SS3). The most critical enabling factor is use of a Na-rich sulfide/carbonate flux to enhance diffusion and stabilize the layered NaMS₂ topology, supported by CS1 and SS4/TS3. The most critical limiting factor is control of Na/S chemical potentials and Rh competing phases, because Na/S are volatile/reactive [Reasonable Inference] and Rh sulfide structural analogues in SS/TS favor spinel-type structures rather than layered Caswellsilverite (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS2). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful sealed or flux conditions rather than a simple open-tube reaction.
