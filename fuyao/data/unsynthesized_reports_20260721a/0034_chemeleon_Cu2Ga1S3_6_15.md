### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the supplied target data, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. TS strongly supports this because top thermodynamic analogues are stable or near-stable tetrahedral Cu sulfides, including CuGaS₂, Cu₂ZnGeS₄, Cu₂GeS₃, Cu₂CdSiS₄, and Cu₂HgGeS₄ (TS1–TS5). CS and SS also provide useful stability support through CuGaS₂ at E_hull = 0 and Cu₂GeS₃ near the hull (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee a unique ordered Cu₂GaS₃ polymorph under laboratory conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed ampoule solid-state conditions rather than high pressure, which is favorable and directly supported by CuGaS₂ and Cu₂GeS₃ syntheses (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS5). The severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and staged/multi-step heating/grinding/annealing (CS1; CS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for >1000 °C or >5 GPa, so those penalties are not applied. The main uncertainty is whether the target requires the very long 35-day treatment used for Cu₂GeS₃ or the shorter multi-day schedule used for CuGaS₂ (CS1; CS3).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental precursors are accessible but less reactive than salts or solution precursors, so precursor activity is moderate; this is consistent with long high-temperature elemental reactions in analogues (CS1; CS3). Diffusion is acceptable because analogous Cu–Ga–S and Cu₂GeS₃ phases form at 850–900 °C without exotic conditions (CS1; CS3; SS5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because both CuGaS₂ and Cu₂GeS₃ are corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfides closely related to the target (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS5). The kinetic risk is slow Cu/Ga ordering and possible competing chalcopyrite CuGaS₂ formation [Reasonable Inference from structural/chemical proximity in CS1].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CVT avoids high pressure and can operate below 1000 °C for related sulfides, which is supported by iodine transport examples (SS2; SS3; TS4; TS5). However, it requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile/corrosive iodine, volatile sulfur species, and a controlled temperature gradient (SS2; SS3; TS4; TS5). Insufficient CS evidence directly confirms iodine transport for Cu₂GaS₃, so the route is plausible but less certain. The higher severity relative to Route 1 comes mainly from iodine handling and crystal-growth control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Vapor transport can enhance mass transport, and several structurally/thermodynamically similar Cu sulfides have been grown by CVT (SS2; SS3; TS4; TS5). Crystallization is plausible because the analogues form single crystals or polyhedral crystals under iodine gradients (SS2; SS3; TS4; TS5). Composition control is the limiting kinetic/processing factor because Cu, Ga, and S may form transport species with different volatilities [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 also indicates that related CVT systems can have polymorph and phase-control challenges.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The solvothermal step is mild, as CS5 reports Cu₂SnS₃ synthesis at 453 K for 20 h. The severity score is reduced because the proposed target route likely requires post-annealing in a sealed sulfur/vacuum ampoule, and because thiourea and Ga chloride handling add chemical hazards [CS5; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient SS and TS evidence directly supports solvothermal synthesis of the target or Ga analogue, so this route is treated as extrapolative. The benefit is low temperature; the drawback is uncertain phase ordering.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solvothermal salts and thiourea provide high precursor activity, as demonstrated for Cu₂SnS₃ (CS5). Solution processing should reduce diffusion lengths and can yield fine sulfide particles [Reasonable Inference from CS5]. However, the target’s ordered Imm2 chalcopyrite-like structure is not demonstrated by CS5, and converting a low-temperature precipitate into the target phase may require substantial reconstruction and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore kinetics for initial sulfide formation are strong, but kinetics for target-phase ordering are uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target and closest analogues are corner-sharing tetrahedral networks, not mixed edge/face-sharing frameworks, which lowers amorphization risk (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS5). The main amorphization/disorder trigger is the Cu⁺/Ga³⁺ cation size and charge contrast [Reasonable Inference], which may promote cation disorder or secondary phases during fast crystallization. The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms based on the simple Cu₂GaS₃ formula and Imm2 description [Reasonable Inference], but exact crystallographic multiplicity is not confirmed by CS/SS/TS. Overall risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₂GaS₃ is likely synthesizable and may be a ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and TS analogues are stable or near-stable tetrahedral Cu sulfides (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for sealed-tube solid-state synthesis: CuGaS₂ provides direct Cu–Ga–S chemical precedent (CS1; SS1; TS1), while Cu₂GeS₃ provides the closest Imm2 chalcopyrite-like structural precedent (CS3; SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing CuS₄/GaS₄ tetrahedral sulfide framework, which is common across the close analogues (CS1; CS3; SS1; SS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and cation ordering, especially because CuGaS₂ is a very stable chemically similar phase and Cu₂GeS₃ analogues require long annealing (CS1; CS3). CVT and solvothermal routes are plausible secondary approaches, but both rely more heavily on extrapolation than the sealed-tube route (SS2–SS3; TS4–TS5; CS5).
