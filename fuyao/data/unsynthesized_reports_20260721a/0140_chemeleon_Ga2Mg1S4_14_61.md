### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. TS provides strong supporting evidence because CdGa₂S₄, ZnGa₂S₄, HgGa₂S₄, CdAl₂S₄, and BaGa₄S₇ analogues are all listed with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS ranks 1–5). CS also supports stability because MgAl₂S₄ is on the hull and CoGa₂S₄/FeGa₂S₄-type Ga sulfides have known synthesis routes, though some chemically similar Fe/Mn phases are above hull and therefore less directly stabilizing (CS ranks 1–5). SS supports the exact topology strongly through ground-state CdGa₂S₄ and ZnGa₂S₄ analogues (SS ranks 1–2).

**F2/F3 — Route 1, binary-sulfide solid state.**  
The severity score is limited by evacuated sealed ampoules, long annealing, moisture-sensitive sulfides, and volatile sulfur control; these requirements are consistent with CoGa₂S₄/ZnGa₂S₄ analogue syntheses (CS rank 4; SS/TS rank 2). Kinetics are moderate because binary sulfides are more preorganized than elements, but analogues still require long firing and sometimes homogenization/annealing, indicating slow cation ordering or diffusion (CS rank 4; SS/TS rank 2). CS gives useful Mg–sulfide precedent through MgAl₂S₄, while SS/TS give the stronger structural precedent through ZnGa₂S₄/CdGa₂S₄ (CS rank 1; SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, elemental ampoule.**  
Elemental synthesis is more severe because sulfur vapor pressure and Mg reactivity require careful low-temperature prereaction and sealed-tube control, as explicitly noted for MgAl₂S₄ synthesis (CS rank 1). Kinetics may be favorable due to liquid/volatile transport from Ga, Mg, and S at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference], but the reaction must form the ordered ternary I-4 framework from scratch. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for elemental synthesis of MgGa₂S₄ specifically, so their route-specific signal is treated as neutral for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, CVT.**  
CVT severity is moderate because it uses evacuated ampoules, halogen transport agents, and multi-day gradients, all documented in Ga–sulfide analogues (CS ranks 3, 5; SS rank 1; TS ranks 1, 4, 5). Kinetics for crystal growth are favorable because same-structure CdGa₂S₄/HgGa₂S₄ analogues are grown by CVT, but Mg transport chemistry is not directly evidenced and may complicate composition control (SS rank 1; TS rank 4; [Reasonable Inference]). CS supports Ga–S CVT generally through FeGa₂S₄/Fe₂Ga₂S₅, but those structures are not exact I-4 MgGa₂S₄ analogues (CS ranks 3, 5).

**F2/F3 — Route 4, Bridgman/melt growth.**  
The route is kinetically attractive because melt or partial-melt processing can reduce diffusion limitations, and BaGa₄S₇ shows Ga–sulfide Bridgman growth is feasible (SS rank 4; TS rank 3). However, severity is high due to >1000 °C operation, sealed ampoules, and volatile Mg/S control [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that MgGa₂S₄ melts congruently, so this route is best viewed as secondary for crystal growth rather than first-pass phase discovery.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target’s tetrahedral Mg/Ga cation radius mismatch triggers one amorphization risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The structure otherwise appears simple and corner-sharing, matching robust I-4 AB₂S₄ analogues such as CoGa₂S₄, CdGa₂S₄, ZnGa₂S₄, and HgGa₂S₄ (CS rank 4; SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2, 4). Therefore, amorphization risk is low to medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgGa₂S₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many TS analogues are also on the hull (TS ranks 1–5). The strongest support comes from structurally identical tetragonal I-4 AB₂S₄ analogues, especially CoGa₂S₄, CdGa₂S₄, ZnGa₂S₄, and HgGa₂S₄, which share the same corner-sharing tetrahedral network (CS rank 4; SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2, 4). The most important enabling factor is the existence of multiple experimentally synthesized I-4 Ga₂S₄ frameworks with divalent tetrahedral cations (CS rank 4; SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2, 4). The main limiting factor is practical sulfide processing: Mg/S volatility, moisture sensitivity of sulfides, and slow cation ordering in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference based on CS rank 1, CS rank 4, and SS/TS rank 2]. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with binary-sulfide sealed-ampoule synthesis as the most reliable first attempt.
