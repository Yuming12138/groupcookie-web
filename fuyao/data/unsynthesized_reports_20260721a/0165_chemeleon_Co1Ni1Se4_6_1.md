### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Multiple chemically and thermodynamically similar selenides are also calculated at E_hull = 0.0, including CuBrSe₃, ZnGa₂Se₄, CrGaSe₃ (CS), and NiSe₂/PtSe₂/AuCuSe₄/Nb₂Pd₃Se₈ analogues (TS). Structurally similar Co₃Se₄ and Ni(RhSe₂)₂ have small positive hull energies yet are experimentally accessible (SS), so the target’s zero-hull prediction is a strong enabling factor. Remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated ordered CoNiSe₄ phase is uniquely stable against experimentally accessible disordered Co–Ni–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State

The route is supported by direct sealed-tube syntheses of Co₃Se₄, Ni(RhSe₂)₂, Fe(RhSe₂)₂, and related selenides (SS), as well as elemental melt/anneal methods for chemically similar selenides (CS). Severity is moderate because it requires vacuum sealing, volatile Se containment, multiple heat/grind/anneal steps, and toxic Se handling. It avoids high pressure, and the recommended target temperature can likely remain ≤950 °C [Reasonable Inference], although some SS analogues used up to 1050–1080 °C. The main uncertainty is whether Co/Ni ordering develops under bulk diffusion conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1: Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State

Elemental Co/Ni powders are less reactive than molecular or salt precursors, so precursor activity is modest [Reasonable Inference], despite successful elemental routes in SS/CS. Diffusion is mitigated by Se vapor/liquid reaction pathways, consistent with selenide analogues forming below cation melting points (SS, CS). Structural reconstruction is not extreme because C2/m Co₃Se₄ and Ni(RhSe₂)₂ contain similar octahedral motifs (SS), but repeated annealing in Co₃Se₄ suggests slow phase equilibration (SS). Thus kinetics are feasible but not ideal.

#### F2 — Route 2: CVT

CVT conditions are supported by CuBrSe₃ and CrGaSe₃ in CS and Nb₂Pd₃Se₈ in TS. The route requires vacuum sealing, halogen transport agents, and long temperature-gradient growth, so condition severity is moderate to challenging. It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, as the cited CVT examples range from 310 °C to below 900 °C (CS, TS). The uncertainty is halogen compatibility with Co/Ni selenides and avoiding metal halide byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: CVT

Vapor transport improves effective precursor activity and reduces diffusion length compared with bulk solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. CVT analogues grew single crystals of chalcogen-rich selenides and complex metal selenides (CS, TS), which supports crystal growth of the target’s 2D layered motif. However, no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates CoNiSe₄ CVT or Co/Ni ordering during transport. The route is kinetically attractive but composition control may be demanding.

#### F2 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / Solvothermal

The hydrothermal NiSe₂ analogue forms at 120–210 °C using common salts and Se sources (SS), so the route has low thermal severity. It does not require evacuated ampoules or high external pressure, only autogenous autoclave pressure (SS). Deductions arise from multi-step precursor/selenization/anneal processing and toxic Co/Ni/Se reagents [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS and TS exists for hydrothermal CoNiSe₄ specifically, but SS gives a strong Ni–Se solution-route signal.

#### F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal / Solvothermal

Soluble Co/Ni salts and reactive selenium species should provide high precursor activity [Reasonable Inference], and the NiSe₂ hydrothermal synthesis demonstrates low-temperature Ni–Se crystallization (SS). Solution-mediated nanoscale conversion minimizes cation diffusion limitations compared with dense elemental routes (SS; [Reasonable Inference]). The lower structural score reflects that an ordered monoclinic CoNiSe₄ omega-derived phase is more specific than simple NiSe₂, and none of CS/SS/TS directly proves that ordered target phase from hydrothermal chemistry. Optional annealing is therefore likely important [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4: Alkali Polyselenide Flux

Flux growth is supported by AuCuSe₄ synthesis in K₂Se/Se flux under vacuum at 310 °C with controlled cooling (TS). The thermal severity is low, but the chemical handling severity is high because K₂Se/K₂Seₓ is air-sensitive and Se-rich sealed ampoules are hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. TS also reports glovebox/vacuum handling and post-growth washing. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS exists for alkali-flux growth of Co–Ni selenides specifically.

#### F3 — Route 4: Alkali Polyselenide Flux

Reactive polyselenide flux should greatly improve mass transport and lower diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], as AuCuSe₄ forms at only 310 °C (TS). However, TS explicitly reports mixed phases and difficulty obtaining pure AuCuSe₄, which lowers the structural/phase-control score. Flux growth could yield crystals but may have a narrow stability window for the target [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is Co/Ni solubility and maintaining exact 1:1 cation incorporation.

#### F2 — Route 5: Sonochemical / Annealing

The PtSe₂ analogue shows an omega-type 2D selenide can be synthesized by ultrasonication at 35 °C followed by 500 °C heat treatment (TS). This route is mild thermally and avoids high pressure. Deductions come from controlled annealing, multiple processing steps, and toxic Co/Ni/Se reagents [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS and SS supports sonochemical synthesis of the target composition.

#### F3 — Route 5: Sonochemical / Annealing

Precursor activity is high because soluble salts and activated selenium are used, and PtSe₂ demonstrates that sonochemical processing can access layered metal selenides (TS). Diffusion distances should be short in nanoscale products [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderately favorable because PtSe₂ is omega-type and 2D (TS), but CoNiSe₄ has mixed cations and monoclinic ordering not directly shown in TS. This route may favor nanoparticles or poorly ordered products unless annealing is optimized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has similar Co–Se and Ni–Se distances, so the Co/Ni size mismatch is likely small and does not trigger the ionic-radius amorphization criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description contains only edge-sharing octahedral sheets, unlike structurally similar Co₃Se₄ where edge/corner/face sharing coexist (SS), so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. There is insufficient explicit unit-cell atom-count evidence from CS/SS/TS, but the formula and site description suggest a small cell [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoNiSe₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar selenides are also stable on the hull (CS, TS). The strongest structural support comes from C2/m Co₃Se₄ and Ni(RhSe₂)₂ analogues containing octahedral Co–Se or Ni–Se motifs (SS), while PtSe₂ provides direct support for omega-type 2D edge-sharing MSe₆ sheets (TS). The most critical enabling factor is the close chemical and geometric compatibility of CoSe₆ and NiSe₆ octahedra in the target, with similar bond lengths and a stable computed hull [Reasonable Inference from target plus SS/TS]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and cation-order control: Co–Ni–Se may form competing binary/ternary selenides or disordered phases before the ordered monoclinic CoNiSe₄ sheet structure forms [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears favorable, with solution selenization plus annealing being the best first attempt and sealed-ampoule/CVT routes as robust alternatives.
