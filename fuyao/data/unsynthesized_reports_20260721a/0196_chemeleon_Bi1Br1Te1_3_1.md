### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom range. TS strongly supports the plausibility of stable layered Bi–Te-like compositions, including SnBi4Te7, TlBiTe2, Bi2Se3, and PbBi4Te7 at or very near the hull (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). CS/SS also contain experimentally synthesized Bi–chalcogen–halogen phases near the hull, including BiSeI and Bi2TeI (CS-1; SS-3; CS-5; SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee the target polymorph is the experimentally accessible polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: CVT / Sealed-Ampoule Vapor Growth

The condition severity is moderate because closely related syntheses use evacuated quartz ampoules at 600–650 °C, not extreme temperatures or high pressures (CS-1; CS-5; SS-1; SS-3). The deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile halide transport, and corrosive/toxic bromide handling [Reasonable Inference; SS-2]. Kinetically, this route is strong because vapor transport reduces diffusion barriers and the closest structural analogue Bi2TeI was made by gas-phase growth (CS-5; SS-1). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, because Bi2TeI synthesis yielded multiple phases in very small amounts and strong twinning (CS-5; SS-1).

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Bridgman / Melt Growth

The condition severity is higher than CVT because a staged melt-growth process likely requires prereaction, homogenization, directional solidification, and annealing [Reasonable Inference], as seen in layered telluride Bridgman routes (TS-1; TS-4). Kinetics are favorable because melt growth reduces solid diffusion limitations, and InTeBr was grown from the melt at 520 °C using a Bridgman arrangement (CS-3). Transferability is moderate because InTeBr is chemically close but structurally different from the target, while the closer Bi2TeI structural analogue was not reported by melt growth (CS-3; SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is whether BiTeBr melts congruently or decomposes/incongruently reacts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Solid-State Sealed Ampoule

The condition severity is moderate because BiBr and BiI analogues form in sealed ampoules at modest temperatures, but the route still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile halides, and possibly regrinding/reannealing (SS-2; SS-4; SS-5; CS-4). Kinetic scoring is lower because direct solid-state reactions from Bi/Te/halide precursors may suffer from incomplete mixing and slow ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The route is supported by BiBr/BiI ampoule chemistry and layered telluride solid-state synthesis (SS-2; SS-4; TS-1), but exact BiTeBr structural selectivity is not directly shown. The main uncertainty is polymorph/phase competition, strongly suggested by BiI multiple modifications and Bi2TeI mixed-phase formation (SS-4; SS-5; CS-5; SS-1).

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Hydrothermal / Solvothermal

The severity score is high because hydrothermal synthesis avoids high temperatures and high external pressures, and AuTeI was formed hydrothermally in hydroiodic acid (CS-2). The kinetics are potentially good due to solution-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference], but structural confidence is limited because AuTeI has different metal coordination and no Bi–Te–Br hydrothermal analogue is provided (CS-2). SS and TS provide insufficient direct support for hydrothermal BiTeBr synthesis and are treated as neutral 50 for route-specific transferability. The major uncertainty is controlling Te redox/speciation and avoiding molecular bismuth bromide or tellurium-rich byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Bi, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The structure is a simple layered R3m BiTeBr phase with edge-sharing BiTe3Br3 octahedra, and the close Bi2TeI analogue also contains edge-sharing BiTe3I3 octahedra (SS-1; CS-5). No evidence indicates a >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network, so the amorphization risk is low. TS offers insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is neutral 50 for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiTeBr is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many layered Bi–Te or Bi–chalcogen–halogen analogues are stable or near-stable (TS-1–TS-4; CS-1; CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from the very close structural analogue Bi2TeI, which has edge-sharing BiTe3I3 octahedral sheets and was grown by gas-phase/CVT methods (CS-5; SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is halide-mediated transport or melt/solution mobility, which can overcome slow ordering in layered Bi–Te–halide sheets [Reasonable Inference from CS-5, SS-1, CS-3, CS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity under volatile bromine chemical potential, because Bi2TeI and BiI analogues show mixed phases, small yields, twinning, and polymorphism (CS-5; SS-1; SS-4; SS-5). Overall, the target appears synthesizable, but obtaining phase-pure bulk material may be harder than obtaining small single crystals.
