### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E\_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which supports a high thermodynamic score of 95 [Target]. Thermodynamic analogues include near-ground-state TiS2-related phases and Li–Ti–S phases, with E\_hull values from ~0.0019 to 0.0146 eV/atom (TS2, TS3, TS4), and a Na-containing dichalcogenide NaNbS2 at E\_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or the specific Na-ordering pattern [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The severity is moderate because analogous sulfides require evacuated quartz tubes and 600–1000 °C annealing for long durations (CS4, SS2, TS5), but no high pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required. Kinetics are only moderate because TiS2/binary sulfide reactions can require repeated firings or long anneals, indicating slow diffusion and ordering (CS4, SS2). CS and SS provide useful signal for this route; TS provides useful alkali-dichalcogenide support via NaNbS2 (TS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: chemical Na intercalation

The route is operationally more complex because TiS2 host preparation plus air-free chemical intercalation and workup constitute a multistep process (CS1). Kinetics are favorable because the structurally closest Li–Ti–S analogue was produced by intercalation into TiS2 at room temperature under Ar, and TiS2 intercalation is generally demonstrated by Li/Hg analogues (CS1, TS3). The main uncertainty is that CS directly supports Li rather than Na, so Na transferability is [Reasonable Inference] even though Na intercalation into dichalcogenides is supported by TS5.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: flux-assisted growth

Flux growth has moderate severity because CuTi2S4 was grown at 750 °C in evacuated silica tubes with KCl/KI flux, and sulfide flux methods operate around 900–1000 °C under controlled gas or sealed conditions (SS3, CS2). Kinetics benefit from a flux/liquid medium, but phase control is a concern because the rhombohedral CuTi2S4 analogue is described as kinetically stabilized and condition-sensitive (SS3). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for halide flux growth of Na–Ti–S specifically, so TS is neutral for this route’s flux-specific feasibility.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: electrochemical or ion-exchange Na intercalation

This route has moderate severity because it avoids high-temperature Na volatility during the key insertion step, but requires inert electrochemical/air-free handling [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/TS3 inert intercalation practice]. Kinetics are the best among the proposed routes because TiS2 intercalation analogues show low-temperature or rapid insertion behavior (CS1, TS3), and Na-containing dichalcogenide intercalation chemistry is thermodynamically and synthetically supported by NaNbS2 (TS5). CS/TS provide strong indirect signal, while SS provides structural justification through TiS2-like edge-sharing TiS6 layers (SS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only the ionic-size mismatch check is clearly triggered because Na has a much larger sulfide coordination environment than Ti in the target structure [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The provided target structure does not demonstrate >40 atoms per unit cell, and it does not report simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence [Target]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to override the target description. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high, although Na ordering disorder could broaden diffraction peaks [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(TiS2)2 is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the reported E\_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The strongest analogue support comes from Li–Ti–S intercalation into TiS2 (CS1/SS1/TS4), Na-containing dichalcogenide intercalation chemistry in NaNbS2 (TS5), and structurally similar TiS2 layers with edge-sharing TiS6 octahedra (SS5/TS2/TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the TiS2-derived framework, which should allow topochemical Na insertion without full reconstructive synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/SS5/TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is precise Na ordering at x = 0.5, because intercalation analogues show phase-control and superlattice sensitivity (CS1/SS1/TS4). Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with electrochemical or controlled chemical Na intercalation being more promising than direct high-temperature sodium sulfide solid-state reaction.
