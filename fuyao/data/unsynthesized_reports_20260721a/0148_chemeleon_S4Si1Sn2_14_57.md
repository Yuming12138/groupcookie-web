### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin and is assessed as a likely thermodynamic ground-state candidate from the supplied target data. This is reinforced by several highly similar analogues also lying on the hull, including SnGeS3 and TlSnPS4 (CS1/SS2/TS2 and SS1/TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or low-temperature persistence, especially for low-symmetry P1 structures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 solid-state

The solid-state route is not temperature-extreme because SnGeS3 forms at 500–600 °C and SnSb4S7 at 650 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS1, CS3, SS2, TS2). Severity arises mainly from vacuum/sealed-tube handling, sulfur volatility, possible regrinding/annealing, and the inferred moisture sensitivity of SiS2 (CS1/SS2/TS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for ambient-pressure open-tube synthesis, so sealed ampoule synthesis is assumed necessary.

#### F3 — Route 1 solid-state kinetics

Binary sulfide precursors are supported by SnGeS3 and SnSb4S7 syntheses (CS1, CS3), giving moderate precursor activity. The main kinetic concern is ordering a mixed Sn–Si sulfide from solids at moderate temperature; SnGeS3 required 3–4 weeks tempering, showing slow but feasible equilibration (CS1/SS2/TS2). The score is not higher because the target substitutes smaller SiS4 for GeS4 and has triclinic P1 ordering, which may increase reconstruction demands [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 melt–quench–anneal

The route stays at or below the 1000 °C threshold because the closest SnGeS3 evidence explicitly describes melting sulfide mixtures up to a maximum of 1000 °C before annealing at 500–600 °C (CS1/SS2/TS2). The same sealed-vacuum, volatile-sulfur, multistep, and air-sensitive precursor penalties apply as for Route 1 (CS1/SS2/TS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for performing the melt step safely without sealed ampoules.

#### F3 — Route 2 melt–quench–anneal kinetics

This route scores highest kinetically because a melt or partial melt improves homogenization and bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, and this exact strategy is reported for the closest SnGeS3 analogue (CS1/SS2/TS2). The post-quench anneal provides time for the ordered Sn–Si–S structure to crystallize, analogous to the 3–4 week tempering of SnGeS3 (CS1/SS2/TS2). The remaining risk is glass formation or crystallization of simpler binaries during quench/anneal, especially because SiS2 is a network-forming sulfide [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 CVT

CVT analogues operate at moderate temperatures, e.g., SnPSe3-related chalcogenides at 650–700 °C with iodine in sealed quartz ampoules (CS4), so high-temperature and high-pressure penalties do not apply. However, CVT requires sealed evacuated tubes, volatile iodine, multi-step operation, and toxic/corrosive transport-agent handling (CS4; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports CVT specifically for Si-containing sulfides, so this route remains more uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3 CVT kinetics

Vapor transport can produce chalcogenide crystals, as shown for InSbS3 and SnPSe3-related compounds (CS2, CS4). The limiting kinetic/compositional issue is whether Si transports at a rate compatible with Sn and S; none of CS/SS/TS confirms volatile Si–S transport chemistry, so the precursor vapor availability and composition-control terms are only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. This route may be more appropriate for crystal growth after a polycrystalline phase has already been established.

#### F2 — Route 4 flux growth

Flux growth avoids >1000 °C conditions if adapted from the TlPbPS4 flux route at 1143 K and Sn–chalcogenide flux precedents (SS3/TS3, SS4/TS4). It still requires sealed ampoules or inert/reduced atmosphere, volatile sulfur management, controlled cooling, and flux removal (SS3/TS3; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS specifically supports Sn–Si–S flux growth, so it is scored as plausible but not as directly grounded as Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 4 flux kinetics

Flux/liquid-mediated growth should improve diffusion and crystallization relative to dry powders, consistent with structurally similar flux-grown chalcogenides (SS3/TS3) and Sn–chalcogenide flux work (SS4/TS4). The score is slightly below Route 2 because no exact Sn–Si–S flux analogue is provided and the flux may preferentially dissolve one component over another [Reasonable Inference]. Controlled cooling is beneficial but also introduces composition and nucleation sensitivity (SS3/TS3).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines small tetrahedral SiS4 units with much larger, stereochemically active low-coordinate Sn–S environments, triggering the cation-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description gives only 14 atoms in the P1 cell, so the atom-count check is not triggered. Mixed polyhedral types are present, but simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing coexistence is not established by the supplied text; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports triggering that check.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close analogues are also hull-stable (target data; CS1/SS2/TS2; SS1/TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from SnGeS3, which is both chemically and structurally close and can be made from elements or binary sulfides in evacuated ampoules, including a melt–quench–anneal variant (CS1/SS2/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the close SnGeS3 analogue: it demonstrates that Sn–tetrel–sulfide frameworks with Sn–S distorted coordination and tetrahedral group-14 sulfide units are experimentally accessible (CS1/SS2/TS2). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase control: SnSb4S7 formed with other phases, and SnGeS3 required long annealing, indicating that competing sulfides and slow ordering are realistic risks (CS3; CS1/SS2/TS2). Overall, the target is judged moderately-to-highly synthesizable, with the best first attempt being sealed-ampoule melt homogenization followed by long annealing.
