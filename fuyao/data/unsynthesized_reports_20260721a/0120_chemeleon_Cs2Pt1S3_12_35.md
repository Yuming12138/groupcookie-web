### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a ground-state thermodynamic assignment and justifies F1 = 100 (Target). The closest SS/TS analogues K₂PtS₂, Rb₂PtS₂, Cs₂S₂, Cs₄S₁₂, and Rb₂S₅ are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, reinforcing that the constituent chemical subspaces host stable alkali platinum sulfides and alkali polysulfides (SS1–SS3, SS5; TS1–TS5). CS analogues are stable or nearly stable CsLnS₂ phases, but because they lack Pt and S–S bonding, they provide only indirect support and insufficient Pt-specific evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 scores high because the direct K/Rb platinum sulfide analogues were made at 950 °C and ambient pressure under dry oxygen-free N₂, which is standard for high-temperature chalcogenide flux work (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The main severity deduction is for air/moisture-sensitive alkali sulfide/polysulfide reagents and products, which are repeatedly flagged in related alkali sulfide systems (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is whether Cs₂PtS₃ requires a sealed system because Cs/S volatility is more severe than K/Rb systems [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate rather than easy because Pt metal is refractory, but the K/Rb analogues demonstrate that alkali sulfide/polysulfide flux can react with Pt at 950 °C (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Structural similarity is favorable due to matching square-planar Pt–S units and close Pt–S distances, but the target’s S–S bonds add a polysulfide/redox dimension absent from K₂PtS₂/Rb₂PtS₂ (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3; Target). The most uncertain kinetic issue is achieving the correct Cs₂PtS₃ stoichiometry rather than crystallizing Cs polysulfides plus Cs₂PtS₂-type phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized for sealed vacuum/ampoule conditions, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive Cs sulfide/polysulfide reagents [Reasonable Inference; SS5/TS4]. Its temperature range is not extreme because relevant analogues span 803 K annealing for Cs polysulfides and 950 °C for K/Rb platinum sulfide flux reactions (SS5, TS4, SS1, SS3). The uncertainty is silica compatibility and pressure buildup from sulfur/Cs polysulfides, neither of which is directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

Using PtS₂ or PtS is expected to improve Pt–S reaction kinetics compared with dense Pt metal [Reasonable Inference], and binary Cs polysulfides provide a chemically preorganized S–S source supported by Cs₂S₂/related structures (SS2, TS2). However, no SS/TS entry reports the exact sealed-ampoule synthesis of a Cs–Pt–polysulfide compound, so structural reconstruction and stoichiometry control remain moderate challenges. CS provides insufficient Pt-specific kinetic information and is neutral for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses H₂S or H₂/S vapor, which is more severe than inert flux because H₂S is toxic and H₂ is reactive/flammable (CS1–CS5; SS4). Carbonate-to-sulfide routes are well precedented for CsLnS₂ and Cs₂CoS₂-type materials, but they introduce gas-handling and conversion steps (CS1–CS5; SS4). The uncertainty is whether Pt reacts sufficiently under these conditions without first forming inert Pt metal residues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are weaker because Cs₂CO₃ and Pt metal are less activated than Cs polysulfide flux or preformed Pt sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 supports carbonate + metal + chalcogen vapor conversion for Cs₂CoS₂, but Co is not Pt and the target requires square-planar Pt–S plus S–S bonding (SS4; Target). CS supports Cs–S formation under H₂S but gives insufficient evidence for Pt sulfide framework formation, so route transferability is partial.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 avoids high temperature but requires NH₃ autoclave conditions, pressure equipment, slow cooling, and air-sensitive/reactive reagents (SS2, TS2, TS5). These are more operationally demanding than inert-atmosphere flux reactions, even though they are below the >5 GPa high-pressure penalty threshold. SS/TS provide direct evidence for alkali polysulfide crystallization but insufficient evidence for Pt-containing ammonothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

Ammonothermal chemistry can generate crystalline Cs/Rb polysulfides and provides solution-mediated transport, which is kinetically favorable for S–S species (SS2, TS2, TS5). The major kinetic limitation is lack of evidence that Pt dissolves or forms square-planar Pt–S frameworks under these ammonia conditions; this is an extrapolation and therefore heavily discounted [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth exploratory option rather than the primary bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large contrast between Cs coordination environments and square-planar Pt–S bonding, giving one amorphization-risk trigger for cation size/coordination mismatch (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). The structure also contains edge- and corner-sharing motifs, but not the full vertex/edge/face coexistence criterion, and the unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, so only one trigger is counted (Target). SS/TS analogues show that related complex alkali polysulfide networks can crystallize, reducing concern about unavoidable amorphization (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4, TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₂PtS₃ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The synthesis case is strongly supported by SS/TS analogues for alkali platinum sulfide flux chemistry and by Cs polysulfide analogues for Cs–S/S–S bonding, though no exact Cs₂PtS₃ experimental analogue is provided (SS1–SS3, SS5; TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is a Cs-rich sulfide/polysulfide flux, which should supply high sulfur activity, mobile Cs/S species, and a medium for Pt sulfidation, as inferred from K₂PtS₂/Rb₂PtS₂ flux synthesis (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among Cs₂PtS₂-like platinum sulfides, Cs₂S₂/Cs polysulfides, and sulfur-rich byproducts [Reasonable Inference supported by SS/TS phase-control comments]. Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, with Route 1 preferred.
