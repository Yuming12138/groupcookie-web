### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility under the given calculation framework. TS analogues are also predominantly on the hull, including Tl5Se2Br, PdTl2Se2, AgTlSe, AgTl3Te2, and Hg3Tl2Se4 (TS-1–TS-5). The main uncertainty is polymorphism: the exact composition AuTl4Se3 appears as a tetragonal I4/mcm analogue with E_hull = 0.1507 eV/atom but E_above_ground_state = 0.0 in CS-1/SS-4, while the target is P1 and listed at E_hull = 0.0. Insufficient evidence from SS for a separate target thermodynamic calculation, so SS is neutral except as structural analogue support.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Melt growth

Condition severity is moderate because exact/near analogues require sealed ampoules, dry Ar/vacuum handling, carbon coating, controlled heating/cooling, and glovebox work (CS-1, SS-4). The temperature itself is not severe, since 600–700 °C is well below the >1000 °C penalty threshold (CS-1, SS-4). Kinetics are favorable because molten stoichiometric mixtures overcome solid-state diffusion limits, but polymorph selection is uncertain because the reported analogue is tetragonal I4/mcm while the target is triclinic P1 (CS-1, SS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS for direct AuTl4Se3 melt polymorph control, so TS is neutral for Route 1 polymorph selectivity.

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Solid-state sealed-tube annealing

Severity is comparable to Route 1 because evacuated sealed tubes and toxic/volatile Tl/Se handling remain required, as in Tl5Se2Br and PdTl2Se2 analogues (SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The kinetic score is lower because solid-state reaction may suffer from slow Au incorporation and long-range ordering [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the very long annealing reported for Tl5Se2Br and the 4-week treatment for PdTl2Se2 (SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). Use of Tl2Se/TlSe can improve precursor activity [Reasonable Inference], supported by thallium chalcogenide precursors in TS-2 and TS-3. Insufficient evidence from CS for this route as a direct target synthesis, so CS is neutral for Route 2.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Tl–Se flux

The severity score remains moderate-to-challenging because flux growth still needs evacuated carbon-coated tubes, toxic Tl-bearing flux, slow cooling, and likely flux separation (TS-5). Kinetics are best among the routes because a Tl–Se liquid flux should enhance Au dissolution and mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and TS-5 demonstrates Tl–metal–chalcogenide crystal growth from Tl2Q flux at moderate temperatures. Flux growth may also help isolate a low-temperature P1 polymorph [Reasonable Inference], although no CS/SS/TS entry proves this target polymorph. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Tl–Se flux growth of AuTl4Se3, so CS is neutral for flux-specific feasibility.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally complex and low symmetry, but the atom count described is only 16 atoms, below the >40 trigger threshold (target structure). Its Au–Se distances near 2.46–2.48 Å and Tl–Se distances spanning 2.73–3.37 Å indicate strong cation bonding/environment mismatch, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description mentions corner-sharing tetrahedra but not simultaneous edge- and face-sharing connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not assigned. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target-specific amorphization behavior, so CS/SS/TS are neutral for direct glass-forming tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Tl–metal–chalcogenides are also on the hull (TS-1–TS-5). The analogue support is strong chemically because an exact AuTl4Se3 composition is reported in CS/SS, but weaker structurally because that analogue is tetragonal I4/mcm while the target is triclinic P1 (CS-1, SS-4). The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated compatibility of Tl–Au–Se chemistry with sealed-ampoule melt growth at moderate temperature (CS-1, SS-4). The most important limiting factor is polymorph control: the desired P1 arrangement may require slow cooling, annealing, or flux-mediated crystallization rather than straightforward recovery of the known tetragonal form [Reasonable Inference]. A Tl–Se flux route is therefore recommended as the best balance of thermodynamic support and kinetic/polymorph-control flexibility (TS-5).
