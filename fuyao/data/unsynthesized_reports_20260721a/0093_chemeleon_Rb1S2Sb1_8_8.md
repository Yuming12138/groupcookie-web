The main uncertainty is selectivity for RbSbS₂ rather than Sb-rich or polysulfide-containing side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Rb/Sb cation-size mismatch, which triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description contains one Rb, one Sb, and one S site with edge/corner sharing but no face-sharing, so C2 and C3 are not clearly triggered; C2 has insufficient direct unit-cell-count evidence from CS/SS/TS but the simple C2/c analogue KSbS₂ supports a small ordered framework (CS2/SS2/TS2). The remaining uncertainty is whether the Rb-expanded C2/c variant is dynamically robust or competes with the known triclinic RbSbS₂ polymorph (CS1/SS1/TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very low-energy phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and exact-composition RbSbS₂ is also reported as stable in the reference data (CS1/SS1/TS1). The strongest synthetic support comes from the isostructural KSbS₂ hydrothermal analogue and the Rb-containing hydrothermal thiostibate Rb₂Sb₄S₇ (CS2/SS2/TS2; SS4/TS4). The most critical enabling factor is solution/flux-mediated Sb–S transport, which allows crystalline alkali thiostibates to form at only 150–180 °C (CS2/SS2/TS2; SS4/TS4). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph/phase selectivity, because known RbSbS₂ is reported as triclinic P1 while the target is monoclinic C2/c (CS1/SS1/TS1). Overall, the target is highly plausible, with the hydrothermal route offering the best balance of structural transferability and kinetic accessibility.
