### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Similar compounds also sit on or very near the hull: Cu₂GeS₃ is on-hull in CS/SS/TS entries (CS-2, SS-5, TS-1), Cu₂SiSe₃ is on-hull (CS-4), and Cu₂ZnSiS₄/Cu₂CdSiS₄ are on-hull thermodynamic analogues (TS-2, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that a zero computed hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of polymorphic/ordering competition [Reasonable Inference], especially because related Cu₂ZnSiS₄ polymorph control is explicitly challenging (TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1: Evacuated-ampoule solid state

The condition severity is moderate because analogous Cu₂GeS₃ and Cu₂GeS₃-like phases require evacuated ampoules, staged heating, high temperatures up to 850–900 °C, and long annealing (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1). Vacuum and volatile sulfur/chalcogen management impose deductions, but no high pressure or >1000 °C condition is required by the closest evidence (CS-2, CS-3, TS-1). CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signal for this route; there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a required toxic precursor in this route, so that item is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1: Evacuated-ampoule solid state

Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Si is refractory and likely slow to react [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long anneals reported for Cu₂GeS₃/Cu₂GeS₃-like analogues (CS-2, CS-3, TS-1). The structural pathway is favorable because the target and analogues are corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide networks (CS-1, CS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-5), but cation ordering in chalcopyrite/stannite-like materials can require annealing and/or quenching (CS-3, TS-5). The uncertainty is whether Cu₃SiS₄ forms directly or first passes through Cu₂S/Cu₂SiS₃/CuS secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: KCl/LiCl flux route

The flux route has moderate condition severity because it operates at only 550 °C in the closest analogues but still requires evacuated sealed tubes, long reaction times, quenching, and washing (CS-1, CS-4, SS-2). CS and SS provide strong direct signal for Cu–Si–chalcogenide flux growth; TS provides insufficient direct flux-route evidence for this exact method and is treated as neutral for route-specific severity. The main severity penalty is the sealed chalcogen/flux process rather than temperature.

#### F3 — Route 2: KCl/LiCl flux route

Flux growth improves kinetics because molten chloride media can enhance mass transport at temperatures far below the melting point of Si [Reasonable Inference], and Cu₂SiSe₃ is successfully grown at 550 °C for 6 weeks (CS-1, CS-4, SS-2). The target has similar tetrahedral Cu–Si–S connectivity to Cu₂SiS₃/Cu₂SiSe₃ analogues (CS-1, SS-2), but the Cu₃SiS₄ stoichiometry and tetragonal I-42m ordering differ from the monoclinic enargite-like flux analogue [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether KCl/LiCl dissolves enough sulfide/Si species to nucleate Cu₃SiS₄ rather than Cu₂SiS₃ or binary sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Iodine CVT

The CVT route is more severe because iodine transport requires a sealed ampoule, toxic/volatile iodine, a stable temperature gradient, and likely a pre-reacted feed charge [Reasonable Inference from SS-1, SS-3, TS-2]. Structurally similar sulfides were transported at 1140/1050 K and thermodynamically similar Cu₂CdSiS₄ at 800→750 °C (SS-1, SS-3, TS-2), so >1000 °C external temperatures are not required. CS provides insufficient direct CVT evidence for Cu–Si–S without extra metals, so CS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 3: Iodine CVT

CVT kinetics are promising because iodine transport successfully grows related Cu–Ni–Si/Ge–S and Cu–Cd–Si–S crystals (SS-1, SS-3, TS-2). The major kinetic uncertainty is composition control during vapor transport, since related Cu₂ZnSiS₄/Cu₂ZnGeS₄ systems show polymorph and phase-control sensitivity (TS-3, TS-5). This route may be better for crystals after a polycrystalline source phase is established rather than for first discovery of bulk phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a relatively simple corner-sharing tetrahedral framework, which lowers amorphization risk; similar chalcopyrite/stannite analogues are ordered tetrahedral sulfides rather than mixed edge/face-sharing networks (CS-1, SS-2, TS-5). One risk trigger is cation-size/charge contrast between Cu and Si tetrahedral sites, reflected by distinct Cu–S and Si–S bond lengths in the target and analogues (CS-1, SS-2, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide the exact target unit-cell atom count beyond the formula/space-group description, but there is insufficient evidence for a >40-atom cell, so that check is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃SiS₄ is likely a ground-state or very strongly thermodynamically allowed phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close Cu–Si/Ge–S analogues are also on or near the hull (CS-2, CS-4, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports accessible tetrahedral Cu–Si/Ge sulfide chemistry, including direct solid-state, flux, and CVT routes (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known corner-sharing CuS₄/SiS₄ or CuS₄/GeS₄ tetrahedral frameworks with very similar bond lengths (CS-1, SS-2, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, because related Cu–Zn–Si/Ge–S compounds show polymorph competition, quenching sensitivity, and difficult pure-phase control (TS-3, TS-5). Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with flux-assisted sealed-tube synthesis the best first route because it balances low temperature with enhanced mass transport (CS-1, CS-4, SS-2).
