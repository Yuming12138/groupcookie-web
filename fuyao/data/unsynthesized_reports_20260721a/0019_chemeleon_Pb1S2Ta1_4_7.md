### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score of 98/100 (target data). The closest Pb-containing thermodynamic analogue Ta₃PbS₆ is also on the hull, and Ta₃SnS₆ is likewise on the hull, indicating stable nearby Ta–p-block–S chemistries (TS1, TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that 0 K hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclude high-temperature entropic stabilization of competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because analogues use evacuated quartz tubes, 450 °C for InTaS₂, and 700–900 °C for broader M–TX₂ compounds, with no high pressure (CS3, CS5, SS3, TS2). The main penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile sulfide/Pb components, and Pb toxicity [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed-tube use in CS3]. Kinetics are less favorable than intercalation because even though TaS₂ provides a useful framework, dry solid-state diffusion and ordering across Pb/Ta/S can require long anneals, consistent with 1–4 week analogue syntheses (CS5, SS3, TS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: CVT

CVT severity is moderate-to-high because SnTaS₂ requires a two-stage powder-preparation plus Cl₂ transport process with a 1200–1000 K gradient for 1 week (CS1, SS2). Related Ta sulfide CVT uses iodine, evacuated silica tubes, 900–1000 °C gradients, and multi-day growth (SS4, TS3). Kinetically, CVT is attractive for crystal growth because SnTaS₂ and Ta₃PbS₆ were grown by vapor transport, but composition control is uncertain for Ta/Pb/S due to different volatile halide transport rates [Reasonable Inference from CS1, SS1, TS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Pb intercalation into TaS₂

This route has moderate severity because CS2 uses evacuated quartz tubes, high temperature near 1000 °C, and a small gradient, but no high pressure or exotic equipment. Its kinetics are strongest because TaS₂ is already the correct edge-sharing TaS₆ framework, and Pb is the species being inserted/reordered rather than requiring complete construction from refractory Ta metal [Reasonable Inference supported by CS2 and TS5]. The uncertainty is whether Pb orders into the exact stoichiometric P-6m2 TaPbS₂ phase rather than forming nonstoichiometric intercalates or Ta₃PbS₆-type phases (CS2, SS1, TS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: PbS/Pb-rich flux

Flux/melt severity is moderate because CS4 shows related heavy p-block sulfide Ta chemistry can be done in sealed ampoules at moderate temperature, but the proposed PbS flux route is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference from CS4]. Kinetics may improve through liquid-phase transport, but insufficient evidence from SS for a PbS flux route and insufficient evidence from TS for a PbS flux route make phase selectivity uncertain. This route is therefore a backup rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small, ordered crystalline description and a simple TaPbS₂ formula, so the >40 atom-count amorphization trigger is not supported (target description). The structure contains edge-sharing TaS₆ units but does not show the mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence that would increase amorphization risk (target description; CS1, CS3). The main amorphization/ordering concern is the large mismatch between Pb coordination preference and TaS₆ framework chemistry, inferred from Pb/Sn/In analogues showing different p-block coordination environments than Ta (CS1, CS3, SS1; [Reasonable Inference]).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaPbS₂ is likely a ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The synthesis case is strongly supported by close chemical and structural analogues: InTaS₂ has the same P-6m2 local motif and is synthesized from In + TaS₂, while SnTaS₂ is very similar and is grown by CVT (CS1, CS3). Pb-containing Ta₃PbS₆ is also stable and synthesized by vapor transport, confirming that Pb–Ta–S chemistry is experimentally accessible (SS1, TS1). The key enabling factor is the pre-existing TaS₂ layered framework, which should reduce reconstruction barriers during Pb intercalation (CS2, TS5). The key limiting factor is stoichiometry/phase control, especially avoiding nonstoichiometric Pb intercalates, PbS, TaS₂ residuals, or Ta₃PbS₆-like competing phases [Reasonable Inference supported by CS2, CS5, SS1].
