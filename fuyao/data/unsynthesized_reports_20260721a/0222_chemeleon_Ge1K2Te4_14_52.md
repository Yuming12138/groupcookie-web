### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored 98/100 from the supplied target thermodynamics. CS supports this because chemically similar KPrTe₄ is E_hull = 0 and Rb₂SnTe₅ is near-hull at ≈0.004 eV/atom (CS2, CS4); SS supports this because several structural analogues are stable or near-stable (SS1–SS3, SS5); TS strongly supports this because all top thermodynamic analogues listed are stable at E_hull = 0 (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee an easily accessible kinetic pathway, especially for ordered polytelluride frameworks [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
The severity is moderate because K₂Te₃ and K₃SbTe₃ analogues require dry Ar or evacuated sealed ampoules but only moderate temperatures of ~600–700 °C (SS2/TS1, SS5/TS3). No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates the need for >1000 °C or GPa pressure, so those deductions are not applied. The main penalties come from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile K/Te chemistry, and air-sensitive alkali precursors (SS2/TS1).

**F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Ge is less reactive than molten/fluxed telluride species [Reasonable Inference], even though K and Te are reactive and low-melting. Structural analogues show K–Te frameworks and tetrel-telluride motifs can form by bulk heating (SS2/TS1, SS5/TS3, TS5). The uncertainty is whether a single anneal yields the triclinic ordered target or instead K–Te/Ge–Te binaries [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 K₂Te₃ flux.**  
The severity is challenging because CS2/CS3 require glovebox handling, high-vacuum sealed ampoules, multi-step A₂Te₃ preparation, long programmed cooling, and quenching/extraction. Temperatures remain moderate at ~720 °C and no high external pressure is used (CS2/CS3). CS gives strong route evidence; SS/TS indirectly support K₂Te₃ stability and accessibility (SS2/TS1).

**F3 — Route 2 K₂Te₃ flux.**  
Kinetics are favorable because a K₂Te₃-rich melt/flux should provide mobile polytelluride species, as A₂Te₃ is used explicitly as both reactant and flux in analogous tellurides (CS2/CS3). Slow cooling from flux supports crystallization of complex polytelluride frameworks, and SS1 provides close structural evidence for tetrel telluride frameworks with Te–Te contacts. The main uncertainty is whether Ge dissolves sufficiently in K₂Te₃ flux and crystallizes as K₂GeTe₄ rather than GeTe/GeTe₂-related phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 solvothermal.**  
This route has the mildest conditions because SS3/TS2 reports Cs₂SnTe₄ at 190 °C for 2 days in an Ar-sealed methanol ampoule. The route avoids high external pressure and high temperature, but the volatile sealed solvent imposes a sealed-vessel penalty (SS3/TS2). CS provides insufficient direct evidence for solvothermal K–Ge–Te synthesis, so the CS signal for this route is neutral.

**F3 — Route 3 solvothermal.**  
The solvothermal pathway offers good transport and precursor activation at low temperature, as shown by Cs₂SnTe₄ formation from Cs₂CO₃, Sn, and Te in methanol (SS3/TS2). However, SS3/TS2 reports only 16% yield and interface-localized crystals, indicating difficult nucleation and phase control. The Ge-for-Sn and K-for-Cs substitutions are chemically plausible but remain [Reasonable Inference], so the kinetics score is high-moderate rather than excellent.

**F2 — Route 4 melt/Bridgman.**  
The route requires controlled atmosphere and sealed ampoules because alkali chalcogenide melts are volatile/reactive, supported by CS5 and CS4/SS1. Temperatures of 750–850 °C are moderate and supported by Rb₂SnTe₅ at 850 °C and alkali chalcogenide melt growth near 850–947 °C (CS4/SS1, CS5). No high-pressure penalty applies because the analogues use sealed ampoules or Bridgman apparatus, not GPa conditions (CS5).

**F3 — Route 4 melt/Bridgman.**  
Kinetics are favorable because melt growth avoids slow solid-state diffusion and CS5 reports single crystals from slow cooling of stoichiometric alkali chalcogenide melts. Structural similarity to Rb₂SnTe₅ is very strong in SS1/CS4, supporting crystallization of a related alkali tetrel polytelluride framework. The limiting uncertainty is composition drift from K/Te vapor and possible glassy or multiphase solidification if cooling is not optimized [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 5 ammonothermal.**  
Although the temperature is low, severity is challenging because SS4/TS4 require supercritical or hypercritical NH₃ at 1000–2000 bar in special autoclaves. The formal >5 GPa deduction is not applied because 1000–2000 bar is below 5 GPa (SS4/TS4). NH₃ reactivity, sealed volatile medium, and alkali-metal hazards drive the F2 deductions (SS4/TS4).

**F3 — Route 5 ammonothermal.**  
Fluid transport in NH₃ gives favorable diffusion and crystallization for alkali polytellurides, as shown by Rb₂Te₅/Rb₂Te₃/Cs₂Te₃ (SS4/TS4). However, SS4/TS4 provide insufficient direct evidence for Ge incorporation or GeTe₄ tetrahedron formation. This route is therefore kinetically plausible for K–Te substructures but uncertain for the full ternary K₂GeTe₄ phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large K/Ge cation size and coordination mismatch, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference], but the described primitive P-1 structure appears small and does not show >40 atoms in the provided description. The target has edge and corner sharing but no face-sharing polyhedral network in the supplied structure description, so C3 is not triggered. The resulting F4 = 78/100 reflects low-to-medium amorphization risk, with the main risk being cation ordering and polytelluride network complexity [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂GeTe₄ is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple alkali telluride/tetrel telluride analogues are also stable or near-stable (CS2/CS4, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis in sealed, oxygen-free environments, especially through alkali telluride flux or melt routes (CS2–CS5, SS1–SS2, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of mobile K–Te/polytelluride media, shown by K₂Te₃ direct synthesis and A₂Te₃ flux growth (SS2/TS1, CS2/CS3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control under sealed conditions because K and Te are volatile/reactive and related solvothermal/flux analogues can have narrow growth windows or low yields (SS3/TS2, CS2/CS3). Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but single-phase bulk material may require iterative optimization of K/Te activity and cooling rate [Reasonable Inference].
