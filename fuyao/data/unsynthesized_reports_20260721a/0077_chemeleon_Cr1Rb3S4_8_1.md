### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest thermodynamic-stability bracket. Chemically similar materials are mostly hull-stable, including Rb₂MoS₄, Rb₂GeS₃, K₃GaS₃, K₂MoS₄, and K₃AlSe₃ (CS-1 to CS-5). Thermodynamic analogues also strongly support hull stability, with Rb₂VAgS₄, Rb₂LiVS₄, Rb₆HgS₄, and Rb₃BiSe₃ at E_hull = 0.0 (TS-2 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Cr oxidation-state control. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state

The route is moderately severe because it needs evacuated or inert sealed ampoules and volatile sulfur control, as in Rb₂GeS₃ and K/K–chalcogenide analogues (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Kinetics are only moderate because Cr metal is refractory and may react slowly, although related MS₄ tetrahedral frameworks form by solid-state routes (CS-2, CS-3). TS-4 supports lower-temperature sealed ampoule synthesis for Rb sulfide phases, but it uses HgS rather than Cr, so transfer to Rb₃CrS₄ is not exact. SS provides insufficient evidence for this specific direct sealed-tube route and is treated as neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Rb polysulfide flux

The flux route has moderate condition severity because it requires glovebox loading, sealed evacuated/Ar ampoules, flux preparation, and washing, as reported for Rb₂VAgS₄ and Rb₂LiVS₄ (SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). Its kinetic score is high because polysulfide fluxes provide liquid/flux-mediated transport and have already crystallized Rb-containing tetrahedral V sulfides at modest temperatures (SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). The main uncertainty is whether Cr can occupy the required high-valent tetrahedral sulfide environment without forming Cr₂S₃ or other chromium sulfides. [Reasonable Inference] CS gives insufficient evidence for Rb polysulfide flux specifically and is scored neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: solvothermal thiometallate

The solvothermal route has favorable low-temperature severity, with Rb₂MoS₄ synthesized at 393 K and K₂MoS₄ at 443 K (CS-1, CS-4). Its kinetic score is high because solution-mediated sulfur chemistry can bypass solid-state diffusion limits and directly assemble MS₄ tetrahedra, as shown for Mo thiometallates (CS-1, CS-4). The route is penalized by phase-control uncertainty: Rb₂MoS₄ was reported as a minor phase in CS-1, and no direct Cr analogue is provided. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for solvothermal Rb–Cr–S synthesis and are scored neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Rb and Cr, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. However, the described target structure uses relatively simple Rb–S and CrS₄ polyhedra with corner-sharing connectivity, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network is reported. Related crystalline alkali thiometallates and sulfides are numerous in CS, SS, and TS, supporting crystallization rather than glass formation. The uncertainty is the exact cell multiplicity of the provided Cm structure; the supplied formula-level description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₃CrS₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar alkali chalcogenides are also hull-stable (CS-1 to CS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The analogue support is good for alkali–sulfide frameworks containing MS₄ tetrahedra, especially Mo, Ge, Ga, V, and Hg sulfides, but direct Cr-based evidence is absent. The most enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with several demonstrated low- to moderate-temperature crystallization routes for Rb/K thiometallates and polysulfide flux phases (CS-1, CS-4, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). The most limiting factor is controlling chromium oxidation state and avoiding competing chromium sulfides or Rb polysulfides. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with solvothermal screening and Rb polysulfide flux growth as the most practical first attempts.
