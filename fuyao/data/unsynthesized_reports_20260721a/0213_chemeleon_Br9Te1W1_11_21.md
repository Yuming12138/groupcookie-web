### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability  
The target has exact E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. The nearest identical-composition WTeBr9 analogue has a very low E_hull of ~0.011 eV/atom, and the TaTeBr9 analogue is also on the hull, reinforcing that this halide cluster chemistry is thermodynamically plausible (CS, SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the P1 polymorph will be the experimentally selected polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because WTeBr9-type analogues are synthesized at only 423–453 K in sealed ampoules (CS, SS, TS). Deductions are driven by evacuated ampoule operation, volatile halide transport, multistep handling, and moisture-sensitive W/Te halides (CS, SS, TS). The route is experimentally accessible but not routine bench chemistry because halide vapor pressure and hydrolysis must be controlled [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1  
Kinetics are favorable because the target is a zero-dimensional molecular cluster and the identical WTeBr9 analogue crystallizes by low-temperature CVT/sealed-ampoule transport (CS, SS, TS). Vapor availability is good for Te/W halide chemistry, but composition/polymorph control is less certain because related bromide phases may require rapid cooling and can show low-yield or poor-crystal behavior (CS, SS, TS). Thus F3 is good but not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 2  
Route 2 uses low-temperature reactive bromides, so no high-temperature penalty is applied; WBr₅-related chemistry is demonstrated at 250 °C in sealed evacuated silica tubes (CS). The penalties again come from vacuum/Ar handling, sealed volatile halides, multi-step tube preparation, and air-sensitive precursors (CS, SS, TS). It is moderate rather than severe because no high pressure, high temperature, or exotic equipment is needed (CS, SS, TS).

#### F3 — Route 2  
This route has the best kinetic score because preformed WBrₓ and TeBr₄ should already contain reactive metal–bromine bonding and can rearrange through molecular/vapor intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. The closest WTeBr9 analogue has essentially the same WBr₆/TeBr₆ face-sharing cluster motif, reducing the structural reconstruction burden (CS, SS, TS). Uncertainty remains in obtaining the triclinic P1 polymorph rather than the known monoclinic P2₁/m form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3  
The direct elemental route is supported only indirectly by related Re–Te–Br and Te–I–metal sealed-ampoule syntheses (TS). It still avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but Br₂ volatility/toxicity, sealed-tube pressure, vacuum handling, and multistep ampoule preparation all incur penalties (TS; [Reasonable Inference]). This route is therefore conditionally feasible but operationally riskier.

#### F3 — Route 3  
Kinetics are weaker because W metal is refractory and must first be brominated before cluster assembly [Reasonable Inference]. Related Re–Te–Br clusters form from elements under sealed-tube conditions, which supports plausibility (TS), but exact WTeBr9 formation from elements is not demonstrated. Competing W bromide phases are a significant risk based on W–Br chemistry showing side products and low yields in related systems (CS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency  
The target has only one discrete 11-atom cluster per P1 cell, so the atom-count criterion is not triggered (target description). The connectivity is face-sharing WBr₆/TeBr₆ rather than mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, reducing glass-forming complexity (target description; CS/SS/TS). The main amorphization concern is the cation size/coordination mismatch reflected by distinct W–Br and Te–Br bond-length regimes in the target and analogues (target description; CS/SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and very close WTeBr9/TaTeBr9 analogues are on or near the convex hull (CS, SS, TS). The strongest evidence is the identical-composition WTeBr9 analogue mp-29715, which contains the same zero-dimensional face-sharing WBr₆/TeBr₆ cluster and has reported sealed-ampoule/CVT synthesis (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility and volatility of W/Te bromide species, which allows cluster crystallization without high-temperature solid-state diffusion (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and phase control: the known analogue is monoclinic P2₁/m, whereas the target is triclinic P1, and related halide systems can require rapid cooling or careful gradients (CS, SS, TS). Overall, the target is highly plausible but will likely require screening of cooling rate, halogen chemical potential, and transport gradient.
