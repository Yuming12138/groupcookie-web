### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues include E_hull = 0 AgCd₂GaS₄, AgHgSI, HgCdAsBr, and Hg₃S₂I₂, demonstrating that similar chalcogenide/soft-cation phases can be stable and experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Chemically similar Cu₂SnS₃ is nearly on hull at 0.0005 eV/atom, also supporting high thermodynamic feasibility (CS-1, CS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed ordered MnCd₄S₅ ground state is preserved experimentally against CdS + MnS or cation-disordered variants [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 solid-state.**  
The solid-state route is strongly supported structurally and thermodynamically by AgCd₂GaS₄, which is a Cd-containing corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide synthesized in evacuated ampoules (SS-2, TS-1). Its severity score is lowered because the analogue used high temperature, sealed vacuum, staged sulfur conversion, and a 500 h anneal (SS-2, TS-1). Kinetically, the route benefits from structural similarity to the target tetrahedral framework, but dense sulfide/element precursors and slow cation ordering make it slower than solution synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for the exact Cd/Mn solid-state ordering pathway, so CS is neutral for that specific kinetic detail.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 solvothermal.**  
The solvothermal route has mild conditions because Cu₂SnS₃ was synthesized at 453 K for 20 h using common metal chloride/thiourea chemistry in a Teflon-lined autoclave (CS-1, CS-2). CdS solution formation under mild chemical bath conditions further supports Cd–S precipitation chemistry, but CdS bath products included mixed phases and impurities (SS-1, TS-2). Kinetics are favorable because soluble Cd/Mn salts and thiourea provide high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether low-temperature crystallization can produce the ordered I-4 MnCd₄S₅ phase rather than CdS/MnS or disordered Cd–Mn sulfide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 CVT.**  
CVT is supported by iodine-aided ZnIn₂S₄ growth in horizontal furnaces and ampoules (CS-4). The route has moderate severity due to evacuated ampoules, I₂ transport, volatility, and long growth times; CS-4 specifically used long gradient processing and reports mixed-phase concerns requiring homogeneity checks. Kinetics for crystal growth can be good if a transportable vapor species forms, but Mn transport chemistry is not directly established in CS/SS/TS, so that part is a [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for MnCd₄S₅-specific CVT behavior, so SS/TS are neutral for Mn transport but supportive of sealed chalcogenide crystallization in general (SS-5, TS-3, TS-5).

**F2/F3 — Route 4 chemical bath/thin film.**  
This route is experimentally mild because CdS was deposited at 80 °C in air for 60 min using CdSO₄, thiourea, and triethanolamine (SS-1, TS-2). The low severity is offset by weak phase-control evidence: the CdS analogue formed mixed cubic/hexagonal phases plus CdO/S impurities (SS-1, TS-2). Kinetically, bath deposition gives high precursor activity and short film diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], but achieving 4:1 Cd:Mn ordering in a chalcopyrite-like phase likely requires post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for chemical-bath MnCd₄S₅ formation, so CS is neutral for this route beyond general thiourea sulfide chemistry (CS-1, CS-2).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCd₄S₅ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull and experimentally synthesized (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The structural support is strong for tetrahedral Cd–S chemistry because CdS and AgCd₂GaS₄ have CdS₄ tetrahedra with Cd–S distances essentially matching the target (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The best enabling factor is the close match between the target’s corner-sharing CdS₄/MnS₄ network and known tetrahedral sulfide frameworks, especially CdS and AgCd₂GaS₄ (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The main limiting factor is cation ordering: no supplied analogue directly demonstrates MnCd₄S₅ or Cd:Mn = 4:1 ordering in the I-4 chalcopyrite-like cell, so phase-pure synthesis may compete with CdS, MnS, or disordered solid solutions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, solvothermal synthesis followed by mild sulfur annealing is the best first attempt, while sealed-ampoule annealing is the most robust fallback for equilibrium ordering [Reasonable Inference].
