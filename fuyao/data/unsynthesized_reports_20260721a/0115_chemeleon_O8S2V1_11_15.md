### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is calculated exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. TS strongly supports this because all top thermodynamic analogues listed have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). CS is supportive but mixed: VOSO4 is low above hull at 0.0276 eV/atom, V2O3(SeO4)2 is on hull, while the Fe sheet analogue has E_hull = 0.225 eV/atom (CS1–CS3). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not specify hydration or redox stability under wet sulfuric acid conditions; that redox/hydration uncertainty is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, 140 °C in CS3 and 230 °C in SS3/TS3, so no high-temperature deduction applies. It requires sealed acid hydrothermal equipment and concentrated H2SO4, but the scoring rubric does not directly penalize autogenous pressure or corrosive acid unless toxic/hazardous precursors are counted; the −10 deduction is therefore assigned for hazardous V/acid handling, supported by the acid use in CS3 and SS3/TS3 plus V toxicity as [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure >5 GPa, reactive atmosphere, or volatile sealed precursor requirements, so those categories score as no deduction.

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal acid solution provides high precursor activity and low diffusion barriers, consistent with Fe sulfate sheet formation in concentrated H2SO4 at 140 °C for 40 days (CS3) and related sulfate framework synthesis at 230 °C for 3 days (SS3/TS3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because CS3 has a very similar 2D M(SO4)2 sheet of MO6 octahedra and SO4 tetrahedra. The uncertainty is metal-specific: Fe(III)/hydronium sulfate chemistry in CS3 is not identical to V(IV) V(SO4)2, and direct V(SO4)2 synthesis evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is mild in temperature and pressure because related sulfates crystallize by room-temperature or 293 K evaporation from concentrated H2SO4 (SS1/TS1; SS4/TS4) and by 80–95 °C isothermal evaporation (TS5). No high-temperature, high-pressure, reactive-atmosphere, sealed-volatile, or multistep deductions apply based on SS1–SS4/TS1–TS5. A −10 deduction is retained for hazardous/corrosive acid and V precursor handling; moisture sensitivity in analogous crystals is reported by glovebox/capillary handling (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4).

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution crystallization gives excellent kinetic accessibility because cation/anion transport occurs in an acidic liquid medium, as demonstrated by direct formation of Na/K/Rb Au sulfate crystals from concentrated sulfuric acid evaporation (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). TS5 further supports low-temperature sulfate crystallization by isothermal evaporation at 80–95 °C. The structural match is not as exact as CS3 because Au/alkali/Eu sulfate analogues differ in coordination and charge balance, so the route remains partly [Reasonable Inference] for V(SO4)2.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses only moderate temperatures, following 130/260/350 °C dehydration under dry nitrogen in CS1, with possible analogy to 350–600 °C or 650 °C solid-state routes in CS2 and CS4. It is penalized for being a three-step process because CS1 explicitly uses three heating stages. It is also penalized for hazardous V precursor handling [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for >1000 °C temperatures, >5 GPa pressure, H2/NH3/vacuum, or mandatory sealed volatile precursors.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are acceptable but weaker than solution routes because CS1 proves VOSO4 hydrate can transform under low-temperature dehydration, yet converting to sulfate-richer V(SO4)2 likely requires additional sulfate incorporation, which is [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 and CS4 support that vanadium polyanion oxides can form by solid-state heating with regrinding, but those analogues require more reconstruction and do not match the target sheet. Phase control is a concern because CS1 notes α/β VOSO4 transformation during heating, implying competing vanadium sulfate phases are plausible.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a corner-sharing sulfate framework with VO6 octahedra and SO4 tetrahedra, closely resembling the ordered Fe(SO4)2 sheet in CS3, which argues against severe amorphization. One amorphization trigger is assigned for the large V/S cation size and coordination contrast [Reasonable Inference], but the target formula is small and the described connectivity is corner-sharing rather than mixed vertex/edge/face. SS/TS analogues include more complex sharing motifs, but because those are not the target topology, they are not used to trigger C3.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The synthesis is well supported in a broad sense by sulfate chemistry: CS1 supports V(IV) sulfate precursors, CS3 supports a very similar 2D M(SO4)2 sheet, and SS/TS support low-temperature crystallization from concentrated sulfuric acid. The most important enabling factor is the high sulfate activity and solution mobility provided by concentrated H2SO4, which is repeatedly successful for sulfate frameworks (CS3; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS5). The most important limiting factor is that V(IV) may preferentially form vanadyl sulfate VOSO4 rather than V(SO4)2, as CS1 demonstrates stable VOSO4 formation but no direct V(SO4)2 synthesis. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with acid evaporation or hydrothermal sulfuric-acid crystallization as the most plausible approaches.
