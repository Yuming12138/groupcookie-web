### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. Chemically similar Caswellsilverite-type NaErS2, LiDyS2, NaYbS2, LiErS2, and LiHoS2 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS #1–#5), supporting the idea that layered alkali–trivalent-cation sulfides can be ground-state phases. TS analogues are mostly stable binary rocksalt sulfides (TS #1–#5), but they do not directly address ternary NaHfS2, so TS is useful only as general sulfide stability support.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits strong structural/chemical precedent from sealed-tube Caswellsilverite ALnS2 syntheses at 900 °C (CS #2/#4/#5). Its severity score is lowered by evacuated sealed-tube handling, volatile sulfur/alkali components, air-sensitive Na/Na2S, and repeated annealing (CS #2/#4/#5; Reasonable Inference). Kinetics are the main weakness because Hf metal is refractory and likely diffuses more slowly than reactive alkali/sulfur species [Reasonable Inference], despite same-structure analogues in CS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is experimentally close to NaErS2 synthesis from oxide + NaCl + NH4Cl under H2/H2S at 900 °C (CS #1). Its condition severity is moderate because no >1000 °C or high pressure is needed, but H2/H2S atmosphere and multistep processing are required (CS #1). The kinetic score is low because HfO2 is expected to be much harder to sulfide/reduce than rare-earth oxides [Reasonable Inference], and neither SS nor TS provides direct evidence that HfO2 converts cleanly to NaHfS2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is favored because flux/liquid-mediated transport can mitigate the refractory Hf diffusion problem [Reasonable Inference], and CS #3 demonstrates alkali sulfide crystal growth under H2S at 1030 °C. The route is penalized for >1000 °C operation, toxic/controlled H2S, and multistep flux processing (CS #3). Phase control remains uncertain because CS #3 explicitly notes impurity issues such as oxysulfide formation in related flux syntheses, so flux improves kinetics but may complicate purity.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is supported by sulfide CVT precedents for Zr and Sc sulfides using I2 and evacuated ampoules (SS #1/#2, TS #1). Its severity is moderate-to-high due to sealed vacuum, volatile/toxic iodine, and the need for a pre-synthesized source charge [SS #1/#2; Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics for crystal growth may be acceptable, but composition control is weakly supported because no CS/SS/TS example shows ternary Na–Hf–S CVT, and Na volatility/fractionation is likely [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically dissimilar Na and Hf cations, so the cation-radius mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference], although CS NaLnS2 analogues show that large A/M size contrast can still crystallize in Caswellsilverite frameworks (CS #1/#3). The target description includes mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, while CS analogues mainly emphasize edge/corner sharing (CS #1–#5), so the target’s more complex connectivity increases disorder/amorphization risk. The exact unit-cell atom count is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as not triggered due to insufficient evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaHfS2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several chemically similar Caswellsilverite ALnS2 compounds are also hull-stable (CS #1–#5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS, especially NaErS2 and NaYbS2, which share the Na–M–S2 layered Caswellsilverite motif (CS #1/#3). SS and TS mainly support general sulfide octahedral chemistry and high-temperature sulfide synthesis, but they provide insufficient direct evidence for ternary NaHfS2 formation. The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic and structural analogy to known Na/Li trivalent-metal sulfides (CS #1–#5). The most critical limiting factor is stabilizing and ordering Hf in a sulfide environment, likely requiring reducing sulfur chemical potential control and enhanced diffusion through flux or repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference].
