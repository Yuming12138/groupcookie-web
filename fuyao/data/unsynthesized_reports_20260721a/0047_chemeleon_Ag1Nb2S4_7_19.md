### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues in the Nb–S family are also often on the hull, including NbS2/Cu1/2NbS2-type, Nb3PbS6, Nb3SnS6, Mn(NbS2)4, and Nb2PdS5 (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Ag–Nb–S polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state.**  
Solid-state analogues use evacuated tubes or quartz encapsulation at 700–1090 °C for days to weeks, so the temperature is moderate but the sealed/vacuum condition and sulfur volatility impose penalties (SS-3; TS-5). Repeat grinding/heating is justified by Nb2PdS5 synthesis using repeated 850 °C treatments and by long anneals in NbS2-related compounds (TS-5; SS-3). The uncertainty is whether AgNb2S4 forms directly or first produces Ag2S/NbS2 mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state.**  
Elemental Nb is refractory and diffusion-limited, which lowers precursor activity and ion-diffusion scores [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is partly rescued because the target has the same NbS2-like edge-sharing NbS6 sheet motif as SS analogues (SS-1 to SS-5). Long annealing requirements in Nb sulfides indicate that ordering of Ag into the sheet/interlayer environment may be slow (SS-3; TS-2; TS-3).

**F2 — Route 2 iodine CVT.**  
CVT analogues use evacuated ampoules, iodine, temperature gradients, and long heating, so deductions arise from vacuum, volatile species, multistep handling, and toxic iodine (SS-5; TS-1). The temperature can likely remain below 1000 °C by analogy to Cu1/2NbS2 at 950–870 °C, avoiding the >1000 °C deduction (SS-5; TS-1). The uncertainty is whether Ag transport is congruent with Nb and S transport under iodine [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 iodine CVT.**  
CVT improves mass transport relative to bulk diffusion, and close NbS2 analogues are successfully grown by this method (SS-1; SS-5; TS-1). However, the method still requires long times up to 3 weeks and may produce non-stoichiometry or require quenching, as noted for Cu1/2NbS2-type growth (SS-5; TS-1). Structural match is strong, but Ag ordering remains a possible kinetic bottleneck [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 KCl flux.**  
The KCl flux route is based on 900 °C processing under N2 or H2S, so it avoids very high temperature but still requires controlled/reactive atmosphere and multistep flux removal (SS-2). If H2S is used, toxicity and gas handling are significant penalties (SS-2). The uncertainty is whether a sealed sulfur-buffered N2/vacuum variant can replace H2S while maintaining sulfide stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 KCl flux.**  
Using preformed NbS2 lowers the reconstruction burden because the target preserves NbS2-like edge-sharing NbS6 sheets (SS-1 to SS-3). Molten KCl provides liquid-phase transport and allows reaction at 900 °C, supporting better kinetics than direct elemental solid-state synthesis (SS-2). The main kinetic uncertainty is Ag ordering and whether Ag inserts into the desired linear S coordination rather than forming Ag2S or metallic Ag [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines very different cation environments, with Nb in six-coordinate NbS6 and Ag in linear S2 coordination, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, the close SS analogues are crystalline layered NbS2 structures with similar Nb–S distances and sheet topology, supporting a relatively low amorphization tendency (SS-1 to SS-5). The target description does not show a large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the final F4 remains favorable.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgNb2S4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic Nb–S analogues are also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The structural support is strong: the target’s NbS6 edge-sharing 2D sheet is very close to NbS2-like analogues with nearly identical Nb–S distances around 2.49–2.50 Å (SS-1 to SS-5). The chemical support for Ag is also meaningful because AgMo6S8 contains linearly coordinated Ag–S with Ag–S = 2.44 Å, close to the target Ag–S = 2.42 Å (CS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing NbS2-like sheet chemistry, which suggests a flux/topotactic route can minimize structural reconstruction (SS-2). The most critical limiting factor is control of Ag incorporation and sulfur chemical potential, because volatile sulfur, possible Ag2S/Ag segregation, and slow cation ordering are plausible competing issues [Reasonable Inference supported by long sealed-tube/CVT protocols in SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5].
