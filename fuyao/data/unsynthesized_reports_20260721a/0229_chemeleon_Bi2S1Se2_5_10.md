### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the predicted convex-hull ground state by the provided target data. This is reinforced by the closest structural and thermodynamic analogue Bi2Se3, which is also E_hull = 0 and experimentally synthesized (SS1, TS1). Additional Bi–chalcogen analogues with E_hull = 0 occur in TS2–TS5, supporting thermodynamic plausibility. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or S/Se site ordering.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 requires evacuated sealed ampoules and controlled heating/cooling, directly supported by Bi2Se3 Bridgman growth under high vacuum and quartz ampoule conditions (SS1, TS1). The main deductions come from vacuum, sealed volatile chalcogens, multistep growth, and hazardous chalcogen handling; no evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. Remaining uncertainty is the optimal Bi–S–Se liquidus and whether sulfur partitions uniformly into the Bi2Se3-type lattice.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Melt growth gives high diffusion kinetics because the reaction proceeds through a liquid phase, as used for Bi2Se3 and layered Bi–Se analogues (SS1, SS2, SS3, TS1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because Bi2Se2S is an R-3m Bi2Se3-type layered structure, but S/Se ordering is an extrapolation from the structural match [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore kinetically strong but not completely risk-free with respect to compositional homogeneity.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses sealed-tube solid-state annealing, supported by Pb–Bi–Se sealed-tube synthesis and Bi2S3-related evacuated ampoule sintering (CS2, TS3). The route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C synthesis but still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile chalcogen conditions, and multistep grinding/annealing. TS3 indicates careful temperature control and complex phase behavior in Bi–S systems, increasing practical severity.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Binary precursors Bi2Se3 and Bi2S3 are chemically matched, but solid-state interdiffusion is slower than melt or flux processing [Reasonable Inference]. Bi2Se3 provides the correct R-3m parent framework (SS1, TS1), while Bi2S3 has a different stibnite-type structure and TS3 reports multiple ordered/disordered phases, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. The route is plausible for powders but may require repeated annealing to reach equilibrium.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux synthesis is supported by K2Bi8Se13 and MnBi2Se4-type Bi chalcogenides grown in alkali/polychalcogenide or chloride fluxes (SS5, TS2, TS4). It uses moderate temperatures but requires dry/inert handling, sealed tubes, long times, and sometimes air-sensitive K2Se/Cs2S-type precursors (SS5, TS2). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalties apply.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth has the best kinetic score because a molten flux lowers diffusion barriers and enables crystal growth at temperatures far below conventional solid-state diffusion limits, as shown in SS5/TS2 and TS4. TS4 explicitly reports flux use to isolate single crystals where phase control was difficult, supporting its kinetic advantage. The uncertainty is transferability to a simple Bi2Se2S phase without alkali incorporation or competing flux-stabilized phases.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
CVT is supported by Bi3Se4Br and Bi–S sulfosalt transport syntheses in sealed ampoules/tubes (CS1, SS4, CS4, CS5). The route avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure but requires vacuum/sealing, volatile transport agents, multiple steps, and hazardous/corrosive halide chemistry. The main practical penalty is the possibility of halide incorporation, since the closest CVT Bi–Se analogue is itself bromide-containing (CS1, SS4).

**F3 — Route 4.**  
CVT improves kinetics by transporting species through the vapor phase rather than relying on bulk solid diffusion, and Bi–Se/Bi–S transport is demonstrated in CS1, SS4, CS4, and CS5. However, the known CVT products are structurally less direct than Bi2Se3: Bi3Se4Br is monoclinic and Br-containing, while the target is R-3m and halide-free. Thus, CVT is useful as a crystal-growth fallback but less ideal for first synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, Bi, so cation radius mismatch is absent by formula. The structure is a simple layered R-3m Bi–chalcogen framework with edge- and corner-sharing octahedra and no reported face-sharing, closely resembling crystalline Bi2Se3 (SS1, TS1). The amorphization risk is low, although thin films or rapid quenching could still produce amorphous Bi–S–Se glasses by [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Bi2Se2S is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Bi2Se3 analogue is also a stable, experimentally synthesized R-3m layered phase (SS1, TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is strong overall: SS/TS provide an almost direct structural parent, while CS/TS show that Bi–Se and Bi–S chalcogenides are accessible by sealed-tube, melt, flux, and CVT routes (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most important enabling factor is the isostructural relationship to Bi2Se3, which strongly supports crystallization of the layered Bi–chalcogen framework (SS1, TS1). The main limiting factor is controlling S/Se stoichiometry and avoiding segregation into Bi2Se3 + Bi2S3 or other Bi–S–Se ordered/disordered phases, a concern supported by phase-control complexity in Bi–S-related systems (TS3). Flux growth may overcome kinetic limitations, while Bridgman/melt growth is the most direct route to the target structure.
