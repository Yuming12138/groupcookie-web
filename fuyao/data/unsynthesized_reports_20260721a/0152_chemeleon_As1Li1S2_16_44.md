### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability bin. The closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue is also LiAsS₂ with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1; SS1; TS1). This strongly supports ground-state synthesizability, although DFT hull stability does not by itself guarantee kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct Combination

The strongest evidence is the exact LiAsS₂ analogue synthesized by direct reaction of Li₂S, As, and S (CS1; SS1; TS1). F2 is reduced because practical synthesis should use sealed/inert handling due to sulfur volatility, arsenic toxicity, and Li₂S air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference], even though CS1/SS1/TS1 do not provide full condition details. F3 is high because the structural analogue is essentially identical and no extensive reconstruction away from the target framework is needed (CS1; SS1; TS1). Remaining uncertainty is mainly the absence of a reported temperature/time window for the exact direct reaction in the provided data (insufficient detailed condition evidence from CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-Tube Solid-State Fusion

This route is supported by LiAsSe₂ being synthesized quantitatively at 500 °C under vacuum and by Li₂TeS₃ being synthesized at 460 °C in sealed ampoules (CS3; SS4/5; TS4/5). F2 is moderate because vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogens, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents add operational severity (CS3; SS4/5; TS4/5; [Reasonable Inference]). F3 is high because LiAsSe₂ shares monoclinic Cc symmetry and analogous LiCh₆/AsCh₃ coordination (CS3; SS5; TS5). The key uncertainty is whether sulfur substitution suppresses or enhances polymorphism relative to the selenide analogue [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sulfur-Rich Flux

Flux growth is plausible because LiAsS₂ was first prepared by alkali-metal polychalcogenide flux, and LiSbS₂ uses excess sulfur as flux to obtain crystalline product (CS1/5; SS1/3; TS1/3). F2 is moderate-to-low because sulfur-rich sealed ampoules require careful pressure and stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is high because a flux can accelerate mass transport and crystal growth, reducing diffusion limitations (CS5; SS3; TS3). Uncertainty remains because exact flux composition, temperature, and separation procedure are not provided for LiAsS₂ in CS1/SS1/TS1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Solution Precipitation + Recrystallization

NaAsS₂ provides evidence for an arsenosulfide precipitation route followed by ampoule recrystallization at 460 °C (CS2; SS2; TS2). F2 is moderate because the route is multi-step and still requires evacuated ampoule treatment (CS2; SS2; TS2). F3 has good precursor mixing but lower structural certainty, since Na⁺ and Li⁺ differ substantially in size and coordination chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The NaAsS₂ reference explicitly indicates prior difficulty obtaining single crystals, so this route is less reliable for target LiAsS₂ phase control (CS2; SS2; TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains Li and As cations with very different bonding preferences—ionic Li–S octahedra and covalent trigonal AsS₃ units—which triggers the cation-size/bonding-mismatch risk [Reasonable Inference; local coordination evidence from target and CS1/SS1/TS1].
