### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which places it on the computed ground-state hull according to the provided target data. Similar K–pnictogen–selenide, K–Sb–sulfide, Cs–Sb–selenide, and K–chalcogenide phases also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS/SS/TS (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). This strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility, although computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic formation as a single phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal/solvothermal.**  
Condition severity is moderate rather than low because analogues require sealed evacuated or inert ampoules and controlled cooling, even though temperatures are only 115–180 °C (CS2; SS2; SS4; TS2; TS4). Toxic Sb/Se handling is a real practical penalty [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide strong support for the temperature and vessel conditions; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact pH or K₂CO₃/K₂Se speciation needed for KSbSe₂, so those details remain uncertain.

**F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal/solvothermal.**  
Kinetics are favorable because solution-mediated hydrothermal transport forms close analogues at low temperature, including KSbS₂ and Cs–Sb–Se (CS2; SS2; SS4; TS2; TS4). Structural reconstruction should be minimal because KSbS₂ has the same C2/c framework and the target is the Se analogue (CS2; SS4; TS4). The remaining uncertainty is whether Sb₂Se₃ dissolves/reacts sufficiently under the selected K₂CO₃/KOH/K₂Se conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 direct sealed-ampoule solid-state.**  
The route is penalized for evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile/reactive K/Se, air-sensitive K, and toxic Se/Sb [Reasonable Inference]. K–Se analogues are synthesized in evacuated graphitized ampoules at 900 °C or 973 K, supporting feasibility but also confirming the need for controlled sealed-tube practice (CS3; SS3; TS3; CS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no direct elemental KSbSe₂ recipe, so the temperature program is extrapolated.

**F3 — Route 2 direct sealed-ampoule solid-state.**  
Diffusion should be manageable because K/Se become mobile under the proposed temperature range and analogous K–Se compounds form in sealed ampoules (CS3; SS3; TS3; CS5). However, starting from elements requires complete framework assembly and stoichiometry control, so structural reconstruction is less favorable than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether competing K–Se, Sb–Se, or off-stoichiometric ternary phases crystallize before KSbSe₂ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 Bridgman/melt growth.**  
Bridgman growth is supported by RbSbS₂, but CS/SS/TS do not provide exact growth temperature, so high-temperature deductions are treated neutrally for that specific criterion (CS4; SS5; TS5). The route still incurs deductions for sealed growth, volatile K/Se, multi-step processing, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. It is therefore more severe than hydrothermal synthesis.

**F3 — Route 3 Bridgman/melt growth.**  
Melt growth can overcome diffusion limits and is demonstrated for the related RbSbS₂ system (CS4; SS5; TS5). However, the K–Sb–Se system may have vapor-pressure and incongruent-melting issues, so phase selection during slow cooling is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The route is better suited for crystal growth after a phase-pure powder charge is established [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains strongly different K and Sb cations, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target and KSbS₂ analogue show mixed edge/corner sharing but no reported face-sharing, so the full mixed-connectivity penalty is not triggered (CS2; SS4; TS4).
