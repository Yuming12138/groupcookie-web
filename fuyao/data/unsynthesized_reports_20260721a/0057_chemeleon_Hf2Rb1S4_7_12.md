### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which gives the maximum thermodynamic score (Target). This is reinforced by several stable sulfide analogues with E_hull = 0.0, including RbCeS2 and Ba2HfS4 (SS-1/TS-1, CS-3). The main uncertainty is that convex-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as CS Ba–Hf–S flux syntheses produced multiple phases despite near-hull energies (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 1 flux severity

Flux growth is directly supported by Ba–Hf–S analogues synthesized in BaCl2 flux at 1050 °C in vacuum-sealed silica ampoules (CS-1, CS-2). The score is lowered because the route requires high temperature, controlled vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive Rb sulfide/Rb flux handling, and flux-removal steps [Reasonable Inference; CS-1/CS-2 and SS-5/TS-4]. The uncertainty is whether RbCl/RbI flux will stabilize the same monoclinic Rb(HfS2)2 phase rather than alternative Rb–Hf–S or HfS2-rich products.

#### F3 — Route 1 flux kinetics

Flux improves kinetics because CS-1 and CS-2 produced complex Ba–Hf–S phases at 1050 °C from Hf powder, well below the melting point of Hf [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-2]. However, Hf metal remains a refractory precursor, and CS-1/CS-2 explicitly report multiphase Ba–Hf–S products, so precursor activity and phase selection are only moderate. The structural match is good at the HfS6-octahedron level but not identical in space group or topology (CS-1–CS-3, Target).

#### F2 — Route 2 solid-state severity

The sealed-tube solid-state route is supported by Ba2HfS4 synthesis from BaS/S/Hf-related mixtures with multiple firings, regrinding, pellet pressing, and high-temperature treatment (CS-3). Severity is high because sulfur/Rb volatility requires sealed handling, the process is multi-step, and Rb sulfides are air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference; drybox/sealed ampoule handling is reported in CS-3]. The uncertainty is whether the target can be formed below the high temperatures used for Ba2HfS4, since Rb volatility likely limits the usable temperature window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 solid-state kinetics

Kinetics are expected to be slow because CS-3 required long firings and repeated processing for Ba2HfS4. Hf metal is a dense refractory precursor, which places precursor activity and diffusion difficulty in the lower range [Reasonable Inference]. The target shares HfS6 coordination with Ba2HfS4, but forming ordered Rb(HfS2)2 from binary/elemental reagents likely requires extensive reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from CS-3 and Target].

#### F2 — Route 3 H2S severity

H2S sulfidation is supported by RbLnS2 and CeScS3 syntheses in flowing H2S at high temperature (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). It avoids sealed sulfur pressure but introduces toxic/reactive gas handling, so the severity remains moderate to high. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates Hf-containing Rb–Hf–S formation by oxide sulfidation; Hf evidence instead points to sealed metal/sulfide routes (CS-1–CS-3).

#### F3 — Route 3 H2S kinetics

Rb carbonate/lanthanide oxide sulfidation works for RbLnS2 (SS-1/TS-1), but HfO2 is likely much less reactive than lanthanide oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Complete conversion from oxide/carbonate to ordered Rb(HfS2)2 requires both anion exchange and framework reconstruction, so structural reconstruction is difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient for direct HfO2-to-Rb–Hf sulfide kinetics, so this score is conservative.

#### F2 — Route 4 topochemical intercalation severity

Topochemical intercalation is inferred from the target formula Rb(HfS2)2 and edge-sharing HfS6 framework (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). It should use lower temperatures than the 1000–1350 °C bulk routes reported for related sulfides (CS-1–CS-3, SS-1/TS-1). Severity remains moderate because Rb metal/Rb sulfides require glovebox handling and sealed reaction containment [Reasonable Inference; glovebox/sealed ampoule practices appear in CS-2/CS-3/SS-5/TS-4].

#### F3 — Route 4 topochemical kinetics

Kinetics are favorable if pre-synthesized HfS2 supplies the HfS6 framework and only Rb insertion/ordering is needed (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). Rb–S distances in target match RbCeS2 closely, supporting chemically reasonable Rb coordination in a sulfide lattice (SS-1/TS-1, Target). The main uncertainty is the absence of a direct topochemical Rb–Hf–S reference in CS/SS/TS, so this route receives an expert penalty despite its high mechanistic plausibility.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Rb and Hf environments, reflected in long Rb–S bonds and shorter Hf–S bonds (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). The target description indicates edge/corner polyhedral connectivity but does not explicitly show face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not fully triggered (Target). Unit-cell atom count is not given, so evidence for C2 is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb(HfS2)2 is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom (Target). The CS data strongly support Hf–S octahedral chemistry because multiple Ba–Hf–S compounds containing HfS6 octahedra have been synthesized, although some were multiphase products (CS-1–CS-3). The SS/TS data support Rb incorporation into sulfide lattices, especially through RbCeS2 with Rb–S distances close to the target (SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the apparent HfS2-like edge-sharing octahedral framework, which suggests a low-reconstruction topochemical route [Reasonable Inference from Target]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Rb stoichiometry and phase selectivity under sulfur-rich, sealed, or flux conditions, since related Ba–Hf–S and alkali/actinide sulfide flux routes report multiphase products and difficult purification (CS-1, CS-2, SS-5, TS-4).
