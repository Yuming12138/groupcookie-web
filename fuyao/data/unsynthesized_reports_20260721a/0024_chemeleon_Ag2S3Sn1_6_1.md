### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state composition under the employed thermodynamic model. Thermodynamic analogues include E_hull = 0 Ag2HgSnSe4 and Cu2ZnSnS4, and near-hull Ag2CdGeS4 at 0.0029 eV/atom, supporting that tetrahedral Ag/Sn/chalcogenide-related frameworks can be thermodynamically stable (TS1, TS2, TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that a zero calculated hull energy does not guarantee easy kinetic access or absence of polymorphic competition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 needs evacuated ampoules because analogous sulfides are synthesized in sealed evacuated quartz or silica ampoules (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The required temperatures are moderate relative to refractory ceramic synthesis, but sulfur volatility and multi-step annealing impose penalties (CS1, SS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any required high pressure for this route; high-pressure CS2 is chemically similar but structurally octahedral and therefore not weighted strongly for the tetrahedral target.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state reaction from elements or binary sulfides has moderate precursor activity and may require long anneals, as seen in CS1, SS1, and SS2/TS1. The tetrahedral network similarity helps structural reconstruction because analogues share corner-sharing AgS4/MS4 units (CS1, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is cation ordering between Ag and Sn, which may be slow in a solid-state route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Bridgman growth requires vacuum/sealed ampoules and careful thermal gradients, consistent with CS3 and CS5. It avoids external high pressure; CS5 discusses high-pressure phase studies, but the Bridgman growth itself uses evacuated ampoules, so the >5 GPa deduction is not applied. The route is operationally more demanding than powder synthesis because it requires controlled solidification [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt or near-melt transport gives better kinetics than dry solid-state diffusion; CS3 and CS5 demonstrate directional solidification for chalcopyrite-like chalcogenides. Structural similarity to chalcopyrite-like AgInS2/AgGaS2 supports crystallization into a tetrahedral framework (CS3, CS5). The uncertainty is whether Ag2SnS3 melts congruently or decomposes/peritectically, which is not specified by CS/SS/TS and is therefore a significant [Reasonable Inference] risk.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The solvothermal route has mild temperature requirements because SS3 uses 200 °C for 24 h and SS5/TS3 uses 180 °C for 20 min. It avoids vacuum ampoules and high pressure above 5 GPa, but it uses toxic/irritant organics and metal salts and includes several workup steps (SS3, SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly proves Ag2SnS3 can be made this way, so the condition score is favorable but not decisive.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Metal chloride/salt and thiourea-type solution precursors provide high precursor activity, directly supported by SS3 and SS5/TS3. Low-temperature nucleation from solution minimizes diffusion distances, explaining short reaction times in the analogues (SS3, SS5). The uncertainty is phase selectivity in the Ag–Sn–S system because the closest solution analogues are Cu/Zn/Sn sulfides, not Ag2SnS3 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Flux synthesis operates at relatively low temperature, consistent with 400–520 °C in TS5. However, it requires evacuated ampoules, sulfur/polysulfide control, air-sensitive alkali sulfide handling, and post-growth washing, giving a lower F2 score (TS5, SS4). The supercritical ammonia route in SS4 is considered supporting evidence for chalcogenide solvothermal chemistry but is not preferred because it involves high-pressure NH3 and product-mixture issues.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Fluxes provide high ion mobility and can crystallize complex Sn sulfide frameworks at lower temperature than direct solid-state reaction, as shown by TS5. The kinetic score is therefore high, but structural specificity is less certain because flux products can depend sensitively on flux composition and temperature (TS5). SS4 also reports phase mixture/contamination in related Ag sulfides, supporting a phase-control penalty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically distinct Ag and Sn cations, and their tetrahedral ionic radii differ enough to trigger the radius-mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. However, the described structure is a corner-sharing tetrahedral network, and structurally similar analogues are also ordered corner-sharing tetrahedral frameworks, reducing amorphization concern (SS1, SS2, SS3). The exact unit-cell atom count is not fully established from CS/SS/TS; based on the target description it is not expected to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2SnS3 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several thermodynamic analogues are also on or near the hull (TS1, TS2, TS3). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis of related Ag- and Sn-containing tetrahedral chalcogenides by sealed ampoule, melt/anneal, Bridgman, flux, and solution routes (CS1, CS3, CS5, SS1, SS2, SS3, SS5, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide framework shared by the target and many analogues (CS1, SS1, SS2, SS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible Ag–Sn–S binaries/ternaries and Ag/Sn ordering, especially because no provided source is a direct Ag2SnS3 synthesis example [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with solvothermal synthesis best for rapid powder discovery and sealed-ampoule/Bridgman routes better for equilibrium phase confirmation.
