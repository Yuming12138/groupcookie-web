### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which gives a near-maximum thermodynamic score under the requested rubric [target data]. Stable analogues NiAsS, NiAsSe, NiPS, and Ni₃S₄ in the CS/SS/TS sets reinforce that Ni-containing chalcogenide/p-block frameworks can be true ground states rather than only metastable products [CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3, SS5, TS1–TS4]. Remaining uncertainty is that the DFT hull does not prove the Pmn2₁ polymorph is accessible under laboratory kinetics or that Ge/S ordering will be clean [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature because closest analogues are made in sealed silica tubes around 800 °C with optional 690 °C long annealing [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile sulfur containment, and multi-step heating/annealing [CS1, SS1, TS1]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful signal for this route because NiAsS is top-ranked in all three categories [CS1, SS1, TS1].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are moderate because elemental Ni/Ge powders are less reactive than solution precursors, while preformed NiS or staged sulfuration can improve chemical intimacy [CS1, SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because NiAsS/NiPS share the same mixed octahedral/tetrahedral connectivity, but long annealing and slow cooling in analogues indicate ordering is not instantaneous [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3]. The main uncertainty is the Ge substitution, for which CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct synthesized NiGeS precedent [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; neutral 50 for Ge-specific kinetic transfer].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The halogen-assisted route also operates near 800 °C and avoids external pressure, which is favorable [CS2]. It is penalized more than Route 1 because Cl₂ is a reactive/toxic transport gas and the reported analogue route uses evacuation, Cl₂ backfill, slow heating/cooling, and repeated grinding/reaction cycles [CS2]. SS and TS give insufficient direct signal for Cl₂-assisted Ge sulfide transport because their closest entries mainly support sealed-tube solid-state or HP routes rather than halogen transport [insufficient evidence from SS/TS; neutral 50 for this route-specific factor].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Halogen transport can improve mass transport and crystal growth compared with purely static elemental powders, consistent with the reported Co₁₋ₓNiₓAsS elemental/Cl₂ route [CS2; transport benefit is Reasonable Inference]. Repeated grinding/reaction cycles in the analogue indicate that complete homogenization and ordering may still be kinetically demanding [CS2]. The lack of direct Ge evidence creates uncertainty in Ge chloride/sulfide volatility and possible off-stoichiometry [insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Ge-specific Cl₂ behavior; neutral 50].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

High-pressure synthesis is clearly severe because related HP analogues require ~60–65 kbar and 1000–1700 °C with specialized belt or tetrahedral-anvil equipment [CS5, SS4, TS5]. This route receives strong deductions for >5 GPa pressure, high temperature, encapsulation of volatile sulfur, and multi-step HP handling [CS5, SS4, TS5; sulfur containment is Reasonable Inference]. Closest NiAsS/NiPS analogues do not require HP, so HP should be considered only if ambient-pressure routes fail [CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High T/P can accelerate densification and enable dense polymorph selection, as seen in HP pnictide/chalcogenide analogues [CS5, SS4, TS5]. However, short HP dwell times and the lack of direct NiGeS/Ge-sulfide HP analogue evidence lower kinetic confidence [CS5, SS4, TS5; insufficient Ge-specific evidence from CS/SS/TS, neutral 50]. The route may be useful for suppressing decomposition or stabilizing a dense distortion, but phase recovery on decompression is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline small-cell ordered framework rather than a large complex cell, and the provided structure description does not indicate >40 atoms per cell [target data]. A radius/coordination mismatch between Ni and Ge is likely significant, which can increase local strain and disorder risk [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS ionic-radius evidence, neutral 50]. The structure has corner and edge sharing but no reported face sharing, so the connectivity risk is moderate rather than high [target data].

NiGeS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target data]. The strongest support comes from NiAsS, which is top-ranked chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically and is synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods as a stable 1:1:1 Ni–p-block–S framework [CS1, SS1, TS1]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple stable synthesized Ni–Pn–Ch analogues with the same Ni-centered octahedral and tetrahedral network motif [CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. Overall, sealed-tube solid-state synthesis with careful sulfur activity and long annealing is the best first attempt [CS1, SS1, TS1].
