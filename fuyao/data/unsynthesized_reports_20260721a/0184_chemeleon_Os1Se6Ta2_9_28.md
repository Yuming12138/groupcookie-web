### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the computed convex hull and justifying a very high thermodynamic score. Thermodynamically similar analogues are also mostly at or near the hull, including Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂, TaSe₂, Ta₂NiSe₅, and Ta₂PdSe₆ (TS). Chemically similar Ta–Pt/Pd/Ni–Se phases include multiple E_hull = 0 or near-zero examples (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Os–Se/Ta–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated sealed-tube solid-state conditions, which are directly supported by Ta₂PtSe₇, Ta₂PdSe₆, Ta₂NiSe₅, and TaSe₂ syntheses (CS/SS/TS). The severity deductions arise from vacuum sealing and volatile Se in sealed ampoules, both present in the cited analogues (CS/SS/TS), plus toxicity concerns for Se/Os handling [Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature deduction is applied, consistent with CS/SS/TS analogues. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Os-specific sealed-tube behavior, so this part is neutral-to-uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Ta and Os powders are expected to react slowly because both are refractory metals [Reasonable Inference], although elemental powder routes are proven for Ta–Pd/Pt/Ni–Se and TaSe₂ analogues (CS/SS/TS). The target’s 2D molybdenite-derived framework closely resembles TaSe₂ and layered Ta–M–Se analogues, improving the structural reconstruction score (SS/TS; CS). Long durations and repeated grinding in Ta₂PdSe₆-type systems indicate kinetic limitations and phase homogenization issues (CS/SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Os diffusion rates, so that portion is inferred.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more operationally severe because it uses vacuum-sealed ampoules plus volatile/corrosive halogens, as in TaSe₂, Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂, and Ta₂Pt₃Se₈ CVT examples (SS/TS; CS). It also requires at least a prereaction and a transport-growth step, matching multi-step CVT workflows in SS/TS. The temperatures are still moderate, typically 700–900 °C, and no high pressure is involved (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for optimal Os halide transport chemistry, so halogen choice remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT improves mass transport compared with bulk diffusion by forming mobile halide/chalcogenide species [Reasonable Inference], and CVT is explicitly successful for TaSe₂ and Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂-type layered selenides (SS/TS). The structural match is strong because both target and TaSe₂ are molybdenite-derived 2D systems (SS/TS). Kinetic risk remains from possible Os–halide volatility mismatch or transport selectivity [Reasonable Inference]. This route scores best overall because transport-assisted recrystallization can overcome slow solid-state cation diffusion suggested by long annealing in CS/SS/TS analogues.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses the same sealed evacuated ampoule conditions as TaSe₂ and ternary Ta–M–Se analogues (CS/SS/TS), but it is penalized as multi-step. Se volatility and vacuum sealing are unavoidable, as shown by the broad use of sealed tubes in CS/SS/TS. Temperature is not especially severe because TaSe₂ and Ta₂NiSe₅-type systems form in the 650–850 °C range (SS/TS; CS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for topochemical Os insertion, so this route is more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Using TaSe₂ as a precursor is structurally attractive because TaSe₂ and the target share molybdenite-derived layered Ta–Se motifs (SS/TS). However, Os incorporation into a preformed framework likely requires slow diffusion and ordering [Reasonable Inference], and Ta₂PdSe₆-related syntheses show repeated heating/grinding may be required to improve homogeneity (CS/SS/TS). The reconstruction score is high, but the diffusion score remains low because Os is refractory and no direct Os analogue is provided [Reasonable Inference]. This route may be useful for exploratory phase formation but is less preferred than CVT for crystal growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline 2D molybdenite-derived selenide with simple 6-coordinate metal environments and 3-coordinate Se environments, similar to crystalline TaSe₂ and layered Ta–M–Se analogues (SS/TS; CS). No evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests glass-forming chemistry, very large unit-cell complexity, or strongly mixed polyhedral connectivity. The cation size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% for 6-coordinate Ta/Os [Reasonable Inference], and the formula unit is small. Remaining uncertainty is that P1 symmetry and cation ordering may introduce local disorder, but not enough to imply high amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₂OsSe₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many thermodynamically similar Ta–Se layered phases are also at the hull (TS). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of layered Ta–M–Se and molybdenite-derived Ta–Se frameworks by evacuated sealed-tube and CVT methods, especially Ta₂PdSe₆, Ta₂NiSe₅, TaSe₂, and Co₂Ta₄PdSe₁₂ (CS/SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ta–Se layered/molybdenite-derived structural motif, which appears repeatedly in stable, synthesizable analogues (SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is the absence of any Os-containing analogue in the provided data, making Os incorporation, cation ordering, and halide transport behavior uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with CVT after prereaction recommended for the best balance of phase equilibration and crystal growth.
