### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 scoring range by the required rubric. TS analogues include several E_hull = 0 compounds, especially PtGeS, IrAs₃, OsP₂, TaIrTe₄, and Ir₂S₃, showing that neighboring Ir/chalcogen and Pt/Ge/S chemistries often form stable crystalline phases (TS1–TS5). The closest analogue PtGeS is also E_hull = 0 and spinel-like, giving strong support (CS1, SS3, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that no source reports the exact GeIrS experimental phase, so the phase diagram competition is inferred rather than directly observed.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The severity score is moderate because the route uses standard evacuated quartz tubes and 600–900 °C anneals, matching PtGeS-family synthesis (CS1–CS3), but vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and grinding/reannealing all add deductions. Kinetics are the main limitation because elemental Ir is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and the PtGeS analogues required 4–6 weeks to approach equilibrium (CS1–CS3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because PtGeS has the same spinel-like Ge–M–S framework type (CS1, SS3, TS1). Insufficient evidence from SS beyond PtGeS for exact GeIrS ordering means the Ir-for-Pt substitution remains uncertain.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth is supported for IrTe₂ using Ir and Te self-flux in evacuated tubes with high-temperature slow cooling (SS1, SS2), but applying this to GeIrS sulfide chemistry is [Reasonable Inference]. Severity is higher than Route 1 because sulfur-rich flux requires sealed containment and may require temperatures near or above 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may improve through liquid-mediated transport, but insufficient evidence from CS and TS directly supports Ge–Ir–S sulfide flux growth. The largest uncertainty is whether a sulfur/Ge–S flux dissolves enough Ir without forming Ir₂S₃ or GeSₓ competitors (TS5; [Reasonable Inference]).

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT is supported by TS4, where an Ir-containing telluride-related entry is associated with vacuum, TeBr₄ transport, and a two-zone furnace. The route is more chemically speculative for GeIrS because no CS source reports CVT of PtGeS-type spinel-like sulfides; this is explicitly [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics could benefit from vapor transport, but halogen chemistry may destabilize the target or introduce impurities [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS confirms any structurally similar Ge–Ir–S CVT pathway.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
High-pressure synthesis is supported by CS5 for OsSbSe-type late-transition-metal chalcogenide/pnictide compounds requiring 35–50 kbar and 850–1050 °C. This route is a fallback because the closest PtGeS analogue forms at ambient pressure (CS1/SS3/TS1), so pressure is not evidently necessary. The severity is high because specialized multi-anvil equipment and pressure-cell containment are required, and sulfur volatility remains an issue [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may improve by densification, but CS5 reports phase-purity challenges and repeated processing, so the route is not preferred.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has chemically distinct Ge and Ir cations, so size/chemistry mismatch is counted as one amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference]. However, PtGeS demonstrates that a Ge + late-transition-metal + chalcogen spinel-like framework can crystallize rather than amorphize (CS1, SS3, TS1). The target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not reported face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully activated. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any glass-forming tendency in this exact GeIrS composition.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GeIrS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the thermodynamic analogue list includes multiple stable Ir/chalcogen and PtGeS-type phases (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from PtGeS, which is chemically close, spinel-like, E_hull = 0, and experimentally synthesized by direct sealed-tube reaction from elements (CS1, SS3, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the very close PtGeS analogue, which validates both the framework topology and a practical 600–900 °C evacuated-ampoule route (CS1). The most critical limiting factor is slow equilibration involving refractory Ir and possible competition with binary Ir sulfides such as Ir₂S₃ (TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, ambient-pressure sealed-tube synthesis should be attempted first, with flux/CVT or high pressure reserved for crystal growth or failed phase-purity cases.
