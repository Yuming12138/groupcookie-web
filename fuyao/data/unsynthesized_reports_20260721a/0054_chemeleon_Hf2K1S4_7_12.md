### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic-stability category. Chemically similar Ba₂HfS₄ is also on the hull, and Ba₅Hf₄S₁₃/Ba₆Hf₅S₁₆ are only ~0.026–0.029 eV/atom above hull (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfides include several hull-stable materials (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the supplied analogues do not directly map the K–Hf–S ternary phase diagram; insufficient exact competing-phase evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral.

**F2 — Route 1, sealed solid state.**  
High-temperature sealed solid-state synthesis is strongly supported for Ba/Hf/S and related sulfides, but the conditions are severe: Ba₂HfS₄ required staged firings and 1350 °C final treatment (CS-3), while many analogues require evacuated ampoules and regrinding (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4; TS-2). The target route likely needs vacuum sealing because sulfur and alkali sulfides are volatile/reactive [Reasonable Inference]. The penalty is high because of K₂S air sensitivity, volatile sulfur, multiple grinding/annealing steps, and high Hf diffusion barriers.

**F3 — Route 1, sealed solid state.**  
The HfS₆ octahedral motif is well supported by Ba/Hf/S analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3), which improves structural feasibility. However, direct solid-state reaction from Hf metal is kinetically slow because Hf is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and the closest Ba₂HfS₄ route required multiple long firings and regrinding (CS-3). The route is feasible but likely slow and phase-competition-prone.

**F2 — Route 2, flux growth.**  
Flux growth reduces the required crystallization temperature compared with direct solid-state synthesis, and both Hf sulfide flux growth and K-containing sulfide flux growth are evidenced (CS-1, CS-2; TS-5). The route still requires glovebox loading, vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/polysulfide control, and flux removal (CS-1, CS-2; TS-5). Its condition severity is moderate rather than easy because K₂S₂/K₂S fluxes are air-sensitive and reactive [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2, flux growth.**  
Flux transport should improve Hf mobility and enable crystallization below the diffusion-limited solid-state regime [Reasonable Inference]. This inference is supported by Ba/Hf/S phases grown in BaCl₂ flux at 1050 °C and by KThCuS₃ grown in K₂S₂ flux at 500 °C (CS-1, CS-2; TS-5). The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether K(HfS₂)₂ has a broad crystallization window or competes with K₂HfS₃/HfS₂/K₂Sₓ phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3, H₂S sulfidation.**  
Flowing H₂S routes are supported for CeScS₃ and RbLnS₂ analogues at high temperature (SS-2, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3). Conditions are operationally hazardous because H₂S is toxic and reactive, but they avoid sealed-tube sulfur pressure and may be performed near 800–1000 °C [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct K–Hf–S evidence from SS/TS makes this route less preferred.

**F3 — Route 3, H₂S sulfidation.**  
H₂S provides high sulfur chemical potential and can convert oxide/carbonate mixtures to sulfides in RbLnS₂ synthesis (SS-5; TS-3). However, HfO₂ is refractory and difficult to sulfurize completely [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates K–Hf–S framework formation under H₂S. Thus the kinetics score is low despite a moderate condition-severity score.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size mismatch between 10-coordinate K and octahedral Hf [Reasonable Inference], triggering one amorphization-risk check. The target description gives ordered K coordination and HfS₆ octahedra with edge/corner sharing, but it does not report face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered. Related complex sulfides are crystalline under sealed or flux synthesis (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-3; TS-5), which supports a low–medium rather than high amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(HfS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically or structurally related sulfides are also hull-stable or near-hull (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The analogue support is good for Hf–S octahedral chemistry and sulfide synthesis generally, but only indirect for the specific K–Hf–S composition because the closest Hf examples are Ba–Hf–S (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The most critical enabling factor is use of a sulfur-rich alkali flux, which can provide K, buffer sulfur activity, and enhance mass transport, as suggested by K₂S₂ flux and BaCl₂ flux precedents (TS-5; CS-1, CS-2). The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving volatile/air-sensitive K–S species and possible competing Hf sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with flux growth the most balanced route.
