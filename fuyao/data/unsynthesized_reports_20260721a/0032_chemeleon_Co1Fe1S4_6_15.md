### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target FeCoS₄ has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues TS1–TS5 include several E_hull = 0.0 phases, supporting that similar sulfide/pnictide-sulfide frameworks can be ground-state phases [TS1–TS5]. CS is mixed because CS1 is substantially above hull while CS2/CS4/CS5 are stable; therefore CS gives partial support but is not the dominant thermodynamic evidence [CS1–CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or Fe/Co ordering under laboratory conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state sulfidation

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because sealed evacuated tubes are required and sulfur volatility must be controlled, as in Fe₂SiS₄, RuS₂, CoAsS, and NiPS/NiAsS syntheses [CS1, SS2/TS2, SS3, SS5/TS3/TS5]. Kinetically, elemental Fe/Co powders are less reactive than solution precursors, and long annealing/regrinding seen in SS3 and SS5/TS3 suggests slow ordering and diffusion. The route is nevertheless plausible because Fe–S and Co–S octahedral frameworks are chemically consistent with several analogues [CS1, SS3, SS5/TS3]. Remaining uncertainty is the sulfur chemical potential window for FeCoS₄ relative to FeS₂, CoS₂, FeS, and CoS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: chalcogen flux growth

Flux growth has similar sealed-tube severity because sulfur and Te require containment and flux removal, as shown for RuS₂ [SS2/TS2]. Kinetically, flux should improve transport and crystallinity, and RuS₂ provides a strong structural/anion-chemistry analogue because it has metal–S octahedra and S–S dimers [SS2/TS2]. The limiting issue is phase control: RuS₂ flux growth required narrow sulfur control and avoidance of RuTe₂ formation [SS2/TS2], so Fe/Co sulfide/telluride competition is a credible risk [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for Te-flux growth of Fe/Co sulfides specifically; CS is therefore treated as neutral for this route-specific flux factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: halogen-assisted CVT

CVT/mineralizer synthesis is conditionally demanding because it uses evacuated ampoules plus reactive halogen/halide species, as in CoAsS and Rh₃S₄ syntheses [SS3, TS4]. Kinetically, vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion and improve crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and TS4 demonstrates sulfide growth from Rh/RhCl₃/S in a temperature gradient. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS4 explicitly reports that Rh₃S₄ could not be obtained without neighboring Rh₁₇S₁₅, and SS3 required repeated grinding to reach homogeneous products. CS2/CS3 support chloride CVT chemistry generally, but because they are chloride aluminates rather than sulfides, CS2/CS3 provide limited direct signal for FeCoS₄ and are treated as weak/near-neutral for phase transferability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: high-pressure synthesis

High pressure strongly penalizes condition severity because SS4 used 65–89 kbar and specialized anvil equipment, while CS5 used 5 GPa belt-press synthesis. Kinetically, high pressure can stabilize dense pyrite/marcasite-like disulfide frameworks, and SS4 directly concerns pyrite-type MnS₂ and related chalcogenides. However, SS4 also reports phase separation, incomplete reactions, and polyphase products, so high pressure is a fallback rather than first-line route. Insufficient evidence from CS for Fe/Co disulfide high-pressure phase selectivity specifically; CS5 only shows that high-pressure sulfide synthesis is feasible in a chemically different Al–P–S system.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has very similar Fe–S and Co–S bond distances, 2.24–2.25 Å for Fe–S and 2.27–2.28 Å for Co–S, so a large cation-size mismatch is not indicated [target data]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeCoS₄ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom [target data]. The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar transition-metal sulfide or pnictide-sulfide frameworks with octahedral metal coordination and, in RuS₂, explicit S–S dimer chemistry [SS2/TS2; target data]. The most enabling factor is the chemically plausible marcasite/pyrite-derived octahedral disulfide topology combined with near-zero Fe/Co size mismatch in the reported bond lengths [target data; Reasonable Inference]. The most limiting factor is phase control under sulfur-rich sealed conditions, because analogous systems report sulfur-window sensitivity, competing phases, and long annealing/regrinding requirements [CS1, SS2/TS2, SS3, SS5/TS3, TS4]. Overall, synthesis appears likely but not trivial, with CVT/mineralizer-assisted sealed-tube growth the best first route for crystallinity and mass transport [SS3, TS4; Reasonable Inference].
