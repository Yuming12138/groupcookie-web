### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives a high F1 score of 95 under the specified rubric. Thermodynamic support is consistent with stable molybdenite-like MoS₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (SS1/TS1) and stable related sulfides such as Ta₂PdS₆ and Nb₂PdS₅ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS4, TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or Mo/W cation ordering, which is [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The condition severity is moderate because sealed vacuum/inert ampoules and sulfur volatility are required, consistent with evacuated silica-tube procedures in CS4, SS2, SS5, and TS4. Kinetically, this is the best route because MoS₂ and WS₂ already contain the target-like layered MS₂ framework; MoS₂ analogues have essentially identical Mo–S distances and edge-sharing MoS₆ sheets (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). The main uncertainty is whether Mo and W order into the predicted monoclinic C2 Mo₂WS₆ arrangement rather than forming a random Mo₁₋ₓWₓS₂ solid solution [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct elemental reaction is well supported for related sulfides, including Ta₂PdS₆, GaMo₄S₈, Nb₂PdS₅, and PbMo₆S₈ (CS3, CS4, SS5, TS4). Its severity is moderate because it requires sealed-vacuum handling and sulfur vapor control but not high pressure. Its kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because elemental Mo and W are refractory and require full lattice construction from metals and sulfur [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT is directly supported for MoS₂-type layered crystals grown at 1040–1120 °C under vacuum for 168–216 h (SS2/TS2). The route has good mass transport but harsher conditions due to high temperature, vacuum, volatile sulfur, and possible halogen transport agents. The key uncertainty is ternary stoichiometry control, since SS2/TS2 demonstrate binary or S/Se-layered growth rather than ordered Mo₂WS₆.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
CVD is supported only at a high level for MoS₂, because SS1/TS1 state that MoS₂ was prepared by CVD but provide no precursor, temperature, atmosphere, or equipment details; therefore, those missing details are treated as insufficient evidence from SS1/TS1 and neutral 50 where applicable. Thin-film sulfurization may enable lower-temperature crystallization and precise Mo:W dosing [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting issue is that ordered monoclinic Mo₂WS₆ may require post-growth annealing and may not form directly during rapid film growth [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains chemically similar group-6 cations Mo and W, so the cation-size mismatch is expected to be small [Reasonable Inference], and structurally similar MoS₂ sheets are simple edge-sharing frameworks (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). The target description does not indicate a very large unit cell or mixed edge/vertex/face-sharing complexity, so amorphization tendency is scored low. Remaining uncertainty is the exact unit-cell atom count from the CIF, which is not provided in the text; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that exact count.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, and stable sulfide analogues are present in CS, SS, and TS datasets. The strongest structural support comes from molybdenite-like MoS₂ analogues with the same 2D sheet motif, edge-sharing MS₆ coordination, and nearly identical M–S bond lengths (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the near-isostructural compatibility of MoS₂/WS₂ with the target Mo₂WS₆ sheet, making a layer-preserving anneal plausible [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is Mo/W cation ordering: a random Mo–W disulfide solid solution may form more readily than the ordered monoclinic C2 derivative [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best odds through sulfide-precursor annealing under controlled sulfur chemical potential.
