### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. The closest chemical analogues Hf(SeCl₆)₂ and Zr(SeCl₆)₂ are also on the hull, while Mo(SeCl₆)₂ is only 0.0339 eV/atom above hull (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; TS-3, TS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar molecular cluster halides are also mostly on or very near the hull (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or survival under volatile chloride conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses very mild temperatures, 140–155 °C, which strongly reduces thermal severity (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-2). The deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile SeCl₄/metal chlorides, and air-sensitive/toxic reagents, all of which are explicit or implied in the analogue CVT preparations (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; TS-5). No high pressure or high temperature is required, so the practical difficulty is mainly handling and ampoule control rather than energy input.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable because Zr/Hf/Mo analogues crystallize over days by vapor transport at 140–155 °C, implying high precursor activity and minimal solid-state diffusion limitation (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-2). The target has the same M(SeCl₆)₂ cluster topology, so structural reconstruction from volatile molecular chloride species should be modest [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/4/5]. The uncertainty is whether OsCl₄ has the same volatility/speciation and oxidation-state stability as Zr/Hf/Mo chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 remains low-temperature because SS-1 uses 150 °C for a structurally similar Mo–Se–Cl cluster reaction. It still requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and volatile, corrosive SOCl₂/SeCl₄, so the same vacuum, sealed-system, and toxicity deductions apply (SS-1; CS-2; CS-1). Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports chlorosolvothermal solvent severity for Os(SeCl₆)₂, so TS is neutral for this route-specific condition assessment.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
SS-1 reports nearly quantitative formation of a structurally similar MoSeOCl₇ cluster in SOCl₂ at 150 °C, supporting good molecular mobility and reaction kinetics. CS-2 also supports rapid dry-solution formation of molecular chloride salts, but it is chemically less similar than the M(SeCl₆)₂ CVT family. The main uncertainty is Os speciation in SOCl₂ and whether oxygen-free Os(SeCl₆)₂ crystallizes instead of solvated/oxo/chloro-osmium species [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 uses higher temperatures, roughly 230–550 °C, but still far below the 1000 °C deduction threshold (CS-3; TS-1; TS-4; SS-5). Deductions come from evacuated sealed tubes, volatile halides, air-sensitive/toxic chloride chemistry, and multi-step thermal programs with controlled cooling or workup (CS-3; TS-1; TS-4; SS-5). No high pressure is reported in the relevant analogues.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The kinetic score is lower because CS-3 reports Re–Se–Cl formation over six months and notes competing phases depending on temperature. TS-1 and TS-4 show that related Re chalcogen halide clusters can form in 48 h to several days, so the route is plausible but less direct. For Os(SeCl₆)₂, higher-temperature sealed-tube chemistry may increase side reactions or decomposition relative to the low-temperature CVT analogue route [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/4/5 versus CS-3].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a discrete zero-dimensional molecular cluster, not an extended mixed-network solid, which reduces amorphization risk. One trigger is assigned for the distinct Os and Se coordination/bonding mismatch, reflected in compact Os–Cl bonds and highly asymmetric short/long Se–Cl contacts in the target and analogues (target description; CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The unit cell/cluster size is not >40 atoms and only face-sharing octahedra are described, so the overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Os(SeCl₆)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state molecular halide phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple close M(SeCl₆)₂ analogues are on or near the hull (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; TS-3, TS-5). The synthesis evidence is unusually strong because Zr, Hf, and Mo analogues with the same M(SeCl₆)₂ chemical motif are made directly from MCl₄ + SeCl₄ in evacuated ampoules at low temperature (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The most important enabling factor is the high activity and volatility of chloride precursors, which allows cluster assembly and crystal transport at only ~140–155 °C (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-2). The main limiting factor is Os-specific chloride chemistry: Os redox state, volatility, and possible competing osmium chlorides/selenides are not directly demonstrated in the provided data [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the target is judged highly but not trivially synthesizable, with Route 1 clearly preferred.
