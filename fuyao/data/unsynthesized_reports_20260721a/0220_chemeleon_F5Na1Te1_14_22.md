### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic-stability score (target thermodynamic data). The nearest ATeF5 analogues KTeF5 and CsTeF5 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that alkali fluorotellurates of this stoichiometry are stable phases (CS rank 1; SS rank 1–2; TS rank 1–2). Remaining uncertainty is that the computed P2₁ NaTeF5 polymorph may not be the experimentally preferred NaTeF5 polymorph under every crystallization condition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because KTeF5 was synthesized by slow evaporation in aqueous HF with no high temperature, high pressure, or special atmosphere reported (CS rank 1; SS rank 2; TS rank 2). The main severity penalty is the use of HF and TeF4, both hazardous, and TeF4/fluoride handling can require dryness because related KTe2F9 synthesis used glovebox and dry argon (CS/SS/TS rank 3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact evaporation duration for NaTeF5, so the duration estimate is neutral rather than penalized.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because the route is solution-mediated, avoiding slow solid-state diffusion, and KTeF5 crystallizes directly from KF + TeF4 in HF (CS/SS/TS). Structural reconstruction is modest because TeF5 square pyramids are already present in KTeF5/CsTeF5 analogues, while the Na target uses NaF6 octahedra and a 2D sheet [Reasonable Inference from target structure plus CS/SS/TS analogues]. The main uncertainty is whether Na+ selects the predicted P2₁ sheet rather than another hydrate or polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is also mild because CsTeF5 and NaSb3F10 crystallize from HF solutions at room temperature/slow evaporation with no high pressure or inert atmosphere reported (SS ranks 1,4; TS ranks 1,4). The main condition penalty is HF toxicity/corrosivity, not thermal severity. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for TeO2-based NaTeF5 specifically, so CS is neutral for this route’s exact precursor transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2

TeO2 is a less direct fluorotellurate precursor than TeF4, but CsTeF5 formation from CsF + TeO2 in HF shows the oxide-to-fluorotellurate conversion is feasible (SS rank 1; TS rank 1). Sodium fluoride phase crystallization from sodium carbonate/oxide sources in 40% HF is supported by NaSb3F10 (SS rank 4; TS rank 4). Remaining uncertainty is stoichiometric phase selection, because Te-rich fluorotellurate phases are known in the K–Te–F system (CS/SS/TS rank 3).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate-to-high severity because related KTe2F9 synthesis used a sealed platinum tube, glovebox handling, dry argon, 200 °C heating, and slow cooling (CS/SS/TS rank 3). The temperature itself is low, so no high-temperature deductions apply. The penalties arise from sealed volatile TeF4 chemistry, dry controlled atmosphere, multistep handling, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors (CS/SS/TS rank 3).

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are still acceptable because KF + TeF4 reacts at only 200 °C to form crystalline KTe2F9, implying reactive fluoride chemistry and short diffusion lengths (CS/SS/TS rank 3). However, the reported sealed-tube analogue is Te-rich, so achieving NaTeF5 requires stricter 1:1 Na:Te stoichiometry and may face competing phase formation [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct sealed-tube NaTeF5 formation, so this route is scored lower than the solution routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains dissimilar cation environments, NaF6 octahedra and TeF5 square pyramids, which triggers a modest amorphization/ordering risk via cation-size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target structure reports corner- and edge-sharing but no face-sharing, so the most severe mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues generally form well-defined single crystals by slow evaporation or controlled cooling, which argues against strong glass-forming tendency in this chemical family (CS/SS/TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaTeF5 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close KTeF5/CsTeF5 analogues are also hull-stable (CS/SS/TS). The analogue support is strong: ATeF5 phases with TeF5 square pyramids are experimentally accessible by HF solution routes for K and Cs, while sodium fluoride frameworks crystallize from 40% HF at room temperature in the Na–Sb–F system (CS/SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is the highly effective HF fluorination/crystallization medium, which can form Te(IV) fluoride polyhedra under mild conditions (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because Te-rich fluorotellurates such as KTe2F9 form under TeF4-rich sealed-tube conditions (CS/SS/TS rank 3). Overall, the target is highly synthesizable, with solution slow evaporation being the most credible first attempt.
