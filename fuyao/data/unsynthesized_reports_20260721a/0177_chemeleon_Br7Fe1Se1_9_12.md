### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum stability score under the provided rubric (target data). Close chemical analogues Ga2Se2Br14 and Al2Se2Br14 are also low above hull, 0.0237 and 0.0173 eV/atom, respectively, supporting thermodynamic plausibility for MBr4SeBr3-type compounds (CS-1, CS-2). TS analogues include multiple E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom cluster halides, which reinforces that halide cluster salts can be true ground states (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT stability may not fully capture halogen volatility, moisture sensitivity, or redox disproportionation during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is low-temperature, since the closest Al analogue is synthesized at 150 °C in a 150 → 120 °C gradient (CS-2, SS-4). The severity deductions come mainly from controlled dry atmosphere, sealed volatile halides, and air/moisture-sensitive precursors, all of which are explicitly indicated for AlBr4SeBr3 and related halide CVT systems (CS-2, SS-1, SS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS for Fe-specific severity is treated as neutral, but TS supports similar moderate-temperature ampoule handling for halide clusters (TS-4, TS-5). The route is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because SeBr4 + AlBr3 yields SeBr3[AlBr4] quantitatively at 150 °C, implying highly reactive halide precursors and vapor/solid transport rather than sluggish cation diffusion (CS-2, SS-4). The target’s FeBr4 + SeBr3 cluster separation is nearly isostructural to Al/Ga MBr4 + SeBr3 analogues, supporting low structural reconstruction penalty (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-4). The main uncertainty is whether FeBr3 stabilizes the same isolated FeBr4 unit under these bromine-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct Fe evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because analogous element/halogen ampoule routes operate at 300–550 °C and sealed-ampoule pressure rather than GPa conditions (TS-1, TS-3). It is penalized for vacuum/Ar ampoule control, volatile Br2, toxic/corrosive halogen handling, and a staged heating program (TS-1, TS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and SS provide insufficient direct support for FeSeBr7 formation from elements, so direct-route feasibility is less certain and treated more conservatively. The route is challenging mainly because bromination and cluster assembly must occur in one pot [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental routes can form halide cluster compounds, as shown by Ga/In–Te–I and Re–Te–Br analogues from elements in sealed ampoules (TS-1, TS-3). However, Fe metal is less kinetically active than preformed FeBr3, and the reaction must first form volatile or molten Fe/Se bromides before crystallizing the target [Reasonable Inference]. Bromine-rich sealed conditions can assist transport and reduce solid-state diffusion barriers, but no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates Fe–Se–Br direct synthesis. This gives moderate kinetics, lower than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 operates at mild temperatures around 180 °C, matching Pd–Se–Br solvothermal and hydrothermal analogues (CS-4, CS-5). It still requires sealed ampoules/autoclaves, Br2 or strong HBr, and toxic halogenated media such as CCl4, giving meaningful severity penalties (CS-4, CS-5). Unlike Route 1, it may not require vacuum as the main controlled atmosphere if N2/Ar is used, so its formal F2 score is higher under the rubric (CS-4). Its practical difficulty remains high because sealed Br2/solvent systems are hazardous [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Se–Br cluster formation in solution/solvothermal media is directly supported by Pd(SeBr3)2 synthesis in Br2/CCl4 at 180 °C (CS-4, SS-5). Kinetically, a solvated/halogenated medium should accelerate transport relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS-4, CS-5]. However, the Pd analogue often gives multiphase mixtures requiring manual crystal separation, and its bonding motif is not as close to the target’s separated FeBr4 + SeBr3 clusters (CS-4, SS-5). This route is kinetically plausible but less phase-selective.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a zero-dimensional molecular cluster solid with isolated FeBr4 and SeBr3 clusters, not a large connected framework, reducing the likelihood of glassy network frustration (target data). The formula unit has only 9 atoms and the described P1 structure has one FeBr4 and one SeBr3 cluster, so the >40 atom criterion is not triggered (target data). Mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing is absent in the target description, unlike some W/Ta–Te–Br octahedral analogues with face-sharing units (SS-1, TS-4, TS-5). Ionic-radius mismatch is not a strong criterion here because Fe and Se occupy separate molecular clusters rather than competing for the same cation sublattice [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct CS/SS/TS radius evidence].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (target data; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from the AlBr4SeBr3 and GaBr4SeBr3 analogues, which share the same separated MBr4 + SeBr3 cluster motif and are reported under mild halide ampoule conditions (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-4). The critical enabling factor is the use of reactive, volatile bromide precursors such as SeBr4 and FeBr3 in a sealed, dry, low-temperature gradient, which should allow cluster assembly without high-temperature diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS-2 and SS-4]. The critical limiting factor is Fe-specific redox/coordination chemistry, because no provided CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates FeSeBr7 or FeBr4SeBr3 synthesis. Moisture sensitivity and bromine/selenium halide volatility are also practical limiting factors, as close analogues require closed ampoules and moisture-sensitive handling (CS-2, SS-4).
