### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives 98/100 under the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues Hg₂CdSeO₆, Hg₂CdSO₆, β-Hg₂SeO₃, and HgHO₄Cl are also stable or nearly stable, showing that Hg oxoanion phases are well represented near the convex hull (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar Hg₂SO₄ and Pb₃SeO₅ are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, giving supporting chemical evidence (CS1, CS3). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a broad aqueous phase field for the specific basic selenate Hg₃SeO₆ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

The 230–260 °C hydrothermal window is strongly supported by Hg₂CdSeO₆ and Hg₂CdSO₆ syntheses at 250 °C for 5 d in Teflon-lined autoclaves (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The target’s Hg–O and Se–O bond lengths closely match those of Hg₂CdSeO₆, making structural transferability unusually strong (SS1, TS1). Kinetically, aqueous hydrothermal transport should overcome the slow diffusion typical of dense oxides [Reasonable Inference], and the close analogues crystallized as single crystals (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is phase competition with other mercury selenates/selenites, since related Hg–Se–O phases exist and can crystallize readily (SS3, TS3, TS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 cooling crystallization

The mildness of cooling crystallization is supported by HgHO₄Cl, which crystallized after dissolving red HgO in 10% HClO₃ with heating and cooling (TS5). HgO/oxyacid chemistry is also supported by hydrothermal Hg₂SO₄ formation from HgO and sulfuric acid (CS1). The route should be kinetically favorable because the reaction proceeds through solution species [Reasonable Inference], but there is insufficient evidence from SS for direct formation of the target topology by ambient cooling crystallization, so structural reconstruction is less certain. The biggest uncertainty is whether a basic anhydrous Hg₃SeO₆ phase crystallizes instead of hydrated/neutral HgSeO₄ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 sealed-tube/flux

Elevated-temperature flux or sealed transport is chemically plausible because Pb₂TeO₅ was flux-grown at 750 °C (CS2), Pb₂SnO₄ was sintered at 700 °C (CS5), and HgAsO₄-related crystals were grown by HgCl₂ vapor transport at 650→550 °C (SS5). However, high-temperature Hg chemistry is more difficult because volatile/toxic mercury species require sealed systems [Reasonable Inference from SS5]. Kinetics can be improved by flux or transport, but no TS source directly supports high-temperature formation of Hg₃SeO₆, so TS is neutral, 50, for direct route transferability. This route is therefore viable mainly as a backup crystal-growth approach after hydrothermal phase identification.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly different cation environments: linear Hg–O, HgO₅ trigonal bipyramids, and SeO₄ tetrahedra, which indicates substantial local structural complexity from the target description. Mixed corner-sharing SeO₄–HgO₅ and edge-sharing HgO₅–HgO₅ connectivity triggers a medium amorphization/poor-crystallinity risk. The closest analogue Hg₂CdSeO₆ crystallizes successfully despite similarly mixed polyhedral motifs, which reduces concern for the hydrothermal route (SS1, TS1). The unit-cell atom count is not provided directly; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for that check, treated neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg₃SeO₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from Hg₂CdSeO₆, which is both structurally and thermodynamically close and was made hydrothermally as single crystals under accessible conditions (SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the excellent match between target Hg–O/Se–O coordination chemistry and the hydrothermal CdXO₄·2HgO family (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in Hg–Se–O aqueous chemistry, because Hg selenite and other mercury selenium oxo phases are known and can form under mild or hydrothermal conditions (SS3, TS3, TS4). Overall, hydrothermal synthesis from HgO and a Se(VI) source is highly plausible, while solution cooling and sealed flux/transport are secondary routes.
