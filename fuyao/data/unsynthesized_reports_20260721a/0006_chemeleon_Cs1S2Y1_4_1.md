### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamically similar phases are also stable or nearly stable: CsErS₂, CsTmS₂ H-phase, RbSmS₂, RbTbS₂, and CsLuS₂ H-phase all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in TS, while CsTbS₂ is only ~0.0018 eV/atom above hull in SS (TS1–TS5; SS1). CS also supports stable related RbLnS₂ Caswellsilverite phases with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee the Caswellsilverite polymorph will be the exclusive experimental product, because SS/TS note multiple CsLnS₂ modifications and narrow phase windows (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS2, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses 1000 °C and flowing H₂S/Ar, matching CsLnS₂ and RbLnS₂ synthesis conditions (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). It avoids high pressure and sealed volatile sulfur, which reduces severity relative to ampoule routes (SS1–SS5). The main deductions are controlled/reactive atmosphere, multi-step gas handling/workup, and H₂S toxicity [Reasonable Inference based on H₂S identity and its use in CS/SS/TS]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact CsYS₂ gas-flow tolerance, so the score remains moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from strong structural transferability because the target and CsTbS₂ have matching Caswellsilverite R-3m topology and nearly identical bond distances (target; SS1). The main kinetic limitation is the use of Y₂O₃, which is likely refractory and slower to sulfurize than preformed sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. The 80:1 alkali carbonate excess reported for analogues likely improves transport through flux-assisted reaction [Reasonable Inference from CS1–CS5, SS2–SS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Y₂O₃ converts as readily as lanthanide oxides under the same H₂S conditions; CS/SS/TS provide rare-earth oxide evidence but not direct Y₂O₃ evidence.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses similar hazards as Route 1 but may require 1025 °C and deliberate slow cooling, based on RbLaS₂ conditions (CS4). It is therefore scored slightly lower in F2 than Route 1 because T >1000 °C can apply, and flux recovery/washing adds procedural steps (CS4, SS2–SS5). The large carbonate excess is well precedented in RbLnS₂ and CsLnS₂-related syntheses (CS1–CS5, SS2–SS5, TS1–TS5). The uncertainty is whether a Cs₂CO₃-rich melt gives the same crystal-growth behavior as Rb₂CO₃-rich mixtures; that substitution is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because the 80:1 alkali carbonate excess supports enhanced transport and single-crystal formation in RbLnS₂ analogues (CS1–CS3, CS5, TS3–TS4). The target structure is strongly supported by same-family CsLnS₂ analogues (SS1–SS2, SS4–SS5). The kinetic penalty is the refractory Y₂O₃ precursor [Reasonable Inference] and possible phase competition between Caswellsilverite and H-phase CsLnS₂-like structures noted in SS/TS (SS3, TS2, TS5). Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for CsYS₂ single-crystal flux growth specifically.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids H₂S flow but introduces sealed-ampoule sulfur vapor and air-sensitive sulfides, so it remains challenging [Reasonable Inference]. Closed ampoules are supported for related chalcogenides but not directly for CsLnS₂ sulfides, where H₂S flow is the reported method (SS2–SS5, TS1–TS2). The score reflects deductions for controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur in a sealed system, multi-step precursor/ampoule handling, and air-sensitive sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports binary sulfide precursors for the target, so the route is lower confidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary sulfides should reduce the chemical reconstruction relative to oxide sulfidation because the anion sublattice is already sulfide [Reasonable Inference]. However, solid-state diffusion without carbonate flux may be slower, and no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates CsYS₂ or CsLnS₂ formation from Cs₂S/Y₂S₃. Structural reconstruction is still plausible because the final Caswellsilverite framework is extensively represented among CsLnS₂/RbLnS₂ analogues (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS2, SS4–SS5, TS1, TS3–TS4). The uncertainty is high for sulfur pressure, Cs volatility, and ampoule compatibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a simple crystalline layered sulfide with edge/corner-sharing octahedra and no reported face-sharing in the target Caswellsilverite description, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization penalty is not fully triggered (target). The large Cs⁺/Y³⁺ size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference], but many analogous alkali–rare-earth sulfides crystallize successfully in ordered R-3m structures (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS2, SS4–SS5). Unit-cell size appears small from the CsYS₂ formula and simple R-3m description, with no evidence for >40 atoms in the target description. Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly measure amorphization tendency; insufficient direct amorphization evidence from CS and TS is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsYS₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar alkali rare-earth sulfides are also on or near the hull (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong: CsTbS₂ is almost a structural match to the target, including Cs–S = 3.49 Å and M–S = 2.79 Å, and it is synthesized by carbonate/oxide sulfidation under H₂S at 1000 °C (target; SS1). The most critical enabling factor is the robust CsLnS₂/RbLnS₂ Caswellsilverite family prepared across multiple lanthanides under similar conditions (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because SS/TS note narrow temperature windows and multiple CsLnS₂ modifications, including H-phase competitors (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS2, TS5). Overall, the target is quite synthesizeable, but best attempted first by H₂S sulfidation of Cs₂CO₃/Y₂O₃ rather than by less-proven sealed sulfide routes.
