### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target KRhS₂ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the provided thermodynamic scale. TS analogues are also mostly E_hull = 0, including K–Cr–S and K–Pt–S sulfides, supporting the plausibility of stable alkali transition-metal sulfides (TS). CS includes one exact-structure KYS₂ analogue at E_hull = 0 and another Caswellsilverite KSnS₂ analogue at modest metastability, giving supportive but not exact evidence (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Rh sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: H₂S sulfidation

Route 1 has significant condition severity because KYS₂ uses 1323 K in H₂S and K–Cr–S analogues use dry H₂S at high temperature (CS, SS, TS). Its kinetics are favorable because KYS₂ is the same Caswellsilverite/R-3m KMS₂ type, so the structural reconstruction burden is low (CS). The main uncertainty is Rh source reactivity, since no Rh-specific H₂S sulfidation analogue is provided; SS/TS provide K–Cr–S rather than K–Rh–S evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: sealed ampoule

Route 2 has moderate condition severity because evacuated quartz-tube alkali chalcogenide synthesis at ~800 °C is documented for KNbS₂-type materials (CS). Its kinetic score is lower because Rh is refractory and the closest sealed-tube analogue is structurally different from the target Caswellsilverite framework [Reasonable Inference; CS]. SS and TS give insufficient evidence for sealed-element KRhS₂ formation specifically, so their route-specific support is treated as neutral. The uncertainty is high for stoichiometry because K and S vapor pressures can shift the effective composition in a sealed tube [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: carbonate/sulfur flux

Route 3 has the best condition score because flux analogues form at 800–900 °C without high pressure, and no toxic H₂S is intrinsically required (CS, SS, TS). Kinetics are favorable because molten alkali carbonate/sulfur fluxes dissolve/transport transition-metal species, as seen in Rb₂Ni₃S₄ and KPt₂S₃ syntheses (CS, SS, TS). The R-3m KPt₂S₃ analogue has noble-metal sulfide octahedra with Pt–S distances close to target Rh–S, supporting structural transfer despite different stoichiometry (SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes KRhS₂ rather than RhS₂, Rh₂S₃, K-rich sulfides, or metallic Rh [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT

Route 4 is severe because the Caswellsilverite KSnS₂ CVT precedent uses a sealed silica tube, iodine transport agent, 1050/900 °C gradient, and 15 days (CS). It may be useful for crystal growth because the CS analogue has the same structure type, but the given CVT precursor snippet is compositionally inconsistent, so the transfer to KRhS₂ is uncertain (CS). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for iodine-mediated transport of KRhS₂, so their route-specific support is neutral. The limiting kinetic factor is composition control under a halogen transport gradient [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small R-3m Caswellsilverite-type phase with octahedral K/Rh/S coordination, and CS analogues KYS₂/KSnS₂ show ordered crystalline structures rather than large disordered frameworks (CS). One amorphization trigger is counted for the large K–Rh cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but the unit cell is not large and face-sharing coexistence is not reported for the target or the closest CS Caswellsilverite analogues (CS). Therefore, amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high. Remaining uncertainty is that rapid quenching from flux could trap glassy K–S-rich residues rather than crystalline KRhS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KRhS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and several TS analogues are also on the convex hull (TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS Caswellsilverite KYS₂/KSnS₂ analogues and from SS/TS flux-grown R-3m noble-metal sulfides such as KPt₂S₃ (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of alkali carbonate/sulfur flux chemistry, which can lower diffusion barriers for refractory transition metals at 800–900 °C (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control in the K–Rh–S system, especially because no exact KRhS₂ synthesis precedent is provided and Rh may form competing sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with flux growth or H₂S sulfidation as the most credible experimental routes.
