### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bracket. The strongest supporting analogues are also on or very near the convex hull: Li₂SnSe₃ has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS1/SS1/TS1, and Na₄SnSe₄ has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS2/SS4/TS2. Remaining uncertainty is computational: the target may still compete experimentally with nearby Li–Sn–Se phases such as Li₂SnSe₃, which is chemically and structurally similar (CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 650 °C, and standard sealed-ampoule/inert solid-state conditions, directly supported by Li₂SnSe₃ synthesis (CS1/SS1/TS1). Deductions are mainly from controlled atmosphere/high-vacuum and air-sensitive selenide handling, both implied by glovebox/Schlenk/ampoule use in CS1/SS1/TS1. There is no evidence for high pressure or >1000 °C processing, so those penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the nearest Li–Sn–Se analogue forms by direct reaction in only 12 h at 650 °C (CS1/SS1/TS1). The target and analogue both use LiSe₄ and SnSe₄ tetrahedral chemistry, so structural reconstruction is expected to be modest rather than complete (SS1; target description). The main uncertainty is whether Li₄SnSe₄ is accessed before or instead of Li₂SnSe₃ under similar conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The Na₄SnSe₄-type protocol is mild in temperature, using 850 K ≈ 577 °C, but it requires vacuum sealing, stepwise heating, and slow cooling over 3 days (CS2/SS4/TS2). Deductions arise from controlled vacuum, sealed volatile Se chemistry, multi-step precursor/thermal processing, and air-sensitive alkali selenide handling [Reasonable Inference from CS2/SS4/TS2]. There is no high-pressure or very-high-temperature penalty.

#### F3 — Route 2

The A₄SnSe₄ stoichiometry provides strong chemical guidance, because Na₄SnSe₄ is an exact alkali selenostannate analogue (CS2/SS4/TS2). However, Na is 5-coordinate while target Li is tetrahedral, so the structural transfer is incomplete and likely requires different packing/reconstruction (CS2/SS4/TS2; target description). Long holds and slow cooling imply moderate kinetics rather than rapid equilibration.

#### F2 — Route 3

Elemental routes are experimentally supported for Na–Sn–Se analogues in evacuated ampoules at 973–1173 K (CS3/SS2/SS3). For Li, the route remains plausible but more hazardous because Li metal is reactive and Se is volatile/toxic [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score remains moderate because no high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required.

#### F3 — Route 3

Direct elemental reaction benefits from high chemical driving force, as shown by Na–Sn–Se compounds formed from stoichiometric elements (CS3/SS2/SS3). However, starting from elements requires complete structural assembly and increases risk of local inhomogeneity or exothermic side reactions [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore kinetically less attractive than starting from Li₂Se.

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux growth operates at relatively low temperature, with TS5 using 773 K for 6 days. Severity deductions come from Ar/vacuum sealing, Se-rich volatile flux, multiple handling/thermal/separation steps, and air-sensitive alkali selenide chemistry (TS5). It is not high-pressure or ultra-high-temperature synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux conditions can strongly improve ion mobility and crystal growth, as demonstrated by alkali selenostannate crystal formation in molten polychalcogenide flux (TS5). The kinetic score is high because diffusion occurs in a liquid/flux environment rather than through dense solids [Reasonable Inference from TS5]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS5 explicitly notes an unexpected product, indicating flux chemistry can access off-stoichiometric or competing selenostannates.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has significant cation size/charge contrast between Li and Sn, which can increase local structural disorder tendency [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target is described as an ordered tetrahedral crystalline framework with LiSe₄ and SnSe₄ units, and similar crystalline tetrahedral selenostannates are well represented in CS/SS/TS, especially CS1/SS1/TS1. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has >40 atoms per unit cell or face-sharing connectivity, so only one amorphization trigger is applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₄SnSe₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close Li–Sn–Se and A₄SnSe₄ analogues are also on the hull (CS1/SS1/TS1; CS2/SS4/TS2). The synthesis evidence is strong: the closest chemical/structural/thermodynamic match Li₂SnSe₃ is directly synthesized from Li₂Se, Sn, and Se under standard sealed/inert solid-state conditions (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the robust tetrahedral Li–Se/Sn–Se chemistry, since both target and closest analogue contain LiSe₄ and SnSe₄ units (SS1; target description). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity within the Li–Sn–Se system, especially competition with Li₂SnSe₃ or other selenostannates [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1/TS1]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible in a competent solid-state chalcogenide lab with rigorous air/moisture exclusion.
