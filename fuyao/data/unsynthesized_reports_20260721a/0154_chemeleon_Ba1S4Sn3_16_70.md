### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin. TS provides strong corroboration because the closest thermodynamic analogues include SnS, BaSnS₂, Tl₂Sn₂S₃, GeS, and BaAgSbS₃ at or near the convex hull, with several exactly 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). SS also supports stability via structurally similar SnS and BaSnS₂ phases, especially BaSnS₂ with E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, SS2). CS is chemically less direct due to Pd-containing analogues, but multiple CS entries are stable sulfides at E_hull = 0.0, so CS is supportive rather than neutral (CS1–CS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state

Route 1 is supported most directly by BaSnS₂, which is synthesized from BaS + SnS at 750 °C for two weeks (SS2/TS2). Its severity score is reduced by the likely need for sealed evacuated conditions and sulfur volatility control, as many sulfide analogues use evacuated sealed tubes or controlled atmospheres (SS1/TS1, CS2–CS5). Kinetically, binary sulfides are more reactive than oxides [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s triclinic P1 cell and multiple inequivalent Sn/S sites imply substantial ordering and possible slow equilibration. Remaining uncertainty is whether BaSn₃S₄ competes strongly with BaSnS₂ + SnS under direct firing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Flux growth

Route 2 is enabled by BaPd₂S₄ flux growth in KSCN/H₂S and by Sn chalcogenide flux/sealed-capsule precedents (CS1, SS1/TS1). The route receives severity penalties for toxic H₂S or thiocyanate decomposition products, controlled atmosphere, flux handling, and possible sulfur volatility. Kinetics are favorable because flux growth reduces diffusion constraints and can help crystallize low-symmetry frameworks [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is identifying a flux that dissolves BaS/SnS sufficiently without incorporating alkali/halide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT

CVT is supported for SnS and GeS analogues, using chlorine or halogen transport in quartz tubes at moderate-to-high temperatures (SS3, SS5, TS4). Severity is moderate because volatile/corrosive halogens and sealed evacuated ampoules are required. Kinetics can be good once volatile transport species form, but Ba transport is much less certain than Sn/Ge transport [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, Route 3 is best viewed as a crystal-growth route after powder synthesis, not the primary discovery route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Hydrazine-hydrothermal

Hydrazine-hydrothermal synthesis is low temperature and experimentally simple, as shown by BaAgSbS₃ synthesis at 160 °C for 7 days (TS5). Its high F2 score reflects low temperature and lack of high-pressure conditions, though hydrazine toxicity is a significant deduction. The F3 score is limited because no provided source demonstrates Ba–Sn–S solvothermal formation, and SnS solubility/transport in hydrazine is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. This route is attractive for exploratory synthesis but less reliable than flux or direct solid-state routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Ba/Sn cation size contrast, which triggers the ionic-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]; the target’s Ba–S and Sn–S distances are very different, consistent with this mismatch. The atom count criterion is not triggered because the described P1 cell contains 16 atoms, below 40. The connectivity criterion is also not triggered because the target and close BaSnS₂ analogue show corner/edge sharing but no face-sharing (SS2/TS2). The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSn₃S₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues in the Ba/Sn/S and Sn/S chemical neighborhood are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from BaSnS₂, which uses the same BaS–SnS precursor chemistry and similar Ba–S/Sn–S bonding (SS2/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive binary sulfide precursors and flux/transport methods that can manage sulfur activity (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against BaSnS₂, SnS, and other Ba–Sn–S compositions, especially because the target is a low-symmetry P1 compound with many inequivalent sites [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is synthetically plausible, with flux-assisted sulfide synthesis recommended as the most practical route for ordered crystalline material.
