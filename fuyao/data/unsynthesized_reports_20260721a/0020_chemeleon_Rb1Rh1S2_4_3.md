### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it scores at the top of the requested thermodynamic scale. Chemically similar Rb/Cs transition-metal sulfides are frequently on or very near the hull, including Rb₂Pd₃S₄ at 0.0 eV/atom and Cs₂Ni₃S₄-related phases at 0.0–0.0057 eV/atom (CS1, CS4, CS5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar entries are also dominated by E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom phases (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry is exactly RbRhS₂, so Rh-specific competing sulfides are not directly assessed by the reference set.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses temperatures of 750–900 °C under dry oxygen-free Ar/N₂, directly matching CS1/SS1/TS1 and SS5/TS5. No high pressure, no >1000 °C treatment, and no complex multi-step process are required by the analogue evidence. The main unscored practical risk is sulfur volatility, but CS1/SS1/TS1 do not require a sealed system for the base Ar/N₂ synthesis. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for any need for extreme pressure or ultrahigh temperature, so those conditions are scored as not applying.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The carbonate + Rh metal + sulfur mixture is expected to be kinetically slower than solution-derived or highly reactive precursors because Rh is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. However, close Rb/Cs transition-metal sulfide analogues form at 750–900 °C, implying that sulfide/alkali melt chemistry can assist transport (CS1, CS5, SS2/TS2). Structural reconstruction is the weakest kinetic factor because listed Rb₂Pd₃S₄ and Rb₂Ni₃S₄ analogues contain square-planar Pd/Ni sulfide units, whereas the target has edge-sharing RhS₆ octahedra (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The route is feasible but may need longer annealing or regrinding [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from the explicitly listed alkali sulfide option in CS1/SS1/TS1, but it introduces sealed-tube and air-sensitive handling burdens. Evacuated sealed tubes and glovebox handling are used for related alkali chalcogenides (SS3/SS4/TS3/TS4), supporting the plausibility but increasing severity. Sulfur volatility and autogenous pressure are additional practical risks [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure or >1000 °C requirements, so those are not penalized.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Using Rb₂S should increase precursor activity by supplying preformed sulfide chemistry, and alkali sulfides are specifically allowed in the thiopalladate route (CS1/SS1/TS1). Sealed sulfur-rich conditions may improve diffusion through molten sulfur/alkali sulfide phases [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction penalty remains because no listed structural analogue has the same P-3m1 RhS₆ octahedral network. This route is kinetically promising but experimentally less forgiving.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is directly supported by the dry H₂S-stream variant in CS1/SS1/TS1 and the hydrogen/chalcogen stream option in CS5/SS2/TS2. It avoids sealed ampoules and high pressure but requires a reactive/toxic gas stream, giving a significant severity penalty. Temperatures remain moderate at 750–850 °C based on CS1/SS1/TS1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no need for multi-step processing or extreme temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A sulfurizing gas should provide high sulfur chemical potential and may sulfidize Rh metal more uniformly than solid sulfur [Reasonable Inference]. The existence of H₂S/chalcogen-gas routes for Pd/Ni/Pt chalcogenides supports kinetic viability (CS1, CS5, SS2/TS2). The limiting kinetic issue remains ordering into the target RhS₆ octahedral P-3m1 structure, which is not directly represented by the closest square-planar Ni/Pd analogues (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). Thus, this route is chemically active but may produce competing Rh sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 uses 800–850 °C Ar flux/fusion conditions that are directly reported for related A₂M₃X₄ chalcogenides (CS5, SS2/TS2). Cs₂Ni₃S₄ is synthesized at 800 °C for 3 h under Ar with excess alkali/sulfur content, supporting manageable condition severity (CS4). No high pressure, vacuum, H₂/NH₃, or >1000 °C treatment is required by the evidence. Sulfur volatility remains a practical consideration, but analogous flux/fusion routes do not mandate severe conditions.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

The flux/fusion route receives the highest kinetic score because excess alkali carbonate/chalcogen can act as a transport medium [Reasonable Inference], and related Rb/Cs transition-metal sulfides are explicitly made by fusion/flux routes at 800–850 °C (CS5, SS2/TS2). This partially offsets the refractory nature of Rh metal [Reasonable Inference]. Slow structural ordering remains possible because the target’s RhS₆ octahedral network differs from the square-planar Pd/Ni sulfide analogues (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). Overall, flux chemistry is the best way to improve mass transport and crystal growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple RbRhS₂ formula and P-3m1 structural description with only one Rb, one Rh, and one S site, so there is no evidence for a large >40-atom cell. The Rh sublattice is described as edge-sharing RhS₆ octahedra, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence is reported. The one amorphization trigger is the large size and bonding-environment contrast between Rb and Rh, reflected by Rb–S = 3.34 Å versus Rh–S = 2.45 Å in the target description. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbRhS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar alkali transition-metal chalcogenides are also on the hull (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The analogue support for synthesis is strong in terms of composition class, since Rb/Cs transition-metal sulfides are repeatedly prepared by carbonate/metal/sulfur fusion or sintering under dry Ar/N₂ (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS2/TS2, SS5/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of alkali carbonate/sulfur flux chemistry at 800–850 °C, which should improve diffusion and crystallization (CS4, CS5, SS2/TS2). The most critical limiting factor is the lack of a Rh-specific analogue and the structural mismatch between RhS₆ octahedra in the target and square-planar Ni/Pd sulfide units in the closest analogues (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). Phase competition and moisture sensitivity should be expected based on related alkali sulfides (CS3, CS5, SS2/TS2).

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile sulfur loss at 750–900 °C | Medium | Use sulfur excess or sealed/covered reaction geometry [Reasonable Inference]; excess chalcogen is used in flux/fusion A₂M₃X₄ routes (CS5, SS2/TS2). |
| 2 | All | Competing Rb–Rh–S or binary Rh sulfide phases | High | Use narrow stoichiometry screening and flux composition variation [Reasonable Inference]; analogous systems show phase-control difficulty and multiple modifications for Rb₂Pd₃S₄/K₂Pd₃S₄ (CS1/SS1/TS1). |
| 3 | All | Slow reaction of refractory Rh metal | Medium | Use fine Rh powder, flux/fusion medium, or H₂S sulfurization [Reasonable Inference]; fusion/flux routes help related A₂M₃X₄ phases form at 800–850 °C (CS5, SS2/TS2). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Incomplete conversion in dry solid-state mixture | Medium | Regrind/anneal or switch to flux route [Reasonable Inference]; direct solid-state analogues use 750–900 °C dry Ar/N₂ (CS1/SS1/TS1). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Air-sensitive Rb₂S handling and sealed-tube pressure | High | Load in glovebox and use evacuated or Ar-backfilled carbon-coated tubes [Reasonable Inference]; glovebox and sealed carbon-coated tubes are used for related alkali chalcogenides (SS3/SS4/TS3/TS4). |
| 6 | Route 3 | Toxic/reactive H₂S or H₂/S gas handling | High | Use dry gas manifold, scrubbing, leak checking, and inert cooldown [Reasonable Inference]; H₂S and hydrogen/chalcogen streams are reported as viable alternatives in analogues (CS1, CS5, SS2/TS2). |
| 7 | Route 4 | Residual alkali carbonate/sulfide flux and product moisture sensitivity | Medium | Wash quickly and dry under vacuum or inert gas [Reasonable Inference]; washing/drying and argon storage are reported for related Cs–Co/Ni sulfides (CS3, CS4, CS5). |
| 8 | All | Crystal growth difficulty / small crystallites | Medium | Use slow cooling from flux/fusion melt [Reasonable Inference]; suitable single crystals and flux/fusion methods are discussed for related A₂M₃X₄ phases (CS5, SS2/TS2), while single-crystal challenges are noted in CS1 and CS5. |
