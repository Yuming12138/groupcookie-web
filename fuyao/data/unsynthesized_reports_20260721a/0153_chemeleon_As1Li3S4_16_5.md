### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bracket. The direct chemical analogue Li3AsS3 is also on the hull (CS-1), and several structurally/thermodynamically similar Li sulfides are on the hull as well (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a large experimental synthesis window, especially in volatile Li–As–S chemistry [Reasonable Inference from phase-control issues in CS-1 and SS-1/TS-1].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because direct and structural analogues form at 200–800 °C in sealed ampoules (CS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-2, TS-5). The score is reduced because Ar/vacuum sealing, volatile S/As-containing reagents, air-sensitive Li2S, and multiple heating/annealing steps are required (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2). CS gives strong positive route evidence through Li3AsS3; SS/TS support the sealed-tube Li sulfide practice but are less chemically direct.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable relative to oxide solid-state synthesis because Li2S, As2S3, and S are chemically reactive sulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1 and SS-2]. Similar Li sulfides form at moderate temperatures, but long annealing times of 96 h to weeks indicate cation ordering and phase equilibration may be slow (CS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-2, TS-5). The tetrahedral framework similarity improves the reconstruction score because closest analogues are corner-sharing LiS4/MS4 sulfide networks (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 also avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions because polysulfide flux analogues are synthesized at 525–750 °C (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3). Deductions arise from sealed vacuum/inert conditions, sulfur-rich volatile flux, toxic arsenic precursors, and flux preparation/removal steps (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS for lithium polysulfide flux specifically in Li3AsS4 is treated as neutral 50 for that subfactor, though LiI flux in Li3AsS3 gives partial support (CS-1).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives a high kinetic score because molten/polysulfide flux improves ion transport and lowers effective diffusion barriers, as demonstrated for Li2CdGeS4 and Li2CdSnS4 (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3). However, those same sources report high phase-control sensitivity to flux ratio and binary impurity formation, which lowers the structural/crystallization component (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3). The route is kinetically attractive but experimentally less forgiving than a stoichiometric sealed-ampoule synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest nominal temperature because CS-2 demonstrates thioarsenate solution formation and precipitation near low temperature to 120 °C. The severity score is still reduced due to ammonia/sulfide atmosphere, toxic arsenic/sulfide reagents, and multi-step isolation/drying/annealing (CS-2). Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for direct solution synthesis of structurally similar Li3AsS4 is treated as neutral 50 for transferability and crystallization confidence.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The solution route has excellent molecular-level mixing and high precursor activity because thioarsenate species can be generated in solution (CS-2). The main kinetic uncertainty is crystallization: CS-2 supports thioarsenate salts, but not the enargite Pmn2_1 Li3AsS4 framework, and related salts can be hygroscopic or phase-mixed (CS-2). Therefore, the route may quickly form a Li–As–S precipitate but may require careful annealing to crystallize the target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a cation-size/chemistry contrast between Li and As, which triggers C1, but its framework is built from simple corner-sharing tetrahedra similar to multiple crystalline analogues (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3). No evidence indicates a very large unit cell or mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity for the target-like framework, and closest analogues are crystalline rather than glass-forming under reported conditions (SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that solution precipitation could yield amorphous lithium thioarsenate even if the crystalline phase is stable [Reasonable Inference from CS-2].
