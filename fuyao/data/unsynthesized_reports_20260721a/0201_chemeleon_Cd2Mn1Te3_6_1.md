### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Multiple chemically and thermodynamically similar tellurides also have E_hull = 0, including Cu₂ZrTe₃, Cu₂TiTe₃, Cu₅Sn₂Te₇, HgIn₂Te₄, AgInTe₂, CdIn₂Te₄, and CdGa₂Te₄ (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5; TS2, TS3, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the ordered I-4m2 cation arrangement is kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The severity score is reduced because sealed vacuum ampoules are consistently required for tellurides in the references (CS1–CS4, TS2–TS5), and Cd/Te volatility requires sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. Solid-state kinetics are moderate because related Mn–Te phases need long sealed-tube anneals and sometimes cation exchange (SS1–SS3), while Cd-containing tellurides can require prolonged equilibration or high-temperature tempering (TS3, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether Mn and Cd will order into the desired chalcopyrite-like Cd₂MnTe₃ arrangement rather than segregating into CdTe/MnTe-type binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Te-rich self-flux / Cd–Te flux

This route has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still uses vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile/toxic Cd/Te precursors (CS1–CS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Its kinetic score is higher because molten flux growth of chemically similar Cu₂MTe₃ compounds is documented at 650–900 °C with slow cooling (CS1, CS2), which should improve mass transport and crystallinity [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that the CS flux analogues contain Cu/Ti/Zr/Hf rather than Cd/Mn, so Cd volatility and Mn solubility in Te-rich flux remain experimental risks [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: melt growth / Bridgman

The condition score is lower because analogous melt/fusion syntheses use >1000 °C, evacuated ampoules, and controlled cooling or quenching (CS3, TS2). Kinetics are favorable because fusion/Bridgman methods promote liquid-state mixing and have produced tetrahedral tellurides such as Cu₂SnTe₃ and chalcopyrite AgInTe₂ (CS3, TS2). The uncertainty is whether Cd₂MnTe₃ melts congruently and whether Cd/Te vapor pressure can be safely controlled at 1000–1100 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: high-pressure conversion

High-pressure telluride phase conversion is supported by CdIn₂Te₄/HgIn₂Te₄ pressure treatment at 40–65 kbar and 400 °C (CS5), and ZnTe high-pressure phases require ~11.5 GPa apparatus (SS5). The route is penalized because >5 GPa pressure requires specialized equipment and because the target is already predicted to be a ground-state phase, so pressure is not clearly necessary. The uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates high-pressure synthesis of Cd–Mn–Te with this stoichiometry; therefore, transferability is mostly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s structure is a crystalline corner-sharing tetrahedral network, and analogous chalcopyrite/defect-chalcopyrite tellurides are ordered tetrahedral frameworks (TS2, TS3, TS5), which supports a low amorphization tendency. One risk trigger is the size mismatch between tetrahedral Cd²⁺ and Mn²⁺, which may promote cation disorder or strain [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom target unit cell, and the target description does not report mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCd₂Te₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from tetrahedral telluride analogues: chalcopyrite AgInTe₂ is made by elemental fusion (TS2), CdIn₂Te₄/CdGa₂Te₄ are stable Cd–Te tetrahedral compounds synthesized in sealed ampoules (TS3, TS5), and chemically similar Cu₂MTe₃ tellurides are grown by Te-containing flux methods (CS1, CS2). The most important enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing tetrahedral telluride chemistry shared by the target and several analogues (target; TS2–TS5). The main limiting factor is practical phase control under sealed-ampoule conditions, especially Cd/Te volatility, Mn incorporation, and possible CdTe/MnTe competing phases [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control difficulties in CS4 and SS1–SS3]. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible, with flux growth as the most balanced route.
