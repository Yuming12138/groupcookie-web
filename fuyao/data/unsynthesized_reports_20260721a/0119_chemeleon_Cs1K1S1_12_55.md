### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided thermodynamic data. The thermodynamic-neighbor set strongly supports this: Cs₂S, CsNaS, RbNaS, and RbKS are all stable or nearly stable with E_hull values from 0.0 to ~0.008 eV/atom (TS #1, #2, #4, #5). Chemical analogues also include multiple hull-stable alkali chalcogenides (CS #1, #2, #5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee an experimentally accessible ordered Cs/K arrangement [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild-to-moderate because close analogues are synthesized at **783–870 K** under Ar, far below 1000 °C and without high pressure (CS #1–#5; SS #1; TS #4–#5). The main penalty is air/moisture sensitivity: related sulfides and products are hygroscopic or air-sensitive, requiring glovebox transfer and sealed crucibles (CS #3, #5; SS #2–#3; TS #1, #4–#5). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Cs₂S or K₂S volatility is a dominant issue at these temperatures, so that deduction is treated as neutral. The route receives a high F2 because the equipment is specialized but standard for alkali chalcogenides.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetic feasibility is strong because the closest structural analogue **RbKS** is made by direct annealing of binary sulfides and shares the target’s Pnma topology and KS₄ tetrahedral network (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). Binary alkali sulfides are more reactive and less refractory than oxides, and related phases form at 623–870 K (SS #2–#3; TS #1; CS #3). The long annealing time, especially 7 days for RbKS, indicates some cation-ordering or crystal-growth sluggishness (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). Overall, diffusion should be favorable, but phase ordering may require prolonged heat treatment [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized because direct elemental synthesis uses evacuated ampoules for related sulfides (SS #2–#3; TS #1), and Cs/K/S are reactive and volatile enough to require careful sealed-tube practice [Reasonable Inference]. Temperatures remain moderate, with binary sulfides formed at 623 K and ternary RbKS at 820 K (SS #2–#3; TS #1; CS #3/SS #1/TS #5). No CS/SS/TS source reports high pressure or >1000 °C treatment, so those deductions are not applied. The main uncertainty is whether a one-pot elemental charge yields homogeneous Cs/K ordering rather than binary sulfide segregation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Elemental alkali metals and sulfur should react readily, consistent with Cs₂S and Rb₂S formation from elements at 623 K for 48 h (SS #2–#3; TS #1). However, the ternary product requires not just sulfide formation but Cs/K distribution into the Pnma CsKS structure, so structural reconstruction is more demanding than in Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS for elemental ternary CsKS synthesis makes CS neutral for this route-specific kinetic claim. Route 2 remains plausible but less kinetically controlled than using preformed Cs₂S/K₂S.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one significant amorphization trigger: a large Cs/K cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], although alkali sulfide analogues accommodate similar large/small cation ordering in crystalline phases such as RbKS and CsNaS (CS #2–#3; SS #1; TS #2, #5). The structure is simple rather than large-cell, as the RbKS analogue has K₄Rb₄S₄ per conventional cell (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). Connectivity is edge/corner sharing, not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, based on the target description and RbKS analogue (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). The remaining uncertainty is that poor stoichiometry or moisture contamination could produce disordered or poorly crystalline sulfide byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsKS is likely a **ground-state phase** because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are hull-stable or nearly hull-stable (TS #1, #2, #4, #5). The analogue support is unusually strong: **RbKS** has the same Pnma structure type and nearly identical coordination pattern, and it is experimentally synthesized from K₂S/Rb₂S under moderate sealed-ampoule conditions (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a directly transferable binary-sulfide solid-state route using alkali sulfides in sealed Ag crucibles (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5). The most important limiting factor is the high air/moisture sensitivity of alkali sulfides and products, which demands rigorously dry handling and sealed containment (CS #3, #5; SS #2–#3; TS #1, #4–#5). A secondary limitation is the need for long annealing to establish cation ordering and single-crystal quality, as seen for RbKS over 7 days (CS #3; SS #1; TS #5).
