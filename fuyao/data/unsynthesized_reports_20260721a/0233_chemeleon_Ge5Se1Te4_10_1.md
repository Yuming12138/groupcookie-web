### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. TS analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 layered chalcogenides/tellurides, including Ge3Te6As2, Ge2Te5As2, SnSb2Te4, and GeS (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). CS and SS also include stable or near-stable close analogues, including Ge3Te6As2 and Ge2Te5As2 at E_hull = 0 and Ge4As2Te7 at ~0.003 eV/atom (CS1/CS3/CS5, SS1–SS3). The main uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Direct solid-state analogues use moderate 700 °C / 350 °C conditions but long anneals in ampoules (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3/SS5, TS1–TS3). No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is supported by CS/SS/TS. Deductions come from sealed/vacuum handling, volatile Se/Te control, multistep annealing, and toxic chalcogen handling; these are partly inferred from the target’s Se/Te composition [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Ge5Te4Se vapor pressure behavior, so the volatility deduction is conservative.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binaries such as GeTe are directly used in analogous solid-state routes, supporting reasonable precursor activity (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3/SS5, TS1–TS3). The low cation melting point of Ge supports manageable diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the analogues crystallize at 700 °C with long anneals (CS/SS/TS). Kinetics are penalized because the target’s 3-coordinate alpha-As-derived layer is not identical to the octahedral MAX-like structures common among the closest analogues (SS1–SS5). Long annealing times indicate slow ordering or phase equilibration (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS3/SS5, TS1–TS3).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Bridgman/melt analogues use evacuated ampoules, controlled gradients, and temperatures around 727–917 °C or ~1000 K, not extreme temperatures (SS4, TS4). The route requires vacuum sealing and careful slow cooling/annealing, so the condition score is limited despite moderate temperatures. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Bridgman growth of the closest Ge–As–Te analogues and is treated neutral for this route. Volatility and toxicity deductions follow from Se/Te chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Melt homogenization should improve reaction kinetics relative to solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and layered chalcogenide/telluride analogues are successfully grown by Bridgman/melt methods (SS4, TS4). Directional solidification can promote layered crystal growth and compositional homogenization if the melt is fully reacted [SS4, TS4; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Ge5Te4Se crystallizes congruently or segregates into GeTe/GeSe-rich phases, which CS/SS/TS do not directly resolve. Therefore Route 2 receives the best F3 but not a near-perfect value.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
CVT of GeS uses only 615/500 °C, supporting low thermal severity (TS5). However, an evacuated sealed tube, transport agent, and multistep pre-reaction/growth sequence are required [TS5; Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient useful evidence for CVT of close Ge–Te–As analogues and are neutral. Toxic/volatile Se/Te and iodine handling lower the score [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
TS5 confirms that layered Ge chalcogenides can be grown by vapor transport, supporting the basic kinetic pathway. The target is ternary and contains both Se and Te, so differential transport can create composition gradients [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source confirms CVT of exact Ge5Te4Se or gives a composition window, so the composition-control score is only moderate. Route 3 is best viewed as a crystal-growth follow-up rather than the first phase-discovery route [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The formal checklist gives no strong trigger: the target has only Ge as a cation, a 10-atom formula unit, and described 3-coordinate bonding rather than mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral connectivity. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Ge5Te4Se, so evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS for glass-forming tendency and they are neutral for that specific factor. A small expert penalty is still reasonable because Ge–Se–Te materials can form glasses or disordered chalcogenides under rapid cooling [Reasonable Inference]. Slow annealing is therefore important.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ge5Te4Se is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar layered chalcogenides are also stable or near-stable (TS1–TS5). The CS and SS analogues strongly support layered Ge–Te-based chemistry and ampoule synthesis, although many closest analogues are As-containing and octahedral rather than the target’s 3-coordinate monoclinic alpha-As-derived structure (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic signal combined with extensive precedent for layered chalcogenide/telluride formation in sealed ampoules and Bridgman setups (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Se/Te volatility and possible competing GeTe/GeSe or disordered chalcogenide phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with the best chance coming from sealed melt homogenization followed by long low-temperature annealing.
