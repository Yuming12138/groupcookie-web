### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom indicates predicted ground-state stability from the provided target data. This is strongly supported by Li₂SnS₃, which is essentially on the hull at E_hull ≈0.0007 eV/atom (CS-2), and by structurally/thermodynamically similar Li₂ZnSnS₄, Li₂CdSnS₄, and Li₂CdGeS₄, all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in SS/TS data (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee the orthorhombic I222 polymorph rather than another Li₄SnS₄ polymorph [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient polymorph-specific evidence from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because analogous Li–Sn–S and Li–Zn–Sn–S solid-state reactions require 700–750 °C, long annealing, Ar glovebox handling, vacuum sealing, and graphite/fused-silica containment (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1). The temperature itself is not extreme, and no high pressure is used in the analogues (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1). The main penalties come from controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur in a sealed system, multi-step heat treatment, and air-sensitive Li₂S (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The Li₂S/Sn/S route has moderate kinetics because it uses direct chemically matched precursors, as shown for Li₂SnS₃ (CS-2). However, solid-state formation of tetrahedral Li/Sn sulfide frameworks requires long holds and slow cooling in related materials, indicating nontrivial ordering and diffusion constraints (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1). The low melting points/reactivity of Li/S/Sn relative to refractory oxide systems help kinetics [Reasonable Inference], but phase competition remains likely.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The flux route uses similar or slightly lower temperatures than dry solid-state synthesis, typically 560–780 °C in related alkali-polysulfide flux syntheses (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-3). It still requires sealed-tube handling due to sulfur/polysulfide volatility and controlled atmosphere for Li₂S/polysulfides [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed evacuated ampoule sulfide syntheses (SS-5; CS-2). The route is penalized for flux preparation/separation and air-sensitive sulfide reagents.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest F3 score because molten polysulfide flux can accelerate mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and closely related Li₂CdSnS₄/Li₂CdGeS₄ were obtained from lithium polysulfide flux at 650–750 °C (SS-2, SS-3; TS-2, TS-3). The limiting kinetic/phase issue is that flux ratio is highly sensitive: small Li₂S:S changes were reported to divert products toward binary impurities in quaternary analogues (SS-2, SS-3; TS-2, TS-3). For the target ternary Li₄SnS₄, the simpler chemistry may reduce cation-ordering difficulty [Reasonable Inference], but the Li-rich composition may create a narrow sulfur chemical potential window.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The elemental ampoule route is chemically plausible because direct elemental chalcogenide synthesis is reported for Na₃InS₃ and Li₃AsS₃-type materials (SS-4; CS-1). The severity is increased by elemental Li reactivity, sulfur volatility, evacuated graphitized ampoules, and staged heating (CS-1; SS-4). Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for elemental Li + Sn + S forming Li₄SnS₄ specifically, so this route is considered more exploratory.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Elemental Li and S are highly reactive, which can help form Li₂S/Li polysulfide intermediates in situ [Reasonable Inference], and staged Li chalcogenide heating is documented for Li₃AsS₃ (CS-1). However, direct elemental synthesis requires complete structural reconstruction and may generate multiple SnSₓ/Li₂S intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. The need for staged heating and long annealing in analogous ampoule routes reduces the kinetics score (CS-1; CS-2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target consists of LiS₄ and SnS₄ tetrahedra with bond lengths comparable to known tetrahedral Li–Sn sulfides, especially Li₂ZnSnS₄ and Li₂CdSnS₄ (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). The structure has corner and edge sharing but no face-sharing connectivity in the target description, while structurally similar stannite-like analogues are largely corner-sharing tetrahedral networks (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any strong glass-forming or amorphization tendency, so F4 is high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₄SnS₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and multiple Li/Na–Sn–S and Li–M–Sn–S analogues are on or very near the hull (CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis by sealed-tube sulfide solid-state methods and by lithium polysulfide flux methods, with the best evidence coming from Li₂SnS₃ and Li₂CdSnS₄/Li₂ZnSnS₄ (CS-2; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the compatibility of LiS₄ and SnS₄ tetrahedra with known sulfide frameworks, including matching Sn–S distances near 2.42–2.43 Å (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in Li–Sn–S space, especially competition with Li₂SnS₃ and binary SnS/SnS₂-type sulfides [Reasonable Inference], consistent with impurity sensitivity in Li₂ZnSnS₄ and Li₂CdSnS₄ syntheses (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). Overall, the target is highly synthesizable, but the orthorhombic I222 polymorph may require careful sulfur chemical potential and cooling-rate control.
