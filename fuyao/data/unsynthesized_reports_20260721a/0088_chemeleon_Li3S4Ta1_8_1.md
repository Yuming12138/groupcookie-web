### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the prompt, and the closest CS/SS/TS analogues are also essentially on the convex hull, including Li₂SnSe₃, Li₅ReN₄, LiHoS₂, Li₃AuS₂, K₂TaAgS₄, KBaNbS₄, and RbBaTaS₄ (CS2–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). This strongly supports a ground-state or near-ground-state phase assignment rather than a highly metastable compound (CS/SS/TS). Remaining uncertainty is that database hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Li–Ta–S phases [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for the exact Li₃TaS₄ phase diagram topology; insufficient evidence from SS for Li-rich Ta sulfide competition; TS provides the strongest thermodynamic-neighbor signal but still not direct experimental synthesis of Li₃TaS₄.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for vacuum/inert sealed-tube handling, sulfur volatility, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive Li₂S, all of which are consistent with CS1/CS2/CS5 procedures. The temperature itself is not severe because analogous Li chalcogenides form at 650–750 °C without high pressure (CS1, CS2). The main uncertainty is whether Ta metal fully reacts at these temperatures; CS gives good Li–chalcogenide process transferability, while SS/TS give only local TaS₄ structural support and insufficient direct evidence for Li–Ta–S direct solid-state synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The elemental-Ta route has the poorest kinetics because Ta metal is refractory and may sulfidize slowly [Reasonable Inference]. CS1/CS2 show lithium chalcogenides can form by direct reaction, but neither uses Ta, so the Ta diffusion/sulfidation penalty remains. SS/TS show TaS₄ units are stable in related sulfides, but they do not prove rapid formation from Ta metal under Li-rich conditions (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). The enargite-like structural match from CS2 helps the structural-reconstruction component.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses lower temperatures around 500 °C, which is favorable and directly supported by KBaNbS₄ and RbBaTaS₄ flux syntheses (SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4). The penalties arise from sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile sulfur/polysulfide flux, multiple steps, and air-sensitive sulfide handling, all reported or implied by the flux analogue procedures (SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4). CS provides supplementary support for sealed lithium sulfide/chalcogenide handling but insufficient evidence for lithium polysulfide flux specifically in Li₃TaS₄. The remaining uncertainty is whether Li polysulfide flux stabilizes the target or instead dissolves/forms other lithium thiotantalates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux chemistry improves mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, as Nb/Ta sulfide crystals form at 500 °C from polysulfide melts (SS2, SS4, TS2, TS4). Ta still presents a refractory source issue if elemental Ta is used, so the ion-diffusion score remains only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is helped by the presence of TaS₄ tetrahedra with nearly target-identical Ta–S distances in SS/TS analogues, but the target’s small Li-only cation framework differs from large-cation K/Rb/Ba analogues (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). Phase selectivity in a Li-rich flux remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has similar sealed-tube/inert-handling penalties as Route 1 because Li₂S and sulfur-rich sulfides require dry handling and likely ampoule sealing [Reasonable Inference; CS1, CS5]. The temperature can likely remain in the 550–700 °C range, guided by CS lithium chalcogenide syntheses and lower-temperature SS/TS sulfide fluxes (CS1, CS2, SS4, TS4). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Li₂S + Ta₂S₅ metathesis route, so this is an extrapolated but chemically conservative route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 receives the best kinetic score because using Ta₂S₅ or TaS₂ should bypass the slowest step: sulfur diffusion into metallic Ta [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues demonstrate that tetrahedral TaS₄ bonding is stable, so a prereacted Ta sulfide is a plausible local-structure precursor (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether the product requires long-range Li/Ta ordering into the tetragonal enargite-like I-42m framework, which may require annealing and regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 provides the best structural analogue for enargite-like lithium chalcogenide ordering.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The amorphization risk is low–medium because the structure is described as a corner-sharing tetrahedral network, which is generally more topologically uniform than mixed edge/face-sharing networks [Reasonable Inference]. The large Li/Ta cation size and charge mismatch triggers one amorphization check, but CS2 shows Li-containing tetrahedral enargite-like chalcogenides are possible, and SS/TS show TaS₄ tetrahedra are stable (CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the target’s exact unit-cell size; based on the provided formula and structure description, the >40 atom trigger is not applied. Uncertainty remains because Li disorder or glassy lithium thiotantalate formation could compete under rapid quenching [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₃TaS₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis is supported chemically by lithium chalcogenide solid-state examples and structurally by several TaS₄-containing sulfides with Ta–S bond lengths nearly identical to the target (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong stability of tetrahedral TaS₄ units, which are repeatedly observed in structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfides (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: formation of a Li-rich ordered thiotantalate from refractory Ta or Ta sulfide sources may require careful sulfur activity, long annealing, and repeated grinding [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with the best chance coming from a prereacted Ta sulfide + Li₂S route or a sulfur-rich Li polysulfide flux route.
