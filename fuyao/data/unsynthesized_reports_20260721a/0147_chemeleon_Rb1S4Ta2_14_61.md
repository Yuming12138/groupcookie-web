### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom places it in the most favorable thermodynamic category. Thermodynamic analogues in Ta–S chemistry include several E_hull = 0 phases, such as Ta₄FeS₈, FeTa₃S₆, Ta₃PbS₆, and Ta₃SnS₆ (TS1–TS4). Structural analogues also include TaS₂-like phases that are stable or near-stable, with E_hull values near 0–0.016 eV/atom (SS1, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exact low-temperature phase selection, especially for Rb-containing compositions not directly represented in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C if the intercalation/anneal is kept at 700–850 °C, which is consistent with FeₓTaS₂ synthesis near 850 °C and Ta–Sn–S solid-state routes at 700–900 °C (SS3/TS2, TS4). The route still requires vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur handling, as TaS₂ and Ta sulfides are made in evacuated quartz tubes (SS1, SS5). The main severity deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile components, multistep TaS₂ preparation/intercalation/reanneal, and Rb air sensitivity, where the Rb-specific part is [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives only indirect support for drybox/sealed handling through sulfide routes (CS4, CS5), while direct Rb–Ta–S evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and treated as neutral 50 for exact handling details.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 benefits from using TaS₂ as a preorganized structural precursor because TaS₂ already has edge-sharing TaS₆ sheets similar to the target (SS1, SS5). Kinetics are still limited by refractory Ta and long cation diffusion distances, a [Reasonable Inference] consistent with repeated homogenization/annealing in Ta sulfide preparations (SS5) and repowdering/reheating for FeₓTaS₂ (SS3/TS2). Rb intercalation may be faster than reconstructive synthesis, but no CS/SS/TS source directly shows Rb insertion into TaS₂, so that part remains uncertain and neutral 50 for direct evidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses well-supported CVT conditions, with TaS₂ grown at 1000→800 °C and FeₓTaS₂-related crystals grown at 1000→900 °C or 950→800 °C (SS1, SS2/TS1, SS3/TS2). It requires evacuated ampoules, volatile I₂, volatile sulfur control, and multistep prereaction/transport, all of which are directly supported by the CVT analogues (SS1, SS2/TS1, SS3/TS2). The route is not penalized for high pressure or >1400 °C because none of the analogues use those conditions (SS1, TS1). Rb-specific air sensitivity and RbI formation risk are [Reasonable Inference] because direct Rb–Ta–S CVT evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT receives the best kinetic score because iodine transport demonstrably mobilizes Ta–S systems and grows TaS₂ or intercalated Ta sulfide crystals over 7–15 days (SS1, SS2/TS1, SS3/TS2). Vapor transport reduces dependence on bulk diffusion of refractory Ta, although the target still requires ordered Rb occupancy and correct TaS₂ layer stacking, which are [Reasonable Inference]. Polytype sensitivity in TaS₂ CVT shows that structural selectivity is not trivial (SS1). Exact Rb stoichiometry control is the largest uncertainty because CS/SS/TS provide no direct Rb–Ta–S CVT precedent and are neutral 50 for that specific issue.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized for T >1000 °C because the closest flux examples use 1050 °C BaCl₂ flux (CS2, CS3). It also requires sealed vacuum ampoules and dry handling, which are standard in Ba–Hf–S flux and other sulfide syntheses (CS2, CS3, CS5). Flux handling adds multistep processing, and volatile S/Rb control requires sealed systems; sulfur sealing is supported by sulfide analogues (CS2, SS5), while Rb volatility is [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is required, consistent with flux analogues (CS2, CS3).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux can enhance kinetics by providing a liquid/ionic transport medium, as demonstrated by formation of refractory Hf sulfides in BaCl₂ flux at 1050 °C (CS2, CS3). This supports a better diffusion score than direct dense-element reaction, but Ta remains refractory and the target requires ordered Rb insertion, both [Reasonable Inference]. Phase selectivity is a concern because the Ba–Hf–S flux reactions produced multiple related phases simultaneously (CS2, CS3). SS/TS analogues support the TaS₂ structural motif, but CS flux chemistry is chemically indirect for Rb–Ta–S and is neutral 50 for exact phase selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one main amorphization trigger: the large cation-size mismatch between Rb and Ta, inferred from the large difference between Rb–S and Ta–S distances in the target and comparable long large-cation sulfide contacts in Ba sulfides (CS2, CS5). The described cell contains 14 atoms, not >40, and the target description emphasizes edge-sharing TaS₆ plus S-centered corner/edge sharing rather than coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing. CS/SS/TS are structurally useful for showing that edge-sharing TaS₆ frameworks crystallize readily (SS1, SS5, TS1–TS4), but they do not directly determine the target’s amorphization risk, so they are neutral 50 for the target-specific C2/C3 checks. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb(TaS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several Ta–S thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS4). The structural evidence is strong because TaS₂ and multiple Ta-rich sulfides contain TaS₆ units with Ta–S distances closely matching the target (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a TaS₂-like layered/edge-sharing framework that can plausibly accept Rb by intercalation or vapor-assisted growth [Reasonable Inference], supported by TaS₂ and FeₓTaS₂ synthesis precedents (SS1–SS3). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Rb chemical potential and avoiding Rb loss, RbI/RbCl salt formation, or competing TaS₂/polytype phases; the Rb-specific risk is [Reasonable Inference] because direct Rb–Ta–S evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. Among the routes, iodine-assisted CVT is preferred for crystallinity and kinetics, but sealed-tube intercalation may be better for bulk stoichiometry if Rb volatility is controlled.
