### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum stability score. This is reinforced by thermodynamically similar stable sodium sulfides such as Na₂PdS₂ and Na₂Mn₂S₃ with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1; TS2), and by chemically similar stable Na₂Mn₂S₃ (CS1). The uncertainty is that layered NaVS₂ analogues are reported slightly above hull in some entries, 0.045–0.061 eV/atom, indicating that nearby Caswellsilverite-type NaMS₂ phases can be marginal or composition-sensitive (CS2; CS3).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild-to-moderate because the Na₂Mn₂S₃ precedent uses 870 K / 597 °C, ambient-pressure reaction-tube conditions, and purified Ar rather than high pressure or vacuum (CS1; SS3; TS2). The main deduction is procedural complexity: preheating, gradual ramping, slow cooling, extraction/washing, and vacuum drying are all reported (CS1; SS3; TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high-pressure equipment or toxic precursors for this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The sulfur-rich flux should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and Na₂Mn₂S₃ forms in only 2 h at 870 K from Na₂CO₃/S/Mn (CS1; SS3; TS2). However, the target structure is R3m NaMnS₂, while the direct Na–Mn–S precedent is monoclinic Na₂Mn₂S₃, so structural reconstruction/phase selection is nontrivial (CS1; SS3; TS2). The reported MnS contamination in the analogue lowers the kinetic and phase-purity confidence (CS1; SS3; TS2).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires evacuated sealed tubes, elemental Na, and elemental S, following the NaVS₂ precedent (CS2; CS3). The temperature is only 600 °C, so there is no high-temperature penalty (CS2; CS3), but vacuum containment, volatile Na/S, multistep heating/quenching, and Na air sensitivity create substantial severity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly supports sealed-tube elemental Na–Mn–S synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: NaVS₂ is Caswellsilverite-like R3m/R-3m and has Na–S and V–S distances very close to the target’s Na–S and Mn–S distances (CS2; CS3). Elemental Na and S are reactive, but Mn metal can form competing MnS [Reasonable Inference], and NaVS₂ reports phase sensitivity and contamination issues (CS2; CS3). The route is therefore kinetically plausible but phase-sensitive.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is based on Na₂PdS₂/Na₂PtS₂ synthesis under dry oxygen-free Ar at 800–900 °C (SS1; TS1). It avoids vacuum and high pressure, but it requires a strictly dry controlled atmosphere and moisture-sensitive sodium sulfide/polysulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference] (SS1; TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS supports Na₂S₂-based Mn synthesis specifically, so the condition score is not raised by direct chemical precedent.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Na₂S₂/Na₂Sₓ should provide a reactive Na–S medium [Reasonable Inference], and Na₂PdS₂/Na₂PtS₂ formation from Na₂S₂ supports this type of sodium sulfide chemistry (SS1; TS1). The route is structurally less direct because Na₂PdS₂ is monoclinic and Pd has square-planar/low-coordinate sulfide chemistry rather than octahedral MnS₆ chemistry (SS1; TS1). Thus the kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 inherits the difficult features of AMnSe₂ cation-exchange synthesis: evacuated ~10⁻⁴ Torr silica tubes, Nb inner tubes, high temperatures of 850–1000 °C, long durations, and controlled cooling/quenching (CS5; SS2; SS4). These conditions are standard for air-sensitive chalcogenides but more demanding than Route 1. Insufficient evidence from TS directly supports sulfide Li→Na exchange for NaMnS₂.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Cation exchange can reduce reconstructive diffusion if the framework is preserved [Reasonable Inference], and AMnSe₂ analogues show that alkali exchange can access phases that direct synthesis struggles to make (CS5; SS2; SS4). However, the selenide analogues involve tetrahedral MnSe₄ environments, whereas the target contains octahedral MnS₆, so framework transfer is uncertain (CS5; SS2; SS4). The need to prepare a suitable Li–Mn–S precursor further reduces confidence [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: a large Na/Mn cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The provided target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell, and it emphasizes edge-sharing octahedral connectivity rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity. Some structurally similar analogues contain mixed polyhedral networks and larger formula units, but this is not established for the target, so the risk remains low-to-medium rather than high (CS1; SS1; TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaMnS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and stable Na–Mn–S / sodium sulfide analogues are present in the CS and TS sets (CS1; TS2). The strongest synthesis support comes from the exact Na–Mn–S flux precedent for Na₂Mn₂S₃ and the close Caswellsilverite NaVS₂ structural analogues (CS1; CS2; CS3; SS3; TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sulfur-rich Na–Mn–S melt/flux chemistry that forms ternary sodium manganese sulfides at moderate temperature (CS1; SS3; TS2). The main limiting factor is phase competition, especially MnS formation and possible stabilization of Na₂Mn₂S₃ instead of NaMnS₂ (CS1; SS3; TS2). Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with flux stoichiometry and thermal profile likely determining success.
