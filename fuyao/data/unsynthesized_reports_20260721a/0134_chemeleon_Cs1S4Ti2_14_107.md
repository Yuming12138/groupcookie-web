### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the provided rubric. This is consistent with several chemically and thermodynamically related analogues that are also on or near the hull, including Rb₂MoS₄, CsTiCl₃, CsCrCl₃, NaNbS₂, LiNbS₂, and Ti₄GaS₈ (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing off-stoichiometric CsₓTiS₂ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity score because it avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, matching alkali intercalation syntheses near 800 °C in evacuated quartz tubes (TS-1, TS-5). The score is reduced for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile sulfur/Cs species, air-sensitive Cs or Cs₂S, and likely multi-step TiS₂ preformation plus intercalation, as analogous LiTiS₂ synthesis involves drying, sealed heating, quenching, and post-treatment (SS-4). Uncertainty remains in whether Cs can be inserted cleanly without forming Cs₂S, Cs polysulfides, or unreacted TiS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable relative to a full reconstructive solid-state reaction because a TiS₂ precursor already contains the needed TiS₆ sheets, and Li/TiS₂-derived analogues are structurally close to the target (SS-1, SS-4). However, long anneals and repeated firings reported in Ti sulfides indicate slow ordering and diffusion limitations (SS-3, SS-4). The large Cs ion may order sluggishly between TiS₂-like slabs, so the route is feasible but not easy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures comparable to CuTi₂S₄ flux growth at 750 °C and KCuZrS₃ reactive flux synthesis over 500–900 °C steps (SS-5, TS-4). The route still requires sealed evacuated/Ar conditions, volatile sulfur containment, air-sensitive Cs sources, and flux removal, so it is not low-severity (SS-5, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates Cs–Ti–S flux growth, so the route is extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 2

The flux should improve mass transport, as shown by sulfide single-crystal growth in BaVS₃ and CuTi₂S₄ analogues (CS-2, SS-5). Phase control is the major kinetic concern because flux methods can stabilize competing polymorphs or minor phases, as BaVS₃ flux growth involves careful cooling and CuTi₂S₄ requires specific flux/temperature conditions (CS-2, SS-5). A Cs-containing flux may help Cs incorporation but could also trap halide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT/vapor-assisted synthesis avoids extreme temperature and pressure but requires evacuated ampoules, volatile I₂/S/Cs species, and multi-step precursor preparation, as in TiS₂ CVT (SS-1). Sealed tantalum containers are used for CsTiCl₃ synthesis at 700 °C, indicating that Cs–Ti halide systems can require robust containment (CS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS directly supports CVT for Cs–Ti sulfides.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT can overcome diffusion limitations because TiS₂ itself is grown by iodine vapor transport over hundreds of hours (SS-1). Cs incorporation during vapor transport is uncertain because CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct Cs–Ti–S CVT analogue; the support comes indirectly from CsTiCl₃ sealed-container synthesis (CS-4). The route may be best for crystals after a bulk phase field is identified [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Solvothermal synthesis has the mildest temperature, supported by Rb₂MoS₄ formation at 393 K for 7 d in 1,2-ethanediamine (CS-1). Deductions arise from sealed autoclave pressure, toxic/air-sensitive reagents such as TiCl₃/Cs₂S, and likely post-annealing if an octahedral TiS₆ framework must crystallize [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports a solvothermal route to the target structure.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solvothermal precursors are highly active, but structural reconstruction into edge-sharing TiS₆ octahedral layers is poorly supported because the CS solvothermal analogue is a tetrahedral MoS₄ thiometallate and was only a minor phase with Ag₂S as the main product (CS-1). This makes phase control and structural transfer weak. The route is exploratory rather than a primary recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Cs/Ti cation-size contrast and mixed edge/corner-sharing environments give a medium amorphization or disorder risk, based on the target bonding description and analogous mixed-connectivity sulfides (SS-1, SS-5, TS-4). The target is not expected to have a very large unit cell based on its 7-atom formula and likely small C2/m cell [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct target cell-size evidence. The main uncertainty is whether Cs ordering between TiS₂ slabs produces superstructures or stacking disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs(TiS₂)₂ is likely a ground-state phase by the supplied E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, not merely a high-energy metastable phase. The strongest support comes from structurally similar TiS₂/LiTiS₂-derived sulfides and thermodynamic alkali–MX₂ intercalation analogues made in evacuated sealed tubes near 800 °C (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the ability to start from TiS₂-like edge-sharing TiS₆ layers, minimizing reconstructive chemistry (SS-1, SS-4). The most critical limiting factor is Cs stoichiometry and ordering control, because large alkali cations and volatile sulfur/Cs species require sealed, moisture-free synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by TS-1, TS-5, SS-4]. Flux and CVT variants are plausible for crystal growth, but the direct sealed-tube intercalation route is the most chemically transferable.
