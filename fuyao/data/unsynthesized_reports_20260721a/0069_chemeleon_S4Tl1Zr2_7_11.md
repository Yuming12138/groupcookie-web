### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin by the provided rubric. Chemically similar Ta/Nb sulfides and structurally/thermodynamically similar Zr sulfides also commonly have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that sulfide compositions of this type can be ground-state phases (CS, SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is that the hull value is computational, and no experimental Tl–Zr–S entry is provided in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1, sealed-tube solid-state

The severity is moderate because analogous sulfides are synthesized at 650–1000 °C rather than at extreme temperatures or pressures (CS, SS, TS). The score is reduced by the need for sealed/vacuum ampoules, sulfur/Tl volatility, likely multi-step grinding/annealing, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS/TS support the general condition window, but no source directly verifies Zr₂TlS₄ formation.

#### F3 — Route 1, sealed-tube solid-state

Kinetics are only moderate because refractory Zr or crystalline ZrS₂ can limit solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, using ZrS₂ partially preserves the target’s ZrS₂ sheet structure, and solid-state analogues form at 700–1000 °C with long anneals (CS, SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether Tl orders into the P-6m2 interlayer/cluster site rather than segregating as Tl₂S or volatilizing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2, iodine-assisted CVT

CVT avoids very high temperatures and high pressure, but it requires a sealed ampoule, iodine transport agent, and volatile/toxic Tl/S/I species (CS, SS, TS). The 900→700 °C ZrS₂ route and 950→800 °C FeTa₃S₆-type iodine transport route show that the required apparatus is specialized but routine for chalcogenide crystal growth (SS, TS, CS). The main severity uncertainty is possible over-transport or loss of Tl [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2, iodine-assisted CVT

Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport bypasses slow bulk diffusion and ZrS₂, the closest sheet analogue, is directly grown by iodine CVT (SS, TS). Chemically similar layered sulfides also use iodine or vapor transport for crystal growth (CS). The uncertainty is compositional control: Tl transport coefficients and iodine chemistry are not provided for the target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3, flux growth

Flux growth uses lower temperature than many solid-state routes and is supported by Eu₅Zr₃S₁₂ synthesis in NaCl/KCl flux at 943 K under Ar (SS, TS). Deductions come from sealed/inert handling, multi-step flux processing, sulfur/Tl volatility, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux growth of chemically similar Tl/Zr sulfides, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3, flux growth

Flux should enhance transport and nucleation relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and a ZrS₂-containing ternary Zr sulfide is successfully grown from NaCl/KCl flux (SS, TS). The structural pathway is plausible because ZrS₂ is already close to the target’s ZrS₂ sheets (SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether Tl partitions into the crystalline target rather than remaining dissolved in flux or forming Tl halides/sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4, H₂S sulfurization

H₂S sulfurization is supported for SrZrS₃ and CeScS₃, but those reactions use high temperatures around 1373–1473 K and a toxic reactive gas stream (SS, TS). Lowering the temperature for Tl retention is a necessary extrapolation rather than directly supported by the data [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for this route and is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4, H₂S sulfurization

H₂S provides high sulfur activity, which helps convert oxides/carbonates to sulfides in Zr-related analogues (SS, TS). However, oxide-to-layered-sulfide conversion requires extensive structural reconstruction and may be kinetically slow, especially from ZrO₂ [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is high because no analogous Tl–Zr layered sulfide sulfurization is reported.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Tl and Zr, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is simple layered P-6m2 with edge-sharing ZrS₆ sheets, and the description does not indicate a large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network (target data). SS/TS analogues such as ZrS₂ and Eu₅Zr₃S₁₂ show crystalline Zr sulfide frameworks, reducing concern for amorphization (SS, TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₂TlS₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar sulfides also lie on or near the hull (CS, SS, TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from ZrS₂, which has nearly identical edge-sharing ZrS₆ sheet chemistry and is directly grown by iodine CVT (SS, TS). Chemically similar Ta/Nb sulfides show that layered transition-metal sulfides with post-transition-metal species can be made by solid-state and vapor-transport approaches (CS). The most critical enabling factor is preservation of the ZrS₂-like layer motif using ZrS₂ precursor and CVT/flux transport (SS, TS). The main limiting factor is controlling Tl and S volatility/toxicity while avoiding segregation into ZrS₂ plus Tl sulfide phases [Reasonable Inference].
