### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is calculated on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the provided target data, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Chemical analogues KLuS₂/RbLuS₂/KHoS₂ are also reported at E_hull = 0 (CS1, CS2, CS4), and Ir sulfide analogues IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ are also hull-stable (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee a broad experimental phase field, especially in K–Ir–S where competing Ir sulfides may form **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is conditionally severe because H₂S flow is toxic/corrosive and high-temperature sulfurization near 1000–1030 °C is required in directly analogous K/Rb–Ln–S₂ preparations (CS1, CS2, CS4). Its kinetics are favorable relative to sealed elemental synthesis because the large alkali carbonate/sulfide excess acts as a flux and same-structure KLnS₂ phases crystallize after short holds (CS1, CS4). The main uncertainty is whether Ir adopts the needed oxidation/coordination state in the Caswellsilverite lattice rather than forming IrS₂/Ir₂S₃, which are stable Ir sulfides (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2) **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 avoids flowing H₂S but requires sealed evacuated tubes and volatile sulfur management, as used for IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ syntheses (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2). Kinetics are weaker because Ir sulfide formation can require long anneals, grinding, and refiring for homogeneity (SS1/TS1). The route has insufficient evidence from CS for direct formation of Caswellsilverite KIrS₂, so the K-ordering and phase-selectivity components are treated as uncertain/neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has the mildest severity score because K–noble-metal sulfide flux synthesis occurs at 800–900 °C without mandatory H₂S or high pressure (SS3/TS3). Kinetics benefit from molten flux transport, but phase selectivity is less certain because KPt₂S₃ is not the same stoichiometry or topology as KIrS₂, and the source notes unsuccessful syntheses for some iridium compounds (SS3/TS3). CS3 provides general alkali chalcogenide flux support, but insufficient direct evidence from CS exists for KIrS₂ Caswellsilverite growth by this exact route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple ordered R-3m ABX₂ description with one K, one Ir, and one S site, similar to crystalline KLuS₂/RbLuS₂/KHoS₂ analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4), so amorphization risk is not high. One risk trigger is the large cation-size mismatch between K and Ir **[Reasonable Inference]**, but this mismatch is structurally accommodated in related Caswellsilverite phases by distinct K–S and M–S coordination environments (CS1, CS4). There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for amorphization behavior in KIrS₂ specifically, so SS/TS are neutral for this factor.

KIrS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple nearby sulfide analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, CS2, CS4; SS1/SS2; TS1–TS5). The strongest synthetic support comes from same-structure Caswellsilverite K/Rb–Ln–S₂ compounds made by alkali carbonate + H₂S sulfurization (CS1, CS2, CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a robust layered R-3m A–M–S₂ sulfide framework that tolerates large alkali cations and octahedral metal sulfide layers (CS1, CS2, CS4). The most critical limiting factor is competition from stable binary Ir sulfides such as IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃, whose synthesis is well supported in SS/TS and which may trap Ir if K incorporation is kinetically slow (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2). A secondary limitation is safety/phase control under H₂S, especially because CS4 reports oxysulfide impurity issues in related K–rare-earth sulfide growth.

| 1 | All | Competing IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ formation | High | Maintain high K activity using large K₂CO₃/K₂S excess, because large alkali excess stabilizes KLnS₂ analogues in CS1/CS2/CS4; use prereacted IrS₂ only as controlled precursor if needed, informed by SS1/TS1. |
| 2 | Route 1 | H₂S toxicity/corrosion | High | Use dry flowing H₂S tube furnace with scrubber and corrosion-resistant alumina/graphite boat setup; H₂S flow setups are used in CS1/CS2/CS4 and TS5. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Oxide/oxysulfide impurity from incomplete sulfurization | Medium–High | Use dry H₂S, fine IrO₂/Ir powder, excess K₂CO₃, and staged heating; CS4 reports oxysulfide impurities and therefore supports careful oxygen exclusion and sulfurization control. |
| 4 | Route 2 | Sulfur volatility and sealed-tube pressure | High | Use slow initial heating and small sulfur excess; SS1/TS1 use stepwise heating in evacuated silica tubes, and SS2/TS2 use slow heating to 900 °C. |
