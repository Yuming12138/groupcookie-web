### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifying a score of 98. The remaining uncertainty is that computational ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 CVT/self-transport

CVT conditions are moderate in temperature because GeAsSe, Sn₂SbS₂I₃, and CuBrSe₃ are transported at roughly 340–450 °C (CS-5, SS-2, TS-5). The score is reduced for vacuum/sealed tubes and volatile iodine transport, both directly reflected in sealed-tube CVT references (CS-5, SS-2, TS-5). Toxic As/I handling is an extrapolated hazard [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 CVT/self-transport

Kinetics are favorable because iodine-mediated transport can move material through vapor species rather than long-range bulk diffusion, as demonstrated for GeAsSe and Sn₂SbS₂I₃ (CS-5, SS-2). The structural match is partial: target AsSeI is 2D and low-dimensional like GeAsSe and Sn₂SbS₂I₃ (CS-5, SS-2), but the exact AsSe₃I₃ edge-sharing octahedral sheet is not directly reported. This gives good but not excellent kinetic confidence.

#### F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal HI

Hydrothermal Te–I analogues operate at only 195–280 °C for 7–10 days, so thermal severity is low (SS-3, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2). Deductions arise from sealed autoclave operation and hazardous HI/I₂/As chemistry [Reasonable Inference], while no multi-GPa pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required. Hydrothermal media provide high precursor activity and solution-mediated transport, supported by Te–I iodide formation in acidic HI (SS-3, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2). The route’s uncertainty is chemical transferability: Te–I hydrothermal chemistry is well supported, but direct As–Se–I hydrothermal crystallization is not shown, so As₂Se₃ dissolution and iodination behavior are [Reasonable Inference]. The low temperature and active iodide medium justify the best overall kinetics among practical routes.

#### F2 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman

InTeBr melt growth at 520 °C supports moderate-temperature Bridgman synthesis for a layered chalcogen halide (CS-3). Deductions arise from sealed ampoule/vacuum handling and volatile/toxic iodine/arsenic species [Reasonable Inference], but the method avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperatures. A melt route has favorable kinetic mobility, as InTeBr crystals were grown from the melt at 520 °C (CS-3). Structural reconstruction is not severe because the target and InTeBr are both low-dimensional chalcogen halides (CS-3), but the coordination differs—InTe₃Br tetrahedra versus AsSe₃I₃ octahedra—so the score is capped [Reasonable Inference]. Chemically similar PdAsSe-family materials require multistage heating to 450 °C and 800 °C and quenching (CS-2), and Te₃(PdBr)₄ requires several days at 700 K with stoichiometry control (CS-1). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AsSeI is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar chalcogen halides are also E_hull = 0 phases (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The analogue support is strong for low-dimensional chalcogen-halide crystallization, especially iodine transport and acidic hydrothermal iodide chemistry (CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility of iodine-containing vapor or hydrothermal species, which can overcome slow solid-state diffusion at moderate temperatures (CS-5, SS-2; TS-1, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining As:Se:I stoichiometry while suppressing volatile iodine/arsenic loss and competing As₂Se₃ or arsenic iodide phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with hydrothermal HI and sealed-tube CVT as the most attractive first trials.

| 3 | All | Competing As₂Se₃, AsI₃, Se-rich, or I-rich phases | Medium–High | Maintain tight stoichiometry; CS-1 reports by-products when stoichiometry deviates in a chemically similar halide/chalcogenide. Screen small iodine excess/deficiency [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 4 | Route 1 | CVT transport imbalance causing deposition of binary phases | Medium | Use a controlled temperature gradient like GeAsSe iodine transport and CuBrSe₃ transport syntheses (CS-5, TS-5). |
