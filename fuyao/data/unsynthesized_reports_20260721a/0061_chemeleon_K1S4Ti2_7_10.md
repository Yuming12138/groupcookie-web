### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is maximal under the provided rubric. This is reinforced by chemically similar K sulfides at or near the hull, including KCr3S5 and KFe2S3 at E_hull = 0 (CS), and by thermodynamic analogues such as NaNbS2, KCuZrS3, SrZrS3, and LiNbS2 at E_hull = 0 (TS). Structurally similar Ti sulfides are also close to the hull, including LiTiS2 and Ti4GaS8 (SS). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of off-stoichiometric KxTiS2 phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses conventional evacuated ampoule sulfide conditions similar to SrTiS3, Ti–Fe–S, LiTiS2, and alkali dichalcogenides (CS, SS, TS). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile S/K components, and air-sensitive K-containing precursors [Reasonable Inference], while the temperature remains below 1000 °C. CS/SS/TS provide strong support for sealed-tube sulfide synthesis, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact K vapor pressure tolerance of this target, so ampoule design remains uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct reaction benefits from TiS2 as a preformed octahedral TiS6 framework, which is structurally close to the target [Reasonable Inference supported by SS TiS2/LiTiS2 descriptions]. However, solid-state Ti sulfides often require long anneals or repeated firings, as seen for LiTiS2 and Ti–Fe–S analogues (SS), and SrTiS3 can require prolonged heating up to weeks (CS). The expected kinetics are moderate, not easy, because K ordering and diffusion into a TiS2-derived lattice may be slow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Flux growth is supported by KCr3S5 in potassium sulfide/polysulfide or carbonate/H2S fluxes (CS), KCuZrS3 reactive K2S flux (TS), and CuTi2S4 KCl/KI flux at 750 °C (SS). Conditions are not ultrahigh-temperature or high-pressure, but the route requires dry loading, sealed tubes, volatile sulfur control, and flux removal. The severity is therefore moderate-to-high despite favorable temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux lowers diffusion barriers by providing liquid-phase transport, as demonstrated in K-containing sulfide flux syntheses (CS, TS) and Ti sulfide flux growth (SS). This supports a higher kinetic score than direct solid-state reaction. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS K–Cr–S chemistry reports narrow phase windows and transformations among KCrS2/KCr3S5/KCr5S8-type phases, so analogous K–Ti–S competing phases may form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses ion exchange/intercalation in sealed evacuated silica, matching the KV5S8 ion-exchange precedent (CS) and alkali dichalcogenide sealed-tube intercalation chemistry (TS). The route avoids very high temperatures but remains multi-step because a TiS2 or LixTiS2 host must be prepared, exchanged, washed, and compositionally verified (SS, CS). Air sensitivity of intercalated alkali sulfides is the main handling penalty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

This route has the best kinetic outlook because it can preserve a TiS2-like octahedral framework while inserting K, minimizing structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference supported by SS LiTiS2/LiTi2S4/TiS2 structures]. Ion exchange is directly demonstrated for KV5S8 using molten alkali halides (CS), while alkali intercalation into dichalcogenides is broadly supported by TS. The uncertainty is that K+ is larger than Li+ and may require different staging/ordering, so K0.5 stoichiometry may need careful temperature and chemical-potential tuning [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Hydrothermal synthesis is supported only indirectly by KFe2S3 at 450–500 °C and 2.7–3.2 kbar in KOH/KCl/S medium (CS). The route avoids high furnace temperatures but requires high-pressure autoclaves and chemically aggressive sulfide/polysulfide media [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for Ti-based hydrothermal analogues makes this a lower-confidence route; those sources are treated as neutral 50 for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Hydrothermal media can enhance transport and crystal growth, as seen for KFe2S3 (CS). However, TiS2 may be hydrolytically or redox sensitive under alkaline hydrothermal sulfur-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference], and no SS/TS Ti analogue supports this specific pathway. The structural reconstruction is likely larger than in topochemical intercalation, so the kinetic score is lower.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large K/Ti cation size mismatch, indicated by K–S distances of ~3.16–3.18 Å and Ti–S distances of ~2.43–2.47 Å in the target, comparable to large K–S versus transition-metal–S separations in CS/TS K sulfides. The formula is small and the target description does not show coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so only one amorphization check is triggered. The main uncertainty is the actual crystallographic cell size from the CIF, because CS/SS/TS do not provide that for the target.

The analogue set supports synthesis well: CS confirms K-containing transition-metal sulfides can be prepared by flux, hydrothermal, and ion-exchange routes; SS confirms TiS6 sulfide frameworks closely related to the target; TS confirms alkali-intercalated sulfides are accessible in evacuated tubes.
