### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the provided ≤0.05 eV/atom window. Thermodynamically similar compounds include zero- or near-zero-hull sulfides such as Ti4GaS8, Ti3Se4, Ti2CoS4, LiTiS2, and Nb3S4 (TS1–TS5). The main uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide finite-temperature sulfur chemical potential or configurational ordering data, so those effects are treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses conditions almost directly transferred from Ti2CoS4: evacuated silica tubes, 600–1000 °C firings, week-long holds, and dry N2 grinding (CS1, SS1, TS3). The severity penalties come from vacuum, sealed volatile sulfur, and multiple firings, all explicitly consistent with the analogue protocol (CS1). It avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, so its severity is moderate rather than severe.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic advantage is the very close structural match to Ti2CoS4, which shares the same C2/m octahedral framework and similar bond lengths (CS1, SS1, TS3). The kinetic limitation is the use of dense elemental Ti/Mn and the need for repeated long firings, which indicates slow diffusion and phase-purity challenges (CS1). Insufficient evidence from TS for route-specific kinetics is treated as neutral beyond the Ti2CoS4 synthesis overlap in TS3.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 avoids free elemental sulfur as a dominant reagent by using TiS2/MnS-type sulfide feedstocks, reducing the sealed-volatile-precursor penalty [Reasonable Inference]. The 900 °C evacuated ampoule condition is directly supported by CS2, where Fe + TiS2 mixtures required repeated firings for complete reaction. The method still requires vacuum and repeated processing, giving moderate severity.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
TiS2 provides a chemically activated Ti–S precursor, supported by CS2 synthesis of Fe–Ti–S phases at 900 °C. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: TiS2/MnS precursors are not isostructural with the target C2/m Ti2MnS4, so cation ordering and framework rearrangement are still needed [Reasonable Inference]. The close Ti2CoS4 analogue reduces concern that the final topology is inaccessible (CS1, SS1, TS3).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is more severe because it intentionally uses >1000 °C annealing, vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur control, and multiple processing steps. High-temperature evacuated-tube sulfide synthesis is supported for TiS and Nb3S4-type phases (SS3, SS5, TS5). This route is viable but more demanding and riskier for sulfur loss than Routes 1–2 [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Higher temperature improves diffusion and equilibration [Reasonable Inference], consistent with Ti and Nb sulfides being synthesized at 1000–1300 °C in sealed tubes (SS3, SS5, TS5). However, Ti sulfides show phase-control complexity, including narrow homogeneity ranges and multiple modifications, which can divert the reaction from the target phase (SS3, SS5). The quench/anneal strategy is therefore kinetically helpful but less selective.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target and Ti2CoS4 analogue contain mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing octahedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity amorphization/crystallization risk (target description; CS1, SS1, TS3). The Mn/Ti cation size/valence mismatch likely exceeds 15% for common octahedral oxidation-state assignments [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not directly report ionic radii, so this carries uncertainty. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2MnS4 is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues include several stable sulfides (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support is the near-isostructural Ti2CoS4 analogue, which has the same monoclinic C2/m MTi2S4-type octahedral topology and was made by conventional sealed-tube solid-state reaction (CS1, SS1, TS3). The most important enabling factor is that Ti–M–S sulfide frameworks with TiS6/MS6 octahedra are experimentally accessible under standard evacuated-ampoule conditions (CS1, CS2, SS1). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: repeated firings, dry grinding, and careful stoichiometry are needed in analogues, and Ti sulfides can exhibit competing phases or narrow homogeneity ranges (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS5). Overall, the target appears plausibly synthesizable by bulk solid-state methods, with Route 2 slightly preferred for improved precursor reactivity.
