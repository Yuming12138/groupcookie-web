### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. This is strongly reinforced by structurally and thermodynamically similar RbLnS₂ analogues with E_hull = 0 and the same Caswellsilverite R-3m structure (SS, TS). CS also supports stability because KYS₂ is Caswellsilverite and E_hull = 0, although KYS₂ contains K/Y rather than Rb/Sc (CS). Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the calculated Sc compound competes with unlisted Rb–Sc–S polymorphs or sulfide binaries [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses high-temperature flowing H₂S, which is a controlled and hazardous atmosphere, but it avoids sealed pressure vessels and high pressure (SS, TS). The closest RbLnS₂ analogues form at 1000 °C in flowing H₂S (SS, TS), while KYS₂ forms at 1323 K under H₂S with Ar cooling (CS). The main severity penalty is H₂S handling; the temperature is high but within standard tube-furnace capability [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence exists for the exact Sc member in CS/SS/TS, so the Sc-specific condition window remains uncertain.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable because the target has the same Caswellsilverite framework as many RbLnS₂ analogues, lowering structural reconstruction burden (SS, TS). The carbonate excess likely acts as a flux/reactive alkali reservoir, as indicated by 70:1 and 80:1 carbonate:oxide protocols (CS, SS, TS). The limiting kinetic factor is Sc₂O₃ sulfidation because Sc₂O₃ is expected to be more refractory than many lanthanide oxides [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports RbScS₂ formation rate, so this remains the largest uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is supported only by CS examples of sealed evacuated ampoule alkali chalcogenide synthesis, such as K₂TiS₃ and K₃GaS₃ (CS). It is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile sulfur/alkali species, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS exists for sealed-ampoule preparation of RbLnS₂ or RbScS₂, so SS and TS are neutral for this route-specific factor.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Using sulfide precursors may bypass oxide sulfidation, but sealed alkali chalcogenide reactions can require long anneals, as K₂TiS₃ required 6 days at 1070 K (CS). Structural formation of Caswellsilverite RbScS₂ from sulfides is plausible but not directly demonstrated [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetics are therefore moderate, with risks from slow cation ordering and Rb/S volatility [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific kinetic evidence for this ampoule method.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 benefits from relatively low temperatures near the Rb₂CoS₂/K₂CoS₂ examples, which used 1030 K for 10 h (CS). It is still penalized for H₂/chalcogen or H₂S gas handling (CS). It does not require high pressure or sealed ampoules, so the condition severity score remains high. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this specific metal-sulfiding route and are scored neutral for route-specific severity.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The carbonate + metal + chalcogen route is chemically plausible because Co analogues form from alkali carbonates and metal powder in hydrogen charged with chalcogen (CS). However, those products have tetrahedral CoS₄-based Ibam structures rather than the target ScS₆ Caswellsilverite framework (CS), making structural transfer weak. Sc metal sulfiding is expected to be slower than Co sulfiding due to passivation/refractory behavior [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are the limiting issue for Route 3.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target and closest RbLnS₂ analogues are crystalline Caswellsilverite R-3m structures with simple Rb–S and M–S coordination environments (SS, TS). The main amorphization trigger is the large Rb/Sc cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], although layered/ordered Caswellsilverite analogues tolerate large Rb/Ln size differences (SS, TS). The structure contains edge and corner sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity in the closest analogues (SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is whether smaller Sc³⁺ induces local distortion or alternative coordination not captured in the analogue set [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbScS₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest RbLnS₂ thermodynamic analogues are also E_hull = 0 (TS). The analogue support is strong structurally: RbTmS₂, RbHoS₂, RbErS₂, RbTbS₂, and RbDyS₂ all adopt the same Caswellsilverite R-3m structure with similar Rb–S environments (SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated RbLnS₂ carbonate/oxide-to-sulfide route in flowing H₂S with large Rb₂CO₃ excess, which should provide both Rb flux and sulfurization conditions (SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is the absence of a direct Sc analogue and the expected refractory nature of Sc₂O₃, which may slow complete sulfidation or favor competing Sc sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with Route 1 clearly preferred.
