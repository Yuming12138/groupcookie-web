### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic score range according to the provided target data. Thermodynamically similar phases Bi₂S₃, AgBi₃S₅, MnBi₄S₇, HgBi₂S₄, and Bi₂OS₂ are also listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), which supports the plausibility of ground-state Bi–S-rich and sulfosalt frameworks. CS evidence is mostly favorable but not perfectly uniform because FeBi₄S₇ is 0.119 eV/atom above hull and Cu₄Bi₇S₁₂ is 0.029 eV/atom above hull, while HgBi₂S₄, MnBi₄S₇, and Pb₂GeS₄ are on the hull (CS1–CS5). SS provides strong structural analogy but insufficient exact thermodynamic evidence for PbBi₂S₄, so SS is neutral for exact F1 scoring.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, because closely related sulfides are synthesized at 923–998 K rather than above 1000 °C (CS2, TS4, SS1, TS1). The main penalties are vacuum sealed-tube operation, multi-step grinding/pelletizing/refiring, and Pb toxicity [Reasonable Inference; sealed-tube and multistep basis from SS1/TS1 and CS1/SS5]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty applies because the analogues use ambient-pressure sealed ampoules and sub-1000 °C temperatures (SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is phase purity, since PbS/Bi₂S₃ systems show multiple ordered/disordered phases (SS1, TS1).

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfide precursors are compositionally close to the target and are directly supported for Pb–Bi–S sintering (SS1, TS1). Diffusion should be manageable because related phases form at 650–725 °C and Pb/Bi sulfide systems can involve low-melting or eutectic-assisted transport [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1]. Structural reconstruction is still nontrivial because the ordered Cmc2₁ PbBi₂S₄ framework must form from binary sulfides, and analogues report long sintering and phase complexity (SS1, TS1). The resulting F3 is favorable but not exceptional.

#### F2 — Route 2

Elemental synthesis is supported by MnBi₄S₇/FeBi₄S₇ sealed-tube syntheses using elemental metals and sulfur (CS1, CS3, SS3, SS5, TS3). The severity is higher than Route 1 because elemental sulfur is volatile and requires slow prereaction or careful sealing, as suggested by prereaction protocols in Pb sulfide synthesis (CS5). Vacuum sealing, multi-step heating, and Pb toxicity all penalize the route (CS1, CS3, CS5 plus [Reasonable Inference]). There is insufficient evidence from TS for elemental PbBi₂S₄ specifically, so TS is neutral for this route’s operational severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental mixtures have lower initial precursor activity because sulfides must first form before the final ternary ordering step [Reasonable Inference]. Similar elemental Bi sulfides require long anneals and slow cooling, indicating that ordering kinetics can be slow (CS1, CS3, SS3, SS5, TS3). Diffusion is helped by low-melting Pb/Bi and sulfide formation at moderate temperature [Reasonable Inference], but complete structural reconstruction from elements lowers the kinetic score. The main uncertainty is whether transient melts improve or worsen phase segregation.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT operates at low nominal temperature, 340 °C, which avoids high-temperature severity penalties (CS4, SS4). However, it uses a sealed tube with a transport agent and a controlled gradient, which introduces reactive-atmosphere and multi-step penalties (CS4, SS4). PbS toxicity and volatile/corrosive transport chemistry add further penalties [Reasonable Inference from HCl transport in CS4/SS4]. Insufficient evidence from TS for Pb–Bi-only CVT means TS is neutral for route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Transport growth can bypass bulk solid-state diffusion and can promote crystallization at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference], which is consistent with the 340 °C CVT analogue (CS4, SS4). The limiting kinetic factor is phase selection: the analogue produced larger Bi₂S₃ crystals along with fine needles of the target-like compound, showing competing nucleation (CS4, SS4). Therefore, precursor mobility is favorable but structural/phase selectivity is uncertain. This route is better for crystals than bulk phase-pure powder [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Bridgman growth of a structurally/thermodynamically similar Bi sulfosalt was performed at 800 °C under vacuum in fused silica tubes (SS2, TS2). The route avoids >1000 °C and high-pressure penalties but requires vacuum sealing, gradient translation, and likely prereaction to control sulfur volatility [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2 and CS5]. Toxic Pb/PbS also penalizes this route [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS for PbBi₂S₄ Bridgman growth makes the route less certain.

#### F3 — Route 4

Melt or partially molten growth should give faster cation transport than solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The AgBi₃S₅ analogue was grown as bulk crystals by Bridgman technique, supporting crystallization of complex Bi sulfide frameworks from a thermal gradient (SS2, TS2). However, no provided source proves congruent melting or melt stability of PbBi₂S₄, so the structural-reconstruction score is capped. The main uncertainty is whether PbBi₂S₄ decomposes peritectically into PbS/Bi₂S₃ or other homologous Pb–Bi–S phases [Reasonable Inference from phase complexity in SS1/TS1].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically distinct Pb and Bi cations and a large, multi-site orthorhombic structure, which increases ordering complexity [Reasonable Inference from target structural description]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PbBi₂S₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Bi sulfides are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but phase-pure bulk powder will likely require careful temperature control, slow cooling, and at least one regrind/refire cycle.
