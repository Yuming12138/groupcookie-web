### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s exact E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum F1 score by the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues include several E_hull = 0 phases, especially Na₂Mn₂S₃, Na₂Mn₂Se₃, AgCd₂GaS₄, and CdCr₂S₄ (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS also supports stable Cd-containing chalcogenides but includes metastable analogues, so CS is supportive but mixed (CS-1 to CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the ordered Imm2 enargite-like polymorph is the experimentally selected phase rather than a competing spinel/rocksalt/binary mixture [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Direct solid-state reaction is penalized because analogous sulfides require evacuated sealed tubes, high temperatures, homogenization, annealing, and slow cooling (CS-2, CS-3, TS-4). Cd and sulfur volatility make sealed-ampoule control important [Reasonable Inference supported by the sealed chalcogenide practices in CS-2/CS-3/TS-4]. No high pressure is required, which keeps the route feasible. The main uncertainty is whether the target orders at accessible temperature before Cd/S loss or binary phase segregation occurs [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Using MnS and CdS improves precursor activity relative to elements, but solid-state cation diffusion and ordering remain moderate barriers [Reasonable Inference]. Analogues made by solid-state methods often use 48 h to hundreds of hours and repeated annealing, indicating nontrivial kinetics (CS-2, CS-3, TS-4). Structural reconstruction from binaries to an ordered enargite-like tetrahedral network is only partially supported by the presence of tetrahedral CdS₄ and MnS₄ motifs in analogues (CS-1, TS-4, SS-2). Therefore the route is feasible but slower than flux growth.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The flux route avoids >1000 °C conditions because the closest Mn sulfide flux analogue Na₂Mn₂S₃ forms at 870 K under purified Ar (SS-2/TS-1). It is penalized for controlled atmosphere, multi-step heating/cooling/washing, sulfur volatility, and Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-2/TS-1 process details]. No high pressure or extreme equipment is indicated. Uncertainty remains over whether an alkali sulfide flux introduces Na contamination or changes the ternary Cd–Mn–S phase field [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because molten sulfur/polysulfide chemistry can greatly enhance mass transport compared with dry powders [Reasonable Inference]. The structurally close Na₂Mn₂S₃ analogue has MnS₄ tetrahedra with Mn–S distances close to the target and forms at a relatively low temperature, supporting a liquid-mediated pathway (SS-2/TS-1). However, the target requires replacing alkali coordination chemistry with tetrahedral CdS₄, so cation ordering and phase selection remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The reported risk of MnS contamination in the analogue lowers confidence in phase-pure synthesis (SS-2/TS-1).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
CVT uses sealed evacuated tubes, halogen transport agents, volatile species, and long growth durations, all of which increase severity (CS-5). It avoids high pressure and can be operated below 1000 °C, which limits severity. Toxic/corrosive iodine or chlorine and toxic Cd species are practical hazards [Reasonable Inference]. The route is more appropriate for crystal growth after a precursor exists than for first discovery synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT improves kinetics through vapor-phase transport rather than bulk diffusion, and sulfide CVT using iodine/chlorine is directly evidenced (CS-5). However, CS-5 also reports phase competition, implying that transport conditions must be tuned carefully. Cd and Mn may form halides with different volatilities, which could disturb stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetics are good for crystal growth but not necessarily for phase-pure bulk formation.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Thin-film deposition is lower temperature than bulk routes but requires vacuum and post-deposition sulfurization [Reasonable Inference; weak vacuum evaporation basis in CS-4]. CS-4 provides insufficient material-specific evidence for Cd–Mn–S thin-film synthesis, so its support is neutral for target crystallization. Cd/S volatility and composition control remain significant hazards [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of high pressure and short diffusion lengths improve practical feasibility.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Film kinetics benefit from short diffusion lengths, but no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct crystallization of the target phase in thin-film form, so evidence is limited. Cd and S vapor-pressure control makes precursor availability and composition control only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Post-annealing is likely required, lowering the crystallization score [Reasonable Inference]. This route is exploratory rather than the recommended primary route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target consists of corner-sharing CdS₄ and MnS₄ tetrahedra, which is a relatively ordered and low-connectivity network rather than a highly mixed edge/face-sharing framework. One amorphization risk is the significant Cd²⁺/Mn²⁺ tetrahedral radius mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but target polyhedral connectivity does not show edge/face coexistence. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve it, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence and neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂CdS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from thermodynamic and structural analogues: Na₂Mn₂S₃ is both stable and flux-grown with similar MnS₄ tetrahedral bond lengths, while AgCd₂GaS₄ demonstrates stable tetrahedral Cd–sulfide frameworks (SS-2/TS-1, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the fully tetrahedral Cd/Mn sulfide topology, which matches known sulfide crystal chemistry and should be accessible under sulfur-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially avoiding MnS/CdS binaries and Cd/S volatility during heating [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-2/TS-1 and sealed-tube practices in CS-2/CS-3/TS-4]. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible, with the flux route offering the best balance of thermodynamics, kinetics, and manageable conditions.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Cd and S volatility can shift stoichiometry during heating. | High | Use sealed ampoules or sulfur overpressure; sealed chalcogenide syntheses are used in CS-2, CS-3, TS-4. |
| 2 | All | Competing MnS and CdS binary phases may persist. | High | Use sulfur-rich flux, preheating, regrinding, and long annealing; MnS contamination control is explicitly noted for Na₂Mn₂S₃ flux synthesis (SS-2/TS-1). |
| 3 | All | Cd/Mn cation ordering in the enargite-like tetrahedral lattice may be slow. | Medium | Apply slow cooling and long low-temperature anneal; slow cooling/annealing are used in CS-2, SS-2/TS-1, and TS-4. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Direct solid-state diffusion from dense metals/binaries may be sluggish. | Medium | Use pre-reacted fine MnS/CdS powders, repeated grinding, and staged heating [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-3]. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Flux incorporation or alkali contamination may alter product chemistry. | Medium | Use removable alkali flux and wash/extract as in Na₂Mn₂S₃ synthesis, while verifying target stability to washing (SS-2/TS-1; [Reasonable Inference]). |
| 6 | Route 3 | CVT may fractionate Cd, Mn, and S due to different halide volatilities. | High | Use pre-reacted source charge, mild iodine loading, small temperature gradient, and sulfur excess [Reasonable Inference from CVT phase competition in CS-5]. |
| 7 | Route 4 | Thin-film composition window may be narrow because Cd and S re-evaporate. | High | Use Cd/S overpressure, low-temperature deposition, and post-sulfurization anneal [Reasonable Inference; vacuum deposition evidence only weakly indicated by CS-4]. |
| 8 | All | Crystal growth of a new ordered ternary phase may be difficult even if powder synthesis succeeds. | Medium | Use Route 2 slow-cooling flux or Route 3 CVT after polycrystalline precursor formation; slow cooling is used in SS-2/TS-1 and vapor transport in CS-5. |
