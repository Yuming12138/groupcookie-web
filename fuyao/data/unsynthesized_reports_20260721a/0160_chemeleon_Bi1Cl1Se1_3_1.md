### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability score range. Similar materials in TS include Bi2Se3 and AgBiSCl2 with E_hull = 0.0, indicating that Bi–chalcogen and Bi–chalcogen–chloride compounds can be true ground-state phases (TS1, TS5). CS/SS analogues BiSeI and Bi3Se4Br are also near-hull, so the chemistry is not strongly destabilized by halide incorporation (CS1, SS1, SS2). Remaining uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee that the specific trigonal R3m polytype is the experimentally preferred polymorph.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT / Vapor Transport

The severity score is limited mainly by vacuum sealing, volatile halide chemistry, and toxic/moisture-sensitive chloride handling; these requirements are directly analogous to BiSeI and Bi3Se4Br sealed-ampoule CVT syntheses (CS1, SS1, SS2). Kinetically, this is the strongest route because vapor transport can equilibrate Bi–Se–halide species and has already produced single crystals in the closest analogue BiSeI (CS1, SS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether Cl, being smaller and more chemically aggressive than I or Br, stabilizes the desired 2D trigonal BiSeCl rather than BiCl3-rich or Bi2Se3-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Melt / Bridgman Growth

The condition severity is moderate because the route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure but still needs sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile Se/Cl containment, as in Bi2Se3 and AgBi2Se3Cl growth (TS1, SS5). Kinetics are favorable in the melt because diffusion is fast, and AgBi2Se3Cl demonstrates that Bi–Se–Cl crystals can be grown by cooling stoichiometric melts (SS5). The uncertainty is phase selection: cooling from a melt may favor more stable binary Bi2Se3 or chloride-rich segregated products unless Cl chemical potential is carefully controlled [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Ionothermal / Lewis-Acidic Halide Flux

This route has mild temperature but higher handling complexity because SS3 uses moisture-sensitive AlCl3/ionic liquid media and glovebox operations. Kinetics are favorable because ionothermal media crystallize a structurally similar 2D Bi–Se–halide framework at only 165 °C over 5 days (SS3). The main uncertainty is chemical transferability: the closest ionothermal analogue contains AlCl4 and Br/Cl constituents, so suppressing Al incorporation and obtaining neutral BiSeCl is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference] from SS3.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Solid-State Ampoule Reaction

This route is synthetically conventional but penalized for multi-step precursor preparation, sealed vacuum, and volatile/air-sensitive chloride use, consistent with BiSCl-derived solid-state syntheses (TS5). Kinetics are less favorable than vapor or melt routes because chloride must diffuse and order into a Bi–Se framework; TS5 reports multi-day sintering/holding for related halide chalcogenides. The uncertainty is whether Bi2Se3 can be transformed topotactically into BiSeCl or whether full reconstructive chemistry is needed [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Bi, so there is no cation-size mismatch trigger. Its reported structure contains edge-sharing BiSe3Cl3 octahedra and no stated coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing motifs, reducing structural frustration. Crystalline low-dimensional analogues BiSeI, Bi3Se4Br, Bi2Se3, and AgBiSCl2 are well documented, supporting a low amorphization risk (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS5). The uncertainty is that the exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given, but the simple BiSeCl stoichiometry and layered description make >40 atoms unlikely [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BiSeCl is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues such as Bi2Se3 and AgBiSCl2 are also stable crystalline phases (TS1, TS5). The analogue support is strong: BiSeI is both chemically and structurally close and has an established CVT synthesis, while Bi3Se4Br and AgBi2Se3Cl show broader Bi–Se–halide accessibility under sealed ampoule conditions (CS1, SS1, SS2, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of established Bi–Se–halide vapor/melt chemistry that forms ordered low-dimensional crystals. The most critical limiting factor is chloride activity control, because Cl-containing reagents such as BiCl3 are volatile/moisture-sensitive and may drive competing chloride-rich or binary Bi2Se3 products [Reasonable Inference] based on sealed/dry halide syntheses (SS4, TS5). Overall, the best first attempt is CVT or transport-assisted sealed-ampoule growth, followed by melt/Bridgman screening if phase-pure powder or crystals are desired.
