### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, so it receives a high thermodynamic score under the provided rubric. Chemically similar Na₂PS₃, Na₃PS₄, Na₃AsS₃, and Li₃BS₃ are also reported at E_hull = 0.0, while NaBS₂ is only 0.009 eV atom⁻¹ above hull, reinforcing that alkali sulfide frameworks of this type can be stable (CS-1–CS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar sets likewise contain E_hull = 0 analogues, including Na₃PS₄ and Na₃GaS₃ (SS-1–SS-5; TS-1–TS-5). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee a wide kinetic phase field or easy isolation from competing Na–Si–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The sulfide solid-state route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, consistent with Na₃AsS₃ at 220 °C and Na₃PS₄ at 350/750 °C (CS-3; CS-2 / SS-1 / TS-1). The score is reduced because sealed vacuum/inert ampoules, volatile sulfur, regrinding/reannealing, and air-sensitive sulfides are expected, all of which are supported by Na₃PS₄ and related sulfide procedures (CS-2 / SS-1 / TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Na₂SiS₃ volatility or decomposition limits, so that part is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Na₂S + SiS₂ should be more kinetically matched than elemental Si because both are sulfides [Reasonable Inference], and binary sulfide + alkali sulfide reaction is directly exemplified by Na₃AsS₃ from As₂S₃ + Na₂S (CS-3). Na₃PS₄ requires long heating and controlled processing for single-phase powders (CS-2 / SS-1 / TS-1), so ordering/reconstruction is not trivial. The target’s corner-sharing SiS₄ framework is plausible by analogy to PS₄ tetrahedral sulfides, but exact structural reconstruction evidence for Na–Si–S is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct elemental reaction is supported at 925 K for Na₃GaS₃ in evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules (SS-3 / TS-3), so no extreme temperature or pressure penalty is needed. The main deductions come from vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, sodium reactivity, and the likely need for staged heating to prevent runaway Na–S reaction [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not provide exact safety or pressure data for Na + Si + S, so those details remain inferred.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Direct elemental synthesis is feasible in a close Na–Ga–S structural/thermodynamic analogue (SS-3 / TS-3), and molten Na/S-containing intermediates may assist diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, elemental Si is expected to be less reactive than SiS₂, giving a lower precursor-activity score [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction from elements is more demanding than from preformed sulfides, and CS/SS/TS do not show a direct Na–Si–S elemental synthesis.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂SiS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, and many close Na–S thiometallate analogues are also on the hull (CS-1–CS-3; SS-1–SS-3; TS-1–TS-3). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support sealed-tube sulfide chemistry, especially Na₃PS₄, Na₂PS₃, Na₃AsS₃, and Na₃GaS₃, but none provides an exact Na–Si–S synthesis, so the final route design involves [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is the use of pre-reacted sulfide precursors or sulfide flux to provide high sulfur activity and accelerate formation of SiS₄ units, consistent with sulfide and flux analogues (CS-3; SS-5 / TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control under sealed-tube conditions because sulfur volatility, sodium reactivity, and moisture sensitivity are repeatedly noted in related systems (CS-2 / SS-1 / TS-1; SS-5 / TS-5). Overall, synthesis is feasible and moderately likely, with flux or sulfide solid-state routes preferred over direct elemental or CVT approaches.
