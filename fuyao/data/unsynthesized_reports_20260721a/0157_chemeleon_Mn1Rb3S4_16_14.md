### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which directly places it at the top of the required thermodynamic stability range. CS provides strong supporting evidence because several chemically similar Rb/K sulfides are also on the hull, including Rb₂MoS₄, Rb₂GeS₃, K₂MoS₄, and K₃GaS₃ (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). TS also supports the stability of alkali chalcogenide phases because Cs₂S₂, Rb₆HgSe₄, Rb₃SbSe₃, Rb₃BiSe₃, and Rb₆HgS₄ all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). SS is less definitive because several structural analogues are slightly above hull but still low energy and reported synthesized, such as Rb₂CoS₂ at 0.0476 eV/atom (SS-1).

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized for sealed vacuum/Ar handling because Rb₂GeS₃ uses an evacuated sealed tube (CS-2), and Rb₆HgS₄ uses sealed Ar ampoules (TS-5). It is also penalized for volatile sulfur and air-sensitive sulfides because sulfur is used in multiple sulfide syntheses (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4), and alkali chalcogenides/products require inert handling in analogues (SS-1, SS-4, TS-5). It avoids high external pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C in the proposed program [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Binary sulfide precursors should reduce reconstruction compared with elemental Mn/Rb/S [Reasonable Inference], and Rb₂S-based syntheses are directly demonstrated for Rb₂GeS₃ and Rb₆HgS₄ (CS-2, TS-5). The target’s tetrahedral MnS₄ motifs resemble tetrahedral MoS₄ and GeS₄ units in Rb₂MoS₄ and Rb₂GeS₃ (CS-1, CS-2). The remaining uncertainty is the target’s specific triclinic arrangement and S–S bond, which are not directly matched by the binary-sulfide analogues [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is penalized for vacuum/sealed-ampoule operation because elemental K/Ga/S synthesis of K₃GaS₃ uses evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules (CS-5). It is penalized for volatile sulfur/Rb and air-sensitive Rb metal [Reasonable Inference]. It is not penalized for >1000 °C because the proposed 650–800 °C range is below that threshold and the CS-5 precedent is 1025 K, i.e., about 752 °C.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Direct elemental reaction has lower precursor activity because Mn metal is a dense, relatively slow-reacting precursor [Reasonable Inference]. Elemental synthesis is nevertheless plausible because K₃GaS₃ was made directly from elements at high temperature (CS-5), and alkali-metal sulfide chemistry from elements is known in Cs₂S₂ albeit under harsh ammonia conditions (TS-1/SS-2). The key uncertainty is whether molten/volatile Rb–S phases can sufficiently transport Mn into the target P1 structure before competing binaries form [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 is penalized for reactive H₂/S atmosphere because Rb₂CoS₂/K₂CoS₂ and Rb₃SbSe₃/Rb₃BiSe₃ analogues require hydrogen streams charged with chalcogen (SS-1, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). It is also penalized for likely regrinding/reannealing because halide/solid-state analogues can require intermediate grindings to achieve single phase (CS-3), although the H₂/S cobalt routes themselves are relatively simple (SS-1, SS-4). It avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The carbonate + metal + chalcogen approach is supported by alkali cobalt sulfide syntheses at 1030 K for 10 h (SS-1, SS-4) and by Rb selenide syntheses from oxides/carbonates in selenium-loaded hydrogen (TS-3, TS-4). However, carbonate conversion and Mn sulfiding add kinetic and redox complexity relative to using preformed Rb₂S/MnS [Reasonable Inference]. The route may be useful for bulk powder but could struggle with exact Rb:S stoichiometry and disulfide formation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
Route 4 benefits from low temperatures because Rb₂MoS₄ forms at 393 K for 7 d in ethylenediamine (CS-1), and K₂MoS₄ forms at 443 K for 2 d in methanol (CS-4). It is penalized for sealed volatile solvent/S systems and likely air-sensitive sulfide handling [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids external H₂/NH₃ gas flow and extreme high pressure, unlike the Cs₂S₂ ammonia route at 2000–3000 bar (TS-1/SS-2).

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
Solution-mediated transport should improve precursor activity and ion mobility [Reasonable Inference], consistent with Rb₂MoS₄ and K₂MoS₄ crystallizing at much lower temperatures than solid-state sulfides (CS-1, CS-4). The major kinetic/phase-selectivity concern is that CS-1 reports Rb₂MoS₄ as a minor phase with Ag₂S as the main product, so crystallization may be sensitive to solvent, mineralizer, and competing sulfides. There is insufficient evidence from SS for solvothermal Rb–Mn–S synthesis, so SS is treated as neutral for this route-specific kinetic factor.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target triggers the cation-size mismatch criterion because Rb and Mn occupy very different coordination environments and ionic sizes [Reasonable Inference], and the target description explicitly has six-coordinate Rb environments and tetrahedral MnS₄. The atom count criterion is not triggered because the described structural contents are 16 atoms. The mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered from the supplied target description because only corner-sharing MnS₄ tetrahedra are stated; there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to claim the target itself contains edge- and face-sharing coexistence, so that check is not applied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₃MnS₄ is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports Rb/K thiometallate and sulfide synthesis with similar Rb–S distances and tetrahedral M–S building units, especially Rb₂MoS₄ and Rb₂GeS₃ (CS-1, CS-2). The TS evidence supports stable alkali chalcogenide chemistry but does not directly include Mn, so it mainly supports thermodynamic and handling expectations rather than exact chemistry (TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with known Rb sulfide/thiometallate formation pathways. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target has low symmetry, multiple inequivalent Rb/S sites, and an S–S bond, and no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Rb–Mn–S at the Rb₃MnS₄ stoichiometry.
