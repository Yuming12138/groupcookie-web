### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for predicted ground-state stability from the target data. TS provides supportive context because several thermodynamically similar Cr sulfides also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including CdCr₂S₄ and ACr₅S₈ phases (TS-1–TS-5). CS and SS show stable or near-stable Cr sulfide analogues, but exact target stability cannot be proven from those analogues alone, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS for target-specific phase-equilibrium details beyond general support.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because related sulfides require evacuated sealed ampoules and high-temperature annealing at roughly 800–1000 °C (CS-2, CS-5). The need for vacuum sealing and repeated grinding follows directly from related Cr sulfide syntheses (CS-4, SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is the sulfur vapor pressure and indium volatility in the actual Cr–In–S system, which are [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly reported in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfide precursors should be more reactive than direct metals and avoid some sulfurization burden [Reasonable Inference], and binary sulfide precursor use is directly supported by CS-2. The target’s spinel lattice is strongly supported by spinel CdCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ analogues with very similar CrS₆ octahedra and tetrahedral A-site sulfide coordination (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, TS-1). Kinetic uncertainty remains because Cr-containing sulfides often require multi-day anneals and recycling to reach single phase (CS-4, SS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Direct elemental reaction is supported by related metal + sulfur syntheses in evacuated tubes (CS-4, SS-1). Its severity is comparable to Route 1 for vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur management, but the prereaction sequence must be more cautious because elemental sulfur is volatile and reactive [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS provide no direct evidence for elemental In behavior in this target, so that part is an uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic score is lower because elemental Cr is refractory and slow to react [Reasonable Inference], consistent with related syntheses requiring several-day heating and repeated recycling (CS-4, SS-1). Formation from elements requires complete structural assembly into the spinel phase, unlike Route 1 where sulfide bonding is preformed [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains plausible because direct metal–sulfur methods have produced related Cr–M–S phases (CS-4, SS-1).

#### F2 — Route 3

H₂S sulfurization is more severe because dry H₂S is toxic/corrosive and requires a controlled gas stream, directly reported for chromium sulfide syntheses (SS-3–SS-5, TS-2–TS-5). Temperatures of 900–1100 °C are common in those analogues (SS-3–SS-5, TS-2–TS-5). The main uncertainty is whether the alkali flux or H₂S chemistry will stabilize the desired ternary spinel rather than alkali chromium sulfides, since the closest examples are not Cr₂InS₄.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux and H₂S routes offer high precursor activity and shorter reaction times for Cr sulfides, as shown by 7–12 h to 12 h syntheses in H₂S/flux environments (SS-3–SS-5, TS-2–TS-5). CrS₆ octahedral motifs are common in these analogues, which supports local bonding transferability (SS-3–SS-5, TS-2–TS-5). However, because these examples are layered/alkali chromium sulfides rather than spinels, structural reconstruction to Cr₂InS₄ remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4

Halide/chalcogenide flux growth avoids >1000 °C operation but still requires evacuated ampoules, volatile components, and flux handling, consistent with high-temperature solution growth in evacuated silica ampoules (CS-3). Toxicity/moisture sensitivity of proposed halide fluxes is a [Reasonable Inference] based on common halide behavior, not directly established by CS/SS/TS for Cr₂InS₄. The CS-3 synthesis metadata are partly inconsistent, so this route receives a penalty despite favorable transport.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux growth should provide the best mass transport and crystallization kinetics [Reasonable Inference], and CS-3 reports slow-cooling solution growth at 780 °C. The structural target is strongly analogous to spinel CoCr₂S₄ in CS-3, but the precursor list in CS-3 does not clearly match that product, giving insufficient evidence from CS-3 for an exact transferable recipe. Thus the kinetics may be favorable, but chemical optimization risk remains high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline spinel with a conventional 56-atom cell, so the atom-count check is triggered [Reasonable Inference from target Fd-3m spinel]. The target contains corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but not face-sharing polyhedra according to the target structural description, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. Spinel analogues CdCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ indicate that similar sulfide polyhedral networks crystallize readily rather than amorphize (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, TS-1), but no CS/SS/TS source directly reports amorphization behavior for Cr₂InS₄.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cr₂InS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Cr sulfides are also on the hull (target data; TS-1–TS-5). The analogue support is strong structurally because CdCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ are true thiospinels with the same Fd-3m CrS₆/octahedral and tetrahedral-cation framework (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the robust thiospinel structural family, which suggests that Cr–S octahedral networks can accommodate different tetrahedral cations (CS-1, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is controlling sulfur chemical potential and mixed Cr valence while avoiding competing Cr sulfides or In sulfide segregation [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible, with sealed sulfide solid-state reaction as the most defensible first attempt.
