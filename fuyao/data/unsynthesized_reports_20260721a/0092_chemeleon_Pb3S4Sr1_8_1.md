### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability bin and justifies a near-maximum F1 score. Many relevant analogues are also computed stable, including CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, SS1/TS1, SS4/TS2, SS5/TS3, and TS4, supporting the idea that Pb-rich sulfides can be thermodynamic ground states. The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not include the exact Sr–Pb–S phase diagram, so **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for competing SrS–PbS ordered phases.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses vacuum-sealed ampoule conditions analogous to SS5/TS3, SS4/TS2, and TS4, so the controlled-atmosphere deduction is required. The temperature is below 1000 °C, avoiding the high-temperature penalty, and the equipment is standard for solid-state sulfides according to SS4/TS2 and SS5/TS3. Deductions are mainly from vacuum sealing, sulfur/PbS volatility risk, and toxic/air-sensitive PbS/SrS handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

PbS is an excellent kinetic precursor because its octahedral Pb–S framework and 2.98–3.00 Å Pb–S distances closely match the target’s ~3.01 Å Pb–S distances (SS1/TS1). Related Pb sulfides form at 600–800 °C or 800 °C in sealed tubes (SS5/TS3, TS4), and more complex Pb/Tl/Zr sulfides form at 1073 K with long holds (SS4/TS2). The main uncertainty is whether Sr/Pb long-range ordering in the P4/mmm Caswellsilverite-like lattice equilibrates rapidly or requires repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Elemental sealed-ampoule reactions are well precedented for sulfides in CS1, CS2, CS5, SS4/TS2, and SS5/TS3. The proposed temperature is kept at or below 950 °C, avoiding the >1000 °C deduction, but sulfur volatility requires sealed-tube handling, and Sr/Pb introduce air-sensitivity/toxicity penalties [Reasonable Inference]. The route is more operationally hazardous than Route 1 because elemental Sr and sulfur can react strongly during heat-up [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental and melt-assisted reactions can be kinetically favorable because liquid Pb and high sulfur activity promote rapid sulfide formation [Reasonable Inference], and CS1–CS2 demonstrate melt/slow-cooling routes for chemically similar sulfides. Slow cooling is important in CS2 and SS4/TS2, suggesting crystallization and phase selection are sensitive. The uncertainty is that melt growth may favor binaries such as PbS and SrS unless the ternary ordering is thermodynamically and kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT requires a prereaction plus a second sealed-tube gradient step, as in CS3 and SS2/SS3, so it receives multi-step and controlled-atmosphere deductions. I₂ or chlorine transport agents are volatile/reactive, and Pb/Sr precursors add toxicity/air-sensitivity concerns [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore more severe operationally than direct solid-state synthesis despite moderate temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can be excellent for crystal growth because it bypasses bulk diffusion, as shown for PbHfS₃ in a 1043–983 K gradient using chlorine transport (CS3) and PbS-related phases using iodine/chlorine transport (SS2, SS3). If polycrystalline SrPb₃S₄ feed is used, the reconstruction burden should be modest [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is congruent Sr transport; **insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** directly supports Sr-containing sulfide CVT, so phase/composition control may be limiting.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure is a simple octahedral sulfide network with corner and edge sharing but no reported face-sharing, which gives a low amorphization-risk signature from the target description. Structurally similar PbS has untilted edge/corner-sharing octahedra (SS1/TS1), supporting the view that such networks crystallize readily rather than forming amorphous solids. Direct CS/SS/TS evidence for Sr/Pb cation-radius mismatch or unit-cell atom count is insufficient, so those checks rely on [Reasonable Inference] and are treated conservatively.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPb₃S₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally/thermodynamically related sulfides also have E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS4/TS2, SS5/TS3). The strongest analogue support comes from Pb–S octahedral structural similarity to PbS (SS1/TS1) and from sealed-tube synthesis of Pb-rich ternary sulfides at 600–850 °C (SS5/TS3, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match between PbS-like octahedral Pb–S bonding and the target’s uniform ~3.01 Å Pb/Sr–S octahedra (SS1/TS1 plus target description). The most critical limiting factor is Sr incorporation and Sr/Pb ordering, because none of CS/SS/TS directly reports Sr–Pb–S synthesis or Sr-containing CVT. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable as a bulk sulfide, with the binary-sulfide sealed-ampoule route preferred.
