### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂Co₃S₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many nearby Rb/K/Cs–Ni/Co–S analogues are stable or nearly stable (target; CS-1, CS-2, CS-3; SS-1, SS-3; TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The strongest support comes from Rb₂Ni₃S₄, which has the same Rb₂M₃S₄ stoichiometry, Fmmm symmetry, and square-planar M–S sheets as the target, making Ni→Co substitution a plausible but not guaranteed extension (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of flux/fusion routes for A₂M₃S₄ sulfides at moderate temperatures under Ar, which should help overcome Co diffusion limitations (CS-2; SS-1; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition from known alkali cobalt sulfides with different stoichiometries and CoS₄ connectivities, especially Rb(CoS)₂ and K₂CoS₂-type materials (CS-1; CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). Overall, synthesis is feasible but will likely require careful Rb:S excess and sulfur fugacity control.
