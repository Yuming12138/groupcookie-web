### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin and justifies F1 = 98. Similar sulfides also show strong thermodynamic support: KPt₂S₃ is on the hull (CS1 / SS5 / TS4), Ir₂S₃ is on the hull (SS3 / TS2), IrS₂ is on the hull (SS4 / TS3), and CuRh₂S₄ is on the hull (SS2 / TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially in Cs–Ir–S where no exact experimental analogue is provided; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact CsIr₂S₄ phase.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state reaction

Route 1 uses conditions similar to Ir₂S₃ and IrS₂ syntheses, which require evacuated sealed quartz/silica tubes and high temperatures around 900–960 °C (SS3 / TS2, SS4 / TS3). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multi-step heating/regrinding, and air-sensitive Cs sulfide handling [Reasonable Inference]. The method avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, which keeps it feasible. The main uncertainty is that SS/TS provide direct evidence for Ir–S binaries but insufficient evidence for Cs incorporation by direct solid-state reaction.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state reaction

Ir metal is refractory and slow-reacting; IrS₂ synthesis requires two weeks at 960 °C plus grinding/refiring for homogeneity (SS4 / TS3). The target shares IrS₆ octahedral chemistry with IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃ (SS3 / TS2, SS4 / TS3), but inserting Cs and forming the monoclinic CsIr₂S₄ topology likely requires substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore kinetics are expected to be poor-to-moderate, with F3 = 33. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for rapid direct formation of any Cs–Ir–S ternary.

#### F2 — Route 2: alkali sulfide / carbonate sulfur flux growth

Route 2 is based on KPt₂S₃ flux growth at 800–900 °C using alkali carbonate and sulfur, followed by slow cooling and water treatment (CS1 / SS5 / TS4). Severity deductions arise from sealed handling of volatile sulfur, controlled atmosphere recommended for Cs sulfide/polysulfide chemistry, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, so the overall condition severity is moderate. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact Cs polysulfide flux behavior with Ir, but the K/Pt/S flux evidence is the strongest available transfer basis.

#### F3 — Route 2: alkali sulfide / carbonate sulfur flux growth

Flux growth should improve kinetics relative to direct solid-state reaction by dissolving/reacting Ir or IrS₂ in an alkali sulfur-rich medium; KPt₂S₃ crystallizes from such a flux at 800–900 °C (CS1 / SS5 / TS4). The structural match is favorable because KPt₂S₃ contains alkali–S coordination and edge-sharing noble-metal sulfide octahedra with M–S distances close to the target Ir–S distances (CS1 / SS5 / TS4). However, Pt and Ir differ in redox chemistry, and Cs is larger than K, so phase selection remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. F3 = 62 reflects a moderate kinetic outlook.

#### F2 — Route 3: Cs-halide flux

Route 3 borrows from BaCl₂ flux synthesis of Ba₆Hf₅S₁₆ and Ba₅Hf₄S₁₃ at 1050 °C for 20 h under vacuum (CS2, CS3). It receives stronger severity penalties because the temperature likely exceeds 1000 °C and still requires vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur containment, drybox loading, and flux washing [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains feasible with standard sealed-ampoule equipment, as CS2 and CS3 use silica ampoules and drybox systems. Insufficient evidence from CS2/CS3 for direct Cs–Ir–S chemistry makes this a secondary route.

#### F3 — Route 3: Cs-halide flux

Halide flux improves mass transport, as shown by Ba/Hf/S sulfide phase formation in BaCl₂ flux at 1050 °C (CS2, CS3). Nevertheless, those analogues are structurally and chemically less close than KPt₂S₃, so the transferability to monoclinic CsIr₂S₄ is only moderate. Ir remains refractory, and Ir-containing target crystallization may require careful sulfur activity control [Reasonable Inference]. F3 = 58 reflects better kinetics than direct solid-state reaction but weaker analogy than alkali sulfide flux.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The largest amorphization risk is the large cation size/coordination mismatch between Cs and Ir, which triggers C1 by [Reasonable Inference] from ionic-radius chemistry. The target appears to have a relatively simple monoclinic crystallographic description, so C2 is not triggered; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell size. The target has edge- and corner-sharing motifs but no reported coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so C3 is not triggered, although Ir₂S₃ demonstrates that face-sharing IrS₆ networks can exist in related Ir sulfides (SS3 / TS2). F4 = 80 indicates low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsIr₂S₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related sulfides are also on the hull (CS1 / SS5 / TS4; SS3 / TS2; SS4 / TS3). The analogue support is strongest for alkali/noble-metal sulfide flux chemistry, especially KPt₂S₃, which combines alkali–S coordination with edge-sharing MS₆ octahedra (CS1 / SS5 / TS4). The most critical enabling factor is use of a sulfur-rich alkali flux, which should enhance Ir sulfide dissolution and Cs incorporation compared with slow direct diffusion [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1 / SS5 / TS4]. The main limiting factor is phase competition with stable IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ binaries, both of which are known stable Ir sulfides (SS3 / TS2, SS4 / TS3). Stoichiometry and sulfur chemical potential control will likely determine whether CsIr₂S₄ forms instead of binary Ir sulfides or Cs polysulfide residues [Reasonable Inference].
