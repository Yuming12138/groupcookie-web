### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided hull data. Several chemically or thermodynamically similar Cs/Rb sulfides are also at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Cs₄In₂S₅, Cs₃BS₃, Cs₂S, Cs₂S₂, and Rb₆HgS₄ (CS2; CS3; SS5; TS1; TS2; TS3; TS4; TS5). Cs₂CoS₂ and Rb₂CoS₂ are slightly metastable yet experimentally synthesized, so small positive hull values are not prohibitive in this chemical family (CS1; SS1; SS2). Uncertainty remains because Fe³⁺ sulfide redox chemistry may introduce competing FeS/FeS₂/Cs₂S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: carbonate–Fe–S under H₂/chalcogen vapor

Condition severity is moderate because the analogue route uses 1030 K and H₂/chalcogen vapor but no high pressure or >1000 °C heating (CS1; SS1; SS2; SS4). Kinetics are favorable relative to ordinary metal diffusion because the Co analogues form in only 10 h in flowing H₂/S vapor, suggesting reactive transport (CS1; SS1; SS2; SS4). Structural transfer is strong for tetrahedral transition-metal sulfide units but incomplete because the target Cs₃FeS₃ stoichiometry differs from Cs₂CoS₂/Rb₂CoS₂ (target; CS1; SS1; SS2). TS provides insufficient direct evidence for this H₂ carbonate route, so TS is neutral for the route-specific condition score.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: sealed-ampoule solid-state sulfide route

Condition severity is lower in temperature but higher in handling burden because Cs₂S, sulfur, sealed low-pressure tubes, and inert conditions are required (CS3; SS5; TS2; TS5). Kinetics are moderate because sulfide precursors are chemically closer to the product than oxides, and Cs₃BS₃ forms at 720 °C in a sealed tube (CS3; TS5). The major uncertainty is Fe incorporation into FeS₄ tetrahedra rather than formation of binary iron sulfides or Cs₂S/Cs₂S₂ impurities [Reasonable Inference], since CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct Cs–Fe–S synthesized analogue.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: azide route

The azide route has moderate temperature but challenging precursor and pressure management because CsN₃ decomposes at 270–350 °C and reactions are sealed under vacuum/inert atmosphere (CS2; TS4). Kinetically, CsN₃ is attractive because it generates reactive Cs without handling elemental Cs, as explicitly stated for heavier alkali metals (CS2; TS4). Structural confidence is only moderate because Cs₄In₂S₅ is a Cs–In–S compound with InS₄ tetrahedra but not the same Cs₃FeS₃ stoichiometry or Fe redox chemistry (CS2; TS4; target). SS provides insufficient direct azide-route signal and is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: alkali sulfide/polysulfide flux

The flux route has the best combined F2/F3 because Rb₂PtS₂ synthesis demonstrates alkali sulfide/polysulfide fusion at 950 °C under dry oxygen-free N₂, avoiding H₂ and high pressure (CS4). Kinetics are favorable because a molten sulfur-rich alkali flux should increase mass transport and help crystallization [Reasonable Inference], consistent with flux/fusion success in CS4 and ampoule thiometallate synthesis in TS3. The primary uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS4 is Rb–Pt–S, not Cs–Fe–S, so the route is transferable by chemical analogy rather than direct demonstration. SS provides insufficient direct flux evidence for Cs₃FeS₃ and is neutral for flux-specific structural matching.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Cs sites and FeS₄ tetrahedra, which triggers the ionic-radius-disparity check by [Reasonable Inference] from standard ionic radii and the target coordination description. The target also has mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, explicitly described for S-centered and Cs/Fe-centered polyhedra (target). Complex crystalline analogues with multiple inequivalent Cs sites and mixed connectivity are known, including Cs₄In₂S₅ and Cs₃BS₃, which reduces concern that complexity necessarily causes amorphization (CS2; CS3; TS4; TS5). Unit-cell atom count cannot be scored because insufficient evidence is provided from CS/SS/TS for the target total cell size.

Cs₃FeS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, and many Cs-rich sulfide analogues are also on the hull (target; CS2; CS3; TS1; TS2; TS3; TS5). The CS/SS evidence strongly supports the existence of crystalline alkali transition-metal sulfides with tetrahedral MS₄ units, especially Cs₂CoS₂/Rb₂CoS₂ and K₆CoS₄ (CS1; SS1; SS2; SS4). The most important enabling factor is the strong Cs–S/transition-metal–S structural compatibility: target Cs–S and Fe–S bond lengths are chemically comparable to analogue Cs/Rb–S and Co–S distances (target; CS1; SS1; SS2). The most important limiting factor is phase and redox control for Fe, since no provided CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates Cs₃FeS₃ synthesis and Fe may form competing sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears substantially synthesizable, with flux or sulfide ampoule routes preferred over oxide-like conventional ceramic synthesis.
