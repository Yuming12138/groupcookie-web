### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the supplied target data, so it falls at the top of the prescribed 90–100 range. This is reinforced by several close analogues that are also on or essentially on the hull: TaTe2, TaOsTe4, TaIrTe4, TaRhTe4, NbIrTe4, and Nb3Te4 have E_hull = 0.0 in the provided evidence, while Ta4Pd3Te16 is essentially stable at 5.1 × 10^-5 eV/atom (SS-1/TS-1; CS-1/2/3/4/5; TS-2/3/4/5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or cation ordering in the Ta/Nb sublattice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses conditions directly comparable to NbIrTe4 and Ta4Pd3Te16: evacuated sealed tubes, 1000 °C, and 12 days or longer reaction schedules (CS-1, CS-4). The severity deductions come from vacuum, volatile Te in a sealed system, multistep heating/regrinding, and Te toxicity/handling risk [Reasonable Inference; Te-containing sealed-tube procedures in CS-1/CS-4]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a direct dry solid-state TaNbTe4 synthesis, so SS/TS are neutral for the exact condition severity of this route beyond supporting related telluride stability.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The main kinetic penalty is the use of refractory Ta and Nb metal powders, which generally have slow interdiffusion [Reasonable Inference], even though elemental powders are successful in NbIrTe4 and Ta4Pd3Te16 analogues (CS-1, CS-4). Repeated heating and regrinding in NbIrTe4 and impurity phases in Ta4Pd3Te16 indicate phase formation and purification can be slow or imperfect (CS-1, CS-4). Structural similarity to molybdenite-like TaTe2 supports the final framework, but not necessarily fast Ta/Nb ordering (SS-1/TS-1; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C because relevant CVT analogues use 873→773 K for TaTe2 or 820/700 °C for layered tellurides (SS-1/TS-1; CS-2/3/5). The severity arises from vacuum ampoule work, volatile Te and halogen transport agents, multistep two-zone operation, and corrosive/toxic I2 or TeBr4 handling (SS-1/TS-1; CS-2/3/5; [Reasonable Inference]). This is moderate rather than extreme because no high pressure, plasma, or >1400 °C conditions are involved, and two-zone CVT is established in the analogues (SS-1/TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

CVT receives the best kinetics score because halogen transport can reduce reliance on solid-state diffusion by generating mobile vapor species [Reasonable Inference], and this is directly supported by TaTe2 single-crystal growth using I2 (SS-1/TS-1). Multiple layered tellurides are described under TeBr4 CVT-like conditions, supporting crystal growth of related Te-rich layered phases (CS-2/3/5; TS-2/3/4). The main uncertainty is whether Ta and Nb transport rates and incorporation are sufficiently matched to produce ordered TaNbTe4 rather than TaTe2/NbTe2-like intergrowths [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The Te-rich flux/molten-salt route uses sealed evacuated quartz conditions and ~1000 °C, consistent with molten-salt Nb chalcogenide synthesis and Te-rich Ta4Pd3Te16 synthesis (TS-5, CS-4). Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Te self-flux, multistep flux removal, and Te/halide handling hazards [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Te-rich flux synthesis of the exact target, so SS is neutral for this route’s condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Molten salt or Te-rich flux should improve cation transport relative to dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], and TS-5 demonstrates molten-salt synthesis of niobium chalcogenides at 1000 °C. However, TS-5 concerns Nb3Te4-related phases rather than TaNbTe4, and CS-4 shows that Te-rich Ta telluride synthesis can still produce impurity phases. Thus the kinetics are judged better than direct solid-state but less certain than CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target uses chemically similar Ta and Nb cations, so a large cation-size mismatch is not expected [Reasonable Inference], and the target structure reports comparable Ta–Te and Nb–Te bond distances. The reported structure is an ordered 2D monoclinic C2/m layered phase with edge-sharing octahedral motifs rather than a highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network; similar edge-sharing layered motifs occur in TaTe2 (SS-1/TS-1). The main uncertainty is the exact conventional cell size and any hidden disorder, because CS/SS/TS do not provide the target cell atom count directly.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaNbTe4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar tellurides are also on the hull (CS-1/2/3/5; SS-1; TS-1/2/3/4/5). The analogue support is strong for layered telluride formation and especially strong for CVT, since molybdenite-like C2/m TaTe2 is directly grown by I2 transport (SS-1/TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high Te chemical potential in a sealed ampoule, which suppresses Te loss and supports layered telluride growth [Reasonable Inference; CS-1/CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is slow and potentially uneven Ta/Nb incorporation or ordering, because both are refractory metals and direct analogues show long reaction times, regrinding, or impurity phases (CS-1, CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, synthesis is feasible, with CVT the most rational first attempt.
