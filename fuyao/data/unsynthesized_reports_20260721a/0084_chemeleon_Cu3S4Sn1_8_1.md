### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, placing it on the computed convex hull and justifying the maximum thermodynamic score (target data). Thermodynamic analogues also have excellent stability: Cu2SnS3 is essentially on hull at 0.00049 eV/atom, while Cu2GeS3, Cu2ZnSnS4, and Cu2ZnGeS4 are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS rank 1–5). CS and SS repeat the same near-hull Cu2SnS3 and related tetrahedral sulfide evidence, so there is no negative thermodynamic signal from CS/SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic stability but whether the predicted tetragonal Cu3SnS4 ordering is experimentally selected over nearby Cu–Sn–S polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solvothermal

The condition severity is low because the closest analogue Cu2SnS3 forms at 453 K / 180 °C for 20 h in a standard Teflon-lined autoclave with no special atmosphere (CS/SS/TS Cu2SnS3). Deductions are mainly for the multi-step wet-chemical workflow and thiourea/CuCl handling risks [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because soluble chlorides and thiourea provide high precursor activity and avoid slow bulk diffusion, as directly shown by Cu2SnS3 formation under mild conditions (CS/SS/TS Cu2SnS3). The uncertainty is Cu-rich phase selectivity, since the demonstrated analogue is Cu2SnS3 rather than Cu3SnS4 (CS/SS/TS Cu2SnS3; target).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Solid-state ampoule

The condition severity is moderate because structurally similar sulfides require evacuated sealed ampoules, staged heating, and long annealing at 600–900 °C (CS rank 3; SS rank 4–5; TS rank 3). Vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur introduce clear deductions, and repeated grinding/annealing is supported by analogue procedures (CS rank 3; SS rank 4–5; TS rank 3). Kinetics are less favorable than solution routes because elemental Cu/Sn are denser and solid-state ordering is slow; analogues require days to weeks or multiple annealing steps (CS rank 3; SS rank 4–5; TS rank 3). The route is robust for bulk phase formation but may need long anneals to avoid cation disorder and secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Microwave-assisted solution synthesis

The condition severity is low because the CZTS thermodynamic analogue was produced at 180 °C for 20 min in ethylene glycol with no special atmosphere reported (TS rank 4). The route still receives deductions for multiple processing steps and thiourea/chloride handling risks [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, this route is very favorable because metal chlorides and thiourea form sulfide nanocrystals rapidly under microwave heating (TS rank 4). The main uncertainty is transferability from Cu2ZnSnS4 to Zn-free, Cu-rich Cu3SnS4, where cation ratio control may be narrower [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Iodine CVT

The condition severity is higher because iodine CVT requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile iodine/sulfur species, and a high-temperature gradient near 777–867 °C in the structural analogue (SS rank 3). Kinetics for crystal growth can be good once transport species form, and SS rank 3 demonstrates single-crystal growth of a tetrahedral Cu sulfide by iodine transport. However, direct Cu–Sn–S CVT evidence is insufficient from CS and TS, so the transferability is weaker than for solvothermal or microwave routes. Composition control is the largest kinetic/practical risk because Cu, Sn, and S may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target framework consists of corner-sharing CuS4 and SnS4 tetrahedra and SCu3Sn tetrahedra, with no reported edge- or face-sharing coexistence, so the connectivity check is low risk (target). The cation size mismatch is not expected to exceed the 15% trigger for tetrahedral Cu/Sn coordination [Reasonable Inference], and the target formula/description does not suggest a >40 atom unit cell [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide many crystalline tetrahedral sulfide analogues synthesized by solvothermal, solid-state, microwave, and CVT methods, indirectly supporting low amorphization risk (CS/SS/TS). Remaining uncertainty is nanoscale disorder or cation disorder in low-temperature products, not full amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu3SnS4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stable phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom (target data). The closest analogue support is unusually strong: Cu2SnS3 appears simultaneously as the top chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue and is experimentally synthesized by a mild solvothermal route (CS rank 1–2; SS rank 1–2; TS rank 1–2). The most critical enabling factor is the shared corner-sharing CuS4/SnS4 tetrahedral sulfide network with nearly matching Cu–S and Sn–S bond lengths between target and analogues (target; CS/SS/TS Cu2SnS3; TS rank 4). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity at Cu-rich composition, because the strongest direct evidence is for Cu2SnS3 and Cu2ZnSnS4 rather than Cu3SnS4 itself (CS/SS/TS Cu2SnS3; TS rank 4; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, synthesis should be feasible, with solvothermal or microwave solution routes best for rapid screening and sealed-ampoule annealing best for confirming bulk equilibrium phase formation [Reasonable Inference from CS/SS/TS route evidence].
