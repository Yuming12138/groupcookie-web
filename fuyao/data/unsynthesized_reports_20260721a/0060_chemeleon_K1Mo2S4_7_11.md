### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target data. This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues MoS₂ at E_hull = 0 or near-zero in TS1/TS2 and by chemically related K/Rb/Cs transition-metal compounds at E_hull = 0 in CS1/CS2/CS4/CS5. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a broad finite-temperature stability field or easy kinetic access [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because evacuated sealed-tube heating is required, as in SS5/TS2/TS3, and sulfur volatility requires containment. Its kinetic score is favorable because MoS₂ already provides the nearest Mo–S framework, supported by SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2. The largest uncertainty is whether K insertion reaches the ordered K(MoS₂)₂ stoichiometry rather than forming K-intercalated nonstoichiometric MoS₂ or residual K₂S [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized by the use of dry H₂S, which is explicitly used in CS4/CS5 but is toxic and requires controlled gas handling. Flux chemistry improves mass transport, as shown for K–Cr sulfides in CS4/CS5, but Mo powder is refractory and the target structure is not the same as the Cr sulfide analogues. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows direct formation of K–Mo–S by this method, so the structural-reconstruction and phase-selectivity components remain uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is harsher because it combines vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/iodine, long gradients, and possibly >1000 °C conditions, all supported for MoS₂/Mo–S CVT in SS2/TS2/TS3. Kinetic barriers are reduced by vapor transport, but the provided data give insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that K is transported and incorporated congruently with Mo and S. The main risk is formation of KI or loss of K from the growth zone [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has moderate-to-challenging severity due to sealed molten salt/polysulfide handling, based on CS1 molten KCl/LiCl synthesis, CS3 flux growth, and CS4 polysulfide melt chemistry. Kinetics are attractive because molten flux can accelerate diffusion and enable crystal growth, while MoS₂ can preserve local Mo–S motifs (SS1/SS2). The uncertainty is possible halide contamination or competition from known K–Mo sulfides/polysulfides [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms halide-flux growth of K(MoS₂)₂.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization risk is the large size/coordination mismatch between K and Mo sites, reflected by K–S distances around 3.17–3.20 Å and Mo–S distances around 2.43–2.44 Å in the target structure. The target does have mixed edge and corner connectivity, but not explicit vertex+edge+face coexistence, so the strict C3 criterion is not triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms a >40 atom unit cell, so C2 is treated as non-trigger/neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(MoS₂)₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and multiple Mo–S and alkali-transition-metal analogues are also on the hull (TS1/TS2, CS1/CS2/CS4/CS5). The strongest support comes from structural and thermodynamic similarity to MoS₂, since the target retains MoS₂-like MoS₆ units with nearly identical Mo–S distances (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is use of preformed MoS₂ or a sulfur-rich flux to preserve the Mo–S framework while inserting/ordering K [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control: K volatility/moisture sensitivity and competing Mo-rich sulfides under reducing conditions are plausible risks, with Mo-rich sulfide formation supported by SS3/SS4. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with Route 1 giving the best balance of thermodynamic driving force, structural continuity, and manageable conditions.
