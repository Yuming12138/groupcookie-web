### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 score range. Thermodynamically similar sulfides/selenides in TS include several E_hull = 0.0 phases, such as Cu₁₂Sb₄S₁₃, CuS, AuCuSe₄, and CuSe₂ (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar thiospinels are often near the hull, with CS1–CS4 around 0.06–0.08 eV/atom and CS5 at 0.0 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee the specific R3m cation ordering will form rather than a cubic or disordered spinel polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits moderate severity from direct sealed-tube sulfide synthesis: CS1 uses 1000 °C under vacuum in sealed carbon-coated quartz, while TS2 demonstrates that sulfur-containing Cu reactions require evacuated sealed tubes. The route avoids external pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C if optimized at 850–950 °C [Reasonable Inference]. The main deductions are vacuum, sulfur volatility, and multiple heating/grinding/annealing steps, consistent with CS1/CS4/SS3. Insufficient evidence from TS for Cu–Mn spinel-specific ordering kinetics, so TS is used mainly for sulfur handling and scored neutral for ordering specificity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Cu/Mn/S precursors are accessible but require full sulfidation and long-range cation diffusion, giving only moderate precursor activity [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 and SS3 show that direct chalcogenide reactions are feasible but often require multiple firings, homogenization, or annealing. Structural reconstruction is plausible because CS2/CS3 show thiospinel frameworks can form by high-temperature routes. Uncertainty remains in whether Cu occupies both tetrahedral and octahedral sites as in the target rather than forming simpler Cu sulfides or Mn sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the same sealed-vacuum and volatile-sulfur penalties as Route 1, supported by evacuated ampoule syntheses in SS2/SS4 and CS3. It avoids >1000 °C temperatures if held in the 750–950 °C range, which is consistent with SS2/SS4. Multi-step processing is still required because CS4 reports grinding/recycling until single phase. Toxicity/air-sensitivity is lower than polysulfide flux routes, although sulfide powders still need careful handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfide precursors improve chemical matching and reduce the burden of sulfidizing metallic Mn/Cu [Reasonable Inference]. SS2/SS4 directly support using binary chalcogenides for ternary Cu/Ag–Cr sulfides in evacuated quartz ampoules, and CS2/CS3 support formation of thiospinel-type sulfide frameworks. Diffusion remains a moderate limitation because cation ordering between tetrahedral and octahedral sites may require multi-day anneals, as suggested by CS1/CS4. This route therefore receives the best combined score for powder synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth reduces crystallization temperature but introduces air-sensitive or moisture-sensitive reagents if alkali sulfide/polysulfide fluxes are used [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 supports high-temperature solution growth in evacuated ampoules, while TS3 shows glovebox handling, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and flux removal for chalcogenide flux growth. These extra operations add severity despite lower temperature. Insufficient evidence from SS for flux growth of Cu–Mn spinel specifically, so SS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Liquid-mediated transport should improve kinetics and may promote larger crystals [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS2 high-temperature solution growth and TS3 chalcogenide flux growth. However, TS3 also reports mixed phases and inability to obtain pure AuCuSe₄, warning that flux phase selection can be difficult. For the target, flux may over-stabilize Cu sulfides or Mn sulfides if sulfur activity and cation ratio are not tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are favorable, but phase-purity confidence is lower than Route 2.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is a contingency method with weak transferability. SS1/TS4 show high-pressure CuS₂-related synthesis at 1.5 GPa and very high temperature, but that record is not chemically aligned with CuMn₂S₄ and does not show a spinel route. The proposed lower-temperature 1–2 GPa route avoids the >5 GPa penalty but still requires sealed containment, high-temperature treatment, and specialized apparatus [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for high-pressure stabilization, so CS is neutral for Route 4.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure can accelerate densification and stabilize dense sulfide frameworks [Reasonable Inference], but the evidence from SS1/TS4 is for CuS₂-like pyrite chemistry, not spinel-like CuMn₂S₄. Because the target is already predicted on the hull, pressure is not clearly needed [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction under pressure may instead drive the material toward pyrite/marcasite-like Cu–S motifs, so this route receives the lowest kinetic/structural confidence. Remaining uncertainty is high because no direct Cu–Mn–S high-pressure analogue is provided.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has mixed tetrahedral/octahedral polyhedral connectivity with corner and edge sharing, which triggers one amorphization-risk check; similar mixed connectivity is present in thiospinel analogues CS2/CS3. The structure is not obviously a very large-cell glass-forming network [Reasonable Inference], and chemically related sulfides crystallize under ordinary solid-state conditions in CS1–CS4 and SS2–SS4. Cation-size mismatch is not clearly above 15% for plausible sulfide coordination environments [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for Cu–Mn-specific amorphization, so TS is neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂CuS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar thiospinels and sulfides synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state methods, especially spinel CoCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ analogues (CS2, CS3), plus binary-chalcogenide ampoule routes for CuCrS₂/AgCrS₂ (SS2, SS4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust sulfide spinel/chalcogenide chemistry: tetrahedral–octahedral M–S frameworks form readily at 800–1000 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS1–CS4, SS2–SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among competing Cu–S, Mn–S, and possibly disordered spinel or layered Cu–Mn–S phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, binary sulfide solid-state sintering is the most credible route for phase-pure powder, while flux growth is attractive but riskier for single crystals.
