### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. CS reinforces this because NaInS₂ and LiNbS₂ are stable on the hull, while Li₂SnS₃ is nearly on the hull (CS2, CS3, CS4). SS/TS also support the stability of Rh–S frameworks because CuRh₂S₄ and NiRh₂S₄ are stable and Rh₂S₃ is nearly stable (SS1, SS2, SS4; TS1, TS2, TS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether P-3m1 LiRhS₂ is the experimentally selected ordered polymorph at finite temperature; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS, neutral 50 for that polymorph-selection issue.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Condition severity is moderate because analogous Li sulfides and Rh sulfides use evacuated sealed tubes at 750–900 °C for long holds (CS3, CS4, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS3). No high pressure or >1000 °C is required by the recommended program, but vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive Li₂S reduce the F2 score [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main limitation because Rh metal is refractory [Reasonable Inference], though successful Rh sulfide formation at 800–900 °C indicates feasibility (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS3). The route is credible for powder synthesis but may require regrinding/annealing, as repeated treatments are common in some sulfide analogues (SS3, SS4, TS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux growth

Flux growth has similar handling severity to sealed-tube synthesis because Li/S volatility and sulfide air sensitivity still require controlled handling [Reasonable Inference], but temperatures can remain in the 800–950 °C range supported by noble-metal sulfide flux work (SS5, TS4). Kinetics score higher than Route 1 because molten flux enhances mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and flux methods are demonstrated for chemically similar NaInS₂ and structurally/thermodynamically similar KPt₂S₃-type sulfides (CS2, SS5, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether LiRhS₂ has a sufficiently wide crystallization window in flux; CS2 and SS5/TS4 report moderate phase sensitivity. This route is selected as best because it balances achievable conditions with improved Rh transport.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: iodine-assisted CVT

CVT is more severe because it may use a >1000 °C hot zone, volatile iodine, sealed evacuated tubes, and long duration (CS1). Kinetically, it is attractive because chemical transport can bypass bulk diffusion, and CS1 directly demonstrates iodine CVT for a chemically similar Li–Sn–S layered material. TS5 further supports gas-mediated formation of P-3m1 LiCrS₂. The uncertainty is composition control: CS1 reports transport-grown crystals with composition/phase-control issues, and Li/Rh/I/S volatility differences could produce off-stoichiometric crystals [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CS₂/Ar sulfidation

This route is directly inspired by LiCrS₂, the closest P-3m1 LiMS₂ analogue in the TS set (TS5). Its severity is elevated by toxic/flammable CS₂ and high-temperature gas flow, and optional 1100 °C crystal growth triggers the >1000 °C penalty (TS5). Kinetically, oxide/carbonate sulfurization gives reactive sulfur chemical potential, but converting Rh oxide/carbonate mixtures into an ordered sulfide is a major reconstructive step [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 also notes contamination/phase issues, so this is a backup rather than the preferred route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple crystalline sulfide with small formula and no evidence of a large unit cell, so unit-cell complexity does not strongly favor amorphization. The P-3m1 LiMS₂ analogues show mixed polyhedral connectivity involving edge/corner/face sharing (TS5, CS4), so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned. The cation size mismatch is probably not extreme for Li⁺/Rh³⁺ in sulfide coordination [Reasonable Inference], but exact radius/oxidation-state confirmation is not provided by CS/SS/TS. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, score 78.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiRhS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple Li/alkali sulfide and Rh sulfide analogues are stable or nearly stable (CS2–CS4, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS3). The strongest analogue support comes from LiCrS₂, which has the same LiMS₂ stoichiometry and P-3m1 symmetry (TS5), and from LiNbS₂/Li₂SnS₃ sealed-tube Li sulfide chemistry (CS3, CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with known Rh–S octahedral bonding near the target Rh–S distance (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: Rh is refractory, sulfur and lithium species are volatile/reactive, and Li/Rh ordering may require slow cooling or flux-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible, with flux growth and sealed-tube solid-state synthesis being the most practical starting points.
