### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are all on the hull, and structurally similar Ag2HgSnSe4, Cu2HgSnSe4, Cu2HgGeSe4, and Cu2CdGeSe4 are also reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1, SS3–SS5). The uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of polymorph competition [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-ampoule solid-state/melt anneal.** The severity is moderate because comparable routes require vacuum ampoules, sealed volatile Se, and long multistep anneals, but not high pressure or >1000 °C conditions (CS5, SS1, TS1). Kinetics are acceptable because Ag/Ge/Se are not refractory at the temperatures considered [Reasonable Inference], but analogues often require days to hundreds of hours for equilibration (CS5, SS1, TS1). The key uncertainty is whether Ag2GeSe3 orders directly into the monoclinic Cc enargite-like phase or forms Ag2Se/GeSe2 intermediates [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, polyselenide flux.** The flux route has moderate condition severity because it uses relatively low temperatures but requires evacuated sealed tubes, reactive polyselenide flux preparation, slow cooling, and washing (CS1, SS2). It receives the best kinetic score because the closest enargite Ag–Se analogue forms by flux at 450–500 °C, indicating liquid-phase transport can overcome slow diffusion (CS1, SS2). Remaining uncertainty is substantial phase selectivity: the analogue had low yield and required precise flux conditions (CS1, SS2).

**F2/F3 — Route 3, Bridgman/melt growth.** The route is moderately severe because Ge-containing selenide Bridgman analogues require vacuum, sealed ampoules, high-temperature melt processing, temperature gradients, pulling, and long anneals (SS5, TS4). Kinetics are good in the melt, but structural and compositional control may be challenging due to primary crystallization fields and possible eutectic or polymorphic behavior in analogous systems (SS5, TS4). This route is best for crystal growth after the phase is confirmed by powder synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4, iodine CVT.** CVT severity is somewhat better in the scoring table because the analogous method can be one-step and uses 800–850 °C rather than extreme temperatures (TS5). The kinetic score is lower because iodine transport is demonstrated for related quaternary stannite selenides, not directly for Ag2GeSe3, and differential transport of Ag/Ge/Se could distort stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is whether stable volatile iodide transport equilibria produce the target rather than AgI-containing or binary selenide byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target is a corner-sharing tetrahedral framework, which generally lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face-sharing networks [Reasonable Inference], and analogous CS/SS/TS structures are mostly ordered tetrahedral selenides (CS1, CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The main amorphization/ordering risk is the large Ag/Ge size and valence contrast [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40-atom target cell, so that check is treated as neutral/non-triggering.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2GeSe3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are also stable on the hull. The synthesis evidence is strong at the framework level: Ag3PSe4 and Cu5Sn2Se7 show enargite or enargite-like tetrahedral selenide chemistry (CS1, CS5), while Ag/Hg/Sn/Ge and Cu/Hg/Ge stannite analogues show robust tetrahedral selenide formation in evacuated ampoules (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the shared corner-sharing AgSe4/GeSe4-type tetrahedral topology, which should be accessible by flux or melt/anneal routes (CS1, CS5, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Ag–Ge–Se system, especially avoiding binary Ag2Se/GeSe2 or off-stoichiometric phases during volatile-Se sealed-tube synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears moderately to highly synthesizable, with flux growth and sealed-ampoule annealing as the most defensible first attempts.
