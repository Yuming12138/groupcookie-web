### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as 0.0 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility. Thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are also on the hull, and chemically/structurally related CS/SS entries are stable or near-stable, e.g. Ta₂PtSe₇ at 0.0076 eV/atom (CS1, SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Os-specific decomposition products, so the score is high but not 100.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate sealed-tube temperatures, because analogous Ta–M–Se phases form at 650 °C to ~800 °C or 1050 K (CS1, CS3, SS4, TS2, TS3, TS5). The penalties come from vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile Se, both directly evidenced in evacuated silica tube syntheses (CS1, CS3, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS3). Toxicity of Os is not evidenced in CS/SS/TS and is treated as [Reasonable Inference], while high pressure and >1000 °C operation are not supported by the analogue data.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The main kinetic penalty is the use of refractory elemental metals, especially Ta and Os [Reasonable Inference], while analogues still require long anneals of 7–14 d or repeated cycles (CS1, CS3, SS3, TS2). The structural similarity is favorable because SS1–SS5 show layered Ta–M–Se motifs with comparable Ta–Se and M–Se coordination. Remaining uncertainty is high because Os is not present in the analogue set and solid-state Os ordering into the specific TaOsSe₄ sheet is unproven.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 is more procedurally severe because Br₂/I₂ transport requires sealed-tube handling, halogen loading, and a controlled gradient, all directly reported in Ta–Pd/Pt/Se or Ta₂NiSe₅ growth (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS3, TS4). The temperature remains moderate for solid-state chalcogenide work, with most relevant gradients around 1000–900 K or 875–800 °C (CS4, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure or >1400 °C conditions.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CVT receives a higher kinetic score than Route 1 because halogen transport and vapor-assisted crystallization reduce long-range diffusion limitations, as demonstrated by single-crystal growth in closely related Ta–Pd/Pt–Se systems (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). The target’s 2D Ta–Os–Se sheet resembles the layered Ta–M–Se structural family in SS1–SS5. The limiting uncertainty is whether Os forms appropriately volatile and reversible halide/selenide transport species under these conditions [Reasonable Inference], since CS/SS/TS provide no direct Os-CVT example.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 shares the sealed-tube and volatile-Se penalties of Routes 1–2, and it also incurs a multi-step penalty because preforming TaSe₃ or OsSe₂ adds at least one synthesis step [Reasonable Inference]. TaSe₃ preparation in evacuated sealed tubes at ~800 °C is supported by TS5, and repeated heating/grinding for Ta–Pd–Se homogeneity is supported by CS3/SS3/TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly supports preformed Os selenide use for TaOsSe₄.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Preformed selenides may improve chemical matching relative to pure metals [Reasonable Inference], and TaSe₃ is structurally/chemically relevant because it is a layered Ta selenide (TS5). However, converting binary selenides into the ternary ordered TaOsSe₄ phase likely requires cation redistribution and sheet reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The repeated grinding and long annealing reported for related Ta₂PdSe₆ synthesis suggest that phase homogenization can be slow (CS3, SS3, TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline layered chalcogenide with ordered Ta/Os/Se coordination, and structurally similar layered crystalline phases are well represented in SS1–SS5 and TS1–TS5. No CS/SS/TS source provides direct amorphization data, unit-cell atom count for the target, or explicit vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence for TaOsSe₄, so these checks are mostly neutral or inferred. The low amorphization risk score reflects the simple ternary crystalline motif and close structural analogues, but this remains partly [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaOsSe₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are also stable on the hull. The CS/SS/TS analogue support is strong for layered Ta–transition-metal selenides in general, especially Ta–Pd/Pt/Ni–Se compounds made by sealed-tube or CVT methods (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural and thermodynamic similarity to known 2D Ta–M–Se sheets, including closely matched Ta–Se and M–Se bond lengths (SS1–SS5). The most critical limiting factor is the absence of direct Os-containing analogue evidence, so Os transport chemistry, oxidation sensitivity, and competing Os–Se phase formation remain [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful sealed-tube stoichiometry and gradient/transport optimization.
