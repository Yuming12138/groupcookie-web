### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and scores near the top of the required 90–100 range. CS supports this because several Na/Li–Ln–S2 analogues have E_hull = 0, including NaYbS2 and NaErS2. SS/TS also support the stability of Ir–S and noble-metal sulfide chemistry because IrS2, Ir2S3, CuRh2S4, and NiRh2S4 include E_hull = 0 examples. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not prove that the exact P-3m1 NaIrS2 ordering is kinetically accessible.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because CS and SS/TS sealed-tube methods require vacuum/evacuated ampoules, staged heating, long anneals, and often grinding/refiring. The maximum temperature is moderate, around 850–960 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty is needed for this route. Na metal and sulfur volatility/reactivity create handling and sealed-tube risks [Reasonable Inference], although CS demonstrates that alkali metal + sulfur reactions can be managed in dry-box/sealed-tube workflows. Insufficient evidence from TS for elemental Na–Ir–S specifically, so TS contributes only indirect support via Ir sulfides.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Ir is the kinetic bottleneck because it is refractory and slow to react [Reasonable Inference], even though SS/TS show Ir sulfides can form from Ir + S at 900–960 °C. CS layered alkali sulfides show the desired alkali-metal sulfide framework is accessible, but the target requires complete construction from elements and likely cation ordering. Long anneals in CS and SS/TS justify a moderate-low kinetics score. Uncertainty remains over whether NaIrS2 forms before competing IrS2/Ir2S3 binaries.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar sealed-tube and air-sensitive precursor penalties to Route 1, but it avoids flowing H2S and temperatures above 1000 °C. CS and SS/TS support evacuated-tube sulfide annealing as standard. Na2S moisture sensitivity and sulfur volatility are practical risks [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS for Na2S + IrS2 specifically, but CS supports the broader alkali sulfide phase space.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 improves kinetics by using IrS2, which already contains Ir–S octahedral bonding similar in bond length to the target; IrS2/Ir2S3 have Ir–S distances of about 2.30–2.43 Å, close to target Ir–S = 2.41 Å (SS/TS). TS also lists IrS2 among sulfide precursors for alkali noble-metal sulfide flux chemistry. The remaining barrier is reconstructing the IrS2 network into layered NaIrS2 and controlling Ir oxidation state [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore the best practical balance of evidence and safety.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 receives a better formal F2 than some sealed-tube routes because it does not require sealed volatile Na/S in the same way, but it uses H2/H2S, which is a major reactive/toxic gas penalty. CS directly supports the 400 °C H2 pretreatment, H2/H2S switch, 900 °C heating, quenching, and repeated heating for NaErS2. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ir oxide/chloride sulfidation to NaIrS2, so they are neutral for this specific route. The main uncertainty is Ir redox chemistry under H2/H2S.

#### F3 — Route 3

CS shows oxide/chloride precursors can be converted into Na-containing layered sulfides under H2/H2S, which supports precursor activity. However, oxide-to-sulfide conversion requires complete anion and structure reconstruction, and no Ir-specific NaIrS2 sulfuration analogue is provided in SS/TS. Ir may preferentially form IrS2 or Ir2S3 under sulfiding conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are moderate-to-poor despite the reactive atmosphere.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is penalized for possible temperatures above 1000 °C because CS flux growth reaches 1030 °C, although TS carbonate/sulfur flux examples operate at 800–900 °C. It is also penalized for H2S or sealed sulfur/alkali flux handling, multistep slow cooling/washing, and toxic/air-sensitive conditions. TS supports flux synthesis of alkali noble-metal sulfides, but also notes phase sensitivity and unsuccessful cases for some compositions. Insufficient direct CS/TS evidence exists for NaIrS2 specifically, so this remains extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux routes score best kinetically because molten alkali sulfur media can overcome slow diffusion of refractory noble metals, as supported by TS flux syntheses and CS flux/H2S routes. The limitation is phase selectivity: the closest TS flux analogue KPt2S3 is alkali/noble-metal sulfide chemistry but not the same NaIrS2 P-3m1 structure. Slow cooling could help crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], but competing IrS2/Ir2S3 or alkali polysulfide phases may form. Therefore F3 is moderate-high, not high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered trigonal structure with only four atoms per formula unit and no evidence for a >40 atom cell, so the size/complexity amorphization risk is low from the target structure. The main triggered risk is the large Na/Ir cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but CS NaLnS2 analogues show layered alkali/metal sulfides can tolerate dissimilar cations. The target description does not indicate coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is not triggered. SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for NaIrS2 and are treated as neutral for that subfactor.
