### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives **100/100**. Chemically similar Ba₂HfS₄ is also on the hull, and Ba₅Hf₄S₁₃/Ba₆Hf₅S₁₆ are only slightly above hull at about 0.026–0.029 eV/atom, supporting accessible Ba–Hf–S thermodynamics (CS). Structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfides such as RbCeS₂, BaCe₂MnS₅, LaS, and CeScS₃ also have E_hull = 0 in the provided data (SS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is that no direct experimental Cs–Hf–S analogue is provided; this is an absence of direct CS/SS/TS evidence for the exact ternary.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state route

The severity score is low because the closest Ba₂HfS₄/Ba₂ZrS₄ route required slow ramps, repeated week-long firings, regrinding, pellet pressing, sealed ampoules, and a final 1350 °C H₂/Ar anneal (CS). Kinetically, Hf metal is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the repeated firing and impurity issues reported for Ba₂MS₄ analogues (CS). The route is nevertheless credible because the target HfS₆ octahedral bond lengths closely match those in Ba₂HfS₄ and Ba–Hf–S analogues (CS). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct Cs–Hf solid-state chemistry limits confidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Flux route

The flux route has similar atmosphere/volatility penalties because the Ba–Hf–S flux analogues used evacuated sealed ampoules, elemental sulfur, slow heating, and flux removal (CS). Its kinetics score is better than direct sintering because Ba₅Hf₄S₁₃ and Ba₆Hf₅S₁₆ formed at 1050 °C for 20 h in BaCl₂ flux, substantially milder than the 1350 °C Ba₂HfS₄ final treatment (CS). The target’s HfS₆ octahedra and Hf–S distances are close to the flux-grown Hf-rich Ba–Hf–S structures (CS), supporting structural transferability. The key uncertainty is whether a CsCl/CsI or Cs₂Sₓ flux stabilizes Cs(HfS₂)₂ rather than competing Cs–S, HfS₂, or other Cs–Hf–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Reactive sulfidation route

H₂S/CS₂ sulfidation is supported by RbLnS₂ preparation from Rb₂CO₃/Ln₂O₃ in H₂S and by sulfide precursor preparation in CS₂/N₂ for BaLn₂TS₅-type compounds (SS/TS). However, the closest Hf-bearing chemical analogues used Hf metal, sulfides, sulfur, and flux/solid-state routes rather than direct HfO₂ sulfurization (CS). Kinetics are penalized because HfO₂ is refractory and may persist as an oxide impurity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with MO₂ impurity concerns in Ba₂MS₄ synthesis (CS). This route is plausible mainly as a precursor-preparation or pre-sulfidation strategy, not as the most direct phase-selective route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a very large alkali cation, Cs, with much smaller Hf in octahedral sulfide coordination, triggering the ionic-radius-mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing HfS₆ octahedra, but not face-sharing, so the full mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered by the target description. The unit-cell atom count is not specified, and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct atom-count evidence for the target; this check is treated as neutral/uncertain. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because many related sulfide frameworks with large cations crystallize under high-temperature or flux conditions (CS/SS/TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs(HfS₂)₂ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar Ba–Hf–S phases that contain HfS₆ octahedra with Hf–S distances close to the target and that have been synthesized by both high-temperature solid-state and flux methods (CS). Structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfides show that large-cation sulfide frameworks can be crystallized under sealed, H₂S, or high-temperature conditions, but they do not directly prove Cs–Hf–S phase formation (SS/TS). The main enabling factor is the excellent thermodynamic stability plus close HfS₆ chemical analogy to known Ba–Hf–S phases (CS). The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control involving volatile Cs/S species and refractory Hf, especially because no direct experimental Cs–Hf–S synthesis analogue is provided [Reasonable Inference].
