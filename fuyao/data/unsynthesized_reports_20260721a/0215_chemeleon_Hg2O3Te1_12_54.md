### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. Thermodynamic analogues include Hg₂SeO₃ and Hg₂OI phases with E_hull = 0 or very small values, indicating that low-dimensional Hg–O chalcogen/anion frameworks are not intrinsically high-energy. (TS1–TS4) The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or protection from solution redox chemistry. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 1 precipitation severity.**  
The precipitation analogues use room-temperature or mild conditions, no pressure vessel, no special atmosphere, and basic drying equipment. (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2) The only formal deduction is mercury precursor toxicity, because Hg₂(NO₃)₂ is used directly in the analogues. (CS1–CS3; [Reasonable Inference]) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact TeO₂ dissolution window, so that uncertainty is handled by the expert adjustment rather than an F2 deduction.

**F3 — Route 1 precipitation kinetics.**  
The Hg₂SeO₃ analogues precipitate directly from concentrated Hg₂(NO₃)₂ and selenous acid, implying fast solution assembly of Hg₂XO₃ motifs. (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2) The target has similar Hg–O distances and trigonal TeO₃ units, supporting good structural transferability. (target structural description; CS1–CS3; SS1–SS2) The main uncertainty is Se→Te substitution, because TeO₂ is less straightforwardly soluble than SeO₂. [Reasonable Inference]

**F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal severity.**  
Hydrothermal analogues operate at 177–250 °C in sealed Teflon-lined or steel autoclaves, which is moderate but not high-pressure by the >5 GPa criterion. (SS5; TS3; CS5; TS5) No reactive gas atmosphere is required in the cited hydrothermal examples. (SS5; TS3; CS5; TS5) The principal deduction is mercury toxicity. (SS5; TS3; TS5; [Reasonable Inference])

**F3 — Route 2 hydrothermal kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal solution transport should improve crystallization relative to dry solid-state diffusion, and related Hg/Pb oxide frameworks crystallize below 250 °C. (SS5; TS3; CS5; TS5) However, the hydrothermal analogues are not exact Hg₂TeO₃ matches, and Hg₂CdSeO₆ data mention competing phases in experiments, implying phase control can be imperfect. (TS5) Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for hydrothermal Hg₂TeO₃ specifically, so the kinetics score is moderate-high rather than very high.

**F2 — Route 3 solid-state severity.**  
The low-temperature solid-state analogues use 280–340 °C, so the temperature is mild. (SS3; CS4) The deductions arise from vacuum/sealed-tube operation in the Hg–Se–O analogue, likely Hg volatility risk, and mercury precursor toxicity. (SS3; [Reasonable Inference]) Insufficient evidence from CS for Hg₂TeO₃ sealed-tube stability makes this route less favored.

**F3 — Route 3 solid-state kinetics.**  
The Hg–Se–O solid-state analogue required 120 h plus controlled heating/cooling, suggesting ordering is slower than precipitation. (SS3) The Tl tellurite analogue forms at 340 °C, supporting low-temperature tellurite chemistry but not directly Hg₂TeO₃ formation. (CS4) Solid-state reaction from oxides is therefore plausible but less kinetically favorable than solution routes. (SS3; CS4; [Reasonable Inference])

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines a large, low-coordinate Hg cation sublattice with trigonal Te(IV)O₃ units, giving one radius/coordination mismatch trigger. (target structural description; [Reasonable Inference]) The target structural listing does not indicate >40 atoms or face-sharing/edge/vertex coexistence in the unit cell, so those checks are not triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists to confirm the exact target cell atom count or detailed polyhedral sharing, so F4 is scored as low–medium risk rather than low risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg₂TeO₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues include stable Hg₂SeO₃ and Hg₂OI phases. (TS1–TS4) The strongest support comes from Hg₂SeO₃ polymorphs, which are chemically and structurally close and are synthesized by mild precipitation with confirmed phase purity. (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS2, SS4; TS1–TS2, TS4) The key enabling factor is the close Hg₂XO₃ analogy: Hg–O low-coordinate motifs and trigonal non-coplanar chalcogenite units are repeatedly observed in the references. (CS1–CS3; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2) The key limiting factor is transfer from Se(IV) to Te(IV), because TeO₂/tellurous acid solubility and Hg(I) redox stability are not directly proven in the provided data. (CS1–CS3; CS4; [Reasonable Inference]) Overall, the material appears highly synthesizable, with precipitation as the most direct first attempt.
