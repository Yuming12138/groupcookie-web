### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score by the rubric. Thermodynamic analogues BaCu4S3, Rb3Cu8S6, Na3Cu4S4, NaCu4S4, and K3Cu8S6 are all reported at or near E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that alkali/alkaline-earth copper sulfide ground states are plausible (TS1–TS5). CS is slightly mixed because NaCu5S3 is reported at 0.168 eV/atom, but RbCu4Se3 is near the hull, so the overall chemical signal is still favorable (CS1, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a large kinetic basin for the specific triclinic P1 ordering [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal.**  
The severity is low because the close NaCu5S3 analogue forms at 413 K in a Teflon vessel over 5 days without extreme atmosphere or pressure (CS1, SS1). Kinetics are favorable because hydrothermal sulfide solution chemistry can transport Cu and alkali species at low temperature, as demonstrated by NaCu5S3 (CS1, SS1). The uncertainty is whether KCu7S4, being more Cu-rich and K-containing rather than Na-containing, nucleates directly or instead forms competing binary/ternary sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2 sealed solid-state.**  
The condition severity is higher because the route uses evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive K sulfide, and controlled cooling, all consistent with BaCu4S3 and AgCuS sealed-tube syntheses (SS2, SS4, TS1). Kinetics are moderate because solid-state diffusion from Cu metal and sulfide powders is slower; BaCu4S3 required six days at 800 °C (SS4, TS1). The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility with potassium sulfides and whether quenching vs slow cooling stabilizes the desired P1 phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 polysulfide flux.**  
The flux route is synthetically more complex than hydrothermal because it requires vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and flux removal under N2, as reported for NaCu4S4 (TS4). Kinetics are strong because polysulfide fluxes enable high mobility at 500 °C and can grow crystalline alkali copper sulfides (TS4). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS4 notes NaCu4S4 was not obtained from other flux reactions, implying that subtle flux composition and cooling conditions matter (TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 4 carbonate melt.**  
The carbonate melt route has mild-to-moderate severity because RbCu4Se3 was synthesized at 650 °C for several hours under protective gas in an Al2O3 crucible (CS5). Kinetics should be favorable due to melt-mediated transport, but structural transfer is less direct because the strongest carbonate evidence is for Rb–Cu–Se rather than K–Cu–S (CS5). The uncertainty is whether sulfur activity and carbonate/basic melt chemistry will stabilize KCu7S4 or instead form other K–Cu–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large K/Cu cation size mismatch, reflected by K–S distances of 3.15–3.41 Å vs Cu–S distances of 2.20–2.43 Å, so one amorphization-risk trigger is applied (target structure; [Reasonable Inference]). No evidence is provided that the unit cell contains >40 atoms, and the target description does not explicitly show coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, so those triggers are not applied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those target-specific checks. The resulting risk is low–medium, mainly reflecting possible cation-ordering disorder rather than intrinsic glass-forming tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KCu7S4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar alkali/alkaline-earth copper sulfides are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The synthesis support is strong: a very close Na–Cu–S analogue is known hydrothermally (CS1, SS1), and multiple Cu-rich sulfide frameworks can be made by sealed solid-state, flux, or melt methods (SS4, TS1, TS4, CS5). The most important enabling factor is the high mobility and structural flexibility of Cu–S networks, evidenced by successful low-temperature hydrothermal and flux crystallization of related phases (CS1, SS1, TS4). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity in a crowded K–Cu–S composition space, especially because analogues report quenching requirements or flux-specific outcomes (SS4, TS1, TS4). Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable, with hydrothermal screening followed by flux/carbonate melt optimization being the most efficient strategy [Reasonable Inference].
