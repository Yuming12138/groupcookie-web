### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase by the supplied hull data. The thermodynamic-neighbor set is strongly favorable: K2Ag4S3, CsAg3S2, RbAg3Se2, CsAg3Se2, and K2Ag4Se3 are all listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in TS (TS-1–TS-5). CS and SS also show several chemically/structurally close alkali silver chalcogenides at 0.0–0.009 eV/atom (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5; SS-1/SS-2/SS-3). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of low-temperature polymorph competition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high condition score because close sulfide analogues are synthesized at 850 °C under Ar in standard corundum crucibles rather than high pressure or extreme temperature (CS-2/CS-5/SS-2/TS-1/TS-2). The main deduction is for the multi-step practical workflow of grinding, heating, washing, and drying, which is reported or implied for the flux/fusion analogues (CS-2/SS-5/TS-3). Sulfur volatility and air sensitivity are possible practical concerns [Reasonable Inference], but the provided sulfide analogues do not require sealed high-pressure systems for Route 1 (CS-2/CS-5/TS-1/TS-2).

#### F3 — Route 1

Flux/fusion chemistry should improve mass transport because alkali carbonate and sulfur are used in excess in the K2Ag4S3 analogue, consistent with a reactive melt/flux environment (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1). The route is kinetically plausible because CsAg3S2 and K2Ag4S3 form at 850 °C under Ar (CS-2/CS-5/SS-2/SS-3/TS-1/TS-2). The structural-reconstruction score is not maximal because the target is trigonal R-3m, whereas the closest AAg3S2 analogues include cubic KAg3S2 and monoclinic CsAg3S2 (target description; CS-1/CS-5/SS-1/SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high temperature because the closest KAg3S2 analogue is synthesized at 300 °C (CS-1/SS-1). However, it requires vacuum-sealed quartz tubes, supercritical ethylenediamine, an autoclave, and 2400 psi Ar counterpressure, making condition severity substantially higher than ordinary flux synthesis (CS-1/SS-1). The source explicitly notes subcritical conditions failed, so the demanding supercritical condition is not optional for that analogue (CS-1/SS-1).

#### F3 — Route 2

The supercritical route has favorable kinetics because solvothermal transport in ethylenediamine can bypass slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the reported analogue reaches crystalline material at only 300 °C over six days (CS-1/SS-1). It is also structurally well supported because KAg3S2 has the highest listed structural similarity to the target, 0.962 (SS-1). The key uncertainty is compositional transfer: the reference route lists Na2S but reports KAg3S2, so the alkali source/incorporation chemistry may require careful correction for NaAg3S2 [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate-to-high severity because sealed-ampoule vacuum synthesis and repeated annealing are required by the structurally similar Ag3SI precedent (SS-4). It avoids >1000 °C temperatures in the proposed adaptation [Reasonable Inference from SS-4], but it uses air-sensitive Na2S and potentially volatile sulfur in a sealed system [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS and TS for this exact sealed-ampoule Na–Ag–S route, so those source contributions are neutral for route severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary sulfide precursors are chemically matched and should reduce redox complexity relative to carbonate/metal fusion [Reasonable Inference], and SS-4 supports the viability of sealed binary-chalcogenide ampoule synthesis for an Ag–S structural analogue. However, SS-4 also reports regrinding, pelletizing, and long annealing for phase stabilization, implying slower ordering kinetics and possible phase-window sensitivity. The route is therefore plausible but less strongly supported than Routes 1 and 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: large cation-size mismatch between Na and Ag in distinct NaS6 versus AgS2/AgS3 coordination environments [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description does not indicate >40 atoms per cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral complexity, so those checks are not triggered. CS-1/SS-1 warns that subcritical reactions can yield amorphous products for a close analogue, but successful flux and supercritical methods show that crystallization is achievable when transport and sulfur activity are controlled (CS-1/SS-1; CS-2/SS-2/TS-1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaAg3S2 is likely a ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS-1–TS-5). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis in the broader alkali–Ag–chalcogen family, especially via carbonate/sulfur flux fusion under Ar and supercritical ethylenediamine routes (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5; SS-1/SS-3; TS-1/TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is soft-chalcogenide flux or solvothermal transport, which helps overcome Ag/alkali ordering and diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference from flux and supercritical successes in CS-1/CS-2/CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among closely related A–Ag–S stoichiometries and polymorphs, since K2Ag4S3 analogues show second-phase/crystal-isolation difficulties (CS-2/SS-2/TS-1). Sodium substitution adds uncertainty because direct experimental NaAg3S2 synthesis is not provided in CS/SS/TS.
