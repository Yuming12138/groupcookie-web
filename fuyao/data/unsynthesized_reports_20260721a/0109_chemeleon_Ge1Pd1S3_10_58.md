### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues in TS are also hull-stable, including Pd3(PS4)2, AuPS4, NaPdPS4, Hg2P2S7, and SnGeS3, which supports the plausibility of stable low-dimensional sulfide frameworks (TS1–TS5). CS provides stable chemically similar sulfides including SnGeS3 at E_hull = 0 and SnSb4S7 near the hull, but CS alone does not determine the target hull, so exact target thermodynamics from CS are insufficient and neutral where target-specific scoring is required. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of low-temperature polymorphism.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 550–650 °C, consistent with SnGeS3 at 500–600 °C and AuPS4/AgVP2S6 at 600 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (CS1; SS2/TS2; SS5). It requires vacuum sealing and sulfur containment, both supported by the ampoule syntheses in CS1 and SS2/TS2, giving controlled-atmosphere and volatile-precursor deductions. No high-pressure device is needed, unlike SS1/TS1, so the severity remains moderate.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because binary sulfides can help activate the Ge–S network, as demonstrated by SnGeS3 synthesis from SnS/GeS2 or elements (CS1; TS5). Dense Pd may react slowly, but square-planar PdS4 motifs form in structurally similar Pd sulfides, supporting eventual ordering (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS3). Long annealing times in the closest Ge analogue and in AgVP2S6 indicate slow diffusion/order formation, limiting F3 (CS1; SS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

The melt-quench route remains below or near 1000 °C, as explicitly allowed in SnGeS3 synthesis, so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty if capped conservatively (CS1; TS5). It receives deductions for vacuum, volatile sulfur in a sealed ampoule, and multi-step processing because CS1 describes melting, quenching, grinding, and tempering. The route is more severe than direct annealing because sulfur vapor pressure and ampoule integrity become more critical at the melt step [Reasonable Inference from CS1 sealed-ampoule procedure].

#### F3 — Route 2

The melt/quench/grind strategy should improve precursor contact and shorten diffusion paths [Reasonable Inference], and the method is directly reported for SnGeS3 (CS1; TS5). However, the need for 3–4 weeks of tempering after quenching shows that structural ordering remains slow (CS1; TS5). The target’s ordered PdS4/GeS4 sheet likely requires controlled crystallization from a disordered sulfide melt, so F3 is good but not excellent [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS1].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth at 650 °C for 10 days is supported by a Pd-containing structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfide (SS3; TS3). Severity deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/polysulfide species, multi-step loading and flux removal, and air-sensitive flux handling as described in the SS3/TS3 detailed reasoning. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, making it less severe than Route 5 but more operationally complex than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth provides the best mass transport among bulk routes and is directly shown to incorporate Pd into sulfide frameworks at 650 °C (SS3; TS3). This raises precursor activity and diffusion scores relative to dry solid-state methods. The limitation is phase control: SS3/TS3 reports disordered/co-crystallized or minor phases, so an alkali-free GePdS3 phase may require careful flux and stoichiometry optimization.

#### F2 — Route 4

CVT is only weakly supported by the chemically similar InSbS3 example, and CS2 lacks exact temperature/atmosphere details. The proposed sealed halogen CVT route receives deductions for vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/transport agent, and toxic/corrosive halogen transport chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports CVT directly for the target, so those source classes are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor transport may aid single-crystal growth, as CS2 shows a ternary sulfide can be prepared by chemical vapor transportation. However, Ge, Pd, and S species are likely to transport at different rates with halogens, making composition control challenging [Reasonable Inference]. Because no close Pd–Ge–S or Pd–Ge sulfide CVT analogue is provided, crystallization-pathway evidence from SS/TS is insufficient and neutral.

#### F2 — Route 5

High-pressure synthesis is directly supported for the structurally and thermodynamically closest Pd–S sheet analogue Pd3(PS4)2, which uses 20–65 kbar and 1000–1200 °C (SS1; TS1). This route receives the largest severity penalty because it can exceed 5 GPa, uses high temperature, and requires specialized high-pressure equipment. It is retained as a fallback route because SS1/TS1 demonstrates that square-planar Pd sulfide frameworks can be stabilized under pressure.

#### F3 — Route 5

High pressure and high temperature can accelerate formation of dense or difficult Pd sulfide phases, and SS1/TS1 reports reaction times near 2 h. However, the target is not the same composition or structure as Pd3(PS4)2, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically viable but experimentally inefficient compared with ampoule or flux synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains two quite different cation environments, square-planar PdS4 and tetrahedral GeS4, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination-mismatch check [Reasonable Inference from target structure; PdS4 analogues in SS1/TS1 and GeS4 analogue in CS1/TS5]. The target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell or coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing networks, so only one amorphization trigger is counted. CS/SS/TS do not directly provide the target cell atom count, so that part is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral except for the target-structure description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GePdS3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamically similar sulfides are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong for the individual building blocks: SnGeS3 validates layered GeS4 sulfide chemistry, while Pd3(PS4)2 and NaPdPS4 validate square-planar PdS4 coordination with nearly identical Pd–S bond lengths (CS1/TS5; SS1/TS1; SS3/TS3). The most critical enabling factor is sealed-ampoule sulfur chemical potential control, because the closest analogues are made in evacuated quartz/silica tubes at moderate temperature (CS1; SS2/TS2; SS5). The most critical limiting factor is slow ordering and phase competition among PdS, GeS2, and possible alternative Pd–Ge–S phases [Reasonable Inference from long anneals in CS1/SS5 and phase-mixture issues in CS5/SS3]. Overall, the target appears experimentally accessible, with Route 1 as the most balanced approach.
