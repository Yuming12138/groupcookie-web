The remaining uncertainty is not bulk thermodynamic stability, but whether the triclinic P1 polymorph rather than the known tetragonal polymorph is experimentally accessible [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct sealed-ampoule route avoids high pressure and avoids >1000 °C temperatures because related Tl–Ge–Te and Tl–Si–Te phases form at 500–800 °C (CS3 / SS5 / TS2 / TS3). The deductions arise from evacuated/sealed ampoule use, volatile telluride components, multi-step heat treatment, and Tl handling risk (CS3 / SS5 / TS2 / TS3 / SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether target Tl₄SnTe₃ specifically requires repeated grinding, so that part is treated as an optional [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Elemental solid-state precursors have moderate activity, but related elemental telluride reactions are successful in sealed ampoules (CS3 / SS5 / TS2 / TS3). Kinetics are aided by the low melting/soft nature of Tl/Sn/Te and by analogue formation at moderate temperature [Reasonable Inference based on CS3 / SS5 / TS2 / TS3]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the exact Tl₄SnTe₃ analogue shares SnTe₆/Tl–Te motifs but has a different tetragonal symmetry (CS1 / SS1 / TS5).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Melt/Bridgman growth uses moderate temperatures near 550–700 °C rather than high-temperature refractory synthesis, as shown for AgTl₃Te₂ and Tl–Te crystals (CS2 / SS3 / TS1 / SS4). **F3 — Route 2.**  
Melt growth provides strong mass transport, and AgTl₃Te₂ is explicitly prepared by reaction in the melt followed by slow cooling (CS2 / SS3 / TS1). The route should overcome diffusion barriers better than direct solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is phase selection, because peritectic behavior and humidity sensitivity complicate AgTl₃Te₂ phase control (CS2 / SS3 / TS1), and similar complications could affect Tl₄SnTe₃ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**
