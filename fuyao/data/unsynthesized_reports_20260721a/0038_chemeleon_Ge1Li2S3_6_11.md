### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, directly supporting a high thermodynamic score from the target data. Similar materials in all evidence categories are also hull-stable or near-hull: Li₂SnS₃ is near-hull at 0.000665 eV/atom (CS-3; SS-3; TS-3), and Li₂PbGeS₄/Li₂CdGeS₄ are on hull (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). CS gives strong chemical precedent for lithium thiometallates but no exact Li₂GeS₃ synthesis; SS/TS give stronger Ge-specific stability evidence through quaternary thiogermanates.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The solid-state route uses moderate high temperatures, 650–750 °C, which is below the >1000 °C penalty threshold and is supported by Li₂SnS₃ synthesis at 750 °C (CS-3; SS-3; TS-3). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multi-step tube handling, and air-sensitive Li₂S, all evidenced by glovebox loading and evacuated fused-silica tubes in Li₂SnS₃ (CS-3; SS-3; TS-3). Remaining uncertainty is whether Li₂GeS₃ requires the same high 750 °C hold or can crystallize lower because GeS₄ tetrahedral networks form at 525–650 °C in flux analogues (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2).

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Li₂S and S are reactive sulfide precursors, but elemental Ge may be diffusion-limited relative to a flux-mediated route [Reasonable Inference], so precursor activity is moderate. Li₂SnS₃ requires 96 h and slow cooling, indicating kinetic ordering demands for Li₂MS₃ sulfides (CS-3; SS-3; TS-3). Structural mismatch between octahedral Li₂SnS₃ and tetrahedral/trigonal-bipyramidal target Li₂GeS₃ lowers the structural reconstruction score (CS-3; target description).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The lithium polysulfide flux route uses 525–650 °C, below the high-temperature penalty threshold, and no high pressure is reported (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). It still requires inert handling, evacuated sealed silica tubes, volatile sulfur/polysulfide flux, and multi-step slow cooling/flux removal (SS-1; TS-1). The evidence also warns that Li₂S:S ratio is very important, which affects difficulty but is accounted mainly under kinetics/phase control rather than F2 severity (SS-2; TS-2).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten lithium polysulfide provides a liquid transport pathway and explicitly dissolves Ge in Li₂PbGeS₄ synthesis (SS-1; TS-1). GeS₄ tetrahedra in Li₂PbGeS₄ and Li₂CdGeS₄ have bond lengths close to the target’s GeS₄ units, supporting structural transferability (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2; target description). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: Li₂CdGeS₄ evidence states the flux ratio is crucial and impurity phases can appear with small changes (SS-2; TS-2).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Direct elemental synthesis is supported by Li₂TeS₃ at 460 °C under Ar/sealed ampoule conditions (CS-5), but adapting to Ge likely requires 500–650 °C based on Ge-containing analogues (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). The penalties are the same sealed-vacuum/volatile-sulfur/multi-step/air-sensitive set, especially because Li metal is more reactive than Li₂S [Reasonable Inference from elemental Li use in CS-5]. No CS/SS/TS evidence supports high pressure or >1000 °C for this chemistry.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Elemental Li and sulfur are highly reactive, which supports good precursor activity by analogy to Li₂TeS₃ (CS-5). However, no source directly demonstrates Li + Ge + S → Li₂GeS₃, so the Ge incorporation kinetics are uncertain; Ge-containing flux analogues suggest Ge reacts well when transported in polysulfide melt (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The structural match is moderate because Li₂TeS₃ has LiS₅/LiS₆ motifs related to the target but has different Te coordination relative to GeS₄ in the target (CS-5; target description).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed LiS₅ and GeS₄ polyhedra with corner and edge sharing, but no face sharing is reported, so only one amorphization check is triggered by cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference; target description]. The target is hull-stable, which supports crystallizability rather than glass formation (target data). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization for Li₂GeS₃ specifically, so uncertainty remains about whether fast quenching or off-stoichiometry could produce amorphous lithium thiogermanate byproducts.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₂GeS₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfides are also on or near the hull (target data; CS-3; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The analogue support is strong but indirect: CS supports Li₂MS₃ ternary sulfides such as Li₂SnS₃ and Li₂TeS₃, while SS/TS strongly support GeS₄-containing lithium thiogermanate crystallization from solid-state or polysulfide flux routes (CS-3; CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of molten lithium polysulfide flux, which improves Ge transport and promotes crystalline Ge–S frameworks (SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially sulfur chemical potential and Li₂S:S ratio, because similar quaternary Ge sulfides show impurity sensitivity when flux ratios vary (SS-2; TS-2). Overall, the target is realistically synthesizable, with the best chance coming from flux-assisted sealed-tube synthesis followed by careful powder XRD/Rietveld confirmation.
