## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and was scored 98/100. This is consistent with several chemically and thermodynamically similar references also having E_hull = 0, including GeAsSe, PdAsSe, Te₂Br, and CuBrSe₃ (CS1, CS2, TS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT / Vapor-assisted Growth

CVT has strong precedent for layered chalcogenides and halochalcogenides at moderate temperatures: GeAsSe grows as platelets at 450 °C (CS1), Sn–Sb–S–I forms in a 430→350 °C gradient (SS3), and CuBrSe₃ forms at 340→290 °C under vacuum (TS3). F2 is reduced because vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile bromide precursors, and toxic arsenic species are required [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is high because vapor transport reduces diffusion limitations, but exact AsSeBr topology is not experimentally represented, so phase selectivity remains uncertain.

### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State Ampoule

Solid-state sealed-tube synthesis is feasible in related ternary pnictide/chalcogenide systems, including PdAsSe and PdSbSe (CS2, CS5), and a bromochalcogenide Te₃(PdBr)₄ forms in sealed quartz at ~427 °C (CS3). F2 is lower due to vacuum sealing, volatile bromide pressure, toxic precursors, and likely multi-step annealing/regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is moderate because bulk diffusion and competing phase formation are more problematic than in solution/vapor routes, and CS3 explicitly notes stoichiometry sensitivity.

### F2/F3 — Route 3: Hydrothermal HBr

Hydrothermal bromide chemistry is directly supported by Pd(Se₃Br)₂ synthesis in HBr/HCl at 453 K for 5 days (SS4), and acidic hydrothermal synthesis is favored for tellurium iodide analogues (TS4, TS5). F2 is favorable because temperature is low and no external high pressure is used, although sealed acid autoclave conditions and toxic As/HBr handling reduce the score [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is high because solution transport should rapidly redistribute As/Se/Br species, but the lack of direct As–Se–Br hydrothermal evidence introduces uncertainty; insufficient evidence from CS for this specific route.

### F2/F3 — Route 4: Ionothermal RTIL

Ionothermal synthesis of Te₂Br in Lewis-acidic RTILs at 20–140 °C demonstrates that chalcogen bromides can crystallize under very mild thermal conditions (TS1). F2 is good because temperature and pressure are mild, but inert handling, Lewis-acidic media, and toxic/moisture-sensitive AsBr₃ add complexity [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is strong for transport but weaker for structural predictability, because there is insufficient evidence from CS and SS for ionothermal As–Se–Br crystallization.

### F2/F3 — Route 5: High Pressure

High-pressure synthesis is structurally relevant because As₂Se₃ can form phases with edge-sharing AsSe₆ octahedra at 3–7 GPa (SS1), resembling the target’s edge-sharing AsSe₃Br₃ octahedra. However, F2 is poor because multi-GPa equipment and high-temperature encapsulation are required (SS1, SS2). F3 is reasonably high under high P–T, but this route is likely unnecessary for a predicted ground-state target unless ambient-pressure routes fail [Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a small AsSeBr formula and a simple reported P3m1 layered structure, so the >40 atom-cell amorphization trigger is not supported by the target description [Reasonable Inference]. It has a single cation-like species, As, so cation radius mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The target is described as edge-sharing AsSe₃Br₃ octahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, giving low amorphization tendency from the defined checks.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogues support synthesis well at the chemical level: As–Se layered compounds form by CVT (CS1), Se–Br low-dimensional compounds form by hydrothermal or transport routes (SS4, TS3), and thermodynamic halide analogues are stable on hull (TS1, TS3–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature transport media—HBr hydrothermal solution, halide vapor, or Lewis-acidic ionic liquid—that can move volatile As/Se/Br species without needing refractory solid-state diffusion (SS4, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and volatility control, especially avoiding loss or overactivity of bromine/AsBr₃ and suppressing As₂Se₃ or bromide-rich side phases [Reasonable Inference; stoichiometry sensitivity supported by CS3]. Overall, hydrothermal HBr and CVT are the most realistic first attempts, with high pressure reserved only as a fallback.
