### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. Chemically similar phases such as Bi₄Pd₆S₄, Rh₃Pb₂S₂, and PtGeS are also reported at or near E_hull = 0 (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3), and structurally/thermodynamically similar OsSbTe/OsSbSe analogues are also on the hull (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee ambient-pressure accessibility, as several closest structural/thermodynamic analogues require high pressure (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The severity is moderate because multiple CS analogues are made in evacuated quartz ampoules at 600–1000 °C rather than extreme temperature or pressure (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The deductions arise from vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and multi-step heating/regrinding/slow-cooling procedures supported by CS-2/CS-3/CS-5. The main uncertainty is whether Ir diffusion is too slow for ambient-pressure equilibration [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetics are the limiting factor because Ir is refractory and elemental solid-state reaction is inherently slow [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest positive signal is PtGeS, a spinel-like analogue prepared from elements in evacuated tubes (CS-3), but its 4–6 week heat treatment and regrinding indicate slow equilibration (CS-3, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly confirms ambient-pressure formation of this exact Ir–Sn–S spinel-like framework, so the kinetic score remains moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 2: High-pressure solid-state synthesis

The HP route is experimentally demanding because closest structural/thermodynamic analogues use 35–50 kbar and 850–1050 °C with specialized tetrahedral-anvil equipment (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). Under the specified rubric, no >5 GPa deduction is applied because the reported range reaches about 5 GPa but does not clearly exceed it (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). The source evidence nevertheless indicates operational challenge through capsule loading, cold pressing, heating cycles, and recycling (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5).

#### F3 — Route 2: High-pressure solid-state synthesis

High pressure improves the likelihood of forming dense octahedral/tetrahedral frameworks analogous to OsSbTe and OsSbSe (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). The structural match is strong because these analogues have M–Pn/Ch octahedra sharing corners/edges with tetrahedral units, similar to the target’s IrSn₃S₃ octahedral and Sn/S tetrahedral network (SS-1, TS-5). The limiting kinetic uncertainty is still Ir/Sn/S interdiffusion and possible need for repeated cycles [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Sn-rich flux growth

Flux growth has lower diffusion severity but higher procedural severity due to sealed/inert conditions, slow cooling, decanting, and possible toxic halide/mineralizer use (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2). The 1050 °C OsSb₂ flux example triggers the >1000 °C deduction (SS-4, TS-2). CS gives insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of chemically similar SnIrS-type sulfides, so the transferability penalty remains.

#### F3 — Route 3: Sn-rich flux growth

The kinetic score is comparatively favorable because flux growth can dissolve refractory metals and transport them at temperatures far below their melting points, as shown for IrTe₂ self-flux growth (SS-2, SS-3). A Sn-rich liquid should enhance Ir–Sn–S reaction contact [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural selectivity is uncertain because the closest flux analogues are IrTe₂ sheets or OsSb₂ binaries, not the target spinel-like ternary framework (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4).

#### F2 — Route 4: Chemical vapor transport

CVT has moderate severity because the closest analogue uses vacuum, sealed quartz, a two-zone furnace, and TeBr₄ transport agent over 8 days (SS-5, TS-3). The proposed sulfide adaptation requires halogen/halide transport agents, which are hazardous and composition-sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for CVT in chemically similar sulfides, so confidence is lower than for Routes 1–2.

#### F3 — Route 4: Chemical vapor transport

The vapor-route kinetics are uncertain because Ir transport under sulfide CVT conditions is not directly established by the provided CS/SS/TS set. TaIrTe₄/WTe₂-type CVT demonstrates that Ir-containing chalcogenides can be grown in two-zone sealed tubes (SS-5, TS-3), but the target’s Sn–Ir–S stoichiometry may be harder to control due to different halide volatilities [Reasonable Inference]. This route is more appropriate for crystal growth after phase discovery than for first synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s small formula and likely small unit cell reduce amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference], and the structure is a well-defined crystalline octahedral/tetrahedral network in the target description. One risk trigger is assigned for cation size mismatch between Sn and Ir in distinct coordination environments [Reasonable Inference]. The structure has corner and edge sharing but no face sharing in the target description, so the strict mixed-connectivity criterion requiring vertex + edge + face coexistence is not fully met.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SnIrS is predicted as a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from the combination of a chemically close spinel-like PtGeS analogue prepared by evacuated-tube solid-state reaction (CS-3) and structurally/thermodynamically close OsSbTe/OsSbSe analogues stabilized by high pressure (SS-1, TS-1, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic driving force plus structural precedent for dense octahedral/tetrahedral chalcogenide frameworks. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase-selectivity difficulty arising from refractory Ir, sulfur volatility, and possible competing Ir–S/Sn–S binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with high-pressure solid-state synthesis the safest discovery route and sealed-tube/flux methods reasonable ambient-pressure follow-ups.

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sulfur volatility and loss of stoichiometry | High | Use sealed evacuated ampoules as in CS-2/CS-3/CS-5, or encapsulated BN high-pressure capsules as in SS-1/TS-1/TS-5; consider slight S excess or SnS buffer [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | All | Competing Ir–S, Sn–S, or Ir–Sn binary phases | High | Use long anneals and slow cooling based on CS-2/CS-3/CS-5; use HP stabilization if ambient-pressure products are multiphase, following SS-1/TS-1/TS-5. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Slow Ir diffusion and incomplete reaction | High | Use fine Ir powder, pelletizing, intermediate grinding/reheating, and long 4–6 week anneals analogous to PtGeS/PtGeSe preparation (CS-3, CS-5). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Specialized high-pressure equipment requirement | High | Follow tetrahedral-anvil/BN capsule methodology from SS-1/TS-1/TS-5; screen shorter 6–24 h cycles before recycling. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Narrow pressure–temperature stability window | Medium–High | Use 35–50 kbar and 850–1050 °C windows from SS-1/TS-1/TS-5; quench under pressure to preserve dense phase [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Route 3 | Sn flux inclusion or residual Sn metal | Medium | Decant/centrifuge molten flux and acid-wash residual flux, analogous to flux-removal strategies in SS-4/TS-2; verify target acid stability first [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Poor nucleation of spinel-like ternary framework from flux | Medium | Seed with pre-reacted SnIrS powder if available [Reasonable Inference]; use slow cooling rates similar to IrTe₂ flux growth (SS-2, SS-3). |
| 8 | Route 4 | Different vapor transport rates for Sn, Ir, and S | High | Start from pre-reacted source charge and tune halogen loading/temperature gradient, following two-zone CVT control in SS-5/TS-3 [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Halogen corrosion or parasitic halide formation | Medium | Use low transport-agent loading as in TeBr₄ CVT analogue (SS-5/TS-3), and test I₂/SnI₄ loading series [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 10 | All | Crystal growth difficulty versus powder synthesis | Medium | Use Route 2 or Route 1 for phase discovery, then Route 3 or 4 for crystal growth; this sequencing is inferred from CS solid-state phase synthesis and SS/TS flux/CVT crystal-growth precedents [Reasonable Inference; CS-3, SS-2/SS-5, TS-3]. |
