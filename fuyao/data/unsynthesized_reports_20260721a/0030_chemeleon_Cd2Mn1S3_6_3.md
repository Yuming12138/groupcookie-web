### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase by the provided target data. This is consistent with several related sulfides being on or near the hull: AgCd2GaS4 has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-2, TS-1), AgGaS2 has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in one chemical analogue (CS-4), Cu12Sb4S13 has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-5), and CdS is near-hull (SS-1, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Cd–Mn–S phase diagram, so direct competing-phase topology remains uncertain and that exact-factor signal is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses vacuum sealed ampoules, which are directly supported by AgCd2GaS4 and Cu12Sb4S13 synthesis (SS-2, TS-1, CS-5). The severity score is reduced because sealed sulfide reactions must manage volatile sulfur and toxic Cd-containing reagents [Reasonable Inference], and analogous solid-state sulfide syntheses used long annealing or repeated processing (SS-2, TS-1, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact decomposition/vapor-pressure window of MnCd2S3, so the proposed 850–950 °C range is an extrapolated conservative estimate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary sulfide precursors should reduce sulfurization barriers compared with elemental Mn/Cd/S mixtures [Reasonable Inference], and CdS already has the same tetrahedral CdS4 motif with Cd–S distances close to target (SS-1, TS-2). However, analogous multication sulfides often require long anneals, such as 500 h for AgCd2GaS4 and several weeks for Cu12Sb4S13 (SS-2, TS-1, CS-5), implying slow cation ordering/diffusion. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Mn/Cd ordering kinetics specifically, so the kinetics score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 has mild temperature and no imposed vacuum/high pressure, following the Cu2SnS3 solvothermal synthesis at 453 K/20 h and the CdS bath deposition at 80 °C/60 min (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-2). The main deductions are multi-step wet processing and Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether a post-anneal is mandatory for MnCd2S3; therefore, the optional anneal is not fully penalized but remains an uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution precursors and thiourea are kinetically favorable because CS shows ternary Cu2SnS3 formation from metal chlorides and thiourea under solvothermal conditions (CS-1, CS-2), while SS/TS show CdS formation from Cd salt and thiourea at low temperature (SS-1, TS-2). The principal uncertainty is not sulfide formation but ordered ternary phase selection, because CdS chemical bath products included cubic/hexagonal mixtures and impurities (SS-1, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms solvothermal synthesis of MnCd2S3, so cation ordering is treated as the limiting kinetic subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is more severe because Bridgman synthesis requires evacuated ampoules and high temperatures, as shown for AgGaS2 Bridgman growth (CS-3, CS-4). The score is further reduced for volatile Cd/S containment and Cd toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS defines the MnCd2S3 liquidus, so the 900–1100 °C estimate is extrapolated from analogous sulfide melt/ampoule routes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Melt growth improves cation transport relative to solid-state pellets [Reasonable Inference], and chalcopyrite AgGaS2 provides a strong structural analogue for corner-sharing tetrahedral crystal growth (CS-3, CS-4). Binary sulfide precursors are directly supported in AgGaS2 Bridgman synthesis using Ag2S and Ga2S3 (CS-3). The remaining uncertainty is chemical transferability from Ag/Ga to Cd/Mn, because CS provides no direct Cd–Mn melt-growth evidence; this exact chemical factor is neutral/uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target consists of corner-sharing MnS4 and CdS4 tetrahedra, and similar tetrahedral sulfides such as CdS and AgCd2GaS4 also use corner-sharing tetrahedral networks (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2), which lowers amorphization risk. One risk is the significant Cd2+/Mn2+ tetrahedral radius mismatch, estimated above 15% [Reasonable Inference], which can promote disorder or local strain. Insufficient explicit unit-cell atom-count evidence from CS/SS/TS exists, but the simple MnCd2S3 formula and corner-sharing description do not indicate a large complex cell, so only one F4 trigger is applied [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCd2S3 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the provided target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom. CS/SS/TS support synthesis of related tetrahedral sulfides by solvothermal, sealed-ampoule solid-state, and Bridgman routes, with especially strong structural support from CdS and AgCd2GaS4 containing CdS4/chalcogenide tetrahedral frameworks (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the target’s thermodynamic stability combined with close Cd–S bond-length compatibility to known CdS-containing tetrahedral sulfides (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is achieving ordered Mn/Cd distribution rather than mixtures of CdS, MnS, or disordered ternary sulfide [Reasonable Inference]. Solvothermal synthesis is the best first attempt because it offers high precursor activity and mild conditions, but a post-synthesis sulfur-buffered anneal may be needed to improve crystallinity and cation ordering [Reasonable Inference].
