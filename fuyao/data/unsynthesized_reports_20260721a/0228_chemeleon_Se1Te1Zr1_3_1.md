### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the computed convex hull and giving a high thermodynamic score. This is reinforced by ZrTe₂, HfTe₂, TiSe₂, ZrGeSe, and several selenide analogues also being reported at or near E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5, CS2, CS3). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee that the ordered mixed-anion ZrTeSe layer is the experimentally selected low-temperature phase rather than a disordered or phase-separated ZrTe₂/ZrSe₂ assemblage [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct experimental formation of exactly ZrTeSe, so that specific point is neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state

The route avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C because close analogues form at 480–900 °C or 900 °C in sealed tubes (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). Deductions arise from evacuated/dry-Ar conditions, volatile Se/Te, and repeated grinding/reheating, all consistent with sealed-tube chalcogenide practice (SS1, SS5, TS1). CS2 gives a useful chemical precedent for Zr–chalcogen solid-state synthesis but at 1100 °C and a different Matlockite topology, so it is supportive but less directly transferable. The main uncertainty is whether mixed Se/Te ordering equilibrates on practical timescales [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state

Kinetics are limited by refractory Zr and the need for interdiffusion in a solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, layered ZrTe₂/TiSe₂/TiZrTe₄ analogues form from elements with long anneals and repeated grinding, showing the pathway is feasible (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target shares the edge-sharing octahedral 2D omega-derived framework with ZrTe₂/HfTe₂/TiSe₂ (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). The uncertainty is that binaries ZrTe₂ and ZrSe₂-like regions may nucleate faster than an ordered ternary [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Iodine-assisted CVT

The temperature severity is moderate because HfTe₂ CVT is reported at 700 °C, and CeAsSe iodine mineralization occurs at 900 °C in sealed ampoules (SS2, TS2, CS3). The route is penalized for vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, I₂ volatility/corrosivity, and multi-step pre-reaction plus transport [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give strong signal for CVT of the closest structural analogue HfTe₂; CS supports iodine mineralization in selenides but with different cation/anion chemistry (CS3). Remaining uncertainty is the transport stoichiometry of Zr, Se, and Te, which is not directly established in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2: Iodine-assisted CVT

CVT improves kinetics by transporting material through the vapor phase rather than relying solely on bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and this is directly supported by HfTe₂ crystal growth using iodine vapor transport (SS2, TS2). The target topology is strongly supported by ZrTe₂/HfTe₂/TiSe₂ structural analogues (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). The main kinetic risk is not crystallization of a layered phase, but maintaining the intended 1:1 Se:Te ratio during transport [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 and CS5 indicate CVT selenides can require careful optimization and may show disorder/twinning, lowering confidence modestly.

#### F2 — Route 3: Chalcogen self-flux

This extrapolated route uses moderate temperatures comparable to layered chalcogenide syntheses and sealed ampoules (SS1, SS4, TS1). It is penalized for controlled vacuum conditions, volatile/toxic excess chalcogen, multi-step flux removal, and possible mineralizer hazards [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 provides some support for using excess Se to manage Zr–Se phase formation, but no direct Te–Se self-flux synthesis of ZrTeSe is provided. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for this exact route is treated as neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3: Chalcogen self-flux

A Te–Se-rich melt could improve precursor activity and Zr transport [Reasonable Inference], which is why the kinetics score is above Route 1. However, the evidence is indirect: SS4 uses excess Se but not a true Te–Se self-flux for a layered 1T phase. Structural compatibility remains good because the target resembles stable 1T dichalcogenides (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2), but flux conditions could favor binary ZrTe₂/ZrSe₂ crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore plausible but less evidence-backed than CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, a small formula unit, and a simple edge-sharing octahedral layered network, so none of the three amorphization checks are triggered [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Structurally similar ZrTe₂/HfTe₂/TiSe₂ are simple layered edge-sharing octahedral phases, supporting low structural complexity (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). The only caution is that SrZrSe₃ synthesis reports a competing amorphous Zr–Se phase, but that material is a different 3D alkaline-earth zirconium selenide chemistry (SS4). Insufficient direct amorphization evidence from CS is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrTeSe is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close layered group-IV chalcogenide analogues are also hull-stable (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from structural and thermodynamic analogues ZrTe₂, HfTe₂, and TiSe₂, which share the 2D edge-sharing octahedral omega-derived framework (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). The most enabling factor is the excellent structural match to experimentally synthesized 1T dichalcogenides, particularly ZrTe₂/HfTe₂ (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The key limiting factor is mixed-anion Se/Te stoichiometry and ordering, because CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate ZrTeSe and CVT/solid-state chalcogenides can show phase-control issues (CS3, CS5, SS4, TS5). Overall, iodine-assisted CVT after a pre-reaction is the best route because it combines close structural precedent with enhanced crystallization kinetics (SS2, TS2).
