### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest 90–100 thermodynamic-stability range from the required rubric. Similar phases are also frequently stable or nearly stable: CuAlS₂ is E_hull = 0.0 (CS1 / TS3), CuGaS₂ is E_hull = 0.0 (CS2 / SS2 / TS1), and several stannite-like Cu sulfides are at or very near the hull (SS3 / SS4 / TS2 / TS4 / TS5). The remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature phase competition and cation disorder, for which CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for the exact AlCu₂S₃ composition.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state

The direct sealed-tube route receives moderate F2 because analogous Cu sulfides require vacuum sealing, staged high-temperature reaction, and multiday annealing, but not high pressure or >1000 °C for the main proposed program (CS2 / SS2 / TS1 / TS4). Kinetics are moderate because elemental Cu and Al are less reactive than molecular precursors [Reasonable Inference], but the structural target is very close to CuAlS₂/CuGaS₂ corner-sharing tetrahedral frameworks (CS1 / CS2 / TS1 / TS3). Uncertainty remains in whether AlCu₂S₃ orders cleanly rather than forming CuAlS₂ + Cu₂S or Al-rich sulfides, a risk suggested by CuAlS₂ high-pressure decomposition products (CS1 / TS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 iodine CVT

CVT has harsher handling than simple solid-state because it requires iodine, sealed evacuated ampoules, and a thermal gradient; these requirements are directly supported by iodine transport examples (CS4 / SS1 / SS3 / SS5 / TS2). Its kinetics score is high because vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and multiple structurally similar tetrahedral Cu sulfides crystallize by iodine transport (SS1 / SS3 / SS5 / TS2). The key uncertainty is whether Al-containing AlCu₂S₃ forms volatile transport species at usable rates without decomposing, because direct AlCu₂S₃ CVT evidence is absent; ZnAl₂S₄ CVT partially supports Al sulfide transport (CS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 flux growth

The flux route has moderate F2 because it uses sealed ampoules and volatile sulfur/chalcogen chemistry, but can operate below typical solid-state temperatures; CS5 demonstrates KCl/LiCl flux growth at 550 °C for a chemically similar Cu chalcogenide. Its F3 is high because a molten halide medium should improve mass transport and cation ordering [Reasonable Inference], which is valuable for a tetrahedral Cu/Al sulfide. The main uncertainty is transferability from a Cu–Si–Se flux analogue to Cu–Al–S, since CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct flux evidence for AlCu₂S₃.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 high pressure

The high-pressure route is penalized strongly in F2 because CuAlS₂ high-pressure experiments require ~80 kbar and specialized apparatus (CS1 / TS3). Kinetically, pressure-assisted transformation may help access dense or ordered phases [Reasonable Inference], and CuAlS₂ is chemically very close to the target (CS1 / TS3). However, the same evidence explicitly reports CuAlS₂ decomposition at 80 kbar, 600 °C, so this route has significant phase-control uncertainty and is not recommended as the first attempt (CS1 / TS3).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a corner-sharing tetrahedral network, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face-sharing networks; this connectivity is stated for the target and mirrored by CuAlS₂/CuGaS₂ analogues (CS1 / CS2 / TS1 / TS3). One risk trigger is the substantial Cu⁺/Al³⁺ tetrahedral size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], which may promote disorder or local strain. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell atom count, but the described Imm2 site multiplicity suggests a compact ordered cell [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlCu₂S₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the provided target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally similar Cu sulfides are also hull-stable (CS1 / CS2 / TS1 / TS3 / TS4 / TS5). The strongest support comes from the close CuAlS₂ analogue, which has the same CuS₄/AlS₄ tetrahedral chemistry and matching bond lengths (CS1 / TS3), plus CuGaS₂ and stannite-like Cu sulfides that are routinely made in sealed tubes or by CVT (CS2 / SS2 / SS3 / TS1 / TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing tetrahedral chalcogenide framework common across the analogues (CS1 / CS2 / SS1 / SS3 / TS1 / TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Cu–Al–S system, especially avoiding CuAlS₂, Cu₂S, and Al-rich sulfides; CuAlS₂ decomposition under high pressure highlights this risk (CS1 / TS3). Overall, synthesis is credible, with flux-assisted or sealed-ampoule routes preferred before high-pressure exploration.
