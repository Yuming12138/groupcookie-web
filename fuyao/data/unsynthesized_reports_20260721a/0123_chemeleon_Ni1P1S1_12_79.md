### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is the strongest possible thermodynamic indicator under the provided rubric. The closest thermodynamic analogue is also NiPS with E_hull = 0.0 and high similarity, and several nearby Ni–pnictide/chalcogenide phases are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: the known NiPS analogue is cubic P2₁3, while the target is orthorhombic Pca2₁, so synthesis may yield the cubic phase unless the distortion is thermodynamically or kinetically selected [Reasonable Inference] (CS1, SS1, TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is penalized for evacuated sealed tubes, volatile P/S handling, and a multi-step heating/annealing schedule; these features are explicitly present in the closest NiPS synthesis protocol (CS1, SS1, TS1). It is not penalized for high pressure or >1000 °C reaction temperature because the effective synthesis/annealing temperatures are 690–800 °C, although silica drying near 1000 °C is mentioned in related protocols (CS1, CS5, SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether the orthorhombic target requires a different thermal history than the cubic analogue [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are moderate because solid-state diffusion involving Ni is slow, and the analogue requires long annealing up to 20 days (CS1, SS1, TS1). However, the target topology is nearly identical to known NiPS, so structural reconstruction should be minimal compared with forming a new framework (CS1, SS1, TS1). Volatile P/S may assist transport but also complicate stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Flux growth avoids high pressure and can operate below 1000 °C, as shown for NiP3 flux growth in evacuated silica tubes (SS3, TS3). It still requires sealed tubes, volatile P/S management, slow cooling, and post-growth flux separation [Reasonable Inference] (SS3, TS3). The lack of direct flux-grown NiPS evidence is the principal uncertainty; insufficient direct evidence from CS for flux synthesis of NiPS.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux transport can improve mass transport and crystal quality, and NiP3 is reported as well crystallized using Sn flux (SS3, TS3). The kinetic score is limited because NiP3 is not the same structure or composition as NiPS, so flux may favor binary phosphides or other competing phases rather than the ternary spinel-like phase [Reasonable Inference] (SS3, TS3). Composition control with volatile S in flux is also uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

The high-pressure route is strongly penalized because the supporting NiP2 analogue uses 65 kbar and 1200–1400 °C (SS4, TS4). It also requires specialized high-pressure equipment such as an anvil press, BN crucible, graphite heater, and thermocouple assembly (SS4, TS4). This route is likely unnecessary for a zero-hull NiPS phase with an ambient sealed-tube analogue [Reasonable Inference] (CS1, SS1, TS1).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High temperature can accelerate reaction kinetics, but the structural analogue is only partially relevant because NiP2 is pyrite-type and binary, whereas the target is ternary spinel-like NiPS (SS4, TS4). Short HPHT dwell times may not permit cation/anion ordering comparable to the long anneals used for NiPS-family phases [Reasonable Inference] (CS1, SS1, TS1; SS4, TS4). The route may be useful only if the orthorhombic distortion is pressure-stabilized, which is speculative [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The amorphization risk is low because the target has only one cation type, avoiding cation-radius mismatch, and the closest NiPS analogue has a small 12-atom formula cell (CS1, SS1, TS1). The target and closest analogue are dominated by corner-sharing octahedral/tetrahedral networks rather than mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity (CS1, SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is not amorphization but polymorph/order control between cubic and orthorhombic NiPS [Reasonable Inference].

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogue, cubic NiPS, is also on the hull (TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually strong because the nearest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue is compositionally identical NiPS with nearly the same spinel-like NiP3S3 octahedral framework (CS1, SS1, TS1). Volatile P/S control in sealed ampoules is the key practical safety and stoichiometry issue [Reasonable Inference] supported by the use of sealed evacuated tubes in the analogues (CS1, SS1, TS1).
