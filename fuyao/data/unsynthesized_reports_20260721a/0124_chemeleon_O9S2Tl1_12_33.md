### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high F1 score under the rubric. Multiple analogues also lie on the hull, including CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, and TS-4. The remaining uncertainty is that computed 0 K hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or retention of the discrete O2 unit under laboratory conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal oxoanion syntheses are repeatedly supported at moderate temperatures: ~170 °C for one week in CS-1/CS-2, 495 K for CS-3, 140 °C for SS-2, and 230 °C for TS-3. The route has favorable kinetics because solution-mediated crystallization reduces diffusion barriers, as demonstrated by these hydrothermal analogues. The main penalty is the need for toxic Tl salts [Reasonable Inference] and likely oxidant/O2 control for the target’s O2 cluster [Reasonable Inference]; SS-2 also warns that sulfate hydrothermal systems may suffer phase-control issues.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 evaporation.**  
This route has the mildest conditions because sulfate analogues RbAu(SO4)2 and NaAu(SO4)2 crystallize by evaporation from concentrated sulfuric acid in SS-4/SS-5 and TS-1/TS-2, and CS-4 shows room-temperature aqueous evaporation can yield oxoanion crystals. Kinetics are strong because precursors are dissolved and crystallize directly from solution, avoiding solid-state diffusion. The uncertainty is that the O2 molecule/cluster in TlS2O9 is not represented in the Au sulfate analogues, so oxidant selection and O2 retention are [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 sealed ampoule/melt.**  
CS-5 demonstrates that an SO7-type oxosulfate can form from hot concentrated sulfuric acid in a sealed ampoule at 365 °C, and SS-3 shows sulfate melt crystallization at 500 °C. The flux/melt environment should improve ion mobility relative to solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The route is penalized because hot sealed acid plus oxidant can generate pressure [Reasonable Inference], and SS-3 reports polymorph/phase control problems in sulfate melt chemistry.

**F2/F3 — Route 4 room-temperature oxidative precipitation.**  
SS-1 supports room-temperature aqueous reaction, evaporation, and successive crystallization for a dithionate-related Ba–S–O material, and CS-4 supports slow evaporation of oxoanion salts at room temperature. This route has mild severity and good diffusion kinetics because species are solution-borne. The weakness is that the target is an anhydrous TlS2O9 framework, and room-temperature aqueous media may preferentially crystallize hydrated Tl sulfates or simple sulfate salts [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms direct formation of TlS2O9 by this route.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: very different Tl and S cation sizes/coordination environments, with Tl–O distances of 2.73–2.91 Å and S–O distances of 1.45–1.69 Å in the target; the ionic-radius interpretation is [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula contains only 12 atoms, and the provided structure description does not establish a >40-atom unit cell. The connectivity described is mainly corner-sharing sulfate tetrahedra and Tl–O coordination, not simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so F4 is low–medium risk.

The CS/SS/TS data support synthesis of related oxoanion frameworks by hydrothermal, evaporation, precipitation, and hot-acid/flux routes, with the strongest practical support for solution routes (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). The most important enabling factor is that sulfate/oxoanion frameworks crystallize readily from acidic aqueous or concentrated H2SO4 media under mild to moderate conditions (CS-1, CS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). The most important limiting factor is incorporation and retention of the discrete O2 unit, because the provided analogues do not directly demonstrate O2 inclusion in a Tl sulfate framework; this is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than direct CS/SS/TS evidence. A secondary limitation is thallium toxicity and waste handling, which increases practical synthesis difficulty [Reasonable Inference].
