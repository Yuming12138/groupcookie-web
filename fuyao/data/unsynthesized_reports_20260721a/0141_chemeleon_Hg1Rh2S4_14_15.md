### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has computed E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. The closest structural/thermodynamic analogue CuRh₂S₄ is also a ground-state spinel with E_hull = 0.0 (SS, TS), and NiRh₂S₄ is likewise E_hull = 0.0 (SS, TS). Chemically similar HgIn₂S₄ and HgGa₂S₄ are also E_hull = 0.0 (CS), supporting stability of Hg-containing sulfides. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of volatile Hg loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated-tube solid-state synthesis, which is supported by CuRh₂S₄ at 800 °C for 48 h and FeRh₂S₄ through staged 450–850 °C treatments (SS, TS). The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/Hg-containing species, multistep grinding/firing, and toxic Hg/HgS handling [Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C operation is required if the CuRh₂S₄-type protocol transfers (SS, TS). Uncertainty is mostly the maximum safe temperature for Hg retention [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Rh is refractory, so precursor activity is low [Reasonable Inference], but CuRh₂S₄ and FeRh₂S₄ demonstrate that Rh spinel sulfides can form from elements in evacuated ampoules (SS, TS). The spinel structural match is excellent because the target and CuRh₂S₄ share Fd-3m RhS₆ octahedra linked to tetrahedral A-site sulfide units (SS, TS). Repeated heating may be needed, as FeRh₂S₄-type syntheses use multiple treatments (SS). The main uncertainty is whether Hg incorporation is kinetically competitive with formation of binary Rh sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 still requires a sealed evacuated ampoule because HgS/S species are volatile and toxic [Reasonable Inference], and evacuated or H₂S atmospheres are reported for HgIn₂S₄ synthesis (CS). The route avoids applied high pressure and can likely stay below 1000 °C by combining the CuRh₂S₄ 800 °C precedent (SS, TS) with Rh₂S₃ formation near 900 °C (SS, TS). It remains multistep because Rh₂S₃ preparation, mixing, pelletizing, annealing, and regrinding may be needed [Reasonable Inference]. Evidence from CS/SS/TS is strong for sulfide solid-state synthesis, but no source directly reports HgRh₂S₄.

#### F3: Route 2 kinetics

HgS + Rh₂S₃ is stoichiometrically matched to HgRh₂S₄, which should reduce large-scale sulfur redistribution compared with elemental synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. HgIn₂S₄ formation from In₂S₃ and HgS-containing binary sulfide mixtures supports the use of binary sulfide metathesis/combination for Hg spinels (CS). Rh₂S₃ provides preformed Rh–S bonding close to target Rh–S distances, and Rh₂S₃ is readily synthesized in evacuated quartz tubes (SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is cation ordering into the normal spinel arrangement, which may require repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

CVT requires evacuated sealed ampoules, halide transport agents, and thermal gradients; these are reported for HgGa₂S₄ using iodine (CS) and Rh₃S₄ using RhCl₃/S in an evacuated quartz ampoule (TS). The route is penalized for volatile/toxic species, vacuum operation, multistep setup, and possible >1000 °C operation if Rh transport chemistry requires Rh₃S₄-like temperatures (TS). Lower temperatures may be necessary for Hg retention, but that is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is therefore higher than conventional solid-state synthesis.

#### F3: Route 3 kinetics

CVT may improve mass transport relative to solid-state diffusion because volatile iodine/chloride species can move material through the vapor phase [Reasonable Inference], and both HgGa₂S₄ and Rh₃S₄ have CVT precedents (CS, TS). However, Rh₃S₄ CVT reports difficulty obtaining phase-pure material without Rh₁₇S₁₅ (TS), indicating composition/phase-window sensitivity. Combining Hg and Rh transport chemistries is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS, so composition control is the limiting kinetic factor. This route is better suited for crystal growth after a bulk phase field is identified [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 4 condition severity

High-pressure treatment is supported only indirectly by related NiRh₂S₄ spinel-sulfide work that mentions high pressure up to 80 kbar and quenching (SS, TS). It incurs a major severity penalty for >5 GPa pressure, in addition to sealed containment and toxic HgS handling. Because CuRh₂S₄ forms without high pressure (SS, TS), high pressure is not the preferred first route. Its role is a contingency if ambient-pressure synthesis fails [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 4 kinetics

High pressure can promote densification and stabilize compact spinel frameworks [Reasonable Inference], but the provided high-pressure analogue also reports repeated processing until single phase, implying difficult phase control (SS, TS). If pre-reacted HgRh₂S₄ powder is used, structural reconstruction may be limited [Reasonable Inference]. However, Hg volatility and capsule compatibility remain serious concerns [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are moderate, but practical implementation is challenging.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The spinel target contains a large conventional Fd-3m cell and a substantial cation size/coordination contrast between tetrahedral Hg and octahedral Rh [Reasonable Inference], triggering two amorphization checks. The structure has edge-sharing RhS₆ octahedra and corner-sharing HgS₄–RhS₆ connections, but no face-sharing is described, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered. Spinel analogues such as HgIn₂S₄ and CuRh₂S₄ crystallize successfully (CS, SS, TS), reducing concern that the framework is intrinsically glass-forming. Remaining uncertainty is whether Hg disorder or off-stoichiometry could broaden the phase field [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgRh₂S₄ is likely a true ground-state phase rather than a strongly metastable phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple close analogues are also on or near the hull (CS, SS, TS). The analogue support is unusually strong: HgIn₂S₄ confirms tetrahedral HgS₄ in a spinel sulfide (CS), while CuRh₂S₄ and FeRh₂S₄ confirm the Rh₂S₄ spinel framework (SS, TS). The most important enabling factor is the direct structural match to known Fd-3m sulfide spinels with very similar Rh–S and Hg–S bond lengths (CS, SS, TS). The most important limiting factor is practical control of Hg/S volatility in sealed high-temperature synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. A binary-sulfide route using HgS + Rh₂S₃ is therefore the best first attempt because it combines chemical compatibility with manageable solid-state conditions (CS, SS, TS).
