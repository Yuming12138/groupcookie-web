### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound and receives a high F1 score of 98. Thermodynamically similar sulfides also include multiple E_hull = 0 examples, such as CeScS₃, BaCe₂MnS₅, Ce₂S₃, Ce(LuS₂)₃, and Ce₃CuSiS₇ (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar sulfides include both stable and near-stable examples, e.g., Lu₂ZnS₄, CdTm₂S₄, and CdEr₂S₄ at E_hull = 0 and Tm₂ZnS₄ near the hull (CS1–CS4). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of Na-ordering complications [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because high-temperature sealed sulfide synthesis commonly requires 900–1200 °C-equivalent conditions, long holds, and controlled vacuum/H₂S atmospheres in the analogues (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS2). The target additionally contains Na, so Na₂S volatility/moisture sensitivity and Na loss are important extrapolated risks [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids high pressure, which is favorable and consistent with the ampoule/tube methods in CS1, CS3, CS4, SS2, and SS4. Overall, severity is moderate-to-high, giving F2 = 45.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfide precursors are chemically appropriate because many analogues use binary sulfides (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS2), but these are not highly reactive low-temperature precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Hf diffusion is expected to be slow due to refractory Hf chemistry [Reasonable Inference], consistent with analogue syntheses requiring several days to a week or repeated annealing (CS1, CS3, SS1, SS5). Structural reconstruction from HfS₂-like units may be only partial, but Na ordering and layered P3m1 formation remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 = 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from lower proposed temperatures because KBr flux growth was successful at 1120 K for Tm₂ZnS₄ and at 1000 °C for CdEr₂S₄ crystal growth (CS1, CS4). It still requires sealed/vacuum conditions and slow cooling, as in CS1 and CS4, and moisture-sensitive Na₂S/halide handling is an extrapolated penalty [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is required, matching CS1, CS4, SS5, and TS5. This gives a moderate F2 = 55.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux should improve mass transport and crystallization relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/CS4 directly show halide flux can improve sulfide crystal growth. The main kinetic issue remains incorporation and ordering of Na with HfS₆ layers, which is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Na–Hf layered flux growth. Slow cooling and extended annealing are likely needed, consistent with CS1, CS4, SS5, and TS5. F3 is therefore 58.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest thermal budget because it targets Na insertion into a preformed HfS₂-like layered host at 200–500 °C [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires stringent oxygen/moisture exclusion and reactive Na handling [Reasonable Inference], and CS/SS/TS provide only general support for sealed sulfide processing rather than direct intercalation evidence. No high pressure or >1000 °C step is required after host formation [Reasonable Inference]. F2 = 65.

#### F3 — Route 3

Topochemical Na intercalation has the strongest kinetic logic because Na can move without requiring long-range Hf diffusion if the HfS₂ framework is retained [Reasonable Inference from target layered structure]. The target’s 2D sheet and edge-sharing HfS₆ octahedra support a host-preserving pathway [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact intercalation mechanism, so uncertainty remains significant. F3 = 80.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: Na and Hf have very different ionic sizes and coordination preferences [Reasonable Inference from target bonding]. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom cell or simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered based on available evidence. Some SS/TS analogues have complex mixed connectivities (SS2, TS4), but that complexity is not shown for the target. F4 = 80, indicating low-to-medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(HfS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of synthesizing ternary/quaternary sulfides by sealed solid-state, H₂S-assisted, and flux-assisted routes, but they do not directly prove synthesis of a Na–Hf layered sulfide. The most critical enabling factor is the target’s HfS₂-like two-dimensional edge-sharing HfS₆ framework, which suggests a lower-barrier Na intercalation pathway [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is Na chemical-potential control, because Na loss, overintercalation, or Na₂S/HfS₂ competing phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with topochemical intercalation preferred and flux/solid-state routes as backups.
