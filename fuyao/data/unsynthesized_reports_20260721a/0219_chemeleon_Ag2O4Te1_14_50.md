### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a very high thermodynamic stability score. Thermodynamically similar compounds Ag2Te2O7, Ag4CuTeO6, Ag2SeO4, AgSbO3, and Ag2BiO3 are stable or near-stable with E_hull values from 0 to ~0.0055 eV/atom for the closest TS examples (TS-1 to TS-5). CS and SS also contain stable/near-stable Ag–Te–O or Ag–oxide analogues, supporting that this chemistry is not intrinsically inaccessible (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1 to SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclude a more easily crystallized polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, 380–600 °C in analogues, which avoids high-temperature severity penalties (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The main severity is high-pressure oxygen/autoclave operation: Ag2Te2O7 needs 500 MPa O2 for pure phase, and Ag4CuTeO6 uses ~380–420 MPa O2 (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The scoring rubric does not penalize pressures below 5 GPa, but the controlled oxidizing atmosphere justifies a deduction. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates volatile precursor sealing as a major factor, so that item is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable for a solid-state oxide route because very similar Ag–Te–O phases form within 36 h to several days at only 380–600 °C (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The target’s TeO6 octahedra match the octahedral tellurate motifs in Ag2Te2O7 and Ag4CuTeO6 (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The remaining kinetic risk is phase selectivity among Ag2TeO3, AgTeO3, Ag2Te2O7, and the target Ag2TeO4, since CS notes phase-control difficulty for some silver tellurates (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Solution precipitation is mild because Ag2SeO4 is formed by aqueous mixing and drying at 110 °C under ambient atmosphere (CS-3, TS-3). Ag2TeO3 also forms by precipitation from AgNO3 and K2TeO3, showing that Ag–tellurium oxoanion precipitation is chemically plausible (CS-4). The penalty is mainly multi-step handling—precipitation, isolation, washing, drying, and possible recrystallization. Insufficient evidence from SS supports structurally similar precipitation, so SS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Aqueous AgNO3/tellurate precipitation would have excellent precursor activity and minimal diffusion barriers, following the Ag2SeO4 precedent (CS-3, TS-3). However, the target is not a simple isolated tetrahedral oxoanion salt; it contains edge-sharing TeO6 octahedra, whereas Ag2SeO4 contains tetrahedral SeO4 units (CS-3). Ag2TeO3 precipitation also produced poorly formed crystals, suggesting that crystallization and phase purity may be problematic (CS-4). Thus kinetics for nucleation are favorable, but kinetics for ordering into the predicted P-1 framework are uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3

The alkaline hydrothermal route is less severe thermally, supported by Ag2BiO3 formation at 90–130 °C in concentrated KOH (TS-5). It likely requires controlled oxidizing conditions because high-valent Ag–Te–O analogues are stabilized under elevated O2 pressure, especially Ag2Te2O7 (CS-2, TS-1). It is also multi-step because precursor preparation, alkaline reaction, washing, and drying are involved, similar to TS-5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS for a direct Ag2TeO4 hydrothermal method makes this route more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal/alkaline chemistry should reduce diffusion barriers and improve crystallization relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], consistent with well-crystalline Ag2BiO3 powders from low-temperature alkaline solution (TS-5). Strong base may solubilize tellurium oxide/tellurate species and allow TeO6 network assembly [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no provided CS/SS/TS example demonstrates a hydrothermal Ag–Te(VI) phase with the target’s edge-sharing TeO6 connectivity. Therefore, kinetics are promising but less directly validated than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 4

Ambient-pressure ceramic oxidation avoids high pressure and high temperature, and ceramic AgSbO3 analogues form at 500–750 °C by standard solid-state methods (CS-5, TS-4). However, the closest Ag–Te(VI) analogue Ag2Te2O7 requires high oxygen pressure and decomposes in Ar at 560 °C, indicating oxygen chemical potential is a serious condition variable (CS-2, TS-1). Flowing O2 is therefore treated as a controlled/reactive atmosphere. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports ambient-pressure Ag2TeO4 formation directly.

#### F3 — Route 4

Oxide precursors are accessible, but the absence of high oxygen pressure may slow or prevent oxidation of Te(IV) to Te(VI) [Reasonable Inference based on CS-2/TS-1]. Similar Ag–Te–O phases do form at moderate temperatures, but under elevated oxygen pressure rather than ambient pressure (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1). Structural reconstruction into edge-sharing TeO6 octahedra is therefore much less assured than in Route 1. Competing lower-valent Ag2TeO3 is a plausible kinetic sink because Ag2TeO3 is easily precipitated and known as an Ag–Te–O phase (CS-4) [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines low-coordinate Ag–O units with TeO6 octahedra, and similar Ag–Te–O structures show diverse Ag coordination and TeO5/TeO6 environments (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1). The cation size/coordination mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge-sharing TeO6 octahedra but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2, so it is treated as not triggered but uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag2TeO4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also stable or nearly stable (TS-1 to TS-5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for high-O2-pressure routes: chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close Ag–Te–O analogues repeatedly require elevated oxygen pressure at 380–600 °C (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential, which should stabilize Te(VI)-like TeO6 octahedra and suppress lower-valent tellurite phases [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-2/TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, since related silver tellurates show sensitivity to oxygen pressure and some difficulty obtaining pure phases (CS-1, CS-2). Overall, the target is quite synthesizeable in a specialized high-pressure oxygen apparatus, but less certain by mild precipitation or ambient-pressure ceramic methods.
