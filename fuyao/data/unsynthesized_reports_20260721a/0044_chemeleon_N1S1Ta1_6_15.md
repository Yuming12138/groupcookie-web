### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s computed energy above hull is **0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase by the supplied target data. CS analogues provide supportive but not decisive evidence: HfNBr, UNCl, and UPS include E_hull = 0 phases (CS2, CS4, CS5), while TaPS is only slightly above hull at 0.0586 eV/atom (CS1). SS/TS analogues likewise include stable or near-stable Ta–S/Ta–N phases, including Ta9Ni2S6 at E_hull = 0 (SS2/TS1), Ta9Co2S6 at 0.005 eV/atom (SS5/TS2), and TaN at 0.0206 eV/atom (TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or finite-temperature stability against S/N loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because the closest R-3m layered analogue β-HfNBr requires **3–5 GPa and 1000–1100 °C** (CS3), and chemically related TaPS requires **1200 °C and 65 kbar** (CS1). Specialized capsules, BN crucibles, multianvil equipment, controlled cooling, and quenching are explicitly part of the β-HfNBr synthesis (CS3). The score is reduced strongly because sulfur-bearing fluxes are volatile and toxic by extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether TaNS truly needs high pressure; if the target’s E_hull = 0 phase is stable at ambient pressure, the severity would be overestimated.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids external high pressure, which improves F2 relative to Route 1. However, CVT still requires sealed ampoules, a controlled temperature gradient, and volatile halogen transport agents, as shown for UPS single-crystal growth using I/Br carriers in closed silica tubes around 1000 °C (CS5). Sulfur volatility and possible ampoule pressure are inferred risks [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sulfur mass-loss issues in Ta sulfide analogues (SS2/SS3/TS1–TS3). The main uncertainty is that no provided source demonstrates Ta–N–S CVT directly.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses lower temperatures than the high-pressure route, based on UNCl ammonolysis at 525–650 °C (CS4) and Ta nitride formation from NH3/N2 sources (TS4). The score is still reduced by the need for reactive NH3/H2S or sulfur-buffered atmospheres [Reasonable Inference]. SS4/TS5 show that high-pressure NH3 chemistry can yield pure Ta nitrides but requires 0.6 GPa and 5 days, indicating nontrivial condition control. Remaining uncertainty is whether ammonolysis will form the ordered TaNS phase rather than binary TaN/TaSx mixtures.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from the close structural match between target TaNS and β-HfNBr: both are R-3m, 2D, and have 7-coordinate metal centers with tetrahedral N environments (CS3). High-pressure flux synthesis can accelerate crystallization, as β-HfNBr single crystals form in 1–1.5 h under NH4X flux (CS3). However, Ta3N5/Ta2S5 precursors are refractory solids [Reasonable Inference], and forming Ta–N–S ordering may still require significant diffusion. The main uncertainty is whether sulfur substitution for Br preserves the same reconstructive pathway.

#### F3 — Route 2

CVT can improve transport kinetics by mobilizing species, as UPS crystals are grown using iodine or bromine transport (CS5). Nevertheless, the target contains both N and S, and no source shows simultaneous N/S control in a Ta CVT system; therefore composition control and structural reconstruction receive modest scores. The sealed-tube α-HfNBr analogue is Pmmn rather than R-3m (CS2), while the R-3m version requires high pressure (CS3), which weakens structural transferability. Remaining uncertainty is high because the route may produce binary sulfides/nitrides or halogenated Ta species [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

NH3 is a chemically active nitrogen source, supported by UNCl ammonolysis (CS4) and TaN precursor formation from NH3/N2 (TS4). Gas–solid reactions, however, are commonly diffusion-limited in refractory Ta compounds [Reasonable Inference], and Li2Ta3N5 synthesis takes 5 days under NH3 pressure (SS4/TS5). Structural reconstruction into the target R-3m layered TaNS is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS. The route is plausible because it can tune N chemical potential, but it must also maintain sulfur activity to prevent sulfur depletion [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ta, so cation-size disorder is absent by formula, and the target description does not report a large multication cell. The target’s polyhedral network includes edge- and corner-sharing NTa4 tetrahedra, with no face-sharing reported; β-HfNBr analogously contains edge/corner-sharing NHf4 tetrahedra (CS3). CS/SS/TS do not directly provide the target unit-cell atom count, so that check is supported primarily by the target formula/structure and only indirectly by structural analogy. The amorphization risk is therefore low, although thin-film or low-temperature routes could still yield amorphous Ta–S–N without sufficient annealing [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaNS is predicted to be a **ground-state phase** because the supplied target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The best structural support comes from β-HfNBr, which shares the R-3m layered motif and 7-coordinate metal / tetrahedral nitrogen framework, but that analogue requires high-pressure synthesis (CS3). The strongest chemical support comes from the existence of stable Ta–S and Ta–N analogues, including Ta9Ni2S6 and TaN near the hull (SS2/TS1, TS4), plus TaPS high-pressure synthesis (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic driving force against decomposition, supported by the target E_hull and stable analogues (CS2, CS4, CS5, SS2/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is **simultaneous control of sulfur and nitrogen chemical potentials** while crystallizing the R-3m layered phase, because sulfur volatility and phase competition are documented concerns in Ta sulfide analogues (SS2/SS3/TS1–TS3), while R-3m nitride-halide analogues require pressure and quenching (CS3).
