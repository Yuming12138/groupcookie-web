### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the predicted convex-hull ground state. The nearest Nb-containing analogues Cs₂LiNbS₄ and Cs₂NbAgS₄ are also E_hull = 0.0, providing a strong thermodynamic signal for stable Cs–Nb–S tetrahedral sulfides (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). CS additionally shows several Cs thiometallates/chalcogenides at E_hull = 0.0, so CS supports the general stability of Cs-rich sulfides but is less specific to Nb (CS1–CS4).

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Flux growth uses moderate temperature, 773 K in the Cs₂LiNbS₄ analogue, so there is no high-temperature penalty (SS1, TS1). The penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/alkali sulfides, glovebox handling, and multiple processing steps including washing (SS1, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS for the exact Cs₃NbS₄ flux route, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity score.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Flux media should improve reaction kinetics relative to direct reaction of refractory Nb metal because Cs₂LiNbS₄ formed at 773 K from Nb/S/Li₂S/A₂S₃ (SS1, TS1). Nb–S distances in the target match Cs₂LiNbS₄ and Cs₂NbAgS₄, supporting accessible NbS₄ tetrahedral formation (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). The main uncertainty is that Nb/Ta flux products were lower-yield and some related compositions failed, implying narrow phase stability/composition windows (SS1, TS1).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Direct solid-state synthesis is not extreme in temperature because Cs₃BS₃ was made at 720 °C and related sealed-tube chalcogenides at ~670 K to 720 °C (CS3, SS3, TS3, SS2, TS2). Severity remains moderate because sealed low-pressure tubes, inert handling, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive Cs₂S are required (CS3, SS3, TS3). TS provides similar sealed-tube evidence through thermodynamic analogues, while CS/SS are more direct for practical conditions.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Binary sulfides should reduce redox burden compared with Nb metal [Reasonable Inference], but solid-state cation diffusion and ordering remain slower than solution or flux routes. Cs₃BS₃ confirms Cs-rich sulfides can form by solid-state sintering, and Cs₂LiNbS₄/Cs₂NbAgS₄ confirm compatible NbS₄ units (CS3, SS3, TS3, SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). The uncertainty is that no exact Cs₃NbS₄ binary-sulfide synthesis is given, so competing phases such as Cs₂LiNbS₄-like or other Cs–Nb sulfides may intervene [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The azide route runs at only ~500 °C but requires controlled azide decomposition, sealed ampoules, vacuum/inert atmosphere, and multi-step handling (CS1). These features make the route more hazardous than the nominal temperature suggests. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for azide processing, so they are neutral for this route-specific severity score.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The kinetic advantage is high precursor activity because CS1 states the azide route introduces highly reactive heavy alkali metals without using elemental Cs. This can help sulfidation and Cs incorporation at moderate temperature [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. However, CS1 is In-based rather than Nb-based, so structural reconstruction into NbS₄ tetrahedral salts remains uncertain; insufficient direct Nb signal from CS1 is treated as a limitation.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
The solution/hydrothermal route has the mildest condition severity because Cs₂MoS₄ formed at 423 K/24 h and Cs₂WS₄ crystallized from dilute aqueous solution under air (CS2, CS4). Deductions remain for possible sealed hydrothermal operation, sulfide/polysulfide toxicity, and the likely need to pre-synthesize a thioniobate precursor [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not directly address wet-chemical synthesis for Nb thiometallates, so their condition-severity signal is insufficient and neutral.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Solution crystallization should avoid slow solid-state diffusion, and CS2/CS4 show Cs thiometallate crystals can form under mild conditions. The target’s isolated NbS₄ tetrahedron makes a preformed [NbS₄]³⁻ precursor especially attractive [Reasonable Inference from target coordination and analog thiometallate chemistry in CS4]. The uncertainty is Nb(V) hydrolysis/oxo-sulfide competition, which is not resolved by the Mo/W analogues (CS2, CS4).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The major amorphization/structural-disorder trigger is the large Cs–Nb cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target does not appear to require a large >40-atom cell based on its Cs₃NbS₄ formula unit, and its primary covalent unit is a simple NbS₄ tetrahedron, which lowers glass-forming concern [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 show more complex mixed edge/corner connectivity in Cs₂LiNbS₄, but that is a different Li-containing network, so it is not taken as decisive evidence for high amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃NbS₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close Cs–Nb–S analogues are also on the hull (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). The strongest structural support comes from Cs₂LiNbS₄ and Cs₂NbAgS₄, both of which contain NbS₄ tetrahedra with Nb–S distances essentially matching the target (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). The most enabling factor is the simple charge-balanced thioniobate description Cs₃[NbS₄], which suggests solution crystallization from a preformed [NbS₄]³⁻ source may bypass sluggish solid-state Nb diffusion [Reasonable Inference supported by CS2/CS4]. The most limiting factor is phase and hydrolysis control: Nb(V) sulfides may form oxo-sulfides or competing Cs–Nb–S frameworks if sulfur activity, pH, or Cs:Nb ratio is not tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with Route 4 preferred and flux/solid-state routes as credible fallbacks.
