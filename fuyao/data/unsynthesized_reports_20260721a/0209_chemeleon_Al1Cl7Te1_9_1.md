### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the computed convex hull and warranting a high F1 score. Stable analogues are abundant: CS1–CS4 report E_hull = 0.0, and SS1/SS3/SS4 plus TS1–TS5 also include hull-stable or near-hull chloride phases. CS5 is a chemically similar TeCl₆ cluster compound but has high E_hull = 1.078 eV/atom, so the chemical-similarity evidence is not uniformly positive; nevertheless, Al-containing and structurally/thermodynamically closer analogues are more supportive (CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability for molecular/low-dimensional chlorides can be sensitive to reference phases and dispersion effects [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Molten-Salt / Chloroaluminate Flux

The route is kinetically attractive because AlCl₃/NaCl melts produce Al–chalcogen–chloride phases at only 130 °C over 3–6 days (SS1, SS3; TS1, TS2, TS4). The severity score is reduced because these analogues require Ar gloveboxes, sealed ampoules, corrosive melts, and multi-step crystal separation (SS1, SS3; TS1, TS2). The local AlCl₄ motif transfers well, but the target’s 1D AlCl₄–TeCl₆ ribbon is not directly observed in the analogues, which are mainly 0D/tetragonal cluster phases (SS1, SS3; TS1, TS2). The main uncertainty is whether the melt stabilizes AlTeCl₇ rather than known Al–Bi–Te–Cl or simple AlCl₄⁻/TeCl₆²⁻ salts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-Ampoule CVT

CVT is supported by chemically similar SeCl₄ + metal chloride reactions forming chloride-rich chalcogen complexes at 140–155 °C in sealed ampoules (CS2, CS3) and by Te–Bi–Cl transport at 170 → 100 °C (SS5). Kinetics should be favorable because volatile chlorides bypass bulk diffusion, but the method requires vacuum sealing and a temperature gradient, lowering F2 (CS2, CS3, SS5). The structural match is moderate because CS2/CS3 have chalcogen chloride octahedral motifs, but not AlTeCl₇ ribbons. Uncertainty centers on AlCl₃/TeCl₄ vapor pressures and whether transport yields the correct stoichiometric 1:1:7 phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Dry Solution Lewis-Acid Crystallization

This route has the best weighted score because it uses mild conditions: CS1 demonstrates dry solution mixing of reactive chloride Lewis acids at room temperature/reflux, and TS5 demonstrates Group-13 chloride crystallization after brief heating at 80 °C. F2 remains penalized because all operations require very dry conditions and air-sensitive chlorides (CS1, TS5). Kinetically, solution assembly is favorable, but structural reconstruction is uncertain because CS1 and TS5 support 0D cluster/crystal formation rather than a confirmed 1D AlTeCl₇ ribbon. The key uncertainty is whether the target’s extended ribbon can nucleate from solution rather than isolating molecular AlCl₃·TeCl₄ adducts or solvates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Direct Sealed Melt Growth

CS4 supports direct chloride melt growth from AlCl₃-containing binary chloride mixtures at 250 °C with slow cooling under Ar. SS4/TS3 further support sealed ampoule sublimation/crystal growth for volatile metal halides at 100–150 °C. Kinetics should be reasonable in a molten/volatile chloride medium, and the target’s AlCl₄–octahedron corner/edge sharing resembles CS4’s AlCl₄–CdCl₆ motif. The principal uncertainty is chemical transferability because TeCl₄ is not simply a CdCl₂ analogue and may generate competing molecular tellurium chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger: a large cation size/coordination mismatch between small tetrahedral Al and larger octahedral Te, inferred from standard ionic-radius chemistry and the reported AlCl₄/TeCl₆ environments [Reasonable Inference]. The atom count trigger is not activated because the supplied target description contains one Al, one Te, and seven Cl sites rather than a >40-atom cell. The mixed-connectivity trigger is also not fully activated because the target reports corner and edge sharing but no face sharing. CS/SS/TS provide analogues with corner/edge-sharing motifs, especially CS4 and SS3, but do not provide direct target amorphization evidence, so the F4 uncertainty remains moderate.

AlTeCl₇ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close chloride analogues are also hull-stable (CS1–CS4; SS1/SS3/SS4; TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from low-temperature chloride chemistry: dry solution mixing of Lewis-acidic chlorides (CS1, TS5), AlCl₃/NaCl molten-salt growth of Al–chalcogen–chloride phases (SS1, SS3; TS1, TS2), and sealed-ampoule chalcogen chloride transport (CS2, CS3, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of AlCl₃/TeCl₄ in dry solution, molten chloride, or vapor-transport conditions, which should allow assembly at low temperature. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target is a specific 1D AlCl₄–TeCl₆ ribbon, while many analogues form 0D molecular clusters or different chloroaluminate salts (CS1, SS1, SS3, TS5). Moisture sensitivity and volatile chloride stoichiometry control are additional practical limitations common to all plausible routes.
