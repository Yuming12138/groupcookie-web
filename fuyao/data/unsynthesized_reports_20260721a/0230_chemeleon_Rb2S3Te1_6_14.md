### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 stability bracket. The strongest analogue K₂TeS₃ is also E_hull = 0.0 and appears in CS, SS, and TS as the closest analogue (CS2, SS1, TS1). Other Rb/Cs/K chalcogenide analogues in the datasets also have E_hull = 0.0, so CS/SS/TS consistently support thermodynamic accessibility rather than metastability (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is computational/finite-temperature stability, not static hull stability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The flux route is mild in temperature because K₂TeS₃ forms at 573 K, so no high-temperature deduction is applied (CS2, SS1, TS1). The deductions arise from evacuated/Ar sealed ampoule operation, volatile sulfur/tellurium chemistry, multi-step flux growth/washing, and air-sensitive chalcogenide handling (CS2, SS1, TS1; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure requirements, so pressure is scored as non-applicable rather than penalized.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because the polysulfide flux provides a liquid/reactive medium and the K₂TeS₃ analogue crystallizes at only ~300 °C (CS2, SS1, TS1). Structural reconstruction is minimal because K₂TeS₃ has the same A₂TeS₃ motif and similar Te–S bond lengths to the target (CS2, SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Rb₂TeS₃ selects the same low-symmetry polymorph rather than an alternative Rb–Te–S phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The elemental ampoule route is below 1000 °C when adapted from K₂TeSe₃ at 900 °C, so no extreme-temperature deduction applies (SS5, TS5). It still requires vacuum sealing, inert handling, management of volatile chalcogens, and reactive Rb metal, leading to substantial condition-severity deductions (SS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS for exact elemental synthesis of Rb₂TeS₃, but CS5 supports elemental Rb sulfide crystal growth generally.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The elemental route benefits from low-melting/reactive components and precedent for alkali tellurochalcogenide formation from elements (SS5, TS5). The K₂TeSe₃ analogue is structurally similar but uses Se, so the structural reconstruction score is slightly lower than the exact sulfide flux analogue (SS5, TS5). Phase competition among Rb sulfides/tellurides remains the main uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The Rb₂S route uses sealed evacuated tubes at ~800 °C based on Rb₂GeS₃ synthesis, so it avoids >1000 °C penalties (CS1). It is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multi-step Rb₂S preparation/handling, and air-sensitive sulfide precursors (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Rb₂S + Te + S synthesis of A₂TeS₃, so they are neutral for this route’s condition evidence.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Rb₂S is a chemically active sulfide source, and Rb₂GeS₃ formation from Rb₂S/Ge/S shows workable Rb–S solid-state kinetics (CS1). However, converting Rb₂S + Te + S into isolated TeS₃ units is less structurally direct than copying K₂TeS₃, so structural reconstruction is only moderate (CS1 versus CS2/SS1/TS1). The uncertainty is whether Te is incorporated as TeS₃ rather than forming binary tellurides or polysulfides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Solvothermal synthesis has mild temperature, as Cs₂GeSe₃ formed at 170 °C over 7 d (CS3). It is penalized for sealed autoclave operation, multi-step handling, and toxic amine/thiophenol-type solvents (CS3; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient solvothermal evidence for the target family, so they are neutral for Route 4 condition transferability.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Solution-mediated transport can be kinetically favorable and avoids bulk diffusion, consistent with Cs₂GeSe₃ solvothermal crystallization (CS3). However, no provided structural or thermodynamic analogue demonstrates solvothermal synthesis of A₂TeS₃, and Te–S redox/speciation control is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, Route 4 has favorable mild conditions but weaker structural predictability.

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target has a small composition and no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell, reducing amorphization risk from structural complexity. It has edge/corner-sharing RbS₆ octahedra and TeS₃ trigonal non-coplanar units, but no face-sharing connectivity is reported in the target; K₂TeS₃ similarly has edge/corner-sharing alkali sulfide octahedra (CS2, SS1, TS1). The one triggered risk is the large cation size/chemistry contrast between Rb and Te [Reasonable Inference], giving a low–medium amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂TeS₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest analogues in CS/SS/TS are also reported at E_hull = 0.0. The evidence is particularly strong because K₂TeS₃ is simultaneously chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close and has an experimentally demonstrated polysulfide flux synthesis (CS2, SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of the TeS₃ trigonal non-coplanar motif in a stable A₂TeS₃ analogue, which strongly supports transfer to Rb₂TeS₃ (CS2, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Rb/S/Te volatility and air sensitivity during sealed-tube or flux synthesis, which is repeatedly indicated by evacuated ampoule, glovebox, and air-sensitive handling requirements in analogues (CS2, SS1, TS1, SS5, TS5). Overall, synthesis is quite plausible, with flux growth the most targeted route.
