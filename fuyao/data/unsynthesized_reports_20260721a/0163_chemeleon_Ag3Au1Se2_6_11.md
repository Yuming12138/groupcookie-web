### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a high score (target data). Thermodynamically similar compounds Ag8SnSe6, RbAg3Se2, Au4Tl2S3, and RbAu3Se2 also have E_hull = 0.0 or near-zero, supporting the idea that related Ag/Au/chalcogenide phases can be stable (TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee an easily accessible phase field or absence of kinetic traps [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct solid-state route uses standard sealed-ampoule chemistry, as in Ag3CuS2 and Ag8SnSe6 syntheses (CS-2, CS-5/SS-1/TS-1). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Se containment, and multi-step annealing/regrinding, all consistent with chalcogenide ampoule routes (CS-5/TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact safe Se vapor pressure window of Ag3AuSe2, so the temperature program is extrapolated.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Dense Ag and Au metals lower precursor activity, but direct elemental reactions do succeed for Ag8SnSe6 and Ag3CuS2 (CS-2, CS-5/SS-1/TS-1). Diffusion should be moderate because Ag–Se systems are relatively mobile [Reasonable Inference], but Au incorporation and layered ordering may be slower. Long annealing in related phases motivates a moderate kinetics score rather than a high one (CS-1/SS-4, CS-5/TS-1).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed-tube melt route has similar ampoule and Se-volatility penalties to Route 1, but does not require >1000 °C in the proposed implementation [Reasonable Inference]. Melt and slow-cooling routes are supported for Ag3SI, RbAg3Se2, Ag8SnSe6, and Au4Tl2S3 (CS-4/SS-2, TS-3, CS-5/TS-1, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on whether Ag3AuSe2 melts congruently, so the proposed “melt/partial melt” window is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Binary Ag2Se and a liquid/partial-liquid regime should improve reaction kinetics relative to dry elemental diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Analogues demonstrate that slow cooling from high temperature can crystallize Ag and Au chalcogenides (CS-4/SS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The score is limited by possible competing Ag2Se/AuSe/Au metal phase formation, since analogous Ag3SI melt growth produced phase mixtures requiring crystal separation (CS-4/SS-2).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Mechanochemical synthesis avoids high furnace temperatures but still requires inert milling, sealed annealing, and toxic/volatile Se management [Reasonable Inference]. Its reference basis is strong only for Ag3CuS2, where ball milling helped produce high-purity material (CS-1/SS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for mechanochemical Au–Se formation, so transferability is moderate.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Ball milling improves precursor activity and shortens diffusion lengths, supported by high-purity Ag3CuS2 obtained by mechanical alloying (CS-1/SS-4). However, Ag3CuS2 still needed annealing or careful processing, and Ag3SI analogues show ordering can require prolonged anneals (CS-1/SS-4, CS-3/SS-5). The target’s 2D ordered Ag/Au/Se sheet likely requires post-milling crystallization rather than direct formation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Supercritical ethylenediamine operates at lower temperature, but requires sealed tubes, autoclave pressure, toxic solvent, and controlled atmosphere (SS-3, TS-2). The formal scoring table does not penalize 2400 psi as “high pressure” because it is far below 5 GPa, but it is still experimentally specialized [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for alkali-free Ag–Au–Se solvothermal formation.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Solution-mediated transport in supercritical ethylenediamine gives favorable kinetics for Ag chalcogenides, including KAg3S2 and RbAg5Se3 (SS-3, TS-2). The route is limited by unclear Au solubility/speciation and by possible alkali incorporation if polyselenide mineralizers are used [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, it receives good kinetic but weaker transferability confidence.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has relatively simple stoichiometry and no evidence for a >40-atom cell or complex mixed face/edge/vertex-sharing framework, which supports a low-to-medium amorphization risk (target description; insufficient exact cell-size evidence from CS/SS/TS). One risk trigger is cation mismatch/electronic preference between Ag and Au coordination environments [Reasonable Inference], consistent with varied Au–S/Au–Se bonding in thermodynamic analogues (TS-4, TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is whether the monoclinic 2D sheet orders only over a narrow thermal window.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag3AuSe2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The synthesis is supported best by Ag-rich selenide and Au chalcogenide analogues made in evacuated quartz tubes, controlled-cooling melts, and direct solid-state reactions (CS-5/SS-1/TS-1, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with chemically soft Ag/Au–Se bonding that should permit formation under sealed chalcogenide conditions [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-5/TS-1/TS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: Ag–Se binaries, Au–Se binaries, elemental Au, or off-stoichiometric Ag-rich selenides may compete [Reasonable Inference; phase-mixture/phase-control issues are reported in CS-4/SS-2 and CS-5/TS-1]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with the sealed-tube melt/slow-cooling route as the most practical first attempt.
