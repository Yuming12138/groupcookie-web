### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the supplied target data, which by the given rubric warrants 90–100 and is scored 100. Thermodynamic analogues reinforce this: Cu₂GeS₃ is on the hull in TS1, and tetragonal Cu₂ZnGeS₄ is also on the hull in TS4. CS and SS both contain multiple near-hull sulfide analogues, including Cu₂GeS₃ and Cu₂SnS₃, supporting that tetrahedral Cu chalcogenides are thermodynamically accessible (CS1, SS2, SS4). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or unique ordering of Cu₃GeS₄ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state elements

Condition severity is moderate because the best direct Cu–Ge–S analogue uses high but standard ampoule temperatures of 600–850 °C, vacuum sealing, and long anneals (CS1, SS3, TS1). Kinetics are slower because elemental Cu/Ge/S reactions require interdiffusion and long cation ordering, evidenced by 30–35 day treatments in Cu₂GeS₃ analogues (CS1, CS2, SS2). Structurally, this route is strongly supported because both target and analogues are tetrahedral CuS₄/GeS₄ networks (CS1, SS3, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Cu-rich Cu₃GeS₄ competes with Cu₂S or Cu₂GeS₃ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary-sulfide sintering

This route has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still requires sealed evacuated ampoules, heating, grinding, and annealing, as seen for Cu₂ZnGeS₄ and related tetragonal sulfides (TS4, CS5). Kinetics are better than elemental reaction because preformed sulfides should provide higher precursor activity and shorter diffusion paths [Reasonable Inference], while TS4 demonstrates a close I-42m Cu–Zn–Ge–S framework synthesized at 700 °C. Structural reconstruction is favorable due to the close stannite/chalcopyrite-like framework match with TS4. Remaining uncertainty is whether removing Zn and increasing Cu content destabilizes the cation order [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

Condition severity is moderate: CS3 uses a sealed evacuated silica tube, KCl/LiCl flux, 550 °C, and 6 weeks, so temperature is mild but duration and sealed flux handling are nontrivial. Kinetics benefit from flux-mediated mass transport, which can enable crystal growth at lower temperatures than dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference from CS3]. Structural support is indirect because CS3 concerns a related Cu–Si–chalcogenide rather than exact Cu–Ge–S. The uncertainty is flux solubility and whether Cu₃GeS₄ nucleates instead of Cu₂GeS₃/Cu₂S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Iodine CVT

Severity is higher than flux/solid-state because iodine is volatile/toxic and a two-zone sealed ampoule with controlled transport is required, even though temperatures are below 1000 °C (SS1). Kinetically, CVT can be effective because SS1 reports single crystals of structurally similar Cu-containing tetrahedral sulfides grown by iodine transport. Composition control is the largest weakness, since different volatile iodides/sulfide species may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for exact Cu₃GeS₄ CVT behavior, so this route is best viewed as a crystal-growth follow-up after bulk synthesis.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Solvothermal synthesis

F2 is favorable because SS4 and TS5 show analogous Cu sulfides forming near 180 °C without special gas atmosphere, using standard autoclave or microwave solution equipment. F3 is high because soluble metal salts and thiourea provide reactive species and avoid long-range solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated for Cu₂SnS₃ and CZTS analogues (SS4, TS5). The key uncertainty is that supplied data do not show direct solvothermal Cu–Ge–S synthesis; Ge⁴⁺ hydrolysis and formation of amorphous Ge–S species may complicate phase purity [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, a post-anneal under sulfur may be required to achieve the ordered tetragonal phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is built from corner-sharing CuS₄ and GeS₄ tetrahedra, a motif repeatedly found in synthesized analogues, which lowers amorphization risk (CS1, SS2, SS3, TS4). One risk trigger is the size/charge mismatch between Cu⁺ and Ge⁴⁺ in tetrahedral sites [Reasonable Inference], which may encourage disorder or glassy sulfide formation at low temperature. However, there is no evidence for edge/face-sharing mixed connectivity in the target, and analogous corner-sharing sulfide frameworks crystallize successfully (SS3, TS4). Exact target unit-cell size is not established by CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as neutral/no trigger rather than a penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃GeS₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Cu–Ge–S and Cu–Zn–Ge–S thermodynamic analogues are also on or near the hull (TS1, TS4). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis of Cu–Ge–S tetrahedral frameworks, especially through sealed-ampoule solid-state chemistry (CS1, SS3, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the robust corner-sharing CuS₄/GeS₄ tetrahedral network that appears across chemically and structurally similar sulfides (CS1, SS2, SS3, TS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase and cation-order control, because close analogues require long annealing or repeated firing, and Cu-rich stoichiometry may compete with Cu₂S/Cu₂GeS₃ [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS2, TS4]. The solvothermal route offers the highest practical score, but it is more extrapolative than sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis because direct Cu–Ge–S solution evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.
