### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports high thermodynamic synthesizability. CS supports this because SnB₄O₇ is also on hull (CS-3), while SS/TS include multiple hull-stable or near-hull borates such as Pb borate and Sr/Ba borates (SS-1/TS-1, TS-3/TS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclusion of hydration/glass formation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 solid-state.**  
CS-5 demonstrates sealed-ampoule solid-state oxide synthesis at 973 K for 100 h, and SS-5 demonstrates high-temperature borate solid-state synthesis at 900 °C for 3 days. The deductions come from sealed/vacuum conditions, likely TeO₂ volatility, multiple processing steps, and TeO₂ toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS provide useful route evidence; TS is less directly useful for ambient solid-state B–Te oxide synthesis, so TS is scored neutral at 50 for this route-specific factor.

**F3 — Route 1 solid-state.**  
Oxide precursors are chemically direct and supported by CS-3/CS-5, but long sintering times in CS-5 and SS-5 indicate slow bulk kinetics. The target requires formation of a mixed BO₃/BO₄–TeO₃ network, so structural reconstruction is substantial [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether partial melting of B₂O₃/TeO₂ improves kinetics enough to avoid amorphous borotellurite glass [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 hydrothermal.**  
SS-1/TS-1 gives strong support for low-severity hydrothermal borate synthesis at 170 °C for 6 days under autogenous pressure, and CS-2 supports hydrothermal oxide synthesis at 200 °C. The main severity penalty is TeO₂ toxicity [Reasonable Inference], not pressure or temperature. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful positive evidence for hydrothermal feasibility.

**F3 — Route 2 hydrothermal.**  
H₃BO₃ is a reactive boron precursor in SS-1/TS-1 and SS-2/TS-2, and solution-mediated transport reduces diffusion limitations. The structural motif is plausible because hydrothermal borates with BO₃/BO₄ units are known in SS-1/TS-1 and 3-coordinate lone-pair oxide cations occur in CS-2/CS-4. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS example is a direct B–Te–O hydrothermal analogue, so Te(IV) speciation must be optimized [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 flux.**  
SS-2/TS-2 directly supports boric-acid flux growth at 200–240 °C for 7 days in closed Teflon autoclaves. The route is moderate in severity because closed vessels and toxic TeO₂ are required [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives only indirect support for flux chemistry, so CS is neutral at 50 for this route-specific severity factor.

**F3 — Route 3 flux.**  
Flux growth should lower kinetic barriers and aid crystallization, consistent with SS-2/TS-2 and controlled-cooling borate crystallization in SS-5. Phase control is not trivial because SS-2/TS-2 reports product dependence on reactant ratios and temperature. Direct Te flux evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so TeO₂ self-flux behavior is a [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 high pressure.**  
CS-1 and CS-3 strongly support high-pressure borate-related synthesis at ~7.5–8 GPa and up to 1100 °C. These conditions are severe because they require multi-anvil/DAC-type equipment, capsules, and controlled thermal schedules. SS and TS do not provide direct high-pressure evidence for this target, so SS and TS are neutral at 50 for this route-specific factor.

**F3 — Route 4 high pressure.**  
CS-3 shows that high pressure can crystallize borates rapidly once pressure/temperature conditions are reached. However, the target contains both trigonal BO₃ and tetrahedral BO₄ units, while high pressure may favor denser BO₄-rich alternatives [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetic success is plausible but equipment demands and possible phase mis-selection reduce the route score.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The cation-size/coordination mismatch between small B³⁺ and larger lone-pair Te⁴⁺ triggers one amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate >40 atoms per unit cell or edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. Borate/tellurite glasses are plausible competing products in melt/solid-state routes [Reasonable Inference], but hydrothermal/flux routes mitigate this by crystallizing from solution.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

B₂TeO₅ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogues support synthesis well in a general sense: CS provides chemically related oxide and borate examples, SS provides close borate framework motifs with BO₃/BO₄ units, and TS provides multiple hull-stable borate analogues. The strongest enabling factor is the combination of thermodynamic stability and low-temperature solution/flux routes that can assemble borate networks without long solid-state diffusion, as shown in SS-1/TS-1 and SS-2/TS-2. The main limiting factor is the absence of a direct B–Te–O reference synthesis in CS/SS/TS, making Te(IV) speciation and competing tellurite/borate phase formation uncertain. Overall, hydrothermal synthesis is the preferred first attempt, followed closely by boric-acid/TeO₂ flux growth.
