### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 scoring range by the provided rubric. The strongest analogue, LiCrS2, is also E_hull = 0.0 and has the same P-3m1 Caswellsilverite-like LiS6/MS6 topology (CS1, SS1, TS1). LiTiS2 and LiVS2 are also near-hull LiMS2 sulfides, supporting the chemical plausibility of stable Li–transition-metal sulfides (CS2, CS3). Remaining uncertainty is mainly redox/phase competition for Mn3+ sulfide chemistry, since LiMnSe2 is reported as a related but structurally different phase with higher E_hull in the data (CS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a low F2 because the closest exact analogue LiCrS2 uses 800–1100 °C and Ar saturated with CS2, including high-temperature gas handling and slow cooling (CS1/SS1/TS1). Its F3 is relatively high because the reference demonstrates formation of the same structure type from carbonate/oxide precursors, so the kinetic pathway is experimentally validated for an almost identical LiMS2 lattice (CS1/SS1/TS1). The main uncertainty is that Mn3+ may not respond identically to Cr3+ under CS2-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best F2 because the H2S analogue uses only 500 and 700 °C rather than >1000 °C, although the H2S atmosphere is toxic and controlled (CS2). Its F3 is favorable because two-stage H2S sulfidation with intermediate grinding is already used for LiMS2-type materials, and the target has the same LiMS2 stoichiometry and octahedral sulfide framework as the closest analogues (CS2, CS1/SS1/TS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Mn oxide converts cleanly to LiMnS2 or competes with MnS/Li2S phases [Reasonable Inference supported by CS5 phase-competition note].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because sealed ampoule sulfide synthesis avoids flowing toxic H2S but introduces sulfur vapor pressure, vacuum sealing, and air-sensitive Li2S/Li handling, as shown in LiTiS2 and Li2TeS3 analogues (CS3, SS5/TS3). Its kinetics score is lower because LiTiS2 required a two-week anneal and additional H2S treatment, and phase-pure material was difficult to prepare (CS3). The target’s exact P-3m1 structure is better supported by LiCrS2 than by the R-3m LiTiS2 analogue, so structural transfer is less direct (CS1/SS1/TS1 vs CS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has moderate condition severity because flux syntheses use sulfur-rich melts, slow cooling, inert or H2S atmospheres, and post-treatment steps (CS5, TS5). Its kinetics are favorable because flux/liquid media improve transport and have produced alkali transition-metal sulfides, including Mn sulfides (CS5, TS5). However, its structural transfer is weaker because the Na–Mn–S flux analogue is not the same P-3m1 LiMnS2 structure and MnS contamination was explicitly noted as a phase-purity issue (CS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure contains mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing octahedral connectivity, which increases structural frustration/amorphization risk during nonequilibrium synthesis relative to simple vertex-sharing networks (target data; CS1/SS1/TS1). The small P-3m1 cell reduces amorphization risk compared with large complex cells [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The Li/Mn cation size difference is slightly above the 15% trigger using common octahedral radii, so the final F4 is medium risk rather than low [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiMnS2 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the exact structural analogue LiCrS2 is also on the hull (target data; CS1/SS1/TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is strong because LiCrS2 matches the target’s P-3m1 Caswellsilverite-like octahedral framework almost exactly, including Li–S and M–S bond lengths (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of experimentally synthesized LiMS2 Caswellsilverite-like sulfides under both CS2 and H2S sulfidizing conditions (CS1, CS2). The main limiting factor is Mn-specific phase/redox control, especially suppression of MnS or alternative Li–Mn–S/selenide-type structures, since Mn sulfide flux chemistry reports impurity challenges and LiMnSe2 adopts a different structure (CS5, CS4). Overall, the best first attempt is H2S sulfidation because it combines direct LiMS2 precedent with lower temperature than CS2-CVT (CS2 vs CS1/SS1/TS1).
