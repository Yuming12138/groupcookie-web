### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum stability score under the required rubric. This is consistent with several related halides at or near the hull: Hf(SeCl6)2 and Zr(SeCl6)2 are on the hull (CS3, TS2, TS3), while TaTeCl9/WTeCl9/WTeBr9 are near-hull phases (CS1, CS2, SS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the provided thermodynamics do not specify competing AuCl, AuCl3, TeCl4, or Au–Te–Cl molecular phases, so Au-specific decomposition evidence from CS/SS/TS is insufficient; neutral source score 50 for that sub-question.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the relevant analogues require evacuated sealed ampoules, inert loading, volatile halides, and controlled temperature gradients (CS1, CS2, SS3). Its kinetic score is high because vapor-phase halide transport enables crystallization at only ~130–180 °C and because the target’s 1D ribbon topology closely matches Te–M–Cl ribbons in CS1 and CS2. The main uncertainty is whether Au(III) remains stable during transport rather than reducing to Au(I) or Au metal [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 because SS2 uses an evacuated sealed ampoule with SOCl2 at 150 °C, and such solvents/halides are volatile and reactive. Kinetics should be favorable because solvothermal chloride media allow molecular transport and near-quantitative conversion in the SS2 analogue. The uncertainty is structural: SS2 gives a zero-dimensional cluster, so the method may not template the 1D AuTeCl7 ribbon.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is experimentally feasible because thermodynamic analogues are made in evacuated sealed tubes at 280–400 °C with controlled cooling (TS1, TS5). Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because direct solid-state/vapor-assisted reaction may require larger structural reorganization, and TS1/TS5 are not close structural analogues to the 1D target. The key uncertainty is competing decomposition of AuCl3 or formation of simpler binary chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 has mild nominal conditions because CS4, CS5, and SS5 show chloride salts crystallizing from solution or slow evaporation under ambient conditions. Its kinetic mobility is excellent, but the structural-reconstruction score is low because the solution analogues are mainly zero-dimensional salts/hydrates rather than anhydrous 1D ribbons (CS4, CS5, SS5). The largest uncertainty is hydrolysis/solvation of Te(IV) chloride species and stabilization of alternative molecular chlorometallates [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has chemically distinct Au and Te coordination environments: Au is 4-coordinate rectangular see-saw-like, whereas Te is 6-coordinate distorted octahedral, supporting one amorphization trigger via cation size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. No evidence indicates a >40-atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence in the target, so those checks are not triggered. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AuTeCl7 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis case is strongly supported by chemically similar 1D Te–M–Cl ribbon compounds made by low-temperature sealed-ampoule CVT, especially TaTeCl9 and WTeCl9 (CS1, CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of molecular halide precursors under sealed chloride-rich vapor conditions, which allows crystallization at only ~130–180 °C (CS1, CS2, SS3). The most critical limiting factor is Au redox/volatility control, since Au(III) chloride chemistry may compete with Au(I) chloride or metallic Au formation [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable, with the best strategy being chloride-rich, dry, sealed-ampoule vapor transport rather than aqueous precipitation.
