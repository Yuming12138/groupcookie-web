### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin and supports the interpretation that ReSSe may be a ground-state phase in the computed phase diagram (target data). Thermodynamic analogues strongly reinforce this: ReSe₂, the closest analogue, is also E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1), and other TS analogues are also on hull (TS2–TS5). Chemically similar entries are somewhat mixed, with several stable or near-stable compounds but also TaPS and TaS₂In above hull; thus CS supports stability but less specifically than SS/TS (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is computational: mixed-anion ordering energies and finite-temperature S/Se disorder are not directly resolved by the analogue data [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1 CVT

CVT conditions are moderately severe because the closest ReSe₂ analogue uses high-temperature gradients in evacuated quartz-glass tubes with halogen transport agents and excess chalcogen (SS1, TS1). Related chalcogenides also use evacuated ampoules and halogens at week-scale durations (CS2–CS4; TS4–TS5), supporting the F2 deductions for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed reagents, and toxic halogens. Kinetically, CVT is favorable relative to direct solid-state diffusion because halogen-mediated vapor transport can move Re/chalcogen species despite refractory Re [Reasonable Inference], and the structural reconstruction penalty is low due to the close ReSe₂ topology (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is transport chemistry for a mixed S/Se compound, where S and Se may transport at different rates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2 Direct sealed ampoule

Direct sealed-tube synthesis is less chemically elaborate than CVT but still requires vacuum sealing and careful management of volatile S/Se, which is consistent with Re-containing ampoule reactions and OsSe₂-type chalcogenide syntheses (SS2; SS4, TS2). Its F2 is better than CVT because no halogen transport agent is required and temperatures can likely remain below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference based on SS2/TS2]. F3 is lower because elemental Re is dense and refractory, so reaction kinetics and homogenization are expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], and SS2 reports long heating and controlled cooling for Re–S/Se-containing products. The uncertainty is whether direct annealing yields ordered ReSSe rather than mixtures of ReS₂/ReSe₂ or a disordered solid solution [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3 Binary-dichalcogenide alloying

This route scores best because it avoids reacting all Re from metal in the final step and begins from structurally compatible layered Re dichalcogenides [Reasonable Inference], while ReSe₂ is directly confirmed as a close 2D molybdenite-like Re dichalcogenide (SS1, TS1). F2 remains moderate because prior binary synthesis and sealed-tube annealing add steps and because chalcogen vapor pressure must be controlled [Reasonable Inference]. F3 benefits from reduced structural reconstruction: the Re–chalcogen framework is already present, and only S/Se redistribution/order is required [Reasonable Inference]. The primary uncertainty is anion ordering: the target P1 arrangement may compete with random S/Se occupancy or phase separation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4 Thin-film/CVD

The thin-film route is plausible because TS3 reports molybdenite-like MoS₂ prepared by CVD, but TS3 gives insufficient evidence for precursors, temperature, atmosphere, equipment, or phase-control details; therefore, that source is neutral for most operational scoring. The route is mainly a [Reasonable Inference] from 2D transition-metal dichalcogenide growth chemistry and the target’s layered structure. F2 is penalized for controlled atmosphere, volatile chalcogen sources, multi-step processing, and potential toxic chalcogen gases [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is limited by uncertain Re precursor volatility and S/Se composition control, neither of which is directly addressed in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5 High pressure

High-pressure synthesis is supported only weakly by chemical similarity: CS1 reports TaPS-type compounds made from elements at 1200 °C and 65 kbar, but the target is structurally and chemically closer to ambient-pressure Re dichalcogenides (SS1, TS1). F2 is heavily penalized for >5 GPa pressure, high temperature, sealed/contained volatile chalcogens, and multi-step press operation (CS1). F3 is not especially favorable because high pressure does not directly solve mixed-anion ordering and may stabilize a denser non-layered polymorph [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best viewed as a fallback if ambient-pressure routes fail, not as the primary path.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a single cation, Re, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger (target data). The described P1 cell contains four Re, four S, and four Se sites, giving a small 12-atom ordered cell rather than a >40-atom complex cell (target data). The Re-centered polyhedra are described as edge-sharing distorted octahedra, with no face-sharing and no simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is absent (target data). The remaining amorphization risk is therefore low; the more important risk is crystalline anion disorder rather than amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ReSSe is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom (target data), and the closest ReSe₂ analogue is also on hull and experimentally accessible by CVT (SS1, TS1). The analogue support is strong structurally and thermodynamically through ReSe₂, which shares the layered molybdenite-derived Re–chalcogen framework and similar Re–Se bond lengths (SS1, TS1; target data). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of established Re dichalcogenide sealed-tube/CVT chemistry using halogen transport and excess chalcogen, which should help overcome the refractory nature of Re (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is S/Se stoichiometry and ordering: different chalcogen volatilities and transport rates could yield ReS₂/ReSe₂ mixtures or disordered ReS₂₋ₓSeₓ rather than the ordered P1 ReSSe phase [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with the best route being preformed ReS₂/ReSe₂ alloying followed by CVT or annealing optimization.
