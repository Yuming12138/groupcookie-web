### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports high equilibrium synthesizability (target data). Close chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogues include multiple E_hull = 0.0 phases, such as CrGaSe₃ (CS1), ZnCr₂Se₄ (CS3), CuSe₂ (SS1/TS1), AuCuSe₄ (SS2/TS2), and CuBrSe₃ (SS4/TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially for mixed Mn/Cu/Se redox and Se–Se bonding [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the route uses ordinary sealed ampoules and sub-1000 °C temperatures, as shown for CrFe₂Se₄ at 950 K and MnSe₂ at 520–850 °C (CS2, SS3). Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, repeated firings, and toxic/volatile Se handling (CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3; Reasonable Inference). Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Mn/Cu diffusion and ordering into a ternary edge-sharing octahedral phase may require long anneals, consistent with 6–8 day or multiple-firing analogue syntheses (CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Melt-and-anneal

Condition severity is higher because the closest Mn-containing melt route uses 1150 °C under vacuum, followed by annealing and slow cooling (CS4). The kinetic score is better than direct solid-state because melt homogenization reduces diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and CS4 demonstrates such a pathway for a Mn selenide. The uncertainty is that the target contains Cu and Se–Se bonding, so high-temperature melting could instead favor Cu–Se binaries or disrupt Se–Se units during cooling [Reasonable Inference; SS1/TS1 shows stable CuSe₂ chemistry].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Polyselenide flux

Condition severity is moderated by low temperature but penalized by K₂Se air sensitivity, vacuum sealing, flux removal, and multi-step handling, all seen in AuCuSe₄ and KPdCuSe₅ syntheses (SS2/TS2, SS5/TS4). Kinetics are favorable because K₂Se/K₂Seₓ flux provides reactive Se-rich liquid/flux transport, and AuCuSe₄ forms at 310 °C with slow cooling (SS2/TS2). The main uncertainty is that supplied flux analogues stabilize Cu/Au/Pd polychalcogenides rather than a dense MnCuSe₄ edge-sharing octahedral framework (SS2/TS2, SS5/TS4; target data).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT / transport-assisted growth

CVT condition severity is moderate: it requires vacuum ampoules, volatile transport agents, and long gradients, but not high pressure or >1000 °C temperatures; CrGaSe₃ grows at 1170–1020 K and CuBrSe₃ at 340–290 °C (CS1, SS4/TS3). Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion barrier and can improve crystal growth once a source phase exists [Reasonable Inference based on CS1 and SS4/TS3]. The main uncertainty is transport chemistry: I₂ may preferentially transport Cu or Mn and may form competing halides if loading/gradient are not tuned [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Solvothermal polychalcogenide route

Condition severity is moderate despite low temperature because KPdCuSe₅ synthesis requires glovebox handling, K₂Se, superheated ethylenediamine, vacuum sealing, and post-synthesis workup (SS5/TS4). Kinetics are strong for soluble/reactive polychalcogenide species, and the target’s Se–Se bond makes this chemically appealing (target data; SS5/TS4). The uncertainty is high because the supplied solvothermal analogue is a more open K/Pd/Cu polyselenide, not a dense Mn/Cu octahedral binary-cation framework (SS5/TS4; target data).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple formula and described ordered P2/m crystalline structure with MnSe₆ and CuSe₆ octahedra sharing edges (target data). No supplied structural description indicates a very large unit cell, and the target connectivity does not combine vertex, edge, and face sharing in the way specified by the amorphization check (target data). Ionic-radius mismatch is likely below the >15% trigger for plausible octahedral Mn/Cu states [Reasonable Inference], but uncertainty remains because the formal oxidation states are not explicitly given.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCuSe₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The analogue set supports synthesis well: chemically similar transition-metal selenides are made by sealed ampoule, melt/anneal, and CVT routes (CS1–CS4), while structurally/thermodynamically similar Cu–Se/Se–Se compounds support low-temperature Se-rich and CVT/flux strategies (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS4). The most important enabling factor is the combination of zero hull energy and known synthesizability of related Se-rich Cu and Mn selenides in sealed systems (target data; CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3). The main limiting factor is phase control, especially competition among Cu–Se binaries, Mn–Se binaries, and Se-rich polyselenide phases [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS1, SS3, SS2/TS2]. The best practical approach is to first make phase-pure powder by sealed annealing and then use CVT for crystal growth if needed (CS1, CS2, SS3).
