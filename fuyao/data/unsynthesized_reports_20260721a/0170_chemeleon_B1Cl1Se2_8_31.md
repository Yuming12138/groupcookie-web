### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data (Target data). Stable analogues exist in both boron-halide and boron-selenide spaces, including BBr3, Ba(BSe3)2, TlBSe3, and RbBSe3 with E_hull = 0.0 (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or molecular persistence under experimental conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.** Sealed evacuated tubes, carbon coating, and long controlled cooling are directly supported for B–Se analogues (SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). The route is penalized for controlled/vacuum atmosphere, volatile Se/chloride components, multistep sealed-tube handling, and air/moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference, supported by SS-3/SS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether a low-dimensional BSe2Cl molecule can be isolated before extended B–Se networks form [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.** Solid-state formation is kinetically limited by refractory boron [Reasonable Inference], even though B–Se analogues form in sealed tubes with long cooling profiles (SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). The mismatch between isolated trigonal-planar BSe2Cl and extended BSe4 frameworks lowers the structural-reconstruction score (SS-3, SS-5). Vapor/liquid transport from Se/chloride species may help, but this is inferred rather than directly shown for BSe2Cl [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.** Copyrolysis has a moderate thermal requirement because the B2AsCl2 analogue forms at 330 °C in about 1 h (CS-2; SS-4). Severity arises from volatile reactive halides, inert/high-vacuum glassware, multiple purification steps, and strong temperature/ratio sensitivity (CS-2; SS-4). The uncertainty is that selenium chlorides may undergo different redox chemistry than AsCl3 [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** Molecular halide copyrolysis should have favorable kinetics because B2Cl4 and AsCl3 react rapidly at 330 °C (CS-2; SS-4). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the analogue is also 0D, triclinic P-1, and boron-chloride based (CS-2; SS-4). The kinetic risk is competing redox/product distribution, which is explicitly reported for the analogue (CS-2; SS-4).

**F2 — Route 3.** Low-temperature crystallization avoids high-temperature severity, and BBr3 crystallization at −78 °C under inert atmosphere is directly supported (SS-2; TS-1). Deductions remain for glovebox/Schlenk work, volatile boron halides, multiple molecular-synthesis/crystallization steps, and toxic air-sensitive Se reagents [Reasonable Inference]. The route is milder than sealed-tube B–Se synthesis but chemically less directly proven.

**F3 — Route 3.** Solution/molecular chemistry avoids bulk diffusion, which is advantageous compared with long solid-state B–Se routes (SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-5). The 0D trigonal-planar boron-halide motif transfers well from BBr3 to a BSe2Cl molecular target (SS-2; TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether the desired neutral BSe2Cl composition is isolable rather than oligomerizing or disproportionating [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.** Solvothermal conditions are moderate in temperature because In–Se analogues form at 185–200 °C in sealed Teflon-lined autoclaves (CS-1). The route is penalized for sealed/autogenous pressure, likely volatile boron chloride handling, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides chemical activation evidence but insufficient direct structural signal for BSe2Cl clusters, so structural transfer is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 4.** Solvothermal media can improve precursor activity and reduce diffusion barriers, as In–Se phases form under 200 °C in CS-1. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BSe2Cl is likely predicted as a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from the combination of 0D boron-halide crystallization in BBr3 (SS-2; TS-1), 0D B–Cl cluster copyrolysis in B2AsCl2 (CS-2; SS-4), and stable B–Se frameworks made in sealed tubes (SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is chemical speciation: selenium/chloride reagents may form extended B–Se networks, redox products, or volatile/disproportionated species instead of discrete BSe2Cl molecules [Reasonable Inference, with product-distribution sensitivity supported by CS-2/SS-4]. Overall, the material is plausible but will likely require molecular precursor control rather than brute-force solid-state synthesis.
