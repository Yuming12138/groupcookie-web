### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. Thermodynamic analogues also include several stable sulfides on the hull, including MnNb₃S₆, Mn(NbS₂)₄, Ti₄GaS₈, and NbS₂ (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). Chemically and structurally related Ti sulfides are also near the hull, such as Ti₄GaS₈ at 0.0045 eV/atom and CuTi₂S₄ at 0.0219 eV/atom (CS1, SS3). Remaining uncertainty is computational: the hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct cation ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses conventional evacuated-ampoule sulfide synthesis, supported by Ti₃FeS₆, Ti₂CoS₄, and Ti₄GaS₈ examples (CS2, CS3, CS1). The severity is moderate because it requires vacuum sealing, sulfur containment, long high-temperature firing, and repeated grinding, but not high pressure or >1000 °C operation in the recommended program. Insufficient evidence from TS indicates no need for high-pressure synthesis, so that penalty is neutral. The main uncertainty is whether Mn reacts and orders as cleanly as Fe/Co/Ga analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because TiS₂ + Mn should be more reactive than Ti metal + S alone [Reasonable Inference], and Ti₃FeS₆ was made from Fe + TiS₂ at 900 °C (CS2, SS2). Repeated firings were usually needed for complete reaction in Ti₃FeS₆ (CS2, SS2), and Ti₂CoS₄ required multiple firings with intermediate grinding (CS3), so diffusion/order kinetics are not trivial. The octahedral TiS₆/MnS₆ target motif is strongly supported by Ti₃FeS₆ octahedral networks (CS2, SS2). Remaining uncertainty is Mn valence/site preference.

#### F2 — Route 2

The flux route is not thermally severe because the closest R-3m Ti sulfide flux analogue was synthesized at 750 °C (SS3). It still requires evacuated sealed tubes and sulfur containment, and slow cooling plus flux handling adds procedural complexity (SS3). Insufficient evidence from CS and TS directly confirms KCl/KI flux for Ti–Mn–S, so that part is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure requirement is indicated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because a halide flux enabled R-3m CuTi₂S₄ crystallization at 750 °C with week-long slow cooling (SS3). Flux likely enhances cation transport and lowers the effective crystallization barrier [Reasonable Inference]. The main structural advantage is the R-3m Ti sulfide framework similarity (SS3), but the limitation is that Cu is tetrahedral in the analogue while target Mn is octahedral, so site chemistry is not identical [Reasonable Inference]. This route best balances crystallinity, phase selection, and manageable conditions.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT is more demanding because it involves iodine, sealed ampoules, temperature gradients, and long durations of 10 days to 3 weeks in analogues (SS5, TS1, TS5). MnNb₃S₆ requires a 1050→900 °C gradient for 15 days (TS1), triggering the >1000 °C penalty. Insufficient direct evidence from CS supports Ti–Mn–S CVT specifically, so transfer is uncertain. No high-pressure conditions are indicated by the CVT analogues.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT can overcome bulk diffusion and produce crystals, as shown for TiS₂-related materials, MnNb₃S₆, and NbS₂-related sulfides (SS5, TS1, TS5). However, the long growth times and need for iodine gradients indicate slow crystallization and careful transport control (SS5, TS1, TS5). Because no direct Ti₄MnS₈ CVT analogue is provided, the structural selection score is lower than flux. The uncertainty is whether iodine forms competing Mn/Ti iodide transport equilibria that shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

The oxide-to-sulfide route is conditionally harsh because it uses toxic H₂S and may require temperatures up to 1150 °C, as in Mn–Nb–S synthesis (TS2). CS5 shows a lower-temperature H₂S route for V-containing sulfides, but still with multiple heating, grinding, sulfur addition, and sealed-tube annealing. Insufficient evidence from SS supports this route for the exact target structure. The route is therefore plausible but less attractive than direct ampoule or flux synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

Oxide precursors are stable and accessible, and oxide-to-sulfide conversion is demonstrated in CS5 and TS2. The kinetic penalty arises because TiO₂-type precursors are refractory and require full anion exchange/reduction to form a sulfide framework [Reasonable Inference]. TS2 reports single-phase products in related Mn–Nb sulfides, which supports feasibility, but target Ti–Mn–S phase selection is not directly shown. Residual oxides or binary sulfides are likely competing products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a notable cation-size mismatch, inferred from longer Mn–S bonds of 2.55 Å compared with Ti–S bonds of 2.43–2.46 Å in the target structure. The target connectivity includes corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not fully triggered by the target description. The provided formula has 13 atoms per formula unit, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40-atom unit cell. Thus amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₄MnS₈ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stabilized phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis case is well supported by chemically similar Ti–transition-metal sulfides made by evacuated-ampoule solid-state reaction (CS2, CS3) and by a structurally relevant R-3m Ti sulfide obtained by KCl/KI flux growth (SS3). The most critical enabling factor is the robust TiS₆/MnS₆ sulfide octahedral framework, which has close analogues in Ti₃FeS₆ and related sulfides (CS2, SS2). The most critical limiting factor is cation ordering/site selectivity for Mn in an octahedral site rather than formation of binary MnS, TiS₂, or alternative spinel-like phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with flux-assisted sealed-tube growth as the preferred first attempt.
