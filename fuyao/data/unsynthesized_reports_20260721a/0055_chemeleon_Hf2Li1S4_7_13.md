### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports assigning the maximum thermodynamic score. Chemically similar sulfides in CS include multiple E_hull = 0 phases, and structurally/thermodynamically similar references in SS/TS also include E_hull = 0 or near-zero sulfides. The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to Li/S loss during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses sealed-ampoule solid-state processing similar to CS and SS/TS examples, where binary sulfides are reacted at 900–1273 K for days. The severity is moderate because it avoids external high pressure and can likely stay near or below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], but it still requires vacuum sealing and air-sensitive Li₂S handling [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly address Li volatility, so that penalty is inferred rather than directly observed.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by the use of Hf metal and/or Hf sulfide, because Hf is refractory and likely slow to equilibrate [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS demonstrate that multication sulfides can form by long solid-state reactions, but many analogues require several days, regrinding, or annealing. The target’s layered triclinic structure is not the same as the spinel/perovskite/rare-earth sulfide analogues, implying substantial structural reconstruction.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because H₂S atmospheres are directly used in CS/SS/TS at high temperatures, and H₂S is toxic and operationally demanding. The oxide/sulfidation option is supported by SS/TS for rare-earth systems, but applying it to Hf–Li sulfide requires additional controls for Li loss [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore lower than Route 1 and Route 3.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are expected to be challenging because HfO₂ or partially oxidized Hf precursors are refractory and may sulfide incompletely [Reasonable Inference]. Although SS/TS show oxide-to-sulfide conversion under H₂S, those systems are rare-earth sulfides and not direct Li–Hf analogues. The route also requires full reconstruction into a layered Li-containing sulfide, with insufficient direct support from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 retains the sealed-system and air-sensitive-precursor penalties, but it can plausibly operate below 1000 °C because fluxes reduce reaction temperature [Reasonable Inference]. CS directly supports KBr-assisted sulfide crystal growth with slow cooling. No high pressure is required, consistent with CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux assistance receives the best kinetic score because CS shows KBr improves crystal growth for related sulfides. A molten halide flux can enhance mass transport and possibly enable layered Li/Hf ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS analogue has the exact Li(HfS₂)₂ structure, so the structural reconstruction penalty remains significant.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The largest amorphization risk comes from the strong size and coordination mismatch between Li and Hf [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell, and it does not report simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so only one of the three checks is triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, so the assessment relies mainly on the target structural description and ionic-size reasoning.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li(HfS₂)₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. CS, SS, and TS collectively support that multication sulfides are accessible by high-temperature sealed-tube, H₂S, and flux-assisted solid-state methods, but none directly demonstrate Li–Hf–S layered chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is the strong computed thermodynamic stability combined with the broad precedent for sulfide formation from binary sulfides in sealed or sulfiding atmospheres. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: Li/S volatility, Li₂S air sensitivity, and possible Hf redox/ordering requirements are not directly solved by the analogues. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed-tube or flux optimization.
