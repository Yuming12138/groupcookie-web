### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for thermodynamic ground-state stability under the computational phase diagram used. Several analogues in CS/SS/TS also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Hf(SeCl6)2, Zr(SeCl6)2, and RuS3Cl8 (CS; SS; TS). The remaining uncertainty is that molecular chlorides can be sensitive to finite-temperature chlorine chemical potential and volatility, which is not fully captured by the 0 K hull [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1  
Route 1 uses low temperature, so there is no high-temperature penalty, consistent with 140–155 °C CVT analogues (CS; SS; TS). The penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/chalcogen chlorides, multistep ampoule handling, and air-sensitive/toxic chlorides, all of which are documented for analogous sealed-ampoule chloride syntheses (CS; SS; TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact OsCl4 volatility and Os–S–Cl pressure behavior, so that component is treated by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1  
CVT analogues directly crystallize molecular chloride phases in temperature gradients over days, supporting favorable crystallization kinetics (CS; SS; TS). However, composition control is less favorable because the closest sulfur-containing Ru–S–Cl analogue forms multiple ternary products requiring separation (SS; TS). Insufficient evidence from CS for sulfur-chloride speciation in the target reduces confidence, so the F3 score is moderate-high rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2  
The Ru–S–Cl solvothermal analogue uses 140 °C, N2 handling, closed silica ampoules, and a steel autoclave for 7 days (SS; TS). The route avoids extreme temperature and true high pressure, but deductions apply for controlled atmosphere, volatile SCl2, multiple handling/crystallization steps, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents (SS; TS). Insufficient evidence from CS exists for this sulfur-specific route, so CS is neutral for route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 2  
This route scores highest kinetically because SCl2 is both reagent and liquid medium, and the closest Ru–S–Cl structural analogue forms at 140 °C rather than requiring refractory diffusion (SS; TS). The target’s S–Cl distances of 2.03–2.06 Å are very close to the Ru–S–Cl analogue’s 2.04–2.07 Å, supporting transferability of the sulfur-chloride coordination environment (SS; target structure). The main uncertainty is whether Os prefers isolated OsCl6 units rather than mixed Os–S–Cl clusters [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3  
The sealed-tube solid-state/chlorination route is based on Re chalcogen chloride analogues synthesized at 350–400 °C in evacuated fused-silica tubes with controlled cooling and washing (TS). It is not thermally extreme, but it still requires vacuum sealing, volatile chlorinating reagents, multiple processing steps, and toxic/air-sensitive chlorides (TS; CS). Insufficient evidence from SS exists for a direct Os solid-state analogue, so SS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 3  
Re chalcogen chloride analogues show that sealed-tube reactions of metal chlorides and elemental chalcogens can give hull-stable zero-dimensional chalcogen chloride clusters in good yield (TS). Kinetics are less favorable than Route 2 because elemental sulfur and Os chloride may react heterogeneously, and the target molecular SCl3 units likely require controlled sulfur-chloride speciation [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports sealed-ampoule chloride formation but not direct sulfur incorporation, so the route is plausible but less preferred (CS; TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency  
The target is zero-dimensional and consists of discrete OsCl6 and SCl3 clusters, which generally reduces extended-network amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. One trigger is assigned for the large size/coordination mismatch between Os and S centers, but no evidence indicates a >40-atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity in the target description. CS/SS/TS provide many crystalline zero-dimensional molecular chloride analogues, supporting that crystallization is feasible despite molecular complexity (CS; SS; TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a computational ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is fairly strong: CS and TS show hull-stable M(SeCl6)2 / (SeCl3)2MCl6 molecular chloride phases made in sealed ampoules, while SS/TS provide a sulfur-chloride Ru analogue made from SCl2 at 140 °C. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature molecular chloride chemistry, which avoids refractory Os diffusion and allows cluster crystallization in sealed ampoules. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in sulfur-chloride media, since the Ru–S–Cl analogue forms multiple competing ternary products and requires careful separation. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but should be approached as moisture-free, sealed-ampoule molecular crystal growth rather than conventional ceramic synthesis.
