### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the predicted ground state and supporting a very high F1 score. This is reinforced by structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfate analogues that are on-hull, including NaAu(SO₄)₂, RbAu(SO₄)₂, KAu(SO₄)₂, MnH₂(SO₄)₂, and HgSO₄ (SS-1–4; TS-1–5). CS is slightly more mixed because CdSO₄ is near-hull but CdSO₃ polymorphs are ~0.096–0.099 eV/atom (CS-1–3), so CS supports synthesizability but less strongly than TS/SS. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or hydration/SO₃ stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS and TS show sulfate crystals grown by hot concentrated H₂SO₄ slow evaporation around 200 °C with final 350 °C tempering (CS-1; TS-5), while SS/TS Au sulfate analogues form by concentrated-H₂SO₄ evaporation at room temperature/293 K (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The only significant F2 deduction is multi-step handling, because dissolution/evaporation/tempering is more involved than a single crystallization step (CS-1; TS-5). Kinetics are favorable because the acid solution/flux medium should provide high ion mobility and direct sulfate condensation pathways [Reasonable Inference], consistent with single-crystal growth in CS/SS/TS analogues. The main uncertainty is whether Ag₂S₂O₇ rather than Ag₂SO₄, AgHSO₄, or hydrated acid sulfate phases crystallizes under the chosen water/SO₃ activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has moderate-to-high severity because CS-4/5 require sealed glass ampoules at 350 °C for 72 h and slow cooling, and oleum/SO₃ would add volatile corrosive reagent handling [Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetics remain strong because high SO₃ activity should directly favor disulfate/pyrosulfate formation from sulfate species [Reasonable Inference], and sealed acidic oxysalt crystallization is demonstrated for Pd–Se–O analogues (CS-4/5). Structural reconstruction is likely limited because sulfate tetrahedra remain intact while S–O–S bridges form [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is pressure buildup and the absence of a direct Ag₂S₂O₇ sealed-ampoule precedent in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is mild in temperature and pressure because SS-5 shows Na₂S₂O₈ recrystallization by slow evaporation from aqueous solution. However, F2 is penalized for possible need to synthesize Ag₂S₂O₈ and for oxidizing peroxodisulfate chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, solution recrystallization is favorable, but the structural conversion from peroxodisulfate S₂O₈²⁻ to target S₂O₇²⁻ is not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS and likely requires nontrivial decomposition/dehydration [Reasonable Inference]. Thus this route is plausible but less direct than concentrated sulfuric acid crystallization.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has weaker support because no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates solid-state Ag₂S₂O₇ synthesis; insufficient evidence from CS for direct solid-state Ag₂S₂O₇ formation, insufficient evidence from SS for direct solid-state Ag₂S₂O₇ formation, and insufficient evidence from TS for direct solid-state Ag₂S₂O₇ formation. Its F2 score is reduced by likely need for dry atmosphere/SO₃ control, possible sealed conditions, and multiple grind/anneal steps [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is only moderate because solid-state diffusion and dehydration can be slower and more sensitive to local water/SO₃ activity than solution crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. The route may still work as a powder synthesis if a bisulfate precursor can be prepared cleanly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a chemically simple 11-atom formula unit with AgO₅/AgO₆ and SO₄/disulfate motifs, so there is no strong evidence for a large amorphization-prone unit cell. The main amorphization trigger is the large mismatch between Ag⁺ coordination polyhedra and S⁶⁺ tetrahedral units [Reasonable Inference]. Mixed corner/edge/face sharing is not reported for the target, reducing amorphization risk, although some structural analogues contain more complex alkali polyhedral sharing (SS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that P1 symmetry can imply low-symmetry ordering, which may require careful slow crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag₂S₂O₇ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, and many structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfate oxysalts are also on-hull (SS-1–4; TS-1–5). The strongest analogue support comes from concentrated sulfuric-acid evaporation routes that produce sulfate single crystals under mild-to-moderate temperatures (CS-1; SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). The most important enabling factor is the availability of solution/acid-flux chemistry, which avoids slow solid-state diffusion and can stabilize sulfate/disulfate tetrahedral frameworks. The main limiting factor is chemical speciation: Ag₂S₂O₇ formation likely depends sensitively on water activity and SO₃ activity, and the references do not directly demonstrate the exact Ag₂S₂O₇ phase. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, with Route 1 preferred for initial attempts and Route 2 as a stronger but more hazardous disulfate-stabilizing backup.
