### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the required rubric. This is reinforced by multiple stable analogous sulfides, including KPt₂S₃, Ir₂S₃, IrS₂, and CuRh₂S₄, each with E_hull = 0.0 in the provided data (CS1, SS1, SS3–SS5; TS1–TS4). The main uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide kinetic formation window, especially for Rb/Ir/S phase competition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions because Ir₂S₃ and IrS₂ analogues form at 900–960 °C (SS3–SS4; TS2–TS3). However, it requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile sulfur containment, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive Rb sulfide handling [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sulfide sealed-tube/glovebox practices in SS/TS and CS2/CS5. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure, so no high-pressure penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Using IrS₂ as a precursor should partially preserve IrS₆ octahedral chemistry because both target and IrS₂ contain IrS₆ units with similar Ir–S distances (target description; SS4, TS3). Still, solid-state diffusion involving refractory Ir is slow, and IrS₂ synthesis itself required long annealing and grinding/refiring for homogeneity (SS4, TS3). The kinetic uncertainty is whether Rb can intercalate/order into the Ir–S framework without forming separate Rb sulfides or IrS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from moderate 800–900 °C alkali/sulfur flux conditions directly supported by KPt₂S₃ (CS1, SS5, TS4). The severity deductions arise from sealed/inert handling, volatile sulfur/Rb species, flux washing, and air-sensitive Rb sulfide/polysulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 reports open/air melting for KPt₂S₃, but this is not fully transferable to Rb sulfides because Rb compounds are expected to be more moisture/volatility sensitive [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because liquid flux transport can overcome slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and flux routes successfully produced KPt₂S₃ and Ba/Hf/S sulfides (CS1, CS3, CS4). The structural similarity to KPt₂S₃ is useful: both have alkali–S coordination and edge-sharing late-transition-metal sulfide octahedra (CS1, SS5, TS4; target description). The uncertainty is phase selectivity, since CS1 notes sensitivity and unsuccessful related iridium cases, and CS3/CS4 show flux reactions can produce multiple closely related phases.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Direct elemental synthesis uses supported sealed-tube conditions for Ir–S and Rh–S compounds at 800–960 °C (SS1, SS3–SS4; TS1–TS3). The deductions are similar to Route 1 because evacuated tubes, volatile sulfur/Rb containment, staged heating, and air-sensitive Rb handling are required [Reasonable Inference]. No evidence from CS/SS/TS requires >1000 °C or high pressure for the closest Ir–S analogues.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The direct elemental route has the weakest kinetics because it starts from refractory Ir metal and requires complete framework formation [Reasonable Inference]. IrS₂ synthesis from elements needed 960 °C, 2 weeks, stepwise heating, and possible grinding/refiring (SS4, TS3). Cu/Rh sulfide formation from elements can be clean at 800 °C for 48 h (SS1, TS1), but that analogue lacks volatile alkali incorporation, so it is less transferable to RbIr₂S₄ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Rb and Ir, supported structurally by long Rb–S bonds of 3.27–3.42 Å versus Ir–S bonds of 2.38–2.41 Å in the target description. The target description reports corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for a >40-atom target unit cell, so the atom-count check is treated as not triggered rather than penalized.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb(IrS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally/thermodynamically related sulfides are also hull-stable (CS1; SS1, SS3–SS5; TS1–TS4). The analogues support synthesis reasonably well: Ir–S frameworks are experimentally accessible by sealed-tube annealing (SS3–SS4; TS2–TS3), and alkali noble-metal sulfide chemistry is supported by KPt₂S₃ flux growth (CS1, SS5, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is a sulfur-rich alkali flux, which should improve transport and stabilize Rb/S chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference based on CS1 and CS3/CS4]. The main limiting factor is phase competition with IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ and Rb sulfides, especially because CS1 notes sensitivity/unsuccessful related iridium cases and SS4/TS3 show IrS₂ requires long homogenization. Overall, the best expectation is that the phase is synthesizable but requires careful sealed flux chemistry rather than a simple one-shot ceramic reaction.
