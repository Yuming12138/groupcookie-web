### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, so the F1 score is near the maximum under the required rubric. TS analogues are strongly supportive because all top TS examples listed are also stable or nearly stable sulfides, including Li₂MnSnS₄, AgCd₂GaS₄, Li₂CdGeS₄, Na₂Mn₂S₃, and Li₂CdSnS₄ with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS). CS is more mixed because chemically similar Cu/V/Cr sulfides include metastable hull energies up to 0.223 eV/atom, while MgAl₂S₄ is stable (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that computed zero hull does not guarantee the R3m cation-ordered polymorph is the easiest kinetic product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Route 1 uses temperatures similar to CuCrS₂ and Li₂MnSnS₄ syntheses, 750–1000 °C, and below the >1000 °C penalty threshold if optimized at 800–950 °C (CS; SS/TS). It still requires vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur control, and multistep grinding/annealing, as seen in evacuated ampoule protocols for MgAl₂S₄, CuCrS₂, Li₂MnSnS₄, and AgCd₂GaS₄ (CS; SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether Mg₂MnS₃ can be made in one firing, so multistep processing is assumed [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

Binary sulfide precursors should be moderately reactive, supported by CuCrS₂ synthesis from binary chalcogenides (CS), but dense MgS/MnS may require long diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s MnS₄ tetrahedra have Mn–S distances close to Li₂MnSnS₄, supporting structural compatibility (SS/TS). However, cation ordering into Mg(1), Mg(2), and Mn sites in R3m may be slow, and long anneals are reported for structurally similar Li₂MnSnS₄ and AgCd₂GaS₄ analogues (SS/TS). This leads to a mid-range F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2: sulfide / polysulfide flux growth

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C processing because flux analogues operate around 650–750 °C for Li₂CdGeS₄/Li₂CdSnS₄ and 750 °C for CuTi₂S₄ (CS; SS/TS). It nevertheless requires controlled atmosphere or sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/polysulfide handling, and post-growth flux separation, all consistent with CS/SS/TS flux protocols. Flux ratio sensitivity is explicitly noted for Li₂CdGeS₄/Li₂CdSnS₄, so condition control is nontrivial (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly identifies a Mg–Mn–S flux, so the exact flux chemistry is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: sulfide / polysulfide flux growth

Flux chemistry improves reaction kinetics because it offers a liquid/reactive sulfide medium, as demonstrated by CuTi₂S₄ flux growth at 750 °C and Li₂CdGeS₄/Li₂CdSnS₄ polysulfide flux syntheses at 650–750 °C (CS; SS/TS). This directly supports the +10 to +15 diffusion benefit allowed by the rubric for flux/liquid mechanisms. The tetrahedral sulfide framework of the target is well aligned with stannite-like tetrahedral SS/TS analogues, but exact Mg/Mn ordering is not directly proven. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether MgS has adequate solubility and mobility in the selected flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

CS gives strong evidence that CVT can grow related Cu–V–S and Cu–Ti–S sulfides in evacuated quartz/silica ampoules using iodine, chlorine, TeCl₄, or AlCl₃/I₂ at 700–1030 °C (CS). The proposed Mg₂MnS₃ route can likely be kept near 800–900 °C, avoiding the >1000 °C penalty [Reasonable Inference]. However, it requires vacuum sealing, volatile transport agents, and a multistep feed-plus-growth sequence, giving the same severity deductions as Routes 1–2. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports CVT for the specific tetrahedral Mg–Mn sulfide chemistry, so this route is less certain.

#### F3 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

CVT is kinetically useful for crystal growth of chemically similar sulfides, as shown by CuV₂S₄, V₄Cu₃S₈, and CuTi₂S₄ analogues (CS). For Mg₂MnS₃, the vapor species are uncertain, and Mg may preferentially form volatile or stable halides such as MgI₂/MgCl₂, which could decouple Mg and Mn transport [Reasonable Inference]. CS also notes phase coexistence and phase-control difficulty for Cu–V–S CVT analogues. Therefore, the F3 score is lower than flux growth despite plausible transport chemistry.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ordered corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide network, which is favorable against amorphization because no edge/face-sharing mixed connectivity is described for MgS₄/MnS₄ in the target. A modest cation-size mismatch between tetrahedral Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺ likely triggers the ionic-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar SS/TS materials include ordered tetrahedral sulfide frameworks, which supports crystallization, but some analogues require long annealing or precise flux ratios, indicating ordering challenges rather than strong amorphization tendency (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS provides a direct amorphization observation for Mg₂MnS₃, so the F4 score is moderately high but not maximal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₂MnS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS tetrahedral sulfide analogues such as Li₂MnSnS₄ and Li₂CdGeS₄, which show that ordered corner-sharing sulfide frameworks can be synthesized by sealed-tube and flux methods (SS/TS). CS provides useful but less direct support through MgAl₂S₄ and several transition-metal sulfides, showing that Mg sulfides and trigonal/rhombohedral sulfides are accessible under sealed high-temperature conditions (CS). The most critical enabling factor is the use of a reactive sulfur-rich flux or sealed sulfide environment to enhance diffusion and maintain sulfur chemical potential (CS; SS/TS). The main limiting factor is phase purity: MgS, MnS, and possible alternative Mg–Mn sulfides may compete, and flux ratio/stoichiometry sensitivity is documented in related tetrahedral sulfides (SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]).
