### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the predicted convex hull, which strongly supports synthesizability. TS gives strong support because the top thermodynamic analogues include near-zero-hull ZnS polytypes and stable AgCd2GaS4 (TS-1 to TS-4). SS similarly supports stability through structurally close near-hull ZnS/stannite-like sulfides (SS-2 to SS-5). CS is useful but mixed: Cu4TiS4 and Cu2GeS3 are stable or nearly stable, whereas CuCrS2 and CuFeS2 have positive E_hull in the provided data (CS-1 to CS-5).

**F2/F3 — Route 1: direct elemental ampoule.**  
Condition severity is moderate because CS analogues use evacuated sealed tubes, high but accessible temperatures, and long holds/refiring rather than high pressure (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). Kinetics are moderate: elemental sulfur is reactive, but Mn/Zn ordering into a chalcopyrite-like ternary requires diffusion and likely repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference], consistent with regrinding/refiring and long anneals in CS-2 and CS-5. SS/TS give strong structural support for tetrahedral Zn–S connectivity but insufficient direct evidence for MnZn2S3 elemental synthesis; those source-specific gaps are treated as neutral for the direct-route kinetic uncertainty.

**F2/F3 — Route 2: binary-sulfide route.**  
Severity remains moderate because binary chalcogenide syntheses in evacuated ampoules at 800–1000 °C are directly reported (CS-1). Kinetics are somewhat worse than Route 1 because MnS and ZnS are already stable sulfides, so cation interdiffusion and ordering may be slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS ZnS analogues provide strong structural compatibility for the ZnS subframework but insufficient direct evidence that MnS + ZnS converts cleanly to MnZn2S3; this uncertainty lowers F3.

**F2/F3 — Route 3: sulfur-rich flux.**  
TS-5 provides direct evidence that Mn sulfides can crystallize from sulfur-rich sodium flux under purified Ar at 870 K with slow cooling and washing. This lowers kinetic barriers because the flux provides a reactive liquid medium [Reasonable Inference], giving the highest F3 among the proposed routes. However, CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for Mn–Zn chalcopyrite-like flux crystallization; this is the main uncertainty and motivates the −2 expert adjustment.

**F2/F3 — Route 4: vapor/CVT.**  
SS and TS strongly show ZnS polytypes can be grown by vapor/sublimation at ~1200 °C (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-2). However, these data are for binary ZnS, not ternary MnZn2S3, so composition control and Mn transport are insufficiently evidenced from CS/SS/TS and scored conservatively. The route is more appropriate for crystal growth after a bulk feed has been made [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple corner-sharing tetrahedral network, which normally has low amorphization tendency compared with mixed edge/face-sharing frameworks [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar ZnS and stannite-like sulfides are also tetrahedral corner-sharing frameworks (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-4). The target has no evidence for a >40 atom cell or mixed polyhedral connectivity, and the Mn²⁺/Zn²⁺ tetrahedral radius mismatch is estimated below 15% [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is effectively neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnZn2S3 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from chalcopyrite/tetrahedral sulfides made by sealed-tube solid-state reactions, especially CuFeS2 and Cu2GeS3-type materials (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5), and from stable tetrahedral ZnS/stannite-like structural analogues (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-4). The most important enabling factor is the chemically simple, charge-balanced tetrahedral sulfide framework with Mn²⁺/Zn²⁺/S²⁻ compatibility [Reasonable Inference], supported by the close Zn–S and Mn–S tetrahedral bond environments in SS/TS analogues and the target description. The main limiting factor is cation ordering and stoichiometry control under sulfur/Zn volatility, because sealed ampoules, long anneals, and phase-control issues recur in CS and TS examples (CS-1 to CS-5; TS-5). Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with flux or sealed-ampoule routes most promising.
