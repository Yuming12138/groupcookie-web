### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound by the supplied thermodynamic data. Chemically similar Ba₂TiS₄ is also on the hull, and Ba₃Zr₂S₇/Ba₂ZrS₄ are near-hull (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Structurally and thermodynamically similar sulfides include multiple stable examples, especially RbCeS₂, LaS, BaCe₂MnS₅, Ce₂S₃, and CeScS₃ (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic.

**Route 1 F2/F3 — sealed solid-state.**  
CS-2 and CS-3 directly support Zr sulfide synthesis from sulfide/metal/sulfur mixtures in evacuated ampoules, but the required high temperature, vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and repeated processing justify a severe F2 penalty. Kinetically, Zr metal is refractory and the target requires reconstructing a layered mixed Cs/Zr sulfide network, so F3 is low–moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS for Cs–Zr–S specifically, so SS is neutral for exact phase selectivity but useful for sealed-tube methodology (SS-4, SS-5).

**Route 2 F2/F3 — flux growth.**  
CS-2 provides the strongest direct precedent because Ba₃Zr₂S₇ was grown using BaCl₂ flux at 1323 K. A CsCl/CsI flux is an extrapolation, but it is chemically sensible because it buffers Cs and avoids Ba incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is better than Route 1 because flux reduces diffusion limitations, but phase purity remains uncertain because the target is not the same RP-like Ba–Zr sulfide phase as CS-2.

**Route 3 F2/F3 — H₂S sulfurization.**  
SS-1/TS-1 demonstrate alkali/lanthanide sulfides from carbonate/oxide precursors in flowing H₂S at 1000 °C, and TS-5 demonstrates H₂S firing for CeScS₃. However, transferring this to Zr is uncertain because ZrO₂ is refractory and difficult to fully sulfurize [Reasonable Inference]. This route scores better in equipment simplicity than high-pressure methods but worse in kinetics due to oxide-to-sulfide reconstruction.

**Route 4 F2/F3 — CVT/vapor-assisted growth.**  
TS-4 supports sulfide CVT using sealed tubes, vacuum, and hot–cold vapor-pressure control, but it does not directly involve Cs–Zr–S. Vapor transport can reduce bulk diffusion barriers and may improve crystal quality [Reasonable Inference], giving the best F3 score. The limiting uncertainty is chemical selectivity: Cs halides and Zr halides may form instead of transporting the intact ternary phase [Reasonable Inference].

**Route 5 F2/F3 — HPHT.**  
CS-4 and CS-5 show that high pressure can access chemically similar alkaline-earth transition-metal sulfides that are difficult by conventional routes. For this target, HPHT is a fallback because the target is already predicted on the hull and has a 2D structure, so pressure may not be required and may destabilize the layered motif [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is penalized strongly for pressure and air-sensitive sulfides, while F3 remains limited by refractory Zr and short reaction times.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large size/coordination mismatch between Cs–S and Zr–S environments, consistent with Cs–S distances of 3.32–3.41 Å and Zr–S distances of 2.56–2.69 Å from the target description. The target also contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing sulfur-centered polyhedra, which increases ordering complexity. The cell-size criterion is not triggered because the formula unit is small and there is insufficient evidence that the crystallographic cell contains >40 atoms. Thus F4 is medium risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs(ZrS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The chemical evidence is fairly strong for Zr sulfide formation under sealed ampoule or flux conditions, especially from Ba₃Zr₂S₇ and Ba₂ZrS₄ analogues (CS-2, CS-3), while the structural/thermodynamic evidence supports the broader feasibility of layered or octahedral sulfides made by solid-state, H₂S, and sealed-tube methods (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining Cs and S chemical potentials in a sealed or flux-assisted environment [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against binary Cs₂S/ZrS₂, Ba–Zr-like competing stoichiometries, or halide/flux side products [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not trivial, with flux or vapor-assisted growth preferred over a dry solid-state route.
