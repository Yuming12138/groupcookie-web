### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. TS includes several E_hull = 0.0 sulfide/selenide analogues, and the closest BaVS3 analogue is experimentally accessible despite E_hull = 0.0142 eV/atom (CS, SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated 0 K stability does not guarantee a unique finite-temperature phase, especially for Ba–Mn–S where multiple manganese sulfide oxidation states may compete [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state

The condition severity is moderate because structurally similar BaNiS2 and BaCoS2 form in sealed tubes at 820–900 °C without external pressure, but they require vacuum/Ar control and multi-day heating (SS). Kinetics are only moderate because BaS/metal/S mixtures require solid-state diffusion, and analogues report several days or repeated treatment (SS). The route is attractive because it is experimentally accessible, but it may suffer from incomplete reaction or competing binary sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux growth

The severity score is lower because BaVS3 flux growth uses 1050 °C, evacuated double tubes, initial sulfurization, controlled cooling, and post-growth washing (CS, SS, TS). Kinetics score highest because the flux route is directly supported by the closest structural analogue BaVS3 and because halide flux improves mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference from CS/SS/TS]. The main uncertainty is chloride incorporation or stabilization of a V-like analogue structure that may not map perfectly to Mn [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: HPHT sulfide synthesis

The HPHT route scores well in the formal F2 rubric because 3–5 GPa is severe experimentally but does not trigger the specified “>5 GPa” deduction; this is why an expert −2 adjustment is applied [Reasonable Inference]. CS shows Ba–Cr sulfides can form from BaS + transition metal + sulfur at 5 GPa/1200 °C, and BaSnS3 high-pressure synthesis confines sulfur and stabilizes dense sulfide frameworks (CS). Kinetics are improved by high temperature and pressure, but transferability is less direct than BaVS3 flux growth because BaVS3 itself was not HPHT synthesized (CS/SS/TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Ba/Mn size contrast triggers one amorphization/disorder risk factor [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii], while the simple high-symmetry BaMnS3 formula likely avoids a large-cell penalty [Reasonable Inference]. The target explicitly contains mixed corner/face and distorted edge/corner/face-sharing environments, triggering the mixed-connectivity penalty. BaVS3 analogues with similar mixed connectivity are crystalline, which moderates but does not eliminate this risk (CS, SS, TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaMnS3 is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is fairly strong: BaVS3 is a very close chemical/structural analogue with the same BaS12/MS6 mixed-connectivity framework (CS, SS, TS), and multiple Ba–transition-metal sulfides are experimentally made by sealed-tube, flux, or high-pressure methods (CS, SS). The most important enabling factor is sulfur confinement, because sulfur volatility is repeatedly handled by sealed tubes, flux tubes, or pressure capsules in the analogues (CS, SS, TS). The most important limiting factor is phase control among Ba–Mn–S sulfides with different Mn oxidation states and sulfur chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with HPHT giving the best robustness and BaCl2 flux giving the best chance of high-quality crystals.
