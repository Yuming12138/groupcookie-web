### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. CS strongly supports this because all five chemically similar Au–alkali chalcogenides listed have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-5). SS/TS also support synthesizability of on-hull alkali-containing materials, with RbSb, RbAs, Rb₃SbSe₃, Rb₆HgS₄, and Rb₆HgSe₄ mostly at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic stability but whether the predicted R3m polymorph is the experimentally selected one [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses a moderate 600 °C ampoule condition directly supported by CsAuS/RbAuS/CsAuSe syntheses (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5), so no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty is warranted. Deductions are applied for a sealed volatile sulfur system, multiple handling/heating/tempering steps, and toxic/air-sensitive Cs/Tl chemistry [Reasonable Inference; air-sensitive product/handling issues are consistent with CS-2/CS-5]. SS and TS give insufficient direct chemical signal for CsTlS elemental synthesis, so their route-specific condition contribution is neutral, but they do confirm that inert ampoule reactions are common for alkali phases (SS-1/SS-2; TS-1/TS-2). The main uncertainty is ampoule compatibility and controlling exothermic Cs–S reaction.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from reactive elemental Cs/S and likely molten Tl/Cs-containing intermediates [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS fusion reactions at 600 °C (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5). The diffusion penalty is low because CS analogues form by melt/fusion rather than by refractory oxide diffusion (CS-1/CS-2). Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic factor: CsAuS analogues are chemically close but structurally Cmcm, while the target is R3m calaverite-derived; CS-3 does contain SCs₃Au₃ octahedral units resembling the target SCs₃Tl₃ motif, but this is only partial structural support. SS/TS are structurally/thermodynamically useful but chemically less direct for Tl sulfide ordering.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids direct elemental Cs/S reaction but requires air-sensitive sulfide precursors and sealed/vacuum handling. CS thioaurates used Cs₂S/Rb₂S in evacuated silica tubes at 500–800 K for one week (CS-3/CS-4), while Rb₆HgS₄ analogues used Rb₂S + HgS in sealed Duran ampoules at 360–380 °C for 7–10 days (SS-5/TS-4). The vacuum/controlled-atmosphere and multi-step sulfide preparation/handling justify a lower F2 score. Uncertainty remains because Tl₂S behavior is inferred rather than demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfides provide chemically preorganized Cs–S and Tl–S bonds, which should improve reaction selectivity compared with totally elemental mixing [Reasonable Inference]. The long anneals in CS-3/CS-4 and SS-5/TS-4 imply acceptable but slow diffusion and ordering. Structural reconstruction remains moderate because the reactants are simple binaries and the product is an ordered ternary R3m phase; CS/SS/TS do not report this exact structure. The largest uncertainty is whether binary sulfide mixtures favor CsTlS rather than Cs₂S/Tl₂S residuals or other Cs–Tl–S stoichiometries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses flux/melt concepts supported indirectly by SS-4/TS-3 and by long tempering/crystal-growth practice in CS-1/CS-5. The condition score is penalized for controlled sealed/possibly vacuum operation, volatile sulfur, multi-step flux handling/removal, and toxic/air-sensitive components [Reasonable Inference; related air sensitivity is supported by CS-3/CS-4 and SS-5/TS-4]. The temperature itself is moderate and no high pressure is required, consistent with SS-4/TS-3 and CS routes. The main uncertainty is absence of a directly demonstrated Cs–Tl–S flux recipe.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux conditions should provide the best mass transport and may promote crystal growth/order formation [Reasonable Inference], consistent with flux/melt synthesis of Rb₃SbSe₃ (SS-4/TS-3) and tempering-based crystal growth in CsAuS/CsAuSe analogues (CS-1/CS-5). Structural reconstruction is still not ideal because no CS/SS/TS entry matches the R3m CsTlS target exactly; transfer is inferred from related chalcogenide melt chemistry. The uncertainty is flux solubility and whether excess Cs/S shifts the product away from 1:1:1 stoichiometry.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple reported local structure with Cs, Tl, and S each on one site and edge-sharing SCs₃Tl₃ octahedra, with no mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity stated in the target description. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsTlS is likely a ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically similar alkali–metal chalcogenides are also on-hull and experimentally synthesized (CS-1 to CS-5). The strongest support comes from CS, especially CsAuS and RbAuS, which are close 1:1:1 alkali–metal sulfides made directly from elements in sealed ampoules at 600 °C (CS-1/CS-2). SS and TS provide useful but less direct support: they show that alkali-containing sulfides/pnictides with similar coordination environments can be obtained by inert solid-state, flux, or sealed-ampoule routes (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The critical enabling factor is the availability of moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule chemistry with reactive/partly molten components, which should overcome diffusion barriers (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5). The critical limiting factor is phase selectivity under volatile, toxic, air-sensitive Cs–Tl–S conditions, especially because no provided analogue is the exact CsTlS R3m structure [Reasonable Inference].
