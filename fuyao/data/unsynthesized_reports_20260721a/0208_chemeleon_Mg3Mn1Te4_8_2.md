### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target Mg₃MnTe₄ is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum stability score. TS also lists multiple thermodynamically similar tetrahedral tellurides at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including LiAlTe₂, LiGaTe₂, CdAl₂Te₄, AgAlTe₂, and LiInTe₂. The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or cation ordering, which is a [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 direct elements.**  
The temperature is moderate because comparable direct telluride syntheses occur at 650–900 °C for Cu₂MTe₃ (CS), 750 °C for LiMnTe₂ (CS), and 1275 K for LiInTe₂ (TS). The route is penalized for vacuum sealing and volatile/reactive components because evacuated ampoules are used in CS/TS and Mg/Te volatility is a [Reasonable Inference]. SS Bridgman analogues provide insufficient detailed temperature/atmosphere information for this route and are treated as neutral evidence where specific severity is concerned.

**F3 — Route 1 direct elements.**  
Elemental precursors are chemically active enough for direct telluride formation, as shown by Cu₂MTe₃, LiMnTe₂, and LiInTe₂ syntheses (CS, TS). However, Mn metal diffusion and complete reconstruction from elements make kinetics only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Long reaction times in LiMnTe₂ and LiInTe₂ syntheses support the deduction for slow ordering/diffusion (CS, TS).

**F2 — Route 2 binary tellurides.**  
The binary telluride route uses similar severity to reported solid-state chalcogenide reactions: CuCr₂Te₄ required 600–800 °C for 2–4 weeks in evacuated quartz ampoules (CS), and Cd/Ag/Al tellurides used evacuated quartz bombs/tube furnaces (SS, TS). It is penalized for vacuum, sealed volatile tellurides, multistep precursor preparation, and likely MgTe handling sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, so no deductions are applied there.

**F3 — Route 2 binary tellurides.**  
Preformed Mg–Te and Mn–Te bonds should reduce structural reconstruction relative to elements [Reasonable Inference]. Nevertheless, solid-state diffusion through tellurides is slow, consistent with the 2–4 week CuCr₂Te₄ reaction time and extended tempering/phase-control issues in Cd/Ag/Al tellurides (CS, SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether MgTe and MnTe interdiffuse into the ordered Lazarevićite framework without trapping binary or spinel-like competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 Te-rich flux.**  
The preferred flux route is based on Cu₂MTe₃ molten flux growth at 650–900 °C with controlled cooling in evacuated tubes (CS). It is still penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Te/Mg, multi-step slow-cooling/flux removal, and air-sensitive Mg [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS Bridgman evidence supports that tetrahedral tellurides can be grown from melts, but SS Bridgman condition details are insufficient and are treated as neutral for exact condition severity.

**F3 — Route 3 Te-rich flux.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because a Te-rich melt can enhance mass transport and cation ordering [Reasonable Inference], and Cu₂MTe₃ analogues were successfully grown from a molten flux (CS). Structurally similar tetrahedral tellurides are known synthesized phases in SS/TS, supporting crystallization of a corner-sharing tetrahedral network. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Mg remains in the melt and orders on the correct tetrahedral sites instead of forming MgTe-rich or MnTe-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Only one amorphization trigger is applied: the Mg²⁺/Mn²⁺ tetrahedral radius mismatch is slightly above 15% [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description gives only corner-sharing tetrahedra and no edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered. The target formula is small, and there is insufficient evidence for a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₃MnTe₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is fairly strong: CS shows that chemically related transition-metal tellurides and Mn tellurides can be made in sealed ampoules, while SS/TS show many stable tetrahedral telluride frameworks with similar MTe₄ corner-sharing motifs. The most important enabling factor is the thermodynamic stability combined with the compatibility of Mg²⁺/Mn²⁺ in tetrahedral Te coordination [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is maintaining Mg chemical potential and Mg:Mn ordering during high-temperature telluride synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. A Te-rich flux route is preferred because it improves diffusion and crystal growth while staying within the temperature range demonstrated for related tellurides (CS).
