### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score by the requested rubric (target data). Thermodynamic analogues Bi2Se3 and K2Bi8Se13 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting stable Bi–Se layered/low-dimensional chemistry (TS1, TS2). The chemically identical reported Bi3Se4Br entry has a small E_hull ≈ 0.0276 eV/atom and E_above_ground_state = 0.0, supporting chemical accessibility but introducing polymorph ambiguity because that structure is monoclinic C2/m rather than the target R3m (CS1, SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes finite-temperature stability of the R3m polymorph specifically, so uncertainty remains in polymorph selection.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures of 350–550 °C and standard sealed quartz ampoules, which is favorable compared with high-temperature ceramic synthesis (CS1, SS2). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile/corrosive bromide handling, since the reported Bi3Se4Br route requires evacuated sealed ampoules and BiBr3 (CS1, SS2; [Reasonable Inference] for BiBr3 volatility/corrosivity). The route is not high-pressure and does not exceed 1000 °C (CS1, SS2). The remaining uncertainty is whether modifying the reported CVT profile can access the target R3m polymorph rather than the known C2/m polymorph (CS1, SS2).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Bi2Se3 and BiBr3 are reactive binary precursors that have already yielded Bi3Se4Br, supporting good precursor activity (CS1, SS2). CVT/halide transport reduces diffusion limitations relative to dense solid-state reactions [Reasonable Inference], and the reported synthesis occurs at only 350–550 °C (CS1, SS2). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target contains Bi2Se3-like sheets, which are strongly supported by Bi2Se3 analogues (SS1, TS1), but the exact reported composition crystallizes as a C2/m ribbon phase, not the target R3m layered phase (CS1, SS2). Thus kinetics are favorable for forming Bi–Se–Br material, but not guaranteed for the desired stacking/polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The direct melt/Bridgman route uses vacuum-sealed ampoules and 750–850 °C temperatures inferred from related Bi–Se layered chalcogenide growth near 800 °C (SS3, SS5, TS4, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). It avoids high pressure and does not require >1000 °C, but it does require vacuum, staged heating, volatile Se/Br retention, and hazardous BiBr3/Se handling (SS1, TS1, CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The multi-step nature of prereaction, melting, slow cooling, and annealing creates a severity penalty (SS1, TS1, SS3, TS4). Insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS proves Br-containing R3m Bi3Se4Br can be melt-grown, so this route is less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melting improves diffusion and is successful for Bi2Se3 and layered Bi–Se analogues, supporting good crystallization kinetics in the Bi–Se subsystem (SS1, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS4, TS5). Precursor activity is lower than Route 1 because elemental/melt synthesis is less preorganized than Bi2Se3 + BiBr3 and must assemble the full Bi–Se–Br framework [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is more demanding because the target’s ordered Bi2Se3/BiSeBr layering must form from a melt rather than from a close binary/halide precursor combination (target description; CS1/SS2). The largest uncertainty is whether Br partitions into the desired layers or forms separate bismuth bromide/oxybromide/halide-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures and sealed tubes, as shown for MnBi2Se4 and K2Bi8Se13 flux syntheses (CS3, TS3, SS4, TS2). It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, but it requires controlled atmosphere, long slow cooling, flux handling, and post-growth flux separation (CS3, TS3, SS4, TS2). If alkali chalcogenide fluxes are used, air-sensitive handling and glovebox operation are required (SS4, TS2). If BiBr3-rich flux is used, bromide volatility/corrosivity remains a penalty [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/SS2].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth improves ion mobility and crystal growth at lower effective diffusion barriers, as demonstrated by good crystals of MnBi2Se4 from LiCl–RbCl flux and K2Bi8Se13 from polychalcogenide flux (CS3, TS3, SS4, TS2). The precursor activity is reasonably high because molten flux can dissolve/react Bi–Se species and enable slow ordering (CS3, TS3, SS4, TS2). Structural reconstruction is moderate because flux routes support low-dimensional Bi–Se framework formation, but no provided flux example directly forms Br-containing R3m Bi3Se4Br (insufficient direct evidence from CS3/SS4/TS2/TS3). The key uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes the desired layered R3m polymorph or instead the known C2/m Bi3Se4Br polymorph (CS1, SS2; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Bi, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. No provided target or analogue data demonstrates a unit cell atom count above 40; the formula unit contains 8 atoms, so the cell-size amorphization trigger is not supported by the supplied information (target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS). The mixed connectivity trigger is activated because the target contains edge-, corner-, and face-sharing BiSe6 octahedra, which can create multiple local motifs and stacking possibilities (target description). Similar Bi–Se analogues commonly show edge/corner sharing and layered motifs, supporting crystallizability despite some configurational complexity (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically viable because its calculated E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, but the experimentally demonstrated same-composition Bi3Se4Br analogue is a different C2/m polymorph, so the R3m target may be a ground-state polymorph in the calculation or a polymorph requiring careful kinetic selection (target data; CS1, SS2). The synthesis evidence is strong chemically because Bi3Se4Br has already been made from Bi2Se3 and BiBr3 in sealed ampoules (CS1, SS2), and strong structurally because Bi2Se3-like layered motifs are highly similar to the target (SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a direct Bi2Se3–BiBr3 precursor system that supplies both the Bi2Se3 sheet motif and the Bi–Br component (CS1, SS2; target description). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph competition between the known monoclinic ribbon structure and the desired trigonal layered R3m structure (CS1, SS2; target description). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best odds via sealed Bi2Se3–BiBr3 reaction/CVT plus systematic control of Br chemical potential and cooling/transport profile.
