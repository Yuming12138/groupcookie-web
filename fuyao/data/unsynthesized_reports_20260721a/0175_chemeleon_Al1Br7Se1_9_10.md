### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is listed with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score (Target data). Thermodynamic analogues in TS include several E_hull = 0.0 molecular halides, including Ta₂Te₂Br₁₈, Al₂Te₂I₁₄, SbBr₃, and Ga₂Te₂I₁₄, showing that related halide cluster phases can be ground-state compounds (TS1–TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic persistence under moisture or halide-loss conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because the exact analogue is synthesized at 150 °C with a 150 → 120 °C gradient in a closed ampoule (CS1, SS1). The score is reduced because the route needs controlled Ar/sealed-ampoule handling, volatile halide precursors, multiple handling steps, and air/moisture-sensitive reagents/products (CS1, SS1, TS2). Insufficient evidence from TS on exact bromide precursor handling beyond the related iodide/bromide ampoule methods is treated as supportive but not more severe than CS/SS; neutral where route-specific details are absent (TS: 50 for exact SeBr₄/AlBr₃ handling).

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable because SeBr₄ + AlBr₃ form the exact AlBr₄/SeBr₃ cluster salt quantitatively at only 150 °C (CS1, SS1). Molecular/vapor transport avoids slow solid-state diffusion and preserves local cluster chemistry, giving a high precursor activity and structural reconstruction score (CS1, SS1). Remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: the target P1 setting may be a different ordering of the same cluster salt relative to Pc mp-29408 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is still low-temperature compared with ceramic synthesis, but sealed bromination from Br₂ requires volatile/toxic halogen handling and staged heating [Reasonable Inference from TS4 and CS4]. TS4 supports sealed elemental halogen ampoule synthesis for Te–I–Ga/In analogues, while CS4 supports Br₂ use in sealed Se–Br chemistry. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for elemental Al + Se + Br₂ synthesis of AlSeBr₇, so that specific transfer is partially extrapolated and scored less confidently (CS/SS neutral 50 for the elemental route).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Al must first be brominated to AlBr₃ and Se must form selenium bromides before the target salt assembles [Reasonable Inference]. Once molecular halides form, transport and crystallization should be relatively fast, as molecular halide analogues crystallize at low temperature (CS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is uncontrolled local stoichiometry and pressure during in-situ Br₂ consumption [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 operates at modest temperature, about 150–180 °C, consistent with CS4 solvothermal bromide chemistry and CS1 low-temperature Al–Se–Br chemistry. It is penalized for sealed ampoule/autoclave operation, volatile/toxic Br₂/CCl₄, controlled N₂ atmosphere, and multi-step isolation (CS4). SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific solvothermal evidence for AlSeBr₇ and are scored neutral for that route component (SS: 50; TS: 50).

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Solvothermal media can enhance transport at low temperature, and CS4 demonstrates formation of Se–Br cluster compounds under sealed CCl₄/Br₂ conditions. However, CS4 also reports multiphase mixtures and manual crystal separation, lowering the structural reconstruction and phase-control confidence for AlSeBr₇. The route is therefore kinetically plausible but less selective than direct SeBr₄ + AlBr₃ ampoule transport (CS4 vs CS1/SS1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target consists of discrete molecular clusters rather than a polymeric network, which generally lowers amorphization risk because long-range cation/anion network reconstruction is limited [Reasonable Inference from Target data and CS1/SS1]. One risk trigger is assigned because Al and Se have distinct coordination environments and effective cation sizes/chemistries, but the unit cell is small and there is no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity (Target data; CS1/SS1). The main uncertainty is polymorphic packing disorder among zero-dimensional clusters [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom (Target data). The CS and SS evidence are unusually strong because the top analogue is the same Al–Se–Br composition expressed as Al₂Se₂Br₁₄ and contains the same AlBr₄ and SeBr₃ clusters, synthesized quantitatively by low-temperature closed-ampoule transport (CS1, SS1). The most critical enabling factor is the high volatility/reactivity of molecular bromide precursors, which allows crystallization at ~150 °C without refractory solid-state diffusion (CS1, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is handling sensitivity: AlBr₃, SeBr₄, and the product are moisture-sensitive, and sealed ampoule stoichiometry/temperature-gradient control is required (CS1, SS1). A secondary uncertainty is whether the predicted P1 polymorph is the isolated polymorph or whether the known Pc analogue dominates experimentally [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1 vs Target P1 description].
