### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the 90–100 range by the required rubric. This is reinforced by multiple stable/near-stable related Hg/telluride/chalcogenide phases, including Hg₃Tl₂Te₄ at 0.0186 eV/atom (CS4/SS1), Cu₂HgSiTe₄ at 0 eV/atom (TS4), HgCdAsBr at 0 eV/atom (SS4/TS1), and Hg₃Se₂I₂ at 0 eV/atom (SS5/TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing ordered variants [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 requires vacuum ampoule handling, which is common in telluride syntheses (CS1, CS2, CS5) and Hg-containing chalcogenides (SS3, SS5/TS2). It also uses toxic Hg-containing precursors and a sealed system for volatile Hg/Te control, which is consistent with HgQ and sealed ampoule use in CS4/SS1 and SS3. The temperature is moderate, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Solid-state diffusion is plausible because similar tellurides form at 450–800 °C in sealed tubes (CS1, CS5), but long annealing and regrinding are often needed, as shown for TlCrTe₂ (CS1). The target requires ordered corner-sharing MnTe₄/HgTe₄ tetrahedra, and tetrahedral telluride networks are known in CdTl₂Te₄ and Cu₂HgSiTe₄ (CS3, TS4). Uncertainty remains high because Mn/Hg ordering on tetrahedral sites has no direct analogue in the provided CS/SS/TS set [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Flux growth uses sealed evacuated ampoules, directly supported by Hg₃Tl₂Te₄ flux growth in evacuated carbon-coated fused-silica tubes (CS4/SS1). The route avoids very high temperature but still requires volatile/toxic Hg handling and slow-cooling/flux-removal operations (CS4/SS1). Therefore, the condition severity is moderate rather than easy.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because Hg₃Tl₂Te₄ crystallized from excess Tl₂Q flux over a 14-day cooling schedule (CS4/SS1), indicating liquid-assisted transport is effective for Hg–Te chemistry. The target’s tetrahedral Hg–Te distances and corner-sharing framework are compatible with the Hg–Te tetrahedral motifs in Hg₃Tl₂Te₄ and Cu₂HgSiTe₄ (CS4/SS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether MnTe dissolves sufficiently and orders correctly during slow cooling [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Bridgman/melt growth is supported for a related telluride growth method (CS3) and high-temperature evacuated ampoule telluride synthesis (TS4), but Hg volatility makes the sealed-ampoule requirement more severe [Reasonable Inference]. The proposed 700–850 °C range avoids the >1000 °C penalty, unlike the highest schedules reported for Cu₂HgSiTe₄-type synthesis up to 1050 °C (TS4). Toxic Hg precursors remain a fixed penalty.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A melt or partially molten route can improve homogenization relative to powder sintering, consistent with Bridgman telluride growth (CS3). However, TS4 explicitly reports severe phase-purity difficulty for Cu₂HgSiTe₄-type materials, with impurities such as elemental Te and binary tellurides, so structural ordering from a melt is not guaranteed. This makes Route 3 competitive but riskier than flux growth.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target framework is entirely corner-sharing tetrahedra according to the target structural description, so it does not trigger the mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity penalty. The likely Mn/Hg cation size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius evidence and are scored neutral for that specific radius check. The compact formula and ordered tetragonal framework suggest low-to-medium amorphization tendency rather than high risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnHg₂Te₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom from the target data. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis well: Hg-containing tellurides/chalcogenides are accessible in evacuated ampoules (CS4/SS1, SS3, SS5/TS2), and tetrahedral Hg–Te telluride frameworks exist in Hg₃Tl₂Te₄ and Cu₂HgSiTe₄ (CS4/SS1, TS4). The most important limiting factor is control of Hg/Te volatility and Mn/Hg site ordering during slow crystallization [Reasonable Inference], especially because related tellurides sometimes show phase segregation or persistent binary/Te impurities (CS1, TS4). Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with Te-rich flux growth offering the best balance between mobility, temperature, and phase control.
