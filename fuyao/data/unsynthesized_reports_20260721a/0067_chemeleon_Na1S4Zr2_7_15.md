### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied hull data. This is reinforced by multiple close analogues with E_hull = 0.0, including Ca₂SnS₄ (CS-1), SrZrS₃ (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2), CeScS₃ (SS-1; TS-1), and BaCe₂MnS₅ (SS-4; TS-3). The uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the specific R-3m layered polymorph will form under accessible kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled evacuated ampoule conditions, volatile/air-sensitive sulfide handling, and multistep grinding/sealing/annealing, all of which are common in the CS/SS/TS analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4; SS-2, SS-5; TS-2). It avoids temperatures above 1000 °C if run near 850–950 °C, consistent with SrZrS₃ and SrTiS₃-type sulfide routes (CS-2, CS-4; SS-3; TS-2). The largest uncertainty is whether Na₂S/ZrS₂/Zr₂S₃ can access the target redox and layered ordering without secondary ZrS₂ or Na₂Sₓ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfides have moderate precursor activity, as shown by successful sulfide–sulfide solid-state reactions in CS-1, CS-4, SS-1–5, and TS-1–3. Kinetics are still limited by Zr-containing framework formation and by the absence of a directly matching R-3m structural analogue; SrZrS₃ analogues use ZrS₆ octahedra but in Pnma networks (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2). Long anneals and regrinding in analogues suggest ordering is not instantaneous (CS-2; SS-2, SS-5; TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Elemental synthesis is directly supported for SrZrS₃ using Sr, Zr powder, and sulfur in evacuated silica tubes (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2). The severity score is reduced by the need for a controlled sealed atmosphere, volatile sulfur/Na management, and air-sensitive Na handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route does not require >1000 °C if modeled after the 950 °C SrZrS₃ route (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental Zr powder is kinetically sluggish because it is refractory [Reasonable Inference], although SrZrS₃ does form from Zr powder after long heating and slow cooling (CS-2; SS-3; TS-2). Complete structure formation from elements is a larger reconstruction burden than reacting preformed sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore Route 2 is feasible but kinetically less attractive than Route 1 or 4.

#### F2 — Route 3

The oxide/carbonate sulfidation route is penalized by high temperature and toxic/reactive gas atmospheres, as H₂S firing at about 1373 K is used for SrZrS₃ (CS-3), and H₂S or CS₂/N₂ routes are used for related sulfides (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5; TS-1). It is experimentally established for sulfide formation, but not specifically for Na-containing layered zirconium sulfides. Additional post-sulfidation annealing is likely required [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

ZrO₂ and carbonate precursors can be converted to sulfides under H₂S according to CS-3, but ZrO₂ is refractory and may sulfidize incompletely [Reasonable Inference]. The transformation from oxide/carbonate to layered NaZr₂S₄ requires major anion and cation rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the lowest kinetic score among the routes despite strong precedent for making perovskite-type sulfides (CS-3; SS-1; TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses lower temperature, which avoids the >1000 °C penalty [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires sealed ampoules and strict air-free handling, consistent with the sealed sulfide analogues in CS-1, CS-4, SS-2, SS-5, and TS-3. Direct flux evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the reduced temperature benefit is based on [Reasonable Inference] rather than direct analogue proof.

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux or polysulfide media should improve mass transport and Na incorporation relative to dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is supported as a crystallization strategy by Ca₂SnS₄ and SrZrS₃ analogues (CS-1, CS-2; SS-3; TS-2). The uncertainty is phase selectivity: no CS/SS/TS example directly shows flux formation of the target R-3m Na–Zr sulfide.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The major amorphization risk is the large Na/Zr cation size and charge contrast [Reasonable Inference]. The target does not appear to exceed 40 atoms per unit cell and does not show simultaneous corner/edge/face sharing in the provided description, so only one of three checks is triggered. The layered R-3m description suggests crystallographic simplicity, but Na disorder or stacking faults remain possible [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(ZrS₂)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, or thermodynamically similar sulfides are also on or near the hull (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-4; TS-1, TS-3). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support high-temperature sealed-tube or reactive-gas synthesis of ternary and quaternary sulfides, especially ZrS₆-containing SrZrS₃ (CS-2, CS-3; SS-3; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the robust precedent for forming crystalline metal sulfides in evacuated ampoules with long annealing and slow cooling (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4; SS-2–5; TS-2–3). The most critical limiting factor is not thermodynamics but kinetic/chemical control of Na volatility, sulfur fugacity, and the unusual layered R-3m topology [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance likely coming from a Na-rich low-temperature sulfide flux followed by sealed-tube annealing.
