### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom according to the provided target data, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. The closest references include exact-composition K₂Ni₃S₄ with E_hull = 0.0, Rb₂Ni₃S₄ with E_hull = 0.0, Cs₂Ni₃S₄/Cs₄Ni₆S₈ with E_hull = 0.0, and K₂Ni₃Se₄ with E_hull = 0.0, supporting stability across the A₂Ni₃X₄ family (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The main uncertainty is not thermodynamic existence but polymorph selection, because the target is monoclinic C2/m while the closest exact reference is orthorhombic Fddd (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, sulfur/polysulfide volatility, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive K₂S/K-polysulfide handling, all consistent with the reported K₂S₃ flux synthesis (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). It avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the synthesis is at 750 °C in a silica tube, not above 1000 °C and not under high pressure (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The uncertainty is whether the same conditions stabilize the monoclinic target rather than the reported orthorhombic polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Flux growth improves reaction kinetics by providing liquid-mediated transport, and the exact K₂Ni₃S₄ analogue was grown from K₂S₃ flux (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Dense Ni powder can be slow to react [Reasonable Inference], but the 6-day hold and flux environment mitigate this. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the reference has the same K₂Ni₃S₄ composition and square-planar NiS₄ units, though polymorph conversion from Fddd to C2/m remains uncertain (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures, 800–850 °C, and standard Ar atmosphere rather than high pressure or >1000 °C treatment (CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). It is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sulfur volatility, and post-processing/washing complexity because A₂M₃X₄ analogues require Ar and excess alkali carbonate/chalcogen with washing/drying noted in analogue reasoning (CS-5, SS-5, TS-5). The route benefits from stable, accessible precursors such as K₂CO₃, Ni, and S, unlike K₂S-based flux growth (CS-5, SS-3, TS-3).

#### F3 — Route 2

K₂CO₃/S fusion likely creates a reactive melt/flux environment that helps overcome Ni diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference], and Rb₂Ni₃S₄/Cs₂Ni₃S₄/K₂Ni₃Se₄ analogues form under these conditions (CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The structural pathway is credible because the analogues share square-planar Ni–chalcogen units and A₂M₃X₄ stoichiometry (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5). The main uncertainty is that carbonate fusion is demonstrated mainly for Rb/Cs or K–Se analogues, while exact K₂Ni₃S₄ is directly documented by K₂S₃ flux rather than carbonate fusion (CS-1 versus CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for H₂S/H₂–S reactive gas handling, volatile sulfur transport, multi-step processing, and toxicity of H₂S (CS-4, CS-5, TS-4). It does not require high pressure or extreme temperature because KFeS₂ was made at 1000 K and A₂M₃X₄ analogues at 800–850 °C (CS-4, CS-5, TS-4). The safety and equipment burden is higher than Route 2 despite comparable temperatures (CS-4, CS-5).

#### F3 — Route 3

A sulfiding gas atmosphere provides high sulfur activity and can convert alkali carbonate/metal mixtures into ternary sulfides, as shown for KFeS₂ and related chalcogenide routes (CS-4, CS-5, TS-4). However, the specific K₂Ni₃S₄ phase is not reported from this method, so phase-selective crystallization is less certain than in Route 1 or Route 2 (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). Dense Ni powder remains a likely kinetic limitation unless finely divided or pre-reacted [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large K–Ni cation-size/coordination contrast triggers one amorphization-risk criterion; target and analogue descriptions show long K–S contacts around 3.3–3.4 Å and short Ni–S contacts around 2.2 Å (target description; CS-1, SS-1, SS-2). There is insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS to determine whether the target unit cell contains more than 40 atoms, and insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for mixed vertex/edge/face sharing in the target. The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high because the family contains many crystalline sulfide analogues (CS-1, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3).
