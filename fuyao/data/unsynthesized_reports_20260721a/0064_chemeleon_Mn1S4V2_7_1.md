### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase (Target data). TS analogues include several E_hull = 0 sulfides/chalcogenides, supporting that related V–S frameworks can be stable (TS1–TS5). CS and SS analogues show low but sometimes positive hull energies, so they support synthesizability but leave uncertainty about finite-temperature phase competition (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5).

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 conditions are moderately severe because the closest analogue requires 800–1000 °C, evacuated silica tubes, and grinding between firings (CS1/SS1). Kinetics are limited by solid-state diffusion of refractory V-containing mixtures [Reasonable Inference], but the exact structural analogue V₂CrS₄ is made by this route and therefore strongly supports transferability (CS1/SS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Mn/V ordering competes with other Mn–V–S phases [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for MnV₂S₄ itself].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The H₂S route is more hazardous because SS3/SS5 require dry H₂S, multiple thermal steps, sulfur adjustment, and sealed-tube annealing. Kinetically it is attractive because sulfidation of MV₂O₆-type oxides is reported to complete near 750 °C and NiV₂S₄ has the same C2/m topology as the target (SS3). The uncertainty is whether a MnV₂O₆ precursor converts cleanly to Mn(VS₂)₂ rather than forming MnS, VS₂, or spinel-like side phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT requires sealed evacuated tubes, iodine, and a stable temperature gradient, as shown for V₅S₈ and MNb₃S₆-type sulfides (SS4, TS4). It improves mass transport and crystal growth compared with direct sintering, but structural evidence is less direct than Route 1 because V₅S₈ is related but not the same ordered MnV₂S₄ structure (SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS for iodine CVT of Mn–V–S specifically, so the route is plausible but not primary.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Flux growth has mild temperature severity, with TS3 demonstrating V–S chalcogenide crystallization at 350 °C in alkali polychalcogenide flux. Kinetics should be favorable because the flux provides high mobility [Reasonable Inference], but phase selectivity is the largest issue because TS3 notes polymorphs and secondary phases for a different V–Ag–Te–S chemistry. Insufficient evidence from SS for a flux route to the exact C2/m MnV₂S₄ family, so this route is exploratory.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target’s octahedral framework contains corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion (Target structure). The likely Mn²⁺/V³⁺ charge balance gives substantially different octahedral cation radii, which can increase local strain/disorder [Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom-count criterion cannot be confirmed from the provided data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2 in this target, so only two triggers are counted.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn(VS₂)₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target data). The strongest support comes from V₂CrS₄, which is both chemically and structurally very close, shares the monoclinic C2/m octahedral framework, and has an established sealed-tube elemental synthesis (CS1/SS1). Additional support comes from NiV₂S₄ and MnCr₂S₄ analogues showing that both V₂MS₄ and Mn-containing C2/m sulfides are accessible by solid-state or sulfidation routes (SS3, CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the direct structural match to known C2/m octahedral sulfides (CS1/SS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under sulfur volatility, especially ensuring Mn/V ordering rather than competing binary or spinel-like sulfides [Reasonable Inference from repeated firing/phase-purity issues in CS2/CS3 and sulfur-control steps in SS3].
