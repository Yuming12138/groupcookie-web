### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which falls in the 90–100 scoring range. Thermodynamic analogues include Re(TeBr2)2, CuBrSe3, CuBrSe2, and TeI with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that related low-dimensional chalcogen-halide compounds can be ground-state phases (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). Pd(Se3Br)2 is nearly on the hull at 0.0035 eV/atom and experimentally made hydrothermally, reinforcing stability of the Pd–Se–Br structural chemistry (CS4, TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that the computed Ir phase may compete with molecular SeBrx/IrBrx phases not represented by the nearest analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, about 180–200 °C, matching Pd(SeBr3)2 and Pd chalcogen-halide syntheses (CS1, SS1, SS5). The severity deductions arise from volatile Br2/CCl4 or SeBr4, sealed ampoule handling, multi-step processing, and toxic/moisture-sensitive bromide chemistry (CS1, CS5, SS5). No high temperature or high pressure is required, which keeps the score moderate rather than poor (CS1, SS1). The key uncertainty is whether Ir can react sufficiently at the Pd analogue temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The liquid/solvothermal halogen medium should improve precursor activity, as related Pd–Se–Br compounds form at 180–200 °C (CS1, SS1). However, the target changes from square-planar Pd to octahedral Ir and from 0D Pd(SeBr3)2 clusters to a 1D ribbon, so structural reconstruction is nontrivial (CS1, SS1; target structure). The analogous route produced multiphase mixtures requiring manual crystal separation, so kinetics and nucleation are not fully controlled (CS1, SS1). This gives useful but imperfect kinetic support.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is mild in temperature because Pd(Se3Br)2 was synthesized at 453 K, about 180 °C, for 5 days (CS4, TS4). It still requires sealed ampoules, concentrated HBr, autogenous pressure, and hazardous selenium/acid chemistry, justifying deductions for sealed volatile/corrosive media and multi-step handling (CS4, TS4). No GPa pressure, >1000 °C heating, or reactive gas atmosphere is involved (CS4, TS4). The severity is therefore moderate.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal HBr chemistry provides reactive soluble Se/Br species and bypasses bulk diffusion, as shown by the Pd(Se3Br)2 analogue (CS4, TS4). The structural analogy is favorable because both target and Pd(Se3Br)2 are 1D transition-metal selenium bromide materials, although the target is Br-richer and contains octahedral Ir rather than square-planar Pd (CS4, TS4; target structure). Some contamination and twinning were reported for the analogue, indicating phase selection is not trivial (CS4, TS4). Overall, this is the best-supported kinetic pathway.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT/halide transport routes operate at moderate temperature gradients, 150→120 °C for SeBr3[AlBr4] and 340→290 °C for CuBrSe3 (CS5, TS2). The route requires vacuum or sealed ampoules, volatile SeBr4/Br2 species, dry handling, and a temperature gradient, causing larger condition-severity deductions (CS5, TS2). It avoids high pressure and high temperature, which keeps it feasible (CS5, TS2). The main uncertainty is whether Ir halides transport under these conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport can provide excellent mass transport and single-crystal growth, as shown for CuBrSe3 in a vacuum Vycor tube gradient (TS2). SeBr3 molecular chemistry is directly supported by quantitative SeBr3[AlBr4] growth from SeBr4/AlBr3 (CS5). However, the exact Ir–Se–Br target has no direct CVT precedent in CS/SS/TS, so crystallization pathway and composition control are uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics could be good if Ir transport is achieved, but transferability is less certain than Route 2.

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct sealed-tube bromination is supported by Re(TeBr2)2 synthesis at 550 °C in evacuated glass ampoules and CuBrSe2 synthesis at 550 °C in evacuated quartz ampoules (TS1, SS4, TS3). Deductions come from vacuum, volatile Br2/Se species, toxicity, and slow/repeated temperature programs (TS1, SS4, TS3). The route does not require >1000 °C or high pressure, but the sealed bromine pressure hazard is significant [Reasonable Inference based on TS1/TS3 conditions]. Therefore, F2 is lower than for hydrothermal synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4

Elemental ampoule reactions can form metal chalcogen halides, as shown for Re–Te–Br and Cu–Se–Br (TS1, TS3). However, Ir metal is expected to be sluggish and refractory compared with pre-halogenated or solution-phase precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s SeBr3-rich framework may also be less compatible with 550 °C direct bromination because volatile SeBrx loss or decomposition could occur [Reasonable Inference].
