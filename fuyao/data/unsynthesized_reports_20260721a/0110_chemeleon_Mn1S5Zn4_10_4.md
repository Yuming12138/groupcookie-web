### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is explicitly 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest scoring thermodynamic bin. Thermodynamic analogues include on-hull or near-hull ZnS/ZnSe and quaternary sulfides, such as ZnS polytypes at ~0.0003–0.0006 eV/atom (TS #1–#3), AgCd₂GaS₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS #4), and ZnSe at 0.0 eV/atom (TS #5). Chemically similar materials also include several stable sulfides, including Cu₄TiS₄ and ZnGa₂S₄ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS #1, CS #3). The uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct cation ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: binary sulfide sealed-ampoule solid-state

Route 1 uses moderate-to-high but standard sulfide solid-state conditions: 600–1000 °C sealed evacuated ampoules are directly supported by CS #3 and TS #5. The severity score is reduced because vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multiple grinding/annealing steps, and Mn compound handling are nontrivial [Reasonable Inference], while CS #1, CS #3, and CS #5 show that long annealing is often needed. Kinetically, binary sulfides are plausible because ZnS/MnS-type precursors are already tetrahedral sulfide building blocks (TS #5), and ZnGa₂S₄ provides an I-4 tetrahedral sulfide analogue (CS #3). The main uncertainty is whether Mn and Zn order into MnZn₄S₅ rather than forming ZnS-rich or MnS/ZnS solid-solution/secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: elemental direct reaction

Elemental sealed-tube sulfide synthesis is well supported for chemically similar materials: Cu₄TiS₄ from elements at 500 °C with long annealing (CS #1), Cu₂GeS₃ from elements at 900 °C for 35 days (CS #5), and AgCd₂GaS₄ from elements with high-T melt and long anneal (TS #4). The route is penalized because elemental sulfur and zinc are volatile at high temperature and require careful staged prereaction and sealing [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are less favorable than Route 1 because the target tetrahedral framework must form from elements rather than from preformed sulfide precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is exothermic sulfide formation and possible ampoule pressure/inhomogeneity, which are mitigated in TS #4 by staged sulfur conversion.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: vapor/sublimation growth

Vapor and sublimation growth are directly supported for structurally and thermodynamically similar ZnS polytypes at ~1200 °C from ZnS powder (SS #1, SS #2, TS #1, TS #2). The severity is higher because the route uses >1000 °C, likely sealed/vacuum conditions, and volatile Zn/S species [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, crystallization of tetrahedral ZnS frameworks from vapor is favorable in SS #1 and SS #2, but composition control for ordered MnZn₄S₅ is not demonstrated. The main uncertainty is whether Mn transports and incorporates congruently with Zn to maintain Mn:Zn = 1:4 [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from SS/TS on ternary vapor growth.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: hydrothermal/solvothermal exploratory route

Hydrothermal synthesis has weak support because CS #4 lists hydrothermal conditions but the evidence and precursors correspond to unrelated borate/oxide chemistry rather than Mn–Zn–S chemistry. Therefore, CS #4 provides insufficient useful signal for the target, and SS/TS provide no direct hydrothermal support for MnZn₄S₅. The route may benefit from reactive soluble precursors [Reasonable Inference], but ordering into an I-4 chalcopyrite-like bulk sulfide likely requires post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is high, and this route is best considered exploratory.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target framework is simple corner-sharing tetrahedral MnS₄/ZnS₄, and structurally similar ZnS analogues are also corner-sharing tetrahedral networks (SS #1, SS #2, TS #1, TS #2). No target description indicates edge/face-sharing polyhedral coexistence, and the formula unit is small, so amorphization tendency is low [Reasonable Inference]. Zn²⁺ and Mn²⁺ are chemically compatible tetrahedral divalent cations with modest ionic-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is the exact crystallographic cell size and whether disorder between Mn/Zn sites could produce diffuse or partially amorphous products [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnZn₄S₅ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from chemically similar tetrahedral sulfides synthesized by evacuated sealed-tube solid-state methods, especially I-4 ZnGa₂S₄ from binary sulfides (CS #3), chalcopyrite-like Cu₂GeS₃ from elements (CS #5), and Mn/Zn chalcogenide mixtures from ZnS/MnS-type precursors (TS #5). The most critical enabling factor is the target’s simple corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide framework, matching robust ZnS-like and chalcopyrite-like structural chemistry (SS #1–#2, CS #3, CS #5). The most critical limiting factor is cation ordering and phase competition among ZnS, MnS, and possible Mn-doped ZnS-type phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with binary-sulfide sealed-ampoule annealing as the best first attempt.
