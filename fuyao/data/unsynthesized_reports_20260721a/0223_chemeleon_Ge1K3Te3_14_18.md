### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score (Target data). Multiple high-similarity analogues, including K3BiTe3, K3SbTe3, K3AlTe3, Cs4Sn2Te7, and Cs2SnTe4, are also reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), indicating that related alkali telluride frameworks are not generally metastable. Insufficient evidence from CS for detailed target-specific competing phase equilibria, so CS is neutral for decomposition pathway assessment. Insufficient evidence from SS for target-specific impurity phase boundaries, so SS is neutral for detailed decomposition pathway assessment.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate because the best analogues use high but standard solid-state temperatures of ~700–802 °C and evacuated graphitized ampoules rather than high pressure (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The main deductions are vacuum/sealed ampoule operation and air-sensitive/volatile K–Te chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/CS2 sealed ampoule practice]. Kinetically, this route is favorable because direct elemental synthesis works for K3SbTe3, K3BiTe3, and K3AlTe3 (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The uncertainty is whether Ge will adopt the predicted Ge–Ge bonded tetrahedral motif rather than forming competing binary or polytelluride phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: K2Te-assisted solid-state reaction

The route is somewhat more severe procedurally because it requires air-sensitive K2Te and likely a precursor-preparation step, although CS3 shows this strategy works for K2ZrTe3 at 1000 °C under Ar/sealed ampoule conditions. Kinetics are potentially strong because K2Te pre-forms K–Te bonding and should reduce the need to generate the alkali telluride matrix from elemental K and Te [Reasonable Inference based on CS3]. The main uncertainty is structural transfer: CS3’s K2ZrTe3 has ZrTe6 octahedra, while target K3GeTe3 has GeGeTe3 tetrahedra, so this route is chemically plausible but less structurally directive (CS3; target structure). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for K2Te-assisted synthesis of a Ge analogue specifically, so SS/TS are neutral for this route’s precursor choice.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

Flux growth has moderate-to-high severity because CS5 used 900 °C, Ar handling, dynamic vacuum sealing, long 10-day annealing, and controlled cooling. Kinetically, flux growth can enhance transport and single-crystal formation [Reasonable Inference], and CS5 demonstrates single-crystalline KYTe2 formation using KBr flux. However, phase control is a concern because KYTe2 appeared as a by-product rather than the intended target phase (CS5). Insufficient evidence from TS for flux stabilization of K3GeTe3 specifically, so TS is neutral for route-specific phase selection.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Solvothermal / methanolothermal route

The solvothermal route has low temperature severity because SS5/TS5 report Cs2SnTe4 formation at 190 °C, but the sealed solvent system, Ar atmosphere, and low-yield crystal growth add practical difficulty. Kinetics may benefit from solution transport [Reasonable Inference], but Ge solubility and redox chemistry in methanol are unproven. SS5/TS5 also report difficult phase control and low yield for Cs2SnTe4, lowering confidence in direct transfer. Insufficient evidence from CS for solvothermal K–Ge–Te synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The amorphization/structural disorder score is moderate because the target combines a very large alkali cation with smaller Ge-centered covalent units, satisfying the cation size mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target and close K3SbTe3/K3BiTe3 analogues show mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, satisfying the mixed-connectivity check (target structure; CS1, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2). The unit-cell atom-count check is not clearly triggered because close analogues have 28 atoms per conventional formula listing, below the >40 threshold (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3), but the exact target cell atom count is not explicitly provided. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K3GeTe3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target data), and the closest thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from K3SbTe3 and K3BiTe3, which are highly similar K3MTe3 tellurides made from elements in graphitized evacuated quartz ampoules at 973 K (CS1, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple stable K-rich ternary tellurides with similar K–Te coordination and TeK6M polyhedral environments (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase control around Ge, because the target requires Ge–Ge bonded GeGeTe3 tetrahedra rather than the simpler trigonal Sb/Bi motifs in the closest analogues [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS1/SS1–SS2]. Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable by sealed-tube solid-state chemistry, with moderate optimization likely required.
