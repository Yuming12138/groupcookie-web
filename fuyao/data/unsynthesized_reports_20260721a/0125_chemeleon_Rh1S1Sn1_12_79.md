Route 1 has moderate condition severity because analogous sulfides are made in evacuated sealed ampoules at 800–1000 °C, but vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and regrinding/staged heating are needed (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Kinetics are less favorable because elemental Rh is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and the chemical analogues in CS are mostly R-3m shandite/parkerite structures rather than the target Hausmannite-derived P2₁ structure (CS-1–CS-5). The main uncertainty is whether the 1:1:1 RhSnS phase can be selectively accessed before the stable Rh₃Sn₂S₂ phase forms (CS-1, TS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized by T >1000 °C because CS-1/TS-3 report high-temperature synthesis optimized near the melting behavior of Sn₂Rh₃S₂ around 1144 °C. Kinetically, the higher temperature should improve diffusion and perhaps generate Sn/S-rich transport or melt-assisted contact [Reasonable Inference], but it also increases sulfur loss risk and may favor the known Rh₃Sn₂S₂ shandite chemistry (CS-1, TS-3). The uncertainty is whether high-T annealing stabilizes the target or drives it toward more common Rh–Sn–S compositions.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the strongest structural evidence: PtGeS and PtGeSe have similar octahedral–tetrahedral spinel-like frameworks and are synthesized in evacuated quartz tubes at 600/900 °C for 4–6 weeks (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The long duration and optional regrinding imply slow ordering kinetics, but they also provide a plausible path to the lower-symmetry ordered monoclinic target [Reasonable Inference]. Chemical transfer is uncertain because Pt–Ge–S/Se is not Rh–Sn–S, so SS evidence is structurally useful but chemically incomplete.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4’s severity is moderate-to-high because iodine CVT uses vacuum-sealed tubes and volatile/corrosive iodine, as reported for the structurally similar chalcogenide CVT synthesis at 700 °C for 6 days (SS-4). Its kinetic score is high among the routes because vapor/mineralizer transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion and grow crystals at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-4]. However, SS-4 is chemically indirect, and there is insufficient CS/SS/TS evidence that RhSnS forms volatile species in the right ratio under iodine CVT conditions.

#### F2/F3 — Route 5

Route 5 is conditionally plausible because Hausmannite-derived CoSbS is made under high pressure around 60 kbar and 1000–1700 °C (SS-3, SS-5). Its condition score is low because the route requires specialized belt/multi-anvil-style apparatus, high temperature, and controlled high-pressure cooling (SS-3, SS-5). The structural relevance is strong, but chemistry is indirect and CS/TS show Rh–Sn–S compounds can already be synthesized at ambient pressure, so high pressure is best viewed as a fallback rather than first choice (CS-1, TS-3).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target’s low calculated hull energy and the existence of crystalline structural analogues with octahedral–tetrahedral frameworks reduce amorphization concern (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). One size-mismatch trigger is applied cautiously for Rh/Sn [Reasonable Inference], while the atom count appears below 40 and the target description does not show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing. The uncertainty is that the target’s monoclinic distortion and multiple inequivalent sites may slow ordering even if it does not favor amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RhSnS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple chemically or structurally related analogues also sit on the hull (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1–TS-3). The CS evidence strongly supports the feasibility of Rh–Sn–S solid-state chemistry, especially because Rh₃Sn₂S₂ is known and stable (CS-1, TS-3), but it also reveals a likely competing phase. The SS/TS evidence supports the octahedral–tetrahedral Hausmannite/spinel-like framework, especially PtGeS/PtGeSe and CoSbS analogues (SS-1–SS-3, TS-1, TS-2). The most important enabling factor is the target’s zero hull energy combined with demonstrated evacuated-tube chalcogenide synthesis in adjacent chemistries (CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-2). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity against known Rh–Sn–S shandite chemistry and slow ordering of the low-symmetry P2₁ framework [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and SS-1/SS-2 long anneals].
