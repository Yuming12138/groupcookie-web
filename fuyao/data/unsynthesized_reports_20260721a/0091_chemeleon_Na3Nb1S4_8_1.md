### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is computed at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it on the convex hull and supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility according to the provided target data. Thermodynamically similar Nb sulfide compounds also lie on or near the hull, including K₂NbAgS₄, Rb₂NbAgS₄, Cs₂LiNbS₄, and Cs₂NbAgS₄ with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of competing Na–Nb–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, because chemically similar Na sulfides form at 220–500 °C and related sealed-tube chalcogenides form under moderate furnace conditions (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, TS-1). The deductions arise from sealed vacuum/inert handling, volatile sulfur, and moisture-sensitive sulfide precursors (CS-1, CS-5; volatility/air sensitivity partly [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from TS on exact Na₃NbS₄ processing severity, so TS is treated as supportive but not definitive.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by using Na₂S and sulfur, which are reactive sulfide sources in Na sulfide synthesis (CS-1, CS-5). Kinetics are limited by Nb conversion, because Nb metal is refractory [Reasonable Inference], but NbS₄ tetrahedra form in related alkali thiometallates under sealed/flux conditions (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-4). The structural reconstruction score is high because Na₃SbS₄ has the same I-43m framework and similar Na–S/M–S tetrahedral bonding (CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses standard sealed ampoule conditions established for direct elemental Na chalcogenide syntheses (CS-3, SS-3, SS-4). The severity is moderate because Na and sulfur require sealed, inert/vacuum handling, but no high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is intrinsically required for the proposed first attempt [Reasonable Inference based on CS-3/SS-3/SS-4]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that direct elemental Na₃NbS₄ specifically forms without secondary phases.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental synthesis has lower kinetic score because Nb powder is less reactive than preformed sulfides or solution precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Direct elemental analogues show Na–chalcogen frameworks can form in graphitized ampoules at 775–1173 K (CS-3, SS-3, SS-4), but those analogues use Si/In rather than refractory Nb. Structural reconstruction from elements is more demanding than converting sulfide precursors, even though the final Na₃MS₄ motif is strongly supported by CS-2 and NbS₄ analogues (SS-1, TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses controlled vacuum/inert atmospheres, sealed tubes, slow cooling, and post-synthesis washing, which are all reported for Nb thiometallate flux syntheses (SS-2, TS-2, TS-4). It does not require high pressure or extreme temperature, but it is multi-step and uses air-sensitive alkali sulfide/polysulfide media (SS-2, TS-4; air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]). TS evidence is useful for Nb sulfide flux chemistry, while CS gives insufficient direct signal for this exact flux route and is treated as neutral for flux-specific details.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux conditions improve precursor activity and diffusion, consistent with Rb₂NbAgS₄ and Cs₂LiNbS₄-type compounds forming from reactive chalcogenide fluxes at 350 °C to 773 K (SS-2, TS-2, TS-4). The limiting uncertainty is whether a sodium-rich flux will crystallize the simple Na₃NbS₄ phase rather than other Na–Nb–S phases [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control difficulty is supported by flux literature noting composition sensitivity and failures for some analogues (TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 4

The solution route is low-temperature, based on Na₃SbS₄ precipitation/evaporation chemistry at up to 120 °C in sulfide/ammonia media (CS-2). Severity deductions come from NH₃/H₂S/sulfide atmosphere, multistep handling, and toxic or moisture-sensitive reagents (CS-2; NbCl₅/H₂S hazards partly [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for solution synthesis of Nb analogues, so they are scored neutral for this route’s condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 4

Solution routes offer high precursor mixing and negligible solid-state diffusion barriers, as implied by the precipitation route for Na₃SbS₄ (CS-2). However, CS-2 also reports phase mixtures and hygroscopicity issues for Na₃SbS₄-related materials, lowering confidence in phase-pure crystallization. No CS/SS/TS source demonstrates a niobium solution thio-salt synthesis, and Nb(V) hydrolysis/oxo-sulfide formation is a major extrapolated risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is triggered: the Na/Nb cation size/charge contrast is large [Reasonable Inference], but the structure is a simple Na₃NbS₄ framework with one Na, one Nb, and one S site rather than a large complex cell. The target and exact Na₃SbS₄ analogue show edge and corner sharing but not face sharing (target description, CS-2), so the mixed-connectivity criterion requiring vertex+edge+face coexistence is not fully met. Remaining uncertainty is that alkali sulfides can be glass-forming under rapid quench, but the crystalline analogues indicate controlled cooling should favor crystallization (CS-2, SS-1, TS-4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₃NbS₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Nb sulfides also lie on the hull (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest support comes from Na₃SbS₄, which has the same cubic I-43m Na₃MS₄ structure type and very similar local Na–S/M–S coordination (CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the strong chemical compatibility of tetrahedral NbS₄ units, which are present with Nb–S ≈2.31 Å in related alkali Nb thiometallates (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase control: Nb is refractory, sulfur is volatile, sodium sulfides are air-sensitive, and several analogues require sealed tubes, controlled atmospheres, or show impurity/phase-selection issues (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, TS-4). Overall, synthesis is plausible and moderately favorable, especially via sealed sulfide solid-state reaction or reactive sulfide flux.
