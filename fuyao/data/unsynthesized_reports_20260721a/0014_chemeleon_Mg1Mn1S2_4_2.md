### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The strongest point is the target’s reported E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 thermodynamic score band. Stable or near-stable sulfide analogues are common in SS/TS: MnBiS2Cl has E_hull = 0.0 (SS4/TS3), NaInS2 has E_hull = 0.0 and is Caswellsilverite structured (TS5), and MnBiS2Br/NaEuAsS4 are near zero (SS2/TS1, SS3/TS2). CS is mixed because CuScS2 is stable (CS4), but CuCrS2/CuCrSe2 are significantly above hull (CS1, CS5), so the final F1 score is high but not 100.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

The route uses moderate temperatures similar to SS1, SS4/TS3, and CS1/CS5, but it requires evacuated sealed ampoules, which triggers controlled-atmosphere severity. Regrinding/reannealing is likely because SS1 uses two 6-day anneals and CS5 describes extended high-temperature treatment. The main uncertainty is MgS/MnS reactivity and sulfur stoichiometry; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Mg sulfide handling, so that part is treated as [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state

Binary sulfides are more reactive than refractory oxides but less reactive than molecular or solution precursors, consistent with the long anneals in SS1 and CS5. Diffusion is moderate because analogous sulfides form between 500 and 1000 °C (SS1, SS4/TS3, CS1/CS5), but dry solid-state Mg/Mn cation ordering may be slow [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is nontrivial because MgS/MnS must form a layered Caswellsilverite-like ternary phase rather than a simple binary mixture [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: flux growth

The flux route avoids >1000 °C operation because CS3 demonstrates sulfide flux growth at 750 °C and SS3/TS2 at 650 °C. It still requires sealed tubes, volatile sulfur control, and multistep processing, supported by the sealed flux methods in CS3 and SS3/TS2. The use of MgS or polysulfide flux introduces air/moisture sensitivity by [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS directly support sensitivity for Na2S/polysulfide handling in SS3/TS2.

#### F3 — Route 2: flux growth

Flux-mediated transport improves kinetics relative to dry pellets, as shown by CS3 and SS3/TS2. The strongest structural support is TS5, where Caswellsilverite NaInS2 is flux-derived; CS3 also shows flux can stabilize rhombohedral sulfide variants. The uncertainty is whether Mg/Mn cation ordering in P-3m1 can be achieved rather than producing MnS, MgS, or spinel-like impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: CVT

CVT is harsher because CS4 uses a 1050–950 °C gradient and CS2 uses 700–1030 °C with halogen transport agents. I2/Cl2 transport requires evacuated sealed tubes and toxic/volatile transport agents, directly supported by CS2 and CS4. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Mg-containing CVT, so route severity is penalized for uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3: CVT

Halogen transport can overcome bulk diffusion limitations, as shown for CuScS2 and V–Cu–S crystals (CS4, CS2). However, the target may require first preparing a phase-pure or near-phase-pure powder charge, analogous to the multistep approach in CS2. Composition control is the key kinetic uncertainty because Mg and Mn may transport at different rates as iodides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The simple MgMnS2 formula and small likely unit cell reduce amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference], unlike large-cell complex examples in SS3/TS2 and SS5/TS4. The target’s mixed corner/edge/face-sharing octahedral network increases disorder risk; structurally similar materials often contain mixed edge/corner connectivity (SS1, TS5), but target face-sharing is a stronger local constraint. The Mg/Mn octahedral radius mismatch is just over the 15% trigger by [Reasonable Inference], and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct Mg/Mn radius evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgMnS2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several SS/TS analogues are also stable or near-stable sulfides (SS4/TS3, TS5, SS2/TS1). The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: CS and SS show many ternary sulfides are accessible in sealed tubes, and TS5 specifically supports a Caswellsilverite-type sulfide made by flux methods. The most critical enabling factor is flux- or sealed-tube control of sulfur chemical potential, supported by CS3, SS3/TS2, and TS5. The most critical limiting factor is achieving Mg/Mn ordering in an octahedral sulfide lattice without forming MgS/MnS binaries or Mn-rich competing sulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with flux growth or reactive flux annealing preferred over direct dry sintering.
