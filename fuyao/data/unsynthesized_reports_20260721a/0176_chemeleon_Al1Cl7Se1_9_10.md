### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which maps to the top stability bracket. Many close halide molecular analogues are experimentally synthesized with E_hull near zero, including Sn2P2Cl18 (CS1), Zr2Se4Cl24 (CS2/TS2), Hf2Se4Cl24 (TS1), and Ta2Te2Br18 (TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability for molecular halides may not fully capture volatility, hydrolysis, or sublimation equilibria [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses only moderate temperatures, matching SeCl4/metal chloride ampoule syntheses at 140–155 °C (CS2, SS1, TS1, TS2) and the AlBr4SeBr3 ampoule synthesis at 150 °C (SS2). The deductions arise from vacuum/sealed operation, volatile halide reagents, dry handling, and multi-operation ampoule growth, all of which are explicitly present in the analogue procedures (SS2, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether AlCl3/SeCl4 vapor pressures and stoichiometry will produce the simple AlCl4SeCl3 salt rather than higher-coordinate SeCl6-type chlorometallates [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because the reaction proceeds through molecular volatile halides rather than solid-state diffusion, as indicated by crystal deposition in temperature gradients (SS1, TS1). The exact Br analogue has the same separated AlX4/SeX3 cluster topology and crystallizes at 150 °C (SS2), strongly reducing the structural reconstruction penalty. Uncertainty remains because replacing Br by Cl changes Lewis acidity, volatility, and lattice packing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The chloride melt route is low temperature, supported by Bi4Te4[AlCl4]4 formation at 130 °C in Na[AlCl4]/AlCl3 melt (TS5) and Cd[AlCl4]2 synthesis at 250 °C (CS3). It is penalized for inert/sealed conditions, volatile/corrosive halides, multiple processing steps, and flux separation, all consistent with TS5. The uncertainty is that SeCl4 may react with the melt or volatilize instead of cleanly forming SeCl3+ [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics should be good because the chloride melt provides a mobile ionic/liquid medium, and TS5 demonstrates low-temperature crystallization of AlCl4-containing clusters. Structural formation is plausible because AlCl4− is directly supported by TS5/CS3, while Se chloride cluster formation is supported separately by SeCl4 ampoule chemistry (CS2, SS1, TS1). The weakness is that no provided source reports Al–Se–Cl crystallization from chloride melt, so transferability is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The solution route has the mildest physical conditions because CS1 reports chemically similar molecular chloride salts forming by dry solution mixing at room temperature or reflux. It still requires drybox/Schlenk methods, air-sensitive/toxic chlorides, and crystallization/filtration operations, supported by CS1 and SS5. The uncertainty is solution speciation: donor solvents or trace moisture can consume AlCl3 or hydrolyze SeCl4 [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solution kinetics are expected to be rapid because CS1 reports mixing and reflux of reactive chlorides over minutes to hours. Diffusion limitations are minimal in solution, but structural selectivity is less certain because the exact AlBr4SeBr3 structural analogue was obtained by closed-ampoule thermal crystallization rather than by solution (SS2). Therefore, Route 3 scores highly but receives an expert penalty for phase-selective crystallization uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a 0D molecular salt with isolated AlCl4 and SeCl3 clusters, not a connected mixed-polyhedral framework, which lowers amorphization risk; this motif is directly analogous to AlBr4SeBr3 (SS2). Only the cation size/coordination disparity check is triggered [Reasonable Inference], while unit-cell atom count and mixed connectivity are not triggered from the target structural description and SS2. The main uncertainty is that molecular halides may crystallize readily but may also form glasses/oils if solvent or temperature gradients are poorly controlled [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state molecular halide because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close halide analogues with E_hull ≈ 0 are experimentally realized (CS1, CS2, TS1, TS2). The strongest synthesis support is the exact structural analogue AlBr4SeBr3, which contains separated AlBr4 and SeBr3 clusters and forms quantitatively in a closed ampoule at 150 °C (SS2). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of molecular halides such as SeCl4 and AlCl3, which enables low-temperature ion-pair crystallization rather than refractory solid-state diffusion (CS2, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is moisture/volatility control, because analogous chloride and bromide molecular salts are moisture-sensitive and require dry sealed or drybox conditions (CS1, SS2, CS4, TS1). Overall, synthesis is feasible, with Route 3 mildest and Route 1 most structurally validated.
