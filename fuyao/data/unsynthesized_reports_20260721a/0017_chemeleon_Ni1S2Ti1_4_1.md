### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. TS analogues are strongly supportive because Ti₂CoS₄ is near the hull at 0.016 eV/atom (TS1), while Ta₂Ni₃S₈, Nb₃VS₆, and FeNb₃S₆ are on the hull (TS3–TS5). CS is mixed: CuScS₂ is on the hull and structurally very relevant (CS4), but CuCrS₂ and CuCrSe₂ are substantially above the hull (CS1, CS5), so CS alone would not prove stability. Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed P3m1 TiNiS₂ ordering is the experimentally selected ground-state ordering rather than a competing Ti–Ni–S polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard evacuated silica-tube elemental reaction conditions, which are well precedented for Ti–M–S/Se analogues (SS1/TS1) and CuFeS₂ (CS3). The main deductions are vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile sulfur, and repeated grinding/firing, all supported by SS1/TS1 and CS3. There is insufficient evidence from CS for exact TiNiS₂ elemental synthesis, so CS contributes only general sulfide solid-state support. The route avoids high pressure and avoids intentionally toxic transport agents, keeping severity moderate.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by the low activity of elemental Ti/Ni powders and the need to build an ordered mixed-cation sulfide from dense solids [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 demonstrates that elemental Ti–M–S reactions are feasible at 600–1000 °C, but it also reports phase-control difficulty and disordered phases from stoichiometry deviations. CS3 supports the general viability of elemental sulfide reaction in evacuated tubes, but CuFeS₂ has a simpler tetrahedral chalcopyrite network than the target. Thus, Route 1 is feasible but slow and likely requires multiple anneals.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar sealed-tube/vacuum and sulfur-volatility penalties as Route 1, based on CS1, CS5, SS2, and SS3. Its severity is not dominated by temperature because the likely main anneal is 850–900 °C, supported by SS2 and SS3. If TiS₂ and NiS are purchased or pre-made separately, H₂S can be avoided; if they are synthesized by sulfidation, SS2 indicates H₂S may be involved and would increase risk. Overall severity is moderate.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfide precursors improve chemical proximity and shorten diffusion/reconstruction distances relative to pure elements [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by CS1 and CS5, where binary chalcogenides are used for layered ternary chalcogenides, and by SS3, where TiS₂ is a direct precursor to Fe–Ti–S phases. The score is not higher because SS2 and SS3 both indicate long annealing, sulfur adjustment, or repeated firing are needed. The key uncertainty is which nickel sulfide precursor best buffers Ni chemical potential without stabilizing competing Ni–S binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT has the highest condition severity among the proposed routes because it uses halogen transport, vacuum sealing, a temperature gradient, and often temperatures around or above 1000 °C. These requirements are directly supported by CuScS₂ CVT at 1050–950 °C with I₂ (CS4), V–Cu–S CVT with iodine/chlorine (CS2), Ta–Ni–S CVT with Br₂ (TS3), and FeNb₃S₆ CVT with I₂ (TS5). The method does not require high pressure, but ampoule safety and halogen transport control are significant. Insufficient evidence from SS for exact P3m1 TiNiS₂ CVT, though SS supports related Ti sulfides.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT receives the best kinetics score because vapor transport can bypass slow bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest evidence is CS4: P3m1 CuScS₂, the closest structural analogue to the target, is grown by iodine transport. TS3 and TS5 further show Ni-containing or ilmenite-like sulfides can be crystallized by halogen transport. The main uncertainty is whether Ti and Ni transport at compatible rates under I₂, since unequal volatile iodide formation could shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux synthesis is less thermally severe because SS4 reports Ti–Cu–S flux growth at 750 °C. It still requires sealed evacuated silica tubes due to sulfur volatility and to maintain a controlled halide/sulfide environment [Reasonable Inference], as in SS4. Multi-step handling is required because the product must be separated from KCl/KI flux [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS and TS for KCl/KI flux synthesis of TiNiS₂ specifically, so the route remains extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 4

KCl/KI flux should enhance mass transport and may promote crystalline ordering at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 supports this concept for a Ti–Cu–S sulfide formed at 750 °C with slow cooling. However, the SS4 product is CuTi₂S₄ rather than TiNiS₂ and has a different stoichiometry and structure, so structural reconstruction support is only moderate. The uncertainty is whether TiNiS₂ has sufficient solubility and nucleation preference in KCl/KI flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a relatively simple formula and no evidence for a large unit cell, which lowers amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The main risk is cation size/coordination mismatch between octahedral Ti and tetrahedral Ni, reflected in substantially longer Ti–S than Ni–S bonds in the target structure and analogous octahedral/tetrahedral mismatch in CuScS₂ (CS4). The target has corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, while some SS/TS analogues include face sharing (SS1/TS1), so the target appears less topologically frustrated than those analogues. Remaining uncertainty is the exact oxidation-state/radius assignment for Ti/Ni in TiNiS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiNiS₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also stable or near-stable (TS1, TS3–TS5). The synthesis is well supported at the broader chemical level by Ti–M–S solid-state analogues containing Ni (SS1/TS1) and by binary-sulfide routes for layered ternary chalcogenides (CS1, CS5, SS3). The closest structural support is P3m1 CuScS₂, which has the same octahedral/tetrahedral sulfide framework and was synthesized by iodine CVT (CS4). The most important enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability plus known accessibility of related transition-metal sulfides in sealed ampoules (TS1, SS1, SS3). The most important limiting factor is cation ordering/phase competition in the Ti–Ni–S system, because related Ti–M–S materials can show stoichiometry-sensitive disorder and require repeated firings (SS1/TS1).
