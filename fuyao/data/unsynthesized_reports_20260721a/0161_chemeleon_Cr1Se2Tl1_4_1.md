### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 range by the requested rubric. CS gives additional favorable thermodynamic signal because Tl3VSe4, Tl3NbSe4, Tl2HfSe3, and Tl3TaSe4 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS-1/CS-2/CS-3/CS-5. SS/TS also support stable Cr–Se chemistries, with KCr5Se8, RbCr5Se8, ZnCr2Se4, CsCr5Se8, and MnCr2Se4 at or near the hull in SS/TS. The remaining uncertainty is that neither CS nor SS nor TS directly confirms experimental TlCrSe2, so the score is high but not 100.

#### F2 — Route 1, direct sealed-ampoule solid-state

The severity is moderate because analogous Tl chalcogenides and Cr selenides use sealed evacuated tubes or quartz ampoules rather than high pressure, as shown in CS-3 and TS-5. The proposed 800–900 °C range is below the >1000 °C penalty threshold and is consistent with TS-5 and CS-1/CS-5. The main deductions arise from vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, volatile Tl/Se retention, multiple thermal steps, and toxic Tl, supported directly or inferentially by CS-1/CS-3/CS-5 and TS-5. Insufficient evidence from SS for Tl handling specifically, so SS is neutral for Tl volatility and toxicity.

#### F3 — Route 1, direct sealed-ampoule solid-state

Kinetics are limited by elemental Cr, which is refractory by physicochemical knowledge [Reasonable Inference], and by the need to form an ordered ternary layered phase. TS-5 demonstrates that Cr selenides can form from elemental powders at 800 °C, and SS-4/TS-3 show CrSe6 octahedral frameworks are accessible at 600–1000 °C. However, CS/SS/TS do not show the exact TlCrSe2 structure, so structural reconstruction is only partially supported. The resulting kinetics score is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2, Bridgman/melt growth

CS gives strong condition precedent because Tl3VSe4, Tl3NbSe4, and Tl3TaSe4 are made from high-purity elements by Bridgman–Stockbarger at about 800 °C under Ar in sealed ampoules (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5). The route avoids high pressure and avoids >1000 °C synthesis, so no deductions apply for those factors. Deductions remain for controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile Tl/Se chemistry, multi-step crystal growth, and toxic Tl handling, all consistent with CS-1/CS-2/CS-5 and [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Bridgman growth of TlCrSe2 specifically, so SS/TS are neutral on Bridgman transferability.

#### F3 — Route 2, Bridgman/melt growth

Melt growth improves kinetics because liquid transport reduces diffusion limitations, and CS-1/CS-2/CS-5 directly report successful Tl–transition-metal–Se crystal growth from elemental melts. The main uncertainty is that the CS analogues are Tl3BSe4 tetrahedral frameworks, whereas the target is a layered CrSe6 octahedral Caswellsilverite phase. SS/TS strongly support the CrSe6 octahedral motif in related Cr selenides, but not the TlCrSe2 stoichiometry. Therefore, kinetics are favorable but phase selection remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3, Tl–Se self-flux

Flux/fusion Cr selenide growth is supported by SS-5/TS-4, and Tl-containing melt chemistry is supported by CS-1/CS-5. The route is kept below or near 850 °C by [Reasonable Inference], so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty despite some SS-5/TS-4 examples reaching 1060 °C. It still requires sealed volatile Tl/Se chemistry, controlled atmosphere, multiple steps, and toxic Tl handling. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Tl–Se self-flux growth of TlCrSe2 makes this route more speculative than Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 3, Tl–Se self-flux

The flux route may have the best kinetic profile because liquid Tl–Se can dissolve/transport Cr and allow slow crystallization, which is a [Reasonable Inference] supported indirectly by SS-5/TS-4 flux/fusion growth of chromium selenides. SS-5/TS-4 demonstrate that crystalline Cr–Se frameworks can grow from flux/fusion media in hours. However, neither CS nor SS nor TS confirms that Tl–Se flux stabilizes the TlCrSe2 Caswellsilverite phase rather than binaries or other ternaries. Thus, the kinetics score is high, but the confidence is lower.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ordered ABX2-type structure with edge-sharing CrSe6 octahedra, and the target description does not indicate a large unit cell or mixed edge/corner/face connectivity. A cation-size mismatch between Tl and Cr is significant by [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the very different Tl–Se and Cr–Se bond lengths in the target structure. SS/TS analogues often show more complex mixed connectivity, but the target itself does not, so only one amorphization trigger is counted. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target cell size is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlCrSe2 is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically or thermodynamically related Tl–Se and Cr–Se compounds are also on the hull in CS/SS/TS. The CS data strongly support sealed Tl–Se melt chemistry, especially via Bridgman growth from high-purity elements at ~800 °C, while SS/TS strongly support the accessibility of CrSe6 octahedral selenide frameworks. The most important enabling factor is the combination of favorable thermodynamics and liquid/sealed-ampoule transport, supported by CS-1/CS-2/CS-5 and TS-5. The most important limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control under volatile Tl/Se conditions, with supercooling and impurity inclusions reported for Tl chalcogenide crystal growth in CS-1/CS-2/CS-5. Overall, synthesis is plausible but should be approached as a controlled sealed-ampoule melt or flux experiment rather than a simple open-tube reaction.
