The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a near-ground-state assignment and justifies a high F1 score. Multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar tellurides are also on the hull, including K2ZrTe3 (CS-1), K3SbTe3 (CS-2; SS-3; TS-3), Cs4Sn2Te7 (SS-1; TS-1), and Cs2SnTe4 (SS-5; TS-5). The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclude low-temperature competing K–Sn–Te polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 direct elemental route

Route 1 is moderately severe because K3SbTe3/K3BiTe3 require evacuated graphitized ampoules at 973 K (CS-2; SS-3; TS-3; SS-4; TS-4), and volatile/air-sensitive K handling requires sealed inert preparation [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is required by the closest elemental analogues. Remaining uncertainty is whether K2SnTe3 needs quenching like K2ZrTe3, which used quenching from 800 °C (CS-1).

#### F3 — Route 1 direct elemental route

Direct elements are reactive enough for related K–Te ternaries (CS-2; SS-3; TS-3), but full nucleation of a triclinic SnTe4/K–Te framework from elements is kinetically nontrivial [Reasonable Inference]. Low melting K/Sn improves transport [Reasonable Inference], while Te volatility can complicate homogenization. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a same-structure K2SnTe3 precursor, so structural reconstruction is penalized.

#### F2 — Route 2 alkali telluride route

The K2Te route benefits from direct K2MTe3 precedent: K2ZrTe3 was made from K2Te, Zr, and Te at 1000 °C under Ar in evacuated ampoules (CS-1). Severity is reduced because no high pressure or ultrahigh temperature is required, but K2Te is air-sensitive and the sealed system is important [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Sn substitution permits lower temperature or instead promotes competing K–Sn–Te phases.

#### F3 — Route 2 alkali telluride route

K2Te should pre-form the K–Te chemical environment and improve precursor activity versus elemental K [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 validates this strategy for K2ZrTe3. However, K2ZrTe3 contains ZrTe6 octahedra, whereas the target contains edge-sharing SnTe4 tetrahedra, so the structural transformation is only partially transferable (CS-1; target). Kinetic risk is moderate rather than low.

#### F2 — Route 3 acetate reactive sintering

The acetate route scores best on condition severity because Cs/Rb stannate tellurides are made at 700–850 °C under Ar without high pressure (SS-1; TS-1). P2O5 water scavenging and dry handling are required because water from acetate pyrolysis must be controlled and products decompose in moist air (SS-1; TS-1). The route avoids direct handling of K metal, which is a practical advantage [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 acetate reactive sintering

Acetate precursors are high-activity by the scoring rubric and are directly validated for alkali–Sn–Te formation in SS-1/TS-1. SnTe4-based alkali stannate frameworks in the analogues support transferability to K2SnTe3 (SS-1; TS-1), although exact stoichiometry and smaller K+ require optimization [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether acetate decomposition yields the correct K activity without trapping secondary tellurides.

#### F2 — Route 4 solvothermal route

The solvothermal route is mild thermally because Cs2SnTe4 forms at 190 °C for 2 days in methanol under Ar (SS-5; TS-5). It still requires sealed ampoules with volatile methanol and careful fill level/cooling control (SS-5; TS-5). Severity is therefore moderate, not easy.

#### F3 — Route 4 solvothermal route

Solution-mediated transport gives favorable kinetics and low diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], supported by the low-temperature crystallization of Cs2SnTe4 (SS-5; TS-5). The target shares SnTe4 tetrahedral chemistry with Cs2SnTe4 (SS-5; TS-5), but K2SnTe3 is less Te-rich and structurally distinct. SS-5/TS-5 report low yield and interface crystal growth, so phase-pure scaling is uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 5 halide flux

Halide-flux synthesis of K tellurides is supported by KInTe2 and KYTe2 references (CS-4; CS-5). Temperatures of ~900 °C are manageable and no high pressure is required (CS-5), but sealed ampoules and air-free handling remain necessary. The route is less severe than direct vacuum elemental synthesis but more operationally complex than a simple solid-state batch [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 5 halide flux

Flux growth improves ion transport and crystal growth, as seen for KInTe2 and KYTe2 single crystals (CS-4; CS-5). However, KYTe2 was obtained as an unintended by-product, so the route has phase-selectivity risk (CS-5). For K2SnTe3, flux may be best for exploratory single crystals rather than high-yield phase-pure powder [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a strong size/coordination contrast between large K coordination and small tetrahedral Sn coordination [Reasonable Inference], triggering C1. The target structure description does not provide evidence for >40 atoms per cell, and it does not state coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing; therefore C2 and C3 are not triggered. The amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K2SnTe3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close K/Cs/Rb telluride analogues are also on the hull (CS-1; CS-2; SS-1; TS-1; SS-5; TS-5). The analogue support is strong: CS provides K–Te ternary synthesis precedent, while SS/TS provide SnTe4-containing alkali stannate telluride precedents. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive alkali–telluride chemistry, especially via acetate decomposition or K2Te precursors (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The main limiting factor is phase control among competing K–Sn–Te tellurides and polytellurides, as low yield or by-product formation is reported for several related systems (CS-5; SS-5; TS-5). Overall, the material appears realistically synthesizable, with Route 3 the best balance of reactivity, temperature, and practical handling.
