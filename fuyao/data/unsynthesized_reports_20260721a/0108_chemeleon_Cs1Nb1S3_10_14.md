### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. Chemically similar sulfides and halides include several E_hull = 0.0 materials, such as Cs₂MoS₄, Rb₂WS₄, Rb₂MoS₄, and CsGaS₃ (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Cs/Rb–Nb/Ta sulfides are also hull-stable (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5), so thermodynamics strongly supports synthesizability.

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 350–500 °C, consistent with Cs₂LiNbS₄ and Rb₂NbAgS₄ flux analogues (SS1, TS1, SS5, TS5). The penalties come from vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, glovebox/air-sensitive sulfide handling, and multistep flux work, all present in the analogous flux syntheses (SS1, TS1). There is insufficient evidence from CS for exact CsNbS₃ flux conditions, so CS contributes only indirect chemical support.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Flux chemistry should improve transport relative to direct solid-state reaction, as Cs₂LiNbS₄ was synthesized far below the melting point of Nb using alkali sulfide flux (SS1, TS1). However, target CsNbS₃ contains distorted edge-sharing NbS₅ trigonal bipyramids, whereas the closest Nb analogue has NbS₄ tetrahedra (SS1, TS1), so structural reconstruction remains moderate. The main uncertainty is whether eliminating Li from the flux analogue destabilizes the desired framework [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
Route 2 uses sealed-tube conditions comparable to CS5 and SS2/TS2, without extreme pressure or >1000 °C temperatures. Severity arises from vacuum sealing, sulfur vapor pressure, air-sensitive Cs sulfides, and prolonged heating/slow cooling (CS5, SS2, TS2). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a direct binary-sulfide synthesis of CsNbS₃, so the route is less directly supported.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Binary sulfides are chemically closer than oxides [Reasonable Inference], but solid-state cation diffusion and ordering around refractory Nb are likely slower than in a true liquid flux [Reasonable Inference]. Analogous sealed-tube preparations require long holds or slow cooling, indicating nontrivial kinetic demands (CS5, SS2, TS2). The lack of a direct ternary CsNbS₃ analogue increases uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
Route 3 benefits from low temperatures demonstrated by Cs₂MoS₄ hydrothermal synthesis at 423 K and Rb₂MoS₄ solvothermal synthesis at 393 K (CS1, CS4). It avoids high-temperature vacuum tubes, but still requires sealed autoclaves/ampoules, sulfur/polysulfide chemistry, and potentially toxic or air-sensitive reagents such as ethylenediamine or NbCl₅ [Reasonable Inference; CS4]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for solvothermal NbS₅ framework formation, so they are neutral for this route’s condition severity.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Liquid-mediated synthesis gives favorable kinetics, supported by low-temperature crystallization of Cs₂MoS₄ and Rb₂MoS₄ (CS1, CS4). The limitation is structural transferability: the CS analogues are primarily tetrahedral thiometallates, while target CsNbS₃ has edge-sharing NbS₅ units. CS4 also reports Rb₂MoS₄ as a minor phase, so phase selectivity is a significant uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 4 severity.**  
Route 4 is based on the evacuated quartz-tube CsGaS₃ synthesis at 780 °C for four days with slow cooling (CS5). It is not extreme in temperature or pressure, but vacuum sealing and sulfur volatility add severity (CS5). SS/TS provide only general alkali sulfide sealed-tube support, not carbonate-based Nb sulfide evidence, so their signal is limited.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
A sulfur-rich melt/flux can assist crystallization, as CS5 produced crystalline CsGaS₃ under sealed-tube conditions. However, Nb sulfide framework formation differs from GaS₄ tetrahedral chemistry, so precursor conversion and structural reconstruction are more uncertain (CS5; target description). Carbonate decomposition and oxygen contamination are additional risks [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines very large Cs coordination with much shorter Nb–S bonding, implying a large cation size contrast [Reasonable Inference supported by target and SS1/TS1 bond-distance patterns]. The target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face mixed connectivity; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for those checks, scored neutral 50. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

The strongest direct support comes from Cs₂LiNbS₄, which contains Cs–Nb–S chemistry and was synthesized by alkali sulfide flux methods (SS1, TS1). The most enabling factor is the availability of reactive alkali polysulfide fluxes that can mobilize refractory Nb at moderate temperature (SS1, TS1, SS5, TS5). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Nb/Ta analogues show lower yields and some failed compositions (SS1, TS1), and solvothermal Mo analogues can appear only as minor phases (CS4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful control of sulfur chemical potential, Cs activity, and cooling rate.
