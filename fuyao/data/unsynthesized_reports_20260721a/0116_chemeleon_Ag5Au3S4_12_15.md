### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range and justifying a score of 98. CS provides supportive but not exact evidence: several chemically similar chalcogenides have E_hull = 0.0 or near-zero, including TlAgSe/TlAgS-type and Ni/Bi/S analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4), while CS has insufficient evidence for the exact Ag–Au–S phase. SS/TS provide stronger relevant support because stable Au–S analogues such as K2Cd(AuS)4, Au4Tl2S3, and LiAuS also lie on the hull (SS-3/TS-1, SS-4/TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is mostly kinetic/phase-selection rather than thermodynamic.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to successful sealed-tube sulfide syntheses: 570 K for TlAgSe/TlAgS-type materials (CS-1), ~550 K for Ag3CuS2 (SS-5), and 650 °C for Au4Tl2S3 (SS-4/TS-2). Deductions are assigned for vacuum and sulfur volatility because the analogues use evacuated sealed tubes with sulfur/chalcogen components (CS-1, SS-5, TS-2). CS, SS, and TS do not indicate a need for high pressure for this specific Ag–Au–S target, so high-pressure penalties are not applied. Uncertainty remains because exact Ag5Au3S4 is not reported in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfides and fine metal powders should be moderately active, supported by Ag2S/Cu2S synthesis of Ag3CuS2 and elemental syntheses of chalcogenides (SS-5, TS-3). Diffusion is manageable because multiple analogues form at 550–650 °C in sealed tubes (SS-5, SS-4/TS-2), though Au metal may react sluggishly [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is the limiting kinetic component because the target has ordered inequivalent Au and Ag sites in triclinic P1, while related Ag sulfides required long annealing or mechanical alloying for high purity (SS-5). CS provides insufficient direct structural reconstruction evidence for Ag–Au ordering, so that component is partly extrapolated.

#### F2 — Route 2

The flux route uses 500–550 °C conditions similar to K2Cd(AuS)4 and LiAuS/NaAuS flux syntheses (SS-3/TS-1, TS-5), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Penalties are assigned for vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, multi-step flux preparation/removal, and air-sensitive alkali sulfides because TS-5 explicitly uses evacuated Pyrex tubes, glovebox/Schlenk handling, and N2 isolation. SS/TS do not indicate high external pressure for alkali sulfide flux growth, so no pressure penalty is applied. The main uncertainty is whether alkali ions can be fully excluded from the final Ag–Au–S product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives a high kinetic score because A2Sx fluxes dissolve and transport Au–S species at moderate temperature (SS-3/TS-1, TS-5). Au–S bond distances in flux-grown analogues, ~2.32–2.34 Å, closely match target Au–S distances of 2.30–2.32 Å, supporting structural transferability (SS-3/TS-1, TS-5, target structure). The structural reconstruction score is not maximal because the target requires mixed Ag/Au sulfide tetrahedra, while the closest flux analogues include alkali/Cd/Tl components rather than Ag5Au3S4 (SS-3/TS-1, SS-4/TS-2, TS-5). CS gives insufficient direct support for flux growth of Ag–Au–S and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3

Melt/Bridgman synthesis is supported by Ag3SI growth at ~700 °C in a sealed quartz ampoule (SS-1) and by controlled cooling of Ag8SnSe6 from high temperature (TS-3). The route is penalized for vacuum, sulfur volatility, and multistep crystallization/gradient cooling. It avoids high external pressure and very high temperatures in the proposed program. The uncertainty is sulfur vapor pressure and possible incongruent melting of Ag–Au–S, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Liquid or partially molten growth should give favorable mass transport, as shown by Bridgman growth of Ag3SI (SS-1). However, SS-1 also reports that crystals were separated from a mixture of phases, so phase selection is a real concern. TS-3 supports controlled cooling/annealing for Ag chalcogenides, but it also shows that long anneals may be needed for the desired modification. Structural reconstruction is therefore scored moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 4

Mechanochemical synthesis plus annealing avoids high temperatures and high pressure, as SS-5 reports Ag3CuS2 production through mechanical alloying and moderate-temperature annealing. Penalties are assigned for inert/vacuum handling, sulfur volatility, and multi-step processing. No toxic precursor penalty is used because Ag, Au, S, and Ag2S are not identified as toxic/air-sensitive in CS/SS/TS. The key uncertainty is whether Au reacts sufficiently under low-temperature annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

SS-5 provides strong evidence that mechanical alloying can improve phase purity in an Ag-rich sulfide. Milling should improve precursor activity and shorten diffusion length [Reasonable Inference from SS-5]. The score is limited by likely slow ordering of Ag and Au over multiple inequivalent sites in triclinic P1, and by SS-5 evidence that long processing was required for high-purity Ag3CuS2. CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for mechanochemical Ag–Au–S synthesis and are treated as neutral here.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has no evidence for a large unit cell or mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral connectivity; the target description reports corner-sharing tetrahedra only. Ag and Au are chemically similar coinage metals, so large cation-size mismatch is unlikely [Reasonable Inference]. SS contains some edge/face-sharing analogues such as Ag3SI and Ag3CuS2 (SS-1, SS-5), but those structural features do not appear in the target description. Remaining uncertainty comes from the low-symmetry P1 ordering, which may broaden diffraction peaks or lead to disorder, but it does not strongly imply amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state phase because the provided target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom. CS, SS, and TS collectively support synthesis of related Ag chalcogenides, Au sulfides, and mixed-metal sulfides under sealed-tube, flux, and melt-growth conditions, although no source gives direct experimental precedent for Ag5Au3S4. The strongest enabling factor is the close match of target Au–S bond lengths to stable Au–S analogues such as K2Cd(AuS)4, Au4Tl2S3, and LiAuS (SS-3/TS-1, SS-4/TS-2, TS-5). The main limiting factor is kinetic phase selection: ordered triclinic Ag/Au distribution may compete with Ag2S, Au–S phases, and metallic Au/Ag [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears favorable, with sealed-tube solid-state reaction as the best first attempt and flux growth as a strong backup for crystals.
