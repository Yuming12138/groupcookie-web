### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. This is reinforced by chemically similar K–M–Se phases with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1–CS-5) and structurally/thermodynamically similar V/Nb chalcogenide phases with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Direct sealed-ampoule routes require inert/vacuum handling and sealed quartz/silica ampoules, as shown for K3GaSe3, K3SbSe4, K3GeSe3, and K3SnSe3 analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). Temperatures are moderate for solid-state chemistry, ~650–900 °C, and no high pressure is reported (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The severity score is reduced mainly by K/Se volatility, air sensitivity, vacuum sealing, and multistep ampoule handling.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct elemental synthesis benefits from reactive K and Se, and CS examples show K–M–Se compounds can form from elements (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). However, V metal is refractory and may limit reaction kinetics [Reasonable Inference], and CS data give insufficient evidence for direct formation of K–V–Se without flux. The close VSe4 local geometry in SS/TS analogues supports the desired coordination but not direct-route kinetics (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Reactive flux growth avoids >1000 °C temperatures because K2VAgSe4/Rb2VAgSe4 analogues crystallize at 350 °C for 6 days (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). Severity remains moderate because the route requires glovebox handling, pre-synthesized alkali polychalcogenide flux, evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, and potentially dry solvent washing (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). No high pressure is needed in the SS/TS flux examples.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because alkali-polyselenide fluxes provide high selenium activity and liquid/flux-mediated transport, as demonstrated by V-containing K/Rb selenide analogues at 350 °C (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The VSe4 tetrahedron is nearly identical in bond length to the target, 2.32 Å versus 2.30 Å, supporting local structural compatibility (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The uncertainty is that K3VSe4 removes the Ag sublattice present in K2VAgSe4, so stoichiometry and charge balance must be redirected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Gas selenization uses 750–850 °C and H2 saturated with Se, based on K3AsSe3 and RbK2BiSe3 analogues (CS-2, SS-3, TS-3). It avoids sealed high-pressure ampoules, but the H2/Se atmosphere is hazardous and controlled. The route is scored less severe than sealed elemental synthesis in equipment terms, but more chemically uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Oxide/carbonate precursors are reasonably reactive under H2/Se in As/Bi analogues (CS-2, SS-3, TS-3). However, no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates conversion of vanadium oxide plus K carbonate into a K–V–Se selenovanadate, so V redox and oxide removal are uncertain. Structural reconstruction from oxide/carbonate precursors into K3VSe4 is likely substantial [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: large cation-size mismatch between K and tetrahedral V [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient quantitative ionic-radius evidence. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing only, not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity high-risk criterion is not fully met. The likely small formula-unit complexity also argues against severe glass-forming tendency, but the exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K3VSe4 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar K/Rb/Cs selenides are also hull-stable (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from the SS/TS analogues K2VAgSe4 and Rb2VAgSe4, which contain tetrahedral VSe4 units with V–Se distances nearly identical to the target (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the use of reactive alkali-polyselenide flux to overcome sluggish V diffusion and stabilize VSe4 tetrahedra at moderate temperature (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: removing Ag from the known K2VAgSe4 framework and reaching K3VSe4 may favor competing K–Se or V–Se phases unless K/Se activity is tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears moderately likely to be synthesizable, with flux growth preferred over direct elemental firing.
