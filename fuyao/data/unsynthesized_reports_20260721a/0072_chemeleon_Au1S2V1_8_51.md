### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. Multiple similar sulfides are also hull-stable, including TaInS2 and Nb3(BiS3)2 in CS, V3Ag2TeS6 and VS2 in SS, and the same V3Ag2TeS6/VS2 plus GaV4S8/Nb2PdS5 in TS. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or the absence of Au/V/S off-stoichiometric competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate sealed-tube solid-state conditions supported by CS1 and CS3, with no high pressure and temperatures generally below 1000 °C. The severity deductions come from vacuum encapsulation, volatile sulfur, and likely repeated annealing/grinding [Reasonable Inference from TS3/TS4 and SS4/SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether Au specifically causes ampoule corrosion or unusual vapor pressure behavior, so that was not over-penalized.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from using VS2 as a preorganized sulfide precursor, analogous to TaS2 use in CS1. The main limitation is dense Au metal diffusion and possible segregation [Reasonable Inference], while the structural reconstruction is small because CS1 has the same P-6m2 connectivity. Long annealing durations in CS1/CS3 reduce confidence slightly.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar sealed-tube/vacuum penalties to Route 1 because CS2 uses evacuated quartz tubes and a temperature gradient. It avoids very high temperature and high pressure, but it likely requires multiple operations: VS2 preparation, Au intercalation, sulfur-buffered annealing, and cooling [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 provides useful evidence for intercalation conditions but also flags phase-control difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best kinetic score because VS2 already contains the target-like V–S network, with V–S = 2.36 Å in SS2/TS2 versus 2.35 Å in the target. CS2 supports cation intercalation into TaS2-type layered sulfides, suggesting Au incorporation may occur without complete framework reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Au orders into a stoichiometric linear site rather than forming nonstoichiometric or segregated phases; CS2 explicitly notes nonstoichiometry and homogeneous-product issues.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because CVT requires halogen transport agents and a temperature gradient, as in CS4, SS3, and TS5. The halogens and sulfur are volatile/toxic enough to require sealed-ampoule handling, and the method is at least two-stage if powder is prepared first. No high pressure is required in the analogue CVT syntheses.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT improves mass transport by forming volatile species, which is supported by successful SnTaS2, V–Cu–S, and Nb–Fe–S crystal growth in CS4, SS3, and TS5. However, crystallization of the exact VAuS2 phase depends on stable Au–S–halogen transport chemistry, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence. This route is therefore better as a crystal-growth optimization after Route 1 or Route 2 establishes the phase.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 has low thermal severity because SS1/TS1 use only 350 °C, but it requires air-sensitive alkali flux, sealed tubes, inert extraction, and solvent handling. These requirements justify deductions for controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed components, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive reagents. No high pressure or >1000 °C step is involved.

#### F3 — Route 4

The flux route has favorable kinetics because mixed-polychalcogenide flux enables low-temperature crystallization of a V-containing sulfide analogue (SS1/TS1). The limitation is structural selectivity: SS1/TS1 report polymorphs, positional disorder, and secondary phases, which lower confidence that the flux will isolate simple VAuS2 rather than more complex Au/V/S or alkali-containing phases. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Au-only VAuS2 flux stability.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small formula and a relatively simple P-6m2 ordered structure, so amorphization risk from cell complexity is low [Reasonable Inference]. One risk trigger is the large difference in cation size/coordination between six-coordinate V and linearly coordinated Au, which may promote local disorder [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The target lacks reported simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the connectivity-based amorphization risk is not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VAuS2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS1, where TaInS2 has the same P-6m2 structural motif and was synthesized by a standard sealed-tube route, and from SS2/TS2, where VS2 provides a stable, closely matched V–S framework. The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing layered/edge-sharing VS2 sulfide scaffold, which should reduce the structural reconstruction needed for Au incorporation [Reasonable Inference from CS2 and SS2/TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometric Au ordering into the linear sulfur-coordinated site rather than forming metallic Au, binary Au sulfides, or nonstoichiometric intercalates [Reasonable Inference; CS2 reports nonstoichiometry/phase-window issues]. Overall, the target appears reasonably synthesizable, with the best strategy being VS2-based intercalation/annealing followed by sealed-tube optimization.
