### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 range by the required rubric. Similar K–Ag–S and alkali–Ag–chalcogenide analogues are also on or very near the hull: K2Ag4S3, CsAg3S2, RbAg3Se2, and CsAg3Se2 are E_hull = 0.0, while cubic KAg3S2 is E_hull ≈ 0.009 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection: the data support the composition strongly, but the target trigonal polymorph must compete with known cubic/monoclinic structure types (CS-1 to CS-3; SS-1 to SS-3; TS-1 to TS-3).

**F2 — Route 1 flux/fusion severity.**  
The best-supported route uses 850 °C and pure Ar in a corundum crucible, which is moderate but not extreme and does not require high pressure (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). The main severity deduction is for multiple processing steps, because analogous routes involve mixing, heating, cooling, washing, and drying (CS-2, CS-5; TS-1, TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that sealed ampoules are required for this route, so the volatile-sealed-system criterion is not triggered and would be neutral 50 if evaluated solely from absent evidence.

**F3 — Route 1 flux/fusion kinetics.**  
K2CO3/S flux chemistry gives good chemical activity, but Ag metal is dense and can limit reaction rate, so precursor activity is not scored at the nitrate/sol-gel level (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). The fact that a related K–Ag–S phase forms in only 4 h at 850 °C supports favorable diffusion under flux/fusion conditions (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target shares K–S/Ag–S motifs with analogues, but not the same space group (CS-1 to CS-3; SS-1 to SS-3).

**F2 — Route 2 supercritical ethylenediamine severity.**  
The temperature is mild at 300 °C, but the reported route requires a vacuum-sealed quartz tube, high-pressure autoclave, Ar counterpressure to 2400 psi, and six days (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3). The rubric does not penalize 2400 psi as “>5 GPa,” but it does penalize sealed/controlled atmosphere, volatile sealed reagents, and multi-step handling. Ethylenediamine hazard and the inferred use of K2S instead of Na2S add practical difficulty [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 supercritical ethylenediamine kinetics.**  
This route has strong kinetic support because the exact-composition KAg3S2 analogue forms under supercritical ethylenediamine, and subcritical reactions gave amorphous products, indicating the solvent state is enabling (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3). Solvothermal transport reduces long-range solid-state diffusion limitations, but the six-day duration shows kinetics are not trivial (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3). The largest uncertainty is polymorph control because the reported exact-composition phase is cubic rather than trigonal (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3).

**F2 — Route 3 sealed-ampoule solid-state severity.**  
The proposed 700–850 °C window is not extreme and is consistent with related K–Ag–S synthesis temperatures (CS-2; SS-2; TS-1). However, sealed vacuum/inert ampoule handling, sulfur vapor pressure, air-sensitive K2S, and repeated grinding/annealing justify substantial deductions; the sealed-ampoule/regrinding precedent comes from structurally similar Ag3SI synthesis (SS-5). Insufficient direct CS/TS evidence exists for KAg3S2 from binary sulfides, so this route carries more extrapolation.

**F3 — Route 3 sealed-ampoule solid-state kinetics.**  
Binary sulfides are chemically closer to the target than oxides or carbonates, but they are solid, relatively low-activity precursors compared with flux/solvothermal media [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar Ag3SI solid-state synthesis needed grinding, sealing, high-temperature treatment, regrinding/pelletizing, and prolonged annealing for phase control, indicating possible slow ordering kinetics (SS-5). The absence of direct CS/TS evidence for this exact route makes the kinetic score conservative.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large alkali/coinage cation size and coordination mismatch, as reflected in long K–S distances and short linear Ag–S distances; analogous structures also separate alkali–S and Ag–S coordination environments (CS-1 to CS-3; SS-1 to SS-3). The target formula unit is small, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell or coexistence of all vertex/edge/face-sharing modes, so only one amorphization trigger is counted. The main uncertainty is that the supercritical analogue explicitly reports amorphous products under subcritical conditions, which suggests kinetic amorphization is possible if transport conditions are poor (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KAg3S2 is likely thermodynamically accessible because the target is computed exactly on the convex hull, and several K/Rb/Cs–Ag–S/Se analogues are also stable or nearly stable (CS-1 to CS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest support comes from the existence of an exact-composition KAg3S2 analogue plus multiple closely related alkali silver sulfides synthesized by flux/fusion or supercritical routes (CS-1 to CS-3; SS-1 to SS-3; TS-1 to TS-3). The most enabling factor is the soft chalcogenide chemistry: Ag–S coordination networks and alkali–S environments form readily under sulfur-rich flux or supercritical sulfide conditions (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). The main limiting factor is polymorph control, because the provided exact-composition KAg3S2 reference is cubic Fd-3m, whereas the target is trigonal P-3m1 (CS-1; SS-1; TS-3). A secondary limitation is air/moisture sensitivity and sulfur volatility, both suggested by related alkali silver chalcogenide syntheses requiring inert atmospheres, washing/drying, and careful crystal isolation (CS-2 to CS-5; TS-1, TS-4, TS-5).
