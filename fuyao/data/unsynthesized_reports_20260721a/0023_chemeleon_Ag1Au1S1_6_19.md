### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the calculated convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, which strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues Tl₂Au₄S₃, K₂Cd(AuS)₄, and LiAuS are also reported with E_hull = 0 and successful syntheses (TS1, TS2, TS5). Chemically similar chalcogenides include several stable or near-stable phases, including CS1, CS2, CS4, and CS5. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee the specific monoclinic Pc polymorph will be the experimentally selected polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, 500–650 °C, consistent with sealed-tube Ag/Tl and Au sulfide analogues (CS1, SS1/TS1). The major deductions are for vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur, both common in the cited chalcogenide syntheses (CS1, CS3, SS4). No high pressure is needed because the closest Au–S analogues are made in sealed tubes rather than belt/DAC systems (SS1/TS1, TS5). Uncertainty remains in whether dry solid-state Au diffusion is sufficient without flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Ag₂S is a plausible reactive precursor because Ag₂S is used in Ag₃SI and Ag₃CuS₂ syntheses (SS3, SS4). Au metal, however, can be kinetically sluggish as a dense noble metal [Reasonable Inference], so the precursor activity is only moderate. Structurally, the target’s Au–S bond length matches known Au sulfides well, but ordering Ag and Au into the Pc structure may require long annealing; long times or mechanical alloying were needed for high-purity Ag₃CuS₂ in SS4. This gives a moderate kinetic score rather than an easy one.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Flux growth proceeds at only 500–650 °C in the closest Au–S analogues, which is mild in temperature (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, TS5). The difficulty comes from evacuated sealed tubes, controlled cooling, air-sensitive alkali sulfides, and flux removal under inert atmosphere, all described or implied in SS2/TS2 and TS5. Use of Tl₂S would add toxicity, as Tl sulfide flux chemistry is used in SS1/TS1, so a Li/Na/K sulfide flux is preferred [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct flux-growth evidence for AgAuS and is treated as neutral = 50 for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth has the best kinetic outlook because alkali sulfide/polysulfide fluxes successfully form Au–S frameworks at 500–550 °C, well below the melting point of Au (SS2/TS2, TS5). Au–S distances in those analogues, 2.32–2.34 Å, match the target Au–S distance of 2.32 Å, supporting structural transferability (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, TS5). The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether Ag and Au will order into AgAuS rather than forming Ag₂S, Au sulfide motifs, or flux-stabilized quaternary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The melt/Bridgman route is supported most directly by Ag₃SI growth near 700 °C in a sealed quartz ampoule (SS3). It requires vacuum sealing, handling sulfur vapor pressure, and a thermal gradient or slow pulling step, giving more procedural complexity than Route 1. No high pressure or highly toxic flux is needed if using Ag₂S/Au/S [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct Bridgman evidence for AgAuS and are neutral = 50 for this route factor.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Melt-assisted growth can reduce diffusion barriers relative to dry powder reaction, as shown by Bridgman growth of Ag₃SI from Ag₂S/AgI (SS3). However, SS3 also reports phase mixtures from which crystals are separated, which indicates a risk of multiphase crystallization. For AgAuS, the melt route may help single-crystal formation but may not be the easiest way to obtain phase-pure bulk powder [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple AgAuS composition and the structural description indicates corner-sharing SAg₂Au₂ tetrahedra, so the atom-count and mixed-connectivity triggers are not activated by the provided structure. A single cation-size/coordination mismatch trigger is retained because Ag and Au can have distinct bonding preferences despite both being chalcophile noble metals [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization evidence for this target and are neutral = 50 for F4. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgAuS is likely a ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, and several Au–S thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull and experimentally accessible (TS1, TS2, TS5). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: Au–S frameworks with matching Au–S distances are made by sealed-tube flux routes (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, TS5), while Ag sulfides are accessible by evacuated sealed-tube and melt methods (SS3, SS4, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the excellent Au–S bond-length compatibility, with target Au–S = 2.32 Å matching 2.32–2.34 Å in known Au sulfides (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, TS5). The main limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: Ag₂S, metallic Au, and possible Au–S or Ag–S competing phases may coexist unless sulfur activity and annealing are well controlled [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target should be experimentally plausible, with sealed-tube solid-state synthesis as the simplest first attempt and flux growth as the best route if crystallinity or larger crystals are needed.
