### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 range and justifies a score of 100. The closest thermodynamic analogue is also K₃GaS₃ with E_hull = 0.0, and it appears as the top CS, SS, and TS hit (CS/SS/TS rank 1). This strongly suggests the composition is not merely metastable relative to competing K–Ga–S phases, although the target C2/m polymorph may still compete with the known P2₁/c polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the exact reported direct elemental synthesis of K₃GaS₃ at 1025 K in evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules (CS/SS/TS rank 1). The main severity deductions are from vacuum sealing, volatile K/S, and air-sensitive K metal [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence for high pressure or >1000 °C synthesis in CS/SS/TS, so those penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because the direct elemental reaction already gives the same formula K₃GaS₃ (CS/SS/TS rank 1). K, Ga, and S are expected to be highly reactive and partly molten/volatile at the synthesis temperature, improving mixing and transport [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is polymorph selection: the reference is P2₁/c while the target is C2/m, so cooling rate and annealing temperature may determine which polymorph forms [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids elemental K but uses air-sensitive sulfides and sealed ampoule handling [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature severity is modest because related alkali sulfide sintering occurs at 783–875 K and K₂TiS₃ is synthesized at 1070 K, all below 1000 °C (CS rank 4; SS/TS ranks 3–5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for high-pressure requirements, so high pressure is neutral and no deduction is taken.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfides should provide chemically preorganized K–S and Ga–S bonding, but solid-state diffusion through K₂S/Ga₂S₃ is less activated than direct molten-element chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Related alkali sulfides require multi-day anneals or repeated cycles, indicating moderate ordering kinetics rather than rapid reaction (SS/TS ranks 4–5). The uncertainty is that no provided CS/SS/TS entry directly reports K₃GaS₃ synthesis from K₂S + Ga₂S₃.

#### F2 — Route 3

The azide route has the mildest temperature but the most operational complexity. Related Cs₄In₂S₅ synthesis uses vacuum/inert gas, azide decomposition, sealed quartz ampoules, long annealing, and sometimes additional homogenization (SS/TS rank 2). The deductions therefore come from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile/gas-evolving chemistry, multistep processing, and hazardous/air-sensitive precursors.

#### F3 — Route 3

Azide decomposition creates reactive alkali species, which can help form alkali sulfides at relatively low temperature (SS/TS rank 2). However, the structurally similar azide example is not K₃GaS₃ and not the same structure, so structural reconstruction is more substantial than for Route 1 (SS/TS rank 2 vs CS/SS/TS rank 1). The long annealing durations in the azide route imply kinetic limitations despite high precursor activity (SS/TS rank 2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation environments—large, high-coordinate K–S sites and tetrahedral GaS₄—which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination contrast criterion [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The target also has mixed edge/corner/face-sharing connectivity in S-centered polyhedra and edge/corner sharing between K and Ga polyhedra, triggering the mixed-connectivity criterion (target description). The unit cell atom count is not provided, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as neutral rather than triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₃GaS₃ is very likely synthesizable, and the target is thermodynamically credible because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest TS analogue is also a stable K₃GaS₃ phase with E_hull = 0.0 (TS rank 1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually strong because the top analogue has identical formula and near-identical local bonding, though it is P2₁/c rather than the target C2/m structure (CS/SS/TS rank 1). The most critical enabling factor is the already demonstrated direct elemental synthesis of K₃GaS₃ in evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules (CS/SS/TS rank 1). The main limiting factor is polymorph and phase control between the known P2₁/c K₃GaS₃ and the target C2/m K₃GaS₃ [Reasonable Inference]. Secondary limitations are K/S volatility, alkali-metal air sensitivity, and possible glass/ampoule reactions during high-temperature sealed synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by the need for graphitized ampoules in CS/SS/TS rank 1].
