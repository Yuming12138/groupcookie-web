### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Several thermodynamically similar sulfides are also reported at E_hull = 0, including CuS, Cu₁₂Sb₄S₁₃, Cu₂GeS₃, Cu₂ZnGeS₄, and Cu₂HgGeS₄ (TS). This supports the interpretation that Cu–sulfide/Ga–sulfide chemistry can form ground-state crystalline phases. Remaining uncertainty is mainly computational/experimental: hull stability does not guarantee rapid cation ordering or absence of competing polymorphs.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct elemental solid-state reaction

The condition severity is moderate because direct element routes require evacuated sealed ampoules, staged heating, and long annealing, as shown for Cu₂GeS₃, CuS, Cu₂CoGeS₄, and Cu₂ZnGeS₄ analogues (CS, SS, TS). The temperature range is not extreme, since comparable materials form at 620 K to 900 °C without high pressure (CS, SS, TS). Kinetically, elemental Cu and Ga/S must reconstruct into a tetrahedral ternary sulfide lattice, and long anneals in Cu₂GeS₃ and related analogues suggest slow ordering (CS, SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether Ga reacts cleanly with Cu/S without forming persistent Cu₂S or Ga₂S₃ secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary sulfide solid-state sintering

This route has somewhat harsher precursor-handling requirements because Ga₂S₃ should be handled dry and sulfur requires sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference], while CS explicitly supports Ga₂S₃-based sulfide sintering for ZnGa₂S₄. Kinetics are better than for elements because Cu–S and Ga–S bonds are preformed, and the target’s CuS₄/GaS₄ tetrahedral framework is closely related to GaS₄ networks in ZnGa₂S₄ and CuS₄ networks in Cu–Ge–S analogues (CS, SS, TS). Repeated firing remains likely because CS ZnGa₂S₄ required long firing periods and SS/TS analogues often use homogenization. The uncertainty is phase competition among Cu sulfides, Ga₂S₃, and possible Cu–Ga–S polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Chemical vapor transport

CVT is well supported for similar Cu sulfides using iodine, including Cu₄NiGe₂S₇ and Cu₂HgGeS₄ (SS, TS). Its condition severity is moderate-to-challenging because it requires evacuated ampoules, toxic/corrosive I₂, a two-zone temperature gradient, and pre-synthesized feed (SS, TS). Kinetically, vapor transport can grow crystals efficiently once transport equilibria are established, but multication composition control is less certain; TS reports polymorph/homogeneity complications for Cu₂HgGeS₄. There is insufficient evidence from CS for Cu–Ga–S CVT specifically, so transferability relies on [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Solvothermal synthesis

The solvothermal route benefits from mild temperature and highly reactive chloride/amine chemistry, as shown for Cu₂CoSnS₄ at 200 °C under nitrogen in diethylenetriamine (SS). However, SS also states phase/nucleation control is difficult for quaternary sulfide nanowires, and no direct GaCu₃S₄ solvothermal analogue is provided. The CS hydrothermal entry is not chemically useful for Cu–Ga–S because its evidence concerns unrelated borate/oxide precursors, so it is treated as insufficient evidence from CS and neutral for this route. This route may be useful for nanocrystalline material but is less reliable for phase-pure bulk Cu₃GaS₄ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a relatively simple corner-sharing tetrahedral sulfide, and the provided target structure does not indicate edge- or face-sharing polyhedral mixtures. Only the Cu/Ga tetrahedral cation radius mismatch criterion is triggered [Reasonable Inference], giving low–medium amorphization risk. Structurally similar materials with corner-sharing tetrahedral CuS₄/MS₄ frameworks are crystalline and synthesizable by solid-state, CVT, or solvothermal methods (CS, SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is cation disorder between Cu and Ga sites at high temperature [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃GaS₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Cu sulfides are also hull-stable (TS). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support sealed-ampoule sulfide synthesis: Ga₂S₃-based sulfide sintering is demonstrated for ZnGa₂S₄ (CS), while Cu-rich sulfides and tetrahedral Cu–M–S frameworks are repeatedly synthesized in evacuated ampoules (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the structural match to corner-sharing CuS₄/GaS₄ tetrahedral networks, which are common in the cited analogues (CS, SS, TS). The main limiting factor is phase/composition control under sulfur vapor pressure, especially avoiding Cu sulfide or Ga sulfide impurities during long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, bulk synthesis appears feasible, with binary sulfide sintering preferred over solution routes for phase-pure crystalline material.
