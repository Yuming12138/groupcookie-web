### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target RbTlS has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the specified ≤0.05 eV/atom range (target data). Multiple chemically similar Rb/Cs/K gold sulfide/selenide analogues are also on the hull, including RbAuS, CsAuS, KAuS, Rb4Au6S5, and RbAuSe (CS). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Rb chalcogenides and pnictides are also mostly on the hull, supporting the idea that Rb-containing polar chalcogenide frameworks can be stable ground states (SS/TS). Remaining uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS contains Tl in the exact Rb–Tl–S composition, so Tl-specific competing phases are not directly benchmarked; for that Tl-specific aspect, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence and are neutral, score 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 600 °C and ordinary sealed-ampoule pressure, matching successful RbAuS/CsAuS synthesis by elemental fusion (CS). The main deductions are for a volatile sulfur-containing sealed system and hazardous Rb/Tl handling [Reasonable Inference], while inert ampoule handling is directly supported by CS and SS/TS. No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is needed, so the condition severity is moderate rather than severe (CS/SS/TS). Remaining uncertainty is ampoule compatibility with Tl/S/Rb melts, which is not directly addressed by the provided sources.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental fusion should provide good mass transport because Rb and Tl are low-melting and sulfur is reactive [Reasonable Inference], and chemically similar RbAuS/CsAuS form by fusion at 600 °C (CS). Diffusion is expected to be favorable relative to refractory oxide synthesis, especially because even Au-containing analogues crystallize at 600–750 °C by melt/fusion pathways (CS). Structural reconstruction is the weaker kinetic term because the closest chemical analogues are orthorhombic Cmcm RbAuS/CsAuS rather than triclinic P-1 RbTlS (CS + target data). Thus the kinetics are good but not trivial.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from relatively low annealing temperatures, since Rb6HgS4-type analogues form at 360–380 °C for 7–10 days in Ar-sealed ampoules (SS/TS). However, the route incurs deductions for sealed/vacuum or rigorously controlled atmosphere, multi-step precursor preparation, and air-sensitive/toxic sulfide handling (CS/SS/TS + [Reasonable Inference]). The lower thermal severity is offset by practical handling complexity. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Tl2S + Rb2S conversion to RbTlS, so the Tl-specific compatibility signal is neutral, score 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfides provide preformed Rb–S and Tl–S bonding motifs, which should help avoid violent elemental Rb–S reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Rb2S-based syntheses of Rb thioaurates and Rb6HgS4 analogues show that binary sulfide routes are feasible for Rb sulfide frameworks (CS/SS/TS). The kinetic penalty is that solid-state annealing at 350–450 °C may require long diffusion times, consistent with 7–10 day anneals in the Hg analogues (SS/TS). The target structure is not isostructural with those analogues, so structural rearrangement remains a moderate limitation.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures, slow cooling, and no high pressure, consistent with melt/flux-like alkali chalcogenide synthesis in the analogue set (CS/SS/TS). Deductions arise from controlled sealed-tube conditions, volatile sulfur or sulfide flux, multi-step growth/annealing/flux separation, and hazardous Rb/Tl chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed ampoule and inert handling in CS/SS/TS]. This makes Route 3 more complex than Route 1 despite similar temperatures. It is better suited for single-crystal growth than rapid phase-pure powder synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux or self-flux growth improves kinetics by increasing ion mobility and enabling slow crystallization, as suggested by RbAuS/CsAuS and KAuS slow-cooling/tempering protocols (CS). The diffusion score is high because a melt or flux can bypass long-range solid-state diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference], and analogues crystallize from melt/fusion conditions (CS/SS/TS). The structural reconstruction score is moderate because no exact RbTlS structure analogue is provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact isostructural growth, neutral-to-moderate signal. The route may be kinetically favorable but compositionally less controlled.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Rb⁺ and Tl⁺, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference], but the reported target description contains only one crystallographic Rb, Tl, and S site, so there is no evidence for a >40 atom cell (target data). The target coordination description does not explicitly state mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so C3 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target connectivity is neutral, score 50. Related CS/SS/TS analogues often show crystalline products after slow cooling or annealing, which reduces concern about glass formation in Rb chalcogenide chemistry (CS/SS/TS). Overall amorphization risk is low-to-medium.

RbTlS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data).
