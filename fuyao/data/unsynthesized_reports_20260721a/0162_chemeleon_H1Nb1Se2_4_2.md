### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E\(_\text{hull}\)=0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 range by the required rubric. This is reinforced by stable hydride selenide CeHSe in CS-4 and stable Nb–Se layered/related phases in SS-1/SS-2 and TS-1/TS-3. The main uncertainty is that DFT stability does not guarantee H-ordering kinetics or retention during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is penalized because CS-4 uses sealed, arc-welded Ta capsules protected by evacuated silica vessels at 700–850 °C for 7 d. The temperature is moderate for solid-state chemistry, but hydride/Se sealed-tube handling and flux preparation add complexity [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient hydride-specific severity evidence for this route, neutral 50, because their analogues are mostly non-hydride Nb/transition-metal selenides.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS-4 supports reasonable kinetics because NaCl/CsCl flux “secures complete and fast reactions” for hydride selenides over 7 d. However, adaptation from lanthanide hydride selenide to NbHSe\(_2\) requires different bonding and stoichiometry, so structural reconstruction is only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. The flux mechanism is a major enabling factor relative to direct Nb diffusion.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 benefits from standard evacuated-tube conditions used for Nb–Se analogues in SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, and TS-4. It is still penalized for vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile Se control, both implied by the consistent use of evacuated tubes in SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports hydride use but indicates more specialized encapsulation than ordinary quartz.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct solid-state Nb–Se reactions are well documented in SS-2/SS-3/SS-5, but they require long holds or high temperatures. Dense/refractory Nb chemistry makes cation diffusion difficult [Reasonable Inference], and H ordering adds an extra kinetic constraint not addressed in SS/TS. This gives Route 2 the lowest kinetics score despite its conceptual simplicity.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is supported by SS-4/TS-3 vapor transport growth of niobium selenide layer structures and by SS-1/TS-1 or SS-2/TS-2 halogen-assisted transport. The route is penalized for evacuated sealed tubes, volatile/toxic halogen transport agents, and multi-step growth. CS provides insufficient support for hydride CVT, neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport improves mass transport and crystal growth, which is supported by SS-4/TS-3 reporting excellent niobium selenide crystals. However, H incorporation during halogen transport is uncertain and may be disfavored by volatile H-containing species or halogen reactions [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics for Nb–Se crystallization are good, but target stoichiometry control remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

Route 4 avoids high-temperature hydride/Se co-reaction by first making NbSe\(_2\), which is well supported by SS-3/SS-4/TS-3. The hydrogenation step requires controlled H atmosphere but can plausibly occur at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the best F2 score, although the method itself is extrapolated rather than directly reported in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Route 4 scores highest kinetically because the Nb–Se framework is preformed and only H must be inserted [Reasonable Inference]. The structural similarity is strong: NbSe\(_2\) analogues have layered structures and Nb–Se distances near 2.62 Å, close to the target 2.66 Å (SS-3, SS-4, TS-3). The uncertainty is whether the Nb\(_3\)H trigonal site is thermodynamically accessible under low-temperature hydrogenation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one cation species and a simple 2D P-6m2 sheet, so the cation-size-disorder and large-cell checks are not triggered by the target data. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is not present in the target description, unlike some complex SS/TS analogues with edge/face/corner-sharing polyhedra. The amorphization risk is therefore low, although H disorder could still complicate diffraction refinement [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NbHSe\(_2\) is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the provided target E\(_\text{hull}\) is 0.0 eV/atom. The strongest direct chemical support is CS-4, which demonstrates that crystalline metal hydride selenides can be synthesized from metal hydrides and Se in chloride flux, while SS/TS strongly support the robustness of layered Nb–Se frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to known NbSe\(_2\)-type layered phases, especially the similar Nb–Se bond lengths in SS-3/SS-4/TS-3. The most critical limiting factor is hydrogen stoichiometry and retention, because SS/TS analogues lack H and CS-4 involves lanthanides rather than Nb. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with the best practical route being low-temperature hydrogenation of well-crystallized NbSe\(_2\), followed by flux synthesis as the most chemically evidenced backup.
