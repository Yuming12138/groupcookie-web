### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is computed exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. Hull-stable analogues occur among MoS₂, Ta₂PdS₆, Nb₃PbS₆, and U–Mo–S sulfides, which supports that nearby sulfide chemistries are not intrinsically metastable (SS1, TS1, CS1, CS2, CS5, TS3, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not prove kinetic accessibility or Mo/W ordering at finite temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
The sealed-ampoule route avoids >1000 °C and high pressure by using a 850–950 °C anneal inferred from related sealed sulfide reactions (CS4, TS4) and layered sulfide stability (SS1, SS2). It still requires vacuum or Ar sealing because sulfur is volatile and related sulfides use evacuated silica/quartz tubes (CS1, CS4, SS2, TS4). The severity score is reduced mainly for controlled atmosphere, sealed sulfur handling, and repeated grinding/annealing.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Using MoS₂ and WS₂ is kinetically more favorable than starting from Mo and W metals because the target already contains MoS₂- and WS₂-like sheets [Reasonable Inference from target structure and SS1/SS2]. However, Mo/W redistribution or ordered stacking may be slow because transition-metal sulfides often require long anneals or repeated cycles (CS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether the target forms as an ordered compound or remains a physical mixture/intergrowth of MoS₂ and WS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
CVT is directly supported for layered MoS₂ at 1040–1120 °C and 168–216 h in evacuated quartz ampoules (SS2, TS2). The high temperature, vacuum ampoule, sulfur vapor, and multistep gradient growth justify a lower F2 than Route 1. No high-pressure penalty is applied because the CVT analogue uses quartz ampoules rather than multi-anvil conditions (SS2, TS2).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Vapor transport can overcome solid-state diffusion limitations and is proven for the closest MoS₂ analogue (SS2, TS2). The score is limited by the need to transport both Mo and W at controlled rates and to obtain a 1:1 layered arrangement rather than separate MoS₂ and WS₂ crystals [Reasonable Inference]. The provided data support MoS₂ and mixed chalcogenide vapor growth but do not directly prove Mo/W cation ordering (SS2, TS2).

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
CVD is identified for the closest MoS₂ analogue, but SS1/TS1 provide insufficient evidence for the actual CVD temperature, pressure, precursors, or equipment. A moderate severity score is assigned because common Mo/W sulfide CVD uses controlled gas flows and possibly toxic or air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids sealed bulk ampoule pressure but requires careful gas-phase precursor control [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
CVD can directly crystallize layered MoS₂ according to the closest structural/thermodynamic analogue record (SS1, TS1). Kinetics are favorable for thin films because diffusion lengths are short [Reasonable Inference], but composition and layer-sequence control are difficult for an ordered MoS₂/WS₂ target [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS provide no direct synthesis evidence for ordered MoWS₄ by CVD.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has simple edge-sharing MoS₆ and WS₆ sheet connectivity, matching molybdenite-like MoS₂ analogues and not showing mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity (target structure; SS1, SS2). Mo and W are chemically similar congeners, so a large cation-size mismatch is unlikely [Reasonable Inference]. The amorphization risk is low, although disorder between Mo- and W-containing layers remains possible [Reasonable Inference].
