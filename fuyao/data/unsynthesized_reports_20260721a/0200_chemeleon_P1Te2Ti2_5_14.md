### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly indicates thermodynamic accessibility as a ground-state phase according to the provided hull criterion. Several close analogues are also hull-stable, including Ti₂SbP (SS1, TS1), TiTe₂ (SS2, TS2), and Zr/Hf–Si/Ge–Te phases (CS2–CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of binary Ti–Te/Ti–P competitors. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard sealed-tube sintering, which is well supported by Ti₂SbP, TiTe₂, TiZrTe₄, and Zr/Hf telluride syntheses (SS1, SS2, SS4, CS2–CS5). Its score is reduced because P and Te are volatile/toxic and because analogues commonly require sealed tubes, inert/vacuum handling, regrinding, or long anneals (SS2, SS4, CS2–CS5). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that Ti₂PTe₂ can be made in a single short firing.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic limitation is Ti diffusion and possible slow ordering of P/Te within Ti-centered octahedral sheets. Ti₂SbP forms at 800 °C with repeated sintering (SS1, TS1), while TiZrTe₄ and TiTe₂ require longer or more severe processing (SS2, SS4), supporting only moderate kinetics. The structural match to Ti₂SbP and TiTe₂ improves feasibility, but the exact target ordering is not proven by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

The flux route avoids temperatures above 1000 °C, following TiAsTe synthesis at 923 K for 72 h (CS1, SS3). Deductions remain for sealed/vacuum handling, volatile P/Te, multistep flux washing, and toxicity. The route is milder than TiTe₂ high-temperature protocols (SS2, TS2).

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because LiCl/KCl flux successfully produces TiAsTe from elemental Ti/As/Te with high yield and purity (CS1, SS3). The molten flux is expected to enhance transport and nucleation relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is structural: TiAsTe is not P-3m1 and has Ti 8-coordinate environments rather than the target TiTe₃P₃ octahedra (CS1, SS3).

#### F2 — Route 3

The TiTe₂-derived route is penalized for high temperatures, quenching, vacuum sealing, volatile P/Te, and multiple thermal steps. TiTe₂ protocols reach 1400–1500 °C, include quenching, and use long tempering at 600 °C for up to 25 d (SS2, TS2). This makes the route physically plausible but experimentally demanding.

#### F3 — Route 3

This route has a strong structural basis because TiTe₂ is P-3m1 and omega-structured like the target, with closely matching Ti–Te distances (SS2, TS2; target data). However, converting a TiTe₂-like framework into Ti₂PTe₂ requires P incorporation and a different Ti:Te ratio, which may require substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. TiTe₂’s reported homogeneity and phase issues also lower the score (SS2, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 4

The I₂-assisted route is not extremely hot but requires vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic I₂/P/Te, and a multistep prereaction plus transport protocol. I₂ transport is demonstrated for FeTaTe₃-type telluride growth (SS5), but not for Ti–P–Te. Therefore, route severity is moderate while uncertainty is high.

#### F3 — Route 4

CVT can improve crystallinity for layered tellurides, as shown by I₂-assisted FeTaTe₃ growth (SS5). However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ti₂PTe₂ vapor transport equilibria, and multicomponent transport may fractionate Ti/P/Te. This route is better considered a crystal-growth method after bulk phase identification rather than the first synthesis attempt. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a small, ordered layered P-3m1 framework and only one cation species, so no cation-radius mismatch trigger applies. The target has edge- and corner-sharing octahedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity, while the related Ti₂SbP analogue tolerates even more complex face/edge/corner sharing (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is that layered chalcogenides can form stacking faults or turbostratic disorder even when not amorphous. [Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₂PTe₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the provided target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple related Ti/Zr/Hf tellurides are also hull-stable (CS2–CS5, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2). The synthesis evidence is fairly strong: structurally, Ti₂SbP and TiTe₂ support Ti-centered octahedral layered frameworks (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2), while chemically, TiAsTe supports Ti–pnictogen–Te formation in LiCl/KCl flux (CS1, SS3). The most critical enabling factor is the flux or molten chalcogen-mediated transport, which can overcome sluggish Ti diffusion and promote crystalline layered products [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1/SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under volatile P/Te conditions, because related tellurides require sealed tubes, long anneals, quenching, or repeated grinding to manage phase formation (SS2, SS4, CS2–CS5). Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with flux growth recommended as the first attempt.
