### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest stability band. Chemically and structurally related K/Rb/Cs chalcogenides are also reported on or near the hull, including KAuS/K4Au6S5 (CS-1, CS-3), K2HgS2 (SS-2, TS-1), K3AuSe2 (SS-4, TS-3), and RbK2BiSe3 (TS-5). The main uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee that the specific P-1 ilmenite-like polymorph will be the experimentally selected polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because analogous sealed-ampoule sulfide reactions occur at 600–750 °C or 970 K (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2/TS-1). The score is lowered by vacuum/controlled atmosphere, sealed sulfur chemistry, K2S air sensitivity, multi-step precursor handling, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference for Tl hazard]. CS, SS, and TS all support moderate sealed-ampoule difficulty rather than severe high-pressure difficulty.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Binary sulfides such as K2S are reactive but still require solid-state interdiffusion and annealing, as shown in K–Au–S and K–Hg–S syntheses (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2/TS-1). K/Tl sulfides should be kinetically more mobile than refractory oxide systems [Reasonable Inference], but the target structure is not identical to the analogues, so structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable. Long holds and slow cooling in the analogues suggest ordering kinetics are nontrivial (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2/TS-1).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct elemental fusion is supported by alkali metal gold chalcogenide synthesis at 600 °C in ampoules (CS-2, CS-5) and by KAuS melt synthesis at 750 °C (CS-1). It has no high-pressure penalty, but the use of K metal, sulfur vapor, sealed ampoules, and toxic Tl makes it operationally more hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct elemental KTlS fusion specifically, so SS/TS are neutral 50 for direct elemental-route transferability.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
Elemental fusion should provide high reactivity and liquid-assisted transport, consistent with CS reports of fusion reactions forming alkali Au chalcogenides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). However, nucleating the target P-1 KTlS phase from a melt is a full structural assembly problem, and CS analogues often need slow cooling and annealing for crystallization (CS-1, CS-5). The main uncertainty is whether competing binary Tl sulfides or K polysulfides crystallize first [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The sulfide-flux route uses moderate temperatures similar to K2CuSbS3 flux growth at 823 K and K–Au–S melts at 600–750 °C (SS-1, CS-1, CS-3). It still requires inert/vacuum sealed handling, volatile sulfur control, and air-sensitive K2S, as reported for related syntheses (SS-1, CS-1, CS-4). Tl toxicity adds a further practical hazard [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because K2S/K2Sx melt should enhance diffusion and crystal growth, and structurally similar K sulfides have been made by reactive flux (SS-1). The drawback is phase competition: SS-1 reports ~50% yield and side products, showing that K sulfide flux can produce competing phases. Overall, the flux mechanism is the best compromise between mobility and chemical relevance to KTlS.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
Solvothermal synthesis is mild in temperature, with structurally/thermodynamically similar chalcogenides prepared at 145–150 °C in sealed autoclaves (SS-5, TS-4). The route is penalized for sealed solvent pressure, multistep washing/crystallization, and toxic Tl precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient evidence for solvothermal synthesis of chemically similar simple K–Tl–S materials, so CS is neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics.**  
Solvothermal conditions offer high precursor activity and short-range solution transport, as shown by SS-5/TS-4. However, the structure/chemistry transfer is weak because SS-5/TS-4 involve complex Ag/As sulfide frameworks and organic mineralizers, not dense binary-sulfide-derived KTlS. Thus kinetics of dissolution are favorable, but crystallization into the target P-1 phase is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a small-composition, ordered ternary sulfide with no reported mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity and likely a small primitive cell [Reasonable Inference from target description]. SS and TS analogues show that K chalcogenides can form crystalline extended polyhedral networks rather than amorphous products (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-5). The main uncertainty is polymorphism or glassy sulfide formation under quenching; slow cooling mitigates this, as repeatedly used in CS/SS/TS analogues.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KTlS is likely a ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar K-containing chalcogenides are also on or near the hull (CS-1 to CS-5; SS-1 to SS-4; TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from sealed-ampoule sulfide/melt and reactive-flux analogues using K2S or alkali metals with chalcogens at 500–750 °C (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and reactivity of alkali sulfide/sulfur melts, which should help crystallize a simple KTlS phase [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-3/SS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is handling and stoichiometry control: K2S is air-sensitive, sulfur is volatile, and Tl is toxic and may form competing Tl sulfides [Reasonable Inference; air sensitivity supported by CS-2, CS-4, SS-1]. Overall, the target is highly plausible synthetically, with the best experimental starting point being a sealed K2S/Tl2S/S flux or binary-sulfide reaction.
