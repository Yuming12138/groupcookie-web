### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has exact E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it scores at the top of the provided stability rubric. Chemically similar Cu₂SnS₃ is almost exactly on hull, E_hull ≈ 0.00049 eV/atom, while CuInS₂, CuGaS₂, Cu₁₂Sb₄S₁₃, CuS, Cu₂HgGeS₄, and Cu₂ZnGeS₄ analogues are listed as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom across CS/SS/TS. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 solvothermal severity.**  
The Cu₂SnS₃ analogue forms at 453 K for 20 h in ethanol in a Teflon-lined autoclave with no special atmosphere (CS), so thermal severity is low. Deductions arise from a sealed solvothermal system, multiple processing steps, and thiourea toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for solvothermal Cu–In–S synthesis specifically, so those sources are neutral for this route’s condition severity.

**F3 — Route 1 solvothermal kinetics.**  
The soluble chloride/thiourea chemistry used for Cu₂SnS₃ provides high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths (CS). The closest analogue is Enargite-like and tetrahedral, making structural reconstruction modest for target Cu₂InS₃ [Reasonable Inference from CS]. The uncertainty is whether In³⁺ hydrolysis/complexation changes nucleation relative to Sn⁴⁺ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 sealed-tube solid-state severity.**  
Analogues use evacuated sealed tubes and multi-day or multi-week annealing: CuS at 620–670 K for 24 h (SS/TS), Cu₁₂Sb₄S₁₃ at 873 K plus 673 K for weeks (CS/SS/TS), and Cu₂ZnGeS₄ at 700 °C for 3 + 2 days (SS/TS). These conditions are not extreme in temperature but require vacuum sealing and sulfur-vapor management. The main uncertainty is the exact temperature window for Cu₂InS₃ rather than CuInS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 sealed-tube solid-state kinetics.**  
Elemental Cu-based reactions are feasible in sealed tubes, but analogues often need annealing and intermediate grinding, indicating moderate kinetic limitations (CS/SS/TS). Sulfur and indium can form liquid or reactive sulfide intermediates that may accelerate reaction below the Cu melting point [Reasonable Inference]. Cation ordering into the Enargite-like Cu₂InS₃ arrangement may require prolonged annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman severity.**  
CuInS₂ is prepared from a melt by Bridgman/Czochralski using high-purity elements (CS/SS/TS), supporting melt growth as feasible for Cu–In–S chemistry. The likely need for sealed ampoules and high temperature gives moderate severity because sulfur volatility must be managed [Reasonable Inference]. The provided CuInS₂ source lacks explicit temperature/time details, so CS/SS/TS evidence for exact melt parameters is incomplete and partially neutral.

**F3 — Route 3 melt/Bridgman kinetics.**  
Melt growth largely removes solid-state diffusion barriers and is directly supported for CuInS₂ (CS/SS/TS). The crystallization pathway into a tetrahedral Cu–In–S framework is plausible because CuInS₂ contains corner-sharing CuS₄/InS₄ tetrahedra (CS/SS/TS).
