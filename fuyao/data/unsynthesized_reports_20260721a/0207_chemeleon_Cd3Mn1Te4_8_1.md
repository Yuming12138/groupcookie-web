### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase rather than a high-energy metastable structure. Several thermodynamic analogues are also exactly on the hull, including HgCdAsBr, AgInTe2, CdIn2Te4, CdGa2Te4, and AgGaTe2 (TS1–TS5). Chemically and structurally related tellurides likewise include many hull-stable examples (CS1–CS4; SS2; SS4; SS5), so the high F1 score is strongly supported. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee facile ordering of Cd/Mn over the chalcopyrite-like cation sublattice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard evacuated quartz ampoule conditions, which are common for CdIn2Te4/ZnIn2Te4/CdGa2Te4-type tellurides (CS4; SS5; TS3; TS4). The severity comes from vacuum sealing, Cd/Te volatility, toxic Cd and Te, and repeated high-temperature annealing/regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and can likely stay below 1000 °C, so it is less severe than melt or high-pressure routes. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Cd3MnTe4 decomposition temperature, so the thermal ceiling is estimated by analogy and treated cautiously.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solid-state diffusion is only moderate because binary tellurides are not as reactive as molecular or solution precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Related CdIn2Te4/CdGa2Te4 materials required long anneals, sometimes 4–5 weeks or 40–220 days, indicating slow equilibration and possible ordering limitations (CS4; SS5; TS3; TS4). The structural match to tetrahedral CdTe4 frameworks helps, but Cd/Mn cation ordering may remain slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS3 supports that Mn–Te sealed-tube solid-state chemistry is feasible, but also indicates long reaction times and phase-control difficulty.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized for temperatures above 1000 °C, vacuum ampoules, volatile Cd/Te, and toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature range is based on AgInTe2 fusion at 1050 °C and Cu2SnTe3 Bridgman growth at 1150 °C (SS4; TS2; CS5). The method avoids high pressure but requires more demanding ampoule safety and thermal control than Route 1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly proves that MnCd3Te4 melts congruently, so this route carries decomposition/segregation uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt growth improves homogenization and diffusion, supported by successful fusion or Bridgman synthesis of related tellurides (SS4; TS2; CS5). The chalcopyrite analogue AgInTe2 is especially relevant because it is a tetrahedral telluride obtained by elemental fusion/quenching (SS4; TS2). However, CS5 reports secondary SnTe in Cu2SnTe3, showing that melt-derived tellurides can still have competing phase issues. For MnCd3Te4, controlled slow cooling and post-annealing are likely needed to establish Cd/Mn ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C synthesis and high pressure, which improves its condition severity relative to melt and high-pressure routes. It still requires vacuum sealing and contains volatile/toxic Cd–Te components, as broadly supported by telluride ampoule syntheses (CS2; CS3; SS4; TS2). The multi-step nature—loading, sealing, heating, slow cooling, flux separation, and possible annealing—adds moderate complexity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically supports flux conditions, so TS is neutral for Route 3 condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetics score because molten telluride flux should enhance transport and allow crystallization below the full melt temperature [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 directly demonstrates telluride flux growth at 650–900 °C with slow cooling. This route may also improve crystal quality and reduce the long equilibrium anneals seen in CdGa2Te4-type phase-diagram work (TS4). The uncertainty is that the demonstrated CS2 flux is Cu-rich and chemically different, so Te/CdTe flux transferability to Cd–Mn–Te is inferred rather than proven [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 is severe because high-pressure telluride synthesis requires specialized multi-anvil or diamond-anvil-type equipment, as shown by CS1 and SS1. CS1 reports 40–65 kbar at 400 °C, while SS1 reports 11.5 GPa for a ZnTe high-pressure polymorph. Toxic Cd/Te precursors and pre-synthesis steps remain necessary [Reasonable Inference]. Because the target is predicted on the hull at ambient pressure, the high-pressure route is likely a fallback rather than the preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

High-pressure conversion can be fast when starting from a structurally related precursor, as CS1 reports quantitative conversion after only 3 h at 400 °C. However, low temperature under pressure may not permit extensive Cd/Mn redistribution unless the feed is already homogeneous [Reasonable Inference]. SS1 also indicates that high-pressure phases can have narrow stability windows, increasing the risk of incomplete or non-recoverable products. Thus, kinetics are acceptable for polymorph conversion but uncertain for forming a newly ordered quaternary/ternary cation arrangement [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure is composed of corner-sharing MnTe4 and CdTe4 tetrahedra, with no edge/face-sharing coexistence reported in the target description, giving low network-frustration risk. The unit formula is small, and there is no evidence for a >40 atom unit cell from the target description [Reasonable Inference]. The one trigger is the Cd²⁺/Mn²⁺ tetrahedral ionic radius mismatch of roughly 15%, which may promote disorder or broaden peaks but should not strongly favor amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally related tetrahedral tellurides crystallize readily under ampoule or melt routes (SS4; SS5; TS2; TS3), supporting a relatively high F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnCd3Te4 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and many thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: structurally close chalcopyrite/tetrahedral tellurides such as AgInTe2 and CdIn2Te4 are known from ampoule fusion or solid-state routes (SS4; SS5; TS2; TS3), while Mn–Te chemistry is supported in sealed-tube tellurides (SS3). The most critical enabling factor is the simple corner-sharing tetrahedral Cd/Mn–Te framework, which matches several known telluride structure families (SS4; SS5; TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under Cd/Te volatility, especially avoiding residual CdTe, MnTe, or Te-rich secondary phases [Reasonable Inference], a concern consistent with impurity problems in related tellurides (CS3; CS5; TS4). Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with Te-rich flux growth or carefully annealed sealed-ampoule synthesis being the most practical starting points.
