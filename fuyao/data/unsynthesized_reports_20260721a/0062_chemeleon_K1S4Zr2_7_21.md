### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability bracket. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull = 0 are experimentally synthesized, including CeScS3 by H2S solid-state sintering (TS-1), BaCe2MnS5 by sealed solid-state reaction (TS-2), and RbCeS2 by H2S synthesis (TS-3). The main uncertainty is that the target’s mixed-valence K–Zr sulfide chemistry is not directly represented in TS; insufficient evidence from TS for exact K–Zr–S phase competition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses temperatures similar to CS-2 and CS-4, below the harshest >1000 °C penalty if kept at 850–950 °C. It still requires vacuum sealing and inert sulfide handling, consistent with CS-2, CS-4, SS-3, and SS-5. The severity score is lowered by K/S volatility and air-sensitive K2S [Reasonable Inference], while no high-pressure equipment is required based on the analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary sulfides are viable precursors in CS-4, SS-1, SS-3, and SS-5, and elemental Zr/S synthesis works for SrZrS3 in CS-2. Kinetics remain moderate because Zr metal is refractory [Reasonable Inference] and because the target requires ordered K/Zr/S framework formation. The long anneals and regrinding used in CS-2, CS-4, SS-3, and SS-5 suggest diffusion and homogeneity are manageable but not fast.

#### F2 — Route 2

H2S sulfidation is directly supported by CS-1 for SrZrS3 and by SS-4/TS-3 for RbLnS2, but H2S introduces a significant reactive/toxic atmosphere penalty. If ZrO2 conversion requires temperatures near or above 1000 °C, an additional severity deduction is appropriate [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that H2S sulfidation can stabilize mixed-valence KZr2S4 specifically, so the route is more severe and less direct.

#### F3 — Route 2

Oxide/carbonate starting materials are less kinetically favorable than preformed sulfides because ZrO2 is refractory [Reasonable Inference], despite successful carbonate/dioxide sulfidation in CS-1. The route requires full conversion from oxide/carbonate to sulfide and likely substantial structural reconstruction. The remaining uncertainty is whether the H2S/carbon environment can tune Zr to the target average valence rather than producing ZrS2 or K-containing side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids very high temperatures, supported by low-temperature K2MoS4 solvothermal synthesis in CS-3 and lower-temperature sulfide annealing windows in CS-4. However, sealed solvents or polysulfide fluxes impose pressure/volatility and air-sensitivity penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports this exact flux/solvothermal route for K–Zr–S, so the apparent mild conditions are balanced by experimental uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3

K2S-containing media are chemically active enough to crystallize K2MoS4 at 443 K in CS-3, giving a good precursor-activity score. The weakness is structural reconstruction: CS-3 yields isolated/tetrahedral Mo sulfide chemistry, not an extended KZr2S4 Zr–S framework, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows solvothermal Zr sulfide framework crystallization. Post-annealing could improve crystallinity [Reasonable Inference], but this makes the route more exploratory.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines large K+ with much smaller Zr cations [Reasonable Inference], triggering the cation-size mismatch criterion. The reported target connectivity includes mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing S-centered polyhedra, and similar mixed connectivities appear in SS-1 and SS-5. The unit cell does not appear large from the formula and Immm description, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms the exact atom count; this check is treated as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(ZrS2)2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several TS analogues with E_hull = 0 are experimentally accessible by conventional sulfide synthesis (TS-1 to TS-3). The analogues support synthesis best through sealed or flowing-gas solid-state sulfide chemistry: Zr sulfides are made from elements or oxide/carbonate precursors under vacuum or H2S conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with precedent for Zr–S octahedral frameworks forming at 850–1000 °C (CS-2, CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining K and S stoichiometry in a sealed high-temperature reaction while controlling the Zr redox state [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is moderately synthesizeable, with direct sealed-ampoule sulfide synthesis preferred over oxide sulfidation or exploratory flux/solvothermal methods.
