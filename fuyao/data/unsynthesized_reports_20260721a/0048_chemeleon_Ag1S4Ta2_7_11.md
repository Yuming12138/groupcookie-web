### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a high F1 score. Chemically similar AgTaS₃ analogues are also at or essentially at the hull, E_hull = 0–0.0002 eV/atom (CS1/CS2). Structurally and thermodynamically related TaS₂ and intercalated Ta–S compounds are also stable or near-stable (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS3), supporting the view that the Ta–Ag–S composition is not thermodynamically implausible. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the monoclinic C2 ordering can be accessed kinetically [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from direct same-chemistry precedent: Ag–Ta–S sulfides were synthesized at 500–600 °C for 1–4 days in evacuated silica tubes (CS1). Its F2 is reduced by vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and multistep TaSₓ precursor preparation, all present in CS1 and SS1/TS1. Kinetics are the limiting issue because Ta is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and TaS₂ analogues show equilibrium/phase-purity challenges requiring homogenization and annealing (SS1/TS1). The route is feasible but likely needs repeated grinding and sulfur activity control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by a very close chemical analogue, AgTaS₃, grown by AgCl/LiCl flux at 630 °C for 3 days under vacuum with slow cooling (CS2). F2 is better than Route 1 because flux growth is a single main thermal process, though it still requires sealed vacuum and volatile sulfur management from CS2. F3 is improved by molten-salt transport [Reasonable Inference] and by the Ag–S = 2.43 Å match between CS2 and target, but Ta incorporation remains diffusion-limited relative to low-melting cations [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes Ta₂AgS₄ rather than AgTaS₃ or TaS₂.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is grounded in structurally similar Ta–S layered CVT growth using I₂ at 1000/900 °C for 9 days (SS5/TS3) and gas-transport synthesis of related layered materials (SS4). F2 is penalized for toxic/volatile I₂, sealed evacuated ampoules, and multistep feed preparation, consistent with SS5/TS3. F3 is moderate because vapor transport can improve mass transport [Reasonable Inference], but no CS analogue demonstrates Ag–Ta–S CVT. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth/recrystallization strategy after phase discovery rather than the first synthesis attempt.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 obtains the highest score because it uses a preformed layered TaS₂ framework whose TaS₆ edge-sharing sheets and Ta–S ≈ 2.47 Å closely match the target (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The ion-exchange precedent in layered TaS₂-derived materials gives good kinetic support: two-stage exchange in salt solutions produced single-phase products (SS2/TS2). F2 penalties come from multistep host/intercalate preparation and possible air/moisture sensitivity of intercalated phases [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that Ag⁺ exchange to the exact Ta₂AgS₄ stoichiometry is not directly shown in CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one major amorphization risk: very different cation sizes and coordination preferences between linearly coordinated Ag and high-valent Ta in TaS₆ polyhedra [Reasonable Inference], which is consistent with distinct Ag and Ta coordination environments in CS2 and the target description. The target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target cell atom count. The target also does not show simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, unlike some structurally related complex Ta–Fe–S materials (SS5/TS3). Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₂AgS₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close Ag–Ta–S and Ta–S analogues are also stable or nearly stable (CS1/CS2, TS1/TS3). The strongest support comes from the combination of same-chemistry AgTaS₃ syntheses at moderate temperatures (CS1/CS2) and nearly identical TaS₂-like edge-sharing TaS₆ sheets in the structural analogues (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing layered TaS₂ framework, which should allow Ag insertion with limited Ta–S reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among AgTaS₃, Ag₂S, TaS₂, and possible Ag-intercalated TaS₂ compositions [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS2 and SS1/TS1 phase-control comments]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with ion exchange or low-temperature flux being preferred over high-temperature direct sintering.
