### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability category by the required rubric. Several structurally or thermodynamically similar tetrahedral sulfides are also reported with E_hull = 0, including Li₂CdGeS₄, Li₂MnSnS₄, Li₂CdSnS₄, and AgCd₂GaS₄ (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1–TS-4). Chemically similar sulfides include both stable and mildly metastable examples, such as MgAl₂S₄ at E_hull = 0 and CuTi₂S₄ at 0.0219 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2), so CS supports sulfide accessibility but also warns that polymorph/phase control may matter.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: evacuated solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because similar routes require sealed evacuated tubes, high temperatures, and long holds, as shown for MgAl₂S₄ at 1250 °C (CS-2), Li₂MnSnS₄ at 750–800 °C (SS-3, TS-2), and AgCd₂GaS₄ with prolonged annealing (TS-4). The route avoids high pressure, but sulfur volatility and dry sulfide handling require sealed-tube precautions [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, MgS/MnS should reduce chemical mismatch relative to elements [Reasonable Inference], but cation ordering into an R3m chalcopyrite-like framework may be slow, consistent with reannealing/long annealing requirements in CS-5 and SS-3/TS-2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: alkali polysulfide flux

Flux synthesis has lower thermal severity because related tetrahedral quaternary sulfides are made at 525–750 °C (SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-3, TS-5). It still requires sealed vacuum or inert loading and air-sensitive sulfide/polysulfide reagents, as supported by glovebox/vacuum procedures for Li₂EuGeS₄ and Li₂MnSnS₄ (TS-5, SS-3/TS-2). Kinetically, the molten polysulfide medium should enhance cation mobility and crystallization [Reasonable Inference], which is why this route receives the highest F3 score; the main uncertainty is whether Mg partitions into the target rather than remaining in flux or forming MgS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: halide flux

Halide flux conditions are directly supported by rhombohedral CuTi₂S₄ formation at 750 °C using KCl/KI in evacuated sealed silica tubes followed by week-long slow cooling (CS-1). This route has similar severity deductions to polysulfide flux because vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, multistep flux handling, and dry Mg sulfide handling remain necessary [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, KCl/KI flux should promote crystallization, but CS-1 is spinel-like rather than chalcopyrite-like, so structural transferability is weaker than for the stannite/chalcopyrite-like tetrahedral sulfide analogues in SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: CVT

CVT is plausible because V–Cu–S and TiS₂/TiSe₂ analogues are grown by iodine or chlorine vapor transport in sealed gradient furnaces for long durations (CS-3, CS-4). Condition severity is significant due to halogen transport agents, sealed tubes, long times, and sulfur volatility (CS-3, CS-4). Kinetically, vapor transport can overcome bulk diffusion limitations, but there is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence that Mg–Mn–S transports congruently; competing phase formation during V–Cu–S CVT is an important warning (CS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: high pressure

High pressure is supported only by the structurally similar Sr₂ZnS₃ case, which used a high-pressure cell, dry glovebox loading, and H₂S–Ar precursor processing (SS-1). This gives poor condition severity because high-pressure equipment and controlled hazardous sulfide atmospheres are required (SS-1). Kinetically, pressure can aid densification [Reasonable Inference], but because the target is predicted to be on the hull, high pressure is not strongly justified by TS/CS/SS evidence.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target contains Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺ in tetrahedral sulfide environments, and their ionic radius mismatch is just over the 15% threshold [Reasonable Inference], triggering C1. The target description indicates corner-sharing tetrahedra only, unlike mixed edge/face/corner polyhedral networks in some spinel-like analogues (CS-1, CS-2), so C3 is not triggered for the target. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has a >40-atom unit cell; the described formula and coordination network are treated as a smaller ordered cell, so C2 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₃MnS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar tetrahedral sulfides such as Li₂MnSnS₄, Li₂CdGeS₄, and Li₂CdSnS₄, which are experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state or polysulfide flux methods (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1–TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the simple sulfide chemistry with Mg²⁺/Mn²⁺ in tetrahedral sulfur coordination, consistent with common sulfide environments in the analogues (CS-2, SS-3, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase-pure cation ordering in a Mg/Mn-only chalcopyrite-like R3m framework, because quaternary tetrahedral sulfides often show binary/ternary impurity competition and narrow flux-ratio windows (SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-3). A polysulfide flux route is therefore recommended because it balances moderate temperature, high sulfur chemical potential, and improved cation mobility.
