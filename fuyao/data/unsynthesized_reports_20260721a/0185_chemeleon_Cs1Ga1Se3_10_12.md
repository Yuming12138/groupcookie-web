### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the convex hull and therefore strongly thermodynamically favored by the supplied calculation. The closest CsGaSe3 analogue also has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS-1, SS-1, and TS-1, and several other similar Cs/Rb selenides are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is polymorph-specific: the known analogue is P2_1/c CsGaSe3, while the target is Cc CsGaSe3, so relative polymorph stability is not fully resolved [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 550 °C, but requires vacuum sealing, controlled cooling, and glovebox handling, as reported for direct CsGaSe3 synthesis (CS-1/SS-1/TS-1). The main deductions come from vacuum atmosphere, volatile Se in a sealed tube, multistep precursor/flux processing, and air-sensitive Cs2Se/toxic optional As2Se3 (CS-1/SS-1/TS-1; toxicity [Reasonable Inference]). The source evidence is strong and internally consistent across CS, SS, and TS because the same direct analogue appears in all three.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because the direct analogue forms by flux at 550 °C over 4 days, indicating effective mass transport through a molten polychalcogenide medium (CS-1/SS-1/TS-1). The target and analogue both contain GaSe4 tetrahedral motifs with similar Ga–Se distances, so structural reconstruction is modest rather than complete (CS-1/SS-1/TS-1 and target structure). The remaining uncertainty is polymorph selection between P2_1/c and Cc, likely controlled by flux composition and cooling schedule [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is inferred from sealed-ampoule Cs–Se solid-state analogues at ~600 °C and direct CsGaSe3 flux temperature near 550 °C (SS-5/TS-5; CS-1/SS-1/TS-1). It still requires vacuum/reduced pressure, sealed Se handling, air-sensitive Cs2Se, and multiple operations. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for non-flux solid-state CsGaSe3 synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary selenide precursors should be moderately reactive, but solid-state diffusion and nucleation are less assisted than in flux growth [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5/TS-5 show that sealed Cs–P–Se product distributions can be sensitive to stoichiometry and heating profile, suggesting phase-purity risk for a Cs–Ga–Se solid-state route. The main uncertainty is whether Cc CsGaSe3 can nucleate as a bulk powder without flux-mediated crystal growth [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The solvothermal route has mild temperature, 115–130 °C, and simple sealed-tube conditions in CsSnSe3 analogues (CS-2/CS-3). It avoids high-temperature vacuum ampoules, giving high condition-severity score, but still uses sealed volatile solvent. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ga-based solvothermal CsGaSe3 formation, so those sources are neutral for this route-specific condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 3

CsSnSe3 solvothermal analogues demonstrate that Cs–Se frameworks can crystallize under low-temperature solution conditions (CS-2/CS-3). However, the Sn analogue uses SnSe5 trigonal bipyramids, while the target requires GaSe4 tetrahedra, so structural transfer is weak [Reasonable Inference]. The major kinetic uncertainty is Ga2Se3 dissolution/speciation and whether Ga can be incorporated into the desired CsGaSe3 framework under methanolothermal conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large Cs/Ga size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from standard ionic radii]. The target description reports corner-sharing GaSe4 tetrahedra and edge/corner-sharing Se-centered polyhedra, but not the full vertex + edge + face-sharing coexistence criterion, so that check is not fully triggered.
