### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the strongest stability bin. This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues NiRh₂S₄ and CuRh₂S₄, both E_hull = 0.0 (TS#1, TS#2), and Rh₂S₃ is nearly on hull at 0.0012 eV/atom (TS#3). The main uncertainty is that FeRh₂S₄ is 0.077 eV/atom above hull (CS#1, SS#1), showing that not every A-site Rh₂S₄ spinel is equally stable.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Direct sealed-tube synthesis is strongly supported by CuRh₂S₄ at 800 °C/48 h and FeRh₂S₄ at 450–850 °C with staged treatments (CS#4, SS#3, TS#2; CS#1, SS#1). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, sulfur volatility, and multistep heating, all evidenced by the ampoule procedures and sulfur deposition control (CS#1, SS#1). Kinetics are moderate because refractory Rh metal is slow to react [Reasonable Inference], but close spinel analogues form under similar conditions (CS#1, CS#4; SS#1, SS#3; TS#2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The binary sulfide route is less severe because it avoids large amounts of elemental sulfur and follows MnIn₂S₄-type sulfide reactions in evacuated quartz at 900–1200 °C (CS#2). F2 is penalized for vacuum and possible >1000 °C heating, but not for high pressure. F3 is stronger than the elemental route because MnS/Rh₂S₃ should be more chemically activated than dense metals [Reasonable Inference], while MnIn₂S₄ confirms tetrahedral Mn spinel chemistry (CS#2) and Rh₂S₃ confirms accessible Rh–S chemistry at 900 °C (SS#4, TS#3).

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CVT is plausible because Rh₃S₄ is grown from Rh/RhCl₃/S in evacuated quartz with a 1320 K → 1290 K gradient (SS#5, TS#4). It is penalized for >1000 °C operation, sealed halide vapor chemistry, multistep RhCl₃ handling, and possible toxic/corrosive chloride chemistry (SS#5, TS#4; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics may be favorable for crystal growth, but composition control is uncertain because pure Rh₃S₄ was difficult and neighboring Rh₁₇S₁₅ formed (SS#5, TS#4).

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
High pressure is only a contingency route, supported indirectly by NiRh₂S₄-related data mentioning high pressure up to 80 kbar and quenching (CS#3, SS#2, TS#1). F2 is therefore low due to high pressure, vacuum prereaction, volatile sulfur, and multistep processing (CS#3, SS#2, TS#1). F3 is moderate because pressure may aid phase selection [Reasonable Inference], but the analogue required recycling until single phase, indicating slow kinetics and phase-control difficulty (CS#3, SS#2, TS#1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target and close analogues are crystalline spinels with ordered tetrahedral and octahedral sites (CS#1–4; SS#1–3; TS#1–2), which lowers amorphization concern. The main trigger is the large conventional spinel cell count [Reasonable Inference], while the described connectivity lacks face sharing and therefore does not meet the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion (CS#1, CS#3, CS#4; SS#1–3). Remaining uncertainty is that Mn/Rh ordering and Rh–S competing phases could cause disorder or impurity formation rather than true amorphization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MnRh₂S₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic Rh spinel analogues NiRh₂S₄ and CuRh₂S₄ are also on the hull (TS#1, TS#2). The synthesis is well supported structurally: FeRh₂S₄, NiRh₂S₄, CuRh₂S₄, and MnIn₂S₄ are all Fd-3m spinels with comparable tetrahedral/octahedral sulfide coordination motifs (CS#1–4; SS#1–3; TS#1–2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple normal sulfide spinel analogues containing either Rh₂S₄ frameworks or tetrahedral MnS₄ units (CS#1–4; SS#1–3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition in the Rh–S subsystem, since Rh₂S₃ and Rh₃S₄ are stable or near-stable and Rh₃S₄ has a narrow phase window with Rh₁₇S₁₅ co-formation (SS#4–5; TS#3–4). Overall, the target appears practically synthesizable, with the binary sulfide route offering the best balance of thermodynamic driving force, precursor reactivity, and manageable conditions.
