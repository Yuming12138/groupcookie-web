### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the maximum by the provided rubric. This is reinforced by stable or nearly stable TiS₂/TiSe₂ analogues: TiSe₂ has E_hull = 0.0 (SS4/TS3), TiS₂-like entries have E_hull ≈ 0.0019–0.0045 eV/atom (SS1/SS2; TS1/TS2), and omega-derived TiGeS₃ is also on hull (CS4). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated 0 K stability does not guarantee retention of S/Se ordering at synthesis temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
The severity score is reduced because direct routes require evacuated sealed tubes and dry handling, as shown for TiSe₂/TiS₂-related solid-state preparations (SS4/TS3) and MTi₂X₄ syntheses (CS2). The route avoids high pressure and does not need >1000 °C if adapted to TiSe₂/TiS₂ ranges (SS4/TS3), but long anneals, chalcogen volatility, and repeated grinding reduce practicality. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Ti₃S₄Se₂ decomposition temperature, so temperature optimization remains uncertain.

**F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
Kinetics are moderate-to-poor because elemental Ti is refractory and direct reaction from dense elements tends to be slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with analogues needing week-long firings and grinding (CS2, SS4/TS3). The structural motif is favorable because TiS₂/TiSe₂ have the same 2D edge-sharing TiX₆ layers (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS3), but mixed S/Se ordering likely needs extended equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 1 is feasible but not the fastest or most controllable route.

**F2 — Route 2 CVT.**  
CVT requires evacuated sealed quartz ampoules, iodine, long high-temperature gradients, and multistep source/crystal-growth handling, all shown in TiS₂ and NbS₂ CVT analogues (SS1/TS1, TS5). It avoids external high pressure and >1000 °C in the TiS₂-like protocol (SS1/TS1), but iodine and chalcogens impose sealed-tube and toxicity/corrosion concerns [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an optimized iodine loading for Ti₃S₄Se₂ specifically, so initial experiments should screen low I₂ concentrations.

**F3 — Route 2 CVT.**  
CVT receives the best kinetic score because iodine vapor transport improves mass transport and crystal growth relative to bulk diffusion, as demonstrated for TiS₂ crystallization (SS1/TS1) and NbS₂ single-crystal growth (TS5). The structural match is excellent: TiS₂ and TiSe₂ are 2D edge-sharing TiX₆ layered compounds with very similar Ti–S/Ti–Se distances to the target (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS3). The main kinetic uncertainty is anion fractionation or competing binary layer growth during transport [Reasonable Inference], especially because chalcogenide CVT phase control can be sensitive (CS1, SS1/TS1).

**F2 — Route 3 flux.**  
Flux synthesis uses lower temperatures than many direct/CVT routes and is supported by TiGeS₃ at 923 K and CuTi₂S₄ at 750 °C (CS4, SS5). Severity remains moderate because sealed evacuated tubes, volatile S/Se, multistep heating/cooling, and flux washing are involved (CS4, SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether KCl or KCl/KI stabilizes the exact Ti₃S₄Se₂ sheet rather than a different Ti chalcogenide phase.

**F3 — Route 3 flux.**  
Flux improves kinetics by providing a liquid/ionic transport medium [Reasonable Inference], and TiGeS₃/CuTi₂S₄ analogues show Ti sulfide crystals can form in halide fluxes (CS4, SS5). The omega-derived character of TiGeS₃ is particularly supportive for the target’s omega-derived Ti–chalcogen framework (CS4). The score is below CVT because flux composition may stabilize alternative phases or incorporate halides/alkalis [Reasonable Inference], while SS5 notes kinetic stabilization and phase-control issues for CuTi₂S₄.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Ti, so the cation-size-disorder amorphization criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description emphasizes edge-sharing octahedra rather than mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity, lowering glass-forming/amorphization risk relative to more connectivity-complex analogues such as Ti₂CoS₄ and LiTi₃S₆ (CS2, SS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly addresses amorphization of Ti₃S₄Se₂, but layered TiS₂/TiSe₂ analogues are crystalline and readily synthesized (SS1/SS2/SS4; TS1–TS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti₃(SeS₂)₂ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest TiS₂/TiSe₂ thermodynamic analogues are also stable or nearly stable (SS1/SS2/SS4; TS1–TS3). The synthesis case is strongly supported structurally because the target’s 2D edge-sharing TiSe/S octahedral sheets closely match TiS₂ and TiSe₂ layered octahedral motifs (SS1/TS1, SS4/TS3). The most important enabling factor is the robust TiX₂-type layered chemistry and very compatible Ti–S/Ti–Se octahedral bond lengths (SS1/SS4; TS1–TS3). The major limiting factor is phase/composition control of mixed S/Se ordering, especially under sealed-tube or CVT conditions where S and Se chemical potentials may differ [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with iodine-assisted CVT or flux growth preferred for crystallinity and kinetic transport, but direct sealed-tube annealing may be the simplest first screening route.
