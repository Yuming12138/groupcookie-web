### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0000\) eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic scoring bin. Nearby thermodynamic references include on-hull Cs₂MoS₄, Cs₂S₂, Rb₂MoS₄, Cs₂S, and Cs₄In₂S₅ or related stable sulfides (TS#1–TS#5). The remaining uncertainty is not hull stability but whether the calculated Cs₃MoS₄ phase is accessible under practical chemical potentials rather than decomposing into experimentally easier Cs₂MoS₄ + Cs sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because the closest analogue Cs₂MoS₄ forms at 423 K for 24 h in sealed hydrothermal conditions (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The main severity penalties are the sealed vessel and careful handling of polysulfide chemistry, since K₂S₆/K₂Se₄-type reagents were used in the analogue route (CS#1; TS#1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no need for H₂, NH₃, vacuum, or GPa pressure for this route, so those items are treated as neutral/no penalty.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because hydrothermal solution chemistry allows reactive transport and Cs₂MoS₄ single crystals formed directly at only 423 K (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The MoS₄ tetrahedron in the target closely matches the MoS₄ tetrahedron in Cs₂MoS₄, with Mo–S distances near 2.18–2.19 Å in the analogue and 2.20–2.26 Å in the target (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1; target data). The uncertainty is that Cs₃MoS₄ is more Cs-rich than Cs₂MoS₄ and may require Mo(V) or a reduced/polysulfide-balanced chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 also uses low temperature: Rb₂MoS₄ formed at 393 K for 7 d in 1,2-ethanediamine in Teflon-lined autoclaves (SS#3; TS#3). The severity penalties come from sealed solvothermal operation and toxic/volatile amine solvent [Reasonable Inference], not from high temperature or GPa pressure. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS indicates no imposed H₂/NH₃/vacuum atmosphere in the Rb₂MoS₄ solvothermal route, so controlled-atmosphere severity is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

The use of \((NH₄)₂MoS₄\) provides a preassembled thiomolybdate unit, which is kinetically favorable for MoS₄-based products (SS#3; TS#3). However, the Rb₂MoS₄ analogue was only a minor phase, with Ag₂S as the main product, indicating substantial phase competition and lowering the kinetics/phase-control score (SS#3; TS#3). Replacement of Rb by Cs is chemically plausible but remains an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux/solid-state route has moderate temperatures relative to refractory Mo chemistry: Rb₂WS₄ used 923 K and Cs₂S used 623 K, while Cs₃BS₃ used 720 °C (CS#5; TS#4; CS#4). The main severity penalties are vacuum/Ar handling, sealed sulfur-containing tubes, air-sensitive Cs₂S/Cs₂Sₓ, and multistep precursor preparation (CS#5; TS#4; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests no need for >1000 °C or GPa pressures.

#### F3 — Route 3

Reactive sulfide flux can overcome otherwise slow diffusion, as Rb₂WS₄ was grown from Rb₂S₃/W/S at 923 K despite W being refractory (CS#5). For Mo, direct solid-state reaction from Mo powder would still face sluggish kinetics without a reactive sulfide flux [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because thiometallate tetrahedra are plausible, but the target stoichiometry is not the same as the demonstrated Rb₂WS₄ or Cs₂MoS₄ phases (CS#5; CS#1).

#### F2 — Route 4

The azide route avoids elemental Cs handling and has precedent for Cs-rich sulfides such as Cs₄In₂S₅ (CS#2; TS#5). Its severity is high because it requires vacuum/inert handling, sealed ampoules, azide decomposition, long annealing, and pressure management from gas evolution (CS#2; TS#5). It does not require extreme temperature or GPa pressure based on the reference data (CS#2; TS#5).

#### F3 — Route 4

Azide decomposition creates reactive alkali metal species, which can help form Cs-rich sulfides (CS#2; TS#5). However, the analogue system is Cs–In–S rather than Cs–Mo–S, so the MoS₄ structural pathway is indirect and less certain [Reasonable Inference]. The long 8–15 d annealing in the azide analogue suggests ordering and crystallization may be slow (CS#2; TS#5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization risk is the large size/coordination mismatch between Cs and Mo, which triggers the cation-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a very large unit cell or coexistence of face-, edge-, and corner-sharing networks, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents a more severe amorphization penalty, and the existence of crystalline Cs₂MoS₄ and Rb₂MoS₄ analogues supports crystallizability of alkali thiomolybdate frameworks (CS#1; SS#3; TS#1; TS#3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃MoS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related Cs/Rb–Mo/S or Cs–S compounds are also on hull (TS#1–TS#5). The strongest synthesis support comes from Cs₂MoS₄, which is chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically closest and forms as single crystals under mild hydrothermal conditions (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing stability and crystallization tendency of isolated tetrahedral MoS₄ units in large-alkali sulfide lattices (CS#1; SS#1; TS#1). The most critical limiting factor is that the target is Cs-richer than the known Cs₂MoS₄ analogue, so Cs activity and Mo/S redox balance must be carefully controlled to avoid crystallizing Cs₂MoS₄, Cs₂S, or polysulfide phases [Reasonable Inference supported by TS#1/TS#4]. Overall, the best route is a Cs-rich hydrothermal/polysulfide route with slow cooling.
