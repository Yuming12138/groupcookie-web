### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the provided target data, so it receives a near-maximal thermodynamic score. This is reinforced by stable telluride analogues in CS/SS/TS, including Cr₂Ge₂Te₆ with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1/TS4), MnTe₂ with E_hull = 0.0 (SS1/TS1), CrAuTe₄ with E_hull = 0.0 (SS2/TS2), and TlCrTe₂ with E_hull = 0.0 (SS5/TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee the specific monoclinic C2/m polymorph will nucleate first; high-pressure MnGa₂Te₄/MnIn₂Te₄ analogues show polymorphism can be important (CS3/SS3, CS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to CS1/TS4, CS2, and SS1/TS1, so no high-temperature deduction is applied. Vacuum/sealed-tube operation is required because essentially all directly synthesized telluride analogues used evacuated sealed tubes or ampoules (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). Te volatility and toxicity create additional severity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the recurring need for sealed systems in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct element reactions are proven for tellurides such as MnTe₂, Cr₂Ge₂Te₆, CrAuTe₄, and Bi₂MnTe₄ (SS1/TS1, CS1/TS4, SS2/TS2, SS4). The kinetic penalty comes from dense Mn metal and the need to build an ordered mixed Mn/Sn octahedral framework [Reasonable Inference]. Long reaction times and repeated anneals in CS1, CS2, and SS2/TS2 justify only a moderate kinetics score.

#### F2 — Route 2

The melt–quench route uses controlled vacuum ampoules, quenching, and annealing, directly paralleling Bi₂MnTe₄ synthesis (SS4). It is more operationally severe than Route 1 because it has multiple steps: melting, quenching, resealing or annealing, and possible regrinding, as shown by SS4. No high-pressure or >1000 °C deduction is needed for the proposed adaptation.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt processing should improve Mn/Sn/Te contact and short-range diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and Bi₂MnTe₄ demonstrates that Mn-containing tellurides can form by melt–quench–anneal processing (SS4). The limiting kinetic step is crystallization into the correct C2/m ordering rather than a glassy or multiphase telluride product [Reasonable Inference]. SS4’s need for annealing after quenching supports a moderate structural reconstruction penalty.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure synthesis is experimentally supported for MnGa₂Te₄-II and MnIn₂Te₄-II at about 1.5 GPa and ~800 °C (CS3/SS3, CS5). The scoring table only penalizes pressures above 5 GPa, so the proposed 1–2 GPa route avoids that specific deduction, but it still requires encapsulation, multiple steps, and specialized equipment as described in CS3/SS3 and CS5. The route remains practically challenging despite the formal F2 score.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can help access dense octahedral frameworks or polymorphs, as shown by quenchable high-pressure MnGa₂Te₄-II and MnIn₂Te₄-II (CS3/SS3, CS5). Kinetics are helped by using a pre-reacted precursor, analogous to MnIn₂Te₄-I → MnIn₂Te₄-II (CS5). Uncertainty is high because the target is already predicted on-hull, so pressure may be unnecessary or may stabilize a competing dense polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux/CVT requires sealed evacuated tubes and multiple steps, supported by CS4 and TS5. It also introduces volatile/toxic Te and potentially hygroscopic/corrosive transport agents such as AlCl₃, which CS4 used for CVT; handling risk is therefore moderate. No high-pressure or >1000 °C deduction is needed for the recommended lower-temperature flux/CVT adaptation.

#### F3 — Route 4

Route 4 receives the best kinetics score because flux or vapor transport bypasses slow dense-metal diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS4 specifically reports that chemical vapor transport was used when prereaction did not yield a single-phase MnIn₂Te₄-type material, and TS5 shows telluride flux growth can produce complex multication tellurides. The main uncertainty is composition control for Mn₂SnTe₄, because CS/SS/TS do not include an exact Mn–Sn–Te flux/CVT precedent.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing octahedral network triggers a structural-complexity penalty from the target structure, and mixed-connectivity analogues occur in MnGa₂Te₄ and Bi₂MnTe₄ (CS3/SS3, SS4). The Mn/Sn octahedral ionic-radius mismatch likely exceeds 15% [Reasonable Inference], adding a second trigger. The full unit-cell atom count is not provided, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for that check, so it is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂SnTe₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically, structurally, or thermodynamically similar tellurides are also on or very near the hull (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, CS3/SS3). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support telluride synthesis in evacuated ampoules, flux/CVT, and, if needed, high-pressure routes, but none is an exact Mn–Sn–Te structural match. The most important enabling factor is the established stability and accessibility of Mn–Te octahedral frameworks, demonstrated in MnTe₂, MnGa₂Te₄, and Bi₂MnTe₄ analogues (SS1/TS1, CS3/SS3, SS4). The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control in a volatile Te-containing multication system, as similar tellurides often require long anneals, CVT, flux growth, or repeated processing to reach single phase (CS2, CS4, SS2/TS2, SS5/TS3). Overall, synthesis appears plausible but likely requires careful Te chemical-potential control and slow equilibration.
