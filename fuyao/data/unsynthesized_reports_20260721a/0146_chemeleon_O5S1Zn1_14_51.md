### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be thermodynamically stable by the supplied hull criterion. Thermodynamically similar examples also have E_hull = 0.0, including NaAu(SO4)2, Zn3O(SO4)2, RbAu(SO4)2, MnH2(SO4)2, and Na2Cd(SO4)2 in TS-1 through TS-5. Chemically similar ZnSO4 and Zn3O(SO4)2 are also stable or near-stable in CS-1, CS-2, and CS-4. Remaining uncertainty is that a 0 K hull does not guarantee retention of a weakly bound molecular O2 cluster under synthesis conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT

CVT severity is moderate because analogous ZnSO4 and Zn3O(SO4)2 syntheses operate at 620–660 °C, below severe ceramic temperatures (CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, SS-4). The main F2 penalties are the sealed volatile Cl2 transport agent and Cl2 toxicity, both directly supported by the CVT analogues (CS-2, CS-4). Kinetics are favorable because vapor transport reduces diffusion limitations and crystallizes Zn–sulfate frameworks directly (CS-2, CS-4). The uncertainty is that the O2 cluster may not survive or incorporate at CVT temperatures, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for molecular O2 incorporation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Concentrated-acid oxidizing evaporation

Route 2 has mild thermal severity because structurally/thermodynamically similar sulfates crystallize by evaporation from concentrated H2SO4 at room temperature or low temperature (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3), while CS-1 shows sulfate evaporation at ca. 250 °C. Kinetics are strong because solution crystallization avoids bulk diffusion and enables slow ordering of sulfate frameworks, as shown by evaporation-derived single crystals in SS-1/TS-1 and SS-3/TS-3. The main limitation is chemical rather than kinetic: no CS/SS/TS source proves that an O2 molecule can be trapped in the lattice, so the use of O2/H2O2/O3 is [Reasonable Inference]. The source CS-1 has an internal inconsistency between a ZnSO4 formula and a CuSO4 synthesis snippet, so it is weighted less than direct Zn CVT evidence for Zn-specific chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Dehydration plus oxygen trapping

Route 3 is moderately severe because related anhydrous selenates are obtained by dehydration at 290–340 °C without extreme pressure (CS-3), and sulfate crystallization under dry conditions is reported in SS-5/TS-4. The multi-step nature and potential use of reactive oxygen donors reduce F2 [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, dehydration can produce reactive anhydrous sulfate solids and avoids refractory oxide precursors, but O2 insertion into a crystalline ZnSO4-derived sheet is unverified in CS/SS/TS. This route is therefore plausible but less directly supported than solution crystallization or CVT.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Mild high-O2-pressure solid-gas synthesis

Route 4 has acceptable nominal F2 if pressure is kept below 5 GPa, because CS-5 demonstrates sulfate/selenate high-pressure treatment at 40 kbar and 400 °C. The kinetic score is lower because a dense anhydrous ZnSO4 lattice may not readily incorporate O2 without dissolution or vapor transport, and no CS/SS/TS analogue shows this transformation. The route could be useful if high oxygen chemical potential is the key stabilizing variable [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the desired 2D ZnSO4 + O2-cluster arrangement or instead drives known sulfate polymorphs/oxide sulfate phases, as phase competition is evident in CS-4/TS-2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one metal cation plus sulfur as a sulfate-forming cation, and CS/SS/TS show many crystalline sulfate frameworks despite large cation-size/coordination differences (CS-2, CS-4, SS-1, TS-2). The target’s described cell is not large enough to trigger the >40 atom criterion, based on the target structural description. The network is mainly corner-sharing ZnO4/SO4 plus a molecular O2 unit, so mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing frustration is not triggered by the target description. Remaining uncertainty is that trapped O2 may introduce disorder or partial occupancy during real synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and many thermodynamically similar sulfate analogues are also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The CS and SS data strongly support synthesis of Zn–sulfate frameworks, especially ZnSO4 and Zn3O(SO4)2 by CVT and evaporation/dehydration routes (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, SS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust sulfate tetrahedral chemistry and known crystallization of Zn–sulfate frameworks under both CVT and solution/acid conditions (CS-2, CS-4, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is the target’s uncoordinated O2 cluster, because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates incorporation of molecular O2 into a sulfate lattice. Therefore, low-temperature oxidizing crystallization is preferred over high-temperature CVT, because it is more likely to retain a weakly bound O2 species [Reasonable Inference].
