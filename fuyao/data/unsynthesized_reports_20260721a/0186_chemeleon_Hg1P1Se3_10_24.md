### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has a reported E_hull of 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic stability score. Similar stable compounds include Hg₂P₂S₇ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1), PbPSe₃ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-2), CdPSe₃ at E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1, TS-1), AgIn(PSe₃)₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (SS-2, TS-2), and ScAg(PSe₃)₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (SS-5, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not prove kinetic accessibility or phase purity; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows direct experimental synthesis of HgPSe₃ itself.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses low temperature, so no >1000 °C or high-pressure penalty applies; this is consistent with Hg₂P₂S₇ synthesis at 240 °C in an evacuated ampoule (CS-1). The vacuum/sealed-system and toxic/volatile penalties are justified because Hg–P–chalcogen syntheses use sealed evacuated ampoules and Hg-containing precursors (CS-1, CS-3), while Hg/Se/P volatility and toxicity are [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS specifies this exact low-temperature HgPSe₃ route, so their route-specific severity signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The low-temperature Hg₂P₂S₇ synthesis demonstrates that Hg–P–chalcogen frameworks can form slowly under sealed conditions, likely assisted by chalcogen/vapor transport [Reasonable Inference] (CS-1). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target and Hg₂P₂S₇ both contain HgCh₄ and PCh₄-type units, but the target requires a different stoichiometry and selenide framework (CS-1). The long 4-week anneal in CS-1 reduces the kinetics score because it indicates slow ordering or transport.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct elemental MPSe₃ routes require evacuated ampoules at 300–800 °C over extended times (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1), so the controlled-atmosphere penalty applies. Toxic/volatile precursor and sealed-system penalties are justified for elemental Hg/P/Se by [Reasonable Inference] and by the repeated use of sealed evacuated ampoules in analogous chalcogenide syntheses (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The multi-step penalty applies because analogous MPSe₃ preparations may include grinding, pelletizing, and re-tempering (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental metal/red P/chalcogen routes are well supported for PbPSe₃ and CdPSe₃-type materials (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1), so precursor compatibility is good but not as activated as solution chemistry. Diffusion is not expected to be prohibitive because analogues form at 300–800 °C rather than extreme temperatures (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: many MPSe₃ analogues have octahedral metal coordination, while the target has tetrahedral HgSe₄, although HgSe₄ environments are known in HgAl₂Se₄ and Tl₂HgSiSe₄ (CS-3, SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The melt/Bridgman route uses 700–750 °C and evacuated ampoules, matching selenodiphosphate melt growth conditions (SS-2, TS-2). Vacuum, sealed volatile precursors, multi-step annealing, and toxic Hg handling all penalize the route; the Hg volatility component is a [Reasonable Inference] supported indirectly by Hg-containing sealed-ampoule syntheses (SS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms safe or congruent melting of HgPSe₃ specifically, increasing practical uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The melt route has favorable transport because selenodiphosphates can be crystallized from heated evacuated tubes with controlled cooling and annealing (SS-2, TS-2). Precursor activity is high relative to conventional powder diffusion because a Se-rich melt/vapor phase should improve mixing [Reasonable Inference]. The structural score is limited because SS-2/TS-2 report incongruent melting/metastable phase behavior and require post-annealing, and no CS/SS/TS source confirms the melt behavior of HgPSe₃.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very different cation types: Hg in HgSe₄ tetrahedra and P in PPSe₃ tetrahedra, so cation size/chemistry mismatch is significant [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing tetrahedral connectivity, which can increase reconstruction complexity; analogous layered P–Se frameworks show edge/corner-sharing motifs (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1). The unit cell size criterion is not triggered because insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a >40 atom unit cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HgPSe₃ is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar chalcogenophosphate analogues are also on the hull (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The analogue support is strong for P–P–Se₃ layered chemistry and sealed-ampoule synthesis, especially from CdPSe₃/PbPSe₃ and Ag-containing PSe₃ frameworks (CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The closest chemical enabling factor is the experimental existence of Hg₂P₂S₇, which demonstrates Hg–P–chalcogen tetrahedral framework formation under mild sealed conditions (CS-1). The most important limiting factor is Hg volatility/toxicity and possible stoichiometry drift during sealed-ampoule heating [Reasonable Inference], compounded by the target’s low-symmetry tetrahedral HgSe₄ network. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with the low-temperature HgSe/red P/Se ampoule route preferred over hotter melt routes.
