### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state synthesizability. Chemically similar Sr₃(BS₃)₂, SrPSe₃, GeCa₂S₄, and CaPSe₃ are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1/CS-2/CS-4/CS-5), and thermodynamic analogues LiAsS₂, NaAsS₂, BaAgSbS₃, TlEuPS₄, and SrTlPS₄ are also stable or near-stable (TS-1–TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that a computed hull position does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exclude polymorph competition [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The main severity comes from vacuum-sealed ampoule operation and toxic/air-sensitive sulfide/arsenic reagents, both consistent with analogous sealed Sr sulfide syntheses (CS-1, TS-5). Kinetically, the binary-sulfide route is favorable because SrS and As₂S₃ already contain the required Sr–S and As–S bonds [Reasonable Inference], and related Sr sulfides form at 750–850 °C (CS-1/CS-2/CS-5). Uncertainty is the exact P1 ordering and the possibility of competing SrS/As₂S₃-derived phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct elemental synthesis is supported by SrPSe₃/CaPSe₃ elemental ampoule reactions at 750 °C for 60 h (CS-2/CS-5), but it is penalized for staged heating, volatile As/S, and reactive Sr metal [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because the reaction must first form binary sulfide intermediates before the ternary framework assembles [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is vapor transport/segregation of sulfur and arsenic during initial reaction [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth is strongly supported structurally by LiEuAsS₃, which contains trigonal AsS₃ and seven-coordinate large-cation sulfide environments similar to the target (SS-1). The flux should improve ion transport and crystal growth at 600–700 °C [Reasonable Inference], but SS-1 reports low yield and failed pure-phase attempts, so the route is penalized for phase control. Remaining uncertainty is whether alkali flux will incorporate into or compete with the Sr₂As₂S₅ phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Hydrazine solvothermal synthesis is mild in temperature and is supported by BaAgSbS₃ formation at 160 °C for 7 days (SS-4/TS-3). However, the target has no direct Sr–As–S solvothermal analogue in the supplied data, so transferability is a significant extrapolation. The major uncertainty is whether Sr²⁺ and arsenic sulfide species reach the correct solubility/reactivity balance without producing amorphous sulfide or soluble thioarsenate salts [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines large Sr-centered SrS₆/SrS₇ polyhedra with smaller trigonal AsS₃ units, giving one amorphization-risk trigger from cation size disparity [Reasonable Inference from the target structural description]. The primitive target description contains only 9 inequivalent atoms and reports corner/edge sharing but no face-sharing, so the other two checks are not triggered. Structurally similar sulfides crystallize successfully despite mixed coordination networks (SS-1–SS-5), reducing concern.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₂As₂S₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom. The supplied analogues strongly support synthesis of Sr sulfides in sealed ampoules (CS-1, TS-5) and As–S trigonal-pyramidal frameworks in alkali arsenic sulfides (SS-2/SS-3; TS-1/TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is that a simple binary-sulfide stoichiometry, 2 SrS + As₂S₃, exactly matches Sr₂As₂S₅ [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is volatile/toxic As–S chemistry in sealed ampoules, which can cause pressure, stoichiometry drift, or competing arsenic sulfide phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite plausible synthetically, with the best first attempt being a sealed-ampoule binary-sulfide reaction followed by slow cooling.
