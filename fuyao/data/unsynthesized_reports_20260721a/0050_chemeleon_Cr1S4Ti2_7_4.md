### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Chemically/structurally adjacent Ti₂CoS₄ and V₂CrS₄ are also near the hull, with E_hull values of 0.016 and 0.035 eV/atom, respectively (CS1, SS1, CS3), and several thermodynamic analogues are exactly on the hull (TS1, TS2, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field, especially in mixed Ti/Cr sulfides where cation ordering and sulfur chemical potential can matter [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct elemental solid-state reaction

Route 1 receives a moderate severity score because the best analogues use evacuated silica tubes, sulfur, and repeated high-temperature firings up to 1000 °C (CS1, CS3). No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is required by the closest CS/SS evidence (CS1, SS1, CS3), but vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur impose clear penalties. The uncertainty is whether Ti₂CrS₄ requires exactly the same staged 600/800/1000 °C program as Ti₂CoS₄ or a narrower sulfur chemical potential window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct elemental solid-state reaction

Kinetics are limited by the use of elemental Ti and Cr, which are refractory and likely oxide-passivated [Reasonable Inference], even though CS1 and CS3 show direct elemental routes are feasible. The structural reconstruction score is helped by the target’s close match to Ti₂CoS₄ and V₂CrS₄ C2/m octahedral frameworks (CS1, SS1, CS3). Repeated grinding/firing in those analogues indicates slow homogenization and ordering, so F3 is not high (CS1, CS3).

#### F2 — Route 2: Binary sulfide-assisted solid-state reaction

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it still uses evacuated ampoules and sulfur-bearing reagents at high temperature (CS2). It avoids no major equipment burden: evacuated quartz ampoules and furnaces are standard for the Fe–Ti–S and Ti–M–S examples (CS1, CS2). Insufficient evidence from TS for Ti–Cr–S-specific sulfide precursor safety or sensitivity is treated neutrally.

#### F3 — Route 2: Binary sulfide-assisted solid-state reaction

The use of TiS₂ is directly supported in Fe–Ti–S synthesis at 900 °C (CS2), and it should improve precursor activity relative to elemental Ti [Reasonable Inference]. However, repeated firings were usually required for complete reaction in the TiS₂-based analogue system (CS2), so cation diffusion and ordering remain limiting. Because the final Ti₂CrS₄ structure differs from simple TiS₂, some reconstruction is required [Reasonable Inference], but the same evacuated-tube chemistry is well supported by CS1–CS3.

#### F2 — Route 3: High-temperature anneal / sulfur-buffered ordering

Route 3 retains the same sealed-tube/vacuum/volatile sulfur penalties as the other routes, and it is inherently multi-step because it starts from pre-reacted material and uses extended reannealing (CS1, CS4, SS2). Long annealing near 990–1000 °C is supported by Ti₄GaS₈ and Ti₂CoS₄ analogues (SS2, TS3, CS1). No high-pressure or >1400 °C condition is supported or required by the analogues.

#### F3 — Route 3: High-temperature anneal / sulfur-buffered ordering

This route should be effective for improving ordering if the correct framework has already formed [Reasonable Inference], and same-framework Ti₂CoS₄ strongly supports the structural target (CS1, SS1). Kinetics remain only moderate because several analogues require long anneals, regrinding, or recycling until single phase (CS1, CS4, SS2). The main uncertainty is whether annealing will transform competing phases into Ti₂CrS₄ or merely equilibrate to a different Ti–Cr–S assemblage [Reasonable Inference].
