### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is near the maximum by the required rubric. This is reinforced by Hg3S2I2 at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS3, TS1), Hg3Se2I2 at E_hull = 0.0 (SS4, TS2), and Hg–S–Br–Cl at E_hull ≈ 0.00145 eV/atom (SS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the specific cubic I2_13 polymorph will be the experimentally preferred polymorph, especially because related Br/Cl phases show polymorphism (SS1, TS3).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperature and simple sealed-ampoule heating, matching the 260 °C, 2–3 day Hg3S2I2 synthesis (CS1, SS3, TS1). The main deductions are for vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic Hg halide handling, and mercury-compound toxicity (CS1, SS1, TS1/TS3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any need for high pressure or >1000 °C treatment, so those items are scored as non-applicable/neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics should be favorable because the analogous Hg3S2I2 phase forms at only 260 °C from binary precursors in 2–3 days (CS1, SS3, TS1). The target’s Hg–S distance of 2.46 Å matches close analogues with Hg–S ≈ 2.42–2.46 Å, and its Hg–Br distance is within the range of Hg–Br contacts in Hg–S–Br–Cl analogues (CS1, SS1, TS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether Br substitution favors the cubic target or a competing lower-symmetry bromide polymorph, since polymorphism is reported in Br/Cl analogues (SS1, TS3).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it uses 500–540 °C, a temperature gradient, repeated sublimation/distillation, and evacuated quartz ampoules (SS1, TS3). It still avoids high pressure and ultrahigh temperature, which SS1/TS3 do not require. The main burden is operational: CVT requires precise gradient control and handles volatile toxic Hg halides (SS1, SS2, TS3).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Vapor transport can overcome solid-state diffusion limits because close Hg–S–Br–Cl and Hg–S–I–Cl analogues are grown from the gas phase (SS1, SS2, TS3). Crystallization is strongly supported by the very high structural similarity of SS1 to the target and by similar Hg–S/Hg–Br coordination environments (SS1, TS3). Composition control is the weak point because the closest Br-containing analogue contains mixed Br/Cl occupation and reports polymorphous modifications (SS1, TS3).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses standard evacuated ampoule annealing, similar to long-duration Hg–Se–Br/I, Hg–Te–Br, and AgHgSBr/AgHgSI syntheses (SS5, TS4, TS5). It is penalized for vacuum, volatile HgBr2, toxicity, and multistep/long thermal programming (SS5, TS4, TS5). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that pressure above sealed-tube autogenous pressure is required, so high pressure is not penalized.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Long annealing can help approach equilibrium, as shown by 400 h anneals in Hg–Se–Br/I systems and one-week anneals in AgHgSBr/AgHgSI systems (SS5, TS5). However, high-temperature annealing may increase vapor redistribution and can broaden the phase space, making polymorph and stoichiometry control harder than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS3 polymorphism and SS5 long annealing]. The kinetic score is therefore good but not as high as the direct low-temperature route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, so cation-radius mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. Similar Hg–S–halide and Hg–chalcogenide–halide materials are experimentally crystalline under ampoule/CVT conditions (CS1, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5), which argues against a strong amorphization tendency. The remaining uncertainty is the exact target unit-cell size and connectivity; CS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for those target-specific F4 checks, so they are not used to trigger additional penalties.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hg3(SBr)2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple Hg–chalcogenide–halide analogues are also on or very near the hull (CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The synthesis is well supported by chemical similarity to Hg3S2I2, structural similarity to Hg–S–Br–Cl frameworks, and thermodynamic similarity to Hg3S2I2/Hg3Se2I2/Hg–S–Br–Cl phases (CS1, SS1, SS3, TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated low-temperature formation of closely related Hg3Q2X2 frameworks in evacuated sealed ampoules (CS1, SS3, TS1). The main limiting factor is phase/polymorph and halogen stoichiometry control, because the closest Br-containing analogue shows mixed-halide occupancy and polymorphous behavior (SS1, TS3). Overall, the target appears substantially synthesizable, with the direct sealed-ampoule HgS + HgBr2 route preferred for minimizing thermal decomposition and Br loss [Reasonable Inference from CS1/SS1/TS1/TS3].
