### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, so it is scored as a predicted ground-state phase in the highest stability bin. Chemically similar ternary sulfides with E_hull around 0.06–0.083 eV/atom are still reported as synthesized, so the target’s thermodynamic position is favorable relative to those analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). Thermodynamic analogues include several E_hull = 0.0 sulfides made experimentally, which supports that low-hull sulfides are synthetically accessible (TS-1 through TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a unique low-temperature ordering of Mn/Co over spinel sites [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state route

The severity score is reduced because analogous solid-state sulfides require vacuum-sealed tubes and sulfur-containing volatile feeds (CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, SS-3). Route 1 also inherits multi-step grinding, pelletizing, heating, and recycling from FeCr₂S₄-type synthesis protocols (CS-2, SS-2). Kinetically, the route is plausible because AB₂S₄ sulfides form at 700–1000 °C in sealed ampoules (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4), but dense elemental precursors and repeated annealing indicate moderate diffusion limitations (CS-2, SS-2). TS provides only indirect support through sealed-ampoule sulfide sintering at 1000 °C (TS-4), while high-pressure TS examples are not needed for this route.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: flux / high-temperature solution route

The route is less severe than high-pressure synthesis because the closest spinel analogue CoCr₂S₄ is grown at 780 °C in evacuated silica, with slow cooling and no external pressure (CS-1, SS-5). It still incurs penalties for vacuum sealing, volatile chalcogen control, and multi-step flux handling, all reflected in the CoCr₂S₄ and flux-sulfide precedents (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). The kinetic score is highest because molten flux/solution growth should improve cation transport and crystallinity compared with direct ceramic diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the closest analogue has the same spinel framework with tetrahedral Co and octahedral transition-metal sulfide network (CS-1, SS-5). The main uncertainty is whether a Cd-free flux will dissolve Mn/Co sulfides sufficiently, because the exact CoCr₂S₄ precedent used Cd-containing solvent chemistry (CS-1, SS-5).

#### F2/F3 Route 3: high-pressure route

High-pressure synthesis receives a low F2 score because SS and TS analogues require 2–8.9 GPa, specialized anvil devices, quenching, and sometimes CS₂ extraction (SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). The method can suppress sulfur volatility and access dense sulfide phases [Reasonable Inference], but the closest chemical/spinel analogues do not require high pressure, so CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure necessity and is treated neutrally for this route. Kinetically, high pressure can promote reaction but may not provide the slow cation equilibration needed for spinel site ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the desired spinel or diverts the composition into pyrite/monoclinic sulfide polymorphs, as structurally similar high-pressure sulfides are not spinels (SS-4, TS-2, TS-3).

#### F4: amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline spinel analogue of known crystalline CoCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ spinels, so the structural family itself is not strongly glass-forming (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5). The unit-cell size criterion likely triggers because conventional Fd-3m spinels contain more than 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference from spinel structure type in CS-1/CS-3/SS-5]. The target has corner- and edge-sharing polyhedral connectivity but no stated face-sharing, so the highest amorphization connectivity penalty is not triggered by the target description or close spinel analogues (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5). TS provides insufficient direct amorphization evidence and is treated as neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂CoS₄ is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, which is more favorable than several experimentally synthesized chemically similar sulfides (CS-1 through CS-4). The strongest synthetic support comes from spinel CoCr₂S₄ and FeCr₂S₄ analogues with the same Fd-3m AB₂S₄ topology and comparable octahedral/tetrahedral sulfide coordination (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5). The most important enabling factor is the robust spinel sulfide framework, since close Co/Fe/Cr sulfide spinels are already synthesized by both solid-state and solution-growth routes (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5). The main limiting factor is phase purity and site ordering in a Mn–Co sulfide system, because analogous solid-state sulfides often require repeated grinding/annealing and careful stoichiometry control (CS-2, SS-2). Overall, the target appears moderately to highly synthesizable, with flux growth or sealed-ampoule annealing being more appropriate than high-pressure synthesis.
