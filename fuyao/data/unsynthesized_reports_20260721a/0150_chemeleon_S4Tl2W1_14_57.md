### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability scoring range. TS provides strong support because the nearest thermodynamic analogues NbTl₃S₄, Tl₃TaSe₄, Tl₃VS₄, Rb₂WS₄, and Tl₃Cu₃Ta₂S₈ are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV atom⁻¹ (TS1–TS5). CS and SS also show multiple stable Tl–transition-metal chalcogenides and a stable W thiotungstate analogue (CS1, CS2, SS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or uniqueness of the low-temperature phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, direct sealed-ampoule reaction.**  
The severity score is reduced mainly by vacuum sealing, volatile Tl/S, and Tl toxicity; sealed evacuated tubes are explicitly used for Tl₃VS₄ and Tl₃Cu₃Ta₂S₈ (CS2, SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5). Kinetics are the main limitation because direct reaction from elemental W requires transport from a refractory metal [Reasonable Inference]. The method is supported by direct synthesis analogues, but inhomogeneity in the Tl₃Cu₃Ta₂S₈ product indicates phase and crystallinity challenges (SS5, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a directly reported Tl₂WS₄ powder synthesis, so extrapolation is required.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, reactive sulfide flux.**  
The flux route has moderate condition severity because it requires sealed tubes, controlled atmosphere, toxic Tl sulfides, and multiple handling steps [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the glovebox/sealed fused-silica procedure for Rb₂WS₄ (SS4, TS4). Kinetically, it is more favorable than direct solid-state synthesis because the Rb₂WS₄ analogue demonstrates W incorporation into tetrahedral WS₄ at only 923 K via reactive flux (SS4, TS4). The target’s W–S distances are close to Rb₂WS₄, supporting structural transferability (SS4, TS4, target description). The uncertainty is whether Tl₂Sₓ behaves as a sufficiently benign flux without forming competing Tl sulfides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, Bridgman–Stockbarger melt growth.**  
The Bridgman route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile components, toxicity, and multi-step growth control; these requirements are explicit in Tl₃BX₄ Bridgman analogues (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). Kinetically, melt growth improves long-range transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], explaining the relatively high F3 despite refractory W concerns. However, phase control is difficult because the analogues show strong supercooling, stoichiometry sensitivity, and impurity inclusions (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for congruent melting of Tl₂WS₄ specifically, so the route is promising but risky.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target’s simple 14-atom P1 cell and well-defined W–S tetrahedra lower amorphization risk, while the large Tl/W size and coordination mismatch creates one trigger [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 shows that related W sulfide frameworks can crystallize as ordered materials, and CS/SS/TS include several crystalline Tl chalcogenides (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The target is low symmetry and 1D, which may broaden the crystallization window less than high-symmetry analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists to assert mixed face/edge/corner sharing in the target, so C3 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl₂WS₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The analogue support is good but not perfect: Tl₃VS₄/NbTl₃S₄ support Tl–M–S tetrahedral chemistry (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3), while Rb₂WS₄ specifically supports stable tetrahedral WS₄ chemistry (SS4, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for crystalline thallium thiometallates and thiotungstates in sealed-tube or flux environments (CS1–CS3, SS4, TS4). The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under Tl/S volatility, especially because related Tl₃BX₄ growth shows supercooling and impurity inclusions (CS1, SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The best practical approach is likely to start with powder/flux screening, then use Bridgman or slow-cooling growth after the composition window is established [Reasonable Inference].
