### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a near-maximum thermodynamic score. This is consistent with TS, where several thermodynamically similar halide/chalcogen-halide materials are also on the hull, including Zr(SeCl6)2 and Hf(SeCl6)2 (TS-1, TS-2). CS also includes several E_hull = 0.0 chemically similar compounds, supporting that chloride-rich chalcogen/metal salts can be stable phases (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to hydrolysis; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct environmental stability data for FeSeCl7.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses mild temperatures of 140–155 °C, which is strongly favorable relative to high-temperature solid-state synthesis (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1). However, the route requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile SeCl4/metal chlorides, and air-sensitive handling, all explicitly present in close analogues (CS-3, CS-5; TS-2). The score is therefore reduced mainly by vacuum/sealed-system and precursor sensitivity penalties. The uncertainty is Fe-specific: CS/SS/TS show Zr/Hf/Mo but not Fe.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because related SeCl4 + metal chloride reactions form crystals over days at only 140–155 °C (CS-3; SS-1). Vapor transport and halide redistribution bypass slow cation diffusion in refractory solids [Reasonable Inference based on CS-3/SS-1]. Structural reconstruction is not trivial because the target is 1D P1 with tetrahedral FeCl4 units, whereas closest CVT analogues are mainly 0D or different metal coordination environments (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5; SS-1). This justifies a high but not maximum F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has the mildest severity because CS-2 demonstrates chloride salt synthesis at room temperature or reflux under dry conditions rather than sealed evacuated ampoules. It still requires toxic, moisture-sensitive chloride reagents and a multi-step crystallization workflow, as described in CS-2. No high temperature, high pressure, or vacuum penalty is applied for the preferred ambient-pressure solution variant. The uncertainty is that CS-2 is chemically similar but not Se–Fe-specific.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution-phase chloride assembly should have fast kinetics because CS-2 reports solution preparation, mixing, filtering, and spectroscopic sampling under drybox conditions on minutes-to-hours timescales. Diffusion barriers are low in solution, so ion transport is not expected to limit formation [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic risk is nucleation of the exact 1D FeSeCl7 ribbon rather than simpler molecular chloroferrate or selenium chloride adducts; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for that specific ribbon topology. This route receives the highest total score because its condition severity is substantially lower.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures, 140–150 °C, matching SS-2 and CS-4. Its deductions come from evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile SOCl2/halide reagents, and toxic/moisture-sensitive chemistry, all indicated by SS-2 and CS-4. It is more severe than Route 2 because of sealed autogenous pressure. CS-4 also warns that solvothermal chalcogen-halide chemistry can require specialized separation and handling.

#### F3 — Route 3

SS-2 is a strong kinetic precedent because a structurally similar MoSeOCl7 cluster forms nearly quantitatively in sealed SOCl2 at 150 °C in 3 days. The SOCl2 medium likely enhances chloride mobility and maintains a dehydrating/chlorinating environment [Reasonable Inference from SS-2]. However, Fe substitution and conversion from a Mo–O-containing cluster precedent to FeSeCl7 is nontrivial; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct Fe–Se–Cl solvothermal evidence. The score is high but limited by composition and phase-selectivity uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a low-dimensional chloride with small formula size and discrete/bridged molecular motifs, which generally lowers amorphization risk compared with large multication oxide networks [Reasonable Inference]. SS analogues are reported as crystalline molecular clusters with resolved structures, including Mo(SeCl6)2 and MoSeOCl7, supporting crystallizability of related chloride frameworks (SS-1, SS-2). The formal F4 checks do not clearly trigger: no >40 atom cell is indicated, no clear mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence is reported, and the ionic-radius criterion is poorly applicable to a Fe/Se covalent chloride. Direct amorphization data are absent, so CS/SS/TS are insufficient on this exact factor and are treated as neutral where not informative.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeSeCl7 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS includes multiple related halide/chalcogen-halide phases also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The analogue support is strong for chloride-rich Se–metal chemistry using SeCl4 and metal chlorides at low temperature, especially from CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, and TS-1/TS-2. The most critical enabling factor is the high activity and mobility of molecular chloride precursors, which allows crystalline products at 140–155 °C or even solution/reflux conditions (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2). The main limiting factor is absence of a direct Fe–Se–Cl synthesis precedent in CS/SS/TS, so competing FeCl3/FeCl4− salts or selenium chloride phases may form [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical first attempt is dry solution crystallization from FeCl3 and SeCl4, followed by sealed-ampoule CVT optimization if crystals do not form.
