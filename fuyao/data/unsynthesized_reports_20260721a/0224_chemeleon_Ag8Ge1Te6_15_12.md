### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. Multiple close analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including AgInTe₂ (CS1/TS5), AgGaTe₂ (CS3/TS4), AgTlTe (SS1/TS1), RbAg₃Te₂ (SS4/TS3), and Ag₈SnSe₆ (CS5/SS2/TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee that this specific low-symmetry P1 ordering is experimentally accessible without slow annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Sealed ampoule synthesis is strongly supported by AgInTe₂, AgIn₅Te₈, and Ag₈SnSe₆ analogues made from elements in evacuated quartz or quartz containers (CS1, CS2, CS5/SS2/TS2). Severity is moderate because vacuum sealing, Te volatility, and multi-step annealing are required (CS1/CS2/CS5). Kinetics are acceptable because Ag–Te/Ge–Te binaries can react through partial melt pathways [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s eight Ag sites imply slow ordering (Target; CS5/SS2/TS2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
High-temperature fusion is directly supported by 1050 °C Ag–In–Te analogues (CS1/CS2/TS5), so kinetics are good after melt homogenization. The severity penalty is larger because the proposed temperature exceeds 1000 °C and Te vapor pressure requires sealed tubes [CS1/CS2; Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty is whether quenching preserves or suppresses the fully ordered triclinic P1 target [Reasonable Inference; SS1/TS1 reports Ag non-stoichiometry and polymorphic difficulties in AgTlTe].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth is supported by chalcogenide flux synthesis of related tellurides with slow cooling (SS3/TS3), and slow crystallization is also important for AgTlTe (SS1/TS1). This route scores well because it lowers temperature while maintaining liquid-mediated diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS example directly reports Ag–Ge–Te flux growth, so Ge incorporation and target phase selection remain extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Supercritical ethylenediamine synthesis is supported for RbAg₃Te₂ at 300 °C and 2400 psi Ar (SS4/TS3). The route has low thermal severity but added pressure/solvent complexity and uncertain Ge chemistry; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly supports Ge-containing product formation by this route. Kinetics may be favorable due to solution transport [Reasonable Inference], but structural fidelity to Ag₈GeTe₆ is less certain than for melt/flux routes.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a significant Ag/Ge cation size and coordination mismatch, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The 15-atom formula and lack of explicit mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity avoid the other two triggers [Target]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for Ag₈GeTe₆, so the F4 score relies mainly on target structural descriptors and reasonable crystal-chemistry inference.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag₈GeTe₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong chemically and structurally: Ag–Te compounds are repeatedly synthesized by evacuated ampoule fusion, slow cooling, or flux methods (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS3), and Ag₈SnSe₆ provides a close Ag₈MCh₆ structural/chemical precedent (CS5/SS2/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is liquid-mediated transport in Te-rich melt/flux, which should overcome slow solid-state diffusion and help equilibrate Ag/Ge/Te stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference supported by SS3/TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Te volatility and possible Ag non-stoichiometry, as seen in AgTlTe growth (SS1/TS1). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires sealed-tube stoichiometry control and slow annealing rather than a short, open solid-state firing.
