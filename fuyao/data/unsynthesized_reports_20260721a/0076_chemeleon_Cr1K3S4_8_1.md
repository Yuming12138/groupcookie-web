### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase by the provided target thermodynamic data. Many chemically and thermodynamically related K chalcogenides are also on the hull, including K₂TiS₃, K₃GaS₃, K₂MoS₄, and K₃AlSe₃ (CS1–CS4), as well as K₂VAgS₄, Rb₂VAgS₄, Rb₂LiVS₄, RbK₂BiSe₃, and K₂VCuS₄ (TS1–TS5). SS analogues are slightly metastable but close to the hull, with several Co sulfides/selenides at ~0.02–0.05 eV/atom (SS1–SS4), which does not contradict feasible synthesis because these analogues are experimentally reported. The remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee a wide kinetic formation window for Cr(V) sulfide chemistry; that concern is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate-low F2 score because direct K chalcogenide synthesis typically uses evacuated sealed ampoules, high temperature around 750–800 °C, long annealing, and controlled cooling (CS1, CS2, CS4). The route is not penalized for >1000 °C or high pressure because analogues use 1025–1075 K, not >1000 °C, and no high-pressure synthesis is reported (CS1, CS2, CS4). It is penalized for vacuum, sealed volatile K/S-containing reagents, multi-step K₂S handling, and air-sensitive sulfide handling (CS1, CS3). The main uncertainty is whether Cr metal reacts completely under these conditions, which is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct solid-state kinetics are only moderate because the K/S components are reactive, but Cr metal is refractory and may diffuse slowly; the refractory-metal limitation is a [Reasonable Inference]. Related direct solid-state syntheses require multi-day annealing at 1025–1070 K (CS1, CS2), which supports the need for extended time. The target’s CrS₄ tetrahedral unit is locally analogous to MoS₄/VS₄/CoS₄ tetrahedra (CS3, SS1, SS5, TS1), but the exact Fmm2 K₃CrS₄ framework is not demonstrated. Therefore, structural reconstruction risk remains moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The flux route avoids high temperature because related K₂VAgS₄ and Rb₂VAgS₄ are made at 350 °C, and Rb₂LiVS₄-type phases at 773 K (SS5, TS1–TS3). It is still penalized for evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile sulfur/polysulfide media, multi-step drybox/sealing/heating/washing operations, and air-sensitive reagents/products (SS5, TS1–TS3). CS gives insufficient direct flux evidence for K₃CrS₄, so CS is treated as neutral for this route. Overall severity is moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Polysulfide fluxes are kinetically favorable because they provide a reactive sulfur-rich liquid/transport medium, as shown by K₂VAgS₄ and Rb₂VAgS₄ formation at 350 °C (SS5, TS1, TS2). Flux synthesis reduces long-range diffusion barriers relative to direct solid-state reaction, which is supported by low-temperature successful crystallization in SS5 and TS1–TS3. The local tetrahedral VS₄ units in flux analogues are a strong structural signal for forming tetrahedral CrS₄ units (SS5, TS1–TS3), but Cr-specific redox behavior is not evidenced. The remaining uncertainty is whether Cr(V) is stabilized in the same sulfur-rich flux; that is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The solvothermal route has the best F2 score because K₂MoS₄ forms at only 443 K for 2 d in methanol, far milder than high-temperature solid-state routes (CS3). It is penalized for sealed-tube operation, multi-step K₂S handling, and air-sensitive sulfide reagents (CS3). SS and TS provide insufficient direct solvothermal evidence, so they are neutral for route-specific condition severity. No high pressure beyond autogenous sealed-tube pressure is evidenced in CS3.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

K₂MoS₄ formation from MoO₃ and K₂S in methanol at 443 K indicates a favorable low-temperature pathway to tetrahedral thiometallate units (CS3). Solution-mediated chemistry greatly reduces cation diffusion constraints relative to direct solid-state reaction; this interpretation follows from the low synthesis temperature and short 2 d duration in CS3. The structural analogy between MoS₄ tetrahedra and target CrS₄ tetrahedra is strong at the local coordination level (CS3), although no Cr analogue is supplied. The biggest uncertainty is Cr redox chemistry in sulfide/methanol media, which is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Gas sulfidation is supported by KFeS₂ synthesis in H₂S at 1000 K and K/Co sulfide synthesis in H₂/chalcogen streams at 1030 K (CS5, SS1–SS4). The route is not high-pressure and does not exceed 1000 °C, so those penalties do not apply (CS5, SS1–SS4). It is penalized for reactive/toxic gas atmosphere, multi-step gas-flow/post-treatment handling, and air-sensitive products (CS5, SS1–SS4). This route is operationally more hazardous than Routes 2–3 because of H₂S/H₂ use; hazard interpretation is a [Reasonable Inference] based on the reported atmospheres.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Gas sulfidation can be kinetically effective because related KFeS₂ and K/Co sulfides form in 4–10 h at 1000–1030 K (CS5, SS1–SS4). However, carbonate/metal gas sulfidation is still a solid-state process and may suffer from incomplete Cr conversion; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. The structural match is weaker because the reported gas-route products are KFeS₂/KCoS₂/K₆CoS₄-type phases rather than K₃CrS₄ (CS5, SS1–SS4). Reducing H₂/H₂S atmospheres may also oppose stabilization of formal Cr(V), which is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, K and Cr, so the cation-size mismatch check is triggered by [Reasonable Inference]. This mismatch is not prohibitive because many related K–transition-metal sulfides with large alkali/transition-metal size differences are crystalline and experimentally synthesized (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The unit-cell-size and mixed-connectivity checks are not triggered because the target description does not provide evidence for >40 atoms per cell or coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedra; CS/SS/TS are insufficient for those target-specific checks. Thus amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₃CrS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because the provided target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/thermodynamically similar K chalcogenides are also on or near the hull (CS1–CS4, TS1–TS5, SS1–SS5). The analogue support is strong for K–S frameworks and tetrahedral transition-metal sulfide units, especially K₂MoS₄ and K₂VAgS₄-type compounds (CS3, SS5, TS1–TS3). The most enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature sulfide/thiometallate chemistry through K₂S solvothermal synthesis or polysulfide fluxes, which avoids the slow kinetics of Cr metal diffusion (CS3, SS5, TS1–TS3). The most limiting factor is Cr redox control, because K₃CrS₄ formally requires high-valent Cr in a sulfide environment, and no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates a K–Cr–S analogue; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with solvothermal screening followed by polysulfide flux optimization as the best practical strategy.
