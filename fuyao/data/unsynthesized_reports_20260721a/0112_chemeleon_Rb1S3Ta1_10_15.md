### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin by the required rubric. Multiple related compounds also lie on the hull, including Rb₂WS₄ (CS-1), RbBaTaS₄ (SS-1, TS-1), KBaNbS₄ (SS-2, TS-2), and Rb₂TaAgS₄ (SS-4, TS-4), which supports the thermodynamic plausibility of alkali tantalum sulfides. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Rb–Ta–S polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for controlled atmosphere/vacuum, volatile sulfur in a sealed system, multi-step handling, and air-sensitive alkali sulfide/polysulfide reagents, all consistent with Rb₂WS₄ and RbBaTaS₄ flux syntheses (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). It is not penalized for high temperature or high pressure because the relevant analogues form at 500 °C or 923 K under sealed-tube conditions rather than >1000 °C or GPa pressure (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The main uncertainty is whether the target RbTaS₃ phase forms directly or competes with Rb₂TaS₄-like/other thiotantalate compositions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Flux growth improves precursor activity and transport because Rb₂S₃/S flux is explicitly described as effective for metal chalcogenides in Rb₂WS₄ synthesis and produces RbBaTaS₄ from Ta at 500 °C (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Kinetics are still only moderate because Ta metal is refractory and target TaS₅ edge-sharing polyhedra differ from the tetrahedral TaS₄ environments seen in the closest analogues (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). The flux route is therefore the most credible but still requires optimization of Ta dissolution and Rb:Ta:S stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The sealed-tube solid-state route uses moderate temperature and no high pressure, consistent with one-week, 670 K vacuum-sealed chalcogenide syntheses in SS-3–SS-5 and TS-3–TS-5. It is penalized for vacuum, sulfur volatility, precursor preparation, and air-sensitive alkali sulfides, similar to inert/sealed handling in the related flux and sealed-tube examples (CS-1, SS-1, SS-3–SS-5, TS-1, TS-3–TS-5). The provided SS/TS precursor listings for some analogues appear formula-inconsistent, so they are weighted mainly as evidence for sealed-tube processing rather than exact chemical transfer.

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct solid-state kinetics are poor because Ta metal is refractory and, without flux, long-range diffusion and structural reconstruction are expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The SS/TS sealed-tube analogues involve one-week holds and controlled cooling, supporting that long anneals are needed even for related chalcogenides (SS-3–SS-5, TS-3–TS-5). The target’s TaS₅ edge-sharing geometry is not the same as the tetrahedral TaS₄ motifs in Rb₂TaAgS₄ or RbBaTaS₄, adding reconstruction difficulty (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 3

Hydrothermal conditions are mild in temperature and pressure, as shown by Cs₂MoS₄ formation at 423 K for 24 h in a sealed quartz ampoule (CS-3). The route is penalized for sealed sulfide/polysulfide chemistry, likely precursor-generation steps, and toxic/air-sensitive sulfide reagents [Reasonable Inference from CS-3]. There is insufficient direct evidence from SS and TS for hydrothermal Rb–Ta–S formation, so the mildness is not enough to make it the preferred route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution-mediated hydrothermal chemistry can improve ion transport, as Cs₂MoS₄ crystallizes under mild hydrothermal conditions from Mo/polysulfide/Cs acetate (CS-3). However, Ta sulfide solubility and thiotantalate speciation are not demonstrated in the supplied analogues, and the target’s TaS₅ edge-sharing structure is not directly matched by the MoS₄ tetrahedral analogue (CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence for hydrothermal kinetics, so the score remains moderate despite mild conditions.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The largest amorphization risk is the large cation size/charge mismatch between Rb and Ta [Reasonable Inference], though related crystalline Rb–Ta sulfides show that such mismatch can be accommodated (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity; CS/SS/TS do not provide direct target-specific evidence for these checks, so they are not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that low-symmetry P-1 structures can sometimes have narrow crystallization windows [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbTaS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally related alkali transition-metal sulfides are also on the hull (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1, SS-4, TS-4). The strongest synthesis support comes from RbBaTaS₄, a directly related Rb–Ta–S phase grown from Ta/Rb₂S₃/S flux at 500 °C (SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is a reactive alkali polysulfide/sulfur flux, which can dissolve/reconstruct refractory Ta into crystalline thiotantalate motifs at moderate temperature (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: target RbTaS₃ has TaS₅ edge-sharing trigonal bipyramids, whereas many analogues contain tetrahedral TaS₄/MoS₄/WS₄ units (CS-1, SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful Rb:S chemical potential and slow-cooling optimization.
