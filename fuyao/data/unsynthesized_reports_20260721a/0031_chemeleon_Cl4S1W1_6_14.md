### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a high thermodynamic score. TS analogues include hull-stable W₂NCl₈, NbOCl₃, BaUI₆, and HfI₄, showing that low-dimensional metal halide phases can be computed as stable in related chemical spaces (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for a molecular P1 phase with volatile chlorine/sulfur components [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses only moderate temperatures because W₂NCl₈ forms at 240 °C and WCl₆ + Te chemistry proceeds at 170–190 °C (CS2, CS5, TS1). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile chloride/chalcogen components, and air-sensitive WCl₆ handling, all of which are supported by sealed/protective-gas analogue procedures (CS2, CS5, SS3, TS1). Insufficient evidence from SS for direct W–S sealed-ampoule chemistry makes sulfur transfer an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory solid-state synthesis because WCl₆ is a reactive molecular precursor and the analogues form at 170–240 °C, far below temperatures typical for tungsten diffusion in dense solids (CS2, CS5, TS1). The pathway likely proceeds through molecular/vapor species rather than long-range cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction: the target is 5-coordinate WSCl₄, while W₂NCl₈ and WOCl₄ analogues are mostly octahedral/chain-like (CS2, SS3, SS4).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is thermally mild because WOCl₄ sublimation/crystal growth occurs at 130–140 °C and WCl₆ + Te chemistry occurs at 170–190 °C (SS4, CS5). It is more operationally severe than Route 1 because a temperature gradient, sublimation/transport step, and volatile species control are required (CS5, SS4). Insufficient evidence from TS for a direct WSCl₄ vapor-transport route adds uncertainty, so the route is scored more conservatively.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Vapor mobility is plausible because WCl₆-based chalcogen chloride chemistry and WOCl₄ sublimation both work at low temperatures (CS5, SS4). Crystallization by transport is supported by well-developed crystals in related systems (CS5, SS4). The limiting kinetic/compositional factor is the narrow chemical-potential window, since CS5 reports reversible/temperature-sensitive behavior and WCl₄ side-product risk.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest temperature requirement because SS1 reports WCl₆ reacting in CH₂Cl₂ at room temperature over several weeks. The route still requires inert/dry handling because WCl₆-based analogues use protective gas or sealed ampoules, and tungsten oxychlorides are hydrolysis sensitive (CS2, SS1, SS4, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS and TS for a direct sulfur-transfer solution route is the main reason no higher F2 is assigned.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and avoids solid-state diffusion, supported by SS1 where WCl₆ forms crystalline W–N–Cl material in dichloromethane at room temperature. The target’s zero-dimensional molecular nature is compatible with solution crystallization [Reasonable Inference], and discrete molecular/cluster products are seen in CS3 and CS5. The uncertainty is that sulfur-donor identity and chloride balance are not provided by CS/SS/TS, so optimization would be necessary [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, a very small formula unit, and no extended mixed polyhedral connectivity, so the amorphization risk is low. Zero-dimensional molecular chloride/chalcogenide or chloride/nitrido clusters are represented among the analogues, supporting crystallizable discrete units (CS3, CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS and TS directly addresses amorphization of WSCl₄, so those sources are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

WSCl₄ is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is moderately strong: WCl₆-based routes successfully make W–N–Cl and W–Te–Cl compounds under mild sealed or solution conditions (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS3, TS1), and W–Cl bond lengths in the target match several tungsten chloride analogues (CS2, SS1, SS3, SS4). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive molecular WCl₆ chemistry, which avoids refractory tungsten diffusion and allows low-temperature assembly (CS2, CS5, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is control of sulfur and chlorine chemical potentials because volatile components and competing reduced tungsten chlorides are plausible, and side-product/mixed-phase behavior is reported in W₂NCl₈/W₂NCl₉ and WCl₆/Te chemistry (CS2, CS5, TS1). Overall, the best route is a dry low-temperature solution sulfur-transfer synthesis followed by slow crystallization, with sealed-ampoule chalcogenation as the most credible backup.
