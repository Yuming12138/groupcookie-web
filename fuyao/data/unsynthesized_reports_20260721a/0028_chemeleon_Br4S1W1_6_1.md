### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues are also stable or near-stable: W₂NCl₈ has E_hull = 0.0 (TS #1), WTeBr₉ has E_hull ≈ 0.0114 eV/atom (TS #3), and WCl₆ has E_hull ≈ 0.00037 eV/atom (TS #5). This supports a high thermodynamic score, but computed hull stability does not prove kinetic accessibility, which remains uncertain from TS alone.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses non-extreme temperatures, because Ta₃SBr₇ forms at 550 °C and WCl₆ forms at 800 °C in sealed tubes (CS #3, TS #5). The severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Br₂, multi-step ampoule handling, and hazardous halogen chemistry, all consistent with Ta₃SBr₇ synthesis details (CS #3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any need for high pressure, so that term is neutral/no deduction.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct synthesis from elements has slow kinetics because W is refractory and the target requires assembling a molecular WSBr₄ cluster from elemental W, S, and Br₂ [Reasonable Inference]. Ta₃SBr₇ required 550 °C for 2 weeks and additional gradient growth, showing that sulfide bromide ordering can be slow even when successful (CS #3). The volatile Br₂/S environment may assist transport, but CS #3 also reports phase/crystal-growth challenges, justifying a low-to-moderate F3.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from low temperatures, because W₂NCl₈ and WBr₄/WBr₅ analogues form near 240–250 °C in sealed ampoules (CS #1, CS #4, SS #4, TS #1). The main severity penalties are sealed vacuum/Ar handling, air-sensitive WBr₆, possible toxic H₂S or reactive sulfide donor, and the multi-step preparation/handling of WBr₆, all supported or inferred from WBr₆-based bromide synthesis (CS #4 / SS #4). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure requirements, so high-pressure deductions are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 2

WBr₆ is a chemically active molecular precursor, and W–Br clusters form at 250 °C from WBr₆/Sb (CS #4 / SS #4), so precursor activity is high. The target W–Br distances of 2.46–2.47 Å match the WBr₄/WBr₅/WTeBr₉ range of roughly 2.42–2.67 Å, supporting partial structural continuity from W–Br molecular precursors (CS #4, SS #4, SS #1). The remaining uncertainty is whether a terminal W=S ligand can be inserted cleanly without disproportionation to WBr₄/WBr₅ or WS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses mild temperatures because WTeBr₉-type W–Br cluster compounds are grown at 423–453 K in evacuated ampoules (CS #5 / SS #1 / TS #3). Deductions are due to vacuum ampoules, volatile halide transport, air-sensitive halide precursors, and multistep CVT setup (CS #5 / SS #1 / TS #3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high external pressure or >1000 °C requirements, so those deductions are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 3

The structural match is favorable because both the target and WTeBr₉ are 0D Indium-like W–Br cluster materials (SS #1). WTeBr₉ analogues demonstrate that low-temperature vapor/ampoule transport can crystallize W–Br clusters (CS #5 / SS #1 / TS #3). However, the same data also mention byproducts, rapid cooling requirements, low yields, and poor diffraction quality for some related phases, so composition control is assigned only moderate quality (CS #5 / SS #1 / TS #3).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a small 0D molecular unit, one W cation, and no extended mixed-connectivity polyhedral framework according to the target structure. Related 0D W–Br cluster analogues, including WTeBr₉ and WBr₅, are crystalline in SS data (SS #1, SS #4), which indirectly supports low amorphization tendency. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, so the high F4 score is mainly structural and should be treated as a [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

WSBr₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are stable or nearly stable (TS #1, TS #3, TS #5). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports the accessibility of W–Br molecular cluster chemistry in sealed ampoules and CVT-like environments, especially WBr₄/WBr₅ and WTeBr₉ analogues (CS #4, SS #4, CS #5 / SS #1 / TS #3). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive molecular tungsten bromide precursors, particularly WBr₆/WBr₅, which can react at 150–250 °C rather than relying on refractory W diffusion (CS #4 / SS #4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among WBr₄, WBr₅, W₄Br₁₀-type bromides, sulfur-rich WS₂, and possible over-brominated species [Reasonable Inference], with competing W bromides directly evidenced in WBr₆/Sb syntheses (CS #4 / SS #4). Overall, the material is plausible but likely requires careful low-temperature sealed-ampoule optimization rather than brute-force high-temperature synthesis.
