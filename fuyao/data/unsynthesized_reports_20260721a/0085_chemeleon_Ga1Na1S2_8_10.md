### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is exactly on the computed convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the target data, so it falls in the highest 90–100 scoring bin. Thermodynamic analogues Na₃GaS₃, Na₂Ga₂GeS₆, Na₂In₂SiS₆, Na₃PS₄, and Na₂In₂GeS₆ are also stable with E_hull = 0.0 and experimentally synthesized, supporting the idea that this Na–Ga–S-type chemistry is accessible (TS1–TS5). CS gives broader support that Na sulfides can be synthesized even when slightly above hull, such as NaVS₂ polymorphs with positive hull energies (CS2, CS3). The main uncertainty is whether the specific Amm2 NaGaS₂ polymorph is the experimentally preferred ground-state polymorph or one of multiple close-lying NaGaS₂ arrangements [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires evacuated sealed ampoules and reactive Na/S elemental chemistry, giving deductions for vacuum, volatile sealed-system operation, and air-sensitive Na [Reasonable Inference, CS2, CS3, SS1, TS1]. The temperature is moderate, because the closest Na–Ga–S analogue is synthesized at 925 K / 652 °C and NaVS₂ at 600 °C (CS2, CS3, SS1, TS1). No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is indicated by any CS/SS/TS source. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact reaction time for Na₃GaS₃, so a neutral/moderate assumption is used for duration.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Na, Ga, and S are kinetically active at moderate temperature because Na/Ga/S have low melting or high reactivity under sealed-tube conditions [Reasonable Inference], and direct elemental synthesis is proven for Na₃GaS₃ and NaVS₂ (SS1, TS1, CS2, CS3). Diffusion difficulty is favorable because the synthesis temperatures are not extreme and cation melting points are low [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction from elements is still substantial, so the structural component is only moderate. Remaining uncertainty is phase selection between NaGaS₂ and Na-rich Na₃GaS₃-like phases [Reasonable Inference, SS1, TS1].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses 550–850 °C solid-state sintering conditions, controlled atmospheres, sealed quartz tubes, grinding, and sometimes CS₂ vapor, all reported for structurally and thermodynamically similar Na chalcogenides (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS5). It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, which improves severity. Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed sulfur chemistry, multi-step grinding/reheating, and potentially air-sensitive sulfides or toxic CS₂ variants [Reasonable Inference, SS2, SS3, SS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact binary-sulfide route to NaGaS₂, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary sulfides such as Ga₂S₃ are chemically closer to the product but can be less reactive than fluxed or molten systems [Reasonable Inference]. Related Na₂M₂M′S₆ phases require 24 h–4 days and intermittent grinding, implying moderate kinetic barriers (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS5). Diffusion remains manageable because the processing temperatures are 550–850 °C and no refractory cation network is required [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether binary precursors reconstruct cleanly into the Amm2 NaGaS₂ framework rather than forming other Na–Ga–S stoichiometries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest overall severity because CS4 demonstrates a Na sulfide flux synthesis at 870 K / 597 °C under purified Ar, and related Na sulfide frameworks use molten sodium thiosulfate flux or high-temperature solid-state variants (CS4, SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). It avoids vacuum if performed under Ar/N₂, and it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions. Deductions are retained for volatile sulfur-rich conditions and multi-step flux removal/cooling. The main uncertainty is whether water washing from CS4 is compatible with NaGaS₂; dry washing is recommended as a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux conditions improve precursor activity and mass transport relative to dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], and CS4 directly shows slow-cooled flux growth of a Na sulfide. The molten sodium/sulfur medium should reduce diffusion limitations, so the diffusion score is high [Reasonable Inference, CS4]. Structural reconstruction is not trivial because the target is a specific ordered Amm2 phase, and CS4 notes impurity issues such as MnS contamination for the analogue. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaGaS₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamically similar Na sulfides are also stable and synthesized (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from the exact Na–Ga–S analogue Na₃GaS₃, which is synthesized from Na/Ga/S in evacuated graphitized ampoules and has E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1). Structurally similar Na–Ga-containing chalcogenides also form by 550–850 °C solid-state/flux routes, supporting transferability to the target (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the combination of thermodynamic stability and moderate-temperature sulfide synthesis chemistry. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control under volatile Na/S conditions, especially avoiding Na-rich competing phases and hydrolysis during workup [Reasonable Inference, CS4, SS1, TS1].
