### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives 100/100 by the required rubric. Chemically and thermodynamically similar Ge–Te–As compounds also include on-hull examples, such as Ge₂Te₅As₂ and Ge₃Te₆As₂ (CS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2), which supports plausibility of stable layered Ge chalcogenide chemistry. SS gives a less decisive signal because structurally similar GeSe and GeTe entries are above hull by ~0.061 and ~0.114 eV/atom (SS ranks 1 and 5), but these values do not override the target’s stated hull stability. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee the phase is experimentally accessible before competing binaries nucleate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because analogous direct reactions occur at 600 °C for GeSe (SS rank 1) and 700/350 °C for Ge–Te–As analogues (CS ranks 1–4; TS ranks 1–3). The main deductions are for vacuum/sealed ampoule use, volatile chalcogens, multi-stage heating, and Se/Te handling risk; these are supported by evacuated ampoule synthesis in SS rank 1 and ampoule routes in CS/TS, with volatility/toxicity interpreted as [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or >1000 °C step is supported by CS/SS/TS, so no deductions are applied there. The uncertainty is whether a simple anneal reaches the monoclinic ordered target rather than GeSe/GeTe mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction is feasible because elements/binaries react in multiple analogues: GeSe from Ge + Se (SS rank 1), and Ge–Te–As phases from elements or As₂Te₃/GeTe (CS ranks 1–4; TS ranks 1–3). Kinetics are helped by chalcogenide reactivity and moderate Ge melting point [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s monoclinic Cc structure is not identical to the trigonal MAX-like analogues, so structural reconstruction is only partial. Long anneals of two weeks in CS/TS analogues imply slow ordering or narrow phase windows, lowering the structural-reconstruction subscore. Remaining uncertainty is the degree of Se/Te ordering needed in Ge₂TeSe.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses Bridgman-style vacuum ampoule growth, supported by SnSb₂Te₄ at 1000 K, 10⁻⁴ Torr, and controlled translation (CS rank 5; TS rank 5), and by GeSe/GeTe single-crystal growth using Bridgman (SS rank 5). The temperature does not exceed 1000 °C based on analogue conditions, so thermal severity is moderate. Deductions arise from vacuum, sealed volatile chalcogens, multi-step directional growth, and Se/Te toxicity [Reasonable Inference supported by CS/TS/SS ampoule practice]. The major uncertainty is whether Ge₂TeSe melts congruently; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ternary phase-diagram evidence, so that aspect is not over-penalized.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt growth gives the best kinetic score because liquid homogenization reduces diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and the SnSb₂Te₄ analogue is reported as a homogeneous alloy suitable for Bridgman growth (CS rank 5; TS rank 5). GeSe and GeTe binaries can be grown as crystals by Bridgman (SS rank 5), supporting transferability to Ge–Se–Te chemistry. The structural pathway is still uncertain because the target monoclinic Cc phase is not directly shown in CS/SS/TS. Composition control during slow cooling is likely the limiting kinetic/processing issue because Se and Te can segregate or volatilize [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT/transport temperatures are moderate because GeS crystals are grown at 615/500 °C (TS rank 4), and GeSe processing uses a 600 + 500 °C sublimation gradient (SS rank 1). The route still requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile transport species, giving deductions for controlled atmosphere, sealed system, multi-step procedure, and toxic/corrosive materials [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct support for CVT and is therefore neutral for route-specific CVT severity. No high-pressure or >1000 °C condition is indicated by TS rank 4 or SS rank 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CVT has favorable transport kinetics because vapor species avoid bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and Ge chalcogenide transport/sublimation behavior is supported by TS rank 4 and SS rank 1. However, structural reconstruction is less certain than in Bridgman growth because no provided CS/SS/TS entry reports CVT synthesis of a Ge–Se–Te ternary phase. Differential Se/Te volatility may cause composition drift during transport [Reasonable Inference], reducing the composition/structural reliability. Thus, Route 3 is plausible but more exploratory than Routes 1–2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The rubric checks give a low amorphization risk because there is only one cation, Ge, so cation-radius mismatch is absent [Reasonable Inference], and the target description does not indicate a >40 atom cell. CS/SS/TS analogues often show corner and edge sharing but do not show the full vertex + edge + face sharing coexistence required to trigger C3 (CS ranks 1–4; SS ranks 1–5; TS ranks 1–5). A caveat is that Ge–Se–Te chalcogenides can form glasses in other compositions [Reasonable Inference], but that is not one of the specified F4 triggers and is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS. Therefore F4 remains high at 95/100.

Ge₂TeSe is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom. The CS and TS analogues strongly support accessible Ge–Te chalcogenide chemistry using ampoule reactions and melt/Bridgman methods, while SS analogues support Ge–Se and Ge–Te binary synthesis and crystal growth. Overall, the target is promising, with the best practical route being sealed melt homogenization/Bridgman followed by low-temperature annealing.
