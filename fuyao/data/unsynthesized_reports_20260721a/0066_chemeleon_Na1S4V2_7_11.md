### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a near-maximum thermodynamic score under the required rubric. TS strongly supports that related V–S and chalcogenide frameworks can be thermodynamic ground states because VS2, V3Ag2TeS6, GaV4S8, V4GaSe8, and NbS3 are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in TS1–TS5. CS and SS are consistent rather than contradictory: KCr3S5 and KCr5S8 are stable in CS1/CS5, while K/Rb/Tl V5S8 analogues are slightly above hull but experimentally synthesized in CS2/CS4/SS1/SS2/SS4. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of low-temperature ordering barriers.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Na ion exchange

The severity score is reduced because the K/Rb analogues require evacuated sealed silica tubes, molten alkali halides, a temperature gradient, and week-long reactions (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS2). The kinetics score is relatively high because ion exchange preserves much of the V–S host framework, and K/Rb V5S8 analogues are reported as pure phases with high ion-exchange efficiency (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is whether Na, being smaller than K/Rb, can occupy the target’s 6-coordinate Na–S sites without reconstructing into binary VS2 + Na2S or a different Na–V–S phase [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for the actual ion-exchange method, so TS is neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct high-temperature sulfidation

The severity is moderate because CS1 and CS5 use high temperatures and dry H2S, which is reactive and toxic, but not high pressure. Kinetics are less favorable because V metal is refractory and direct construction of the Na–V–S framework requires complete reaction, unlike topotactic ion exchange [Reasonable Inference]. CS1/CS5 show that molten alkali carbonate/polysulfide and H2S can form alkali transition-metal sulfides, which supports transferability. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for carbonate/H2S synthesis of Na–V–S specifically, so those sources are neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Na polysulfide flux

The severity score is lowered by sealed-tube, volatile sulfur, and air/moisture-sensitive polysulfide handling, but the temperature is mild compared with direct sulfidation. Kinetics are favorable because SS3/TS1 show low-temperature alkali polychalcogenide flux can form V–S frameworks at 350 °C, and flux transport bypasses slow solid-state diffusion. The structural uncertainty is significant because SS3/TS1 report polymorphs, disorder, and secondary phases, so phase selection for the exact Na(VS2)2 framework may require screening. CS1 gives additional support for alkali polysulfide melts, but insufficient evidence from CS for low-temperature Na-specific V sulfide flux growth, so the Na adaptation is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Direct sealed-tube/CVT

The severity is moderate-to-high because SS4/SS5 and CS3 involve vacuum sealing, high temperatures, long anneals, and sometimes halogen transport agents. Kinetics are only moderate because the route depends on vapor/solid transport and nucleation of the correct ternary Na–V–S phase, and analogous CVT routes require 8–21 days or 10-day anneals (SS4, SS5). This route is credible for crystal growth of V sulfides but less attractive for Na because Na and sulfur volatility complicate stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for CVT growth of Na–V–S specifically, so the Na transfer is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant cation-size mismatch between Na and V, which can promote local structural disorder [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target description does not show a very large unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence; it primarily reports edge-sharing VS6 units and mixed edge/corner SNa2V3 connectivity. Structurally similar K/Rb/Tl V sulfides do show complex edge/corner/face-sharing motifs (SS1, SS2, SS4), so disorder risk is not negligible. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for amorphous byproduct formation, so the F4 penalty is moderate rather than severe.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(VS2)2 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar V–S/chalcogenide compounds are also on hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthetic support comes from K/Rb V sulfides made by solid-state ion exchange, because those routes directly demonstrate alkali insertion/exchange into V–S frameworks under accessible sealed-tube conditions (CS2, CS4, SS1, SS2). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing stability and flexibility of VS2-like V–S frameworks, reflected by stable VS2 with matching V–S distances around 2.36 Å (TS2). The most critical limiting factor is Na stoichiometry and phase selectivity, since no provided source directly demonstrates Na(VS2)2 synthesis and Na is smaller/more volatile than K/Rb under many sulfide synthesis conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the phase appears plausibly synthesizable, with ion exchange or polysulfide flux screening preferred over direct elemental CVT.
