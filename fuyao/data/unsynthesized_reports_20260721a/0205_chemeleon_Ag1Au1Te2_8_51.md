### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability bin. Many close analogues also have E_hull = 0, including AgGaTe₂, AgInTe₂, TlBiTe₂, AgTl₃Te₂, AuTeI, AuTe₂I, CrAuTe₄, and Ag₈SnSe₆ (CS2–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature phase stability and possible Ag/Au ordering, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, vacuum-sealed solid-state/melt–anneal.** The route is supported by evacuated-tube reactions for CrAuTe₄, Ag₈SnSe₆, and AgInTe₂ (SS3, TS3, SS5, TS4, CS4). The severity score is reduced by vacuum sealing, Te volatility, and likely multi-step annealing, all reflected in analogous telluride syntheses (CS4, SS3, TS3, SS5, TS4). Kinetically, dense Au/Ag metals make initial reaction slower [Reasonable Inference], and repeated annealing seen for CrAuTe₄ and Ag₈SnSe₆ indicates ordering/phase purity may be nontrivial (SS3, TS3, SS5, TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, Te-rich self-flux.** Flux/melt telluride synthesis is supported by Hg–Tl–Te flux growth and Tl₃AgTe₂ slow-cooling melt growth (CS1, SS4, CS5). The route has moderate condition severity because it still requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile Te containment, slow cooling, and flux removal (CS1, SS4, CS5). Kinetically, molten Te should improve transport and ordering relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], which is consistent with successful low-temperature telluride crystal growth in the analogues (CS1, SS4, CS5).

**F2/F3 — Route 3, hydrothermal/halide-mediated.** The route is strongly supported structurally for Au–Te motifs because AuTeI, AuTe₂I, and AuTe₂Cl are hydrothermally prepared and contain Au–Te distances close to the target (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2, TS5). Severity is not high in temperature or pressure, but corrosive hydrohalic acids and sealed hydrothermal operation reduce practicality [Reasonable Inference].
