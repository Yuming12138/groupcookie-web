### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0\) eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin by the required rubric. TS strongly supports feasibility of on-hull fluoride phases because AsClF8, MnGaH4O2F5, and Ge(BrF5)2 are also reported with \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0\) (TS1, TS4, TS5). CS is chemically relevant but thermodynamically mixed: several chemically similar entries have high hull energies despite being experimentally crystallized (CS1, CS4, CS5), so CS is not used as the main F1 driver. The remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to decomposition during handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high condition score because analogous sublimation syntheses occur at mild temperature, including 80 °C overnight in sealed capillaries for AsClF8 (CS2/SS2/TS1) and temperature-gradient sublimation in capillaries for Ge(BrF5)2 (CS3/TS5). The main deductions are volatile sealed-system operation and toxic/air-sensitive fluoride precursors, both clearly signaled by the reactive fluoride analogues ClF3/AsF5 and BrF3/GeF4 (CS2, CS3, TS1, TS5). SS provides supportive structural transferability through AsClF8 as a structurally similar sublimed fluoride (SS2), while SS1/SS3/SS4/SS5 provide insufficient evidence for this exact volatile B–S fluoride route and are neutral. The principal uncertainty is whether SF4/BF3 produces the target cleanly rather than other sulfur fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because gas/liquid molecular fluorides bypass refractory cation diffusion, as shown by overnight sublimation growth in CS2/SS2/TS1 and capillary temperature-gradient growth in CS3/TS5. The target is zero-dimensional, so formation by molecular ion-pair assembly is likely faster than solid-state reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. BF4 tetrahedral units are structurally validated by NBF4-like fluoroborates with similar B–F distances (CS5). The remaining uncertainty is nucleation/selectivity of the SF3 cluster, which is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized heavily despite mild temperatures because anhydrous HF crystallization requires dry-box, all-Teflon/PFA vacuum-line handling, and month-long evaporation in CS1. Similar slow evaporation under inert/dry conditions is supported by SS1/TS2, which used liquid SO2, helium atmosphere, and two-month crystallization. CS4 and CS5 show that HF/HBF4-containing solution crystallization can be mild and effective for fluorosilicate/fluoroborate salts, but those examples are aqueous and therefore less transferable to a moisture-sensitive SF3⁺ target [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the SF3 fragment survives HF solution conditions without side reactions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution crystallization gives good kinetic access because ions/molecular fluoride species are mobile in HF or related solvents, as demonstrated by slow evaporation crystallization in CS1, CS4, CS5, and SS1/TS2. BF4 formation is expected to be facile in fluoride-rich acidic media because fluoroborate crystallization is directly supported by CS5. The target’s zero-dimensional cluster structure should require little extended network reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains high for sulfur speciation in HF because no CS/SS/TS entry provides direct SF3⁺ solution chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses mild temperature but is penalized for controlled dry atmosphere, volatile or hazardous solvents, multiple processing steps, and toxic/air-sensitive SF3⁺/fluoroanion reagents [Reasonable Inference]. Slow evaporation and recrystallization of fluoride salts are supported by CS5 and SS1/TS2, while anhydrous handling requirements are supported by CS1. TS3/TS4 provide insufficient evidence for salt metathesis of molecular B–S fluorides specifically and are neutral. The main practical limitation is availability and purity of a preformed SF3⁺ precursor [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Metathesis should be kinetically reasonable because solution ion exchange avoids high-temperature diffusion, consistent with slow evaporation/recrystallization analogues in CS5 and SS1/TS2. BF4⁻ can be introduced directly using a fluoroborate salt, and the known BF4 tetrahedral motif is supported by CS5. However, the structural reconstruction score is lower because SF3⁺ retention and complete anion exchange are not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS. The uncertainty is competing formation of SbF6⁻/AsF6⁻ salts or decomposition to other sulfur fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is zero-dimensional and contains discrete BF4 and SF3 clusters, so it lacks the mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing extended connectivity that often promotes glassy or disordered products [from target description]. The described cell contents are small, with two BF4 and two SF3 clusters, so the atom-count criterion is not triggered [from target description]. A cation-size/chemistry mismatch between B and S is triggered by chemical reasoning [Reasonable Inference], but CS5 shows BF4 units can crystallize cleanly and CS2/CS3 show molecular fluorides can form crystals. The main uncertainty is orientational disorder of molecular clusters, which CS/SS/TS do not directly address for the target.
