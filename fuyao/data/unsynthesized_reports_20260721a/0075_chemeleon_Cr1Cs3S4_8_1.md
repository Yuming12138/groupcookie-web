### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and is scored 98/100 by the requested rubric. CS analogues Cs4In2S5, Cs2MoS4, Cs3BS3, and Cs2WS4 are also reported with E_hull = 0 or nearly 0, supporting that Cs-rich sulfides/thiometallates can be stable (CS-1 to CS-4). TS analogues also include stable Cs/Rb sulfides and thiometallates (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly prove the target phase, so they are supportive rather than definitive.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because sealed ampoules, inert/vacuum handling, volatile sulfur, and air-sensitive cesium sulfides are required; these requirements are consistent with CS-3, TS-2, and TS-5. Kinetics are less favorable because Cr metal is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and forming isolated CrS4 tetrahedra from binary/elemental precursors likely requires substantial reconstruction. Long annealing in related solid-state routes, such as 7–10 d for Rb6HgS4 (TS-2) and high-temperature sealed reactions for Cs3BS3 (CS-3), supports a moderate-to-slow kinetics score. Remaining uncertainty is the Cr oxidation state and whether competing Cs2S/Cs2Sx/Cr sulfide phases dominate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar atmosphere and handling penalties to Route 1, but better kinetics because alkali polychalcogenide fluxes create a reactive sulfide medium. TS-4 directly shows that Cs-containing tetrahedral thiometallates can be grown at only 350 °C using A2Q3 reactive flux. This supports high precursor activity and improved diffusion relative to dry solid-state synthesis. The main uncertainty is whether Cr can be stabilized in the target formal oxidation state rather than precipitating lower-valent chromium sulfides. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

The azide route is chemically credible because CS-1 demonstrates Cs-rich sulfide synthesis by decomposing CsN3 and annealing at 500 °C for 8–15 d. It is penalized for azide hazard, pressure evolution, multistep handling, and sealed vacuum/inert conditions. Kinetically, reactive Cs generation is favorable, but Cr incorporation and CrS4 tetrahedron formation remain extrapolated. The long annealing time in CS-1 suggests that crystallization and phase ordering may still be slow.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Hydrothermal/solvothermal synthesis scores well for temperature and diffusion because CS-2 obtained Cs2MoS4 crystals at 423 K in 24 h and CS-4 crystallized Cs2WS4 from dilute aqueous solution. Structurally, MoS4/WS4 tetrahedral salts are strong analogues for an isolated thiometallate anion environment. Chemically, however, Cr(V) sulfide redox stability is not established in CS/SS/TS, and sulfide solutions can reduce or precipitate chromium species [Reasonable Inference]. This redox uncertainty is why an expert penalty is applied despite favorable numerical kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Cs and Cr, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula/description suggests a relatively small ordered cell and does not explicitly show complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior and are neutral for this factor. Overall amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

Cs3CrS4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue set supports synthesis of Cs-rich sulfides, isolated tetrahedral thiometallates, and alkali sulfide phases across sealed solid-state, flux, azide, and hydrothermal routes (CS-1 to CS-4; TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). Therefore, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with reactive polysulfide flux as the most chemically robust first attempt and hydrothermal crystallization as a higher-risk crystal-growth screen.
