### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for ground-state thermodynamic stability. TS strongly supports the plausibility of stable Cu–sulfide tetrahedral/related phases, with several analogues at E_hull = 0.0 or near zero (TS1–TS5). CS/SS similarly include stable or near-stable Cu–Ge/Sn/Si sulfides (CS2, CS3, SS5), but they do not include B, so B-specific thermodynamic transferability remains uncertain.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, solid-state.**  
The severity score is reduced because analogous syntheses require evacuated ampoules, sulfur containment, staged heating, and long anneals (CS2, CS3, SS4, TS1). Kinetics are the main weakness: elemental Cu–Ge–S analogues can require 35 days or 30-day anneals (CS2, CS3, TS1), and B is likely more refractory than Ge/Sn [Reasonable Inference]. The route is credible but may produce Cu₂S/CuS plus boron sulfide intermediates before the ordered Cu₃BS₄ phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, CVT.**  
CVT has stronger crystal-growth precedent than direct solid-state because iodine transport grows structurally similar Cu–Si/Ge sulfides and stannite-like compounds (SS1, SS3, TS2). The condition score is lower due to iodine toxicity, sealed ampoules, and volatile chalcogen/transport chemistry (TS2, CS5). Kinetic benefits are offset by uncertainty in whether B-containing species transport effectively, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux.**  
The flux route is supported by CS1, where a chemically similar Cu–Si chalcogenide was grown in KCl/LiCl flux at 550 °C for 6 weeks. Flux lowers diffusion barriers relative to dry solid-state reaction, but the long hold in CS1 indicates slow nucleation/growth. B₂S₃ or boron sulfide speciation in chloride flux is not established in CS/SS/TS and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference] with uncertainty.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, solvothermal.**  
TS5 shows that a Cu–M–S sulfide can form under mild solvothermal conditions from CuCl and thiourea at 453 K, which gives this route the best condition and kinetic scores. However, TS5 is Cu₂SnS₃, not Cu₃BS₄, and no CS/SS/TS data confirms boron incorporation under solvothermal sulfide chemistry. The route is attractive for rapid screening, but an annealing step may be needed to convert nanocrystalline or amorphous products into Lazarevićite Cu₃BS₄ [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target’s corner-sharing tetrahedral network is structurally simple, which lowers amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face-sharing frameworks. The main risk is the large Cu–B cation size/chemistry mismatch, inferred from the very different Cu–S and B–S bond lengths in the target description. CS/SS/TS analogues are mostly Cu–Si/Ge/Sn/M sulfides and do not directly quantify B-induced amorphization, so that component remains uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cu₃BS₄ is likely a ground-state phase because the reported energy above hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues include several stable Cu sulfides and Cu–tetrel sulfides (TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS evidence supports the broader feasibility of tetrahedral Cu–S frameworks, especially CuS₄–MS₄ corner-sharing networks made by sealed-tube, CVT, flux, and solvothermal routes (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with structural analogy to known tetrahedral Cu chalcogenides. The most critical limiting factor is the lack of direct B-containing analogue evidence, making B sulfidation, BS₄ formation, and Cu/B ordering the major uncertainties. A solvothermal route is best for rapid kinetic screening, while flux or sealed-ampoule annealing is likely needed if crystallinity/order is poor.
