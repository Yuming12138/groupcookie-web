### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin and receives 98/100 from the provided target data. Stable synthesized analogues include YCuS2 and CuScS2 in CS (CS-2, CS-4), and Nb3S4, Nb3VS6, Ta2NiS5, and CeMo6S8 in SS/TS (SS-1/TS-1, SS-2/TS-2, SS-4/TS-3, SS-5/TS-4). Some chemically similar materials are metastable yet synthesized, such as CuCrS2 and CuFeS2 (CS-1, CS-5), which further supports feasibility. Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of low-temperature competing polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct elements

The direct elemental route is supported by CS-5, SS-3, and SS-4/TS-3, all of which use elemental metals and sulfur/chalcogen in evacuated silica or quartz tubes. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing and volatile sulfur, both directly reflected in sealed-tube sulfide routes (CS-5, SS-4/TS-3), plus air-reactive Zr powder as [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the weak point because Zr is refractory and elemental diffusion is slow; related routes require long firings and sometimes regrinding (CS-5, SS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct formation of the exact P3m1 ZrNiS2 phase, so exact phase selectivity remains uncertain and is treated conservatively.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Binary sulfides

The binary-sulfide route is supported by CS-1, where corresponding binary chalcogenides are reacted in evacuated quartz ampoules to form ternary sulfides, and by CS-4/SS-5/TS-4 where preformed sulfides are used. Severity is moderate because the method still needs sealed vacuum ampoules and sulfur activity control, but it avoids the most reactive elemental sulfur/metal coupling [Reasonable Inference].
