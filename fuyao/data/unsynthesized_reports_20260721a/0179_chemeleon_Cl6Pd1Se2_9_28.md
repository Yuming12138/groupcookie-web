Thermodynamic similar materials include multiple stable or near-stable chalcogen-halides: Ru₂S₆Cl₁₆ at E_hull = 0.0, ReTeSeCl₄ at E_hull = 0.0, Zr₂Se₄Cl₂₄ at E_hull = 0.0, and Mo₂Se₄Cl₂₄ at E_hull ≈ 0.034 eV/atom (TS1, TS2, TS5, SS1). CS2 and CS4 have high hull energies and molecular salt chemistry, so they provide insufficient useful stability support for Pd–Se–Cl ribbon formation and are treated as weak/neutral signals for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is not thermally severe because the closest Pd–Se–Cl analogue is synthesized at 180 °C and Ru–S–Cl chemistry occurs at 140 °C (CS3, CS1/TS1). The score is reduced because the route requires sealed ampoules, vacuum or N₂ control, volatile selenium chlorides, and moisture-sensitive/toxic reagents, all of which are reported across the analogous syntheses (CS3, SS4, CS1/TS1). The remaining uncertainty is ampoule overpressure and the exact SeCl₄ activity needed to avoid Se-rich Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ (CS3).

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is favorable because reactive selenium halide media produce Pd–Se–halide complexes at low temperature without needing high-temperature bulk diffusion (CS3, SS4). The target’s Pd–Se and Pd–Cl distances exactly match the closest Pd(Se₃Cl)₂ analogue, which supports local coordination transferability (CS3). The main kinetic uncertainty is long-range ribbon assembly and phase selection, because the closest analogues report multiphase products and manual crystal separation (CS3, SS4).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses mild temperatures of ~140–170 °C based on SeCl₄ CVT analogues, so there is no high-temperature penalty (SS1, TS5). SeCl₄ vapor transport is strongly supported by structurally similar Mo/Zr Se–Cl complexes that crystallize directly in gradients over days (SS1, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature reactive SeCl₄/halogen media, which can form Se–Cl and Pd–Se–halide coordination motifs without high-temperature diffusion (CS3, SS1/TS5, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because Pd–Se–halide analogues form multiphase mixtures and require manual separation or careful chemical-potential tuning (CS3, SS4). Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, but likely as small crystals from sealed-ampoule chemistry rather than as a straightforward bulk powder.
