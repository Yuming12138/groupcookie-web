### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase at 0 K by the supplied thermodynamic data. Similar compounds in CS/SS/TS are also frequently on the hull, including Cs4Ga2Se5, Cs2GeSe3, Rb3SbSe3, Rb3BiSe3, and Rb6HgSe4 (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5; SS1, SS2, SS4, SS5; TS1–TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on finite-temperature decomposition or polymorph competition for Cs3GeSe3, so uncertainty remains in experimental phase selection.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly because the Cs–Ga–Se analogue requires vacuum/protective gas, evacuated quartz ampoules, long annealing, and intermediate grinding (CS1, SS1, TS1). The temperature itself is not extreme because the analogue range is 600–700 °C (CS1, SS1, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact Cs3GeSe3 volatility or ampoule pressure, so sealed-system risk is treated by [Reasonable Inference] from selenium-containing alkali chalcogenides.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary selenides should reduce chemical reconstruction relative to oxides, and Cs–Ga–Se analogues demonstrate successful solid-state formation from Cs2Se + M2Se3 binaries (CS1, SS1, TS1). However, long annealing times of weeks to months and intermediate grinding indicate slow ordering or phase-control limitations (CS1, SS1, TS1). The absence of a direct solid-state Cs3GeSe3 analogue leaves uncertainty in the Ge coordination change [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The solvothermal route has mild temperature, with direct Cs2GeSe3 evidence at 170 °C for 7 days (CS2, TS5). It is penalized for sealed volatile organic solvents, multiple handling/isolation steps, and hazardous amine/thiophenol media [Reasonable Inference] based on the listed solvents and sealed autoclave method (CS2, TS5). Insufficient evidence from SS for solvothermal target crystallization because Cs2GeSe3 appears as a thermodynamic/chemical analogue but not as a structural nearest neighbor in SS top five; SS is therefore neutral for this route-specific severity factor.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution-mediated transport should greatly improve kinetics compared with solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the closest Cs–Ge–Se analogue is experimentally accessible solvothermally (CS2, TS5). The shared Cs–Ge–Se chemistry and C2/m symmetry support transferability, but the target has Cs3GeSe3 stoichiometry and three-coordinate Ge rather than the Cs2GeSe3 GeSe4 tetrahedral environment (CS2, TS5; target description). Phase selectivity may depend on mineralizers and solvent composition, as the analogue evidence reports variable yields and mineralizer effects (CS2, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

The oxide/carbonate selenization route is penalized for H2 and Se-loaded gas atmosphere, which is explicitly required in A3SbSe3 and A3BiSe3 analogues (CS4, CS5, SS2, SS4, TS2, TS3). The temperature of 750–850 °C is high but below the >1000 °C deduction threshold (CS4, CS5, TS2, TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that GeO2 behaves identically to Sb2O3/Bi2O3 under these conditions, so the Ge transfer is an extrapolation.

#### F3 — Route 3

The A3MSe3 analogues show short synthesis times of around 9 h at 750–850 °C, implying favorable reaction kinetics in a molten/reactive selenizing environment (CS4, CS5, SS2, SS4, TS2, TS3). Structurally, Rb3SbSe3 and Rb3BiSe3 contain trigonal non-coplanar MSe3 units and SeA6M pentagonal-bipyramidal coordination resembling the target (CS4, CS5, SS2, SS4; target description). The limiting uncertainty is whether Ge forms the same A3GeSe3 framework rather than competing Cs2GeSe3 or GeSe2 phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct element reaction requires high vacuum and sealed ampoule control, as shown for Cs2Se synthesis from elemental Cs and chalcogen (SS3). The route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, but it uses hazardous Cs metal and volatile Se [Reasonable Inference] based on SS3. Cs and Se can react readily, but controlled Cs2Se synthesis still required slow heating and 7 days, indicating kinetic/safety constraints (SS3). Ge incorporation from dense elemental Ge may be slower than from a preformed selenide [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates ternary Cs3GeSe3 formation from elements. This route is therefore viable but less preferred than solvothermal or reactive selenization approaches.

Cs3GeSe3 is likely a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close Cs/Rb selenide analogues are also on the hull (target data; CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5; TS1–TS5). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Cs–Ge–Se chemistry already supports Cs2GeSe3, so stabilizing Cs3GeSe3 may require precise Cs chemical potential, solvent/basicity, or Se activity control [Reasonable Inference] (CS2, TS5).
