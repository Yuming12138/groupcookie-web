### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic stability. Similar Tl oxoanion and Tl oxide phases are also stable or nearly stable: Tl2SnO3 has E_hull = 0.0 (CS1), Tl3AsO4 has E_hull = 0.0 (CS2 / SS1 / TS1), Tl2O is essentially on the hull (SS3 / TS2), and Tl2Te3O7 is near the hull (SS5). The remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide oxygen chemical-potential stability for Te(VI) versus Te(IV) phases, so finite-temperature redox stability is inferred rather than proven [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Route 1 is mild because analogous Tl arsenates form at 503 K for 96 h in water without special gas atmosphere (CS2 / SS1 / TS1). The main severity penalty is thallium toxicity, because Tl2CO3 is used in the analogue and all Tl salts require strict containment [Reasonable Inference]. **Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS** exists for direct hydrothermal Tl–Te(VI) volatility or decomposition issues, so no additional severity penalty was applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal

Hydrothermal chemistry gives high precursor activity and solution-mediated transport, as shown by single-crystal Tl3AsO4 and related arsenates at 503 K (CS2 / SS1 / TS1). Tl coordination transfer is favorable because Tl3AsO4 has T-shaped Tl–O environments and bond lengths close to the target (CS2 / SS1 / TS1). The kinetic uncertainty is the change from AsO4 tetrahedra to TeO6 octahedra, which may require different condensation chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 solid-state

Solid-state synthesis is not temperature-severe because relevant Tl oxide/tellurite/stannate analogues form at 340–650 °C (SS5 / CS1 / SS3). However, Tl oxide chemistry can require inert/vacuum handling and special containers, and Tl2O phase control is difficult (SS3 / TS2). The route is penalized for toxic Tl precursors and likely volatile/sealed-system concerns [Reasonable Inference supported by SS3 / TS2 / TS4].

#### F3 — Route 2 solid-state

Tl2CO3 has demonstrated reactivity with SnO2 and TeO2 in solid-state syntheses (CS1 / SS5), but carbonate/oxide reactions are less kinetically active than hydrothermal or nitrate precipitation routes [Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion should be manageable because analogous Tl oxides form at low-to-moderate temperatures (CS1 / SS5 / SS3). Structural reconstruction is the main kinetic limitation because SS5 reports a glassy Tl–Te–O mass, indicating crystallization and phase selection may be nontrivial.

#### F2 — Route 3 precipitation

The precipitation route has very mild temperature and pressure, as Tl3VO4 forms by pH adjustment, mixing, heating at 100 °C, filtration, washing, and drying (TS5). Hg2SeO3 precipitation analogues also form under mild, non-special-atmosphere conditions (CS3 / CS4 / CS5). Deductions are applied for multiple wet-chemistry handling steps and soluble Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference; TlNO3 use from TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 precipitation

Soluble TlNO3 and oxoanion solutions provide high precursor activity, directly supported by Tl3VO4 precipitation (TS5). Molecular-scale mixing minimizes diffusion barriers, and Hg2SeO3 analogues show precipitation can yield single-phase polycrystalline products (CS3 / CS4 / CS5). The uncertainty is whether the exact Tl6TeO6 ribbon crystallizes directly rather than forming hydrated/basic tellurates or amorphous precipitates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains highly size-mismatched Tl⁺ and Te⁶⁺ cations, triggering the ionic-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The apparent primitive atom count is not >40, and the target description does not demonstrate simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity, so only one formal check is triggered. SS5 nevertheless warns that Tl–Te–O compositions may vitrify because Tl2Te3O7 was first obtained as a glassy mass, increasing practical caution despite a moderate F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl6TeO6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Tl oxoanion analogues are also on or near the hull (CS1 / CS2 / SS5 / TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from hydrothermal Tl3AsO4/Tl–Cu arsenate analogues with similar Tl–O coordination and mild crystallization conditions (CS2 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the compatibility of Tl⁺ with low-coordinate, stereochemically active Tl–O environments, which are repeatedly observed in CS/SS/TS analogues (CS1 / CS2 / SS3 / TS2). The main limiting factor is Te oxidation-state and phase control, because the provided Tl–Te–O structural analogue is Tl2Te3O7 with different Te coordination and glass-forming behavior (SS5). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with hydrothermal tellurate crystallization the best first attempt.
