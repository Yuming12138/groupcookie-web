### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. CS/SS/TS analogues also include many E_hull = 0 materials, including Hf2P2O9 (CS2), TaOPO4 polymorphs (CS4), and Lu/Nd/Sm sulfate oxysalts (SS1, SS4; TS1, TS4, TS5), supporting that related oxysalt frameworks can be thermodynamic ground states. The remaining uncertainty is that the calculated ground state includes a discrete O2 cluster, and none of CS/SS/TS directly proves such a cluster is experimentally accessible in HfSO6.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Hydrothermal conditions are mild relative to solid-state synthesis: 230 °C for 3 days in PTFE-lined autoclaves is directly supported by sulfate-containing structural and thermodynamic analogues (SS1, SS4, SS5; TS1, TS4, TS5). The deductions arise from multi-step hydrothermal handling and the need to manage H2O2/O2 pressure for the target O2 cluster [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports hydrothermal oxysalt gels generally (CS1), but CS gives insufficient direct evidence for HfSO6 peroxide/sulfate crystallization, neutral signal 50.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Reaction kinetics should be favorable because soluble Hf salts and acid sulfate media allow molecular-level mixing [Reasonable Inference], and SS/TS sulfate oxysalts crystallize hydrothermally at 230 °C (SS1, SS4, SS5; TS1, TS4, TS5). Solution transport reduces the Hf diffusion penalty compared with HfO2 solid-state routes [Reasonable Inference]. The largest kinetic uncertainty is nucleating the exact HfSO5 framework around an O2 cluster rather than forming hydrated hafnium sulfate or amorphous Hf hydroxysulfate [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
CS2 demonstrates a Hf oxysalt gel/precipitation route using HfCl4 and high-temperature annealing, and CS1 supports oxide dissolution/neutralization to hydrous gels. The route is less severe if adapted to 300–600 °C for sulfate retention [Reasonable Inference], but it remains multi-step and uses moisture-sensitive/corrosive HfCl4 if chosen [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for precipitation-gel Hf sulfate synthesis, neutral signal 50.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Gel chemistry gives high precursor activity, as shown by solution-gel Hf phosphate processing in CS2. However, CS2 required 1200 °C/24 h for Hf phosphate crystallization, while sulfate frameworks and O2 clusters may not survive such high temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are moderate: intimate mixing is favorable, but crystallization at sulfate-compatible temperatures is uncertain.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-tube reactions at 700–750 °C are supported by structurally and thermodynamically similar oxides (SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3). The route is penalized because it requires controlled vacuum/O2 sealing, volatile sulfate/SOx retention, and multi-day heating [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for sealed-tube Hf sulfate synthesis, neutral signal 50.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solid-state reaction from HfO2 is kinetically difficult because HfO2 is refractory and Hf–O diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference]. SS3/TS3 show that sealed-tube oxysalts can form after 6–7 days at 700–750 °C, but those analogues involve more volatile/reactive MoO3/SeO2/TeO2 components rather than HfO2. Structural reconstruction is likely substantial, so this route is less attractive than hydrothermal synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target combines small tetrahedral S6+ and larger 6-coordinate Hf4+, giving a large cation-size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. However, the described target cell is small, about 16 atoms, and no explicit coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing is reported for the target. SS/TS analogues show complex low-symmetry sulfate oxysalts can crystallize hydrothermally despite mixed cation environments (SS1, SS4, SS5; TS1, TS4, TS5), reducing concern but not eliminating amorphous Hf sulfate gel formation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom from the supplied target thermodynamics. The CS/SS/TS analogues support oxysalt framework synthesis well: CS2 directly supports Hf oxysalt gel chemistry, while SS/TS strongly support hydrothermal crystallization of sulfate-containing low-symmetry oxide frameworks. The most critical enabling factor is solution-phase sulfate chemistry, which should overcome slow Hf diffusion and allow Hf–O–S framework assembly under mild conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is the discrete O2 cluster with O–O = 1.23 Å, because no CS/SS/TS example directly validates incorporation of such a unit in a Hf sulfate lattice. Overall, hydrothermal peroxide-assisted synthesis is the best first attempt, with sealed-tube and gel-anneal routes as secondary options.
