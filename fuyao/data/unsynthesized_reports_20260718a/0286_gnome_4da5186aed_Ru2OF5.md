### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is predicted at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. Thermodynamically similar materials are also mostly on or very near the convex hull, including CuMoF₆, Cd₂Os₂O₇, LiOsO₃, KOs₂O₆, and OsOF₄-type oxyfluoride analogues (TS-1–TS-5). Chemically similar CrCdF₆ and OsOF₄ are E_hull = 0 and ~0.011 eV/atom, respectively, supporting that related fluoride/oxyfluoride frameworks can be thermodynamically viable (CS-1, CS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee a wide kinetic synthesis window, especially for volatile Ru–F–O chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/SS-1/TS-5].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and GPa pressure because the closest RuO₂ fluorination analogue operates at 400 °C in a Monel flow system (CS-2/SS-1/TS-5). The main severity penalties come from F₂/Ar, rigorous dryness, multi-step gas handling, and toxic/corrosive fluorine, all explicitly described or implied in the analogue setup (CS-2/SS-1/TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a benign atmosphere alternative, so no milder-atmosphere credit is assigned. The resulting F2 score is moderate-low despite low temperature because F₂ handling dominates the practical difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, direct fluorination is favorable because RuO₂ is consumed at 400 °C in the closest analogue, suggesting gas-phase fluorination can bypass very high-temperature solid-state diffusion (CS-2/SS-1/TS-5). However, RuO₂ is still a refractory oxide, and the target requires precise Ru₂OF₅ stoichiometry rather than complete conversion to RuOF₄-like species [Reasonable Inference]. The structural similarity to MOF₅ octahedra supports partial reconstruction feasibility, but exact mixed O/F ordering is uncertain (SS-1). This gives an intermediate F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses temperatures comparable to sealed fluoride solid-state syntheses, around 450–550 °C, rather than >1000 °C (SS-2/TS-1). Severity arises from sealed-tube operation, volatile/reactive fluorides, inert/vacuum control, likely air-sensitive Ru fluorides, and long multi-step heating schedules, all consistent with the CuMoF₆ analogue (SS-2/TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS for a direct sealed Ru₂OF₅ synthesis is noted, so CS contributes neutral support for this specific route. The score is lower than Route 1 because sealed fluoride reactions are long and composition-sensitive.

#### F3 — Route 2

The CuMoF₆ analogue required annealing for up to 3 weeks and careful temperature steps, indicating sluggish diffusion and narrow phase control in complex fluoride frameworks (SS-2/TS-1). RuF₃/RuF₄ would be more reactive than RuO₂ alone, but RuO₂/Ru metal components and Ru cation ordering remain kinetically limiting [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target and analogue both use corner-sharing octahedral fluoride frameworks, yet target oxyfluoride ordering is not demonstrated (SS-2/TS-1). The F3 score is therefore the lowest among the proposed routes.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by F₂/Ar autoclave fluorination at 300 °C and up to 300 atm in Monel equipment (CS-1), plus direct RuO₂ fluorination evidence (CS-2). The pressure is far below the >5 GPa penalty threshold, but F₂/Ar, autoclave operation, toxic gas handling, and multiple operational steps impose significant deductions (CS-1, CS-2). Insufficient evidence from SS for autoclave synthesis of the exact target is noted; SS support is mainly structural rather than procedural. Overall severity is similar to Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3

Pressurized F₂/Ar should enhance fluorination kinetics at modest temperature because CS-1 reports formation of fluorinated octahedral frameworks under 300 °C/300 atm F₂/Ar conditions. CS-2 further shows RuO₂ can be converted under F₂/Ar at 400 °C. The limiting kinetic issue is not anion incorporation alone but stopping at Ru₂OF₅ rather than more fluorinated/oxidized Ru oxyfluorides [Reasonable Inference from CS-2 phase instability and target stoichiometry]. Thus F3 is intermediate.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-size disorder is not expected to drive amorphization [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target is described as corner-sharing RuOF₅ octahedra with no edge/face-sharing coexistence, and structurally similar OsOF₄/CuMoF₆ analogues are also built from corner-sharing octahedral motifs (SS-1, SS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact target cell atom count, but the formula and simple connectivity suggest a low amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The F4 score is therefore high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ru₂OF₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase in the computed phase diagram because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many thermodynamically similar references are also on or near the hull (TS-1–TS-5). The analogue support is strong for Ru/O/F chemistry because RuO₂ + F₂/Ar fluorination is directly documented for a closely related oxyfluoride system (CS-2, SS-1, TS-5), and additional fluoride-framework syntheses support corner-sharing octahedral motifs (CS-1, SS-2, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is highly reactive fluorine chemistry, which allows Ru–O–F framework formation at only ~300–425 °C rather than requiring very high-temperature ceramic diffusion (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase and redox control: the target has Ru average oxidation state +3.5, whereas direct F₂ fluorination can over-fluorinate/oxidize toward RuOF₄-like volatile or unstable products [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/SS-1/TS-5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires specialist fluorine handling and careful fluorine chemical-potential control.
