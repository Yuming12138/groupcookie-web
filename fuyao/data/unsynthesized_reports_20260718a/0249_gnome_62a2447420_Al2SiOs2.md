### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues OsSi, U₂Os₃Si₅, IrAl, YOs₂Si₂, and TmOs₂Si₂ are also on the hull, which supports real ground-state stability in related Os–Si and Al/noble-metal intermetallic spaces (TS1–TS5). Chemical analogues are mostly stable but not uniformly so, because one Zn–Ir–B analogue is 0.186 eV/atom above hull (CS2), so CS provides strong but not perfect support. Remaining uncertainty is that no source directly confirms experimental Al₂SiOs₂.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, arc melting plus annealing.**  
Condition severity is moderate because arc melting and repeated remelting are standard for Os–Si and other metal silicides/intermetallics (SS1/TS1, CS5, TS4/TS5), but the route necessarily involves very high local temperatures and Os handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are helped by the liquid alloying step, as arc melting successfully alloys Os–Si and RE–Os–Si systems (SS1/TS1, TS4/TS5). The main uncertainty is whether the polar tetragonal I4mm ordering of Al₂SiOs₂ survives arc melting or requires a long anneal **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, sealed Ta-ampoule reaction.**  
The F2 score is relatively favorable because analogous ternary intermetallics form at 1100–1230 °C under Ar in Ta ampoules without high pressure or reactive gas (CS1–CS4). Kinetics are less favorable because elemental Os is refractory and slow-diffusing **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS2/CS3 show that related ternary intermetallics can have narrow phase windows and impurity phases. This route is scored best overall because it provides stoichiometry retention and controlled atmosphere while avoiding extreme arc-melt evaporation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, induction/high-temperature sintering.**  
SS2/SS3 support high-temperature Os–Si synthesis at 1340–1400 °C in He, including controlled schedules and crucibles. Kinetically, near-fusion heating can promote Os–Si reaction, but Al loss and stoichiometry drift are likely at these temperatures **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is plausible but less preferred than sealed Ta synthesis because SS3 explicitly notes atmosphere control to suppress Si evaporation, suggesting volatile-component control is important.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, oxide-reduction/H₂ route.**  
SS5/TS3 support high-temperature H₂ reduction routes to Al–noble-metal intermetallics, but the conditions are severe: 1200–1750 °C, purified H₂, specialized furnace/tube arrangements, and long durations. Kinetically, reducing Al₂O₃/SiO₂ and assembling a ternary Os aluminide silicide is substantially harder than reacting elements **[Reasonable Inference]**. This route is therefore a fallback exploratory route rather than a recommended first attempt.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a dense intermetallic with high-coordinate Os/Al/Si environments, and related Os–Si, RE–Os–Si, and Al–Ir phases crystallize readily rather than forming glasses (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). One amorphization risk is size/chemistry mismatch among Al, Si, and Os **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the apparent structural complexity is low to moderate and not a large-cell framework. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for unit-cell atom count or polyhedral sharing in the target, so those checks are treated conservatively.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Al₂SiOs₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many Os–Si/Al–noble-metal analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5, SS1–SS5). The strongest analogue support comes from Os–Si compounds synthesized from elements and REOs₂Si₂ phases synthesized by arc melting plus annealing (SS1–SS3, TS4/TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust formation of Os–Si and Os/intermetallic bonding networks at high temperature, which is repeatedly demonstrated in SS1–SS4 and TS1–TS5. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: Os is refractory, while Al/Si can be lost or oxidized at high temperature **[Reasonable Inference]**, and CS2/CS3 show related ternary intermetallics can suffer from impurity phases and narrow phase windows. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with sealed Ta-ampoule elemental reaction as the most balanced first route.
