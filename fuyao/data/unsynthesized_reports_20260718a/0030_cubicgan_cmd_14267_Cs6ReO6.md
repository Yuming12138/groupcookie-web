### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high-stability cutoff specified in the prompt, so the thermodynamic score is high. TS gives useful supporting signal because several thermodynamic neighbors with comparable or even higher E_hull are reported synthesized, including K3NiO2 at 0.035 eV/atom, Na5WNO4 at 0.048 eV/atom, Cs2Se at 0.075 eV/atom, and Rb2S at 0.088 eV/atom (TS3, TS5, TS1, TS2). CS and SS analogues listed are mostly hull-stable with E_hull = 0.0, which supports the broader viability of Cs-rich oxometallate compositions (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not prove the cubic Cs6ReO6 polymorph specifically.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it avoids >1000 °C and avoids GPa pressure, consistent with Cs oxometallates synthesized at 650–740 °C in sealed Ag containers (CS1, CS3, CS5). The major deductions come from sealed handling of volatile/reactive alkali oxides, multistep precursor preparation/loading/annealing, and air-sensitive Cs oxides, all documented in CS1, CS3, CS5 and SS1/SS3. TS provides insufficient useful signal for Route 1-specific condition selection because the closest TS entries include chalcogenides, nickelates, or nitrido-oxides rather than Cs–Re–O chemistry; TS is treated as neutral for this factor. The unresolved issue is whether ReO3/ReO2 can be retained without volatilization or over/under-reduction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics for Route 1 are limited by solid-state diffusion and by the need to build a new cubic Cs6ReO6 framework, even though Cs oxide media are reactive (CS1, CS3, CS5). The strongest positive signal is local bonding similarity to Cs4IrO4: Cs–O ≈2.93 Å and M–O =1.93 Å match the target’s Cs–O =2.93 Å and Re–O =1.93 Å closely (CS1/SS1). The negative signal is that CS1 required 86 d and careful temperature cycling, showing slow kinetics and narrow phase control for the closest analogue (CS1/SS1). TS is insufficient for detailed kinetics of Cs6ReO6 because thermodynamic neighbors do not contain Re or the same Cs-rich oxide chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 but benefits from flux-mediated transport at only moderate temperatures, as alkali-rich melts are reported at 500–800 °C under Ar (CS4) and suboxometallates form in sealed crucibles under Ar (SS2). The penalties remain significant because Cs metal/Cs oxides are air-sensitive and require sealed handling, and the flux route adds separation and compositional control steps (CS4, SS2). CS4 also notes side-phase/crucible-reaction issues in alkali-rich oxide fluxes, supporting a moderate-to-challenging difficulty rating. TS is insufficient for selecting a Cs oxide flux chemistry and is neutral here.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because flux/self-flux chemistry directly addresses diffusion limitations, as shown for alkali-rich germanates and suboxometallates (CS4, SS2). The method is transferable because the target is extremely Cs-rich, and flux growth favors isolated oxoanion environments in alkali-rich matrices (CS4, SS2). However, no source directly demonstrates ReO6 octahedral ordering in cubic Fm-3m Cs6ReO6, so the structural reconstruction term remains only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the flux composition stabilizes Re(VI) rather than producing competing Re metal, ReO2, perrhenate-like, or Cs-Re-O side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because it deliberately adds redox/oxygen-fugacity control on top of sealed air-sensitive Cs oxide synthesis. The need for controlled oxygen content is inferred from the use of CsO1.8 in Cs4IrO4, CsO0.57 in Cs6In2O6, and Cs/CsO2-derived Cs2O in Cs2ZrO3 (CS1, CS5, CS3). The deduction for controlled atmosphere reflects the internal oxygen buffer rather than external H2/NH3/vacuum [Reasonable Inference]. TS is insufficient for this redox-specific chemistry and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 helps target Re(VI), but it does not inherently improve crystallization relative to flux growth. Cs oxide reactivity is supported by CS1/CS3/CS5, but ReO2/ReO3 buffering may introduce additional competing equilibria [Reasonable Inference]. Because no CS/SS analogue demonstrates cubic Cs6ReO6 or an isostructural Re phase, the structural reconstruction score is low. The route may still be valuable as an optimization branch if Route 2 gives incorrect Re oxidation state.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines very large Cs⁺ with much smaller high-valent octahedral Re⁶⁺, so the cation size contrast trigger is activated [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m conventional cell likely contains more than 40 atoms if multiple Cs6ReO6 formula units occupy the face-centered cell, activating the unit-cell-size trigger [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The third trigger is not clearly supported because the target description does not explicitly state mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity, and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct evidence for that exact connectivity. The resulting medium amorphization risk mainly reflects ordering complexity rather than a strong glass-forming chemistry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs6ReO6 is best viewed as a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, and TS shows synthesized alkali-rich compounds with comparable or higher metastability (TS1–TS5). CS and SS support synthesis strongly at the level of chemical family: Cs-rich oxometallates with similar Cs–O distances and high alkali content are made in sealed Ag/Ta/metal containers under Ar (CS1, CS3, CS5; SS1–SS3). The most critical enabling factor is a Cs-rich oxide melt or self-flux that enhances diffusion and stabilizes isolated high-valent oxoanions, as suggested by CS4 and SS2. The most critical limiting factor is Re oxidation-state/volatility control, because none of CS/SS/TS directly validates a Cs–Re–O analogue and Re(VI) must be maintained [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but experimentally demanding, with the best chance coming from Cs-rich sealed flux growth rather than simple stoichiometric oxide firing.
