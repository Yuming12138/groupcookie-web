#### Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state reaction from Ti/TiN/As

| Field | Details |

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score. CS provides useful supporting signal because V3GeN is also on the hull and V3AsN/V3PN are very close to the hull, indicating the M3PnN Cmcm family is thermodynamically credible (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). SS/TS also provide useful signal because several Ti–metalloid nitrides are on or near the hull and are experimentally synthesized, including Ti2GaN, Ti2InN, Ti2AlN, and Ti3InN (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target Ti3AsN itself is not reported as synthesized in the supplied data.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for evacuated sealed-tube handling, volatile/toxic As, and repeated annealing. The 1000 °C condition is directly supported by V3AsN and V3PN syntheses in sealed quartz tubes, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied if the process is kept at 1000 °C (CS-2, CS-5). SS/TS provide consistent but less direct evidence that sealed-ampoule synthesis is common for Ti–metalloid nitrides (SS-1, SS-4; TS-1, TS-3). The main uncertainty is whether Ti3AsN requires slightly higher temperature than V3AsN because Ti/TiN diffusion may be slower [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because Ti and TiN are refractory powders, and solid-state diffusion is likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. The direct V3AsN analogue strongly improves confidence because it forms by sealed-tube annealing at 1000 °C with regrinding and second annealing (CS-2). Structural reconstruction is favorable because target Ti3AsN and V3AsN share the same Cmcm connectivity and similar As/N coordination (CS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ti–As competing binaries form faster than the ternary nitride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has more severe processing because it adds prealloying, pulverization, and final annealing, analogous to Ti–Al–N master-alloy syntheses (CS-1; SS-5; TS-4). Controlled atmosphere/vacuum and As containment are required [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS show the multi-step prealloy route is feasible for Ti–Al–N, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms Ti–As master-alloy use for Ti3AsN; this missing direct signal is treated as neutral for direct transferability. The score is therefore lower than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2

Prealloying should improve Ti–As mixing and reduce local As loss [Reasonable Inference], but TiN remains a slow-reacting refractory precursor. Ti–Al–N analogues demonstrate that Ti/TiN/metalloid systems can be equilibrated by long high-temperature sintering (CS-1; SS-5; TS-4). However, these references often form H-phase or cubic perovskite structures rather than the target Cmcm structure, so structural selection is less certain than in Route 1 (CS-1; SS-5; TS-4). The major uncertainty is whether Ti–As alloying stabilizes binaries that compete with Ti3AsN [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for the temperature gradient, sealed evacuated tube, volatile/toxic As, optional halogen transport agent, and long duration. Ti2P transport synthesis required separated reactants, an evacuated tube, a month-long treatment, and post-treatment melting, showing this class of route can be demanding (SS-2; TS-2). CS supports only the final nitride-forming anneal aspect, not the CVT aspect (CS-2). Thus, this route is plausible but more operationally complex.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor-assisted As transport may enhance reaction uniformity and pnictogen delivery [Reasonable Inference], and Ti2P demonstrates that Ti–pnictide formation can proceed in a sealed gradient arrangement (SS-2; TS-2). However, Ti2P references also report phase-purity and crystal-growth difficulty, which lowers the kinetics/phase-control score (SS-2; TS-2). Nitrogen incorporation still depends on TiN reaction, supported indirectly by V3AsN/V3PN sealed-tube methods (CS-2, CS-5). The uncertainty is high because no direct Ti–As–N CVT synthesis is provided.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a small, ordered crystalline intermetallic nitride with only one metal cation site type chemically, so cation-radius mismatch is not a significant amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference]. The target and V3AsN-type analogues have ordered corner/edge-sharing nitride octahedral networks rather than highly frustrated mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing topologies (CS-2). No CS/SS/TS evidence suggests glass formation or amorphous products in this family. Remaining uncertainty is small and mainly relates to possible disorder/secondary phases rather than amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti3AsN is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support is V3AsN, which is essentially isostructural in Cmcm and was synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state annealing at 1000 °C with regrinding and a second anneal (CS-2). Additional support comes from V3PN/V3GeN and Ti–metalloid nitride analogues made by sealed-tube or ampoule sintering (CS-3, CS-5; SS-1, SS-4; TS-1, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a very close V3AsN analogue with the same pnictogen and same structural topology (CS-2). The most critical limiting factor is control of As volatility/toxicity and competing Ti–As or TiN-rich phases during long sealed-tube annealing [Reasonable Inference].
