### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, placing it at the top of the favorable/near-hull range and justifying a score of 90. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized or plausible, including CeSi5 at 0.058 eV/atom (TS1), Zr2CuSi4 at 0.0587 eV/atom (TS4), Hf2CuSi4 at 0.0656 eV/atom (TS3), and NaSi6 at 0.0621 eV/atom (TS5). The uncertainty is that many analogues are metastable and require high pressure, so low E_hull does not guarantee ambient-pressure accessibility (TS1, TS2, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because the closest Y/rare-earth Si-rich analogues require 8–15 GPa and 900–1520 K (CS2/SS1/TS2; CS3/SS5; CS4/SS2/TS1; SS4). It avoids volatile alkali precursors and can use relatively stable binary silicides, but the high-pressure apparatus and quench protocol are demanding [Reasonable Inference from CS2/SS1/TS2 and CS4/SS2/TS1]. The main uncertainty is the Ti incorporation window, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderately favorable because YSi2/Si-type precursor chemistry is directly used for YSi3 and related HP silicides (CS2/SS1/TS2), and small high-pressure capsules shorten diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is still needed because the target is cubic F-43m YTiSi6, while the closest analogues are tetragonal/orthorhombic/monoclinic Si-rich frameworks (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). Uncertainty remains high for ordering Y and Ti into distinct YSi12 and TiSi6 sites.

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting is less equipment-intensive than HPHT, and arc/anneal routes exist for Si-rich or ternary silicides (CS1; TS3; TS4). However, it involves very high local temperatures, air-sensitive Y handling [Reasonable Inference], and long anneals up to weeks, as shown by BaRh2Si9 and Hf/Zr–Cu–Si analogues (CS1; TS3; TS4). The conflict is that ambient-pressure arc routes exist for some silicides, but the closest Y/Si-rich structural analogues favor HPHT, so Route 2 is down-weighted (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5).

#### F3 — Route 2

Reaction kinetics are poor because elemental Y/Ti/Si mixtures require extensive melting/remelting and long annealing to homogenize [Reasonable Inference; long anneals in CS1 and TS3/TS4]. The closest HP analogues imply the desired Si-rich framework may not be the ambient-pressure equilibrium phase (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). The main uncertainty is whether the low target E_hull might allow an ambient-pressure polymorph despite the HP trend.

#### F2 — Route 3

Thin-film synthesis avoids GPa pressure and high bulk diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires high vacuum/Ar plasma control, composition calibration, and oxidation control for Y-containing films [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for thin-film transferability, so this route carries a confidence penalty.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor-source availability is good because Y, Ti, and Si are standard sputter targets [Reasonable Inference]. The crystallization pathway is uncertain because no CS/SS/TS analogue reports thin-film crystallization, and the strongest analogue evidence points to HPHT stabilization of metastable Si-rich frameworks (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). Composition control is also uncertain because ternary 1:1:6 ordering may have a narrow phase field [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization risk is the large Y–Ti size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], which can frustrate crystallization or promote disorder if kinetically trapped. The target description shows corner and face sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully activated. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct target cell size or ionic radii, so those specific checks rely partly on [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YTiSi6 is most likely a metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a robust ambient-pressure ground state, because its E_hull is low but nonzero at 0.050 eV/atom and many closest Si-rich analogues require high pressure (TS1, TS2, TS5; CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports that Si-rich rare-earth silicides can be synthesized, but usually by HPHT routes with quenching rather than simple ceramic sintering (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). The most important enabling factor is the availability of closely related HPHT silicide syntheses, especially YSi3 and CeSi5 (CS2/SS1/TS2; CS4/SS2/TS1). The most important limiting factor is phase competition and the need to order Ti into octahedral TiSi6 units within a Y–Si framework, which is not directly demonstrated by the analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is feasible but likely requires high-pressure synthesis and careful quench/stoichiometry control.
