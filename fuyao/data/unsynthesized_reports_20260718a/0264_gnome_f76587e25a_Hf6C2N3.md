### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score of 98/100 from the supplied target data. Chemically similar Hf2SnC and Ta3AlC2 are also hull-stable, supporting the plausibility of dense Hf/Ta carbide frameworks (CS1, CS4). Structurally and thermodynamically similar HfN, ZrN, Th2CrN3, Hf3AlN, and Th2MnN3 are all listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting stability of related nitride frameworks (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the exact ternary Hf–C–N phase has no experimental analogue in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because HfN and ZrN-related analogues require 1500–2200 °C and controlled inert/nitriding atmospheres (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). Long homogenization and hot pressing are also supported by HfN processing at 60–150 h (SS1/TS1). The source data do not show a volatile precursor requirement, so that item is treated as insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and neutral. The final F2 = 55 reflects feasible but severe refractory ceramic processing.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The solid-state route has poor kinetics because Hf, HfC, and HfN are refractory, and HfN analogues require prolonged high-temperature homogenization (SS1/TS1). ZrN carbide/nitride analogues show additional kinetic and phase-control issues from nitrogen loss and C/N exchange (SS2/TS2). Local structural motifs are favorable because HfN provides Hf–N octahedra and Hf2SnC provides CHf6 octahedra (SS1/TS1, CS4), but forming the monoclinic ordered Hf6C2N3 phase still requires partial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. This yields a low F3 = 37.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is supported by arc-melt refractory carbide analogues using 1400–2300 °C and Ar/vacuum treatment (CS2, CS3). Hf3AlN-like melt growth under pure Ar supports using melting/solidification for Hf nitride-related materials (SS4/TS4). The route avoids high pressure but still requires controlled atmosphere, high temperature, and post-annealing. The F2 = 60 reflects high-temperature but operationally standard refractory alloy synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Arc melting improves cation homogenization compared with solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and refractory carbide analogues demonstrate this route experimentally (CS2, CS3). However, graphite and HfN dissolution, nitrogen retention, and C/N ordering remain significant kinetic barriers [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2]. CS2 reports parasitic phases and difficult phase control in Pu–W–C systems, warning that melting does not guarantee single-phase product. F3 = 40 is therefore only slightly better than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids bulk temperatures above 1400 °C by using thin-film deposition and shorter diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires vacuum/reactive N₂ control, and nitrogen activity sensitivity is supported by nitride/carbonitride analogues (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). There is no direct thin-film evidence in CS/SS/TS, so the route basis from those sources is neutral 50. F2 = 85 reflects mild thermal severity but dependence on controlled deposition conditions.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Thin-film growth has better apparent kinetics because diffusion distances are short [Reasonable Inference]. However, crystallization into a monoclinic ordered Hf6C2N3 phase likely requires annealing, and bulk analogues show that nitride/carbonitride equilibration can be slow (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). Composition control is likely the main kinetic/process challenge because C/N exchange and nitrogen loss are known issues in related carbide/nitride systems (SS2/TS2). F3 = 57 reflects moderate kinetic feasibility but high uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so cation-size disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The structure description reports corner- and edge-sharing octahedral/square-pyramidal units but no face-sharing coexistence; HfN analogues also use edge/corner-sharing octahedra (SS1/TS1). The atom-count criterion is not triggered from the provided Hf6C2N3 formula, although the exact conventional cell size is not explicitly given. F4 = 92 reflects low amorphization risk if sufficient thermal energy is supplied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf6C2N3 is likely a ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple related Hf/Ta carbide and Hf/Zr nitride analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, CS4, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2). The strongest direct support comes from HfN-like Hf–N bond lengths and octahedral motifs (SS1/TS1) together with Hf2SnC-like CHf6 octahedral carbon environments (CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability and chemically compatible Hf–C/Hf–N bonding network supported by CS4 and SS1/TS1. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and stoichiometric control: refractory Hf–C–N phases require very high temperatures or thin-film nonequilibrium processing, and carbide/nitride analogues show nitrogen loss and C/N exchange issues (SS2/TS2). Overall, synthesis is plausible, but bulk single-phase synthesis is challenging; thin-film synthesis may be the most accessible exploratory route despite weaker direct precedent.
