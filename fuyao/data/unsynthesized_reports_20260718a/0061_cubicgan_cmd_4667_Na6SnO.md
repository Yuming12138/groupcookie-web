### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the most favorable rubric bin, so a high but not perfect score is justified. Thermodynamic analogues near this value, including Na₃As with E_hull ≈ 0.0425 eV/atom and Na₆FeS₄ with E_hull ≈ 0.0466 eV/atom, are reported as synthesizable (TS). Chemically and structurally similar Na–Sn phases such as Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₂MgSn are on the hull, supporting stability of Na-rich Sn frameworks (CS, SS). The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates Na₆SnO or a close Na–Sn–O phase.

#### Route 1 — F2 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to Na₆ZnSn₂ at 773 K and Na₂MgSn at 823–1023 K, avoiding the >1000 °C penalty (CS, SS). It requires sealed inert handling because Na-containing analogues are made in gloveboxes and sealed ampoules under Ar (CS, SS, TS). The main severity penalties are volatile/air-sensitive Na and air-sensitive Na₂O [Reasonable Inference], while high pressure is unsupported and unnecessary; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure need.

#### Route 1 — F3 kinetics

Direct fusion is kinetically plausible because Na-rich intermetallic analogues form by element fusion in sealed ampoules (CS, SS). However, Na₂O is less reactive than molten Na and Sn, and no CS/SS/TS analogue shows direct formation of the exact Na₆SnO structure, so structural reconstruction is assigned a low-moderate score. Diffusion is helped by molten Na/Sn-rich phases at the proposed temperature [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether oxide dissolves and orders uniformly into the Na-rich framework.

#### Route 2 — F2 condition severity

The Na-flux route keeps temperature moderate and follows sealed-ampoule precedent for Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃Hg₂ crystal growth (CS, SS). It does not require H₂, NH₃, high vacuum inside the reaction ampoule, or high pressure; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high-pressure or reactive-gas necessity. The penalties arise from large excess volatile Na and strong air sensitivity of Na/Na₂O [Reasonable Inference]. Operationally it is more hazardous than Route 1, but the rubric deductions are similar.

#### Route 2 — F3 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten excess Na should provide rapid mass transport and reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. Slow-cooling crystallization of Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃Hg₂ supports the feasibility of crystallizing Na-rich phases from reactive metal melts (CS, SS). Excess alkali aiding crystal growth is also seen in Li₈TeN₂/Li₈SeN₂, where single crystals required excess Li (CS). The main uncertainty is Na₂O solubility/reactivity in molten Na and competition with Na–Sn binaries.

#### Route 3 — F2 condition severity

The two-step route adds process complexity because it requires first making a Na–Sn alloy and then reacting it with Na₂O [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed Ar ampoules and glovebox handling are supported by Na₆ZnSn₂, Na₂MgSn, and Na₃As analogues (CS, SS, TS). It avoids high pressure and very high temperature, but it is penalized for multiple steps, volatile Na-containing materials, and air-sensitive precursors. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any simpler direct Na–Sn–O precursor route.

#### Route 3 — F3 kinetics

Preforming a Na–Sn alloy should reduce the need to create Na–Sn coordination from elemental Sn [Reasonable Inference], and Na–Sn-containing frameworks are accessible in Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₂MgSn (CS, SS). Reaction with Na₂O still requires oxygen incorporation and may leave unreacted oxide or competing Na–Sn binaries [Reasonable Inference]. The route may improve structural reconstruction relative to Route 1 but is less kinetically fluid than Route 2. No CS/SS/TS source directly confirms an alloy + oxide conversion to Na₆SnO, so uncertainty remains substantial.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has a simple formula and cubic symmetry, which tends to reduce amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives an exact target cell atom count, so C2 is not penalized. The size/role mismatch between Na and Sn is significant [Reasonable Inference], but structurally similar Na-rich phases with high-coordinate Sn or heavy atoms crystallize successfully, e.g. Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₂MgSn (CS, SS). The target polyhedral network includes corner and face sharing but not clearly simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so C3 is not fully triggered. Overall risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆SnO is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues around 0.04–0.06 eV/atom are experimentally accessible (TS). The synthesis case is supported well for the Na-rich metallic/Sn sublattice by Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₂MgSn analogues (CS, SS), but support is weaker for the exact Na–Sn–O chemistry because no direct Na₆SnO analogue appears in CS/SS/TS. The most enabling factor is the availability of sealed-ampoule Na-rich melt chemistry, especially Ta/Nb ampoule reactions under Ar with slow cooling (CS, SS). The most limiting factor is controlled oxygen incorporation from Na₂O without forming ordinary sodium oxides, residual Na₂O, Sn metal, or Na–Sn binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with Route 2 offering the best balance of thermodynamics, transport, and crystallization control.
