### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 thermodynamic stability bin. Several analogues are also ground-state phases: Ca₂AsI has E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1), ScAs has E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1, TS-1), Ti₂SbP has E_hull = 0.0 and the same P6₃/mmc topology (TS-2), and Sc₂Sb has E_hull = 0.0 (TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is moderately severe because TS-2 shows the exact H-phase analogue can be made at 800 °C in quartz tubes, and SS-1/TS-1 show Sc pnictides can be made by sealed/inert high-temperature reactions. The main deductions are vacuum/sealed-tube operation and P/Te volatility, which are inferred from P-containing sealed-tube syntheses in TS-2/TS-4 and Te ampoule synthesis in TS-3. CS gives chemically similar Caswellsilverite evidence but not Sc–P–Te volatility-specific conditions, so CS is partial and effectively neutral-to-moderate for this F2 route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are acceptable but not fast because elemental Sc is dense/refractory [Reasonable Inference], while Ti₂SbP formation at 800 °C for 48 h suggests the structure can form by solid-state diffusion (TS-2). The same P6₃/mmc octahedral topology strongly reduces structural-reconstruction penalty (TS-2). Uncertainty remains around ordering of P and Te on distinct anion sites [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is more severe because TS-5 uses sealed Ta ampoule reaction, pelletizing, arc melting twice, and long annealing for Sc₂Sb. The arc step likely exceeds 1400 °C locally [Reasonable Inference], and P/Te volatility makes open or prolonged melting risky [Reasonable Inference from TS-2/TS-3/TS-4]. SS-3 supports high-temperature Ta/RF methods for rare-earth antimonides, but it also indicates high-temperature and phase-control burdens.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Arc melting improves homogenization and reduces diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and TS-5 demonstrates that Sc-rich pnictides can be crystallized after arc melting and annealing. However, Sc₂Sb is structurally different from Sc₂TeP, so structural reconstruction is less directly supported than in TS-2. The major uncertainty is loss of P/Te during melting [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux synthesis is moderately severe because CS-4 uses 960 °C, vacuum sealing, glovebox handling, slow cooling, and decanting for ternary phosphides/arsenides. The process avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, but it adds multi-step handling and possible toxic Pb flux [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of Sc₂TeP, so those route-specific signals are neutral (50).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux should enhance mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS-4 successful flux growth of ternary phosphides/arsenides. The limitation is phase selectivity: CS-4 explicitly reports side products such as AlP/AlAs and some failed target syntheses. Therefore, kinetics are favorable but structural/chemical specificity is uncertain.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 avoids >1000 °C operation if adapted from TS-4, where ZrP is made with a 700–950 °C hot end and 300–400 °C P end in an evacuated quartz tube. It still requires vacuum-sealed ampoules and volatile P/Te management [Reasonable Inference from TS-4 and TS-3]. CS gives insufficient evidence for CVT of chemically similar Sc–P–Te phases, so CS is neutral (50) for Route 4 severity.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Vapor-assisted transport improves precursor availability and crystallization pathways for volatile anions, as shown by ZrP two-zone synthesis (TS-4) and Dy₃Se₄ chemical transportation from elements (SS-2). Composition control is the main uncertainty because both P and Te vapor pressures must be balanced to avoid ScP or Sc–Te binaries [Reasonable Inference]. The route scores highest because it combines moderate temperatures with enhanced mass transport.
