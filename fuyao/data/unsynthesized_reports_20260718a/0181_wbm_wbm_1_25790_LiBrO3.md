### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data (target). The closest thermodynamic analogues NaBrO₃, Mg(O₃Br)₂, and Sr(O₃Br)₂ are also on hull, which supports stable bromate salt chemistry (TS1–TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee hydrate-free crystallization from water [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 has very mild conditions because NaBrO₃ is grown by slow cooling from 40 to 30 °C or room-temperature evaporation, and Mg bromate is also obtained by aqueous evaporation (CS1, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS2). Kinetics are favorable because the reaction proceeds from dissolved ions rather than requiring high-temperature diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the BrO₃ trigonal unit is structurally conserved across close bromate analogues (target; CS1, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The main uncertainty is whether the Li-specific P6₃ framework crystallizes directly or competes with hydrates/polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 adds neutralization and filtration, so it is more operationally complex than direct evaporation but still uses mild aqueous conditions supported by Sr halate and alkali oxyanion acid/base syntheses (SS3, TS3; CS2–CS3). Kinetics should remain fast because Li⁺ and BrO₃⁻ are generated in solution [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is safe handling and stoichiometry control of bromic acid, for which CS/SS/TS give only indirect evidence through other oxyacids such as H₂SeO₄ and iodate reagents (CS2–CS3, SS5, TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is moderately more severe because it uses a sealed autoclave and controlled cooling, as in hydrothermal oxyanion analogues (CS3, SS5, TS4–TS5). Kinetics should be good in solution, and hydrothermal growth may help if ambient crystallization fails [Reasonable Inference]. However, the provided hydrothermal analogues are selenate/iodate systems rather than LiBrO₃, and bromate decomposition under sealed heating is a safety/phase risk [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a small, ordered ionic oxyanion compound with a simple LiBrO₃ formula and a limited number of crystallographic sites, which lowers amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Only the large cation size/chemistry mismatch check is triggered when Li⁺ and Br⁵⁺ are counted as cations [Reasonable Inference], while the target description does not indicate >40 atoms or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence (target). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, so F4 retains moderate uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiBrO₃ is likely a ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar bromates are also on hull (target; TS1–TS3). The synthesis evidence is strong for aqueous crystallization because the closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue NaBrO₃ is grown by slow cooling or slow evaporation under very mild conditions (CS1, SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the high solubility/reactivity expected for bromate salts in water, allowing crystallization from solution rather than solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference; CS1, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3]. The main limiting factor is Li-specific phase selection: Li⁺ may favor hydrates or a different polymorph, and no direct LiBrO₃ synthesis precedent is provided in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target should be highly synthesizable, with direct slow evaporation/cooling as the preferred first attempt.
