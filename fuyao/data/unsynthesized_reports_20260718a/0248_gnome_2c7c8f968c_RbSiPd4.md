### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. CS analogues NdCu₂Si₂, BaRh₂Si₂, NdCo₂Si₂, EuNi₂Si₂, and NdNiSi₂ are all reported with E_hull = 0.0, supporting the broader chemical plausibility of stable ternary silicides (CS1–CS5). SS/TS analogues include multiple stable silicides, especially EuPd₂Si₂ and Ni–Au–Si phases at E_hull = 0.0 (SS2, SS4, SS5; TS1–TS3), though SS1 PdSi being 0.13 eV/atom above hull introduces a minor caution for the Pd–Si framework analogue.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C because SS1 gives a viable 400–1000 °C range for alkali-rich Pd/Pt tetrel phases. It is penalized for volatile Rb requiring sealed containment, multi-step sealed handling, and Rb air sensitivity; SS1 directly supports sealed ampoules and excess alkali, while Rb air sensitivity is a [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient specific evidence for Rb volatility management, so SS1 is weighted most heavily.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, the Rb self-flux route benefits from molten/excess alkali, inferred from SS1’s use of 2–8× alkali excess. The close PdSi structural analogue in SS1, with 5-coordinate Pd/Si and Pd–Si ≈2.46 Å, strongly supports a modest structural reconstruction penalty rather than a complete reconstruction. Remaining uncertainty is whether Rb stabilizes the exact RbPd₄Si₄ topology or instead favors binary PdSi/Rb–Si side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because it includes a high-temperature Pd–Si prereaction, likely by arc melting, followed by sealed Rb annealing [Reasonable Inference from SS5/TS3 and SS2/TS1]. SS5/TS3 demonstrates argon arc melting and vacuum annealing for EuPd₂Si₂, but it does not address Rb volatility. This route is therefore penalized strongly for multi-step processing, high-temperature alloying, controlled atmosphere, and Rb handling.

#### F3 — Route 2

Pre-forming PdSi may reduce the need to build the Pd–Si framework from scratch, supported by SS1’s structural similarity. However, Rb diffusion into a preformed Pd–Si alloy is not directly shown in CS/SS/TS, so the intercalation/reaction step is uncertain and likely slower [Reasonable Inference]. SS5/TS3’s 850 °C/10 d anneal suggests long annealing is realistic for Pd–Si ternary ordering.

#### F2 — Route 3

The Sn-flux route inherits high-temperature and long-duration complexity from CS1, which uses 1050 °C, 3 days hold, and ~25 days slow cooling. It is additionally penalized because Rb is volatile and air-sensitive and would require sealed, compatible containment; this Rb-specific adaptation is inferred from SS1 and Rb chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides insufficient evidence for Sn flux specifically and is scored neutral for that factor.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth improves transport and can aid crystallization, as demonstrated by CS1’s single-crystal RCu₂Si₂ growth in Sn flux. SS1’s alkali-excess method also supports liquid/alkali-assisted synthesis for related systems. The main uncertainty is chemical transferability: CS1 uses rare-earth/Cu silicides, not Rb–Pd silicides, and Sn may introduce competing Sn-containing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct stoichiometric sealed annealing uses standard intermetallic conditions supported by CS2 and SS5/TS3, but the Rb-containing target requires sealed atmosphere and Rb excess/control [Reasonable Inference]. Compared with Route 1, it avoids deliberate flux removal and can be simpler operationally, but it lacks SS1’s explicit alkali-excess advantage. The severity score is moderate because temperature can be kept near 850–950 °C using SS1/SS5-style conditions.

#### F3 — Route 4

The direct route has lower kinetic score because elemental Pd and Si are relatively refractory and may react sluggishly without a large alkali flux [Reasonable Inference]. CS2–CS5 and SS5/TS3 show direct elemental silicide syntheses can work, but many require long anneals, high temperatures, or post-treatment.
