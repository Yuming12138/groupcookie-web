### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. CS analogues include multiple E_hull = 0 intermetallics and a chemically close Y–Fe–Te phase with E_hull = 0 (CS-1 to CS-5). SS/TS analogues include Er5Co2Te2 and YCoGe-type phases at or near the hull, supporting that related rare-earth–Co–p-block compounds are thermodynamically accessible (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the specific P6₃/mmc ordering is the experimentally preferred polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires 1000–1050 °C sealed-tube reaction, which is moderate-to-severe but directly precedented by CS-2 and SS-1/TS-1. The need for Ta/Nb containment and secondary silica sealing reflects Te volatility/reactivity and rare-earth sensitivity (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). No high pressure is required, which is favorable compared with more demanding synthesis classes; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any need for pressure, so pressure severity is scored as absent.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because elemental Y and Co are dense metals and require high-temperature diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 demonstrates that pelletized Y–M–Te mixtures can react at 1050 °C in 84 h, while SS-1/TS-1 shows related Er–Co–Te phases may require weeks and careful ramping. The structural mismatch between Cmcm analogues and the target P6₃/mmc phase lowers the structural-reconstruction score [Reasonable Inference from structures in CS-2 and SS-1].

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting routes are well supported for Y–Co–Ge/Sn and RCoSn analogues under Ar followed by annealing (SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). However, applying arc melting to Te-containing Y2Co2Te is harsher because Te volatility can cause composition drift; the telluride analogues instead favor sealed Ta/Nb tubes (CS-2, SS-1, TS-1). The route is therefore penalized for high temperature, controlled atmosphere, volatility, and multistep processing.

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc melting improves elemental mixing and can accelerate ordering, as seen in YCoGe/YCoSn and RCoSn preparations (SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The kinetic advantage is partly offset by the risk that Te is lost before the target phase equilibrates [Reasonable Inference]. Because no CS/SS/TS example directly arc-melts Y–Co–Te with Te retention, the kinetic score remains moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 3

The binary-precursor route has similar sealed-tube severity to Route 1 but adds precursor-preparation complexity. SS-1/TS-1 explicitly report use of rare-earth telluride and rare-earth/transition-metal binaries, while CS-2 uses Y2Te3 in Y–M–Te synthesis. The benefit is better chemical matching, but the route remains controlled-atmosphere, multistep, and Te-sensitive.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary precursors should improve local chemical homogeneity by pre-forming Y–Te and Y–Co bonds [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by CS-2 using Y2Te3 and SS-1/TS-1 using R2Te3/R3M-type binaries for rare-earth transition-metal tellurides. The uncertainty is that no provided analogue has exactly the target Y2Co2Te/P6₃/mmc structure, so the final ordering may still require long annealing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a compact intermetallic/telluride with a small formula and no evidence for a large, highly frustrated unit cell. The only triggered amorphization check is the large Y–Co size contrast [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS analogues are crystalline after conventional solid-state or arc-melting treatments, but they do not directly measure amorphization tendency; therefore CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for F4 and are treated as mostly neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y2Co2Te is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically close Y–M–Te phases made from elements plus Y2Te3 in sealed Ta tubes (CS-2), and from structurally/thermodynamically related Er5Co2Te2 made in Nb tubes under Ar (SS-1, TS-1). The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for rare-earth transition-metal tellurides forming under sealed high-temperature solid-state conditions. The most important limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Te retention and competition with Y–Te, Y–Co, and Co-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful sealed-tube metallurgy rather than a simple open crucible reaction.
