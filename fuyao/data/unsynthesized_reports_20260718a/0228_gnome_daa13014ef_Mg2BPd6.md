### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which maps to the maximum thermodynamic score under the provided rubric. This is reinforced by stable Pd–B analogues including SrPd4B, BePd3, BePd2, and Li2Pd3B at E_hull = 0.0 (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, SS5/TS4), and the isostructural Ti2Rh6B analogue is also on the hull (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is computational: no experimental Mg2BPd6 report is provided, so the hull prediction may not capture Mg volatility or finite-temperature disorder [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc-melting is well supported by the directly isostructural Ti2Rh6B route and by Pd–B analogue syntheses (CS1, CS3/SS4, SS1/TS1). The severity score is penalized because arc-melting implies very high local temperatures and because Mg is volatile/reactive under such conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The data do not specify exact arc temperatures, so temperature deductions are inferred rather than directly reported; insufficient exact temperature evidence from CS1, scored conservatively.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are helped by liquid/high-temperature homogenization and by the successful repeated-remelt syntheses of analogous intermetallic borides (CS1, CS3/SS4). The exact Fm-3m structural match to Ti2Rh6B strongly lowers the structural-reconstruction barrier (CS1). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Mg remains in the melt long enough to order into Mg2BPd6 rather than forming Mg–Pd binaries or being lost [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Sealed-tube synthesis at 900–950 °C is less thermally severe than arc-melting and is supported by Li2Pd3B and SrPd4B sealed-tube/anneal treatments (SS5/TS4, SS1/TS1). Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed handling, Mg volatility, and possible regrind/anneal cycles [Reasonable Inference]. The provided CS/SS/TS data do not directly report Mg–Pd–B sealed-tube synthesis, so exact risk is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solid-state kinetics are slower because Pd and B are refractory/dense elemental precursors [Reasonable Inference], even though Pd–B compounds can form at 900 °C in sealed Ta tubes (SS5/TS4). Structural motifs such as BPd6 octahedra are common in Li2Pd3B, SrPd4B, and YPd7B2 (SS5/TS4, SS1/TS1, CS3/SS4), which supports eventual crystallization. The uncertainty is whether diffusion at 850–950 °C is sufficient to reach the highly ordered Fm-3m Mg2BPd6 phase without prior melting [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
CVT/iodine-assisted routes operate at lower temperatures in Be–Pd and Pd–Ga analogues (SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, TS5), reducing thermal severity. However, evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile/corrosive halide transport agents add handling penalties (SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct Mg–B–Pd transport chemistry, so the route is speculative.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Transport routes can enhance kinetics for Pd-rich intermetallic crystal growth, as shown by BePd2/BePd3 and Pd2Ga (SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, TS5). For Mg2BPd6, the weakness is B incorporation and Mg iodide side chemistry, neither of which is validated by the supplied analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are scored moderate-low despite potentially favorable low-temperature mobility.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple high-symmetry intermetallic framework with cuboctahedral Mg coordination and octahedral BPd6 units, which generally disfavors amorphization relative to complex mixed-connectivity networks [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. One radius-mismatch trigger is assigned for Mg versus Pd size contrast [Reasonable Inference], but the target description lacks edge-sharing coexistence and appears to have a compact formula unit. SS/TS analogues include crystalline Pd–B phases with similar BPd6 motifs, supporting crystallization rather than glass formation (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS4).
