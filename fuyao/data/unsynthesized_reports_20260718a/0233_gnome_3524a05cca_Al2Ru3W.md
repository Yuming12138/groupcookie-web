### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided thermodynamic data. Chemically similar TaCo₂Al, Co₂HfAl, Ta₃Ru₅B₂, and Nb₃Ru₅B₂ are also on the hull or near it, supporting the idea that related refractory/Al/Ru intermetallics can be thermodynamically stable (CS1–CS5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Ru-containing compounds in SS/TS are mostly E_hull = 0, further supporting a high F1 score (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of nearby competing ordered Al–Ru–W phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires arc melting plus vacuum/inert annealing, which is directly supported for Al-Heusler analogues (CS1, CS2; SS5; TS5). The arc-melting step is severe because W and Ru are refractory metals, and the vacuum annealing contributes a controlled-atmosphere penalty [Reasonable Inference from precursor identity and CS1/CS2]. The use of Al introduces possible volatility and mass-loss concerns at arc temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no evidence for high pressure, so that penalty is not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The elemental metal precursors have low chemical activity compared with molecular or salt precursors, as all relevant analogues rely on elemental arc melting rather than solution chemistry (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). Arc melting partially overcomes slow diffusion by forming a molten alloy, and annealing helps develop ordering, as used for Al-Heusler analogues (CS1, CS2; SS5; TS5). The main uncertainty is whether the trigonal Heusler-like P-3m1 variant orders readily or competes with cubic/disordered Heusler-type arrangements [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is operationally simpler than Route 1 because it avoids vacuum annealing and uses only Ar arc melting with repeated remelting, as reported for Ru–Nb/Ta boride analogues (CS3, CS5; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2). It still incurs high-temperature penalties due to the refractory W/Ru components [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure, toxic precursor, or ≥3-step process is required based on the analogous protocols (CS3, CS5; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2). Al volatility remains a practical concern, but the route does not require a sealed system under the scoring rubric [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct arc melting provides rapid liquid-state mixing, and repeated remelting is explicitly used to improve homogeneity in Ru/refractory analogues (CS3, CS5; SS1–SS2; TS1–TS2). However, without annealing, long-range Heusler-like ordering may be incomplete, because close Al-Heusler analogues often use post-melting vacuum annealing for phase control (CS1, CS2; SS5; TS5). Thus, kinetics are moderate rather than high: melting helps form the alloy, but ordering and phase purification may remain limiting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 adds crushing/grinding/pelletizing and long annealing to the arc-melted product, mirroring Ru silicide analogue protocols (SS3, SS4; TS3, TS4). This increases severity because it is a multi-step route and uses evacuated sealed tubes at high temperature (SS3, SS4; TS3, TS4). The route does not require high pressure, and all precursors are normal elemental metals under standard handling [Reasonable Inference]. The sealed anneal is beneficial but adds process-control demands.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The extended anneal improves ordering and homogenization relative to direct arc melting, which is why structurally similar Ru intermetallics use 900 °C anneals for 7–10 days (SS3, SS4; TS3, TS4). Crushing, grinding, and pelletizing increase reaction interface area and should help overcome W/Ru diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference based on SS3/SS4 protocols]. The remaining kinetic limitation is that W and Ru diffusion at 850–950 °C may still be slow compared with lower-melting alloy systems [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, Route 3 has better kinetic phase-ordering prospects than Route 2 but harsher processing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a compact metallic intermetallic with bcc-like local coordination and no reported large mixed-polyhedral network, which lowers amorphization risk (target description). CS/SS/TS do not directly report amorphization tendencies, so their signal for F4 is insufficient and neutral for the amorphization-specific checks. The formula Al₂Ru₃W is small, and no >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network is indicated, supporting a low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is that rapid quenching from arc melting could trap disorder, but not necessarily amorphous material [Reasonable Inference].

Overall, the target is highly plausible synthetically, with direct arc melting as the best first attempt and annealing as the likely refinement step.
