### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues in TS are also all E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including K₂NiP₂, Rb₂PtAs₂, Cs₂PtSb₂, K₂NaBP₂, and K₂CuP (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar P2₁3 phosphides BaPtP, SrIrP, and BaIrP are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS2, CS4, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 inherits demanding conditions from BaPtP, which requires 950–1050 °C, 1–2 weeks, slow initial heating, and intermediate grinding under Ar (CS5). K-containing phosphide analogues require sealed or protected vessels, such as evacuated silica tubes or welded iron crucibles in quartz ampoules (SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5). The severity score is reduced because K and P are volatile/reactive and because a direct high-temperature elemental reaction may need multiple grind/anneal cycles. The uncertainty is whether KPtP forms below 1000 °C, which would improve F2.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct elemental reaction benefits from highly reactive K and P, but Pt is refractory and can remain unreacted without long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. BaPtP required 1–2 weeks and intermediate grinding to obtain homogeneous preparations (CS5), indicating nontrivial diffusion/order kinetics. The strong positive kinetic factor is that exact P2₁3 analogues exist for BaPtP, SrIrP, and BaIrP (CS2, CS4, CS5). Uncertainty remains because replacing alkaline-earth Ba/Sr by alkali K may alter phase relations.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 avoids the >1000 °C penalty by using a 500 °C prereaction followed by 800–900 °C main anneal, directly supported by alkali Pt pnictide synthesis (SS2/TS2). It still requires sealed/inert handling because K and P are volatile and air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed-vessel K phosphide syntheses (SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5). Multiple operations—prereaction, grinding, annealing—are justified by CS5 and SS2/TS2. The main uncertainty is whether 800–900 °C is sufficient to crystallize the P2₁3 KPtP phase.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The staged prereaction improves kinetics by first forming K–P-containing intermediates before the high-temperature Pt reaction [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the 500 °C prereaction in SS2/TS2. Pt diffusion remains the limiting kinetic factor, but Pt pnictides form at 800–900 °C in SS2/TS2 and BaPtP forms at 950–1050 °C in CS5. Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target has the same cubic P2₁3 topology as BaPtP, SrIrP, and BaIrP (CS2, CS4, CS5). Uncertainty is possible competition from binary K–P and Pt–P phases.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

The binary phosphide route uses a precedent for phosphide precursors in sealed Nb/steel ampoules at 1000 K (SS4/TS4). It also parallels KAuS synthesis, where preformed K₂S is reacted with Au/chalcogen under Ar/sealed conditions (CS1). Severity is penalized because K phosphide requires prior synthesis, sealed handling, and strict inert atmosphere [Reasonable Inference]. The route is plausible but more operationally complex than Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Preformed K phosphide should reduce the uncontrolled direct K–P exotherm and provide a chemically active K–P source [Reasonable Inference]. SS4/TS4 show that binary phosphide routes can form complex K phosphides at about 727 °C, while SS2/TS2 show Pt pnictides forming at 800–900 °C. However, the final P2₁3 KPtP structure is not inherited from the precursor, so reconstruction is more substantial. The main uncertainty is which K phosphide composition gives the correct K/P chemical potential.

#### F2 — Route 4 severity

The alkali-rich route is supported by excess alkali use in K₂CuP synthesis to avoid by-products (SS5/TS5) and by slow cooling/annealing used for crystal growth in alkali gold chalcogenides (CS3). It is penalized for sealed/vacuum handling, excess volatile K, air sensitivity, and multi-step flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids extreme temperature and pressure, so severity is not prohibitive. The uncertainty is whether K-rich conditions stabilize unwanted K–P-rich phases.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Excess K can improve mass transport and compensate volatilization [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the need for excess alkali in SS5/TS5. Slow cooling and low-temperature annealing improve crystallinity in related alkali chalcogenides (CS3). The same P2₁3 Pt phosphide framework is known for BaPtP (CS5), supporting eventual crystallization. The limiting kinetic issue is phase selectivity in a flux-like K-rich melt.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small-cell crystalline intermetallic phosphide rather than a large complex framework, and P2₁3 analogues such as BaPtP/SrIrP/BaIrP have compact 1:1:1 structures (CS2, CS4, CS5). The main amorphization flag is the large K–Pt size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient quantitative ionic-radius evidence, so that subclaim is not directly sourced. No evidence indicates >40 atoms/cell or mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity for the target; CS/SS/TS are insufficient for explicit target polyhedral sharing, so C3 is treated neutrally. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KPtP is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and all closest thermodynamic analogues listed are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The synthesis case is strengthened by exact structural analogues in the cubic P2₁3 family, especially BaPtP with nearly matching Pt–P coordination and bond length (CS5), plus SrIrP and BaIrP (CS2, CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated stability and synthesizability of P2₁3 transition-metal phosphides under conventional sealed-tube solid-state conditions (CS2, CS4, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is handling and retaining volatile, air-sensitive K and P while reacting them with refractory Pt, which likely requires staged heating, sealed ampoules, and repeated annealing (CS5; SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). Overall, the target is experimentally plausible, with the staged prereaction route offering the best balance of phase control and manageable conditions.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy from CS/SS/TS |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Volatile K/P loss and ampoule pressure | High | Use sealed ampoules/crucibles as in K₂NiP₂ and K₂CuP syntheses (SS1, SS5; TS1, TS5); apply slow heating as in BaPtP synthesis to prevent vigorous reaction/crucible cracking (CS5). |
| 2 | All | Air/moisture sensitivity of K-containing reactants/products | High | Handle in Ar glovebox; alkali chalcogenide/pnictide analogues are prepared under Ar or sealed conditions (CS1, CS3; SS2/TS2). |
| 3 | All | Slow Pt incorporation / refractory Pt diffusion | Medium–High | Use fine Pt powder, long annealing, and intermediate grinding; BaPtP required 1–2 weeks and grinding under Ar for homogeneous products (CS5). |
| 4 | All | Competing Pt–P, K–P, or residual Pt phases | Medium | Employ staged prereaction and repeated grinding/tempering; phase-pure samples in related Ir/P systems required multiple tempering cycles and careful control (CS2, CS4). |
| 5 | Route 1 | Violent direct K–P reaction during ramp | High | Slow initial heating over an extended period, modeled on CS5 where heating over two days to about 1000 °C prevented crucible cracking. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Incomplete conversion after 800–900 °C anneal | Medium | Add a second anneal or raise to 950–1000 °C if Pt remains; longer/higher-temperature annealing is supported by BaPtP synthesis at 950–1050 °C (CS5). |
