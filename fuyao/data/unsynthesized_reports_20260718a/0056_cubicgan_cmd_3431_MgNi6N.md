### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the favorable cutoff in the rubric; this supports a high F1 score. CS analogues are generally stable or near-stable, including Mg₃Ni₂₀P₆ at 0 eV/atom and Ni₂₁Sn₂P₆ at 0.00046 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-5). SS analogues also include stable Ni-rich intermetallics and Mg–Ni frameworks, though MgNi₃ is metastable at 0.0936 eV/atom (SS-3). TS evidence is mixed because thermodynamic neighbors include both moderately metastable Mg–Ni entries and stable/near-stable phosphide analogues (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids GPa pressure and avoids >1000 °C holds, which is favorable relative to high-pressure Mg–Ni–H syntheses (SS-3, TS-1, TS-4). However, it needs sealed handling because Mg is volatile at high temperature, and Mg excess was explicitly needed in Mg–Ni–P synthesis (CS-1, TS-2). Multi-step grinding/pre-reaction/annealing is supported by CS-1 and TS-2, but it lowers the F2 score. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the safest nitrogen source, so N-source severity is partly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state kinetics are moderate because Mg–Ni intermetallic analogues form at 900–1100 °C under Ar (CS-2, SS-4, SS-5). The target’s Mg–Ni bond length of 2.57 Å is close to MgNi₃’s 2.58 Å (SS-3), which helps structural plausibility. The main kinetic penalty is that forming MgNi₆N from metal/nitride powders requires anion incorporation and framework ordering, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence. Long annealing/regrinding in analogues supports a cautious F3 score (CS-1, CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 2

High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized because Mg–Ni–H analogues use 2.8–7.4 GPa or >2 GPa conditions (SS-3, TS-1, TS-4). Specialized high-pressure apparatus is explicitly required in those analogues, including toroidal or cubic-anvil systems (SS-3, TS-4). This route may reduce phase competition but is experimentally severe. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for N-specific pressure requirements, so applying the hydride precedent is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic advantage of Route 2 is starting from MgNi₂/MgNi₃-like precursors, which are close to the target Mg–Ni framework (SS-3, TS-1, TS-4). High pressure can stabilize dense interstitial/light-anion Mg–Ni phases in hydride analogues (SS-3, TS-1). However, nitrogen is less mobile and chemically different from hydrogen, so direct transfer is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, F3 is better than Route 1 but not high.

#### F2 — Route 3

Arc melting and remelting are standard for Ni-rich intermetallic analogues (SS-1, SS-2, CS-4), but local temperatures are high and Mg loss is likely. Mg excess is supported by CS-1 and TS-2. A separate nitridation step introduces reactive-atmosphere and multi-step penalties. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence that a nitriding anneal preserves the desired Mg–Ni framework.

#### F3 — Route 3

Pre-alloying improves cation homogeneity, supported by arc-melted Eu–Ni–In and La–Co–Cu analogues (SS-1, SS-2, CS-4). Structural transfer is plausible because the target Mg–Ni distance matches MgNi₃ closely (SS-3). The limiting kinetic step is nitrogen uptake into a dense alloy, which is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS and is therefore [Reasonable Inference]. This gives moderate-to-good kinetics but significant uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film growth avoids high bulk temperatures and GPa pressure, but it requires vacuum/reactive plasma conditions [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no direct thin-film evidence, so their route-specific support is insufficient and neutral. Mg volatility/composition drift is the major processing penalty [Reasonable Inference]. Overall severity is moderate, but the evidence basis is weak.

#### F3 — Route 4

Vapor availability is acceptable for Mg and Ni targets, but Mg composition control is difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Nitrogen incorporation requires reactive N₂ plasma, and no CS/SS/TS analogue demonstrates direct crystallization of MgNi₆N-like films. Post-annealing is likely needed, reducing crystallization-pathway confidence [Reasonable Inference]. Thin films may help metastable stabilization, but kinetics and stoichiometry remain uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple small formula and a highly connected crystalline Mg–Ni/Ni–N framework, which argues against severe amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar Ni-rich cuboctahedral networks crystallize in several SS analogues (SS-1 to SS-5), and chemically similar Ni-rich phosphides/intermetallics crystallize by bulk routes (CS-1 to CS-5). The Mg/Ni size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports amorphous Mg–Ni–N products, so that specific risk has insufficient evidence and is treated cautiously.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgNi₆N is most likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support formation of Ni-rich Mg-containing intermetallic frameworks under Ar at 800–1100 °C, especially with Mg excess (CS-1, CS-2, SS-4, SS-5). The TS analogues add caution because related Mg–Ni light-anion phases often require high pressure or show metastability (TS-1, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the close Mg–Ni framework match to MgNi₃, with Mg–Ni distances of about 2.57–2.58 Å (SS-3, TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is nitrogen incorporation and retention, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Mg–Ni–N synthesis.
