### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

Sc₂PSe has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. CS provides strong supporting evidence because several chemically related phosphides, including ScCoP, Ca₃Ga₂P₄, and CaCo₂P₂, are also E_hull = 0 (CS). TS is also supportive because ZrP, ScS, ZrGeSe, PrSe, and YS are E_hull = 0 thermodynamic analogs (TS). SS is mixed: P6₃/mmc ZrP is E_hull = 0, but the closest rare-earth selenide structural analogs are slightly above hull at ~0.048–0.058 eV/atom, still within a likely accessible range (SS).

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct Solid-State

The direct sealed-ampoule route is moderately severe because vacuum-sealed tubes are required, consistent with ZrGeSe, ZrP, ScCoP, and CaCo₂P₂ analogs (TS, SS, CS). The recommended 850–950 °C range avoids the >1000 °C penalty, although TS includes a 1100 °C ZrGeSe synthesis, so some uncertainty remains if the target requires higher temperature (TS). P and Se volatility make sealing essential [Reasonable Inference], and Sc/P/Se handling adds precursor risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for Sc₂PSe direct solid-state synthesis, so SS is neutral for this route-specific severity beyond the ZrP structural analogy.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct Solid-State

Kinetics are only moderate because Sc metal is refractory and direct reaction with P/Se may require significant solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives partial support because ZrGeSe forms nearly quantitatively after 2 days at 1100 °C, and YSe/YTe can form from elements in sealed ampoules, although impurity issues are reported for YSe (TS). CS does not demonstrate direct Sc₂PSe-like synthesis; ScCoP uses Sn flux, suggesting direct solid-state may be less favorable (CS). SS gives useful structural support via P6₃/mmc ZrP but insufficient evidence for direct Sc–P–Se kinetics, so its route-specific kinetic signal is limited.

#### F2 — Route 2: Flux Growth

Flux growth has lower peak-temperature severity than many solid-state chalcogenide syntheses because ScCoP forms in Sn at 850 °C and CaCo₂P₂ forms in Sn at 950 °C (CS). It still requires evacuated sealed tubes, volatile P/Se control, and multiple steps including slow cooling and flux removal (CS; [Reasonable Inference] for Se-containing target). CS gives the strongest support for this route, while TS and SS provide insufficient direct flux evidence for Sc₂PSe and are treated as neutral for flux-specific severity. The route is therefore moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2: Flux Growth

Flux improves kinetics by providing a liquid medium, which is supported by ScCoP, CaCo₂P₂, and Ca–Ga–P flux syntheses (CS). The ability to synthesize ScCoP at 850 °C in Sn indicates that Sc–P formation can proceed below the temperatures needed for many refractory solid-state reactions (CS). However, CS also reports phase-control challenges, including multiple phosphide phases and impurities in related systems, which limits the kinetics/phase-control score (CS). SS and TS are structurally/thermodynamically supportive but provide insufficient direct signal for flux kinetics and are neutral for this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 3: I₂-CVT

CVT is the most condition-severe route because CuScS₂ uses a 1050–950 °C gradient and Sc sulfide transport uses 950 °C with I₂ in sealed ampoules (CS, TS). It requires vacuum sealing, a two-zone furnace, iodine handling, and typically a prereacted source charge (CS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS ZrP transport evidence supports feasibility of gradient-based synthesis but also confirms the need for controlled temperature zones (SS, TS). Therefore F2 is lower than the flux and direct routes.

#### F3 — Route 3: I₂-CVT

CVT has favorable mass-transport kinetics because iodine-mediated transport avoids relying entirely on solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CuScS₂ and Sc sulfide examples demonstrate that Sc-containing chalcogenide materials can be grown by I₂ transport (CS, TS). Structurally similar P6₃/mmc ZrP was prepared in a temperature-gradient sealed-tube arrangement, supporting crystallization of a related octahedral framework (SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is whether mixed P/Se ordering in Sc₂PSe survives the iodine transport chemistry without disproportionation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization check is triggered: the target explicitly contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing Sc-centered polyhedra. Cation radius mismatch is not applicable because Sc is the only cation [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is expected to be small from the Sc₂PSe formula and P6₃/mmc ZrP analogy (SS, TS), so the >40-atom criterion is not triggered. SS rare-earth selenide analogs with mixed connectivity are crystallized phases, which reduces concern that this connectivity alone prevents crystallization (SS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂PSe is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically or thermodynamically related compounds are also on the hull (CS, TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS flux-grown ScCoP and related phosphides, plus SS/TS P6₃/mmc ZrP, which closely resembles the target’s hexagonal octahedral framework (CS, SS, TS). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a stable, compact Sc–P/Se octahedral network with favorable thermodynamics and bond lengths compatible with known Sc–P and Sc–chalcogen compounds (CS, TS). The main limiting factor is stoichiometry control involving volatile P and Se in the presence of reactive Sc, especially avoiding binary Sc–P, Sc–Se, or flux-incorporated side phases [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues are reported in CS and TS analogs]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with flux growth slightly preferred over direct solid-state and CVT because it balances lower temperature, improved kinetics, and direct Sc–phosphide precedent (CS).
