### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability threshold specified for a 90–100 score. TS analogues show that materials around this metastability range can be synthesized, including AgBiO3 at 0.0499 eV/atom (TS-3) and K2PbO3 at 0.0284 eV/atom (TS-5). CS analogues include stable PbSeO4 and HgSO4 phases at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-4, CS-3), supporting the general stability of heavy-metal oxysalts. Remaining uncertainty is chemical: the target’s SO6 octahedron is not directly supported by the sulfate analogue HgSO4, where sulfur is tetrahedral (CS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1

Hydrothermal analogues use moderate temperatures, 493 K to 250 °C, in Teflon-lined vessels (CS-4, CS-5), so no high-temperature or >5 GPa penalty applies. The only severity deduction is for toxic Pb precursors, which is a [Reasonable Inference] based on standard lead chemistry and the Pb-containing analogue in CS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for H2/NH3/vacuum atmosphere, so that factor is neutral. The resulting F2 is high because hydrothermal synthesis is routine for the closest oxysalt analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because CS-4 and CS-5 demonstrate that Pb/Se–O and Hg/Se–O oxysalts crystallize from aqueous hydrothermal media at moderate temperature. Solution transport reduces cation diffusion limitations relative to bulk solid-state synthesis, as shown by crystal formation in CS-4 and CS-5. The major kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: CS-3 and CS-4 contain tetrahedral sulfate/selenate motifs, whereas the target has SO6 octahedra, so nucleating the target framework may be difficult [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The slow-evaporation route uses moderate hot-acid temperatures, about 200 °C plus 350 °C tempering, as shown for HgSO4 (CS-3). It avoids high pressure and specialized atmosphere, and structurally similar oxysalts can crystallize by mild slow evaporation from aqueous solution (SS-1, SS-5). The main severity penalty is toxic Pb and corrosive acid handling [Reasonable Inference plus CS-3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a need for sealed volatile containment, so that factor is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution growth provides good precursor activity and avoids long-range solid diffusion, supported by HgSO4 crystallization from hot sulfuric acid (CS-3) and Cs selenite single-crystal growth by slow evaporation (SS-5). However, this route is likely to favor conventional sulfate phases because HgSO4 contains tetrahedral SO4 units with S–O ≈1.47–1.49 Å (CS-3). The target’s SO6 octahedral environment is therefore not kinetically supported by the closest sulfate analogue, reducing F3. The route is plausible for exploring Pb–S–O crystallization but may not selectively form cubic PbSO6 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Solid-state Pb oxide analogues are synthesized at 560–700 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies (TS-4, TS-5). However, TS-5 requires dried argon, vacuum sealing, Ag capsules, and long 14–15 d heat treatments, triggering controlled-atmosphere, sealed-system, and multi-step penalties. Toxic Pb precursors add another penalty [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS suggests sulfate solid-state synthesis is preferred; CS instead favors hydrothermal/solution chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state kinetics are weaker because oxide/sulfate solids are less reactive than aqueous precursors, and TS-5 required long annealing times for Pb oxides. Moderate temperatures are accessible, but sulfur oxide fugacity and oxygen stoichiometry may be hard to control in a sealed ampoule [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is likely substantial because the chemically similar sulfate/selenate analogues are not cubic PbSO6-type phases (CS-3, CS-4). Therefore Route 3 is a backup exploratory route rather than the best synthesis path.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-oxygen-pressure synthesis is operationally challenging, but the scoring table only penalizes >5 GPa pressure, while TS-2 reports up to 400 MPa, below that threshold. The route still receives deductions for multi-operation handling and toxic Pb precursors [Reasonable Inference]. TS-2 demonstrates that oxygen-rich heavy-metal oxides may require high oxygen pressure, but provides insufficient direct evidence for Pb–S–O compounds. Thus F2 is moderate-high by the formal rubric but practically challenging.

#### F3 — Route 4

Hydrothermal transport and water mineralization improve kinetics, consistent with CS-4, CS-5, and TS-2. High oxygen pressure may help stabilize oxygen-rich or highly oxidized motifs [Reasonable Inference], addressing the unusual PbSO6 stoichiometry. However, no TS analogue directly shows octahedral sulfur oxysalt formation, and CS sulfate chemistry points to tetrahedral SO4 (CS-3). This keeps F3 below Route 1 despite the stronger oxygen chemical-potential control.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has strongly dissimilar cation coordination environments: Pb is 12-coordinate in PbO12 cuboctahedra, while S is 6-coordinate in SO6 octahedra, suggesting a large cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description reports corner sharing and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the full mixed vertex + edge + face criterion is not triggered. The full unit-cell atom count is not given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the >40-atom check, so it is not counted as a trigger. The resulting low–medium amorphization tendency is mainly from cation-size/coordination mismatch.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a clearly established ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and the closest TS analogue AgBiO3 is also near this range at 0.0499 eV/atom (TS-3). Chemically similar oxysalts strongly support hydrothermal and solution synthesis of Pb/Se–O, Hg/S–O, and Hg/Se–O compounds (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5), but they do not directly validate octahedral SO6 in PbSO6. The most critical enabling factor is moderate-temperature hydrothermal mobility, which produced PbSeO4 at 493 K and HgSeO4 at 200–250 °C (CS-4, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is the unusual target local chemistry: conventional sulfate analogues contain tetrahedral SO4 rather than SO6 octahedra (CS-3). Therefore synthesis is plausible but will require careful oxidizing solution chemistry and phase-control screening.
