### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a near-maximum thermodynamic score. This is reinforced by Hf3AlN, the closest Hf3XN/Cmcm analogue, also having E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). Several Hf–N and Hf–pnictide analogues are also on or near the hull, including HfN and Hf3P2 (SS-5; TS-5; SS-3; TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially with volatile As [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc / Induction Melt Growth

The route is supported by Hf3AlN melt growth under pure Ar with slow cooling (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). Severity is elevated because refractory Hf-containing samples require very high local temperatures and specialized melting equipment, as also seen in Hf3Ir5B2 and Hf phosphide syntheses (CS-4; SS-3). The main uncertainty is As retention during melting, which is not directly addressed by CS/SS/TS and is therefore treated as a [Reasonable Inference] risk.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc / Induction Melt Growth

Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state synthesis because melting bypasses long-range solid diffusion, and Hf3AlN was successfully made by a melt/slow-cool route (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). The structural reconstruction score is high because Hf3AlN has the same Cmcm Hf3XN motif with NHf6 octahedral connectivity (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). The kinetic uncertainty is whether As substitution changes liquidus behavior or promotes competing Hf–As/HfN phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State Reaction

This route avoids the most severe open-melt As loss but requires sealed or evacuated handling, which increases condition severity [Reasonable Inference]. Long sealed anneals are supported for ternary nitrides such as Zr2InN at 500–900 °C for 750–850 h (SS-2; TS-1). HfN-related phases required long high-temperature treatments, indicating that Hf–N equilibration can be demanding (SS-5; TS-5). The uncertainty is whether a sealed ampoule can tolerate the chemical activity of Hf and As without liner reactions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State Reaction

Solid-state kinetics are the main weakness because HfN and Hf metal are refractory, and analogues show long annealing times (SS-2; TS-1; SS-5; TS-5). Preformed HfAs and HfN improve chemical matching relative to elements [Reasonable Inference], but diffusion through dense binary phases remains slow. Structural reconstruction is plausible due to the Hf3AlN analogue (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3), but no direct sealed-ampoule Hf3AsN analogue is provided.

#### F2 — Route 3: Metal Flux Growth

Flux growth is supported by Hf7P4 and Hf3P2, where Fe or Co-assisted synthesis helped obtain crystalline Hf pnictides (SS-1; SS-3; TS-2; TS-4). The route has moderate-to-high severity because it requires high temperature, controlled atmosphere or sealed processing, and flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. The biggest condition uncertainty is whether Fe/Co flux reacts with As/N to form competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Metal Flux Growth

Flux should improve mass transport compared with dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], and Hf phosphide single crystals were obtained using Fe/Co-assisted high-temperature treatments (SS-1; SS-3; TS-2; TS-4). However, the Hf7P4 evidence also shows phase selectivity can be narrow, since Fe was needed and otherwise mixtures formed (SS-1; TS-4). Structural similarity is moderate rather than high because these analogues are Hf pnictides without the target nitride sublattice (SS-1; SS-3).

#### F2 — Route 4: Reactive Sputtering / Co-Sputtering

Sputtering has lower thermal severity than bulk melting, and Ta2N was deposited by reactive sputtering in Ar/N2 at low substrate temperature (CS-5). The route still requires high-vacuum plasma equipment and composition control, as CS-5 shows nitrogen partial pressure is a key parameter. As toxicity and volatility increase handling severity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports sputtering specifically, so SS and TS are neutral = 50 for this route’s deposition-specific condition signal.

#### F3 — Route 4: Reactive Sputtering / Co-Sputtering

The kinetic benefit is that atom-by-atom deposition can bypass bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and Ta2N demonstrates refractory nitride formation by sputtering (CS-5). The weakness is that no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates sputtered ternary Hf–As–N or direct crystallization of Cmcm Hf3XN films, so crystallization may require annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is uncertain because As and N activities must both be controlled, while CS-5 shows nitride phase formation is sensitive to N2 partial pressure.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one metal/cation species, Hf, so cation-size disorder is not expected to drive amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. The closest Hf3AlN analogue has a simple Cmcm Hf3XN structure with corner/edge-sharing NHf6 octahedra and no face-sharing in the reported description (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). Exact target unit-cell atom count is not supplied, and CS/SS/TS do not directly give the target atom count, so that sub-signal is neutral from CS/SS/TS. Overall amorphization risk is low, especially for bulk slow-cooled samples [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf3AsN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Hf3AlN analogue is also hull-stable (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). The strongest synthesis support comes from Hf3AlN, which shares Cmcm symmetry, Hf3XN stoichiometry, similar Hf–N distances, and NHf6 octahedral connectivity (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly analogous Cmcm Hf3AlN phase synthesized by melt growth under Ar (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is arsenic volatility/toxicity during high-temperature Hf processing, which is not directly resolved by the provided analogues and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference] risk. Overall, the material appears synthesizable but likely requires careful As activity control and post-synthesis phase-purity optimization.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Arsenic volatility and toxicity | High | Use pre-synthesized HfAs/HfAs2 rather than elemental As where possible [Reasonable Inference]; sealed/controlled-atmosphere processing is supported by sealed nitride and Hf phosphide analogues (SS-2; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). |
| 2 | All | Competing HfN, HfAs, HfAs2, or Hf-rich binary phases | High | Use stoichiometric binary precursors and repeated annealing/remelting; phase-purity concerns and homogenization steps are supported by Hf3Ir5B2 and HfN-related analogues (CS-4; SS-5; TS-5). |
| 3 | Route 1 | Loss of As during open arc/induction melting | High | Prealloy As into HfAs/HfAs2 and use slight As excess [Reasonable Inference]; use rapid melt/remelt under Ar based on Hf3AlN melt route (CS-1; SS-4; TS-3). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Slow solid-state diffusion through refractory HfN/HfAs | High | Use fine powders, pelletizing, multiple regrinds, and long anneals; long-duration ternary nitride synthesis is supported by Zr2InN at 750–850 h (SS-2; TS-1). |
| 5 | Route 3 | Flux incorporation or formation of Fe/Co arsenides/nitrides | Medium–High | Screen Fe vs Co flux and compare flux-free controls [Reasonable Inference]; Hf7P4 required Fe flux but showed phase-control sensitivity (SS-1; TS-4). |
| 6 | Route 4 | Narrow Hf:As:N composition window in sputtering | High | Calibrate deposition rates and N2 partial pressure; Ta2N sputtering shows nitride phase formation depends on controlled Ar/N2 conditions (CS-5). |
| 7 | Route 4 | Poor crystallinity or amorphous film after deposition | Medium | Use heated substrate or post-anneal under capped/sealed conditions [Reasonable Inference]; sputtering analogue confirms nitride deposition but not Cmcm ternary crystallization (CS-5). |
| 8 | All | Oxygen contamination of Hf/HfN surfaces | Medium | Handle powders under Ar and use high-purity Ar during melting; inert Ar processing is used in Hf3AlN and arc-melted analogues (CS-1; CS-4; SS-4; TS-3). |
