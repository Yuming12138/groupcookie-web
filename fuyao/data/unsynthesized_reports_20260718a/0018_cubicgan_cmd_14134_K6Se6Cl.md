### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the most favorable bin in the scoring rubric. CS analogues K3SbSe4, K3SnSe3, K3AlSe3, K3GaSe3, and K3SbSe3 are all reported with E_hull = 0.0, supporting the stability of chemically related K–Se frameworks (CS-1 to CS-5). SS analogues are also stable alkali selenide frameworks with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1 to SS-5). TS analogues span E_hull ≈ 0.024–0.075 eV/atom and include experimentally reported phases, supporting that phases near 0.05 eV/atom may be accessible, but direct evidence for K–Se–Cl is insufficient from TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the same general synthesis platform as several K–Se ternaries: sealed or evacuated graphitized ampoules at 875–1075 K or about 900 °C (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, SS-2). The severity score is reduced because K and Se are volatile/reactive, sealed ampoules and inert/vacuum handling are required, and related alkali selenides are air/moisture sensitive (CS-2, CS-5, SS-2). Kinetic support is good for K–Se framework formation because analogous K–Se bond lengths and synthesis temperatures are compatible with the target (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4), but no analogue demonstrates Se–Cl bonding, so the structural-reconstruction component remains uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by multiple alkali selenide syntheses using H₂ saturated with selenium at 750–850 °C (CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5). The route has moderate-to-high condition severity because H₂/Se gas handling is reactive and potentially hazardous, and products require inert handling (CS-5, SS-1, SS-3). Kinetics are likely adequate for alkali selenide formation because crystalline products form in about 9 h in several analogues (CS-5, SS-1, SS-3), but chloride incorporation from KCl is not proven in CS/SS and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is motivated by TS evidence that low-temperature solution/autogenous-pressure routes can access alkali selenide phases or phases unavailable by simple direct reaction, such as Cs2Se-II and K6Cd4Sn3Se13 (TS-2, TS-5). Its condition severity is penalized for high-pressure NH₃ or sealed-solvent handling, air-sensitive K2Se, and likely multistep precursor preparation (TS-2, TS-5). Kinetically, preformed K2Se and solvothermal transport may be favorable, but phase selection is uncertain because TS-2 reports pressure-dependent phase formation and TS-5 reports that direct solid-state synthesis failed for the target analogue K6Cd4Sn3Se13.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, K, so cation-size disorder does not contribute to amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The structure description includes corner- and edge-sharing SeK4Cl square pyramids, but not face-sharing, so the full mixed-connectivity trigger is not met from the provided target structure. The likely conventional Fm-3m cell may exceed 40 atoms because K6Se6Cl has 13 atoms per formula unit and a common cubic F-centered Z = 4 would give 52 atoms, but exact Z is not provided, so this is a moderate-uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K6Se6Cl is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a clearly established ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom rather than 0.0. The CS and SS analogues strongly support formation of K–Se frameworks under sealed-ampoule or reactive selenium atmospheres, but they do not directly support the unusual Se–Cl-containing Fm-3m target structure. The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated accessibility of air-sensitive alkali selenides from simple precursors at 600–900 °C in sealed or controlled atmospheres (CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, CS-5). The most important limiting factor is chloride incorporation into a Se-coordinated Cl-centered framework, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS and must be treated as a [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful stoichiometry, sealed conditions, and screening of temperature/cooling profiles.
