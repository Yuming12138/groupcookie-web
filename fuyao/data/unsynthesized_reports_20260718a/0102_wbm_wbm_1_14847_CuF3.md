### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target CuF3 is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a very high thermodynamic score. Several close analogues are also zero-hull, especially NiF3, CuAuF5, Ag2F5, and Ag(AuF4)2 (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1, SS-3, TS-3, SS-4, TS-4, SS-5, TS-5). The main uncertainty is that computed zero-hull stability does not guarantee easy experimental isolation for high-valent fluorides, which can be kinetically or chemically fragile [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable, but it uses anhydrous HF, specialized Teflon/FEP hardware, low-temperature handling, and hazardous fluorocomplex salts (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The NiF3 route also involves multiple operations including dissolution, cooling, incremental addition, precipitation, and extensive washing (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Therefore the condition severity score is limited mainly by safety and handling rather than thermal demand.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution precipitation in anhydrous HF should provide fast mixing and high precursor activity, as shown by the NiF3 precipitation analogue (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The target and NiF3 share corner-sharing MF6 octahedral networks and bent 150° fluoride bridges, which supports structural transfer (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Remaining uncertainty is that CuF3 has R-3c symmetry while the NiF3 analogue is R-3 with two Ni sites, so subtle ordering/tilting differences may affect nucleation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct fluorination is not extremely hot relative to oxide ceramics, but it requires F2 gas, dry Ar transfer, sealed Au tubes or autoclaves, and long annealing times, as shown in CuAuF5 and Ag/Au fluoride syntheses (SS-3, TS-3, SS-5, TS-5). No provided evidence indicates multi-GPa pressure, so no GPa penalty is applied. The major hazards are reactive fluorine gas, sealed systems, and possible pressure buildup [Reasonable Inference from SS-3/TS-3 and SS-5/TS-5].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Gas–solid fluorination of CuO or CuF2 is kinetically slower than solution precipitation because it requires solid conversion and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from SS-3/TS-3]. The CuAuF5 analogue required a multiday fluorination and a 4–5 week anneal, indicating slow equilibration and ordering (SS-3, TS-3). The route is viable but less kinetically attractive than HF solution routes.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

HF/F2 solvothermal chemistry is severe because it combines high-temperature anhydrous HF, fluorine, and Monel autoclaves (SS-4, TS-4). The Ag2F5 analogue used 450 °C for 5 days with slow cooling, so the thermal load is moderate but chemical severity is high (SS-4, TS-4). No evidence supports >5 GPa pressure, so the high-pressure penalty is not applied.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solvothermal HF/F2 should improve transport and crystallization relative to dry solid-state fluorination, because the medium can mineralize fluorides [Reasonable Inference from SS-4/TS-4]. However, the Ag2F5 analogue formed as a byproduct and pure samples/crystals were difficult, indicating phase-control and nucleation uncertainty (SS-4, TS-4). Thus kinetics are better than Route 2 but less controlled than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

The FeF3 soft-chemistry route operates at only 81 °C, which is mild thermally (CS-3). However, it uses Br2/acetonitrile, washing, drying, and vacuum treatment, and a CuF3 adaptation would likely require stronger fluorinating oxidants [Reasonable Inference from CS-3]. This route receives a better F2 score than HF/F2 routes but is less certain chemically.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Soft chemistry can be kinetically favorable if an appropriate Cu–F precursor already resembles the target framework [Reasonable Inference from CS-3]. The FeF3 analogue demonstrates that metastable MF3 frameworks can be accessed by low-temperature oxidation (CS-3). The uncertainty is large because the provided soft-chemistry evidence concerns FeF3, not CuF3, and Cu(III) stabilization likely requires stronger fluorinating conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, so cation-size disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The structure is described as only corner-sharing CuF6 octahedra, so mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity is not present in the target description. The low amorphization risk is supported structurally by the simple MF3 octahedral network, although actual crystallinity will still depend on controlling high-valent fluoride decomposition [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CuF3 is predicted as a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from NiF3, which is chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close and is synthesized in anhydrous HF by low-temperature precipitation (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a very high fluoride chemical potential while suppressing thermal decomposition, which is consistent with NiF3 and high-valent Ag/Au/Cu fluoride analogues (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1, SS-3, TS-3, SS-4, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is the stabilization and isolation of binary Cu(III) fluoride without reduction, fluorine loss, or competing CuF2-based phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but specialized, with low-temperature anhydrous HF precipitation being the best-supported route.
