### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable edge of the requested ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin, so a high F1 score is justified. The closest Ti–Rh–light-element analogue Ti₂Rh₆B is on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS, SS), while several thermodynamic analogues are synthesized despite E_hull values of ~0.066–0.092 eV/atom (TS). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is likely not a deep ground state but a near-hull phase, so competing Ti–Rh, TiN, or Rh-rich phases may still form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has severe conditions because it combines arc melting, controlled Ar atmosphere, crushing, and flowing-N₂ nitrogenation, all of which are supported by Ti₂Rh₆B and Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ processing descriptions (CS, SS). Kinetics are penalized because Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ nitrogenation reports slow diffusion, incomplete nitrogenation, and possible secondary phase precipitation (CS). The strong structural similarity to Ti₂Rh₆B partially offsets this, because the target TiRh₁₂ and NRh₆ motifs closely match the TiRh₁₂ and BRh₆ motifs in the analogue (CS, SS). The main uncertainty is whether a Ti–Rh parent phase can absorb enough nitrogen without decomposing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is less procedurally severe because direct arc melting of Ti–Rh–B is explicitly successful for Ti₂Rh₆B, using simple elemental precursors under Ar and repeated remelting (CS, SS). Kinetics are moderate rather than high because TiN is refractory and may not readily redistribute N through a Rh-rich melt [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is high because Ti₂Rh₆B is the closest known analogue, with nearly identical Ti–Rh and Rh–light-element bond distances to the target (CS, SS). The main uncertainty is that CS/SS prove B incorporation, not N incorporation, in this Ti–Rh framework.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by TS examples of sealed-tube solid-state Rh compounds, including Rh₂B at 1200 °C and Rh₂CoSn requiring long vacuum annealing and reannealing (TS). It is kinetically weaker than arc melting because diffusion through solid Rh/TiN/Ti mixtures is likely slow [Reasonable Inference], and TS analogues confirm that long anneals and pulverization can be necessary (TS). The route may be gentler for N retention than arc melting [Reasonable Inference], but the lack of a directly matching Ti–Rh–N sealed-tube analogue makes transferability uncertain. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS directly supports sealed-tube Ti–Rh–N synthesis, so TS is the main basis for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has mild thermal severity but weak evidence because no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports thin-film Ti–Rh–N synthesis. It is proposed as [Reasonable Inference] because reactive sputtering can kinetically trap near-hull nitride phases and allows independent control of Ti, Rh, and N fluxes. Kinetics are scored moderately because N incorporation is plasma-assisted, but crystallization and long-range Ti/Rh/N ordering are uncertain without a direct analogue. Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS supports this exact deposition pathway, so this route should be considered exploratory rather than primary.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple high-symmetry cubic description with one Ti, one Rh, and one N site, and the reported connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing. The closest structural analogue Ti₂Rh₆B is crystalline and has essentially the same TiRh₁₂/BRh₆ motif (CS, SS), suggesting low amorphization tendency for the ordered framework. Radius mismatch is not expected to be severe for Ti/Rh cations [Reasonable Inference], and the small formula unit further supports crystallization. Remaining uncertainty is that thin-film deposition could still produce amorphous material if substrate temperature is too low [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiRh₆N is best interpreted as a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which is favorable but not deeply stable. The strongest support comes from Ti₂Rh₆B, which is both chemically and structurally close and was synthesized by straightforward arc melting under Ar (CS, SS). The most important enabling factor is the nearly identical Ti–Rh framework and Rh–light-element octahedral environment between Ti₂Rh₆B and TiRh₆N (CS, SS). The most important limiting factor is nitrogen incorporation: the nitride analogue Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ shows that gas-phase nitrogenation can be diffusion-limited and can lead to incomplete nitrogenation or secondary phases (CS). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with direct reactive arc melting from Ti/Rh/TiN as the recommended first attempt, followed by annealing/nitrogenation optimization.
