### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues NbPt₂, Pt₃Zr, HfPt₃, and Ni₃Nb are also on the hull, which strongly supports intrinsic thermodynamic accessibility (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). Chemical similarity gives mixed support because some CS entries are stable or near-stable while HfNiSn is listed as high above hull in the supplied data (CS3, CS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the DFT ground state corresponds to an experimentally accessible ordered phase at finite temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Arc melting plus annealing is well supported, but related systems use controlled Ar/vacuum atmospheres and high temperatures, including 1100–1500 °C treatments for NbPt₂-related alloys (SS1/TS1) and evacuated anneals in CS1 and SS2/TS4. The route is penalized for high temperature, vacuum/sealed anneal, and multi-step remelting/annealing. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for any volatile precursor requirement in Nb–Ni–Pt, so no volatility deduction is applied. The main uncertainty is the exact annealing window needed for ternary ordering [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Elemental-metal precursors are robust and standard but kinetically sluggish, especially for refractory Nb/Pt systems [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing or homogenization is common in analogues, such as 1200 h for Hf₂Ni₂Sn (CS1) and heat treatments for NbPt₂-related alloys (SS1/TS1). The structural match is favorable because the target and analogues share cuboctahedral metallic environments (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS5). Uncertainty remains about whether the monoclinic beta-derived ternary ordering forms rapidly or requires extended low-temperature annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Powder sintering is supported most directly by HfPt₃ reaction-sintering at 1600 °C and 1 × 10⁻⁵ Torr (SS4/TS3), so deductions are taken for >1000 °C, >1400 °C, vacuum, and multi-step processing. No high-pressure deduction is applied because pressing in SS4/TS3 is not >5 GPa. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for volatile precursor loss in elemental Nb/Ni/Pt powders. The route is conditionally feasible but more severe than simple arc melting because it depends on solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
Fine powders improve contact area, but the rubric still treats elemental metal powders as much less reactive than molecular or salt precursors [Reasonable Inference]. HfPt₃ analogues required 1600 °C and still showed multiphase issues requiring further annealing, signaling diffusion and phase-purity challenges (SS4/TS3). Structural similarity helps, because Pt-rich cuboctahedral analogues are known (SS1–SS4/TS1–TS4). The largest uncertainty is whether Nb₂Ni₃Pt₃ nucleates before competing Nb–Pt or Nb–Ni binaries consume the reactants [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Bridgman growth is supported for Ni₃Nb (SS5/TS5), but the target’s Nb/Pt content implies high melt temperatures [Reasonable Inference], and related systems use Ar/vacuum-controlled environments (SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2). The route is penalized for high temperature, controlled atmosphere, and likely pre-alloying/post-anneal steps. SS5/TS5 gives insufficient evidence for the exact Bridgman atmosphere, so the atmosphere deduction is based mainly on the broader SS/TS/CS alloy-processing set. The key uncertainty is whether the target melts congruently [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
Melt growth bypasses much of the solid-state diffusion limitation, and Ni₃Nb was successfully grown by Bridgman (SS5/TS5). Pt₃V, Pt₃Zr, and NbPt₂ analogues are also melt-derived or homogenized from metallic alloys, supporting crystallization of related intermetallic frameworks (SS1–SS3/TS1–TS2/TS4). Composition control is the main kinetic/process concern because a ternary ordered phase is more sensitive than binary analogues [Reasonable Inference]. The route receives the highest F3 because liquid-phase homogenization is more favorable than powder diffusion for refractory elements [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, which triggers a structural-complexity risk; similar cuboctahedral motifs are seen in SS/TS analogues (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS5). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb₂Ni₃Pt₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state intermetallic because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and several thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS5). The CS/SS/TS evidence supports synthesis mainly through conventional metallurgy: arc melting, vacuum or Ar annealing, high-temperature sintering, and melt growth are all represented among related intermetallics (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5/TS1–TS5). The strongest enabling factor is the close structural and thermodynamic analogy to known Nb–Pt, Ni–Nb, and Pt-rich cuboctahedral intermetallics (SS1–SS5/TS1–TS5). The main limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: refractory Nb/Pt diffusion, ternary ordering, and competition with stable binaries such as NbPt₂ and Ni₃Nb may require prolonged annealing or directional solidification [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not trivial, with the best chance coming from melt homogenization followed by controlled annealing or Bridgman-type growth.
