### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score from the required rubric. TS analogues include several E_hull = 0 transition-metal chalcogenides, including IrS₂, RuS₂, Ir₂S₃, Rh₃S₄, and OsSe₂ (TS-1–TS-5), which supports the general stability of noble-metal chalcogenide frameworks. The main uncertainty is that TS does not include an exact As–Ir–S analogue, so phase competition against binary Ir sulfides and arsenic sulfides remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, direct solid-state.**  
Condition severity is moderate-to-high because analogues require evacuated sealed tubes and high temperatures near 900–960 °C (SS-1/TS-1; SS-4/TS-3), and the target requires volatile/toxic As and volatile S [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main weakness: IrS₂ required 2 weeks and grinding/refiring for homogeneity (SS-1/TS-1), consistent with slow diffusion from refractory Ir [Reasonable Inference]. Structural similarity to IrS₂/Ir₂S₃ partially offsets this because target Ir–S distances and octahedral connectivity are close to those analogues (SS-1, SS-4/TS-3).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, flux/self-flux.**  
Flux synthesis has similar sealed-tube and volatile-component penalties, but it can operate in the 850–950 °C range supported by RuS₂ flux growth at 800–1000 °C (SS-3/TS-2). Kinetics improve because a flux or chalcogenide melt can dissolve/react refractory Ir and aid crystal ordering [Reasonable Inference], consistent with successful RuS₂ single-crystal growth in Te flux (SS-3/TS-2). The uncertainty is phase purity: RuS₂ flux growth had a narrow sulfur window and possible Te/RuTe₂ issues (SS-3/TS-2), and AsIr₃S₇ may similarly require careful As/S chemical potential control [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, CVT/halide transport.**  
CVT is more severe because the closest evidence uses a high-temperature gradient at 1320→1290 K and halide chemistry (TS-4). Kinetically, vapor transport can mobilize refractory metals and grow crystals [Reasonable Inference], and TS-4 demonstrates formation of a complex Rh sulfide phase by gradient reaction. The limiting uncertainty is phase selectivity: TS-4 explicitly reports difficulty obtaining pure Rh₃S₄ without neighboring Rh₁₇S₁₅, suggesting that transport growth can amplify narrow stability-window problems.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, high-pressure.**  
High-pressure synthesis is severely penalized because the IrS₂ high-pressure analogue used 60 kbar and 1400–1500 °C in specialized belt apparatus (SS-2). Kinetically, high T/P may accelerate reaction and stabilize dense pyrite-like Ir–S networks (SS-2), but it is experimentally demanding and less justified because the target is already predicted on the hull at ambient-pressure thermodynamic conditions [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best considered a fallback if ambient-pressure routes produce only binaries.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one clear amorphization risk: dissimilar Ir octahedral and As tetrahedral/covalent local environments, implying a significant radius/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, the target structure description indicates ordered corner-sharing octahedral/tetrahedral connectivity rather than mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, which lowers amorphization risk. Unit-cell atom count is not provided; using formula size and likely R3 multiplicity suggests it may not exceed 40 atoms, but this remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].
