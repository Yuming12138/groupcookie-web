### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is scored at 98/100 under the provided rubric. This is reinforced by multiple stable analogous sodium oxides with E_hull = 0.0, including Na2IrO3 (CS-1), Na4NpO5/Na5NpO6 (CS-2, CS-3), Na3Ca2TaO6 (SS-1, TS-1), and K2ZrO3 (TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic, because no direct experimental Na2HfO3 synthesis entry is provided by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard solid-state processing, supported by Na–Np, Na–Ta/Ca, and Na–Ln oxide syntheses in air or O2 flow (CS-2, CS-3, SS-1, SS-5). The penalty comes from possible >1000 °C firing, sodium loss control, and repeated grind/fire steps, which are all consistent with the cited solid-state analogues (CS-2, CS-3, SS-5). CS provides chemically similar Na2MO3-type evidence but not Hf; SS/TS provide structural/thermodynamic analogues but not direct Na2HfO3, so Hf-specific condition severity remains partly inferred.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The main kinetic limitation is the likely low reactivity of HfO2 [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the need for high-temperature treatment of refractory Ta2O5-containing Na3Ca2TaO6 at 1273 K (SS-1, TS-1). Regrinding is explicitly needed in Na–Np and NaLnO2 syntheses (CS-2, CS-3, SS-5), supporting a moderate-to-slow diffusion score. Structural reconstruction is helped by the fact that the target matches Caswellsilverite-like octahedral connectivity seen in Na2IrO3/Na2PtO3 (CS-1, CS-5), but starting from carbonate plus HfO2 requires substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids very high temperature because Na2PtO3 forms at 500 °C under O2-free Ar (CS-5) and NaScO2 forms around 600 °C under dried N2 (SS-2, TS-2). Its penalties are dry/inert handling, possible sealed containment for sodium retention, and the air/moisture sensitivity of Na2O2 [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct Hf peroxide synthesis, so condition transferability is indirect.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Na2O2 should increase precursor activity relative to Na2CO3 [Reasonable Inference], consistent with low-temperature formation of Na2PtO3 and NaScO2 (CS-5, SS-2). The HfO2 component still likely limits kinetics [Reasonable Inference], so the ion diffusion score is not high. Structural compatibility is good because Na2PtO3 and Na2IrO3 are Caswellsilverite-like Na2MO3 materials (CS-5, CS-1), but Hf-specific evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route has moderate severity because analogous hydroxide flux growth occurs at 700 °C with controlled cooling, not extreme temperature or pressure (SS-4, TS-4). The method requires corrosive/hygroscopic flux and multiple operations, including vessel loading, heating, slow cooling, and flux removal (SS-4, TS-4). Direct CS support for flux growth exists through self-flux-grown Na2IrO3 (CS-1), but direct Na2HfO3 flux evidence is absent.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten hydroxide/alkali flux can improve mass transport and dissolve refractory oxides, as shown for Sr3NaTaO6/Sr3NaNbO6 and K2ZrO3 (SS-4, TS-4, TS-5). The ZrO2 → K2ZrO3 flux route is especially relevant by Zr/Hf analogy [Reasonable Inference], though it is not the same sodium hafnate. The remaining uncertainty is whether Na2HfO3 rather than competing sodium hafnates crystallizes from a sodium-rich melt.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Metathesis is thermally mild at about 700 °C but operationally more demanding because CS-4 uses sealed Au tubes and 23-day anneals. Air-sensitive Na2O/Na2O2 reagents add severity [Reasonable Inference], and the lithium hafnate precursor would need prior synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports metathesis for Na2PrO3/Na2TbO3-type materials (CS-4), but SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for topochemical Na2HfO3 formation.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

This route could benefit from a preorganized Hf–O framework if Li2HfO3 or a related precursor is used [Reasonable Inference]. However, the long 23-day metathesis time in CS-4 indicates slow cation exchange or ordering kinetics. The score is therefore moderate despite potentially favorable structural templating.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains octahedral NaO6 and HfO6 units with a substantial Na+/Hf4+ size/charge mismatch [Reasonable Inference], so one amorphization trigger is counted. The target and analogues show corner and edge sharing, but no face sharing is reported, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered; this is supported by the target description and analogous CS/SS descriptions dominated by edge/corner sharing (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1). Unit-cell atom count >40 is not established by CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2HfO3 is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase**, because the provided target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong structurally and chemically: Na2IrO3 is the same Caswellsilverite-like C2/m family (CS-1), Na2PtO3 and Na2TbO3 show related Na2MO3 Caswellsilverite-like chemistry (CS-5, CS-4), and stable sodium octahedral oxides are common in SS/TS (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most enabling factor is the robust octahedral alkali-metal oxide framework, which appears across multiple Na–M–O analogues (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1). The most limiting factor is likely kinetic: HfO2 is refractory and may react sluggishly [Reasonable Inference], making flux or peroxide-assisted routes preferable. Phase competition among sodium hafnates and sodium loss at high temperature are the main uncertainties [Reasonable Inference], since no direct Na2HfO3 synthesis precedent is included in CS/SS/TS.

|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sodium loss / sodium activity control | High | Use Na excess, as Na/Ln = 1.2 is used to prevent Na evaporation in NaLnO2 (SS-5), and Na:Pt = 2.2:1 is used for Na2PtO3 (CS-5). |
| 2 | Routes 1–2 | Slow reaction of HfO2 | High | Use fine HfO2, repeated regrinding, and longer anneals; repeated regrinding is used for Na–Np oxides and NaLnO2 (CS-2, CS-3, SS-5). Flux route can improve transport, as oxide dissolution in alkali flux is shown for K2ZrO3 and Sr3NaTaO6/Sr3NaNbO6 (TS-5, SS-4, TS-4). |
| 3 | Route 1 | Competing sodium hafnate phases from over/under-sodiation | Medium–High | Screen Na excess and temperature; phase sensitivity and impurity formation are noted for Na–Np and Na–Pt analogues (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). |
| 4 | Route 2 | Air/moisture sensitivity of Na2O2 | Medium | Handle under dry Ar/N2; dry N2 and O2-free Ar are used in NaScO2 and Na2PtO3 syntheses (SS-2, CS-5). |
| 5 | Route 3 | Flux corrosion and product separation | Medium | Use Ag/Ni/Au-compatible vessels; silver tubes/crucibles and flux dissolution are used for hydroxide flux growth (SS-4, TS-4). |

| 8 | Route 4 | Long cation-exchange / ordering time | Medium | Use long sealed-tube annealing; CS-4 reports 700 °C for 23 days in welded Au tubes. |
| 9 | All | Structural ordering of Na/Hf octahedral network | Medium | Slow cooling and annealing can help ordering; controlled cooling is used for Na3Ca2TaO6 and hydroxide-flux-grown Sr3NaTaO6 (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). |
