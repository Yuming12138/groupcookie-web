### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a top thermodynamic score. Similar selenate phases also show strong thermodynamic stability: NaPr(SeO₄)₂, MgH₂(SeO₄)₂, NaFe(SeO₄)₂, and tetragonal CaSeO₄ have E_hull = 0.0 in the provided SS/TS data (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that zero computed hull energy does not guarantee easy nucleation or hydrate-free crystallization, especially for Be-containing aqueous systems [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal severity

Hydrothermal selenate analogues are made at relatively mild temperatures: CaSeO₄ at 70–220 °C and NaFe(SeO₄)₂ at 500 K for about one week (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). These conditions do not trigger high-temperature, high-pressure >5 GPa, vacuum, or reactive-atmosphere penalties. The main severity penalty is toxic/corrosive precursor handling because Be compounds and H₂SeO₄ are hazardous [Reasonable Inference], while H₂SeO₄ use itself is directly evidenced in SS/TS selenate syntheses (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4).

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis provides high precursor mobility and avoids solid-state diffusion, which is supported by successful low-temperature crystallization of CaSeO₄ and NaFe(SeO₄)₂ in aqueous acidic media (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). The SeO₄ tetrahedral unit is strongly transferable because analogue Se–O bond lengths around 1.63–1.70 Å match the target’s Se–O = 1.67 Å (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The kinetic uncertainty is Be coordination: most close hydrothermal analogues contain larger cations in 6–9 coordination rather than tetrahedral BeO₄ (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2 evaporation/concentration severity

Evaporation and acid-concentration analogues are mild: Mg oxyhalate crystals form by slow evaporation from aqueous solution (CS-2, CS-4), Mg hydrogen selenates form at 120–140 °C in 90% H₂SeO₄ (SS-3, TS-2), and CoSO₄ crystals form by evaporation in sulfuric acid at ~600 K (CS-5). There is no high-pressure or controlled-atmosphere requirement. Deductions arise from likely multi-step hydrate-to-anhydrous conversion [Reasonable Inference] and toxic/corrosive Be/H₂SeO₄ handling, with hygroscopicity concerns supported by Mg hydrogen selenate analogues (SS-3, TS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2 evaporation/concentration kinetics

Solution concentration offers excellent mixing and high ion mobility, consistent with successful direct crystallization of Mg oxyhalates and CoSO₄ from solution/acid media (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). Mg hydrogen selenate synthesis from metal selenates in concentrated H₂SeO₄ at 120–140 °C indicates that selenate frameworks can crystallize under mild solution conditions (SS-3, TS-2). The main uncertainty is whether neutral anhydrous BeSeO₄ crystallizes rather than hydrated or hydrogen selenate phases, because related hydrogen selenates require careful concentration and can be hygroscopic (SS-3, TS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3 sealed solid-state severity

The proposed solid-state route uses moderate temperatures based on 350–600 °C oxide/oxyanion solid-state synthesis in TS-5 and sealed high-temperature ampoule handling in CS-1. It avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa penalties, and high pressure is not preferred because the high-pressure evidence concerns MgSeO₃ selenite rather than BeSeO₄ selenate (CS-3). Penalties are applied for likely volatile selenium(VI) control in a sealed system [Reasonable Inference], multiple grind/anneal steps analogous to TS-5, and toxic Be/Se handling.

#### F3 — Route 3 sealed solid-state kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because BeO is expected to be refractory and less reactive [Reasonable Inference], while TS-5 requires stepwise annealing with intermediate cooling and regrinding. The reaction must construct a BeO₄–SeO₄ corner-sharing framework rather than simply crystallize from preorganized solution species, so structural reconstruction is moderate-to-difficult [Reasonable Inference]. The evidence for direct solid-state selenate synthesis is weaker than for hydrothermal routes; CS-3 even indicates that Mg–Se–O solid-state chemistry can require high pressure for selenite phases, highlighting possible competing phase issues.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure is simple: BeO₄ and SeO₄ tetrahedra share corners, with no reported edge or face sharing. The reported target description indicates one Be site, one Se site, and one O site, suggesting a compact ordered cell rather than a >40-atom complex framework. Thus none of the three amorphization triggers is clearly activated; the remaining uncertainty is that Be-containing oxide networks can form glassy or amorphous intermediates during dehydration [Reasonable Inference], but the provided structural data favors a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BeSeO₄ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close selenate analogues also lie on or very near the hull (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The CS/SS/TS evidence supports synthesis most strongly through aqueous/hydrothermal selenate chemistry, since CaSeO₄, NaFe(SeO₄)₂, and Mg hydrogen selenates crystallize from H₂SeO₄-containing solutions at mild-to-moderate temperatures (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust SeO₄ tetrahedral chemistry, with analogue Se–O bond lengths closely matching the target’s 1.67 Å Se–O distance (SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is not thermodynamics but phase selection: avoiding hydrated Be selenates, hydrogen selenates, or amorphous dehydration products will require tight acid/water-activity control [Reasonable Inference], consistent with hydration and polymorph sensitivity in related selenates (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4). Toxic Be and strong oxidizing H₂SeO₄ handling further reduce practical ease [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy from CS/SS/TS |
|:-:|:-----:|-----------|:--------:|-----------------------------------|
| 1 | All | Be precursor toxicity and dust/solution exposure risk [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Use closed wet-chemistry handling where possible; hydrothermal and solution routes minimize dry BeO grinding relative to solid-state processing [Reasonable Inference], and analogous hydrothermal routes use sealed Teflon-lined vessels (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). |
| 2 | All | Hydrate or hydrogen selenate competing phases rather than anhydrous BeSeO₄. | High | Control H₂SeO₄ concentration, water activity, and drying; Mg hydrogen selenates require defined 90% H₂SeO₄ concentration and temperature, and related phases can be hygroscopic/air-unstable (SS-3, TS-2). |
| 3 | All | Polymorph/phase competition in selenates. | Medium | Use hydrothermal temperature and acid concentration as phase-control knobs; CaSeO₄ analogues show multiple polymorphs under 70–220 °C conditions and require desiccator storage (SS-2, SS-4, TS-4). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Slow crystallization or small crystal size in hydrothermal synthesis. | Medium | Hold for one to two weeks and slow-cool, following CaSeO₄ and NaFe(SeO₄)₂ hydrothermal protocols (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Over-acidification may stabilize Be hydrogen selenate rather than neutral BeSeO₄ [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Vary H₂SeO₄ concentration and evaporative endpoint; Mg hydrogen selenates form specifically in 90% H₂SeO₄ at 120–140 °C, so reducing acidity/water activity should be screened [Reasonable Inference, SS-3, TS-2]. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Hygroscopicity and air/moisture sensitivity of crystallized product or intermediates. | Medium | Store and cool in a desiccator; hygroscopic/air-unstable behavior is reported for related hydrogen selenates (SS-3, TS-2). |
| 7 | Route 3 | Selenium(VI) loss or decomposition during heating. | High | Use sealed or covered vessels and moderate temperatures; sealed ampoules are used for anhydrous MgSO₄ CVT (CS-1), and moderate 350–600 °C stepwise solid-state synthesis is demonstrated for a thermodynamic oxide analogue (TS-5). |
| 8 | Route 3 | Slow ordering kinetics due to refractory BeO. | High | Use fine BeO, intimate grinding, intermediate regrinding, and possibly pre-form BeSeO₄ hydrate before dehydration [Reasonable Inference]; repeated grinding/stepwise annealing is supported by TS-5. |
| 9 | Route 3 | Competing selenite or lower-valent selenium oxide phases under insufficiently oxidizing conditions. | Medium | Maintain oxidizing dry air/O₂ conditions; TS-5 uses air for oxide/oxyanion solid-state synthesis, while MgSeO₃ evidence shows Se(IV) selenite phases can form under different conditions (CS-3, TS-5). |
