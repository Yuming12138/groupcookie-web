### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is explicitly 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored as a likely ground-state or near-ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. TS supports the plausibility of stable 1:1 sulfide-like phases because ScS, YS, TmS, and DyS analogues are all listed with E_hull = 0 (TS-1 to TS-4). CS is mixed: Zr₃S₄ is above hull by 0.2875 eV/atom, while ZrCl is on hull and TiS/NbS analogues are low-to-moderate above hull (CS-1 to CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that no provided source reports experimental WC-type ZrS directly; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the exact Zr–S phase boundary.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Direct sealed-tube solid-state synthesis

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because analogous TiS synthesis uses sealed vacuum ampoules, sulfur, 1000–1300 °C treatments, and quenching or long annealing (CS-4, CS-5, SS-5). The kinetic score is limited by refractory Zr and solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], but improved by the existence of WC-type or WC-like TiS/NbS analogues (CS-3, CS-4). Phase selection is uncertain because TiS analogues report multiple modifications, narrow homogeneity, and careful phase control (CS-4, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact sealed-tube ZrS route, so TS is treated as neutral for Route 1-specific processing.

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — CVT / transport equilibration

The condition severity is not extreme in temperature because Zr–S/Se CVT uses 870→770 °C and ScS CVT uses 950 °C (CS-1, SS-2, TS-1), but vacuum sealing, volatile halogens, and transport-agent toxicity impose penalties. The kinetic score is highest because CVT can transport material through vapor species, reducing the need for long-range diffusion through dense Zr metal [Reasonable Inference], and this is supported by successful Zr sulfide/selenide and Sc sulfide transport routes (CS-1, SS-2, TS-1). Structural support is strong from WC-type NbS (CS-3), and chemical support is strong from Zr–S bonding in Zr₃S₄ (CS-1, SS-1). The main uncertainty is whether the CVT equilibrium will select stoichiometric WC-type ZrS rather than other Zr sulfides; CS-1 notes composition/morphology sensitivity.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — High-temperature induction/arc-melt plus anneal

The condition severity is highest because high-temperature monosulfide analogues use ~1700 °C induction heating, refractory containers, and controlled atmospheres (SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). Kinetically, high temperature helps diffusion, but sulfur volatility and Zr refractory behavior remain limiting [Reasonable Inference]. Thermodynamic analogues support stable 1:1 sulfide compositions (TS-1 to TS-4), but most TS analogues are rocksalt rather than WC-type, reducing structural confidence. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high-temperature melting specifically stabilizes WC-type ZrS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple binary composition and small WC-type cell, so two amorphization checks are not triggered [target structure description; Reasonable Inference]. The mixed face/corner/edge-sharing polyhedral network triggers one risk factor because the target description explicitly states coexistence of face-, corner-, and edge-sharing ZrS₆/SZr₆ polyhedra. Similar mixed-sharing motifs are present in TiS/NbS-like analogues, so this risk is not severe (CS-3, CS-4, SS-5). The remaining uncertainty is whether local disorder or nonstoichiometry in ZrS would disrupt long-range WC-type ordering [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also listed at E_hull = 0 (TS-1 to TS-4). The strongest synthesis support comes from the combination of chemical similarity to Zr–S CVT chemistry (CS-1, SS-1) and structural similarity to WC-type NbS/TiS analogues (CS-3, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is halogen-assisted transport, which should reduce refractory Zr diffusion limitations and allow equilibration near 800–1000 °C [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1 and SS-2/TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control among possible Zr sulfides, since Zr₃S₄-like chemistry is documented and TiS analogues show multiple modifications or narrow homogeneity ranges (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). Overall, ZrS is predicted to be reasonably synthesizable, with CVT being the most defensible first attempt.
