### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic stability. Thermodynamically similar Pt-rich analogues Pt8In2As, Pt5InAs, CdPt3, and GdPt4In also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and Pt3Al is only 0.0024 eV/atom above hull, supporting stability of related Pt-rich frameworks (TS1–TS5). CS gives mixed support because Zr2Ir6B, CeAu4Zn2, and MgIr2 are on the hull, but PuPd5Al2 is reported at 0.232 eV/atom; therefore CS is not uniformly predictive for thermodynamic stability (CS1, CS3–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether finite-temperature entropy stabilizes competing Mg–Pt or Ga–Pt phases instead of the ordered Mg2GaPt5 phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc melting

Arc melting is well supported for Pt-group intermetallic analogues and requires only Ar atmosphere, elemental precursors, and repeated remelting for homogeneity (CS1, CS3, SS5, TS4). The severity score is reduced by very high local temperature, repeated melting, and Mg/Ga volatility, which is inferred from the need for sealed Ta processing in MgIr2 (CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics are moderate because melting improves mixing, but Pt remains refractory and ternary ordering may require annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically demonstrates open-arc synthesis of Mg–Ga–Pt, so SS/TS are treated as structurally supportive but not directly route-confirming for Mg volatility control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed Ta-tube induction melting

This route is strongly motivated by MgIr2, where Mg + noble metal synthesis used sealed Ta tubes, induction heating at 1300–1400 K, and additional annealing to remove MgIr/Ir impurities (CS5). Ce/Au/Zn intermetallics also used sealed Ta induction melting with staged annealing to achieve phase purity (CS4). The route has moderate severity because it needs sealed metal tubes, high temperature, and multi-step annealing, but no external high pressure. Kinetically it is reasonable because liquid Mg/Ga can wet/react with Pt, but CS5 shows impurity phases and slow equilibration can persist.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed ampoule / CVT-assisted annealing

Ampoule annealing is supported by Pt8In2As/Pt5InAs, but those analogues needed very long Pt-containing anneals of up to 90–120 d and intermediate grinding, indicating slow kinetics (SS2, TS1, TS3). Iodine-assisted Pt–Al CVT works at 400–600 °C, but phase purity depends sensitively on iodine/aluminum halide ratio and temperature, and iodine-containing residues were noted (SS4, TS5). The route has lower thermal severity but higher chemical/operational severity due to vacuum, volatile iodine, sealed systems, and possible Mg/silica incompatibility [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS directly supports Mg–Ga–Pt CVT, so CS contributes neutral route evidence here.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Melt spinning + annealing

This is the best-scoring route because the closest structural analogue Pt3Ga was synthesized by melt-spinning followed by Ar annealing, and its Pt/Ga cuboctahedral motifs are closely related to the target’s Pt/Ga/Mg cuboctahedral network (SS1). Rapid solidification improves kinetic mixing and short diffusion lengths, which can help Pt-rich intermetallic formation [Reasonable Inference]. The severity remains moderate because a master alloy is needed and Mg/Ga loss must be controlled during melting and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that SS1 also reports Ga-composition sensitivity, and no SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates Mg incorporation into the same Pt3Ga-like framework.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically and size-distinct Mg, Ga, and Pt sites, and Mg is appreciably larger than Ga/Pt by metallic-radius reasoning, triggering one amorphization/complexity check [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly contains mixed face- and corner-sharing GaMg4Pt8 cuboctahedra, triggering the mixed-polyhedral-connectivity check. The exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS for the >40 atom criterion and it is not treated as a hard trigger. Overall amorphization risk is medium rather than high because many analogous Pt-rich cuboctahedral frameworks crystallize successfully (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg2GaPt5 is likely a true ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also at or near the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthetic support comes from structurally similar Pt3Ga, which was made by melt-spinning and annealing, and from MgIr2, which shows that Mg–platinum-group intermetallics can be made using sealed Ta high-temperature methods (SS1, CS5). The most important enabling factor is the existence of stable Pt-rich cuboctahedral intermetallic frameworks with Pt–p-block bonding motifs closely related to the target (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS5). The main limiting factor is composition control involving volatile Mg and low-melting Ga in the presence of refractory Pt [Reasonable Inference], a concern reinforced by MgIr2 impurity/annealing requirements and Pt3Ga composition sensitivity (CS5, SS1). Overall, the target should be synthesizable in a well-equipped intermetallic synthesis laboratory, with the best chance via melt-spun or sealed-tube melt-derived samples followed by annealing.
