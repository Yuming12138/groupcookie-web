### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls at the top of the required thermodynamic scoring range. Multiple SS/TS analogues are also on the hull, including Nb₃TeCl₇, Cs₂Nb₆PbCl₁₈, Cs₂Ta₆PbCl₁₈, CsVCl₃, and Rb₄Nb₆Cl₁₈ (SS1, SS3–SS4; TS1–TS5). CS gives weaker direct evidence because the closest V–O–Cl entry is molecular VOCl₃ at E_hull ≈0.081 eV/atom, while the other CS entries are sulfides rather than oxychlorides (CS1–CS5); thus **insufficient direct CS evidence for an extended V₄OCl₇ phase, CS neutral-to-moderate**.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to sealed chloride analogues at 600–750 °C and 973–1090 K, so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty in the proposed program (SS1, SS4–SS5, TS1–TS3). It requires dry Ar/vacuum sealed-tube conditions and air-sensitive metal chlorides, which are common in the provided chloride syntheses (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether V₂O₃ reacts completely without producing residual oxide or simpler V chlorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

VCl₂/VCl₃ should be chemically active, but V₂O₃ is a less reactive oxygen source, and chemically similar oxide-derived V sulfides required prolonged high-temperature treatments and grinding (CS1, CS3, CS5). The closest structural analogue Nb₃TeCl₇ forms quantitatively in a sealed ampoule, supporting eventual crystallization of a related framework (SS1, TS1). Uncertainty remains high because O²⁻ is much smaller and harder than Te, so structural transfer from Nb₃TeCl₇ is topological rather than chemical [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids very high temperatures by using VOCl₃ as a reactive V–O–Cl precursor, supported chemically by CS2. It still requires sealed ampoules, dry handling, and management of volatile/toxic oxychloride/chloride reagents, consistent with the sealed chloride chemistry in SS/TS analogues (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). The largest safety and severity penalty is VOCl₃ volatility and pressure buildup [Reasonable Inference from CS2].

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic advantage is that VOCl₃ already contains V–O–Cl bonding, while VCl₂ provides reduced chloride-rich V, making the precursor set more chemically matched to the target than oxide-only synthesis (CS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Framework formation from metal/metal-chloride mixtures is well supported for Nb/Ta/V chloride analogues (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS5). The remaining kinetic uncertainty is redox control: VOCl₃ contains high-valent V, while the target average V oxidation state is much lower [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits greater severity from Bridgman or vapor-transport requirements, including high temperature, sealed ampoules, gradients, and multi-stage processing (SS2, TS4). CsVCl₃ Bridgman growth uses 1100 °C, triggering the >1000 °C penalty (TS4). This route is therefore less attractive for first synthesis but plausible for single-crystal growth after powder synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Chloride vapor or melt transport can improve kinetics, as shown by Mo chloride CVT and CsVCl₃ Bridgman crystal growth (SS2, TS4). If pre-reacted V₄OCl₇ is used as feed, structural reconstruction may be modest [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the oxychloride remains stable at the elevated temperatures needed for transport/growth, since no CS/SS/TS analogue demonstrates V–O–Cl framework growth by Bridgman.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The described target connectivity is predominantly edge-sharing VCl₅O/VCl₆ octahedra rather than mixed edge/vertex/face sharing, so C3 is not triggered by the target description. CS/SS/TS analogues include some complex mixed-connectivity frameworks, but those are not direct evidence of the target’s amorphization tendency, so **insufficient CS/SS/TS evidence for additional amorphization risk; neutral for unobserved triggers**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₄OCl₇ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many structurally/thermodynamically similar chloride frameworks are also reported on the hull (SS1, SS3–SS4; TS1–TS5). The best support comes from the structural/thermodynamic analogue Nb₃TeCl₇, which has a very similar M₃QCl₇-type topology and is synthesized quantitatively in a sealed ampoule (SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube halide chemistry using metal chlorides/metals, repeatedly demonstrated across SS/TS analogues (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most critical limiting factor is chemical, not thermodynamic: maintaining the unusual V–O–Cl stoichiometry and reduced average V oxidation state while avoiding volatile VOCl₃/chloride loss [Reasonable Inference from CS2 and SS/TS sealed chloride evidence]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with Route 2 slightly preferred because it directly introduces V–O–Cl bonding via VOCl₃ (CS2).
