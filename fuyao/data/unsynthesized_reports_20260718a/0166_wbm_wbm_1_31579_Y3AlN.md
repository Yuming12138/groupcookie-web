### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is listed with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues Sm3AlN, Sm3InN, Tb3InN, Nd3InN, and Pr3AlN are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5), and several chemically/structurally similar entries are also on the hull (CS-1, CS-3–CS-5; SS-1–SS-5). The only notable conflict is Ca3SnN with high E_hull despite being synthetically reported (CS-2), so it is weighted less because it is chemically less close to Y3AlN than the rare-earth AlN analogues.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures of 600–800 °C but requires evacuated sealed tubes, BN crucibles, Mo foil, pellet pressing, and long annealing, all reported for R3AlN analogues (CS-3; SS-1; TS-1). The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed handling, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive rare-earth/Y metal handling [Reasonable Inference from CS-3/SS-1/TS-1]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful evidence for this route because Pr3AlN and Sm3AlN are close analogues.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate because AlN is refractory, but the rare-earth metal + AlN route has already produced Pm-3m R3AlN phases (CS-3; SS-1; SS-5; TS-1; TS-5). Long annealing up to 800 h indicates slow diffusion and ordering (CS-3; SS-1; TS-1). The structural match to Y3AlN is excellent, but Y is not explicitly included in the reported R list, so the Y transfer is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C in the final anneal because the R3InN analogue route uses 1220 K / 947 °C (CS-5; SS-2; TS-2). It is penalized for sealed evacuated ampoules, multiple precursor-preparation steps, and air-sensitive nitride/intermetallic handling (CS-5; SS-2; TS-2). SS and TS support the structural/thermodynamic family well, but the Al-specific precursor route is not directly demonstrated, so some evidence is extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 2

YN + Y2Al is stoichiometrically ideal for Y3AlN [Reasonable Inference], and RN + R2In analogues show that binary nitride/intermetallic precursors can form R3MN phases (CS-5; SS-2; TS-2). However, the need to synthesize RN and R2In separately, arc melt, seal, anneal for a week, and sometimes regrind/reanneal indicates moderate kinetic difficulty (CS-5; SS-3; TS-3). The route is less directly supported than Route 1 because the demonstrated binary precursor chemistry uses In rather than Al.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because CS nitridation analogues require 1000–1300 °C or 1420 K under N2 (CS-1; CS-2). It also requires controlled nitrogen atmosphere, pellet processing, and air-sensitive metal handling, as supported by glovebox and N2 handling in the Ca3SnN analogue route (CS-2). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for elemental Y–Al nitridation, so their signal for this specific route is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Direct nitridation has the weakest kinetic basis because ternary ordering must occur from elemental Y, Al, and N2 [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 explicitly notes difficulty achieving phase-pure Ca/Sr–In nitrides despite broad condition variation, which suggests phase-control risk for direct nitridation. The main uncertainty is whether YN formation blocks formation of the ternary antiperovskite [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

The melt route is penalized for temperatures above 1000 °C and likely above 1400 °C, as melting/induction processing is required [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. CS-4 demonstrates pure-Ar high-frequency furnace processing and slow cooling for Zr3AlN, but SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for melt synthesis of cubic R3AlN, so their melt-route signal is neutral. Specialized furnace/hearth equipment adds practical complexity, though not high pressure.

#### F3 — Route 4

Melt processing should improve cation mixing and diffusion, and CS-4 reports phase-pure Zr3AlN after melting and slow cooling. However, Zr3AlN is orthorhombic Cmcm rather than cubic Pm-3m, so the structural crystallization pathway is uncertain for Y3AlN (CS-4; target data). Nitrogen retention and avoidance of competing Y–Al intermetallics are major uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple Pm-3m five-atom formula cell, so the unit-cell atom-count amorphization check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The structure contains corner- and face-sharing polyhedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered (target data; SS-1; TS-1). The cation size mismatch between Y and Al triggers one risk point [Reasonable Inference], yielding a low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y3AlN is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS-1–TS-5). The synthesis case is strongly supported by exact rare-earth aluminum nitride analogues such as Pr3AlN and Sm3AlN, which share the same cubic Pm-3m R3AlN framework (CS-3; SS-1; SS-5; TS-1; TS-5). The most important enabling factor is the direct precedent for forming R3AlN antiperovskites from rare-earth metal plus AlN under moderate-temperature sealed-ampoule annealing (CS-3; SS-1; TS-1). The most important limiting factor is slow solid-state diffusion/order formation, reflected by annealing times up to 800 h (CS-3; SS-1; TS-1). A secondary limitation is that Y itself is not explicitly listed among the reported R3AlN series, so the Y substitution remains a [Reasonable Inference].
