### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the rubric boundary for high thermodynamic plausibility. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values near or above this value have been synthesized, including Na₅WNO₄ at 0.048 eV/atom, Na₆PbO₅ at 0.034 eV/atom, KNa₂NiO₂-related oxide at 0.078 eV/atom, and Na₄Mo(NO)₂ at 0.086 eV/atom (TS). This supports the view that a 50 meV/atom Na-rich oxide can be experimentally accessible, though it may be metastable rather than a robust ground state (TS). Direct thermodynamic evidence for Na–Ru–O decomposition products is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so the Ru-specific stability field remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses conventional sealed solid-state conditions comparable to Na₅InO₄, Na₄TiO₄, Na₄CoO₃, and Na₄FeO₃ syntheses, which require 450–900 °C and sealed metal containers (CS, SS). The absence of >1000 °C or high pressure keeps severity moderate, but the need for sealed alkali oxide handling and air-sensitive Na₂O/Na₂O₂ lowers the score (CS, TS). CS/SS provide strong evidence for alkali-rich sealed synthesis, but insufficient direct evidence for Ru(VI) stabilization under these conditions.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are likely slow because multiple CS/SS analogues require very long anneals: 25 d for Na₅InO₄, 52 d for Na₄TiO₄, 71 d for Na₄IrO₄, and 75 d for Na₅NbO₅-type synthesis (CS, SS). Binary oxide starting materials require substantial reconstruction, and RuO₂ is expected to be less reactive than alkali flux components [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Na₂O₂ can oxidize and mobilize Ru sufficiently to reduce the anneal time [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 retains moderate temperatures but introduces corrosive/reactive flux chemistry. The method is supported by Na₄GeO₄ alkaline melt synthesis and Na-rich flux/exchange syntheses requiring dry handling, sealed vessels, and sometimes specialized crucibles (CS, SS). No high pressure or >1000 °C is required in the preferred implementation, although some flux analogues use 1000 °C for extended times (CS). Air-sensitive and oxidizing precursors remain the main severity penalty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux/reactive melt conditions should improve precursor activity and cation mobility compared with dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. This inference is consistent with CS evidence that Na₄GeO₄ can be accessed through molten/alkaline processing and that Na-rich phases crystallize under excess alkali media (CS). The remaining kinetic barrier is nucleating the correct cubic Na₆RuO₆ framework rather than lower-ruthenate or binary oxide impurities [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct Ru evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized because it requires synthesis of a Li–Ru–O precursor followed by sealed Na exchange, analogous to the multistep Na₄IrO₄ exchange route (SS). Na₄IrO₄ required Li₂IrO₃ precursor preparation, Na₂O preparation, Ag tube sealing, Ar atmosphere, and 71 d annealing (SS). The target-specific precursor is not provided by CS/SS/TS, so the method is more speculative and operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3

If a structurally related Li–Ru–O precursor exists, exchange could reduce structural reconstruction and improve selectivity [Reasonable Inference]. However, the best SS analogue, Na₄IrO₄, still required 71 d at 800 °C, showing that alkali exchange can be kinetically sluggish (SS). Because no direct Li₆RuO₆ analogue is supplied, precursor availability and structural compatibility remain major uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, Na⁺ and high-valent Ru⁶⁺, creating a large ionic-size/charge contrast that can promote local ordering frustration [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m conventional cell is likely to contain more than 40 atoms if Z ≈ 4 for Na₆RuO₆, adding configurational/ordering complexity [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target description reports only corner- and edge-sharing ONa₄Ru units, not simultaneous face sharing, so the highest amorphization-risk criterion is not fully met.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆RuO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than a clearly guaranteed ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support that Na-rich oxides with unusual coordination environments can be synthesized, but they also show that such phases often need sealed alkali-rich conditions, long anneals, and careful exclusion of air/moisture. The most important enabling factor is the target’s favorable computed energy above hull, reinforced by synthesized TS analogues with similar or higher metastability. The main limiting factor is Ru redox control: charge balance requires Ru(VI), but CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct Na–Ru–O synthesis analogue, so oxidizing basic flux conditions are recommended [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful optimization rather than a simple one-shot ceramic firing.
