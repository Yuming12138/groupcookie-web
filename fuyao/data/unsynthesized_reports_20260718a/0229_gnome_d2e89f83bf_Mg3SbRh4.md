### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic stability score (target data). Thermodynamic and structural analogues MgRh, LiRh₂Ge, Rh₂SnCu, Mg₂Rh, and Sr–Rh–Ga all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5), and chemically similar intermetallics are also mostly hull-stable or near-stable (CS1–CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that all hull values are calculated 0 K thermodynamic metrics and do not prove finite-temperature phase width or kinetic accessibility; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for phase diagrams of Mg–Sb–Rh, so that uncertainty is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C conditions because MgRh forms at 793–873 K and Rh₂SnCu forms after annealing at 700 °C (SS1, TS1, SS3, TS3). It still requires sealed ampoules, Ta foil/liners, possible Zr getter, regrinding, and long annealing, all supported by MgRh and Mg₈Rh₄B synthesis protocols (SS1, TS1, CS3). The main uncertainty is whether Sb incorporation into Mg₃SbRh₄ requires higher temperature than the MgRh binary; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on Mg–Sb–Rh ternary diffusion.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are moderate to slow because Rh is refractory and elemental powders are not highly reactive [Reasonable Inference]. MgRh formation from Mg/Rh powders over one to two weeks shows the Mg–Rh framework can form under manageable conditions (SS1, TS1), while Mg₈Rh₄B required 3–7 days and regrinding at 850–1000 °C (CS3). The target’s Heusler-like/bcc coordination helps structurally, but ordering of three elements may require repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Arc/induction melting raises condition severity because analogous intermetallics used high-temperature melting or annealing, including 1360–1440 K for Zr₅Ni₄Al and 1400 K for Sr–Rh–Ga phases (CS5, SS5, TS5). The method uses specialized equipment and inert atmosphere, as also seen for UFe₆Ga₆ induction melting under Ar (CS1). The major route-specific uncertainty is volatile Mg/Sb loss during melting, which is not directly addressed by the Rh analogues and is therefore an extrapolated risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melting improves homogenization and reduces diffusion limitations, which is why chemically similar intermetallics can be made by induction or arc melting followed by annealing (CS1, CS4, CS5). However, the final ordered Heusler-like trigonal phase may require annealing, analogous to ordering concerns in Zr₅Ni₄Al (CS5). Kinetic uncertainty remains because Mg evaporation could shift composition before the ordered phase nucleates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route inherits demanding sealed-tube handling from LiRh₂Ge, which required Ar glovebox loading, welded Nb tubes, evacuated silica sheaths, controlled heating/cooling, and later flux removal (SS2, TS2). Mg₈Rh₄B also supports the need for Ta containers and evacuated silica ampoules at 850–1000 °C (CS3). Severity is increased by controlled/vacuum sealing and volatile/air-sensitive constituents, but it avoids GPa pressure and avoids routine >1000 °C operation.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux or self-flux transport should improve Rh incorporation and crystal growth compared with purely solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. LiRh₂Ge demonstrates that a Rh-containing Heusler phase can be grown from excess reactive metal flux at 1000 °C with slow cooling (SS2, TS2). The uncertainty is that Mg–Sb self-flux chemistry is not directly demonstrated in the provided data, so transferability from Li flux is a reasoned extrapolation rather than direct evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with relatively simple body-centered-cubic-like coordination motifs, and structurally similar MgRh, LiRh₂Ge, and Rh₂SnCu are known crystalline phases (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). A moderate size mismatch among Mg, Rh, and Sb triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms or that mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity dominates the target, so those checks are treated as neutral/non-triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₃SbRh₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state intermetallic because its calculated E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom (target data). The strongest support comes from MgRh, which shares Mg–Rh body-centered-cubic coordination and is experimentally accessible, and from Rh-based Heusler analogues LiRh₂Ge and Rh₂SnCu, which show Rh–p-block Heusler frameworks can be synthesized (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic signal plus close Mg–Rh bond-length/topology match between the target and MgRh (target; SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under Mg/Sb volatility and possible competition with MgRh or Rh–Sb binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with a best estimated score of 79/100, but it will likely require sealed refractory containment and careful composition control.
