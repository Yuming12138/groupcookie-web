### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state compound and receives a high F1 score. Several thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable, including AuCuSe4, CuBrSe3, KAuS5, CuSe2Br, and SBr (TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or avoidance of competing polymorphs/flux phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Alkali Polysulfide Flux Growth

The route requires vacuum sealing, inert handling, reactive alkali sulfide/polysulfide flux, and post-growth flux removal, so F2 is penalized despite the low temperature (TS1, TS3). Kinetically, this is the strongest route because Au is known to dissolve in molten alkali polychalcogenide media at 250–350 °C, and the very close AuCuSe4 analogue crystallizes by flux at 310 °C (SS2, TS1, TS3). The main uncertainty is phase purity, since AuCuSe4 flux growth produced mixed phases and pure product was difficult (SS2, TS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Iodine-Assisted CVT

The CVT route uses modest temperature but requires vacuum sealing and volatile iodine/sulfur, leading to moderate condition severity (CS4, SS5, TS2). Kinetics should benefit from vapor transport, as HgGa2S4 and CuBrSe3 analogues form single crystals by CVT/transport-type methods (CS4, SS5, TS2). The uncertainty is high because there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that AgAuS4 forms volatile transport species without decomposing or forming AgI/AuI side phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Direct Evacuated-Ampoule Annealing

This route has the simplest equipment and mildest process severity, similar to the evacuated-ampoule sulfur-rich preparation of Hg2P2S7 (CS5). However, kinetics are weaker because direct reaction of Ag2S/Au/S must create ordered Ag–Au–S–S connectivity without a liquid flux, and long annealing may be needed, as shown by the four-week Hg2P2S7 synthesis (CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports direct non-flux formation of the close AuCuSe4-type motif, so this route is less reliable than flux growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has differentiated Ag and Au coordination environments, suggesting cation-size/coordination mismatch and one amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. No evidence indicates a very large unit cell or coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedra, so C2 and C3 are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents a more precise cell-size assessment. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgAuS4 is likely a true ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strongest for flux synthesis: AuCuSe4 provides a close square-planar Au / coinage-metal chalcogenide structural analogue, while KAuS5 confirms accessible Au–S polysulfide chemistry in alkali chalcogenide fluxes (SS2, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the use of a sulfur-rich polysulfide flux, which should both dissolve noble metals and stabilize S–S units [Reasonable Inference from TS3/CS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition, especially formation of alkali gold sulfides, Ag2S, Au sulfides, or off-stoichiometric polysulfide phases; mixed phases were explicitly problematic for AuCuSe4 (SS2, TS1). Overall, the target is feasible but likely requires careful flux composition and slow-cooling optimization.
