### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted to be exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which justifies a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues are particularly strong: TS1–TS5 are all close in thermodynamic space and include multiple E_hull = 0.0 phases. CS evidence is supportive but not perfect because CS2 and CS5 include positive hull energies, although CS1 and CS3 are hull-stable. Remaining uncertainty is that the computed hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or suppression of nearby V–Si/Ir–Si/V–Ir binary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because arc-melting and annealing above 1000 °C are common in the closest analogues (CS1, CS5, SS2, SS5, TS4, TS5). The main deductions are high temperature and multistep remelting/annealing, both supported by the experimental analogue procedures (CS5, SS2). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that V₃SiIr₄ requires high pressure, so that deduction is not applied. The remaining uncertainty is Si loss during arc-melting, which is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly quantified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory V and Ir, but arc-melting provides a liquid-state mixing pathway, which is why many Ir-rich analogues are feasible by this route (CS5, SS2, SS5, TS4). Structurally similar cuboctahedral Ir/transition-metal frameworks in SS1–SS5 support the likelihood that the target framework can be reached after annealing. However, repeated remelting and annealing are often required for homogeneity/order in analogues (CS1, SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3), so the kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it includes vacuum pre-sintering and more processing stages, similar to CS5 and SS3/TS2. High-temperature arc-melting remains necessary, so the >1000 °C and >1400 °C deductions apply. There is no high-pressure requirement in the relevant CS/SS/TS analogues. The route is justified if direct arc-melting gives segregation or incomplete reaction, a problem noted for refractory alloy preparation in SS3/TS2.

#### F3 — Route 2

Pre-sintering improves precursor contact, and powder metallurgy is specifically used in SS3/TS2 to address incomplete melting/mixing issues. Nevertheless, Ir and V diffusion remain slow [Reasonable Inference], and the route still requires melt-assisted mixing or extended homogenization. Structural ordering is plausible because SS2 and CS5 show that Ir-rich intermetallic/boride phases can be obtained after arc-melting plus annealing, but final V₃SiIr₄ ordering may need optimization.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids local arc temperatures above 1400 °C if conducted only as a sealed-tube anneal, which lowers thermal severity relative to Routes 1–2. However, controlled vacuum/Ar and sealed-tube operation are required, as in SS2 and CS2. Volatile Si management is a [Reasonable Inference] that adds a deduction because sealed systems may be needed to preserve stoichiometry. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that sealed-tube solid-state synthesis alone can make this exact Ir-rich silicide, so confidence is lower.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state kinetics are the main weakness because elemental Ir and V are refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 demonstrates that sealed-tube sintering can require very long anneals, although CS2 is chemically less relevant and therefore only weakly supportive. SS2 supports sealed annealing after arc-melting, but not necessarily direct formation from unmelted elements. Thus F3 is low for a purely solid-state route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger: a significant size/chemical mismatch between Si and the transition metals [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not show the exact vertex+edge+face-sharing coexistence required for C3, even though some structurally similar analogues have complex polyhedral sharing (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). There is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence on the target unit-cell atom count and radius mismatch, so those source signals are treated as neutral where not directly inferable. Overall risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₃SiIr₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for arc-melting routes, since chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar Ir-rich metallic compounds are repeatedly reported from elemental arc-melting under Ar followed by remelting or annealing (CS1, CS5, SS2, SS5, TS4, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is melt-assisted homogenization, which overcomes the slow solid-state diffusion expected for refractory V and Ir [Reasonable Inference] and is repeatedly used in analogue syntheses (CS1, SS5, TS4, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially possible competition from V–Si, V–Ir, and Ir–Si binary phases and possible Si loss during arc-melting [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears moderately likely to be synthesizable, with the best first attempt being arc-melting plus a 1000–1150 °C anneal.
