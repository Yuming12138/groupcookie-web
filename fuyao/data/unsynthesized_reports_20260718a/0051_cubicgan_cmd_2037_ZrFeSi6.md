#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the highest stability bin in the required rubric. This is favorable but not unequivocally ground-state-like, because close thermodynamic analogues CeSi₅ and Zr₂CuSi₄ have E_hull values of 0.058 and 0.0587 eV/atom and require nontrivial synthesis routes such as high pressure or long annealing (TS-1, TS-4). The remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic F-43m ZrFeSi₆ polymorph is the experimentally accessible polymorph or a metastable computational minimum [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc Melting + Long Ar Anneal

The severity score is moderate because arc melting involves very high local temperature and multiple remelts, but it avoids high pressure and hazardous volatile elements; this is consistent with BaRh₂Si₉, TbCoSi₃, and Zr/Hf–Cu–Si analogues made by arc melting under Ar (CS-1, SS-3, TS-3, TS-4). Kinetics are limited by refractory Zr and the need for long-range ordering into the target cubic framework [Reasonable Inference]. Analogues requiring 8 days to 8 weeks annealing indicate slow homogenization and phase ordering, so F3 is only moderate despite liquid-state mixing during arc melting (CS-1, SS-3, TS-3, TS-4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-Pressure High-Temperature Synthesis

The severity score is low-to-moderate because the closest Si-rich metastable silicide analogues require 10–15 GPa and specialized Walker/multi-anvil equipment (CS-5, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). Kinetics are better than ordinary solid-state reaction if binary silicides are used, since CeSi₅/YSi₃-type syntheses use silicide + Si precursors and quenching to trap metastable products (CS-5, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes the target cubic ZrFeSi₆ topology or instead favors competing dense Zr–Si/Fe–Si phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Ga-Flux Growth

The severity score benefits from lower temperatures and no high pressure, matching MNiSi₃ flux syntheses at 850–1000 °C (SS-5, TS-5). Kinetics may improve due to liquid flux transport, but there is no direct CS evidence for Ga-flux synthesis of Zr–Fe–Si phases, so the transferability is less secure. The main uncertainty is whether Zr dissolves sufficiently in Ga and whether Ga stabilizes unwanted Zr–Ga or Fe–Ga side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State Reaction

The severity is moderate because the route uses vacuum/Ar sealed tubes and multi-day anneals but no high pressure, similar to LnFe₄As₁₂ ampoule reactions and Zr/Hf–Cu–Si anneals (CS-2, CS-3, TS-3, TS-4). Kinetics are the weakest because diffusion occurs through solid silicide phases and the target structure is not inherited from the starting binaries [Reasonable Inference]. The evidence base is also less direct because CS sealed-tube examples are arsenides, while SS provides insufficient direct silicide sealed-tube evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The main amorphization/crystallization risk comes from the large size and coordination contrast between Zr and Fe, reflected in ZrSi₁₂ cuboctahedra versus FeSi₆ octahedra in the target description [Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom count is not clearly >40 from the supplied data, and the target description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so only one of the three checks is triggered. The residual uncertainty is that the true conventional cell size from the CIF could change the C2 assessment, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on this target’s cell size.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrFeSi₆ is best viewed as a near-hull, likely metastable or marginally stable intermetallic rather than a clearly robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and close thermodynamic analogues near this energy often need long annealing or high pressure (TS-1, TS-4). The synthesis evidence is reasonably supportive: arc melting is strongly represented among chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically related silicides/intermetallics (CS-1, SS-3, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is use of a molten/alloyed state plus extended annealing to overcome Zr/Fe/Si diffusion and ordering barriers, as repeatedly shown by arc-melted analogues (CS-1, TS-3, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among elemental Si, Fe silicides, and Zr silicides, consistent with reported multiphase behavior and difficult phase control in analogous Si-rich intermetallics (CS-1, TS-3, TS-4). High pressure is a viable fallback if ambient-pressure arc annealing fails, because Si-rich metastable silicides such as CeSi₅ and YSi₃ are stabilized by 10–15 GPa methods (CS-5, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2).
