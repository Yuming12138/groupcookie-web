### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, so it receives 90/100 by the required ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin. TS provides relevant support because synthesized thermodynamic analogues include Na₃As at 0.0425 eV/atom (TS1), Na₆FeS₄ at 0.0466 eV/atom (TS3), and even KNa₂NiO₂ at 0.0777 eV/atom (TS2). CS/SS analogues are mostly on-hull or near-hull Na-rich phases, supporting feasibility of alkali-rich compounds (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that Na₆PbO has no direct ternary analogue in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature and high pressure, consistent with Na₆ZnSn₂ at 773 K (CS2/SS2) and Na₃As at 700 °C (TS1). The main severity penalties are sealed containment for volatile Na and air/toxicity hazards from Na/Na₂O/Pb; sealed ampoules are repeatedly required for Na-rich analogues (CS1/SS1, CS2/SS2, SS3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that Na₆PbO specifically can tolerate silica, so Ta/Ni containment is recommended by [Reasonable Inference]. The route is moderate rather than easy due to air sensitivity and stoichiometry control.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be acceptable because Na is molten and reactive at the proposed temperature, and direct fusion works for close Na-rich analogues such as Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃As (CS2/SS2, TS1). Pb is not refractory, and Li–Pb compounds form by melt methods under Ar (CS3, CS5), supporting low diffusion barriers in alkali–Pb systems. The main kinetic uncertainty is incorporating oxygen from Na₂O into the Na–Pb framework, for which CS/SS provide insufficient direct evidence and TS2 only gives a broader alkali oxide precedent. Therefore F3 is solid but not high.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses lower temperatures, consistent with the 520 °C alkali oxide annealing in TS2, so no high-temperature penalty applies. However, TS2 uses long-duration sealed-container processing, and adapting that to Na/Pb/Na₂O likely requires multi-step drying, mixing, sealing, and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Volatile/air-sensitive Na species and toxic Pb impose additional severity penalties, while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that low-temperature annealing alone will form the Na₆PbO framework. This route is the most operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetics are weaker than Route 1 because solid Na₂O and Pb may have limited interfacial contact and slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. TS2’s 47 d anneal indicates that alkali oxide ordering can be slow even at 520 °C. Although Na/Pb are not refractory, no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates rapid solid-state formation of a Na–Pb–O plumbide oxide. The score is therefore moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 remains below 1000 °C, consistent with Na₆ZnSn₂ fusion at 773 K (CS2/SS2), Na₃As at 700 °C (TS1), and Na₃AgIn₂ high-temperature/annealing procedures (SS5). It is penalized for volatile Na, air-sensitive handling, and multi-step flux growth/decanting [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS show that sealed Ta ampoules and slow cooling are effective for Na-rich crystals (CS2/SS2, SS3, SS5). The difficulty is moderate to challenging because flux separation and oxygen transport are uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3

The Na-rich melt should improve kinetics through liquid transport, consistent with direct fusion and slow-cooling success for Na₆ZnSn₂, Na₃Hg₂, and Na₃AgIn₂ (CS2/SS2, SS3, SS5). This route likely has the best crystallization kinetics among the three because diffusion occurs through a liquid Na-rich medium [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for Na₂O solubility in Na–Pb flux, so oxygen incorporation remains uncertain. Thus F3 is high-moderate rather than excellent.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Na₆PbO, so their source-specific signal is neutral. Based on the target structure, the formula is simple and the polyhedral network is not described as simultaneously vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing; only the size/role mismatch between Na and Pb triggers a risk point [Reasonable Inference]. Similar Na-rich crystalline analogues exist in CS/SS, indicating that alkali-rich metallic frameworks can crystallize rather than amorphize (CS1/SS1, CS2/SS2, SS3). Remaining uncertainty comes from the unusual combination of oxide and plumbide chemistry.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆PbO is likely near the ground-state/metastability boundary because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is within the commonly accessible metastability window and at the cutoff of the highest rubric bin. The CS and SS analogues strongly support sealed, inert-atmosphere synthesis of Na-rich metallic/intermetallic frameworks, especially Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃Bi (CS1/SS1, CS2/SS2). TS analogues support the thermodynamic plausibility of synthesizing compounds with similar hull energies, including Na₃As and Na₆FeS₄ (TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is molten/reactive Na in sealed Ta/Ni ampoules, which can promote transport and preserve stoichiometry. The most critical limiting factor is controlling oxygen incorporation from Na₂O without forming Na₂O-rich phases, Pb oxides, or binary Na–Pb intermetallics [Reasonable Inference].
