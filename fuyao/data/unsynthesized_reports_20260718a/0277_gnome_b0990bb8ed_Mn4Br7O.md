### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted at `E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom`, which places it at the computed ground-state hull and warrants the maximum F1 score (Target). Thermodynamic analogues include several stable halide/chalcogenide frameworks with `E_hull = 0.0`, including Zr/Hf/Hg/Se/halide phases and TlMnCl₃ (TS1–TS5). Chemical evidence is partly mixed: MnInBr₃ is essentially stable at 0.004 eV/atom (CS3), while Mo₆Br₁₂O₂ has a high database hull energy but was experimentally prepared under pressure (CS1), so CS alone is not uniformly predictive; insufficient evidence from CS to confirm ambient-pressure thermodynamic accessibility of Mn₄Br₇O, neutral source score 50 for that specific sub-question.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa conditions, consistent with MnInBr₃ and related bromides forming near 450 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS3, CS5, SS2). The deductions arise from the required evacuated sealed tube, volatile Br₂/bromide vapor, multistep drying/loading/heating/cooling, and toxic or moisture-sensitive halide precursors (CS3, CS5, TS4, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the target’s oxygen content can be fixed without overforming Mn oxides or simple Mn bromides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is favorable because molten halide chemistry gives high precursor activity and analogues form at temperatures far below refractory solid-state conditions (CS3, SS2, TS5). Edge-sharing MnBr₆ motifs in MnInBr₃ closely resemble the target’s MnBr₆/MnBr₅O octahedral network, reducing the degree of structural reconstruction (Target, CS3, SS2). The uncertainty is oxygen ordering into one Mn–O site and maintaining the mixed Mn valence implied by Mn₄Br₇O [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

CVT uses moderate temperatures but requires vacuum, volatile halogens, and sealed-ampoule handling, as shown by 360–475 °C evacuated-ampoule CVT analogues (SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1–TS3). The F2 deductions are therefore similar to Route 1, despite the lower nominal temperature. The main uncertainty is that no cited CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates vapor-transport synthesis of an Mn oxybromide; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Mn–O–Br CVT, neutral source score 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Vapor transport can promote crystal growth in related halide frameworks, and several similar materials crystallize from sealed ampoules with temperature gradients (SS1, SS3, SS4, TS1–TS3). However, the same sources report low yields, equilibrium mixtures, and air/moisture sensitivity for some phases, lowering the crystallization and composition-control scores (SS1, TS1). The Br/O ratio in Mn₄Br₇O is likely more sensitive than purely halide frameworks because oxygen transport is not directly supported by the CVT analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure HBr route is chemically relevant because Mo oxybromide was synthesized from Mo and HBr at 700 °C and 3000 atm (CS1). Under the provided scoring rubric, 3000 atm is below the >5 GPa high-pressure deduction threshold, but the route still incurs penalties for reactive sealed HBr, volatile/corrosive reagents, multistep pressure-capsule handling, and toxic/air-sensitive materials (CS1). This route is experimentally more demanding than the numerical F2 score fully captures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

High-pressure HBr should provide strong bromination and may accelerate mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, consistent with CS1 forming oxybromide crystals in 8 h (CS1). The structural kinetic penalty is significant because CS1’s Mo oxybromide contains cluster/square-pyramidal motifs, not the target’s extended edge-sharing Mn octahedra (CS1, Target). Thus the route is chemically enabling but structurally less transferable than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation element, Mn, so the ionic-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from Target formula]. The described target connectivity is dominated by edge-sharing octahedra, not a coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, and the closest Mn bromide analogue also uses edge-sharing MnBr₆ octahedra (Target, CS3, SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves the target unit cell atom count, so that source-specific signal is neutral at 50, but the formula and simple R3m description do not indicate an amorphization-prone large cell [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₄Br₇O is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed energy above hull is 0.0 eV/atom (Target). The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: Mn–Br edge-sharing octahedral frameworks are experimentally accessible by sealed-ampoule molten bromide methods (CS3, SS2), and oxybromide formation is chemically precedented under brominating conditions (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature mobility of halide melts/vapors, which allows Mn halide frameworks to crystallize near 450–500 °C rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (CS3, CS5, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of oxygen incorporation and bromine chemical potential in a mixed-valence Mn oxybromide, which is not directly demonstrated by any single CS/SS/TS analogue [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthesizable, with Route 1 as the most practical first attempt.
