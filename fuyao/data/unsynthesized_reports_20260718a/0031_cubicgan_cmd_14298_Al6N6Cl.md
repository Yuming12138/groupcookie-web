### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.05 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable boundary of the provided 90–100 score range. Thermodynamically similar references include synthesized materials with E_hull values of 0.046–0.107 eV/atom, showing that comparable metastability does not preclude synthesis (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar materials also include synthesized phases at E_hull = 0–0.118 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is that TS chemistry is often unrelated to Al–N–Cl, so TS is strong for energy scale but weaker for route prediction.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because structurally and chemically related nitrides often require 5–12 GPa and 800–1200 °C, using multianvil assemblies and glovebox handling (SS1–SS5, CS3). The high-pressure deduction is strongly supported by SS and TS nitride analogues, especially Zn₂PN₃ at 8 GPa/1200 °C and g-P₃N₅ at 11 GPa/1500 °C (CS3, SS3, TS2). The chlorine precursor volatility/toxicity deductions are [Reasonable Inference] because no source directly gives an Al–N–Cl precursor system. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for HPHT Al–N–Cl but supports that chemically related systems can require controlled sealed environments.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by the likely low activity of AlN and the need to form a new cubic Al–N–Cl network [Reasonable Inference]. HPHT nitride analogues show that short reaction times are possible when reactive nitride precursors are used, but they also require specialized conditions to prevent decomposition or stabilize dense phases (SS3, SS5, TS2). Structural reconstruction is likely substantial because no SS/CS analogue has the same Fm-3m Al₆N₆Cl motif. Remaining uncertainty is whether high pressure stabilizes or destabilizes the N–Cl coordination.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids multianvil pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, which improves condition severity relative to Route 1. Sealed ampoule reactions at 740–1080 °C under vacuum are well supported for chemically similar chalcogenides (CS1–CS4). However, volatile chlorine-source chemistry, vacuum sealing, multistep heating, and moisture-sensitive handling impose deductions [Reasonable Inference], with partial support from volatile iodine transport in CS2. SS and TS nitride sources warn that ambient-pressure synthesis may be insufficient for unusual nitride networks (SS1–SS5, TS2).

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are moderately favorable because volatile chloride chemistry may assist mass transport, analogous to chemical vapor transport in CS2, and long ampoule anneals are effective in CS1–CS4. The main limitation is structural reconstruction from AlN/chloride precursors into the target cubic NAl₄Cl square-pyramidal network, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct match. CS1 and CS4 report phase-control issues and long heat treatments in related solid-state systems, supporting a penalty for ordering kinetics. Evidence from SS is insufficient for ambient-pressure Al–N–Cl formation, so that component is treated cautiously.

#### F2 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition avoids bulk diffusion and HPHT penalties, but it requires a controlled reactive N/Cl atmosphere [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS reference directly supports thin-film growth, so direct source evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS for this route. Corrosive chlorine precursors and narrow process windows create significant practical severity [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains plausible mainly because the target is only 0.05 eV/atom above hull.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics may benefit from non-equilibrium incorporation and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. However, crystallization of the exact cubic phase is unconfirmed by CS/SS/TS, and composition control is likely difficult for a low Cl content [Reasonable Inference]. The lack of thin-film analogues means precursor vapor availability and crystallization scores are based on general synthesis reasoning rather than direct evidence. This uncertainty keeps the kinetic score below the sealed-ampoule route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Al, so cation-radius mismatch is not a source of amorphization [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description explicitly includes mixed corner- and edge-sharing NAl₄Cl square pyramids, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. The reduced formula contains 13 atoms, but the full conventional Fm-3m cell atom count is not provided, so evidence for a >40-atom cell is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and the target description. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best interpreted as a low-energy metastable or near-threshold phase because its E_hull is 0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamic analogues show that phases in this energy range can be synthesized, but their chemistries are diverse, so TS supports feasibility more than route specificity (TS1–TS5). Structurally similar nitride analogues strongly suggest that unusual nitride networks often need high pressure, especially for dense or nitrogen-rich bonding environments (SS1–SS5). Chemically similar CS materials support sealed ampoule solid-state and transport methods, but they are sulfides rather than nitride chlorides, so transferability is incomplete (CS1–CS4). The most critical enabling factor is controlled nitrogen/chlorine chemical potential in a sealed or high-pressure environment. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control involving volatile chlorine species and refractory AlN [Reasonable Inference].
