### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability category. Multiple close analogues are also calculated on the hull, including CsBiO2, K3(PtO2)2, K3Pd2O4, Cs4IrO4, and Cs2RuO4 (CS-1, SS-1/TS-1, SS-2/TS-2, SS-4/TS-4, TS-5). This supports a likely thermodynamic phase rather than a highly metastable target, although computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because comparable Cs/alkali noble-metal oxides form at 500–800 °C in sealed capsules/tubes (CS-1, SS-1/TS-1, SS-4/TS-4, TS-5). The main severity penalties are the need for sealed noble-metal containment, air-sensitive Cs oxide/peroxide precursors, and multi-step handling/sealing, all recurring in the analogues (CS-2, CS-3, SS-4/TS-4, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is the correct oxygen potential for Pt in CsPtO2, which is inferred rather than directly measured [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate because solid-state analogues often require long anneals, including 7 d for K3(PtO2)2, 31 d for Cs2RuO4, 86 d for Cs4IrO4, and up to 3 months for Cs2PbO3 (SS-1/TS-1, TS-5, SS-4/TS-4, CS-2). The target’s Pt–O bond length of 1.99 Å closely matches the Pt–O distances in K3(PtO2)2, suggesting structural compatibility (target description, SS-1/TS-1). However, refractory Pt diffusion and competing Cs–Pt–O phases remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized more strongly because it requires controlled oxygen activity using oxygen-rich Cs oxide/superoxide chemistry, analogous to KO2/PdO under controlled O2 and CsO1.8/IrO2 in sealed Ag-bombs (SS-2/TS-2, SS-4/TS-4). It still avoids high pressure and >1000 °C, consistent with analogous synthesis windows (SS-2/TS-2, SS-4/TS-4). The main uncertainty is container compatibility under oxidizing Cs-rich conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Reactive oxygen-rich precursors should improve kinetics compared with dense oxides [Reasonable Inference], and K3Pd2O4 forms in 20–48 h using KO2 + PdO at 600–700 °C (SS-2/TS-2). Cs4IrO4 nevertheless required 86 d and a complex temperature program, showing that Cs-rich square-planar 5d oxide formation can still be slow and phase-selective (SS-4/TS-4). Thus kinetics are better than conventional oxide mixing but not easy.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses reactive peroxide/superoxide media, lowering temperature but increasing handling severity. The air-sensitive/oxidizing precursor penalty is supported by KO2/Cs oxide analogue handling and sealed-tube use (SS-2/TS-2, CS-2, TS-5). Repeated regrinding is not directly specified in the references and is a [Reasonable Inference] to compensate for kinetic limitations.

#### F3 — Route 3

Peroxide/superoxide precursors give the best estimated kinetics because KO2/PdO analogues form over relatively short 20–48 h holds (SS-2/TS-2). Regrinding and lower diffusion lengths should help Pt incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that Pt may oxidize/reduce differently from Pd under Cs peroxide/superoxide conditions, so phase control may be less predictable [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Cs coordination with compact square-planar PtO4 units, giving a large cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference supported by target Cs–O 3.03/3.31 Å and Pt–O 1.99 Å distances]. The target also explicitly has mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, a known complexity factor for crystallization [Reasonable Inference], and this motif is mirrored in RbAuO2 and K3(PtO2)2 analogues (CS-4, SS-1/TS-1). Unit-cell atom count is not provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is treated as neutral for that check.
