### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for predicted ground-state stability and merits a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Close analogues ScIrSi, ScPtSi, ZrOsSi, ScOsGe, YOs₂Si₂, TbOs₂Si₂, and HfOsSi-related phases are also listed as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, reinforcing that this chemical space supports stable silicides (CS, SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is computational/experimental: E_hull = 0 confirms thermodynamic plausibility but not necessarily absence of competing kinetic products or off-stoichiometric binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses very standard intermetallic conditions: arc-melting under Ar and annealing in evacuated silica/quartz ampoules, directly reported for ScIrSi/ScRuSi and ZrOsSi analogues (CS, SS, TS). The severity deductions arise from high local arc-melting temperatures, vacuum ampoule annealing, repeated melting/annealing steps, and Sc/Os handling concerns (CS, SS, TS; [Reasonable Inference]). The route is not high-pressure, and there is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that volatile precursor retention is required.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are helped by melt homogenization, which is why arc-melting is repeatedly used for ScTSi, ZrOsSi, ScOsGe, and HfTSi-related analogues (CS, SS, TS). However, elemental Os is refractory and elemental starting materials are low-activity compared with molecular or solution precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The very close structural match to ScIrSi and ZrOsSi lowers the reconstruction penalty, but 1–2 week anneals indicate ordering/homogenization is not instantaneous (CS, SS, TS).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids evacuated quartz annealing during the highest-temperature step by using a sealed refractory-metal tube under Ar, as shown for YIrX induction melting (CS). It is still severe because the reported induction temperature, 1900 K, exceeds 1400 °C, and the method requires sealed-tube welding plus handling of Sc/Os elements (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact sealed induction protocol, so those sources are neutral for the route-specific feasibility beyond confirming related Os silicides can be made.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The main kinetic advantage is that sealed induction heating can produce a high-temperature molten or near-molten mixture, improving mixing and reducing diffusion limitations relative to powder annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The YIrX precedent demonstrates that 1:1:1 rare-earth/transition-metal silicides can be processed this way (CS). The uncertainty is that Route 2 is not directly demonstrated for ScOsSi or ZrOsSi, so structural crystallization into the exact Pnma target after induction processing remains extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses conventional ampoule or gettered-Ar annealing, supported by LuTiSi, ScOsGe/ScPtSi-related, and HfTSi-related entries (CS, SS, TS). It avoids the very high-temperature induction step if no arc pre-melting is used, but still needs controlled atmosphere/vacuum, long heat treatment, and likely multi-step grinding/pelletization [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is indicated in any source, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that any volatile precursor is required.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solid-state powder reaction is kinetically weakest because elemental Sc/Os/Si are low-activity and Os diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The long anneals reported across analogues—1–2 weeks for ScT(Si/Ge), one week for ZrOsSi, and up to 21 days for HfTSi-related samples—support the need for extended diffusion/ordering times (CS, SS, TS). The close structural analogue support improves the final crystallization likelihood, but this does not fully overcome the refractory diffusion bottleneck.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with many crystalline analogues synthesized by melt/anneal routes, so amorphization is not strongly indicated by the evidence (CS, SS, TS). A possible size-mismatch trigger is assigned based on Sc vs Os size difference [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii directly. The unit cell does not appear large and no explicit mixed polyhedral connectivity is documented, so these checks are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a high amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScOsSi is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple nearby Sc/Os/Si or T–Os–Si analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS, SS, TS). The analogue support is strong: ScIrSi is nearly isostructural and chemically close in the ScTSi family (CS), while ZrOsSi is an Os-containing Pnma structural and thermodynamic analogue (SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated melt/anneal accessibility of 1:1:1 intermetallic silicides under Ar followed by ampoule annealing (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic homogenization of refractory Os-containing mixtures, requiring high-temperature melting and/or extended annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, ScOsSi should be experimentally accessible in a well-equipped intermetallic synthesis lab, but phase purity will depend strongly on stoichiometry, oxygen exclusion, and annealing time.
