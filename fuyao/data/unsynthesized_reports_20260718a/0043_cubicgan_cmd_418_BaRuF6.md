### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, placing it at the edge of the most favorable stability bin in the required rubric. CS provides strong support because RbRuF₆, CsRuF₆, and BaGeF₆ are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). TS also supports accessibility because Ba₂PdO₂F₂ has E_hull ≈0.052 eV/atom and is experimentally synthesized by fluorination (TS-3), while other thermodynamic analogues up to ~0.105 eV/atom are reported as synthesized (TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that the cubic F-43m BaRuF₆ polymorph may be metastable relative to a lower-symmetry distortion, because the closest RuF₆ analogues are trigonal R-3m rather than cubic (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is chemically direct but condition-severe because it uses elemental F₂, which is explicitly required in RbRuF₆/CsRuF₆ synthesis (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). The temperature is mild at 350 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (CS-1, CS-2). Deductions come from reactive F₂ atmosphere, toxic/reactive handling, and possible volatile Ru fluoride formation [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the phase-control cautions in CS-1/CS-2. TS gives insufficient route-specific evidence for F₂ fluorination and is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic advantage of Route 1 is high precursor activity: RuCl₃ and chloride mixtures are directly fluorinated to RuF₆-based analogues at 350 °C (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). The main kinetic concern is cation ordering and possible formation of lower-symmetry trigonal variants, because the closest analogues are R-3m rather than cubic F-43m (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). Gas-phase fluorination likely lowers diffusion barriers relative to dense binary-fluoride reactions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are favorable-to-moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids elemental F₂ but requires sealed noble-metal tubes and dry handling, which are common in Ba fluoride and transition-metal fluoride syntheses (CS-5; SS-2, SS-3, SS-5). Temperatures of 500–700 °C are below the high-temperature penalty threshold and are consistent with sealed-tube fluoride analogues (CS-5; SS-3). Deductions are assigned for controlled Ar/sealed conditions, possible volatile Ru fluoride species [Reasonable Inference], and moisture-sensitive fluoride handling supported by SS-3. CS/SS support the sealed-fluoride method, but not specifically BaRuF₆.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary fluorides are less reactive than chloride/F₂ systems, so precursor activity is moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed-tube fluorides often require long heating, intermediate grinding, or quenching, as seen in Ba/Pd and Ba/Al fluoride analogues (CS-5; SS-3). The route is structurally plausible because Ba fluoride frameworks form readily in sealed tubes (CS-5; SS-2, SS-3), but the exact cubic BaRuF₆ structure is not demonstrated by CS/SS. Therefore F3 is lower than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild formal conditions because TS-3 demonstrates low-temperature fluorination of Ba₂₋ₓSrₓPdO₃ precursors at 200–350 °C using CuF₂ or PVDF. No high pressure, F₂ gas, or >1000 °C heating is required in the TS-3 analogue. The route still receives a toxicity deduction because fluorinating agents and possible Ru fluorinated intermediates are hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for oxide-to-BaRuF₆ conversion, so they are neutral for this severity assessment.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic weakness of Route 3 is structural reconstruction: TS-3 forms an oxyfluoride, not a fully anhydrous hexafluorometallate. Complete removal of oxygen from a Ba–Ru oxide lattice would likely require deeper reconstruction than the Pd oxyfluoride analogue [Reasonable Inference]. The low temperature helps suppress decomposition but may also trap oxyfluoride or amorphous intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is moderate-low despite good F2.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses mild hydrothermal temperatures of 150–200 °C, directly supported by BaVOF₄ and thermodynamic oxyfluoride syntheses (SS-4; TS-1). No high pressure above 5 GPa or high-temperature penalty applies; the pressure is only autogenous hydrothermal pressure (SS-4; TS-1). The main severity deduction is HF toxicity/corrosivity, which is explicit in SS-4 and TS-1. CS-3 supports solution crystallization of a Ba hexafluorometallate-like fluoride, but not Ru-specific hydrothermal chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 4

Hydrothermal HF conditions provide good precursor mobility and crystal-growth kinetics, as demonstrated for BaVOF₄ and related oxyfluorides (SS-4; TS-1). However, the structural/speciation risk is substantial because the closest hydrothermal examples are oxyfluorides, and Ru⁴⁺ behavior in HF is not established by CS/SS/TS. BaGeF₆ slow evaporation supports Ba hexafluoride crystallization from solution (CS-3), but Ge⁴⁺ is chemically much more benign than Ru⁴⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. This route is attractive experimentally but less certain chemically.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large BaF₁₂ cuboctahedra with compact RuF₆ octahedra, creating a large cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], while analogous RuF₆ phases also contain large A-site cuboctahedra and RuF₆ octahedra (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). Mixed corner and face sharing is present in the target and is also seen in RbRuF₆/CsRuF₆ connectivity motifs (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). The atom-count criterion is not triggered because the formula and F-43m description imply a relatively simple cell [Reasonable Inference], although CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct atom-count evidence for the target cell. Two triggers give a medium amorphization/phase-selection risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaRuF₆ is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline-stable fluoride phase rather than an obviously unstable compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and synthesized TS analogues exist at comparable or higher E_hull values (TS-3, TS-4). The strongest support comes from CS/SS analogues RbRuF₆ and CsRuF₆, which share RuF₆ octahedra, large-cation fluoride coordination, and direct F₂ fluorination chemistry (CS-1, CS-2; SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of aggressive fluorination chemistry that can form Ru–F octahedra at relatively low temperature, as shown for RbRuF₆/CsRuF₆ (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical limiting factor is control of Ru oxidation state and polymorph selection: BaRuF₆ requires Ru⁴⁺, whereas alkali RuF₆ analogues imply Ru⁵⁺ charge balance [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine, and direct fluorination is the most defensible first route even though hydrothermal methods score well on mildness.
