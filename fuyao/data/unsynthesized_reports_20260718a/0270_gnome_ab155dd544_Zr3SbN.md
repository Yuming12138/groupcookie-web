### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data (Target). This is reinforced by multiple close analogues that also lie on the hull, including Zr3AlN, Zr2InN, Zr5Sb3P/S/Si, and Zr6PtSb2 (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature chemical-potential stability against Sb loss, ZrN persistence, or competing Zr–Sb binaries; CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct phase-diagram evidence for Zr–Sb–N, so that uncertainty is not fully resolved.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits a successful melt-growth strategy from isostructural Zr3AlN, where compacted nitride/metal pellets were melted under pure Ar and slow cooled (CS1/SS2/TS2). The severity score is penalized because melting Zr-rich refractory charges implies >1000 °C and likely >1400 °C operation [Reasonable Inference], and Sb volatility requires compensation or very short melt exposure, as demonstrated for Zr–Sb arc-melt work using excess Sb (TS5). No high pressure is needed, which keeps the route feasible with standard arc/induction metallurgy equipment (CS1, TS5).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is that a melt route avoids most solid-state diffusion limitations, consistent with successful Zr3AlN formation by melting and slow cooling (CS1/SS2/TS2). The structural-reconstruction burden is low because the target and Zr3AlN share the same Cmcm topology, Zr-site motifs, and corner/edge-sharing NZr6 framework (CS1/SS2/TS2). The main uncertainty is whether Sb evaporation or segregation during cooling disrupts stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference; Sb-loss concern supported by TS5].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids full melting but uses sealed ampoules, long heating, and possibly vacuum or inert encapsulation, which are standard but controlled conditions (SS1/TS1, CS4). The score is penalized for sealed/vacuum operation, Sb volatility, multi-step grinding/annealing, and Zr/Sb handling risks [Reasonable Inference; volatile-element care and glovebox/vacuum methods in CS4]. The temperature is moderate compared with arc melting, but it may still exceed 1000 °C if dense ZrN reacts slowly (CS4).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are the main limitation because ZrN and Zr are refractory and require long diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. This is consistent with H-phase nitride syntheses requiring 750–850 h at 500–900 °C and Zr5Sn3N-type reactive sintering taking 3–14 days at 1000–1350 °C (SS1/TS1, CS4). The structure is plausible, but no CS/SS/TS entry directly proves sealed-tube synthesis of Cmcm Zr3SbN, so the structural reconstruction score is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route scores best on condition severity because similar Zr–Sb phases were made at 900–1000 °C, below typical arc-melting temperatures (CS2/CS3/CS5). It still requires sealed ampoule handling because Zn and Sb are volatile, and flux removal adds steps (CS2/CS3/CS5). The atmosphere and pressure are manageable because the reference reactions use Ar-sealed alumina/silica assemblies rather than high pressure (CS2/CS3/CS5).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Zn flux should enhance Zr/Sb transport and reduce diffusion barriers, as shown by quantitative Zr–Sb phase formation in Zn-flux reactions (CS2/CS3/CS5). The limiting uncertainty is nitrogen: the flux references are Zr–Sb–P/S/Si/Zn-type compounds rather than Zr–Sb–N Cmcm phases, so ZrN dissolution and N insertion are extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are moderate rather than high despite favorable transport.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 combines high-temperature arc melting with evacuated or Ar-sealed annealing, so it accumulates penalties for high T, controlled atmosphere, volatility, and multiple steps (TS5, SS3/TS3/SS5/TS4). Its advantage is that each individual operation is standard in refractory intermetallic synthesis, as shown by Zr–Sb and Zr–P analogues (TS5, SS3, TS3). The challenge is greater than Route 1 because the annealing stage adds long-duration sealed-tube handling.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Arc melting improves homogeneity, while long annealing can enable equilibrium ordering, consistent with Zr–Sb intermetallic synthesis by arc melting followed by 720 h anneal (TS5). However, the exact nitride topology is not demonstrated by TS5, and ZrN may not fully react without sufficient melt dissolution [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, the route is useful as a fallback for ordering but is not the highest-confidence primary route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple ordered intermetallic nitride with corner- and edge-sharing NZr6 octahedra, and the isostructural analogue Zr3AlN is experimentally phase-pure by powder diffraction (CS1/SS2/TS2). There is no evidence for a large multication radius mismatch, a very large unit cell, or coexistence of vertex/edge/face-sharing networks in the target; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, so this is a structure-based inference. The amorphization risk is therefore low, with a high F4 score.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr3SbN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the supplied target hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close Zr-rich nitride/intermetallic analogues are also on the hull (Target, CS1–CS5, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support is the isostructural Cmcm Zr3AlN analogue, which was obtained by melting AlN and Zr under Ar and slow cooling (CS1/SS2/TS2). The most important enabling factor is the pre-existing Zr3MN framework chemistry: Zr–N distances, NZr6 octahedra, and Cmcm ordering are already demonstrated for Zr3AlN (CS1/SS2/TS2). The main limiting factor is compositional control of volatile Sb during high-temperature processing, which Zr–Sb analogues mitigate using excess Sb and sealed/controlled atmospheres (TS5). A secondary limiting factor is nitrogen incorporation if using flux or solid-state routes, because Zr–Sb–N flux chemistry is not directly demonstrated in the supplied evidence [Reasonable Inference].
