### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the calculated convex-hull ground state. Thermodynamic analogues such as PrSe, YS, La₂PBr₂, DyS, and LaS are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 in the TS set (TS-1 to TS-5). This strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability, although DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state reaction

Condition severity is moderate because the route uses high but not extreme temperatures, sealed Ta/silica capsules, inert handling, and volatile P/Se control. These features are directly supported by rare-earth pnictide-halide synthesis in sealed Ta capsules at 800–1170 °C (TS-3) and Y/rare-earth iodide reactions in sealed Ta tubes at 850–975 °C (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). Kinetics are only moderate because elemental Y is refractory and YSe synthesis may produce Y₂Se₃ impurities (TS-2), but the La₂PBr₂ octahedral rare-earth pnictide-halide analogue provides a useful structural precedent (TS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: molten flux growth

The flux route receives the best kinetic score because molten Ga/Pb fluxes are explicitly used to grow ternary phosphides/arsenides at ~960 °C with slow cooling (CS-3). The liquid medium should improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to a dry Y/P/Se powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is chemical transferability: CS-3 involves AE–Ga/Al–Pn phases rather than Y₂PSe, and flux inclusion or competing Ga/Pb phases may occur [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: chemical vapor transport

CVT has favorable transport kinetics because a structurally similar rare-earth selenide was made from elements by chemical transportation at 1150 → 1070 K (SS-2). However, SS-2 is binary Dy₃Se₄, so simultaneous and stoichiometric transport of Y, P, and Se is not directly demonstrated. The route is therefore plausible but sensitive to transport-agent loading and side formation of yttrium halides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: high-temperature melt/anneal

Melt/anneal routes are supported for rare-earth chalcogenides using induction melting and Ta/Mo containment at ~1700 °C (SS-4, TS-1, TS-5). This demonstrates that refractory rare-earth chalcogenide chemistry can be handled, but it is harsher than needed and risks P/Se volatilization [Reasonable Inference]. Structural ordering into Y₂PSe on cooling is uncertain because the melt evidence is mainly for binary rocksalt-type chalcogenides rather than ternary Caswellsilverite phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple cation sublattice with only Y as the cation, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization trigger is absent. The described structure contains corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, but no face-sharing coexistence, and the formula/space group imply a relatively small ordered cell [Reasonable Inference from target description]. Therefore amorphization risk is low; the larger risk is crystalline impurity formation rather than glass formation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y₂PSe is likely a true ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues in the TS set are also on the hull. The strongest analogue support comes from La₂PBr₂, which shares rare-earth–pnictogen–anion octahedral layered chemistry and is synthesized in sealed Ta/silica ampoules (TS-3), plus Y–Se binary synthesis precedent (TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed high-temperature routes that control volatile pnictogen/chalcogen components while allowing rare-earth octahedral networks to form (CS-2, TS-2, TS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control among Y–P, Y–Se, and possible Y–P–Se phases, analogous to Y₂Se₃ impurity in YSe synthesis (TS-2) and polytypism/side phases in rare-earth pnictide-halides (TS-3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require sealed containment and careful thermal/stoichiometric optimization.
