### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which receives 90/100 under the provided ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values near this range are experimentally synthesized: Na₃As at 0.0425 eV/atom and KMnAs at 0.0547 eV/atom are both reported as direct solid-state products (TS2, TS3). The main uncertainty is that none of the TS entries is Li₆SbN or a Li–Sb–N phase, so TS provides stability-range support but not exact chemical confirmation; insufficient exact target evidence from TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because chemically similar Li₈TeN₂/Li₈SeN₂ form at 1023 K, about 750 °C, in sealed Ta ampoules (CS1, CS2), and Li₃Sb phases form in the 650–800 °C range or related high-temperature inert routes (CS3, CS4). The deductions come from sealed-system need, air-sensitive Li₃N/Li₃Sb handling, and likely prior synthesis of Li₃Sb [Reasonable Inference based on CS1–CS4]. SS gives useful equipment and slow-cooling analogies but insufficient direct chemical signal for Li–Sb–N formation, so SS is neutral for exact chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 1

Li₃N is a proven reactive nitride precursor in CS1/CS2, and Li₃Sb gives a preassembled Li–Sb framework known from CS3/CS4. Diffusion is expected to be manageable because Li-rich pnictides and nitrides are synthesized at moderate high temperatures rather than requiring refractory oxide conditions (CS1–CS4). Structural reconstruction is not fully topotactic because cubic Li₃Sb is Fm-3m while target Li₆SbN is F-43m with ordered NLi₆ octahedra [target data; CS4], so a moderate kinetic penalty is retained.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from direct elemental routes demonstrated for alkali pnictides and thermodynamic analogues under Ar, including Na₃As at 700 °C and AMnX phases at 800–1100 °C (TS1, TS2, TS3). It still requires sealed containment because alkali metals are volatile/reactive and related syntheses use sealed ampoules/crucibles (CS3, CS4, TS2). The primary uncertainty is simultaneous formation of Li–Sb and Li–N ordering; CS and TS support direct alkali pnictide synthesis, but insufficient evidence from CS/TS proves direct Li₆SbN formation.

#### F3 — Route 2

Li metal and Li₃N are reactive, but Sb metal must first alloy or react before the target ternary nitride can order [Reasonable Inference]. Direct elemental synthesis is well established for Li₃Sb and Na/K pnictides (CS3, CS4, TS1–TS3), supporting reasonable diffusion. The structural reconstruction score is lower than Route 1 because the pathway begins from elements plus Li₃N rather than a preformed Li–Sb binary [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses temperatures below 1000 °C and standard sealed Ta/Ar methodology, consistent with Li-rich nitrides and alkali intermetallics (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS5). The deductions arise from volatile/reactive Li flux, multi-step flux growth/separation, and air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides direct support for excess-Li crystal growth in related Li-rich nitrides, while SS provides strong procedural support for sealed Ta slow-cooling crystal growth (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS5).

#### F3 — Route 3

Excess Li should improve kinetics by providing a liquid/reactive self-flux [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/CS2 explicitly report that single crystals of Li₈SeN₂/Li₈TeN₂ were obtained only with excess lithium. Slow cooling and sealed-container flux-like methods are also common in structurally related alkali intermetallic syntheses (SS1, SS5). The remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: excess Li may stabilize competing Li–Sb or Li–N phases, and CS/SS do not provide an exact Li₆SbN precedent.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one small cation, Li, and one heavy p-block element, Sb, with substantially different bonding distances to N/Sb environments; this triggers the ionic-size/bonding-mismatch check [target data; Reasonable Inference]. The target structural description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not met [target data]. The formula has 8 atoms, and no CS/SS/TS entry provides evidence that the target cell exceeds 40 atoms; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a large-cell amorphization penalty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆SbN is best viewed as a borderline low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom [target data]. The strongest support comes from chemically similar Li-rich nitrides synthesized from Li₃N in sealed Ta ampoules (CS1, CS2) and known Li–Sb binaries made under inert high-temperature conditions (CS3, CS4). Thermodynamic analogues near comparable E_hull values are experimentally accessible by direct solid-state reactions, which supports the energy-based feasibility assessment (TS2, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high Li chemical potential under sealed, oxygen-free conditions, as supported by excess-Li crystal growth in Li₈SeN₂/Li₈TeN₂ (CS1, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among Li₃Sb, Li₃N-derived phases, and possible Li-rich side products, because no exact Li–Sb–N analogue is provided; insufficient exact-composition evidence from CS/SS/TS.
