### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability cutoff, so a score of 90 is assigned. TS is especially supportive because synthesized thermodynamic analogues include Li2HgGe at E_hull ≈0.052 eV/atom and Na3As at ≈0.043 eV/atom (TS1–TS2). CS and SS are supportive but less directly calibrated: most CS/SS analogues are on or very near the hull, so they show the chemistry is synthetically accessible but do not prove this exact metastable ternary is stable (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Li6BiP is a true low-temperature phase or a metastable phase competing with Li3P/Li3Bi [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses standard high-temperature sealed solid-state conditions, not high pressure or >1000 °C, consistent with CS5 and TS2. The main severity deductions arise from sealed-system needs for Li/P volatility, staged processing, and air-sensitive/toxic handling; these are supported by sealed Ta/Fe/quartz/glovebox syntheses in CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, and TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Li6BiP decomposition pressure or vapor pressure, so the volatile precursor deduction is partly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental precursors are moderately active: Li can melt/react, but Bi and P still require high-temperature interdiffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with elemental reaction routes in CS1–CS5 and TS1–TS5. Diffusion should be manageable because analogous alkali-rich pnictides/intermetallics form at 650–850 °C rather than requiring refractory temperatures (CS1, CS5, TS1–TS2). Structural reconstruction is the uncertain part because no CS/SS/TS entry has the exact F-43m Li6BiP motif; related cubic alkali pnictides and Li2HgGe provide only partial support (CS5, TS1–TS2).

#### F2 — Route 2

The binary-precursor route avoids free P during the final anneal [Reasonable Inference], but it adds multiple operations: prior binary synthesis, grinding/pelletizing, and final annealing. Inert sealed handling is still needed because alkali pnictides are air/moisture sensitive in analogous systems (CS5, TS5). CS/SS/TS do not directly report Li3P + Li3Bi → Li6BiP, so the route is plausible but not directly evidenced.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary precursors should reduce the chemical distance to the target and may improve stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. However, the reaction still requires redistribution/order of P and Bi around Li in a ternary cubic phase, and no exact structural analogue is present in CS/SS/TS. The kinetics are therefore scored slightly above Route 1 but below the Li-flux route.

#### F2 — Route 3

The Li-rich self-flux route uses the most elaborate thermal program, but its temperature remains below 1000 °C, consistent with CS1 and SS1–SS2. Deductions come from sealed volatile components, multi-step heating/slow cooling/annealing, and air-sensitive Li/P handling. CS1 specifically shows that welded Ta tubes and slow cooling can produce Li-rich crystals, but also that phase purity can be challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3

A Li-rich melt should improve mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and this is strongly suggested by the successful Li-rich slow-cooling synthesis in CS1 and alkali-rich slow-cooling routes in SS1–SS2. The ion-diffusion score is high because the flux/liquid pathway bypasses slow solid-solid diffusion. The structural score is limited because the target phase is not directly represented in CS/SS/TS, and Li-rich fluxes can also stabilize competing binaries or off-stoichiometric phases [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues in CS1 and SS5].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different species, Li, Bi, and P, giving a large effective size/chemistry contrast [Reasonable Inference], so one amorphization-risk trigger is counted. The target structure is high-symmetry cubic and described with ordered BiLi12 and PLi6 polyhedra, which lowers amorphization risk relative to large complex cells. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Li6BiP, so F4 remains moderately uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li6BiP is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a clearly established ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support that Li-/alkali-rich pnictides and intermetallics can be synthesized from elements in sealed inert vessels, especially via Ta ampoules and slow cooling (CS1, CS4–CS5, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS2). The most important enabling factor is the low-temperature, highly reactive Li-rich chemistry, which can provide a liquid/flux-assisted pathway for diffusion and ordering [Reasonable Inference supported by CS1 and SS1–SS2]. The most important limiting factor is phase competition with binary Li3P and Li3Bi, plus P/Li volatility and air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference; sealed handling and phase-control issues are supported by CS1, CS5, SS5, TS5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with the best chance coming from sealed Li-rich slow-cooling experiments followed by powder XRD screening.
