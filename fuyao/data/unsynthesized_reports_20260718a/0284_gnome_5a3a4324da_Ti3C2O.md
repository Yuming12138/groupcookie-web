### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it at the predicted convex-hull ground state. Similar carbides/borocarbides in CS/SS/TS are also stable or nearly stable: Ti5Al2C3 has E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS2, TS2), Nb6C5 has E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1), TaC has E_hull = 0.0 (SS3, TS3), and chemically similar Sc/Ru/Co carbides are at 0–0.002 eV/atom (CS2–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic and phase-selectivity, not thermodynamic, because hull stability does not guarantee accessible ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for high temperature because comparable refractory carbide sintering uses 2300 K for Nb6C5 (SS1, TS1), and Ti–Al–C conversion uses 1500 °C (CS1, SS2, TS2). Vacuum/controlled atmosphere is likely needed to control oxygen activity, consistent with Nb6C5 vacuum synthesis (SS1, TS1) and Ar protection in Ti5Al2C3 synthesis (CS1, SS2, TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact Ti3C2O oxygen-potential window, so the atmosphere severity is partly inferred.

#### F3 — Route 1

The route starts from refractory Ti/TiO2/C powders, so precursor activity is modest by the rubric [Reasonable Inference]. Nb6C5 requires 20 h at 2300 K, supporting slow diffusion/ordering in carbide lattices (SS1, TS1). Structural reconstruction is large because the target ordered Ti–C–O octahedral framework must form from separate elemental/oxide powders [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The carbothermal route is supported by TaC formation at 1350 °C for 30 min under Ar with reduced oxygen partial pressure (SS3, TS3), but adapting it to Ti3C2O requires controlled partial reduction rather than full carbide formation [Reasonable Inference]. It is multi-step if sol-gel or carbon-sphere precursor preparation is used, as in TaC where hydrothermal carbon-sphere synthesis, coating, drying, and reduction are all used (SS3, TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS for carbothermal Ti oxycarbide synthesis is present, so CS is neutral for this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 2

Precursor activity is better than Route 1 because molecular/oxide–carbon mixing improved TaC formation at 1350 °C (SS3, TS3). Diffusion is still nontrivial because Ti–O–C rearrangement and reduction must occur, but nanoscale carbon/oxide contact should reduce diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is stopping at Ti3C2O rather than proceeding to TiC or leaving Ti oxide, analogous to TaC requiring oxygen control to avoid Ta2O5 (SS3, TS3).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the least severe equipment and pressure conditions because Ti5Al2C3 is made in an alumina tube furnace under flowing Ar at 1500 °C for 8 h (CS1, SS2, TS2). The route still receives high-temperature deductions because 1500 °C is above both 1000 and 1400 °C. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct oxygen insertion is present, but SS2/TS2 support the Ti–C topochemical temperature/atmosphere window.

#### F3 — Route 3

Topochemical conversion benefits from a Ti–C precursor whose Ti–C bond lengths and octahedral CTi6 motifs are close to the target’s Ti–C/Ti–O octahedral framework (CS1, SS2, TS2). Structural reconstruction is therefore less severe than elemental synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. However, Ti5Al2C3 synthesis produced a mixture of Ti5Al2C3, Ti2AlC, and Ti3AlC2, indicating phase-control and completion issues (CS1, SS2, TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only Ti as a cation, so cation-size disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference]. The described P-3m1 Caswellsilverite-like structure is a small ordered octahedral network, and the given connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing. CS/SS/TS analogues include well-crystallized octahedral carbide frameworks such as TaC and HfC (SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5), supporting low amorphization risk, though they do not directly prove Ti3C2O crystallization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti3C2O is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support from CS/SS/TS is that multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar refractory carbides are experimentally synthesized and have low hull energies, including Ti5Al2C3, Nb6C5, TaC, and HfC (CS1, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most important enabling factor is the close octahedral Ti–C framework similarity to known Ti carbides and MAX/H-phase materials, especially Ti5Al2C3 (CS1, SS2, TS2). The most important limiting factor is phase control of the oxygen sublattice, because analogues show phase mixtures or sensitivity to oxygen potential, especially Ti5Al2C3 topochemical mixtures and TaC oxygen-control requirements (CS1, SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). Overall, the material appears plausibly synthesizable, but likely requires careful control of Ti:C:O stoichiometry and oxygen chemical potential.
