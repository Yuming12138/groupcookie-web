### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest stability bracket. The closest binary analogues RuS₂ and IrS₂ are also stable on the hull in SS/TS (mp-2030, mp-2833), which reinforces that the Ir–Ru–S composition is not obviously thermodynamically implausible. CS analogues also include several E_hull = 0 compounds, but they are less chemically direct because most contain P, Th, Au, Hf, or halides (CS: mp-14249, mp-30938, mp-14444, mp-1208278, mp-28982). Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the ordered ternary Ir/Ru arrangement has a narrow stability field relative to IrS₂ + RuS₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Direct solid-state

The direct route has moderate condition severity because IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃/Rh₂S₃ analogues form in evacuated sealed tubes at ~900–960 °C without high pressure (SS/TS: mp-2833, mp-1188655, mp-17173). Deductions are driven by vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, and multi-step grinding/refiring, all supported by the IrS₂ synthesis details (SS/TS: mp-2833). Kinetics are the main weakness because Ir and Ru are refractory metals [Reasonable Inference], and IrS₂ required a 2-week hold plus possible refiring for homogeneity (SS/TS: mp-2833). CS provides general support for evacuated-ampoule sulfide synthesis but insufficient direct evidence for Ir/Ru cation ordering, so CS is neutral for the kinetic-ordering issue.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Te-flux growth

The flux route has similar condition severity to direct solid-state because it still uses sealed evacuated quartz tubes and volatile sulfur, but it avoids external high pressure and can operate at 800–1000 °C (SS/TS: mp-2030). Kinetically it is favored because RuS₂ single crystals were obtained using Te flux with slow cooling or flux evaporation, demonstrating a liquid/transport-assisted pathway for a closely related Ru sulfide (SS/TS: mp-2030). The main uncertainty is compositional selectivity: RuS₂ flux synthesis was sensitive to sulfur amount and possible RuTe₂ formation (SS/TS: mp-2030), and the target also requires a precise Ir:Ru ratio [Reasonable Inference]. CS flux evidence from Th(RuP₃)₄ supports flux growth as feasible for refractory P/Ru frameworks but is chemically less direct (CS: mp-1208278).

#### F2/F3 Route 3: CVT / halide transport

CVT is more severe because the Rh₃S₄ analogue used a high-temperature gradient near 1047 °C, evacuated ampoules, and chloride transport chemistry (TS: mp-29841). Kinetically, halide transport could help mobilize refractory metals [Reasonable Inference], and Rh₃S₄ demonstrates noble-metal sulfide transport growth (TS: mp-29841). However, the structural match is weaker than RuS₂/IrS₂, and the Rh₃S₄ source reports a narrow stability window with competing Rh₁₇S₁₅ (TS: mp-29841). CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for CVT of Ir–Ru sulfides, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: High-pressure synthesis

High-pressure synthesis is conditionally severe because pyrite-like IrS₂ required 60 kbar and 1400–1500 °C in a belt apparatus (SS: mp-1102261). Kinetically, high temperature and pressure can favor dense pyrite-like frameworks [Reasonable Inference], and this is directly supported by high-pressure IrS₂ formation (SS: mp-1102261). The limitation is that the target is already predicted at E_hull = 0, and ambient-pressure IrS₂/RuS₂ analogues exist, so high pressure may be unnecessary [Reasonable Inference]. TS gives no separate high-pressure analogue beyond the provided SS high-pressure IrS₂ entry, so TS is insufficient/neutral for high-pressure-specific scoring.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has similar Ru–S and Ir–S bond lengths, mostly 2.35–2.41 Å, which argues against large cation-size mismatch and supports a low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference from target structure; comparable distances in SS/TS: mp-2030, mp-2833]. The target connectivity is dominated by corner-sharing octahedra and S-centered tetrahedra, unlike edge/face-sharing corundum-like Ir₂S₃/Rh₂S₃ analogues (SS/TS: mp-1188655, mp-17173). Exact cell atom count is not explicitly provided in CS/SS/TS, so that check has some uncertainty and is treated neutrally/not triggered. Overall, F4 is high because there is no clear evidence for geometric frustration severe enough to favor amorphization.

The target is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and close RuS₂/IrS₂ sulfide analogues are also stable on the hull (SS/TS: mp-2030, mp-2833). The strongest support comes from SS/TS rather than CS, because SS/TS include directly relevant Ru–S and Ir–S octahedral sulfides with S–S bonding and similar M–S distances. The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to pyrite/marcasite-like RuS₂ and IrS₂ frameworks, which suggests the local bonding requirements are realistic (SS/TS: mp-2030, mp-2833). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: Ir and Ru are refractory, and achieving homogeneous Ir/Ru ordering in a sulfur-rich sealed system may require long annealing or flux-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference]. Te-flux growth is therefore the best first route because it directly addresses diffusion/crystallization while remaining within demonstrated sulfide synthesis conditions (SS/TS: mp-2030).
