### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the highest-score threshold in the provided rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies have been synthesized by arc melting/annealing, including Sm₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.053 eV/atom, Pr₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0598 eV/atom, Nd₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0621 eV/atom, and Ce₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0729 eV/atom (TS). The closest In–Co–N antiperovskite analogue InNCo₃ is more stable at 0.0225 eV/atom and experimentally accessible by ammonolysis (SS). Remaining uncertainty is whether InCo₆N is a true low-temperature phase or a metastable ordered derivative competing with InCo₃N + Co [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild thermal severity because the SS InNCo₃ analogue forms at 600–650 °C and ambient pressure, but the NH₃ atmosphere imposes a controlled/reactive-atmosphere penalty (SS). Kinetically, this route benefits from direct structural similarity: both the analogue and target contain InCo₁₂ cuboctahedra and NCo₆ octahedra, with similar Co–N distances of 1.91 Å in InNCo₃ and 1.89 Å in the target (SS; target description). The main uncertainty is the higher Co content of InCo₆N, which may yield Co metal or the simpler InCo₃N phase instead of the F-43m target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because the analogous Co-rich intermetallic routes require arc melting, remelting, long annealing near 900 °C, sealed/evacuated tubes, and subsequent nitrogenation steps (CS, SS, TS). Kinetically, parent-alloy nitrogenation is supported by Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ formation in flowing N₂ at 450–550 °C, but CS/SS also report slow nitrogen diffusion, incomplete nitrogenation, or Co precipitation risks (CS, SS). This route is chemically plausible for Co-rich nitride formation but structurally less direct than Route 1 because the target is antiperovskite-derived rather than R₂Co₁₇-derived [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 borrows the high-pressure N₂ strategy used for Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃, where 15 MPa N₂ at 250–350 °C improved nitrogen uptake after parent alloy synthesis (CS). The condition severity is high in practice because it combines alloy synthesis, powder processing, and autoclave nitrogenation, even though the pressure is below the >5 GPa rubric penalty (CS). The kinetic benefit is higher nitrogen chemical potential, but no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates high-pressure nitrogenation of In–Co antiperovskites, so transferability remains only moderate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has low thermal severity because sputtering can be performed at moderate substrate temperatures, but this is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS and is therefore based on [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic advantage would be short diffusion lengths and possible metastable trapping, which is useful for a 0.05 eV/atom nitride [Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is crystallization and stoichiometry control; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for thin-film synthesis of this family, so route confidence is lower than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally compact and cubic, and the target description does not indicate a large >40 atom unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face mixed polyhedral connectivity. Size mismatch between In and Co is significant and is counted as one amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference]. The F4 score is therefore low–medium risk rather than high risk. The main uncertainty is whether the Co-rich F-43m ordering requires long-range ordering that may be kinetically bypassed into a disordered or amorphous nitride [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

InCo₆N is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than an obviously robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the SS InNCo₃ antiperovskite analogue, which shares In–Co–N chemistry, InCo₁₂ cuboctahedra, NCo₆ octahedra, similar Co–N distances, and a successful NH₃ solid–gas synthesis route (SS). TS and CS further support that Co-rich intermetallic and nitride phases with E_hull values around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom are experimentally reachable by arc melting, annealing, and nitrogenation (CS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is the known accessibility of In–Co–N antiperovskite chemistry under NH₃ at moderate temperature (SS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target is more Co-rich than the established InCo₃N analogue and may compete with InCo₃N plus metallic Co or other Co–N/In–Co phases [Reasonable Inference].
