### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the 90–100 scoring bracket. CS supports chemical plausibility because Th₂CrN₃ and Th₂MnN₃ are stable E_hull = 0 compounds, and Hf₂N₂S analogues are 0–0.021 eV/atom (CS). SS supports the Hf–N framework through stable Fm-3m HfN and ZrN (SS). TS supports metastability tolerance because NaTa₃N₅, Nb₄N₅, and Hf₃N₄ lie in the ~0.03–0.07 eV/atom range and are experimentally accessible by specialized routes (TS).

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Solid-state nitridation

Condition severity is high because CS and SS analogues require 1200–2100 °C, but ambient N₂ avoids high-pressure penalties (CS, SS). Kinetics are the main weakness: SS HfN/Hf–N synthesis required hot pressing and 60–150 h homogenization, which indicates slow diffusion and difficult phase equilibration (SS). The HfN Fm-3m parent helps structurally, but the target requires Cr incorporation and Hf/Cr ordering, which is not demonstrated directly [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Hf₆CrN₆ competes with HfN + Cr nitrides or forms a narrow ternary phase field.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Reactive thin-film deposition

The condition score is relatively favorable because deposition can occur below the high bulk-sintering temperatures, although vacuum/plasma or NH₃/H₂ reactive atmospheres add severity (TS, [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically, thin-film deposition bypasses long-range bulk diffusion and allows cation flux control, which is advantageous for a metastable 0.05 eV/atom phase [Reasonable Inference]. TS Nb₄N₅ shows that transition-metal nitride films can be made by controlled reactive CVD, but also that phase control is sensitive to composition and nitrogen content (TS). The largest uncertainty is whether the cubic Hf₆CrN₆ phase crystallizes directly or only as amorphous/nanocrystalline Hf–Cr–N.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Topochemical/metathesis

CS Hf₂N₂S demonstrates that Hf nitride halides can react topochemically at 750 °C in sealed evacuated tubes, reducing the temperature penalty relative to bulk sintering (CS). However, the method requires prior α-HfNBr synthesis, sealed-tube handling, washing, and careful heating, all of which increase severity (CS). Kinetics may be improved by reactive halide/salt chemistry, but Cr insertion into a Hf–N framework is not shown in CS/SS/TS and is therefore uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. This route is attractive for preserving Hf–N connectivity but risky for achieving the required cubic Hf₆CrN₆ ordering.

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Nitride flux growth

CS La₃Ta₂N₆ shows that Ga or Li₃N fluxes can synthesize complex nitrides, with Li₃N acting as both flux and nitrogen source (CS). Flux conditions may improve mass transport compared with solid-state sintering, but HfN and Cr sources may have limited solubility and the target structure has no direct flux-grown analogue [Reasonable Inference]. The F2 penalty arises from air-sensitive Li₃N, sealed ampoules, and multistep handling (CS). The uncertainty is whether the flux stabilizes Hf₆CrN₆ rather than crystallizing binary HfN/CrN or dissolving Cr poorly.

#### F2/F3 Route 5: High-pressure nitridation

TS Hf₃N₄ demonstrates that Hf-rich nitrides can require pressures up to 18 GPa and temperatures up to 3000 K in laser-heated DAC synthesis (TS). This strongly supports high-pressure nitridation as a fallback route but imposes severe equipment and condition penalties (TS). Kinetics are poor for ordered ternaries because DAC heating times are short and sample volumes are small [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether high pressure actually favors Hf₆CrN₆ rather than denser Hf₃N₄-like or binary nitride assemblages.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target contains two cations with substantially different sizes, Hf and Cr, which can promote disorder or amorphization during low-temperature growth [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m conventional cell is likely large for Hf₆CrN₆, and the structure contains mixed corner/edge-sharing square pyramids, increasing crystallographic ordering requirements [Reasonable Inference from target]. However, the target description does not include face sharing, so the most severe polyhedral-connectivity trigger is not fully met. CS and SS analogues show that related nitrides can crystallize despite complex coordination, but often require high temperature or careful phase control (CS, SS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₆CrN₆ is likely a **near-hull metastable phase** rather than a clearly robust ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.05 eV/atom. CS provides strong chemical support that Cr-containing ternary nitrides can be synthesized, especially Th₂CrN₃ under N₂, while SS provides strong structural support through stable Fm-3m HfN/ZrN frameworks. TS supports the idea that nitrides in the 0.03–0.07 eV/atom metastability range can be made, but often require topochemical, CVD, or high-pressure conditions. The most critical enabling factor is the stable Hf–N framework, especially the close Hf–N bond lengths relative to HfN (SS and target). The most critical limiting factor is cation ordering/phase competition between HfN, Cr nitrides, and possible Hf–Cr intermetallic or nitride mixtures [Reasonable Inference].
