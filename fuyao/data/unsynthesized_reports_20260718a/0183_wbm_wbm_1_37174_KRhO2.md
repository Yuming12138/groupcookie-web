### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

KRhO₂ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high score of 95 under the rubric. Multiple close analogues are also calculated stable or nearly stable, including NaRhO₂ at E_hull = 0 and RbYO₂ at E_hull = 0 (CS2/SS1/TS1, CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry reports actual KRhO₂ synthesis, so computed thermodynamic stability may not fully capture K volatility or competing K–Rh–O phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

The alkali-oxide solid-state route avoids >1000 °C and GPa pressure because related KYO₂ and KCuO₂ reactions occur at 450–600 °C under controlled atmospheres (CS1, CS5). The severity penalties come from sealed handling, multistep preparation/annealing, and air-sensitive alkali oxides, all of which are described for alkali oxide systems (CS1/CS3/CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this exact K₂O + Rh₂O₃ solid-state route because the closest NaRhO₂ analogue was flux-grown rather than directly sintered (SS1/TS1).

#### F3 — Route 1

K₂O/KOₓ are reactive and can promote reaction, but Rh₂O₃ is a refractory noble-metal oxide and may slow conversion [Reasonable Inference from Rh₂O₃ precursor use in CS2/SS1/TS1]. The target has strong structural support from NaRhO₂ and RbYO₂-type Caswellsilverite analogues (CS2/SS1/TS1, CS5). The main uncertainty is phase control, since CS5 explicitly states alkali oxometallate preparation is difficult and strongly history-dependent.

#### F2 — Route 2

The peroxide/superoxide route uses moderate temperatures because KO₂/PdO reactions occur at 600–700 °C and Na₂PtO₃ is made at 500 °C (CS4, SS5/TS4). Penalties arise from sealed systems and reactive/air-sensitive alkali oxidants; KO₂ is explicitly noted as reactive and sealed-tube synthesis is used (CS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct KO₂ + Rh₂O₃ formation of KRhO₂, so oxygen activity control is an extrapolated risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Peroxide/superoxide precursors provide high chemical activity and internal oxygen buffering, as shown for K palladates, Na₂PtO₃, and Na/Ru oxides (CS4, SS5/TS4, SS3). This should help overcome Rh oxide diffusion limitations compared with purely binary oxide sintering [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that too high oxygen activity or excess K may favor other K–Rh–O stoichiometries, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct phase-map evidence for K–Rh–O.

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route avoids GPa pressure and stays below 1000 °C, but it requires sealed capsule/bomb handling, air-sensitive alkali oxides, and multistep slow-cooling processing (CS2/SS1/TS1). The 950 °C / 18 d NaRhO₂ protocol is demanding but not extreme by the rubric (CS2/SS1/TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact K-based flux composition, so K flux chemistry is inferred [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

This route receives the best kinetic score because the direct NaRhO₂ analogue was grown from alkali oxide flux and has the same Caswellsilverite R-3m RhO₆ framework (CS2/SS1/TS1). Flux transport reduces solid-state diffusion constraints, consistent with the long slow-cooling growth used for NaRhO₂ (CS2/SS1/TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether the larger K⁺ ion destabilizes the NaRhO₂-type layered ordering or introduces competing K-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

The high-pressure route is heavily penalized because NaRh₂O₄/CaRh₂O₄ analogues require 1500 °C and 6 GPa in Pt cells (SS2/SS4/TS2/TS3). Additional penalties come from multistep precursor preparation and K precursor volatility/air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS4/CS5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure is needed for KRhO₂, and the closest exact analogue NaRhO₂ does not require GPa pressure (CS2/SS1/TS1).

#### F3 — Route 4

High temperature improves reaction rates, but the use of refractory Rh oxide precursors and high-pressure equipment indicates significant kinetic barriers in related rhodates (SS2/SS4/TS2/TS3). Structurally, the high-pressure analogues contain RhO₆ octahedra but are Pnma ARh₂O₄ rather than Caswellsilverite ARhO₂, so reconstruction similarity is limited (SS2/SS4/TS2/TS3). This route may form dense competing rhodates instead of the target layered KRhO₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large A-site/B-site size mismatch, indicated by K–O = 2.67 Å versus Rh–O = 2.11 Å, and comparable size differentiation appears in NaRhO₂ with Na–O = 2.35 Å and Rh–O = 2.07 Å (CS2/SS1/TS1). However, the structure is a small-cell ordered Caswellsilverite phase and only edge/corner sharing is reported, not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing in the target. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KRhO₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest NaRhO₂ analogue is also stable and experimentally synthesized (CS2/SS1/TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is strong for the RhO₂ layered Caswellsilverite framework through NaRhO₂ and for alkali oxide chemistry through K/Rb/Cs oxometallates (CS1/CS2/CS5, SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the exact structural and chemical analogy to NaRhO₂, which demonstrates that A¹⁺Rh³⁺O₂ Caswellsilverite chemistry is experimentally accessible (CS2/SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is K volatility/moisture sensitivity and phase control in alkali oxide systems, which CS1/CS3/CS5 repeatedly identify as difficult. The best practical strategy is a sealed alkali-rich flux route adapted from NaRhO₂, with careful K/O activity control and slow cooling (CS2/SS1/TS1).
