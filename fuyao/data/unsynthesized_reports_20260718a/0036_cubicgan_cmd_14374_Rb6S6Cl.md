### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly at 0.0500 eV/atom above hull, which is at the high end of the “highly synthesizable/metastable-accessible” bin in the provided rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable or even higher E_hull are known/synthesized, including Rb₂CoS₂ at 0.0476 eV/atom, K₂CoS₂ at 0.0506 eV/atom, and Cs₂Se at 0.0751 eV/atom (TS-2, TS-3, TS-1). CS and SS provide stable Rb–S/Rb–Cl/Rb-chalcogenide examples but insufficient direct evidence for the Rb–S–Cl phase diagram, so remaining uncertainty is the identity and closeness of competing decomposition phases (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1 to SS-5).

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The low-temperature sealed-ampoule route is supported by Rb₆OCl₄ at 300 °C in Ar (CS-1), Rb₆HgS₄ at 380 °C in sealed Ar ampoules (SS-4), and Rb₂S at 623 K in evacuated quartz (TS-4). Severity deductions mainly come from the need for controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile sulfur chemistry, and air-sensitive Rb sulfide precursors (CS-1, SS-4, TS-4). Kinetics are favorable because analogous Rb salts/chalcogenides form at modest temperatures, but structural reconstruction is uncertain because no direct Rb₆S₆Cl-type analogue is present in CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct elemental reactions are supported for Rb₂S and related alkali chalcogenides/pnictides in evacuated or inert ampoules (TS-4, CS-2, CS-5, SS-1). The main severity is controlled atmosphere plus reactive/volatile Rb and S handling [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed-tube analogues in TS-4]. Kinetically, direct Rb–S formation should be rapid, but forming the ordered S–Cl-containing target from RbCl/elements requires more reconstruction than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Solvothermal/ammonothermal synthesis is plausible because Rb₂MoS₄ was crystallized in ethylenediamine at 393 K and Cs₂Se-II was made in high-pressure NH₃ at 100–300 °C (CS-4, SS-2, TS-1). Severity is higher because autoclaves, reactive solvent media, and multi-step solvent removal are involved (CS-4, SS-2, TS-1). Kinetics can be good due to solution-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference], but phase control is weak because Rb₂MoS₄ was only a minor phase and Cs₂Se-II required narrow high-pressure conditions (CS-4, SS-2, TS-1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₆S₆Cl is likely a marginally metastable phase rather than a deep ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, right at the boundary of the high-synthesizability window (target data; TS-2/TS-3 analogues show comparable metastable synthesized phases). The CS/SS/TS analogues support the feasibility of Rb-rich chloride and sulfide chemistry under sealed, dry, low-to-moderate-temperature conditions, especially Rb₆OCl₄, Rb₆HgS₄, and Rb₂S (CS-1, SS-4, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the low-temperature reactivity of Rb₂S/RbCl/S mixtures, which should reduce diffusion barriers and suppress decomposition [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, SS-4, TS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because no direct Rb–S–Cl structural analogue is provided and sulfur/chloride stoichiometry may favor binaries or polysulfides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require stringent dry handling and careful stoichiometry control.
