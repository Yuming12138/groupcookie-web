### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at 0.0000 eV/atom above hull, so the baseline thermodynamic score is high. Thermodynamic analogues also show strong support, with several 0 eV/atom fluoride phases including LiCaNiF6, BaNiF6, SrNiF6, Ba(AuF4)2, and LiSrNiF6 (TS-1 to TS-5). CS and SS are consistent overall, although the closest Cu analogue CsCuF4 is slightly metastable at ~0.048 eV/atom (SS-1), which leaves some uncertainty about the Cu(III) fluoride phase field.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-pressure oxidative fluorination

This route has severe conditions because F2 pressure and Monel autoclave equipment are required, as shown for CsCuF4 at ~360 bar F2 and 400 °C (SS-1) and for BaNiF6/SrNiF6 analogues under high-pressure fluorination (CS-2, CS-3; TS-2, TS-3). Kinetically, high fluorine chemical potential should help oxidize Cu and form Cu–F networks [Reasonable Inference from SS-1], but the target’s elongated CuF6 octahedra differ from CsCuF4’s square-planar Cu coordination (SS-1; target description). The main uncertainty is whether BaCu2F8 forms directly or competes with BaF2, CuF2, and simpler Cu(III) fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct solid-state fluorination under flowing F2

This route is less severe than autoclave fluorination because CS-5 demonstrates F2-flow synthesis at 450–750 °C without high pressure. It is supported by the same A–M2–F8 stoichiometry class in TbCd2F8 and related compounds (CS-5), but it lacks direct Cu(III) evidence. The kinetic score is moderate because repeated heating under F2 can overcome diffusion barriers (CS-5), while Cu oxidation may still require higher F2 chemical potential than ambient flow, as implied by CsCuF4 synthesis under high-pressure F2 (SS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sealed-tube fluoride solid-state / melt-assisted reaction

Sealed-tube fluoride reactions are well supported for complex fluorides, including Ba2Ni7F18 from BaF2/NiF2 in a sealed Ni tube at 850–1000 °C (CS-1) and RbIn3F10 from fluoride melt/slow cooling in sealed Pt tube (CS-4). This route avoids external high-pressure F2 but requires an internal oxidizer to reach the Cu oxidation state required by BaCu2F8 [Reasonable Inference from target charge balance and SS-1]. The uncertainty is high because CS/SS/TS do not show a sealed-tube synthesis of a Ba–Cu(III) fluoride without gaseous F2.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: F2-assisted crystal growth / transport or flux annealing

Ba(AuF4)2 provides a strong stoichiometric analogue for BaM2F8 and was prepared using F2/N2 fluorination plus sealed Au-tube annealing/tempering (SS-5; TS-4). This route may improve crystallinity and ordering through long anneals and slow cooling [Reasonable Inference from SS-5/TS-4], but it is operationally complex and slow. The main uncertainty is transferability from Au(III) square-planar chemistry to Cu fluoride octahedral chemistry, since the target has corner-sharing CuF6 octahedra rather than isolated AuF4 units (SS-5; target description).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Ba/Cu size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target Ba 10-coordinate and Cu octahedral coordination]. The target structure description does not indicate a very large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing CuF6 network, so only one F4 trigger is applied. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization tendency, so the score is based mainly on target structural features and analogous crystalline fluorides.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaCu2F8 is likely a thermodynamic or near-ground-state phase because the reported hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many thermodynamically similar fluorides are also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The analogues support synthesis of complex Ba/transition-metal fluorides, but they also show that high-valent fluoride chemistry often requires F2, sealed vessels, or high-pressure fluorination (SS-1, CS-2, CS-3, TS-2, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of strongly oxidizing fluorination conditions to stabilize Cu above +2 [Reasonable Inference from SS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining phase purity and stoichiometry while avoiding decomposition, hydrolysis, or competing BaF2/CuF2 phases, as moisture sensitivity and difficult phase control are reported for related high-valent fluorides (SS-1, CS-3). Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible but likely requires specialized fluorine chemistry rather than conventional inert-atmosphere solid-state synthesis.
