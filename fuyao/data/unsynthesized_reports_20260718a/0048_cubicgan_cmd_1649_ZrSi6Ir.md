### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-score threshold, so it is plausibly synthesizable but likely close to a metastability boundary. Thermodynamic analogues CeSi5, Zr2CuSi4, and Hf2CuSi4 have comparable E_hull values around 0.058–0.066 eV/atom and are associated with either high-pressure or controlled high-temperature synthesis (TS). The main uncertainty is whether the computed cubic ZrSi6Ir phase lies on a locally accessible ternary pathway or decomposes into stable Zr–Si and Ir–Si binaries [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, arc melting plus long anneal.**  
This route is strongly supported by SS REIrSi3 analogues prepared by arc melting in purified Ar and annealing at 950 °C for 8 days, and by CS/SS BaRh2Si9 requiring repeated arc melting and eight-week annealing. The severity is moderate because it avoids high pressure but uses very high local arc temperatures and multiple processing steps. Kinetics are limiting because refractory Ir/Zr diffusion and ternary ordering are slow [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long anneals in CS/SS.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, induction/solid-state sintering.**  
CS/SS IrSi3 and TS OsSi2 demonstrate that platinum-group silicides can form by induction heating at 1340–1400 °C under He with short equilibration holds. The route is less time-consuming than long annealing, but Si evaporation control is a known issue, explicitly addressed by He flushing in TS. Kinetically, partial fusion improves diffusion, but forming a specific ternary cubic Zr–Ir–Si phase is more complex than forming binary IrSi3/OsSi2 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, high-pressure synthesis.**  
High-pressure CeSi5, LaSi5, and YSi3 analogues show that Si-rich silicide frameworks can be stabilized at 10–15 GPa and retained by quenching (CS, TS). This route has the harshest F2 penalty because of high pressure and specialized multi-anvil equipment. Its F3 is comparatively better because binary silicide + Si precursors partially preorganize the silicide network, as used in CS/TS high-pressure analogues.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, sealed Ta-tube pre-alloy anneal.**  
CS MgIr3Si7 provides the closest practical precedent for Ir–Si-containing ternary silicide growth in sealed Ta under Ar with slow cooling. Pre-alloying Ir–Si and Zr–Si should reduce diffusion mismatch relative to elemental arc melting [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining kinetic uncertainty is whether the cubic ZrSi6Ir phase nucleates before competing ZrSi2, IrSi3, elemental Si, or other ternary silicides dominate [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one inferred amorphization trigger from the Zr/Ir metallic size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description includes corner- and face-sharing polyhedra but not explicit edge-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully met. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior, so this score is based mainly on structural interpretation and remains moderately uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrSi6Ir is most likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a robust ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS evidence supports synthesis of related Si-rich and Ir-containing silicides by several routes: ambient-pressure arc/anneal methods for REIrSi3 and BaRh2Si9, high-temperature induction for Ir/Os silicides, sealed Ta-tube synthesis for MgIr3Si7, and high-pressure stabilization for Si-rich MSi5/MSi3 phases. The most critical enabling factor is use of pre-alloyed silicide precursors or partial melting to overcome refractory Ir/Zr diffusion barriers. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition in a Si-rich ternary system, especially loss of Si and formation of binary Zr–Si or Ir–Si phases. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful stoichiometry control, repeated annealing, and phase screening.
