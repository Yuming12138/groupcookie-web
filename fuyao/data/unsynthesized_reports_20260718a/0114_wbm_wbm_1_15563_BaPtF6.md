### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
BaPtF6 is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target thermodynamics. Several close analogues are also on the hull, including PtPbF6, BaCrF6, CaPdF6, CdPtF6, RbBiF6, and MgPdF6 (CS2, SS1, SS3, SS4, SS5, TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5). The uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for high-valent Pt fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized heavily because PtPbF6 and BaCrF6 analogues require F2 at hundreds of atmospheres in specialized autoclaves (SS3, SS5, TS2, TS4). The formal scoring rubric does not penalize sub-GPa gas pressure under the “>5 GPa” line, but the F2 atmosphere, multi-step fluorination, and toxic/reactive precursors justify deductions (SS3, TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS specifically supports high-pressure BaPtF6 synthesis, so CS is neutral for this route factor.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The kinetic score is relatively favorable because gas-phase F2 strongly activates fluorination, and close R-3m analogues exist for both PtF6 and BaF12 frameworks (SS3, SS5, TS2, TS4). PtPbF6 is particularly compelling because its Pt–F bond length and R-3m connectivity closely match the target (SS3, TS2). Remaining uncertainty is Ba/Pt phase competition and whether BaPtF6 forms before binary BaF2/PtFx phases are trapped [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 avoids high-pressure equipment and uses only moderate temperatures, consistent with SrPbF6, RbBiF6, CaPdF6, and MgPdF6 F2-stream syntheses (CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1, TS5). It still requires toxic/corrosive F2 and controlled cooling/tempering in F2, so it remains challenging (SS1, TS1, TS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS that ambient F2 reliably stabilizes BaPtF6 specifically is a key uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Direct F2-stream fluorination of intimate mixtures is kinetically effective for Pd, Bi, and Pb fluoride analogues (CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1, TS5). However, the closest Pt R-3m analogue PtPbF6 is tied to high-pressure F2, suggesting ambient pressure may under-oxidize Pt or produce competing Pt fluorides [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 uses sealed noble-metal tubes and high-temperature annealing, as seen for Pt fluoride and fluoroaurate analogues (SS4, TS3, CS3). The route avoids continuous high-pressure F2 during the main anneal but requires pre-fluorinated Pt species and sealed handling, so volatile/air-sensitive and multi-step deductions apply (SS4, TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates BaPtF6 formation by binary fluoride annealing, so confidence is limited.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
BaF2 plus metal fluoride solid-state chemistry is documented for BaTbF6 and Er2BaF8 (CS4, CS5), and sealed Pt tube annealing can crystallize Pt fluorides (SS4, TS3). However, weeks-long Pt fluoride anneals and slow cooling imply sluggish cation ordering and possible reconstruction barriers (SS4, TS3). The route is more suitable as a crystallinity-improvement step after fluorination than as the primary synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains very different cation sizes and coordinations, BaF12 cuboctahedra and PtF6 octahedra, which triggers the ionic-size mismatch check [Reasonable Inference from target coordination and SS5/TS4 analogues]. Mixed corner/edge/face connectivity is explicitly present in the target and in PtPbF6/BaCrF6 analogues, triggering the connectivity check (SS3, SS5, TS2, TS4). The compact R-3m AF6 topology likely avoids a >40-atom-cell trigger, but exact cell size is not directly provided by CS/SS/TS, so uncertainty remains.
