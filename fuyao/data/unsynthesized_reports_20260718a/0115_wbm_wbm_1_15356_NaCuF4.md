### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility under some conditions (target data). Several close fluorides in the reference set are also on or near the hull, including NaCuF3, LiCoF4, LiAuF4, and Cu oxyfluoride analogues (CS, SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is that 0 K hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or air/moisture stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, because CsCuF4 and LiCoF4 analogues form at ~400 °C (SS; CS/SS/TS). The severity penalty is dominated by high-pressure F2, specialized autoclave equipment, and toxic/reactive fluorine handling, all directly supported by the CsCuF4 and LiCoF4 syntheses (SS; CS/SS/TS). Although pressure is high in a chemical sense, it is far below the >5 GPa penalty threshold used in the rubric (SS; CS/SS/TS). The uncertainty is whether NaCuF4 needs the same or higher F2 pressure than CsCuF4 because Na is smaller than Cs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary fluoride precursors are proven for NaCuF3 formation but are less reactive than solution precursors (CS). The isostructural CsCuF4 synthesis suggests that the CuF4 square-planar network can crystallize under fluorinating conditions at modest temperature (SS). Kinetic risk remains because NaCuF3 is the chemically nearest known Na–Cu–F phase and may compete if fluorination is incomplete (CS; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The sealed-ampoule route avoids compressed external F2 and GPa pressures, but still requires vacuum/sealed conditions and likely strong internal fluorinating agents [Reasonable Inference based on CS/SS]. NaCuF3 sealed-ampoule synthesis at 650 °C in Pt is directly supported (CS). The route remains hazardous because the target stoichiometry requires more oxidative fluorination than NaCuF3, which is inferred from the NaCuF4 formula and supported indirectly by F2 requirements for CsCuF4/LiCoF4 (SS; CS/SS/TS).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

NaF/CuF2 mixtures react successfully to NaCuF3 under sealed ampoule conditions (CS), indicating manageable Na–Cu–F interdiffusion. However, no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates formation of NaCuF4 by a low-pressure sealed route, so the structural reconstruction and oxidation steps are uncertain. The most likely kinetic problem is trapping NaCuF3 or CuF2-rich intermediate phases rather than crystallizing I4/mcm NaCuF4 [Reasonable Inference based on CS].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The photochemical route operates at ambient temperature for ACrF6 analogues, avoiding high thermal severity (TS). Its severity instead comes from anhydrous HF, elemental F2, UV irradiation, sealed fluoropolymer vessels, and long reaction times, all described for the Cr fluoride route (TS). The uncertainty is that no CS/SS evidence confirms analogous Cu fluorination or crystallization under these conditions.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

HF/F2 photochemistry provides high precursor activity and avoids bulk diffusion, as ACrF6 compounds form from AF/CrF3 in anhydrous HF under UV at ambient temperature (TS). This could help access a high-valent Cu fluoride at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic weakness is crystallization selectivity: TS reports instability/polymorphism issues for related high-fluoride systems, and no direct Cu analogue is given (TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple I4/mcm structure with one Na site, one Cu site, and one F site, which lowers amorphization risk relative to large, topologically complex structures (target data). The isostructural CsCuF4 analogue also has a simple site topology and no reported mixed edge/face-sharing polyhedral network (SS). The main amorphization risk is the large size/coordination mismatch between 8-coordinate Na and square-planar Cu [Reasonable Inference based on target structure].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaCuF4 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest support comes from the exact structural analogue CsCuF4, which has the same I4/mcm square-planar CuF4 motif and is experimentally synthesized by high-pressure fluorination (SS). Chemical support is also strong because NaCuF3 is experimentally accessible from NaF/CuF2, confirming the Na–Cu–F chemical system is synthetically viable (CS). The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, needed to stabilize the more fluorinated NaCuF4 composition and likely Cu(III) environment [Reasonable Inference supported by SS and CS/SS/TS F2 syntheses]. The most critical limiting factor is handling and phase control under reactive F2/HF conditions, because close analogues are moisture-sensitive or require specialized autoclaves and show impurity/instability risks (SS, TS).
