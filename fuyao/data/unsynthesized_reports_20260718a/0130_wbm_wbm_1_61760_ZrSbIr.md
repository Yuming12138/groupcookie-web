### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the top of the prescribed thermodynamic-stability scoring range. SS/TS analogues include several E_hull = 0.0 materials, including ZrIrSn, HfIrSn, ZrIrSi, HfIrGe, and ThRhSb, which supports thermodynamic plausibility (SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). CS evidence is mixed because HfRhSb is experimentally synthesized but listed at E_hull = 0.484 eV/atom, so CS is treated as chemically supportive but thermodynamically less clean (CS-1).

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting plus annealing requires specialized high-temperature equipment, purified Ar, and vacuum annealing, all directly reported for analogous half-Heuslers and ZrIrSn-type samples (CS-1, SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5). The main severity penalties come from the very high local temperature required to melt Zr/Ir [Reasonable Inference], controlled atmosphere, multi-step processing, and Sb volatility/toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because the cited half-Heusler Sb analogues involve Hf/Th–Rh–Sb rather than exactly Zr–Ir–Sb (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are helped by liquid-state mixing during arc melting and by direct same-structure analogues HfRhSb and ThRhSb (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). Kinetics are penalized because Zr and Ir are refractory and because analogues require weeks-long annealing for ordering/homogeneity (SS-1, TS-1, CS-1). The main uncertainty is whether ZrIrSb adopts the cubic half-Heusler form cleanly rather than a competing hexagonal/orthorhombic ZrIrX variant, since ZrIrSn and HfIrSn analogues in SS/TS are hexagonal rather than cubic (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Solid-state sintering uses lower nominal temperatures than arc melting but still needs sealed Ar/vacuum conditions and multiple grinding/annealing steps, as reported for related Zr/transition-metal/pnictide phases (CS-5). Toxic/volatile Sb and sealed ampoule handling add severity [Reasonable Inference]. Evidence from SS-2/TS-2 gives only minimal synthesis details, so those sources are neutral for exact severity and scored as insufficiently detailed.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Dense Zr/Ir diffusion in a pellet is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], and CS-5 supports this concern by using master alloying, grinding, pelletizing, and week-long sintering. SS-2/TS-2 report elemental solid-phase reactions for HfIrSn-type phases but do not specify temperatures or times, so their kinetic signal is incomplete. The route is feasible but less attractive than arc melting because the target ordering must emerge through solid-state diffusion rather than melt homogenization.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth avoids full melting of refractory Zr/Ir [Reasonable Inference], but it requires sealed evacuated capsules, slow cooling, and flux removal, as shown for related pnictides (CS-2, CS-3). The exact temperatures are not provided in CS-2/CS-3, so the high-temperature penalty is based on reasonable extrapolation rather than direct evidence. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for a flux-grown half-Heusler ZrIrSb-type phase, so transferability is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux transport can improve kinetics relative to dry sintering [Reasonable Inference], and chemically similar pnictides were grown from flux (CS-2, CS-3). However, no provided structurally similar half-Heusler antimonide is demonstrated by flux growth, and flux chemistry may stabilize competing phases or incorporate flux atoms [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate rather than high.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a small-cell ordered half-Heusler, and same-structure crystalline analogues are reported (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). The C2 atom-count trigger is not met, and the target description does not satisfy the strict mixed vertex + edge + face connectivity trigger. There is insufficient direct amorphization evidence from CS/SS/TS, but the repeated success of crystalline intermetallic analogues supports a low amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrIrSb is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from half-Heusler antimonide analogues HfRhSb and ThRhSb, both prepared by arc melting followed by long annealing at 800 °C (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Zr–Ir–Sn and Hf–Ir–Sn phases also show that Zr/Hf–Ir–p-block equiatomic intermetallics are experimentally accessible, though some adopt hexagonal rather than cubic structures (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The key enabling factor is the combination of zero hull energy and a directly precedented half-Heusler antimonide synthesis route. The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control during high-temperature processing, especially because Sb can be lost and Zr/Ir ordering may require long annealing [Reasonable Inference].
