### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 bin in the provided rubric. Synthesized analogues include on-hull Na5OsO6 and Na4IrO4 (CS-1, CS-4), slightly metastable Na6PbO5 at ~0.034 eV/atom (CS-3, TS-5), and thermodynamic-neighbor phases up to ~0.086 eV/atom that are reported synthesized (TS-1, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that a 0.05 eV/atom value does not guarantee the cubic polymorph specifically; it only supports energetic accessibility of the composition/structure relative to competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. CS, SS, and TS all provide useful positive evidence for this factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate-to-severe conditions because it uses dry O2, Na2O2, and controlled high-temperature handling, exactly as in the Na5OsO6 synthesis (CS-1). Kinetically, Na2O2/O2 chemistry is favorable because it can oxidize and transport alkali species, but Ir-containing reagents are refractory and may react slowly [Reasonable Inference; CS-1]. This route is scored best because Na5OsO6 is the closest high-valent alkali-rich 5d oxide analogue and contains MO6 octahedra (CS-1). The main uncertainty is whether Ir can be stabilized in the required oxidation state and cubic Na6IrO6 topology rather than forming Na4IrO4 or IrO2 [Reasonable Inference; CS-4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is directly supported by Na4IrO4, which is an actual Na–Ir–O phase synthesized by exchange reaction using Li2IrO3 and Na2O at 800 °C for 71 days (CS-4, SS-1). Its F2 penalty comes from Ar-sealed Ag/quartz hardware, long processing, air-sensitive Na2O, and precursor preparation (CS-4, SS-1). The F3 score is lower because Na4IrO4 has square-planar IrO4 rather than octahedral IrO6, meaning the target likely requires additional oxygen uptake and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from target data and CS-4]. SS provides strong structural/chemical relevance through Na4IrO4, while TS provides insufficient route-specific evidence for this exact exchange chemistry and is therefore treated as neutral for this route-specific judgment.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Flux growth is supported by Na5TaO5 and Li8PrO6-related alkali-rich oxides, which used sealed metal capsules, alkali oxide/peroxide-rich conditions, and long anneals (CS-5, TS-4). The route may improve crystallinity and mass transport, but the long 75-day precedent indicates slow kinetics and demanding phase control (CS-5). Its limitation is indirect chemical transferability: Ta and rare-earth analogues do not prove Ir oxidation-state stability, so Ir-specific success is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide useful flux evidence, while SS provides insufficient direct flux-specific evidence for Na6IrO6 and is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

High-oxygen-activity sealed synthesis is a targeted extrapolation from Na5OsO6 peroxide/O2 chemistry and Na-rich oxide formation from oxidizing precursors such as PbO2/Na2O (CS-1, CS-3, TS-5). It is kinetically attractive because reactive peroxide and oxidizing conditions can lower formation temperatures, but it adds pressure/compatibility risks in sealed vessels [Reasonable Inference]. This route specifically addresses the suspected need for high-valent octahedral Ir, which Ar exchange conditions may not provide [Reasonable Inference from target charge balance and CS-4]. CS and TS provide useful chemical precedent; SS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-oxygen sealed synthesis and is neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner- and edge-sharing ONa4Ir square-pyramidal units, which directly triggers the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion from the target structural description. A large Na/Ir cation-size mismatch and likely >40 atoms in the conventional Fm-3m cell add two more triggers [Reasonable Inference]. Several analogues require long annealing, sealed containers, or careful phase control, consistent with crystallization challenges in alkali-rich oxides (CS-4, CS-5, SS-4, TS-4). The uncertainty is that high symmetry Fm-3m could also simplify ordering if the phase is truly stable [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na6IrO6 is likely a borderline metastable but accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, and synthesized analogues exist at comparable or larger metastabilities (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The strongest support comes from Na5OsO6, which shows that alkali-rich high-valent 5d MO6 oxides can be made by Na2O2/O2 solid-state chemistry (CS-1). Direct Na–Ir–O precedent also exists through Na4IrO4, but that phase has square-planar IrO4 and required a very long exchange reaction, so it supports Na–Ir–O chemistry more than the exact target topology (CS-4, SS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen/alkali activity from Na2O2 or related oxidizing fluxes, which can stabilize high-valent 5d oxides (CS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing Na–Ir–O phases, IrO2 persistence, Na loss, and moisture sensitivity are all plausible issues based on the analogue behavior (CS-1, CS-4, SS-4). Overall, the target is plausible but not routine, with a best estimated synthesizability of about **64/100**.
