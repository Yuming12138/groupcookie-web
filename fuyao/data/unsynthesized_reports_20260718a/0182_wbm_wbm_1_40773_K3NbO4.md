### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported with **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, which is the strongest positive synthesizability signal in the dataset. CS/SS/TS analogues are also mostly on or near the hull: K2ZrO3 is 0.0 eV/atom (CS2/SS4/TS4), K6Ti2O7 is ~0–0.0003 eV/atom (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2), and K3VO4/K3CrO4 are near-hull/stable entries (CS1/CS3/SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that a 0 K convex-hull prediction does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or moisture stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the closest K3XO4 analogues used K2O, ultra-dry glovebox grinding, sealed noble-metal tubes, and high-temperature treatments up to 1100 °C (CS1/CS3). These conditions are feasible but operationally demanding because K2O handling and sealed-tube alkali oxide chemistry require moisture exclusion (CS1/CS3). Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact K3NbO4 route means TS is neutral for the operational details beyond analogous long-duration oxide syntheses.

#### F3 — Route 1

The exact structure match to K3VO4/K3CrO4 strongly helps structural reconstruction (CS1/CS3/SS5). However, replacing V/Cr with Nb introduces a refractory Nb oxide precursor and likely slower diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and structurally similar alkali titanates/rubidates required 25–51 d anneals in several cases (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). Thus kinetics are the major weakness of the dry solid-state route.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from avoiding direct K2O starting material: K2ZrO3 was made using KNO3 decomposition to form excess K2O in situ at 1000 °C under dry N2 (CS2/SS4/TS4). The route does not require high pressure, H2/NH3/vacuum, or >1000 °C conditions based on the reference. The main severity concern is K-rich flux volatility/corrosivity at high temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The nitrate-derived flux is kinetically favorable because it creates a reactive K2O-rich liquid/flux, and ZrO2 dissolution in that medium produced K2ZrO3 single crystals (CS2/SS4/TS4). Structural transfer is not exact because K2ZrO3 is not I-42m K3XO4 (CS2/SS4/TS4), but the chemical role—dissolving a refractory high-valent oxide in a K-rich medium—is relevant [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains in whether Nb2O5 dissolves and crystallizes as K3NbO4 rather than competing potassium niobates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The KOH flux route is relatively low-temperature, since K2TiO3 crystals were grown at 600 °C from KOH melt (CS5). It still requires chemically resistant vessels such as silver crucibles/tubes and careful flux handling (CS5). CS5 gives insufficient evidence for the exact atmosphere needed, so dry inert/sealed conditions are an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

KOH melt should improve precursor activity and mass transport relative to dry oxides (CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). However, the structural relationship between K2TiO3 and K3NbO4 is weaker than for K3VO4/K3CrO4, so structural reconstruction is only moderately supported (CS5 vs CS1/CS3). The largest uncertainty is whether the very basic hydroxide melt stabilizes K3NbO4 rather than hydrated/hydroxylated or lower-K niobates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

The carbonate route uses easier precursors, and K4V2O7 was prepared from K2CO3/V2O5 at 800 °C followed by 700 °C dynamic vacuum annealing (CS4). The dynamic vacuum condition triggers a severity penalty, and K-rich compositions may require compensation for volatile potassium species [Reasonable Inference]. The route is operationally simpler than direct K2O sealed-tube synthesis but less directly supported for K3XO4.

#### F3 — Route 4

K2CO3 decomposition can provide a reactive K source, and CS4 supports carbonate use in potassium vanadate synthesis. However, Nb2O5 is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and the target K3NbO4 is more K-rich and structurally closer to K3VO4/K3CrO4 routes that used K2O rather than carbonate (CS1/CS3). Competing potassium niobates are a plausible kinetic/phase-purity risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple crystalline alkali niobate framework with only corner-sharing polyhedra in the provided structure description, which lowers amorphization risk. The large K+/Nb5+ size contrast triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference], but the structure does not show mixed edge/face/vertex sharing in the target description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of K3NbO4, so the score is based primarily on the target structure and general radius reasoning.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K3NbO4 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from K3VO4 and K3CrO4, which share the K3XO4 stoichiometry and tetragonal I-42m structure type and were experimentally synthesized by K2O-based solid-state methods (CS1, CS3, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong K3XO4 structural analogy combined with thermodynamic stability. The most critical limiting factor is practical phase control in a very K-rich oxide system, where K volatility, moisture sensitivity, and slow reaction of Nb2O5 can lead to competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. A KNO3-derived K2O flux is recommended first because it balances high K activity with easier precursor handling than direct K2O sealed-tube synthesis (CS2/SS4/TS4).
