### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it directly on the calculated convex hull and gives the maximum thermodynamic score. Chemically similar fluorides such as K2NiF6 and RbFeF4 are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and KFeF4 is very close to the hull at 0.0025 eV/atom (CS1, CS2, CS5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar RbAuF4 is also on the hull and shares the I4/mcm square-planar MF4 topology (SS4, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is mainly chemical: Cu(III) fluoride phases can require high fluorine potential even if thermodynamically stable [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS3].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity score because it uses 350–360 bar F2 at ~400 °C, as reported for isostructural CsCuF4 (SS1). The temperature itself is not severe, but high-pressure fluorine requires specialized Monel/autoclave hardware and strict safety controls (SS1). The route also likely requires precursor preparation and dry protected workup because CsCuF4 decomposes quickly in air/moisture (SS1). Insufficient evidence from CS for the exact Cu(III) KCuF4 pressure requirement, so CS is neutral for that specific pressure question.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable relative to ceramic solid-state routes because fluorination of a chloride precursor can proceed by reactive gas–solid halide exchange rather than slow cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and CS1]. Structural reconstruction is minimal because CsCuF4 is isostructural with the target KCuF4, including I4/mcm symmetry and square-planar CuF4 units (SS1). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the smaller K analogue forms directly or passes through competing K–Cu fluoride phases [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS3].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar chemical hazards to Route 1 because F2 is still required, but it may avoid very high F2 pressure if the Au-fluoride-style staged fluorination is transferable [Reasonable Inference from SS4/TS1]. RbAuF4 and KAuF4-type preparations used 300–400 °C F2 treatments, with initial fluorination at 100 °C being advantageous (SS4, TS1). However, Cu(III) may require a higher fluorine chemical potential than Au(III) analogues, which limits confidence [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS4/TS1]. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Cu-containing KCuF4 synthesis at ambient pressure, so TS is partially neutral for Cu-specific feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetic feasibility is supported by demonstrated chloride-to-fluoride conversion in K2NiF6 and RbAuF4/KAuF4 analogues (CS1, SS4, TS1). The staged low-temperature fluorination could improve precursor activity and avoid passivation [Reasonable Inference from SS4/TS1]. Structural similarity is good for the square-planar MF4 motif, but weaker than Route 1 because the best direct topology analogue with Cu is CsCuF4, not the Au compounds (SS1 versus SS4/TS1). The uncertainty is whether moderate-pressure F2 is sufficient to stabilize Cu(III) in KCuF4 [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS3].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is conditionally severe because it uses F2/Ar high-pressure autoclave conditions and requires CuF3, which itself is made using F2 chemistry (SS3). The analogous Cs2CuF6 preparation required ~350 bar, 400 °C, and extended annealing over weeks (SS3). Although the temperature is modest, the high-pressure F2 environment and precursor synthesis burden reduce the condition score (SS3). Insufficient evidence from CS for CuF3-based KCuF4 formation, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

The use of CuF3 improves chemical activity because Cu is already in a high oxidation state suitable for KCuF4 [Reasonable Inference from SS3]. However, the closest CuF3-based analogue, Cs2CuF6, contains octahedral CuF6 rather than square-planar CuF4, so structural reconstruction is significant (SS3). The reported difficulty of obtaining pure Cs2CuF6 and the 5-week anneal indicate slower kinetics and a narrow phase window (SS3). This makes Route 3 less attractive than chloride fluorination despite its chemically appropriate Cu(III) precursor.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ABF4-type framework with one K site, one Cu site, and one F site in the provided description, which lowers amorphization risk relative to complex multi-site fluorides. The strong size contrast between K+ and Cu3+ triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from standard ionic-size chemistry], but the same simple I4/mcm topology is experimentally realized in CsCuF4 and RbAuF4 analogues (SS1, SS4/TS1). There is insufficient evidence from CS for amorphization behavior of KCuF4 specifically, so CS is neutral for F4. Overall risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KCuF4 is likely a ground-state phase because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the isostructural CsCuF4 analogue, which has the same I4/mcm framework and square-planar CuF4 units and was synthesized by high-pressure fluorination (SS1). Additional support comes from RbAuF4/KAuF4-type square-planar MF4 fluorides made by F2 fluorination and from chemically similar stable K-containing fluorides (SS4, TS1, CS1). The most critical enabling factor is access to sufficiently high fluorine chemical potential to oxidize and stabilize Cu(III) in a fluoride lattice [Reasonable Inference from SS1 and SS3]. The most critical limiting factor is safe and precise control of F2 pressure, moisture exclusion, and phase purity, since closely related Cu and Au fluorides are sensitive and can require specialized autoclave methods (SS1, SS3, SS4/TS1).
