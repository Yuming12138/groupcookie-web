### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. Close thermodynamic analogues FeCl2, FeBr2, CsFeCl3, and Pd(PbCl3)2 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1; TS2; TS3; TS4), supporting a high F1 score. CS3 gives insufficient useful signal because it is an oxychloride/fluorination case with E_hull = 0.239 eV/atom, neutral = 50 for target relevance. The main uncertainty is whether the computed ground state is experimentally accessible before competing binary chlorides crystallize [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed-ampoule route is supported by Pd(PbCl3)2 synthesis from PbX2 and PdX2 in evacuated quartz ampoules with melting, quenching, and 300 °C annealing (CS2; SS5; TS4). Its severity is moderate because it needs vacuum sealing, volatile chloride management, and toxic Pb/air-sensitive Fe(II) handling [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, unlike no analogue requiring extreme synthesis; high-pressure CS3 is not considered transferable and is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary halide precursors are more reactive than refractory oxides, and the Pd/Pb chloride analogue forms from simple halides (CS2; SS5; TS4). FeCl6 octahedra are strongly supported by FeCl2 with identical Fe–Cl distance (SS1; TS1). The kinetic penalty comes from ordering Fe and Pb into the target beta-VN-derived framework, which is not directly demonstrated in the reference set [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Bridgman growth is directly supported for FeCl2 (SS1; TS1), and FeBr2 crystallization under Ar at 773 K supports volatile halide crystal-growth feasibility (SS2; TS2). The route requires controlled atmosphere and sealed operation but does not require high pressure or very high temperature. Toxic PbCl2 and moisture-sensitive FeCl2 remain the main condition penalties [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Melt or near-melt growth gives high precursor activity and bypasses slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with FeCl2 Bridgman growth (SS1; TS1). The target’s Fe–Cl bond length exactly matches FeCl2, improving structural transferability (SS1; TS1). Remaining uncertainty is Pb/Fe cation ordering and whether the target melts congruently or forms competing FeCl2/PbCl2 phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Molten-salt synthesis is supported for KFeCl3 (SS3), and controlled sealed high-temperature synthesis is supported for CsFeCl3 at 750 °C (SS4; TS3). This route has moderate severity because it requires dry flux, inert/sealed conditions, and post-reaction flux handling [Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is applied because the cited Fe chloride analogues form at or below 750 °C (SS4; TS3).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux conditions should enhance ion mobility and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with KFeCl3 molten-salt preparation (SS3). Fe–Cl framework chemistry is well supported by FeCl2, KFeCl3, and CsFeCl3 analogues (SS1; SS3; SS4; TS1; TS3). The main kinetic uncertainty is that alkali chloride fluxes may stabilize alkali ferrichloride/chloroferrate byproducts rather than Fe2PbCl6 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Solution crystallization is mild in temperature, supported by Rb2TeCl6 crystallization from aqueous HCl (TS5) and HgGaCl4 solution crystallization under dry N2 (CS4). Severity is increased by oxygen exclusion, HCl volatility/corrosiveness, Pb toxicity, and Fe(II) oxidation risk [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 provides only weak support because its composition and precursors do not match Fe/Pb chlorides; insufficient target-specific evidence from CS1, neutral = 50.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Solution routes offer excellent ion mobility, as implied by successful chloride crystallization from solution in TS5 and CS4. However, no provided analogue shows solution growth of an extended Fe–Pb chloride octahedral framework, so structural reconstruction confidence is low. The most likely solution failure modes are hydrated Fe/Pb chloride formation, Fe(II) oxidation, or precipitation of PbCl2 rather than the desired framework [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The large Fe–Cl versus Pb–Cl bond-length contrast in the target indicates a large cation-size mismatch, triggering C1. The target description reports corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The low formula complexity, Fe2PbCl6 with 9 atoms per formula unit, gives no evidence for a >40 atom cell; uncertainty remains if the crystallographic cell contains multiple formula units, but the given P-31m description appears simple [Reasonable Inference].
