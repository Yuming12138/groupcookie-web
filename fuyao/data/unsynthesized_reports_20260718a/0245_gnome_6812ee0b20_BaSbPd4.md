The remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of nearby competing Ba–Pd–Sb phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate condition-severity score because SrPd4B/BaPd4B analogues require arc-melting, Ta sealing, 950 °C annealing for 5 days, and quenching under purified Ar (SS1, TS1). These conditions are standard for intermetallics but require inert handling, welding, and high-temperature containment, which are nontrivial (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Sb volatility requires tighter sealed-ampoule control than B-containing analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from arc-melting, which homogenizes refractory Pd with Ba/Sb before annealing [Reasonable Inference], and this approach is validated for SrPd4B/BaPd4B (SS1, TS1). The route still scores only moderate because elemental Pd is refractory and the target requires ordered Ba/Pd/Sb placement rather than just formation of a binary Pd-rich network [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing in SS1/TS1 suggests diffusion/order development is not instantaneous.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has a similar F2 score to Route 1 because it uses higher temperatures but potentially fewer processing steps. BaPdSn3 forms at 1670 K under Ar in alumina crucibles with controlled cooling, and Ba–Ru–Sn forms in sealed Ta under Ar at 1300 K/1100 K (CS2, CS1). The limiting condition penalties arise from high temperature, sealed-system need for Ba/Sb stoichiometry, and hazardous/air-sensitive elements [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct high-temperature reaction offers reasonable kinetics because Ba and Sb can become molten/reactive while Pd remains the refractory framework-forming species [Reasonable Inference]. Ce–Pd–Sb flux growth was performed at 900 °C to 700 °C over multiple days using NaBr/KBr in evacuated quartz (CS4). Flux improves kinetics by enhancing mass transport and crystal growth, and CS4 demonstrates that Pd–Sb intermetallic crystals can be grown from halide flux. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSbPd4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally similar Pd-rich intermetallics are also computed or reported at/near the hull (target data; CS1–CS3, CS5; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS5). The analogues support synthesis especially well for high-temperature elemental intermetallic routes: Ba–Pd–Sn forms directly from elements, Ba–Ru–Sn forms in sealed Ta, and Sr/Ba–Pd–B forms by arc-melt/anneal/quench (CS1, CS2, SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with established Pd-rich alkaline-earth intermetallic synthesis methods (CS2, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control involving air-sensitive/volatile Ba and toxic/volatile Sb in a Pd-rich matrix [Reasonable Inference]. Phase competition remains a concern because some Pd-rich or Pd–pnictide analogues require long annealing, flux growth, or repeated treatment to minimize impurities (CS4, SS3, SS5).

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Ba oxidation/air sensitivity | High | Handle and load in Ar glovebox; SrPd4B/BaPd4B and Ca2Pd3Ge syntheses used argon gloveboxes/low O2-H2O handling and sealed refractory ampoules (SS1, TS1, TS5). |
| 2 | All | Sb volatility/toxicity and stoichiometry drift | High | Use sealed Ta/Nb ampoule or evacuated quartz secondary containment; sealed ampoule practice is common in Ba–Ru–Sn, Sr/Eu–Pd–In, Pd–As, and Ce–Pd–Sb analogues (CS1, CS3, CS5, SS2, TS2, CS4). |
| 3 | Routes 1–2 | Pd refractory diffusion / incomplete homogenization | Medium | Use fine Pd powder, pre-arc-melt or high-temperature dwell; repeated arc-melting and annealing are used for SrPd4B/BaPd4B (SS1, TS1). |
| 4 | Routes 1–2 | Competing Ba–Pd, Pd–Sb, or Ba–Sb phases | Medium–High | Screen slight Ba/Sb excess and annealing temperature; Ba/Sr–Pd–Sn used excess alkaline earth to compensate evaporation, and CS4 shows flux can help separate Pd–Sb-containing phases (CS2, CS4). |
| 5 | Route 1 | Arc-melting loss of Ba/Sb | Medium | Use low-current/short arc exposure, multiple flips, and sealed post-anneal; low-current arc-melting to minimize alkaline-earth evaporation is reported for Sr-containing intermetallics (CS3). |
| 6 | Route 2 | Container compatibility at high temperature | Medium | Prefer Ta or Nb ampoule with quartz jacket; Ta ampoules are used for Ba–Ru–Sn and Pd–In analogues, and Nb ampoules for Ca–Pd–Ge (CS1, CS3, CS5, TS5). |
| 7 | Route 3 | Flux contamination or Ba-halide formation | Medium | Use pre-reacted Ba–Pd–Sb feed and dry NaBr/KBr; NaBr/KBr flux is validated for Ce–Pd–Sb but Ba reactivity requires stricter dryness [Reasonable Inference from CS4]. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Crystal growth may not yield bulk single phase | Medium | Use flux primarily for crystals/phase identification, then optimize bulk solid-state route; CS4 used flux to obtain crystals after multiphase behavior. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Iodine side reactions, especially BaI2 formation | High | Minimize I2 loading, use Ta liner, and start from pre-reacted button; iodine improves Pd intermetallic reactivity in SS5/TS4, but Ba iodide formation is a [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 10 | Route 4 | Transport-induced composition gradients | Medium | Use short ampoule, low I2 concentration, and post-anneal homogenization; SS5 reports regrinding/repeated heat treatments can be needed for phase purity in Pd intermetallic CVT. |
