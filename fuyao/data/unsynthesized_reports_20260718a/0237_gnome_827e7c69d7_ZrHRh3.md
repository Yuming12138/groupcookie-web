### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability. The closest structural and thermodynamic analogues Ti₂Rh₆B, ZrRh₃B, HfRh₃B, and YRh₃B are also hull-stable, giving strong SS/TS support (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3, TS5). CS also supports hydride/intermetallic stability because Ti₃SnH, TlPd₃H, and NaPd₃H₂ are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS2, CS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee H occupancy stability under finite-temperature H₂ chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 requires arc melting, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, and subsequent H₂/D₂ treatment, all of which are supported by SS1/SS2 and CS1. The severity is reduced by the fact that no ultra-high pressure is required, but increased by multi-step processing and atmosphere control. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact ZrRh₃H hydrogenation pressure, so the pressure window is extrapolated from CS1 and CS4.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetically, arc melting helps overcome sluggish Zr/Rh diffusion, as Ti₂Rh₆B and ZrRh₃B are made by melt-assisted metallurgical routes (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: ZrRh₃B is nearly isostructural to the target, so a ZrRh₃X framework is demonstrably accessible (SS2/TS2). The main uncertainty is whether H can be inserted at full stoichiometric occupancy without forming binary hydrides or H-poor ZrRh₃ phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 avoids very high temperatures but requires H₂/vacuum sealing and potentially repeated regrinding, as seen in direct solid-state TlPd₃ synthesis (CS2) and hydride synthesis under H₂ pressure (CS4). The use of ZrH₂ creates hydrogen chemical potential but introduces H-loss and air-sensitivity concerns [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS directly supports ZrH₂ + Rh as a route to the Pm-3m phase, so the score remains moderate-low.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct solid-state formation from ZrH₂ and Rh powder is diffusion-limited because Rh and Zr are refractory metals [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 reports long reaction times and regrinding for TlPd₃, supporting concern about intermetallic homogenization. The route has weaker structural continuity than Routes 1/3 because it must construct the ZrRh₃H lattice from dissimilar starting phases.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has the same alloy-preparation burden as Route 1, plus controlled H₂ pressure, but avoids extreme GPa pressure. The H₂ pressure concept is supported by NaPd₃H₂ at 5 MPa and Ti₃SnH/D pressure-dependent hydride formation (CS4, CS1). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifies exact hydrogen pressure for Zr–Rh hydrides, so MPa conditions are a [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Hydrogenating a preformed ZrRh₃-like alloy should minimize cation redistribution, making H diffusion the key kinetic process [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports hydride formation by gas treatment of a preformed alloy, but also warns of sluggish hydride phase transitions. The ZrRh₃B analogue confirms the ZrRh₃X perovskite topology is accessible, supporting structural conversion to ZrRh₃H (SS2/TS2).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The simple 5-atom Pm-3m cell lowers amorphization risk, and analogous Pm-3m Rh borides are crystalline products by arc melting/annealing (SS2, SS3; TS2, TS3, TS5). However, mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity is present in the target and analogues, increasing disorder risk (SS2/TS2). The cation size mismatch between Zr and Rh is a [Reasonable Inference] because CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic/metallic radii.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrRh₃H is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close Rh-rich analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, CS2, CS4; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3, TS5). The strongest support comes from ZrRh₃B, which has the same cubic Pm-3m ZrRh₃X perovskite framework and nearly matching Rh–X bond length, making the structural analogy particularly compelling (SS2/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable, experimentally synthesized Rh-rich perovskite boride analogues that can be made by standard arc melting and homogenization (SS2/TS2). The main limiting factor is hydrogen stoichiometry control: CS1 shows hydride phases can depend sensitively on D₂ pressure and exhibit sluggish transitions. A secondary limitation is possible competition between Zr–Rh intermetallics, Zr hydrides, and H-deficient phases [Reasonable Inference].
