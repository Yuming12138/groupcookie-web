### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to lie on the convex hull and is scored very high. Thermodynamically similar WIr, WIr₃, NbPt₂, and Ta₃Ru₅B₂ are also E_hull = 0 in SS/TS, and chemically similar CS examples are stable or only slightly metastable up to ~0.046 eV/atom (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee experimental accessibility if competing ordered variants or solid-solution disorder intervene [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + anneal

Route 1 conditions are moderately severe because close analogues require arc-melting and high-temperature homogenization/ordering, including 1600 °C treatment for W–Ir alloys and 1100–1500 °C treatment for Nb/Pt alloys (SS1, SS2, SS4; TS1, TS2, TS4). Kinetics are helped by the molten alloy stage and repeated remelting, which are explicitly used to maximize homogeneity in CS3 and SS5/TS5. The main uncertainty is whether the orthorhombic Pmmm derivative requires a narrow annealing window to order Ta/Ru/Pt over multiple sites [Reasonable Inference from ordered alloy treatments in SS1–SS4/TS1–TS4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Induction / levitation melting

Route 2 has slightly lower formal severity because induction melting plus annealing is described as straightforward for some chemically similar intermetallics (CS1, CS2). Kinetically, induction/levitation melting should provide good mixing, but the target’s Ta-rich refractory composition makes complete melting and homogenization more demanding than the Zr/Hf/In/Sn examples in CS1/CS2 [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is crucible compatibility and whether the alloy couples well enough to induction heating without contamination [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Solid-state sintering

Solid-state elemental reaction is plausible because CS4 reports preparation from elements by solid-phase reactions, and SS4/TS4 uses heat treatment for ordering in Nb/Pt-related systems. However, kinetics are poor because Ta/Ru/Pt are dense metals and Ta is highly refractory, so diffusion without a melt is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is whether repeated grinding and long annealing can overcome diffusion limits before competing binary phases dominate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film co-sputtering

Thin-film synthesis has moderate formal condition severity because it avoids bulk melting but uses vacuum and multiple processing steps [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, co-sputtering gives atom-scale mixing, but crystallization into the ordered beta-Cu₃Ti-derived Pmmm phase is unproven in the supplied CS/SS/TS data. Therefore, its raw score is high but evidence-weighted confidence is lower than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has extensive corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, which triggers the mixed-connectivity check and gives some amorphization/ordering risk. Similar cuboctahedral metallic frameworks are experimentally known in SS1, SS2, SS4 and TS1, TS2, TS4, which lowers concern compared with open-framework or highly ionic systems. Radius mismatch and very large cell size are not expected to trigger based on metallic Ta/Ru/Pt size compatibility and compact intermetallic formula [Reasonable Inference], but CS provides insufficient direct signal for amorphization and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₂(RuPt)₃ is likely a **ground-state intermetallic phase** because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis is well supported by SS/TS analogues: WIr₃ is beta-Cu₃Ti-like and arc-melted/annealed, NbPt₂ is a refractory/Pt cuboctahedral alloy, and Ta₃Ru₅B₂ demonstrates Ta–Ru elemental arc-melting under Ar (SS2, SS4, SS5; TS2, TS4, TS5). The most important enabling factor is the existence of stable, dense cuboctahedral intermetallic analogues accessible by conventional metallurgy. The most important limiting factor is achieving the correct ordered Ta/Ru/Pt distribution rather than a binary mixture or disordered solid solution [Reasonable Inference from ordering/annealing requirements in SS1–SS4/TS1–TS4]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires careful high-temperature melting, repeated homogenization, and annealing.
