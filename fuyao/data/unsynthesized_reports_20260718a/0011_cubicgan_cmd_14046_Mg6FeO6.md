### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability threshold in the requested rubric. CS and TS are supportive because several chemically or thermodynamically similar oxides with E_hull ≈0.04–0.08 eV/atom are reported as synthesized, including MgCu2O3, Ca2Fe9O13, CaFe4O6, Mg2VO4, and Sr3Fe2O5-related phases (CS2, CS4, CS5, TS1, TS4). SS gives mixed evidence: Fm-3m monoxide analogues are synthesizable but often require high pressure and may be metastable (SS2, SS3), while SS1 has high E_hull and is less transferable. The remaining uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not resolve the unusual charge balance of Mg6FeO6 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Controlled-atmosphere solid-state

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because CS4/CS5 reduced ferrites require ~1120 °C, long annealing, and controlled pO2, while TS1 shows simpler Mg oxide spinels can form at 800–1000 °C in a furnace. Kinetics are the main limitation because MgO and Fe oxides are refractory solid precursors, and the target structure is not a simple derivative of either precursor [Reasonable Inference]. CS4/CS5 also show that Fe oxide phase purity can be sensitive to oxygen pressure and stability domain. Insufficient evidence from SS for this exact ambient-pressure Mg–Fe–O route, so SS is treated as neutral for Route 1 feasibility.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Flux-assisted growth

Flux growth has better kinetics because CS2 directly reports MgCu2O3 single crystals from a KCl flux during controlled cooling, and CS3 reports Mg-containing borate crystals from melts. Condition severity remains significant because the target likely requires low-pO2 Fe control, as reduced ferrites in CS4/CS5 do. The main uncertainty is whether Mg6FeO6 dissolves or nucleates in chloride flux rather than forming MgO, Fe metal, spinel, or ferrite impurities [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of the target structure and is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure synthesis

SS2/SS3 and TS3 strongly support high pressure as a route to dense or metastable Fm-3m oxide phases, including PrO/CeO and high-pressure NaAlO2. The route is penalized heavily for condition severity because it requires multi-anvil/Bridgman-type equipment and sealed capsules, as in SS2/SS3/TS3. Kinetically, high pressure can help densification and phase stabilization, but Mg/Fe ordering in a ternary oxide is not directly demonstrated by the cited high-pressure analogues [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure Mg–Fe–O synthesis and is neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small formula atom count and only corner/edge sharing in the given structure description, so it does not trigger the unit-cell-size or vertex+edge+face mixed-connectivity amorphization checks. The cation-radius check is not strongly triggered for common Mg2+/Fe2+/Fe3+ oxide radii [Reasonable Inference], although CS/SS/TS do not provide explicit ionic radii. The unusual Mg square-planar coordination may indicate local bonding strain, but that is a crystallographic/phase-stability concern rather than an amorphization trigger under the specified rubric [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the F4 score is high but not a guarantee of crystallization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg6FeO6 is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, while several analogous oxides near this energy range have reported syntheses (CS2, CS4, CS5, TS1). The CS and TS evidence supports the general feasibility of Mg/transition-metal oxides and reduced ferrites, but no source directly validates this exact Mg-rich Fe oxide stoichiometry. The strongest enabling factor is the low computed E_hull combined with accessible oxide/flux synthesis precedents (CS2, CS3, TS1). The strongest limiting factor is oxygen/charge-balance control: reduced ferrite analogues require narrow pO2 windows (CS4, CS5), and Mg6FeO6 is chemically unusual under conventional Mg2+/O2− valence assumptions [Reasonable Inference]. Flux-assisted growth is the best initial route because it improves kinetics while avoiding the high equipment burden of high-pressure synthesis (CS2, CS3).
