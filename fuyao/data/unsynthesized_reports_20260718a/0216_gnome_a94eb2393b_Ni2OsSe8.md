The remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state

Route 1 uses standard evacuated quartz/silica tube practice that is well supported for selenides in SS3, SS5, TS4, TS5, and CS5. The severity deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Se containment, likely repeated grinding/annealing, and hazardous Se/Os handling [SS3, SS5; Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and can likely stay below 1000 °C initially, but Os diffusion makes the route less benign than ordinary binary selenide synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 sealed-tube solid-state

The kinetic score is limited by refractory Os and dense elemental starting materials [Reasonable Inference], consistent with SS5 where multiple firings and high temperatures are needed for Fe/Rh/Se. Structural reconstruction is not prohibitive because NiSe₂ analogues have similar NiSe₆ octahedra and Se–Se dimers (SS1, SS2, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Os orders into the ternary P-1 framework or remains as OsSe₂/metallic Os [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 flux growth

Route 2 has modest temperature severity because CS1/TS2 uses only 310 °C. However, it requires vacuum sealing, K₂Se/K₂Seₓ air-sensitive handling, Se volatility control, and flux removal, all directly supported by CS1/TS2. The score is therefore moderate rather than high despite the low temperature.

#### F3 — Route 2 flux growth

The flux route receives the best kinetics score because alkali polyselenide flux can provide high chalcogen activity and liquid/flux-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/TS2 demonstrates crystallization of a Se-rich ternary at low temperature. #### F2 — Route 3 iodine-assisted transport

Route 3 has moderate severity because it uses evacuated ampoules, volatile iodine/selenium, and likely prereaction plus transport steps [TS5, CS5; Reasonable Inference]. Iodine improves transport and crystal growth in TS5, where iodine is specifically added to favor single crystals. This could help overcome Os mobility limitations [Reasonable Inference]. #### F2 — Route 4 high-pressure

Route 4 is strongly penalized because the closest dense selenide analogues require 50 kbar or higher pressures and specialized high-pressure apparatus (SS1/TS1, SS4). It also combines high temperature, volatile Se containment, and multi-step precursor preparation [SS1/TS1, SS4; Reasonable Inference]. Although high pressure is chemically plausible, the experimental severity is high.

#### F3 — Route 4 high-pressure

The structural pathway is favorable because NiSe₂ pyrite/marcasite analogues have NiSe₆ octahedra and Se–Se units closely resembling the target motifs (SS1, SS2, TS1). However, kinetics are not necessarily easy because high-pressure dwell times are short and ternary Ni/Os ordering may be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The structural support is strong from NiSe₂ pyrite/marcasite analogues containing octahedral NiSe₆ and Se–Se dimers (SS1, SS2, TS1), but the chemical support for Os-containing ternary selenides is indirect. The most critical enabling factor is the Se-rich, dimer-forming chemistry that can stabilize metal octahedral selenide frameworks, supported by SS1/SS2/TS1 and CS1/TS2. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: Os is refractory and may dissolve/react slowly, making direct solid-state ordering difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Flux or iodine-assisted transport should be attempted first because these routes improve mass transport, but phase purity must be monitored carefully because CS1/TS2 and SS3/SS5 report mixed-phase or repeated-firing challenges.
