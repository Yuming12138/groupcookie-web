### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. CS analogues are mostly reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS5), SS/TS analogues include several stable borides and Hf₂SnC with E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, SS3–SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated convex-hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for volatile-Se-containing refractory compounds [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits moderate severity from Hf₂SnC sealed-ampoule annealing at 1000 °C for 150 h (TS5). The need for a sealed or evacuated ampoule is supported by air-excluded ampoule conditions in TS5 and Ar-sealed solid-state reactions in SS1/TS1. The extra penalty comes from Se volatility and toxicity, which are not directly covered by CS/SS/TS and are therefore [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are limited by refractory Hf/B diffusion, consistent with Ta–B analogues requiring high temperatures or repeated treatments (SS1/TS1, SS2). The H-phase Hf₂SnC analogue forming at 1000 °C suggests the target topology is not intrinsically inaccessible (TS5). The main uncertainty is whether Se incorporation accelerates reaction through transient liquid/vapor transport or instead destabilizes stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Arc melting is well supported for chemically similar intermetallics under Ar or gettered Ar (CS1–CS4) and for a refractory boride analogue (SS5/TS4). However, arc temperatures exceed 1400 °C, and the method requires controlled atmosphere and remelting/homogenization, matching the difficulty described in CS1 and SS5/TS4. The largest route-specific penalty is Se volatility during arc melting, which is a [Reasonable Inference] because no CS/SS/TS source directly handles Se in open arc melting.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Arc melting improves Hf–B mixing relative to powder sintering, consistent with homogeneous intermetallic preparation in CS1–CS4 and SS5/TS4. The two-step pre-alloying concept is supported by CS1, where a high-melting W–Si alloy was first formed before melting with U. The uncertainty is that preformed Hf–B alloys may be too stable and may react sluggishly with Se during post-anneal [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth avoids the extreme 1800–2190 °C conditions seen in some Ta boride solid-state routes (SS1/TS1, SS2), but still requires sealed high-temperature processing. The flux route is multi-step because SS4/TS3 uses high-temperature solution growth, slow cooling, and flux/matrix removal. Se volatility again imposes sealed-system constraints [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth is kinetically favorable because SS4/TS3 shows that difficult boride phases can be accessed through liquid flux when sintering/melting gives phase-control problems. The liquid medium should improve transport of Hf/B/Se species relative to dense refractory powders [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is compositional selectivity: flux may stabilize Hf–Sn, Hf–Al, Hf–Se, or other boride/selenide side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers one amorphization/ordering-risk criterion; similar mixed sharing appears in structurally similar borides (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS4). The target does not appear to have a large unit cell or multi-cation radius mismatch [Reasonable Inference], and the H-phase analogue Hf₂SnC is a simple ordered P6₃/mmc phase (TS5). CS provides insufficient evidence for amorphization tendency and is treated as neutral 50 for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂BSe is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and stable analogues are common in CS/SS/TS datasets. The strongest support comes from TS5, where Hf₂SnC has the same H-phase P6₃/mmc structure, and from SS/TS borides showing that refractory metal–boron networks can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a structurally matched Hf-based H-phase analogue, Hf₂SnC, synthesized by sealed high-temperature annealing (TS5). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of refractory Hf–B kinetics and volatile Se stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Among the proposed routes, flux-assisted sealed synthesis offers the best balance between transport enhancement and Se retention, although it remains experimentally uncertain because no direct Hf–B–Se synthesis analogue is provided.
