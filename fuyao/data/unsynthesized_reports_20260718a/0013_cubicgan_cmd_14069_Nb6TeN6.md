### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the top of the prompt’s highest stability score band, so F1 = 90. Thermodynamic analogues include synthesized nitrides with E_hull near this value, including Nb4N5 at 0.0319 eV/atom and U2CrN3 at 0.0414 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). TS4 even shows a synthesized Ta2N phase at 0.1133 eV/atom, indicating that metastable nitrides above the hull can be experimentally accessible. The uncertainty is that the target’s ternary Nb–Te–N chemistry is not directly represented in TS; insufficient evidence from TS for telluronitride-specific decomposition pathways.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, CVD

The condition severity is significant because Nb4N5 CVD requires NH3/H2, vaporized NbCl5, and controlled gas flows at 950–1000 °C (SS1, TS1). This gives moderate-to-high equipment and handling burden but avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C if optimized. Kinetically, CVD is favorable for Nb–N formation because direct Nb nitride deposition is demonstrated (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is Te delivery and stoichiometric incorporation, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence; Te co-deposition is therefore [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, sealed solid-state

The solid-state route uses familiar high-temperature nitride/chalcogenide logic: CS analogues were made at 950–1300 °C with repeated heat treatments or sealed crucibles (CS1–CS5), and nitride analogues were made at 1200–1600 °C in N2 or by metal + N2 reaction (SS4, TS2, TS4). Its condition score is helped by avoiding NH3/H2 high-pressure chemistry, but Te volatility requires sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are poor because Nb/NbN are refractory and dense, and repeated heat treatments in CS analogues indicate slow completion (CS2–CS5). The largest uncertainty is whether Te can diffuse into and order within a Nb–N framework before forming stable Nb tellurides or binary nitrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, high-pressure NH3

The high-pressure NH3 route is supported by Li2Ta3N5 formation at 550 °C and 0.6 GPa NH3 for 5 days (SS3, TS5), and pressure-stabilized nitride synthesis is also demonstrated for TaN polymorphs (SS2). Its condition score is penalized for NH3 pressure, sealed equipment, and air-sensitive/toxic handling, but it avoids very high temperatures. Kinetically, NH3/mineralizer transport should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and the lower temperature may suppress Te loss. The remaining uncertainty is substantial because no provided analogue proves Nb–Te–N ammonothermal formation; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Te behavior in high-pressure NH3.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically distinct Nb and Te cation environments: Nb is square-planar N4, while Te is octahedral N6, implying a strong size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing NNb4Te square pyramids, which increases geometric frustration and amorphization risk. If the Fm-3m conventional cell contains four formula units, the atom count is likely 52, triggering the unit-cell complexity check [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for Nb–Te–N; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for glass-forming behavior.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Nb6TeN6 is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and synthesized TS analogues exist at similar or higher hull distances (TS1–TS4). The analogues strongly support Nb/Ta nitride formation and high-temperature chalcogenide synthesis, but they do not directly demonstrate Nb–Te–N chemistry, so the evidence is indirect. The most critical enabling factor is the presence of robust Nb–N bonding with target Nb–N distances matching known Nb4N5 distances closely (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Te volatility and nitrogen chemical potential without decomposing into Nb nitrides plus Nb/Te tellurides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausible but requires controlled-atmosphere synthesis rather than routine ceramic firing.
