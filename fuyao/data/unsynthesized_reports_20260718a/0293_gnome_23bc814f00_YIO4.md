### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability class. Chemically similar iodate/periodate phases Ba(IO3)2, NaI3O8, and RbIO4 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5), and many SS/TS analogues are also at or near hull stability (SS-1/SS-3; TS-1/TS-4). The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the specific hydration-free polymorph forms from solution [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal severity.**  
Hydrothermal analogues operate at 170 °C for one week or 230 °C for 3 days, which is moderate and far below severe solid-state temperatures (CS-3; SS-2; TS-5). The route requires an autoclave and oxidizing/acidic media, but no GPa pressure, no reducing atmosphere, and no >1000 °C treatment are indicated (CS-3; SS-2). Remaining uncertainty is iodine oxyanion speciation under hydrothermal pH, which is not directly resolved by SS/TS and only partly constrained by CS-3.

**F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics.**  
Solution transport and acidic mineralization should give good precursor activity, consistent with successful iodate/periodate hydrothermal crystallization in CS-3 and Y/RE hydrothermal oxoanion crystallization in SS-2/TS-5. Kinetics are better than dry solid-state synthesis because ion transport occurs through solution rather than dense Y2O3 lattices [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the exact edge-sharing YO6 Cmcm framework, for which CS/SS/TS provide no exact analogue.

**F2 — Route 2 evaporation severity.**  
This is the mildest route because RbIO4 grows at room temperature by slow evaporation (CS-5), and NaI3O8 grows by evaporation at 60 °C in nitric acid (CS-4). It avoids autoclaves, high temperature, vacuum, and high pressure (CS-4; CS-5). Deductions come from multi-step neutralization/recrystallization and oxidizing/corrosive reagents, especially H5IO6/HNO3 [Reasonable Inference based on CS-4/CS-5].

**F3 — Route 2 evaporation kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable because all species are mobile in solution and crystallization occurs by supersaturation rather than solid diffusion, as shown by CS-4 and CS-5. Periodate chemistry is especially relevant because RbIO4 is made from orthoperiodic acid by neutralization and slow evaporation (CS-5). The limiting kinetic uncertainty is whether Y3+ remains soluble long enough to crystallize YIO4 rather than precipitating amorphous/basic yttrium iodate or hydroxide [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 flux/sealed-tube severity.**  
Flux growth is plausible because CS-1 shows rare-earth oxide + volatile p-block oxide + CsCl flux in evacuated sealed silica at 830 °C. However, the sealed vacuum tube, volatile iodine-source management, multi-step loading/cooling/washing, and oxidizer hazards make this route significantly more severe than Routes 1–2 (CS-1; SS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). There is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence that iodine oxides remain stable at the flux temperature, so this route is conservative and more challenging.

**F3 — Route 3 flux/sealed-tube kinetics.**  
Flux improves ion transport and crystal growth, as shown by the CsCl flux route for Sm2(SeO3)3 (CS-1). Nevertheless, Y2O3 is refractory and the target framework must form from binary oxide/iodine oxide components, so precursor activity and reconstruction are only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates Y–I–O flux synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The main amorphization risk is the strong mismatch between Y-centered high-coordination oxide polyhedra and iodine oxyanion units; this mismatch is evident by comparison between Y/RE–O coordination in SS-2/TS-5 and I–O oxyanion coordination in CS-2/CS-5. The structure is a simple ternary with no reported face-sharing and no evidence for a very large cell, so only one F4 trigger is assigned. The uncertainty is that aqueous Y3+ chemistry can produce gelatinous hydroxide/basic salts if pH is not controlled [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YIO4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar iodate/periodate materials that crystallize from hydrothermal, evaporation, or neutralization routes under mild conditions (CS-2 to CS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Y/RE oxoanion frameworks support the ability of Y/RE cations to crystallize into complex oxoanion frameworks under hydrothermal conditions, but they do not directly prove the exact Y–I–O Cmcm structure (SS-2; TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated iodine oxyanion chemistry, which avoids high-temperature iodine loss and poor Y2O3 diffusion (CS-3; CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is Y3+ hydrolysis/speciation control, because the best mild aqueous routes for alkali iodates may not transfer directly to a trivalent rare-earth cation [Reasonable Inference].
