### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is the strongest thermodynamic signal for synthesizability. Close analogues MgRh, Ti2Ni2In, Zr2Ni2In, LaRh3Ga2, LiRh2Ge, Sr–Rh–Ga, and SrRh2 are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in the supplied data (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5, CS2, CS4). The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or single-phase ordering [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because MgRh was synthesized at 793–873 K under Ar for week-scale holds (SS1, TS1). The penalties come from sealed-system Mg control, multistep grinding/reannealing, and Mg air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS1 protected handling]. CS data provide insufficient direct evidence for Mg–Nb–Rh sintering specifically, so the route relies most heavily on SS1/TS1.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
MgRh demonstrates that Mg–Rh bcc coordination can form by solid-state sintering (SS1, TS1), which supports structural transferability. However, Nb and Rh are refractory metals with slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and the MgRh analogue itself required long heating and regrinding (SS1, TS1). Thus kinetics are the main limitation for this otherwise mild route.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Arc/induction melting plus annealing is strongly supported by chemically similar intermetallics, including CS1–CS5 and Rh-containing SS2/TS2 and SS4/TS4. The severity score is lower because open melting with Mg risks evaporation [Reasonable Inference], and the effective melting/alloying temperatures are high, especially for Nb/Rh [Reasonable Inference]. The provided CS/SS/TS routes generally use Ar and standard metallurgy equipment, so no high-pressure penalty is applied.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Melting greatly improves homogenization, as shown by repeated arc melting/remelting in CS1, CS2, CS4, and Czochralski/induction approaches in CS3 and SS4/TS4. This route is kinetically stronger than purely solid-state sintering because liquid or near-liquid processing reduces diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Mg can be retained accurately enough during melting to reach Mg3NbRh4 stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The sealed Ta/Nb ampoule route borrows containment strategies from MgRh Ta wrapping (SS1/TS1), LiRh2Ge Nb tubes (SS3/TS3), and Sr–Rh–Ga Ta ampoules (SS4/TS4). It avoids the most extreme open arc temperatures but introduces sealed-vapor-pressure and welding complexity [Reasonable Inference]. CS data provide insufficient direct evidence for Mg self-flux Mg–Nb–Rh growth, so this is more extrapolative.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A Mg-rich melt could enhance transport of Nb and Rh relative to dry solid-state sintering [Reasonable Inference], and sealed metal containers are precedent for reactive/volatile intermetallic systems (SS3/TS3, SS4/TS4). However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Nb solubility or Mg–Nb–Rh liquidus behavior, so the route may suffer from incomplete Nb incorporation. Structural formation is plausible because Heusler-type Rh intermetallics exist (CS5, SS3/TS3), but the exact trigonal target is not directly represented.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
Only one amorphization check is triggered: significant metallic radius mismatch among Mg, Nb, and Rh [Reasonable Inference]. The target description uses mostly body-centered-cubic local environments rather than mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, so C3 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any mixed connectivity in the target. The computed ground-state stability and crystalline analogues lower the expected amorphization risk (SS1/TS1, CS5, SS3/TS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg3NbRh4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0 eV/atom. The analogue support is reasonably strong: MgRh directly supports Mg–Rh bcc coordination and low-temperature sealed synthesis (SS1, TS1), while CS Heusler/intermetallic examples support elemental melting plus annealing routes (CS1–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the close MgRh structural/thermodynamic match, especially the nearly identical Mg–Rh bond lengths and body-centered-cubic coordination (SS1, TS1; target data). The most critical limiting factor is the combination of Mg volatility/air sensitivity with refractory Nb/Rh diffusion, which makes stoichiometry and homogeneity difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is synthesizable with moderate-to-high confidence, but phase-pure bulk samples will likely require iterative annealing and careful Mg control.
