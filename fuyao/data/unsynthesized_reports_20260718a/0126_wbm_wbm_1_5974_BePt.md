### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target BePt is exactly on the calculated convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic accessibility [Target]. Chemically similar Pt intermetallics in CS are also all listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Mg₂Pt, LiPt, MgIr₂, IrAl, and Al₃Pt₂ (CS1–CS5). Thermodynamic analogues include Al₃Pt₂ and PtTiAl at 0.0 eV/atom and Pt-rich aluminides only ~0.002 eV/atom above hull (TS1–TS4), reinforcing that Pt intermetallic energy landscapes often contain stable ordered compounds. The uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly confirms the experimental Be–Pt phase diagram or the cubic P2₁3 polymorph.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because melting/arc-melting introduces very high local temperatures, and analogous Pt intermetallic routes use arc-melting or induction melting plus annealing (SS4/TS3; CS5). Sealed or controlled atmospheres are required by the analogues, including Ti-gettered Ar, vacuum sealing, Ta tubes, and quartz jackets (SS4/TS3; CS5). Be volatility and toxicity are not given in CS/SS/TS, so those deductions are [Reasonable Inference] and add substantial practical difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by elemental metal diffusion and ordering, because Pt-containing analogues often require long anneals or repeated thermal cycles (CS2; CS5; SS4/TS3). MgIr₂ initially contained MgIr and Ir impurities and required additional annealing, showing phase purification can be slow (CS5). The target’s exact cubic P2₁3 structure has no direct match in CS/SS/TS, so structural reconstruction is scored conservatively.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids full melting and can stay near ≤1000 °C, consistent with Mg₂Pt synthesis at 650/1000 °C in sealed Nb (CS2). It still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed metal ampoules, and multi-step thermal treatment, all supported by CS2 and CS5. Be hazards and Be volatility remain inferred but important, so the route is not easy despite lower temperature [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The direct solid-state route benefits from intimate contact between Be and Pt powders/chips, but elemental Pt is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference; CS2]. Long annealing in CS2 and impurity-removal annealing in CS5 suggest that binary intermetallic formation is achievable but may be slow. No SS/TS analogue establishes the same P2₁3 ordering motif, so ordering kinetics remain uncertain and are scored moderately low.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has lower temperature severity because Al–Pt CVT analogues operate at 400–600 °C (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS4). However, it requires high-vacuum sealed ampoules, volatile iodine, and careful halide partial-pressure control (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS4). Be halide toxicity/handling is [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS source directly validates BePt CVT.

#### F3 — Route 3

CVT can improve mass transport relative to bulk diffusion, as shown by Al–Pt intermetallic deposition in iodine-containing sealed ampoules (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS4). The main kinetic/compositional weakness is that the Al–Pt analogues form different phases depending on p(AlI₃)/p(I₂), implying a narrow process window (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS4). Be transport chemistry is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS, so the route is plausible but speculative.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple binary intermetallic with a single Be site and a single Pt site in 7-coordinate environments, which suggests relatively low topological complexity [Target]. One amorphization trigger is assigned for the large Be/Pt size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate >40 atoms per unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks [Target], and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct target-cell evidence for those checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BePt is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The CS and TS data strongly support the general stability and synthesizability of Pt intermetallics, especially by sealed-tube direct reaction, arc/anneal processing, and occasionally CVT (CS2–CS5; SS1/TS1; TS2–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong calculated thermodynamic stability combined with precedent for direct elemental Pt-intermetallic formation in sealed Nb/Ta ampoules (CS2; CS5). The most critical limiting factor is Be handling: toxicity, volatility, oxide formation, and stoichiometry retention are all significant [Reasonable Inference], and none of the provided CS/SS/TS sources directly validates Be–Pt synthesis. Overall, the target is feasible but not routine, with best prospects from sealed-ampoule solid-state synthesis rather than open melting or unconstrained vapor transport.
