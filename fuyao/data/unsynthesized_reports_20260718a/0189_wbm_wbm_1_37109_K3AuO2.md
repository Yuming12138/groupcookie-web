### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported as E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom in the provided target data, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability scoring bracket. Closely related oxoaurates and alkali oxide analogues are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including CsK₂AuO₂, Na₃AuO₂, K₃AuO, and Rb₅Au₃O₂ (SS1–SS3, SS5, TS1–TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to air/moisture decomposition, which related analogues often suffer (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the closest analogues use only ~430 °C and no high pressure, but they require sealed Ag cylinders and air-sensitive auride/peroxide precursors (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). Its kinetics are favorable because peroxide oxidation directly supplies oxygen and forms linear AuO₂ units in structurally related phases (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). The main uncertainty is whether K₃AuO₂ has the same stability window as Na₃AuO₂ or whether K-rich competing phases such as K₃AuO intervene (SS2, TS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it relies on oxygen-buffered K₂O/K₂O₂ chemistry and thus needs tighter oxygen-potential control under sealed/inert conditions [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2 and SS3/TS3]. Kinetically, low-temperature formation of related alkali auride oxides at 200–425 °C suggests diffusion is not prohibitive (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). The uncertainty is high because the route may preferentially form K₃AuO or mixed-valence/metal-rich aurides instead of K₃AuO₂ [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2 and SS3/TS3].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has challenging condition severity because alkali-metal flux growth requires sealed ampoules, strict Ar handling, and vacuum distillation of excess alkali metal, as shown for Cs₃AuO (SS2, TS2). Kinetically, a liquid/solution-like alkali-metal medium should enhance transport and crystal growth, as explicitly reported for Cs₃AuO (SS2, TS2). The major uncertainty is that the flux analogue is Cs₃AuO, not K₃AuO₂, so oxygen activity must be controlled carefully to avoid reduced auride oxide phases [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation environments, with alkali–O distances around 2.6–2.9 Å and Au–O distances near 2.0 Å in close analogues, supporting a moderate size/coordination mismatch risk (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). However, the target is an ordered crystalline ground-state candidate and close analogues crystallize as single crystals under sealed solid-state conditions, so amorphization is not expected to dominate (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available to prove a >40-atom target cell, and the target appears to involve corner/edge rather than corner/edge/face coexistence; therefore only one F4 trigger is counted.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₃AuO₂ is likely a ground-state phase because the target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and close thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strong because CsK₂AuO₂ and Na₃AuO₂, both containing linear AuO₂ units and alkali–oxygen frameworks, are experimentally obtained from alkali aurides plus alkali peroxides at 430 °C in sealed Ag cylinders (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the reactive peroxide/auride route, which supplies both oxygen activity and alkali content at low temperature (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS4). The most critical limiting factor is handling and phase control: related alkali auride oxides are air-sensitive, require sealed/inert apparatus, and can form competing reduced or metal-rich Au phases (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable in an experienced alkali-metal solid-state laboratory, but not in an ordinary oxide-sintering workflow.
