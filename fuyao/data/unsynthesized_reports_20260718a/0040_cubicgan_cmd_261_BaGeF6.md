### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the highest score bin in the requested rubric, so F1 is set to 90 rather than a higher value because it is not predicted as a strict ground state (target thermodynamic data). The same-composition trigonal BaGeF6 analogue is reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, giving strong evidence that Ba–Ge–F chemistry is intrinsically stable (CS-1; SS-2). Thermodynamic analogues include CaTbF6 at E_hull ≈ 0.0503 eV/atom and other fluoride phases around 0.06–0.11 eV/atom that are experimentally accessible, supporting that modestly metastable fluorides can be synthesized (TS-1; TS-3; TS-4). The main uncertainty is whether cubic F-43m BaGeF6 can be isolated rather than converting to the known trigonal ground-state polymorph (CS-1; SS-2).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because same-composition BaGeF6 and close BaSiF6 analogues are grown by slow evaporation from dilute HCl solutions at ambient-like conditions (CS-1; CS-2). No high temperature, high pressure, vacuum, H2/NH3, or sealed high-temperature system is required by the reported slow-evaporation analogues (CS-1; CS-2). The only significant deduction is for toxic/corrosive acidic fluoride handling, because HF is used in BaSiF6 and HF-acidified CsSbF6 growth (CS-2; CS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS for slow-evaporation severity is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization avoids long-range solid-state diffusion, and same-composition BaGeF6 already crystallizes from solution, supporting high kinetic feasibility (CS-1; SS-2). BaSiF6 solution chemistry further supports rapid formation of hexafluorometallate units from soluble precursors (CS-2). The kinetic uncertainty is structural rather than compositional: the known BaGeF6 product is trigonal R-3m, while the target is cubic F-43m, so nucleation conditions must favor a metastable/high-symmetry polymorph (CS-1; SS-2). Insufficient evidence from TS for Route 1 kinetics is neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Sealed-tube fluoride reactions are common among the analogues but require dry handling, noble-metal tubes, vacuum or argon, and moderate-to-high temperatures (CS-3; CS-5; SS-4; SS-5; TS-4). The proposed BaGeF6 solid-state route avoids >1000 °C based on analogue temperatures of 550–950 °C, so no high-temperature rubric penalty is applied (SS-5; TS-4). Deductions are assigned for controlled atmosphere/vacuum, volatile Ge–F precursor containment, and toxic fluoride chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS-3/CS-5 sealed dry fluoride protocols]. Insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for solid-state cubic BaGeF6 makes the severity score less favorable than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Fluoride solid-state syntheses can yield crystalline phases and single crystals in sealed tubes, supporting moderate kinetic feasibility (CS-3; CS-5; SS-4; SS-5; TS-4). However, BaF2 is a stable ionic fluoride and solid-state transport can be slower than solution transport [Reasonable Inference], while several analogues require long heating, slow cooling, or sealed noble-metal tubes (CS-3; CS-5; TS-4). The structural reconstruction score is limited because the most chemically relevant BaGeF6 phase is trigonal and thermodynamically stable, so thermal annealing may favor that polymorph over cubic F-43m (CS-1; SS-2). TS supports sealed fluoride accessibility but does not resolve the BaGeF6 polymorph issue (TS-2; TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

HF-assisted hydrothermal synthesis uses moderate temperature and autogenous pressure, as demonstrated by TS-3 at 150 °C for 24 h in FEP/Teflon-lined Parr vessels. The route avoids high temperature, high pressure above 5 GPa, and reactive gas atmospheres (TS-3). Penalties are assigned for volatile/corrosive HF in a sealed vessel and toxic precursor handling, consistent with HF usage in CS-2, CS-4, and TS-3. Insufficient direct SS evidence for hydrothermal BaGeF6 is neutral for severity but increases uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Hydrothermal HF media should provide high precursor activity through soluble fluoride complexes [Reasonable Inference], supported by BaSiF6 HF solution chemistry and TS-3 hydrothermal fluoride/oxyfluoride crystallization (CS-2; TS-3). Ion diffusion barriers are low because crystallization occurs from solution rather than by bulk cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference; TS-3]. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because hydrothermal methods may access metastable phases, but no provided source shows hydrothermal cubic BaGeF6 specifically, and the known BaGeF6 analogue is trigonal (CS-1; SS-2). CS and TS therefore support the chemistry, while SS provides insufficient direct hydrothermal evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an ordered fluoride with simple BaF12 and GeF6 coordination, and the same chemical composition is known to crystallize as trigonal BaGeF6, indicating that amorphization is not inherently dominant (CS-1; SS-2). One amorphization risk is the large cation-size/coordination mismatch between 12-coordinate Ba2+ and octahedral Ge4+ [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target does not appear to have a large >40-atom cell or vertex+edge+face coexistence, because the target description lists a high-symmetry cubic structure with BaF12 corner sharing and Ba–Ge face sharing but no edge sharing (target structure). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for amorphization and is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cubic BaGeF6 is likely a metastable polymorph rather than the ground state because the target is 0.050 eV/atom above hull, while the same-composition trigonal BaGeF6 analogue is on the hull (target thermodynamic data; CS-1; SS-2). The CS evidence is very strong for making BaGeF6 chemically, especially by slow evaporation, but it supports the trigonal phase more directly than the cubic target (CS-1; CS-2). SS evidence reinforces that BaF12/GeF6 fluoride frameworks are accessible, but again the closest BaGeF6 structural analogue is trigonal rather than cubic (SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the ready formation of stable [GeF6]2−/Ba2+ fluoride salt chemistry in acidic solution (CS-1; CS-2; CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: mild solution or hydrothermal conditions may be needed to trap cubic F-43m BaGeF6 before it relaxes to trigonal R-3m [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/SS-2 and target E_hull].
