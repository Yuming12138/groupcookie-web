### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin and is scored 98/100. Thermodynamic analogues CaRh₂Si₂, BaRh₂Si₂, YRh₂Si₂, NdRh₂Si₂, and PrRh₂Si₂ also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the chemical plausibility of stable A–Rh–Si 1:2:2 phases (TS-1–TS-5). CS and SS provide the same zero-hull analogue signal for the listed materials (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of nearby competing Sr–Si/Rh–Si phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct arc-melting

Arc-melting analogues are well supported by YRh₂Si₂, NdRh₂Si₂, and PrRh₂Si₂ routes under Ar (CS-5, SS-3–SS-5, TS-3–TS-5). Severity deductions come from the high local temperature of arc melting and air sensitivity/possible volatility of Sr [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target Sr loss during arc melting, so that specific risk was not additionally penalized beyond air sensitivity. The route remains moderate rather than severe because no high pressure or exotic reactive gas is required (CS-5, SS-4, SS-5, TS-3–TS-5).

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct arc-melting

Kinetics are helped by melting, repeated flipping, and direct access to the ThCr₂Si₂-type topology observed in YRh₂Si₂ and related Rh₂Si₂ analogues (CS-5, SS-3, SS-5, TS-3, TS-5). They are limited by dense elemental Rh and Si, which are less reactive than solution-derived precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is high because the target and key analogues share I4/mmm 1:2:2 structure types (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3–SS-5, TS-1, TS-3–TS-5). Uncertainty remains around whether Sr volatility creates off-stoichiometry during arc melting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed ampoule solid-state reaction

BaRh₂Si₂ demonstrates a simple sealed-ampoule elemental reaction at 1100 °C for 50 h under Ar (CS-2, SS-2, TS-2). The main severity factors are temperature above 1000 °C, the need to contain Sr in a sealed system [Reasonable Inference], and handling air-sensitive Sr [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure is indicated by any CS/SS/TS source, and Ar is a standard inert atmosphere in the analogues (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Sr vapor pressure under these target-specific conditions, so the volatile component penalty is treated conservatively.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed ampoule solid-state reaction

The sealed ampoule route benefits from long dwell times that have produced BaRh₂Si₂ at 1100 °C (CS-2, SS-2, TS-2). However, because the precursors are elemental solids and Rh is refractory, precursor activity is low-to-moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Structural support is mixed: BaRh₂Si₂ has the same composition family but monoclinic symmetry, while CaRh₂Si₂ is I4/mmm and very close to the target (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). This conflict lowers the kinetic/structural transferability score relative to direct arc melting.

#### F2 — Route 3: Pre-melt Rh–Si + Sr anneal

The closest CaRh₂Si₂-related synthesis used pre-melting of transition metal and Si, arc-melting, grinding/pelletizing, and annealing in welded Nb/Ta ampoules under Ar (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). This route is more severe because it is explicitly multi-step and requires sealed ampoule handling. It is still conventional for intermetallic synthesis because no high pressure, solvothermal chemistry, or unusual reactive gases are required (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for whether the pre-melted Rh–Si precursor exactly matches the target pathway, so this is treated as a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Pre-melt Rh–Si + Sr anneal

Pre-melting Rh–Si should improve local Rh/Si homogeneity and reduce the diffusion burden relative to raw Rh + Si [Reasonable Inference]. The route has strong structural transferability because CaRh₂Si₂ is I4/mmm and has a very similar Rh–Si bond length to the target, 2.38 Å vs 2.41 Å (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). However, the same reference reports impurity problems and narrow phase windows, so the structural/kinetic score is not higher (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Sr insertion into a Rh–Si prealloy proceeds cleanly or instead forms SrSi/Rh–Si binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing RhSr₄Si₄ tetrahedra, directly triggering the mixed connectivity criterion; similar mixed connectivity is reported for CaRh₂Si₂ and YRh₂Si₂ (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3). The large size contrast between Sr and the Rh/Si network is treated as a cation-size mismatch trigger [Reasonable Inference]. The small ThCr₂Si₂-type unit cell avoids the large-cell amorphization trigger, consistent with the simple I4/mmm analogues (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly measures amorphization, so F4 is based on structural heuristics.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrRh₂Si₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and all closest thermodynamic analogues listed also have zero hull energies (TS-1–TS-5). The synthesis case is strong because chemically and structurally related Rh₂Si₂ compounds, especially CaRh₂Si₂, YRh₂Si₂, NdRh₂Si₂, and PrRh₂Si₂, are experimentally accessible by elemental arc-melting or solid-state routes under Ar (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3–SS-5, TS-1, TS-3–TS-5). The most important enabling factor is the robust ThCr₂Si₂-type A–Rh–Si framework, which tolerates multiple A-site cations and is directly observed in close analogues (CS-1, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3–SS-5, TS-1, TS-3–TS-5). The most important limiting factor is phase purity, especially possible formation of Sr–Si or Rh–Si binaries and Sr loss during high-temperature processing [Reasonable Inference]; impurity issues and narrow phase windows are explicitly reported in the closest Ca/Sr-containing analogue set (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Overall, the material is judged moderately-to-highly synthesizable, with best prospects using arc melting followed by an anneal.
