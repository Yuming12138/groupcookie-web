### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bracket. The closest analogue Gd₃InN and several R₃InN/R₃AlN analogues are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 in the provided data (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1/SS-2/SS-4, TS-1/TS-2/TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for experimentally reported Gd₃SnN, so the score relies on computed hull stability plus analogue stability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses the same synthesis logic as Gd₃InN: RN + R₂In, arc-furnace fusion, evacuated or Ta/quartz ampoule, and 1220 K for one week (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The temperature is moderate for solid-state nitride synthesis, but the need for inert handling, sealed ampoules, multi-step precursor preparation, and air-sensitive GdN/Gd₂Sn makes the severity moderate. The Sn substitution introduces a volatility/stoichiometry uncertainty that is [Reasonable Inference], because CS/SS/TS do not provide direct Sn analogue synthesis.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetics are helped by the very close structural match to Gd₃InN, which has the same Pm-3m topology and nearly identical Gd–N bond length (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). However, RN and intermetallic precursors are not highly active powders, and the analogue requires arc fusion plus one-week annealing (CS-1). Reports of repeated grinding/annealing for some rare-earth analogues justify a moderate kinetic penalty (CS-2 to CS-5).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is based on R₃AlN synthesis from rare-earth metals and AlN in evacuated quartz tubes at 600–800 °C for long durations (SS-4, TS-4). These conditions avoid extreme temperature or pressure but still require sealed/vacuum handling, air-sensitive Gd, and long multi-step processing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for tin nitride stability or direct SnNₓ use, so the route is less certain than Route 1.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Metal + nitride powders can be reactive enough to form R₃AlN perovskite-type phases under long annealing (SS-4, TS-4). For Gd₃SnN, the structural destination is plausible by analogy to R₃AlN and R₃InN (SS-4, CS-1), but conversion from Gd/Sn/SnNₓ/GdN requires more reconstruction than the RN + Gd₂Sn route. The long annealing times up to 800 h in R₃AlN analogues indicate slow ordering/diffusion kinetics (SS-4, TS-4).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 benefits from arc/high-frequency melting precedents in Gd₃InN and Zr₃AlN analogues (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). Its severity is higher because arc melting locally reaches high temperatures, requires pure Ar, and may cause Sn loss [Reasonable Inference]. It also remains multi-step because the melt product likely still needs sealed annealing, as in Gd₃InN (CS-1).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt-assisted processing can improve cation mixing, as shown by arc-fusion in R₃InN synthesis and melt growth of Zr₃AlN (CS-1, SS-5, TS-5). However, GdN is refractory and direct melt crystallization of Gd₃SnN is not supported by CS/SS/TS. The structural mismatch of the Zr₃AlN analogue and the need for post-annealing in Gd₃InN reduce confidence.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple cubic Pm-3m five-atom perovskite cell, matching the simple topology of Gd₃InN and related R₃InN analogues (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-1/SS-2, TS-1/TS-2). It lacks edge-sharing polyhedral complexity in the provided structure description, reducing amorphization risk. The main amorphization/inhomogeneity concern is the size and bonding mismatch between Gd and Sn [Reasonable Inference], but this is mitigated by the existence of closely related ordered R₃MN analogues.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Gd₃SnN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest Gd₃InN analogue is also zero hull (CS-1, TS-1). The analogue support is strong: Gd₃InN has the same rare-earth element, same cubic Pm-3m perovskite topology, nearly identical Gd–N bond length, and a demonstrated RN + R₂In synthesis route (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the preorganized rare-earth nitride/intermetallic route, which should supply both the Gd₆N octahedral framework and the p-block metal sublattice efficiently [Reasonable Inference from CS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is Sn stoichiometry control, because Sn can melt/segregate and no direct Sn analogue synthesis is provided in CS/SS/TS. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but requires careful sealed-ampoule, oxygen-free solid-state processing.
