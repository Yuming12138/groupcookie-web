### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is the strongest possible thermodynamic signal for synthesizability under the given scoring rubric. The closest structural/thermodynamic analogue, Zr2InN, is also E_hull = 0 and shares the H-phase/P6₃/mmc structure (SS1, TS1). HfN is also stable with E_hull = 0 and has nearly identical Hf–N bonding length to the target, supporting the Hf–N sublattice stability (SS2, TS2). The uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or Hg retention during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids very high temperature because the closest H-phase analogue was synthesized at 500–900 °C rather than >1000 °C (SS1, TS1). However, sealed ampoule conditions are required because the analogue used quartz ampoules and the target contains volatile Hg [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS1]. Toxic Hg and long sealed-tube processing lower the F2 score [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Hg will react constructively with Hf/HfN rather than segregating or overpressurizing the capsule [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The H-phase topology is strongly supported by Zr2InN, reducing structural reconstruction risk (SS1, TS1). Kinetics are still slow because the analogue required 750–850 h, and Zr2InN showed mononitride impurity, indicating incomplete reaction risk (SS1, TS1). HfN is refractory, and Hf–N systems often need high-temperature homogenization, as shown by HfN-related treatments at 1500–2100 °C (SS2, TS2). Hg may assist by vapor/liquid transport, but that is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 draws from Hf-rich arc/induction routes that operate near 1400 °C or 1673 K and under Ar/vacuum conditions (CS4, TS5). These temperatures and controlled atmospheres impose significant severity deductions. Hg volatility makes direct open arc melting unsuitable, requiring sealed-capsule modification [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, despite good precedent for Hf-containing high-temperature synthesis, this is a severe route for Hf2HgN.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

High-temperature induction/arc routes can accelerate diffusion and have produced Hf-containing phases in hours, such as Hf2NiP and Hf3P2 analogues (CS4, TS5). However, HfN-related diffusion and homogenization can be very demanding, as HfN systems required 1500–2100 °C and long treatments (SS2, TS2). The target’s H-phase is not directly demonstrated by arc melting; the same-structure route is instead ampoule annealing of Zr2InN (SS1, TS1). The kinetic score is therefore moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

No CS/SS/TS source directly supports thin-film deposition, so the direct evidence score is neutral 50 for CS, SS, and TS. The route uses lower temperature, which helps avoid extreme thermal penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Vacuum/reactive gas, Hg toxicity, and likely post-anneal/capping steps still make it nontrivial [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether the H-phase can crystallize in a film without bulk equilibrium annealing.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Thin-film synthesis may bypass bulk diffusion limitations by depositing Hf, Hg, and N at near-atomic mixing length scales [Reasonable Inference]. However, Hg composition control is expected to be difficult because Hg is volatile [Reasonable Inference]. Since CS/SS/TS provide no thin-film analogue, crystallization into the H-phase remains speculative and is scored conservatively. The route is attractive mainly as a way to manage Hg loss, not because it is strongly evidenced.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The structure is simple H-phase/P6₃/mmc, and the closest analogue Zr2InN is crystalline in the same topology (SS1, TS1), so a large unit-cell or highly frustrated connectivity penalty is not triggered. The target has edge-sharing NHf6 octahedra, not the full coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing required for the highest amorphization trigger (target data; SS1/TS1 by analogy). The main amorphization or disorder risk comes from mismatch between Hf and Hg bonding preferences and possible Hg sublattice disorder [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf2HgN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest H-phase analogue Zr2InN is also calculated stable at E_hull = 0 (SS1, TS1). The structural support is strong: Zr2InN has the same H-phase/P6₃/mmc structure, and HfN provides a very close Hf–N bond-length match to the target (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing stability of the Hf–N framework and the direct H-phase precedent from Zr2InN (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is Hg retention and stoichiometry control during high-temperature treatment [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, but likely requires sealed-capsule chemistry rather than open arc melting.
