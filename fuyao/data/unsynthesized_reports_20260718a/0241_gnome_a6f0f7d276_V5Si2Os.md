### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports equilibrium synthesizability. TS analogues include multiple zero-hull compounds, including V3Sb, V4CP2, V2P, and NbVP (TS-1 to TS-4), indicating that nearby thermodynamic environments contain stable crystalline phases. CS analogues are also low-hull, with V4SiSb2 at 0.005 eV/atom and Nb4CoSi/Nb4FeSi at 0.013/0.048 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee fast kinetics or single-phase isolation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + annealing

Route 1 has strong precedent because CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5 all use arc-melting under Ar followed by sealed-tube annealing to make ternary intermetallics. The severity score is penalized because arc-melting implies very high local temperatures, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, multiple steps, and Os-related toxicity risk [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate-to-poor because elemental V/Os/Si are refractory and dense, but melting/remelting provides intimate alloying and reduces diffusion limitations compared with purely solid-state annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-purity uncertainty remains because CS-1 and CS-5 report improved purity only after long annealing.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Furnace fusion / ceramic alloying

Route 2 is supported by CS-2 and CS-3, where Nb–TM–Si silicides were prepared by fusion under Ar and long annealing in evacuated quartz ampoules. Its condition severity is somewhat lower than arc melting if the process avoids >1400 °C sustained treatment, but it still requires controlled atmosphere/vacuum and long multi-step annealing. Kinetics are worse than Route 1 because Os may not fully alloy without true melting, and CS-2/CS-3 required 90–750 h anneals even for related silicides. The main uncertainty is whether furnace temperatures near 1100–1300 °C are enough to homogenize Os-containing compositions.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Hot pressing / SPS

Route 3 is supported mainly by SS-1, where V5Si3/Nb5Si3-type refractory silicides require high-temperature hot-processing, and by SS-2/TS-1 where compacting plus high-temperature treatment is used for V-rich intermetallics. Its severity is high because it likely requires >1400 °C, controlled atmosphere/vacuum, and specialized pressure equipment [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics benefit from fine powders, pressure, and short diffusion distances, but Os/V refractory diffusion remains limiting [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates hot pressing for an Os-containing V silicide.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed face/edge-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, so one amorphization-risk check is triggered from the target structural description; related edge/face-sharing motifs appear in V3Sb (SS-2, TS-1). The unit cell is not evidently large, and the V–Os metallic size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% [Reasonable Inference], so only one of three checks is triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target radius mismatch and exact target unit-cell size, so those subchecks are treated neutrally where source-specific evidence is absent. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V5Si2Os is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several TS analogues are also zero-hull phases (TS-1 to TS-4). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS analogues showing ternary intermetallics and silicides made from elemental precursors by arc-melting plus annealing under Ar/vacuum (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the predicted thermodynamic stability combined with direct elemental alloying routes for similar refractory intermetallics. The main limiting factor is sluggish homogenization involving refractory Os and V, which likely requires repeated melting and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limitation is oxygen control because Os oxidation products are hazardous and could disturb stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].
