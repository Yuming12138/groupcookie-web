### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the prompt’s high-score cutoff and is close enough to the convex hull to be considered thermodynamically accessible. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values around 0.035–0.088 eV/atom are reported as synthesized, including K₃NiO₂-type and Rb₂S/Cs₂Se-related phases (TS-3, TS-4, TS-2). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee the specific high-Pt-oxidation-state chemistry required for Cs₆PtO₆; insufficient evidence from TS directly addresses Pt(VI) stabilization.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, consistent with Cs₄IrO₄ synthesis at 740 °C/86 d and related sealed Cs-rich oxide syntheses below 900 °C (CS-1–CS-5). The severity penalty comes from sealed volatile cesium oxides, air-sensitive precursors, and multistep thermal handling, all evident in Cs-rich oxide analogues (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1). Insufficient evidence from TS changes the severity estimate for this chemically specific route, so TS is treated as neutral for F2 Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are only moderate because the closest analogue Cs₄IrO₄ required 86 d, showing slow ordering/crystal-growth kinetics in Cs-rich oxometallates (CS-1; SS-1). PtO₂/Pt conversion to an octahedral PtO₆ environment likely requires more reconstruction than the square-planar IrO₄ analogue, which lowers the structural-reconstruction score [Reasonable Inference based on CS-1/SS-1]. Insufficient evidence from TS directly informs solid-state kinetics for Cs–Pt–O formation.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures but requires highly air-sensitive cesium oxides, sealed Pt tubes, and likely multiple precursor/annealing steps, similar to Cs₆Al₂O₆ synthesis from Cs₂O + CsAlO₂ at 680 °C for 6 months (CS-5). The lack of >1000 °C or >5 GPa conditions keeps the severity score from being lower. Insufficient evidence from TS directly supports the specific Cs₂O flux method, so TS is neutral for this route’s severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth is kinetically favored relative to a dry binary oxide reaction because Cs₂O-rich media have produced Cs-rich oxometallates over long anneals (CS-5). A preformed Cs–Pt–O intermediate should reduce the need for full Pt–O network reconstruction, by analogy to CsAlO₂ use in Cs₆Al₂O₆ synthesis [Reasonable Inference based on CS-5]. The remaining uncertainty is high because no SS entry provides the exact Fm-3m Cs₆PtO₆ structure or PtO₆ octahedral Cs₆ framework.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the highest severity because it deliberately imposes a controlled oxidizing sealed/high-pressure environment, inspired only indirectly by pressure/sealed thermodynamic analogues such as Cs₂Se and KNa₂NiO₂ (TS-2, TS-1). It avoids >5 GPa pressure but adds hazards from oxidizers, autogenous pressure, and air-sensitive cesium oxides (CS-1–CS-5; TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS directly validates high-oxygen-pressure Cs–Pt oxide synthesis, so the route remains speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3

The higher oxygen potential may help access Pt(VI), but crystallization at lower temperature may be slow and may require a subsequent anneal [Reasonable Inference]. TS analogues show sealed/high-pressure environments can stabilize alkali-rich phases, but they are not chemically close to Cs–Pt–O and do not prove the target structure (TS-1, TS-2). Therefore, F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large size/charge mismatch between Cs⁺ and Pt⁶⁺ creates substantial local coordination contrast, increasing ordering difficulty [Reasonable Inference from target composition]. The likely large conventional Fm-3m cell and multiple coordination environments suggest medium amorphization/antisite/disorder risk, though the target description itself is highly symmetric. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly quantifies amorphization tendency for Cs₆PtO₆, so this factor has significant uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₆PtO₆ is likely near-stable or weakly metastable rather than deeply metastable, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues with similar or higher E_hull have been synthesized (TS-1–TS-4). The CS/SS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of Cs-rich oxometallate synthesis in sealed noble-metal containers under Ar, especially using reactive cesium oxides and long anneals (CS-1–CS-5; SS-1, SS-2). The most critical enabling factor is a Cs₂O/Cs₂O₂-rich flux that can supply both cesium activity and oxygen activity while enabling slow crystal growth, as seen in Cs₆Al₂O₆ and Cs₄IrO₄ analogues (CS-5, CS-1). The most critical limiting factor is stabilization of Pt in the formal +6 state within PtO₆ octahedra, because no provided CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates a Cs–Pt(VI) oxide. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires careful sealed-tube/flux chemistry and may have a narrow phase window.
