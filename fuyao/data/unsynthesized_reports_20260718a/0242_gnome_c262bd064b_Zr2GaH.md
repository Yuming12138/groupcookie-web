### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is predicted exactly on the hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports synthesizability under the scoring rubric. Close analogues are also stable or nearly stable: Ti3SnH, Ba(GaH)2, Zr6CuBi2, Zr3Te, and Zr3AlN are on the hull, while ZrH is essentially on the hull at ~0.00025 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially for hydrides with pressure-sensitive H content (CS-1, CS-3).

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized for arc melting, controlled Ar/vacuum/H2 or D2 atmospheres, and multiple processing steps, all of which are present in Ti3SnH-type synthesis (CS-1/SS-5/TS-5). Ga/Al hydride analogues also require autoclaves, glovebox handling, and controlled H2 pressure, supporting moderate severity (CS-4, CS-5). The uncertainty is whether Zr2GaH requires high-temperature alloying or whether a lower-temperature intermetallic precursor can be used.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Hydrogen insertion into a pre-formed intermetallic is kinetically more favorable than building the full ternary from elements, as shown by SrAl2H2/Ba(GaH)2 hydrogenation of intermetallics (CS-4, CS-5). However, Ti3SnH shows pressure-dependent polymorphism and sluggish phase transitions, lowering the structural-reconstruction score (CS-1/SS-5/TS-5). The key uncertainty is whether the correct Zr2Ga parent/intermediate exists and accepts exactly one H per formula.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 avoids external GPa pressure and can operate below 1000 °C, making it less severe than high-pressure synthesis. It still requires sealed ampoules, controlled atmosphere, internal hydride handling, and multiple steps, consistent with sealed Zr intermetallic synthesis and hydride precursor routes (SS-3/TS-3; CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). The main uncertainty is autogenous H pressure and Ga containment.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
ZrH2 provides a chemically proximate Zr–H reservoir, and ZrH analogues confirm HZr4 units can form from hydride sources (CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). Kinetics are limited by Zr–Ga ordering and solid-state diffusion, with Zr-rich intermetallic analogues requiring long anneals up to 200 h or high temperatures (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). The uncertainty is whether ZrH2 decomposes too quickly or instead supplies H at the right chemical potential.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 is directly supported by the closest ZrH analogue but is heavily penalized by the 8 GPa requirement (CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). Although the temperature is moderate, the equipment and encapsulation requirements are severe (CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). The uncertainty is whether pressure is necessary for Zr2GaH or only for binary ZrH phases.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
High pressure can enable hydride formation rapidly, as ZrH is synthesized at 873 K for 1 h (CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). The target shares HZr4 tetrahedral motifs with ZrH, improving structural transferability (target description; CS-3/SS-1/TS-1). The added Ga ordering makes this route less direct than binary ZrH synthesis.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
The thin-film route scores well on severity because it avoids high temperature and high pressure by using sputtering and low-temperature annealing [Reasonable Inference]. However, insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS supports this route, so the condition score is not treated as a reliable synthesis recommendation. The main uncertainty is whether crystalline I4/mcm-derived Zr2GaH can form without bulk diffusion.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
Thin films can shorten diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], but there is no CS/SS/TS thin-film analogue for Zr–Ga–H. Bulk analogues show H/D pressure and composition sensitivity, suggesting film composition control may be difficult (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). Thus, the numerical route score is discounted by expert adjustment.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple reported Zr2GaH composition and crystalline I4/mcm symmetry, which supports low-to-medium amorphization risk. Zr2GaH is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close CS/SS/TS analogues are also on the convex hull.
