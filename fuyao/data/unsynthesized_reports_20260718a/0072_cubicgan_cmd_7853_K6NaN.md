### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the top of the provided 90–100 score bracket. TS analogues with similar thermodynamic positions include Cs₂Se at 0.075 eV/atom, K₃NiO₂ at 0.035 eV/atom, KNa₂NiO₂ at 0.078 eV/atom, and Na₃As at 0.043 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4, TS-5). CS/SS give mixed evidence: K₃P is stable on the hull (CS-1), but K₃N is much higher in E_hull and experimentally metastable (CS-3; SS-1). The remaining uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not guarantee kinetic accessibility for an alkali-rich nitride/electride-like phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits demanding UHV and cryogenic deposition conditions from K₃N LT-ABD (CS-3; SS-1). It avoids high temperature and high pressure but requires controlled nitrogen activation, alkali beams, and air-free transfer (CS-3; SS-1). Insufficient evidence from TS for vapor deposition of analogous alkali nitrides, so TS is neutral for this route. The severity score is therefore reduced mainly by UHV/reactive plasma and air-sensitive alkali handling.

#### F3 — Route 1

K₃N was directly made by K/N low-temperature deposition, so the K–N formation kinetics are experimentally supported (CS-3; SS-1). However, the target requires precise simultaneous K/Na/N control and cubic ternary ordering, which is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS and is a [Reasonable Inference]. The K₃N analogue’s metastability, poor crystallinity, and decomposition behavior lower the crystallization score (CS-3; SS-1). Remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic F-43m phase can nucleate during warming rather than segregating into K₃N plus alkali metal [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures by analogy to K₃P and alkali pnictide sealed syntheses (CS-1; TS-5). The major severity penalties are sealed volatile alkali metals, air-sensitive K/Na/K₃N, and possible multistep preparation of K₃N (CS-3; SS-1). CS-1 supports molten alkali routes, while TS-5 supports sealed ampoule alkali pnictide synthesis; however, CS-3/SS-1 contradict bulk feasibility for K₃N by showing LT-ABD was needed. The score is therefore moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 2

K and Na should provide fast liquid-mediated transport at modest temperature [Reasonable Inference], and molten K reactivity is specifically supported by K₃P formation (CS-1). Structural reconstruction is not complete because K₃N already has NK₆ octahedra and nearly identical K–N distances to the target (CS-3; SS-1), but Na ordering into K₆NaN is not directly demonstrated. Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact nitride chemistry, so TS is neutral for nitride-specific kinetics. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether a crystalline K₆NaN phase forms before decomposition or phase separation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is based on high-pressure ammonothermal synthesis of Cs₂Se-II at 100–300 °C and 300–2000 bar NH₃ (TS-1). Although not >5 GPa, the route requires specialized autoclaves, reactive NH₃, volatile reagents, and strict inert handling (TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for ammonothermal alkali nitride synthesis, so those sources are neutral for this route. The condition severity is comparable to other challenging high-pressure solvent syntheses but below diamond-anvil or multi-GPa severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

A liquid/supercritical NH₃ medium should improve mass transport relative to dry solids [Reasonable Inference], and TS-1 demonstrates that high-pressure NH₃ can enable alkali-rich chalcogenide crystal growth. However, no CS/SS/TS analogue shows K–Na–N formation in NH₃, and alkali amide/imide side reactions are likely [Reasonable Inference]. The route therefore receives good diffusion/activity scores but a lower structural-reconstruction score. The uncertainty is high because NH₃ may chemically compete with nitride/electride formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant K/Na cation size contrast, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports a simple cubic F-43m structure with NK₆ octahedra and NaK₁₂ cuboctahedra, and there is no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell (Target). The connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully met (Target). The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₆NaN is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which falls in the favorable stability bracket, but it is not clearly on the hull (Target). The strongest synthesis support comes from the K₃N analogue, which has nearly identical K–N distances and NK₆ octahedral coordination but was only made by low-temperature UHV deposition and was reported metastable/decomposition-prone (CS-3; SS-1). The best enabling factor is the highly reactive alkali-rich environment, especially molten K/Na or atomic-beam alkali fluxes, which can form unusual alkali pnictides/nitrides (CS-1; CS-3; SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is phase control: the target requires a precise K:Na:N = 6:1:1 alkali-rich composition, and the closest K–N phase has difficult stability and crystallinity behavior (CS-3; SS-1). Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require air-free, low-temperature, alkali-rich processing rather than conventional high-temperature ceramic synthesis [Reasonable Inference].
