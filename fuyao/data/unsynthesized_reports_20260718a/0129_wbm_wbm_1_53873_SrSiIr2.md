### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is calculated at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. This is reinforced by close stable analogues: BaIr₂Si₂, EuIr₂Si₂, LaIr₂Si₂, and ThIr₂Si₂ are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 in the supplied data (CS1, CS2, TS1–TS4). The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee the P4/nmm polymorph will be the experimentally selected polymorph under a given thermal history [Reasonable Inference], as polymorphism is specifically noted for LaIr₂Si₂ (TS3).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 1100 °C-class sealed-tube conditions, directly matching the BaIr₂Si₂ synthesis at 1100 °C for 50 h under Ar (CS1/SS1/TS1). It avoids high pressure and exotic atmospheres, but Sr volatility and air sensitivity require sealed/inert handling [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score is therefore moderately favorable at 70.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic penalty is substantial because Ir is refractory and elemental solid-state diffusion is slow [Reasonable Inference]. However, the closest Ba analogue forms by direct solid-state reaction under similar conditions (CS1/SS1/TS1), and the target has strong structural similarity to known P4/nmm Ir–Si analogues (SS2–SS5). The long 50 h hold suggests manageable but nontrivial ordering kinetics (CS1/SS1/TS1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting and annealing are well supported for P4/nmm R/Th/YIr₂Si₂ analogues (SS2–SS5, TS4), but the process is more severe because it involves high-temperature melting, repeated handling, and long annealing. Vacuum or sealed-tube annealing is reported for related Y/La systems (SS4, TS3), and Sr volatility adds additional practical severity [Reasonable Inference]. This gives a lower condition score of 35.

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc melting improves homogenization for refractory Ir-containing intermetallics, and many P4/nmm Ir–Si analogues are made this way (SS2–SS5, TS4). Long anneals near 950 °C for 8 days are still needed for ordering/homogeneity in several analogues (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS4). Thus kinetics are better than sealed solid-state diffusion but still limited by Sr retention and ordering.

#### F2 — Route 3

Induction melting is supported for related 1:2:2 silicides under He or Ar, including Sr-containing precursor sets in Pt–Si systems and Au–Si systems at 1600–1800 °C (CS3, CS4). The high temperature creates severity and Sr-loss risk [Reasonable Inference], but the route avoids high pressure and reactive gases. The condition score is intermediate at 60.

#### F3 — Route 3

Liquid or partial-liquid formation should improve atomic mixing relative to a purely solid-state route [Reasonable Inference], and induction melting is experimentally used for related silicides (CS3, CS4). However, the exact Sr–Ir–Si P4/nmm structure is not directly demonstrated by induction-melt analogues; the strongest P4/nmm evidence comes from arc-melted and annealed R/Th/Y analogues (SS2–SS5, TS4). This gives a moderate kinetics score of 55.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The supplied data do not report amorphous products, so direct amorphization evidence is insufficient from CS and TS and treated as neutral. Structurally, P4/nmm analogues show mixed edge/face/corner-sharing motifs and mixed Ir/Si coordination environments (SS2–SS5), which can raise crystallization complexity. The target is still a small-cell intermetallic 1:2:2 compound, so amorphization risk is medium rather than high [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(IrSi)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the supplied target hull energy is exactly 0.0 eV/atom, and several closely related Ir–Si 1:2:2 compounds are also on the hull (CS1, CS2, TS1–TS4). The analogue support is strong: BaIr₂Si₂ gives the closest chemical route by sealed elemental reaction (CS1/SS1/TS1), while Sm/Nd/Y/Ho/ThIr₂Si₂ give strong P4/nmm structural precedent (SS2–SS5, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ir–Si 1:2:2 framework, which appears across multiple A-site cations and synthesis methods (CS1, SS2–SS5, TS3–TS4). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Sr during high-temperature synthesis because Sr is air-sensitive and more volatile/reactive than many rare-earth/Y/Th analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Phase competition with Sr silicides or Ir silicides is also a likely practical limitation [Reasonable Inference], especially because impurity or narrow phase-window issues are noted in related systems (CS3, CS4, SS4, TS3, TS5).
