### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability scoring range. Chemically similar RbAuO2 and K3(PtO2)2 are reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS), structurally similar K3(PtO2)2 and K3Pd2O4 are also on the hull (SS), and thermodynamic analogues including K3(PtO2)2, K3Pd2O4, Cs4IrO4, and Na2PtO2 are on the hull (TS). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic, because square-planar Pt–O phases can require careful oxygen and alkali stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because Na2PtO2 forms at 500 °C for 2 d (TS), CsBiO2 forms at 700 °C for 50 h under Ar (CS), and K3(PtO2)2-related analogues are associated with ~500 °C sealed capsule conditions (CS/SS/TS). The main penalties arise from sealed-system handling of volatile/reactive Rb oxides and air-sensitive precursors, which is consistent with Ag capsule/bomb use and moisture sensitivity in alkali oxide analogues (CS/SS/TS). The RbAuO2 CS entry gives excellent structural match but insufficient direct precursor signal because its listed synthesis metadata appears inconsistent with Rb/Au chemistry, so its precursor-specific evidence is treated as neutral/insufficient from CS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The target structure is strongly templated by RbAuO2, which has the same Cmcm structure, Rb coordination, square-planar noble-metal oxygen coordination, and mixed ORb3M2 polyhedral sharing (CS). Square-planar Pt–O chemistry is also supported by K3(PtO2)2 and Na2PtO2 (CS/SS/TS). Kinetic uncertainty remains because Pt powder can leave residual unreacted Pt in Na2PtO2 syntheses (TS), and several alkali oxide analogues require days to weeks of annealing or careful phase optimization (CS/SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it intentionally controls oxygen activity, analogous to K3Pd2O4 synthesis under 0.5–0.7 atm O2 in sealed tubes (SS/TS). It still avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because the relevant analogues form at 500–700 °C without external high pressure (SS/TS). The penalties come from reactive oxidizing alkali peroxides/superoxides, sealed-tube pressure, and air-sensitive handling [Reasonable Inference based on KO2/Na2O2 use in SS/TS].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Peroxide/superoxide reagents should improve oxygen transport and precursor activity relative to simple oxides [Reasonable Inference], and K3Pd2O4 demonstrates that KO2/PdO reactions can form square-planar late-transition-metal alkali oxides at 600–700 °C (SS/TS). Na2PtO2 demonstrates direct Pt incorporation from Na2O2 + Pt at 500 °C (TS). The main uncertainty is whether the correct Pt oxidation state and Rb:Pt:O stoichiometry window are broad enough; the Pd analogue requires optimized oxygen pressure, and Na2PtO2 reports phase-purity challenges (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 follows long alkali-rich Ag-bomb/Ag-capsule approaches, so it carries penalties for sealed controlled atmosphere, air-sensitive alkali oxides, and multi-step processing. Cs4IrO4 requires 740 °C for 86 d in an Ag-bomb under protective gas (SS/TS), K3LiIrO4 requires 71 d with controlled heating/cooling in an Ag cylinder/silica tube (SS/TS), and Cs/Rb/Pb oxides require 14–15 d in Ag capsules under dried Ar (CS). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a flux route is necessary for RbPtO2 specifically, so this route is scored as plausible but not preferred.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

An alkali-rich flux can improve diffusion and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], and long Ag-bomb syntheses successfully produce crystalline Cs4IrO4 and K3LiIrO4 despite refractory noble-metal chemistry (SS/TS). However, the same analogues show very long annealing times and narrow condition windows, implying slow ordering kinetics and significant optimization burden (SS/TS). The route is therefore kinetically viable but experimentally demanding.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains large Rb and much smaller square-planar Pt centers, so the cation-size mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target also explicitly has mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedra, and RbAuO2 has the same type of mixed sharing (CS), so the structural-complexity check is triggered. Exact target cell atom count is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, but the simple Cmcm ABO2 description suggests it is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference].

RbPtO2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close alkali noble-metal oxide analogues are also on the hull (CS/SS/TS). The analogue support is strong: RbAuO2 is essentially isostructural in Cmcm with matching Rb–O coordination and square-planar M–O geometry (CS), while K3(PtO2)2 and Na2PtO2 demonstrate accessible square-planar Pt–O chemistry in alkali oxide frameworks (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is experimental control of air-sensitive/volatile Rb oxides and the oxygen chemical potential needed to stabilize RbPtO2 rather than Pt metal, PtO2, or competing Rb–Pt–O phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is meaningfully synthesizable but likely requires sealed-capsule alkali oxide expertise.
