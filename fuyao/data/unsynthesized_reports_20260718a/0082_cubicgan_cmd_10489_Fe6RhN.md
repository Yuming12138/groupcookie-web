### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the favorable threshold for likely synthesizability under the provided scoring rubric. Chemically and thermodynamically similar Fe-rich nitrides such as Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃, Nd₂Fe₁₇N₃, and Pr₂Fe₁₇N₃ have E_hull values near 0.062–0.063 eV/atom and are reported as synthesized, supporting tolerance for small positive hull energies in this chemistry (CS; TS). Structurally similar carbides have even lower E_hull values but differ chemically and structurally, so SS provides supportive but not decisive evidence. The main uncertainty is that the target is cubic Fe₆RhN, while most analogues are trigonal rare-earth Fe-rich nitrides/carbides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is conditionally severe because closest nitride analogues require multi-step parent alloy preparation, crushing, and high-pressure N₂ nitridation at 15 MPa (CS; TS). It avoids GPa pressure and uses only moderate nitridation temperature, but the high-pressure gas autoclave and controlled atmosphere reduce the F2 score. Kinetically, nitrogen insertion into a pre-alloyed framework is favorable compared with full solid-state cation diffusion, as shown by RE₂Fe₁₇N₃ nitridation at 250–350 °C or ~460 °C (CS; TS). The uncertainty is whether Fe–Rh forms a parent framework that can accept N into the exact cubic Fe₆RhN structure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids the 15 MPa autoclave, but still needs parent alloying, powder activation, controlled purified N₂, and careful temperature control (CS; TS). Its kinetic score is lower because flowing-N₂ nitride analogues can suffer from incomplete nitrogenation and metal precipitation, as reported for Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ (CS). Thermogravimetric monitoring is a strong mitigation strategy because Pr₂Fe₁₇N₃ synthesis used direct mass-gain monitoring to control nitrogen uptake (CS; TS). Remaining uncertainty is high for Rh-containing Fe nitrides because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly contains Rh.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is inspired by structurally similar Fe-rich carbides and thermodynamic Fe-rich intermetallics made by arc melting, annealing, quenching, and sometimes melt-spinning (SS; TS). The severity is moderate-to-high due to high-temperature melting, controlled atmosphere, and multiple processing steps. Kinetics are challenging because SS carbides required very long anneals and still showed impurity phases, indicating slow ordering and narrow phase windows (SS). Rapid quenching may help trap metastable phases, but this is an extrapolation for Fe₆RhN [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has low thermal severity and avoids bulk diffusion limitations, so F2 is high by the scoring rubric [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS/SS/TS provide no direct thin-film precedent, so the route relies on the general ability of reactive sputtering/PLD to access metastable nitrides [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may be favorable for cation mixing because Fe and Rh are co-deposited atomistically, but crystallization and nitrogen stoichiometry are uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The route is best viewed as a discovery/metastability route rather than a proven bulk route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only two cations, Fe and Rh, and no provided evidence indicates a very large unit cell or edge-sharing/vertex-sharing/face-sharing complexity all coexisting in the target. The target description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not activated. The low amorphization score risk is consistent with a compact cubic intermetallic nitride framework [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty comes from the absent full CIF atom count in the prompt.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Fe₆RhN at E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom is best regarded as a near-hull or mildly metastable phase rather than an obviously unstable compound. CS and TS evidence strongly supports that Fe-rich nitrides with similar Fe–N bond lengths and small positive hull energies can be synthesized by parent-alloy nitridation. SS evidence supports the broader feasibility of Fe-rich intermetallic frameworks but also warns that impurity phases and long ordering times are common in related structures. The most critical enabling factor is controlled nitrogen insertion into a pre-formed Fe–Rh metallic framework, ideally using high N₂ activity or reactive thin-film growth. The most critical limiting factor is the absence of a direct Rh-containing nitride analogue and the structural mismatch between cubic Fe₆RhN and the trigonal RE₂Fe₁₇N₃/RE₂Fe₁₇C₃ analogues.
