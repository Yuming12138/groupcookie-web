### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

KAuO is calculated at **E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom**, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. The closest SS/TS analogues KAgO, KCuO, NaAgO, CsAgO, and Na₃AuO₂ are all reported as hull-stable phases, reinforcing that alkali coinage-metal oxides can be true ground-state compounds (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). CS analogues also include several hull-stable K–main-group oxides, but they do not directly address Au chemistry; insufficient Au-specific evidence from CS, neutral Au-chemistry signal = 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate-to-high but not extreme temperature, because KAgO was synthesized at 800 °C then 600 °C rather than >1000 °C (SS1, TS1). It requires sealed metal capsules and strictly dry Ar because KAgO was made in Ag bombs under dry argon (SS1, TS1). The main severity penalties are alkali oxide air sensitivity and sealed-system requirements **[Reasonable Inference based on SS1/TS1]**.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

KAgO being isostructural with KAuO strongly supports a favorable structural pathway (SS1, TS1). However, KAgO required long annealing, 7 d at 800 °C plus 35 d at 600 °C, which indicates slow ordering or diffusion in this structure family (SS1, TS1). Au metal as a dense source may further slow reaction relative to oxide precursors **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is less thermally severe because the Au analogue Na₃AuO₂ forms at 430 °C over 6 d in a sealed Ag cylinder (SS2, TS2). The penalties arise from sealed handling and air-sensitive alkali auride/peroxide chemistry **[Reasonable Inference supported by SS2/TS2]**. No high pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required by the analogue evidence (SS2, TS2).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

NaAu + Na₂O₂ provides direct evidence that auride/peroxide precursors can form Au–O units at relatively low temperature (SS2, TS2). The target structure itself is better matched by KAgO/KCuO I4/mmm analogues than by Na₃AuO₂, so some reconstruction and composition tuning are expected (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The remaining uncertainty is the stability and preparation of KAu relative to NaAu **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from low-to-moderate temperatures because direct oxide analogues KCuO and CsAgO form near 580–600 °C or below (SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5). It is penalized more strongly because Au(I) oxide precursors likely require separate preparation and low-temperature sealed handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. Provided CS/SS/TS sources do not directly demonstrate K₂O + Au₂O synthesis, so direct Au₂O evidence is insufficient and scored neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Direct oxide reaction is kinetically plausible by analogy to KCuO from K₂O + Cu₂O (SS3, TS3). However, Ag oxide systems can produce metallic Ag impurities if conditions are not controlled, suggesting Au metal impurity risk for an Au oxide route (SS5, TS5). Au(I) oxide instability is the largest kinetic/chemical uncertainty **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ordered tetragonal motif and close analogues are crystalline ordered phases, lowering amorphization risk (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Only the cation size/coordination mismatch check is triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The mixed edge/corner-sharing connectivity does not meet the specified face+edge+vertex coexistence criterion, so the amorphization score remains favorable (SS1, TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KAuO is likely a **ground-state phase** because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and multiple close alkali coinage-metal oxide analogues are also hull-stable (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The analogue support is unusually strong: KAgO is essentially the same I4/mmm structure type, while Na₃AuO₂ provides direct evidence for accessible Au–O linear coordination in alkali oxide chemistry (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the structural match to KAgO, including nearly identical K–O coordination and linear noble-metal–O geometry (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Au(I) formation without reducing to Au metal or forming other potassium aurates **[Reasonable Inference]**, a concern supported indirectly by Ag metal impurity issues in CsAgO synthesis and decomposition sensitivity in Na₃AuO₂ (SS2, SS5, TS2, TS5). Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but it will require rigorous air-free sealed-capsule alkali oxide practice.
