### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase by the supplied target data. Close analogues Hf3AlN and Zr3AlN are also on the hull and share the Cmcm nitride framework (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). Hf–transition-metal analogues such as Hf2NiP and Hf6NiSb2 also include hull-stable Hf–late-transition-metal compounds (CS3, SS3, SS5, TS3–4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed 0 K stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or nitrogen stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires arc/RF melting or high-temperature melting, supported by Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN melt growth and Hf2CoP/Hf2NiP arc melting (CS1–3, SS1–2, SS5, TS1–4). The main severity penalties come from >1000 °C, likely >1400 °C local melting, multiple operations, and Hf/Co powder handling risks [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for high pressure, so that factor is neutral/no deduction. The route is demanding but standard for refractory intermetallic nitrides and pnictides (CS1–3, SS1–2, SS5, TS1–4).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is melt assistance, because analogues are successfully made by melting or arc-melting rather than relying only on solid diffusion (CS1–3, SS1–2, SS5, TS1–4). The main kinetic limitation is that Hf and HfN are refractory and may dissolve/react slowly unless fully molten or finely dispersed [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN share the Cmcm framework and NHf6/NZr6 octahedral motifs with the target (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). Uncertainty remains because Co is chemically different from Al and may stabilize competing Hf–Co intermetallics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses sealed ampoules or Ta tubes, long anneals, and controlled vacuum/Ar conditions, consistent with CS5 and Hf2CoP/Hf2NiP annealing protocols (CS2, CS3, CS5, SS5, TS4). It avoids arc melting but still exceeds 1000 °C and requires controlled atmosphere/sealed handling (CS5; CS2, CS3, SS5, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no need for high pressure or volatile sealed precursors beyond nitrogen control. The route is more severe than ordinary ceramic firing because of refractory-metal ampoules and air-sensitive Hf powder handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable because Hf, HfN, and Co must interdiffuse without a liquid phase [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing in rare-earth nitride analogues and high-temperature annealing in Hf2CoP/Hf2NiP support the need for extended thermal treatment (CS5; CS2, CS3, SS5, TS4). Structural formation is plausible because HfN supplies Hf–N units and the target contains NHf6 octahedra [Reasonable Inference], but forming the ordered Cmcm ternary from binaries may require substantial reconstruction. The uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports Hf3CoN from HfN/Hf/Co.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Reactive sputtering avoids the very high temperatures of bulk melting, as TS5 demonstrates Ta2N deposition at low substrate temperature in Ar/N2. The main severity penalty is the need for high-vacuum/reactive-gas control, including N2 partial pressure and plasma parameters (TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure, volatile sealed precursors, or >1000 °C operation for the thin-film route. The route is operationally specialized but not thermally harsh [Reasonable Inference; TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor-phase delivery bypasses slow bulk diffusion, and Hf/Co targets plus N2 are feasible sputtering inputs [Reasonable Inference], analogous to Ta + N2 reactive sputtering for Ta2N (TS5). The crystallization pathway is uncertain because TS5 supports binary Ta2N, not ternary Cmcm Hf3CoN; a post-deposition anneal may be required [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is the limiting kinetic/process factor because Hf:Co:N = 3:1:1 must be maintained and TS5 shows nitride phase formation depends sensitively on N2 partial pressure. Thus F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed Hf and Co cations with a significant size/chemistry contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target framework is not overly complex and is closely related to crystallized Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN Cmcm analogues (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). The octahedra are corner and edge sharing, not simultaneous corner/edge/face sharing, matching the lower-complexity analogue descriptions (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). Remaining uncertainty is greater for sputtered films, where low-temperature deposition can produce amorphous material if annealing is insufficient [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf3CoN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple close Hf/Zr nitride analogues are also hull-stable (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). The strongest synthesis support comes from the isostructural Cmcm Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN analogues, which show that the Hf3MN nitride framework can be realized by melt-based methods (CS1, SS1–2, TS1–2). The most critical enabling factor is the structural match: the target and Hf3AlN share two inequivalent Hf sites, 8-coordinate metal sites, and NHf6 octahedral connectivity (CS1, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is nitrogen and phase control in the presence of Co, because Co may favor Hf–Co intermetallics or N-deficient products [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible, with thin-film sputtering offering the gentlest route and arc-melting offering the strongest direct structural precedent.
