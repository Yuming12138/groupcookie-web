### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic driving force is highly favorable relative to competing phases in the calculation (Target). Several chemically, structurally, or thermodynamically similar nitrides also have E_hull = 0, including Hf₂N₂S, HfN, ZrN, U₂MnN₃, and Ce₃Ni₂B₂N₃ (CS2, CS4; SS1, SS2, SS4; TS1, TS2, TS3). The uncertainty is that calculated 0 eV/atom stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or tolerance to off-stoichiometry, which is a known issue in HfN/ZrN analogues (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because HfN and ZrN analogues require 1500–2200 °C treatments and long homogenization under controlled atmospheres (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). U₂MnN₃ supports nitride + transition-metal synthesis in N₂, but still at 1200–1600 °C (CS4). The main uncertainty is whether Hf₂NiN₂ can form at the lower end of this range or whether it decomposes/segregates into HfN + Ni before ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The precursor activity is low-to-moderate because HfN and Ni are refractory solids, and HfN analogues require long high-temperature homogenization (SS1; TS1). Ion diffusion is difficult because the Hf–N framework is robust and Hf-containing nitride phases show narrow homogeneity ranges and slow equilibration (SS1; TS1). Structural reconstruction is nontrivial because the target has HfN₅ square pyramids and linear Ni–N coordination, not the simple rocksalt HfN octahedral network (SS1/TS1; Target).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from the Hf₂N₂S topochemical precedent at 750 °C, much milder than HfN sintering (CS1, CS2 versus SS1/TS1). It is penalized for vacuum sealing, halide volatility/corrosion risk, precursor preparation, and air-sensitive reductants, all consistent with sealed topochemical/metathesis practice (CS1, CS2; SS4; TS3). The main uncertainty is that no source directly demonstrates Ni insertion into α-HfNBr-derived Hf–N slabs; that transfer is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable relative to direct sintering because α-HfNBr reacts topochemically to Hf₂N₂S at 750 °C, implying a chemically labile Hf–N halide host (CS1, CS2). Ce₃Ni₂B₂N₃ shows that Ni-containing nitrides/boronitrides can be accessed by salt-forming metathesis at 1040 °C (SS4; TS3). Remaining uncertainty is the simultaneous need to reduce/insert Ni while maintaining the Hf₂N₂ framework and avoiding HfN, Ni, or nickel nitride impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the mildest thermal conditions by design, using deposition/annealing below the high temperatures required for HfN/ZrN sintering (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2; [Reasonable Inference]). It is penalized for reactive plasma/nitriding atmosphere, but avoids sealed halides, Li/Na reductants, and >1400 °C bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The key caveat is that CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for thin-film synthesis, so this route receives an expert downward adjustment.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Thin-film deposition improves apparent kinetics by mixing Hf and Ni at nanometer length scales and supplying activated nitrogen through plasma [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct crystallization of Hf₂NiN₂, so crystallization and ordering are only moderately scored. Composition control is likely limiting because refractory nitride analogues show phase and stoichiometry sensitivity, including narrow homogeneity and nitrogen-loss issues (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines dissimilar cation environments, with Hf in HfN₅ square pyramids and Ni in linear NiN₂ coordination, so cation-size/coordination mismatch is a risk [Reasonable Inference from Target]. The structure also contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, a feature present in the target and related nitride networks such as Ce₃Ni₂B₂N₃ and HfN analogues (Target; SS4; SS1/TS1). The small formula and high-symmetry I4/mmm description reduce amorphization risk, but thin-film or low-temperature synthesis may still require annealing to achieve long-range cation order [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂NiN₂ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The strongest direct chemical support comes from Hf₂N₂S topochemical synthesis using α-HfNBr and from HfN stability, while the strongest Ni-containing structural support comes from I4/mmm Ce₃Ni₂B₂N₃ made by metathesis (CS1, CS2; SS1, SS4; TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the robust and well-precedented Hf–N bonding network, with Hf–N distances in HfN close to those in the target (SS1/TS1; Target). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: HfN-type refractory phases equilibrate slowly, and Hf₂N₂S topochemistry shows sensitivity to heating rate and phase window (SS1/TS1; CS1, CS2). Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with the best evidence-backed bulk option being topochemical/metathesis chemistry and the highest-scoring exploratory option being reactive thin-film deposition.
