### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which directly supports a very high F1 score. Multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar intermetallics are also on or very near the hull, including TaCo₂Al, HfCu₂In, MgRh, LaRh₃Ga₂, LiRh₂Ge, and ScRh (CS4, CS5, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3, SS4/TS4). The remaining uncertainty is computational: a 0 K hull point does not guarantee easy ordering or absence of competing finite-temperature phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

Arc melting is strongly supported by Heusler and Rh intermetallic analogues (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS4). The severity score is penalized because arc melting a Ta-containing alloy implies very high local temperatures and because Mg volatility demands careful containment or post-melt annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The route is practical in a metallurgy lab, but Mg loss and inhomogeneity make the conditions relatively severe compared with low-temperature sealed sintering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

Kinetically, arc melting improves mixing and partially overcomes diffusion barriers, as used for Hf/Ni/In, Ta/Co/Al, and Rh-containing alloys (CS1, CS4, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS4). The target’s Heusler-like bcc coordination matches several Heusler analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5), supporting structural reconstruction. Remaining uncertainty is whether Mg remains at the correct stoichiometry during the melt and whether Ta fully homogenizes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed-tube sintering

The condition severity is moderate because MgRh was made at only 793–873 K in sealed Ar tubes, and TaAlNi₂-type powder sintering used 700–900 °C (SS1/TS1, CS2). Deductions arise from Mg volatility, the need for sealed-tube handling, and repeated grinding/annealing (SS1/TS1). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that a single-step low-temperature sinter can directly form Mg₃TaRh₄, so the score is not higher.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube sintering

Solid-state kinetics are the main weakness because Ta and Rh are refractory/dense metals, while Mg is low-melting and volatile [Reasonable Inference]. MgRh required week-scale annealing and reprocessing to become essentially single phase (SS1/TS1), and sintered Ta-containing analogues required 100–240 h (CS2). The structural similarity to MgRh and Heusler analogues helps, but long-range ternary ordering is likely slow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Mg-rich flux

Flux growth has significant handling demands because LiRh₂Ge required glovebox loading, welded Nb tubes, evacuated silica sheaths, and post-reaction sublimation (SS3/TS3). A Mg-rich analogue would similarly require sealed-tube pressure management and inert handling [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids the extreme local temperatures of arc melting but introduces volatile-flux hazards [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Mg-rich flux

Flux or liquid-mediated growth should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated for LiRh₂Ge and Ho₃Si₂Sn₃Rh₉ Rh intermetallic crystals (SS3/TS3, SS5/TS5). This is helpful for forming ordered Rh-rich structures, but the lack of a direct Mg–Ta–Rh flux analogue leaves uncertainty. The largest kinetic uncertainty is Ta dissolution/reactivity in a Mg-rich melt [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple intermetallic coordination description: Mg, Ta, and Rh sites are described mainly as body-centered-cubic environments, which argues against a high amorphization tendency. A size mismatch among Mg/Ta/Rh triggers one risk point [Reasonable Inference], but the target description does not indicate mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks. CS3 shows mixed polyhedral connectivity for UFe₆Ga₆, but that feature is not present in the target description, so it does not strongly increase target risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₃TaRh₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom. The analogue support is fairly strong: chemically similar Ta-containing Heuslers and Hf/In/Ni or Hf/In/Cu Heuslers are synthesized by arc melting/annealing (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5), while the closest Mg–Rh analogue MgRh is synthesized by sealed-tube sintering (SS1/TS1). The most important enabling factor is the Heusler-like bcc local coordination, which is repeatedly represented among successful analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, SS1/TS1). The most important limiting factor is the combination of volatile Mg with refractory Ta/Rh, creating a conflict between Mg retention and high-temperature homogenization [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, but phase-pure bulk material will likely require careful sealed processing and stoichiometry control.
