### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is set to 100. CS and TS provide supportive examples of high-valent fluoride analogues that are also on the hull, including SrNiF6 (CS, TS), while SS gives a very close Cu structural analogue CsCuF4 that is only 0.048 eV/atom above hull (SS). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or air stability, especially for high-valent fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that SrCu2F8 has been experimentally isolated, so the stability claim is computational rather than experimental.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because the closest structural analogue CsCuF4 required high-pressure F2 at ~350–360 bar and 400 °C in a Monel autoclave (SS), while chemically similar SrNiF6 required 140 atm F2/N2 at 280 °C (CS, TS). The temperature is not high by ceramic standards, but F2 pressure, toxicity, and moisture sensitivity are major practical penalties (SS, CS, TS). The route is nonetheless feasible because these exact classes of compounds were synthesized under similar pressure-fluorination conditions (SS, CS, TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a low-pressure SrCu2F8 analogue, so no severity reduction is justified.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 receives a strong kinetics score because fluorinating a pre-assembled Sr–Cu halide should require less cation redistribution than starting from fully separated binary fluorides [Reasonable Inference], and CsCuF4 was obtained from a pre-existing CsCuCl3 precursor (SS). The low synthesis temperatures in CsCuF4 and SrNiF6 indicate that high fluorine chemical potential can drive oxidation/fluorination without extended high-temperature diffusion (SS, CS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether a suitable Sr–Cu chloride precursor with the right Sr:Cu = 1:2 ratio can be prepared and fluorinated without phase separation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids a separate halide precursor synthesis, so it is operationally simpler than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. However, it still requires toxic F2 or F2/N2 under pressure, because high-valent SrNiF6 and square-planar CsCuF4 were synthesized by pressure fluorination (CS, SS, TS). The binary fluoride solid-state analogues show sealed high-temperature fluoride reactions can work (CS), but they do not remove the need for a strong oxidizing fluorine atmosphere for Cu(III) [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports forming SrCu2F8 from SrF2/CuF2 under inert atmosphere alone, so that option is not scored as a mild route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary fluoride precursors are chemically compatible with the product, and CS includes successful binary-fluoride routes for Ba2Ni7F18 and CaFeF5 (CS). The kinetic penalty is that SrF2 and CuF2 must reorganize into a specific I422 Sr–Cu–F framework and oxidize copper to Cu(III), whereas Route 1 may preserve more local Sr–Cu mixing [Reasonable Inference]. Long sealed-tube treatments for CaFeF5 and Ba2Ni7F18 indicate that fluoride solid-state crystallization can be slow or require controlled conditions (CS). The F2 atmosphere helps oxidation but may not fully solve cation-ordering limitations [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is the harshest because high-pressure analogues LiCaNiF6 and LiSrNiF6 require 50 kbar and 300 °C under dry Ar, with glovebox loading, Pt sheets, and Ni rings (SS, TS). It also inherits the prior fluorination hazard needed to make an oxidized Sr–Cu fluoride precursor [Reasonable Inference based on CS/SS/TS]. The route is therefore penalized for both static high pressure and multi-step processing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS shows that SrCu2F8 specifically needs 5 GPa pressure, so this route is considered a contingency rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

If a near-target SrCu2F8 powder is already available, pressure annealing may only need to rearrange or select the tetragonal polymorph, giving favorable reconstruction kinetics [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS high-pressure analogues form at only 300 °C, suggesting pressure-assisted fluoride rearrangements can be kinetically accessible (SS, TS). However, the closest Cu structural analogue CsCuF4 was not made by static 5 GPa annealing but by F2 autoclave fluorination (SS), so transferability is weaker. The route may be useful for phase purification or polymorph control, but not as the first synthesis attempt [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant size/coordination mismatch between Sr and Cu, reflected by Sr–F = 2.53 Å and Cu–F = 1.79 Å in the target structure; analogous large A-site/square-planar Cu size mismatch is also seen in CsCuF4 with Cs–F = 2.99 Å and Cu–F = 1.75 Å (SS). This triggers one amorphization-risk criterion, but the target formula unit is small and the described connectivity does not show explicit mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40 atom unit cell or strongly mixed connectivity for SrCu2F8, so those checks are treated as neutral. The amorphization risk is therefore low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCu2F8 is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong because the closest structural analogue, CsCuF4, contains square-planar CuF4 units and was experimentally made by high-pressure F2 fluorination (SS), while chemically and thermodynamically similar high-valent fluorides such as SrNiF6 were also made by pressure fluorination (CS, TS). The key enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, which is needed to stabilize Cu in a high oxidation state and form short square-planar Cu–F bonds [Reasonable Inference based on SS/CS/TS]. The main limiting factor is practical: F2 pressure fluorination requires specialized Monel equipment and the products/analogues are moisture sensitive or difficult to obtain phase-pure (SS, CS, TS). Overall, the material appears synthesizable in an expert fluorine-chemistry laboratory, but not by routine solid-state ceramic methods.
