### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest thermodynamic score band from the provided target data. TS strongly supports the plausibility of ground-state chloride frameworks because all top TS analogues listed have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including MoCl₅, OsCl₄, FeCl₂, Tl₂PtCl₆, and ReTeSeCl₄ (TS-1 to TS-5). CS is mostly supportive because CuAlCl₄/CuGaCl₄ analogues are near-hull or on-hull (CS-1 to CS-3), but CS-5 is thermodynamically poor and is treated as a weak/contradictory signal. SS is structurally useful but thermodynamically less supportive because top Co-containing structural analogues sit around 0.12 eV/atom above hull (SS-1, SS-2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The severity score is moderate because the route uses vacuum/sealed-tube conditions and toxic/moisture-sensitive cobalt halides [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the need for evacuated/sealed ampoules in CS-2, CS-3, and TS-5. Temperature is not extreme because analogous halide materials form between ~250 °C and 900 °C (CS-2, CS-3, SS-1). Kinetics are favorable relative to oxide ceramics because anhydrous halides are reactive and can partially melt or transport at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference], but CoF₂ incorporation may be slower than chloride equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. The edge-sharing octahedral framework is supported by FeCl₂ and TlCoCl₃ analogues (TS-3, SS-1), lowering structural-reconstruction difficulty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Bridgman / melt growth

The Bridgman route has similar condition severity to Route 1 because it requires sealed evacuated ampoules and volatile halide containment, as directly shown for TlCoCl₃ at 900 °C (SS-1). It has the best kinetics because the melt provides high ion mobility and enables slow ordering/crystal growth, consistent with TlCoCl₃ Bridgman growth and CuAlCl₄ melt-growth behavior (SS-1, CS-1). The major uncertainty is whether CoF₂ dissolves sufficiently in the CoCl₂-rich melt and whether F orders into the unique F site rather than forming CoF₂/CoCl₂ impurities [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is favored because CuAlCl₄ polymorph formation depends strongly on quench vs slow cooling (CS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: CVT / temperature-gradient ampoule

CVT is more severe because it usually requires a pre-reaction step, evacuated ampoules, transport agents, and controlled thermal gradients, all of which are evident in Co₂TeO₃Cl₂ CVT and chloride CVT analogues (SS-2, CS-4, SS-3). Kinetics can be excellent for crystal growth because vapor transport bypasses bulk diffusion and deposits crystals in a temperature gradient, as shown for Co₂TeO₃Cl₂ and Mo/Se chloride phases (SS-2, SS-3). However, the target contains F, and the provided CVT examples do not establish a Co–Cl–F transport equilibrium; this is the largest uncertainty for Route 3 [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, Route 3 is better viewed as a single-crystal optimization route after Route 1 or 2 identifies the phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Co, so cation-size mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described framework uses edge-sharing Co octahedra without simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so topological frustration is low from the target structural description. The simple Co₄Cl₇F formula suggests a modest cell size, although the exact unit-cell atom count is not provided; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the target cell count and are neutral on this subcheck. Overall amorphization risk is low, especially for melt or sealed-ampoule crystallization rather than rapid quenching.

The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally related edge-sharing octahedral halides, especially TlCoCl₃ and FeCl₂, which validate Co/transition-metal chloride octahedral frameworks and Bridgman-type growth (SS-1, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility of halide melts or sealed-tube halide systems, which should allow Co–Cl/F ordering during slow cooling [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is the absence of a direct Co–Cl–F analogue in the provided data, so fluoride incorporation and Cl/F stoichiometry control remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference].
