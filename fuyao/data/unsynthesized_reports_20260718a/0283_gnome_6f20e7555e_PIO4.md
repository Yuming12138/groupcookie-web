### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom places it on the predicted convex hull, giving a high F1 score. Similar stable oxoanion frameworks include Al(IO3)3 and Pb(O3Cl)2 at E_hull = 0.0 in CS-5/CS-4 and several Te/Se/P oxoanion analogues at E_hull = 0.0 in SS/TS, including Zn2Te3O8, Mg2Te3O8, and PdAg2P2O7 (SS-4, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The main uncertainty is that CS also includes I(NO3)2 with E_hull ≈0.307 eV/atom (CS-1), so iodine oxoanion chemistry can include metastable compositions.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Hydrothermal

Hydrothermal synthesis uses mild-to-moderate temperatures and standard Teflon-lined autoclaves, directly supported by CS-5 for iodate chemistry and by SS-1/SS-3/TS-4 for oxoanion frameworks. Kinetics are favorable because soluble oxoacid precursors can assemble in solution rather than requiring refractory solid diffusion, consistent with CS-5 and TS-4. Remaining uncertainty is the target’s unusual I square-planar environment, which is not directly reproduced in the closest iodate CS-5 analogue.

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state

Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis is supported by TS-5 for phosphate oxides and SS-5 for vacuum-sealed oxide frameworks. The penalty is significant because vacuum sealing, volatile iodine oxide control, hygroscopic P4O10, and possible multi-step grinding/drying are required (TS-5, SS-5, [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics are acceptable but not ideal because the target likely requires complete reconstruction from molecular/oxide precursors and long annealing analogous to TS-5.

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Solution crystallization / dehydration

Solution crystallization is supported by CS-1, and slow evaporation/dehydration of halate-type oxoanion phases is supported by CS-4. The conditions are mild, but dehydration/hydration control is a significant uncertainty because CS-4 required vacuum, ethanol extraction, and glove-box handling for some halates. Kinetically, precursor mixing is excellent, but formation of an anhydrous extended PIO4 phase from solution may be less direct than hydrothermal crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a large mismatch between tetrahedral P and larger iodine-centered coordination, which triggers the cation-radius/polyhedral-size criterion [Reasonable Inference]. However, the formula is simple and the structure description indicates P–O–I bridging without explicit mixed edge/face connectivity, so only one amorphization trigger is assigned. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact unit-cell atom count or edge/face-sharing topology of the target, so those checks are treated conservatively.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PIO4 is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS-5, which shows hydrothermal synthesis of iodine oxoanion frameworks, and from SS/TS hydrothermal analogues showing that complex oxoanion networks can crystallize at 170–230 °C under autogenous pressure. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of soluble, reactive phosphate and iodine oxoacid precursors, which should allow low-temperature assembly rather than refractory diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is control of iodine oxidation state and iodine/oxygen volatility, especially because the target contains an unusual square-planar I–O coordination not directly matched by the CS/SS/TS examples. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable, with hydrothermal acidic crystallization as the preferred first attempt.
