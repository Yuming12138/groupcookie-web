### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability band and justifies F1 = 98. Chemically and thermodynamically similar materials such as RbBiF₆, RbAuF₄, Ba(AuF₄)₂, CsRuF₆, Ag₂F₅, K₂NiF₆, and LiAuF₄ also include several E_hull = 0 phases, supporting the plausibility of stable complex fluorides (CS/SS/TS). The uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility under laboratory F₂ conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for experimentally reported CsAuF₆ specifically, so that absence is treated as neutral rather than destabilizing.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C temperatures and multi-GPa pressure, since analogues are synthesized at 300–400 °C or 350 °C in F₂ (CS/SS/TS). The main severity penalties are the use of F₂ and toxic/air-sensitive Au/fluoride precursors, which are explicitly present in RbAuF₄, CsRuF₆, and RbBiF₆ syntheses (CS/SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from SS for the exact CsAuF₆ direct-fluorination window is treated as neutral, but SS still supports Au fluoride fluorination through RbAuF₄ (SS). The resulting severity is challenging but not extreme because no sealed high-pressure autoclave is required.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The route uses reactive chlorides/Au chlorides and F₂, and analogues form in short treatments such as 4 h for RbAuF₄ and 2 h for CsRuF₆ (CS/SS/TS). The target structure is strongly supported by CsRuF₆, which has the same CsF₁₂ cuboctahedra/MF₆ octahedra in R-3m-type connectivity (CS). However, Au must reach a higher formal oxidation state in CsAuF₆ than in RbAuF₄-type AuF₄ phases [Reasonable Inference], so the structural/oxidation reconstruction is not trivial. Insufficient evidence from TS for a direct CsAuF₆ kinetic pathway is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because it uses sealed/autoclave F₂ at 200–350 bar, following Cs₂CuF₆ and LiAuF₄ precedents (SS/TS). It still avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa conditions, so the formal pressure deduction for multi-GPa synthesis does not apply. The route incurs deductions for reactive atmosphere, sealed volatile/reactive fluorides, multistep handling, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents, all consistent with the reported high-pressure fluoride syntheses (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS for high-pressure CsAuF₆ specifically is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Using CsF plus an Au fluoride precursor should reduce anion-exchange burden relative to chlorides [Reasonable Inference], and analogous Cs₂CuF₆ is obtained from CsF + CuF₃ in F₂/Ar autoclave conditions (SS). High-pressure F₂ increases oxidizing power, which is favorable for high-valent fluoride formation [Reasonable Inference], and LiAuF₄ autoclave treatment shows Au fluoride systems tolerate 350 °C/200 bar F₂ processing (TS). The kinetic downside is long annealing: Cs₂CuF₆ required 5 weeks and had reported difficulty in obtaining pure samples (SS). Insufficient evidence from CS for autoclave synthesis of CsAuF₆ is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is severe because it requires pre-synthesis, sealed capsule handling, long annealing, and possible volatile Au fluoride species (CS/SS/TS). Ba(AuF₄)₂-type work uses Au tubes/sealed ampoules, dry argon, F₂/N₂ fluorination, and long heat treatments, establishing the demanding nature of this route (CS/SS/TS). The route avoids ultrahigh temperature and >5 GPa pressure, but the sealed reactive/volatile fluoride environment remains a major penalty. The cited Ba(AuF₄)₂ data include a temperature-control conflict involving high annealing temperatures and AuF₃ sublimation cautions, so conservative lower-temperature exploration is recommended [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

If Route 3 begins from preformed CsAuF₆ powder, structural reconstruction is limited and annealing can improve crystallinity [Reasonable Inference]. However, the analogue Ba(AuF₄)₂ requires 30–40 days and careful heating/cooling for single-crystal work, implying slow ordering or transport kinetics (CS/SS/TS). Au fluoride volatility/sublimation can assist transport but can also deplete Au and shift stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference from AuF₃ sublimation cautions, CS/SS/TS]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for sealed-tube growth of an AuF₆ octahedral CsAuF₆ phase is treated as neutral-to-uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large 12-coordinate Cs environments with much smaller octahedral Au environments, and the target/analogues exhibit mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing connectivity (CS). These features trigger two of the three amorphization checks, giving a medium risk and F4 = 60. The unit cell atom count trigger is not applied because the described R-3m target has only one crystallographic Cs, Au, and F site and no evidence for a >40-atom cell [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest mitigating evidence is that RbBiF₆ and CsRuF₆ with very similar mixed connectivity are crystalline synthesized phases (CS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsAuF₆ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, but experimental realization may still be kinetically and redox limited. The analogues support synthesis well at the framework level: CsRuF₆ and RbBiF₆ show the same ABF₆ cuboctahedral/octahedral topology, while RbAuF₄, Ba(AuF₄)₂, and LiAuF₄ show that Au–F chemistry is experimentally accessible under F₂ conditions (CS/SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of strongly oxidizing, dry F₂ chemistry at 300–400 °C, which is repeatedly successful for related fluorides (CS/SS/TS). The main limiting factor is maintaining the correct Au oxidation state and stoichiometry without loss or conversion to competing AuF₄/AuF₃-related phases [Reasonable Inference from Au fluoride volatility and phase-control issues, CS/SS/TS]. Overall, the target is plausible but should be approached as a hazardous, narrow-window high-valent fluoride synthesis.
