### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. This is reinforced by chemically or structurally related iodides with low hull energies, including TlMnI₃ at 0.017 eV/atom (CS-1/SS-1), Ta₃SeI₇ at 0.010 eV/atom (CS-2), and CdI₂ polytypes near 0 eV/atom (SS-2 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Mn/F/I ordering.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to 350 °C TlMnI₃ annealing and 450 °C iodide ampoule synthesis (CS-1, CS-2). The main severity penalties are sealed vacuum/Ar handling, volatile iodine/iodide species, and air-sensitive halide manipulation, all consistent with dry glovebox and sealed ampoule practices in CS-1/CS-2/CS-3. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for handling MnF₂/MnI₂ in this exact mixed-halide target, so that risk is partly inferred.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary halide precursors should be moderately reactive, and the close Mn–I octahedral analogue TlMnI₃ supports formation of edge-sharing MnI₆ units (CS-1/SS-1). Long annealing and powdering in TlMnI₃/TlFeI₃ indicate that phase equilibration can be slow (CS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether MnF₂ reacts fully and whether F occupies the intended trigonal Mn₃F site rather than forming residual MnF₂.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 requires similar controlled-atmosphere and sealed-ampoule measures to Route 1, and melt/Bridgman processing is explicitly supported for TlMnI₃/TlFeI₃-related work (CS-1). Temperatures remain far below 1000 °C, so thermal severity is moderate. The process is more operationally complex than simple solid-state annealing because crystal growth, slow cooling, and post-annealing are likely needed.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The melt or iodide-rich flux should enhance ion mobility relative to a dry powder reaction, which is why Route 2 receives the highest kinetic score among the credible CS-supported routes. TlMnI₃ formation from melted binary compounds directly supports this approach for Mn–I networks (CS-1/SS-1). The limiting kinetic issue remains F incorporation, for which no direct analogue is supplied.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Chemical vapor transport is performed at moderate temperatures in the NbOI₃ analogue, using 400 → 275 °C in evacuated ampoules (CS-3). Severity arises from I₂ volatility, sealed evacuated tubes, and multi-zone temperature-gradient control. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for Mn–F–I vapor transport, so the practical severity may be underestimated.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport can overcome solid-state diffusion barriers, as shown for NbOI₃ chemical transport with I₂ (CS-3). However, transport selectivity for MnI₂ versus MnF₂ is uncertain, and F-containing species may not transport efficiently [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore plausible for crystal growth but less reliable for stoichiometric bulk synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Solution/gel routes are extremely mild for CdI₂ polytypes, including room-temperature evaporation and silica-gel growth under ordinary conditions (SS-2 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). For Mn₄I₇F, inert or low-moisture conditions are likely needed to prevent hydrolysis/oxidation [Reasonable Inference], which adds severity absent in CdI₂ analogues. Insufficient evidence from CS supports solution growth of Mn₄I₇F specifically, so the high F2 score should not be overinterpreted.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Solution routes have favorable ion mobility and high precursor activity, as demonstrated for CdI₂ crystallization by evaporation and gel methods (SS-2 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The structural similarity of CdI₂-type edge-sharing octahedral layers is useful, but the chemistry differs strongly because CdI₂ lacks Mn and F. The main uncertainty is whether the mixed iodide/fluoride composition can crystallize from solution rather than producing MnF₂, MnI₂ hydrates, or hydrolysis products.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation type, Mn, so cation-size disorder is not a concern. The described structure consists of edge-sharing octahedra and does not show mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity. This gives low amorphization risk, although stacking disorder or polytypism could still occur by analogy to CdI₂ polytypes (SS-3 to SS-5; TS-2 to TS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₄I₇F is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the Mn iodide analogue TlMnI₃, which contains edge-sharing MnI₆ octahedra with similar Mn–I bond lengths and is synthesized by melt/anneal methods (CS-1/SS-1). The most important enabling factor is the soft, iodide-rich lattice chemistry, which permits low- to moderate-temperature synthesis in sealed ampoules, as shown across CS iodide analogues (CS-1 to CS-3). The most important limiting factor is mixed-anion ordering: no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Mn–I–F octahedral ordering, so residual MnF₂ or MnI₂ competing phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is synthesizable with moderate difficulty, best attempted by sealed halide melt/anneal or direct ampoule reaction.
