### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which lies at the favorable boundary of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. Thermodynamic analogues include Nd₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0621 eV/atom, Pr₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0598 eV/atom, Sm₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0531 eV/atom, Ce₂Co₁₅Ga₂ at 0.0729 eV/atom, and Co₂CrAl at 0.0576 eV/atom (TS). Chemically/structurally similar nitrides such as Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ are reported despite E_hull = 0.136 eV/atom (CS, SS), so a 0.050 eV/atom Al–Co nitride is plausible as a ground-state-adjacent or low-metastability phase. Remaining uncertainty is that none of the cited analogues is exactly AlCo₆N in cubic F-43m.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because the Co–Al parent alloy likely requires arc melting, analogous to Co-rich intermetallic syntheses under Ar with remelting and long annealing (CS, SS, TS). The route also requires controlled Ar/vacuum or sealed conditions and a separate N₂ nitrogenation step, similar to R₂Co₁₇N₃ gas-phase nitrogenation (CS, SS). Al volatility during arc melting is a likely issue [Reasonable Inference], and cobalt handling adds health risk [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms AlCo₆N stability under flowing N₂, so the route remains moderately severe.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The cation precursors are dense metals/intermetallics, which have lower activity than solution-derived precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS nitrogenation analogues show N can enter Co-rich frameworks at 450–550 °C, but they also report slow diffusion and incomplete nitrogenation/α-Co precipitation risks for R₂Co₁₇N₃-type systems (CS, SS). The cubic F-43m target differs from R-3m analogues, requiring significant ordering/reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Long 900 °C/125 h anneals used for Co-rich analogues indicate slow ordering kinetics (CS, SS, TS).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids multi-GPa pressure but uses a high-pressure N₂ autoclave around 15 MPa, as reported for Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ (CS). It still requires an arc-melted/homogenized parent alloy, controlled Ar/vacuum handling, particle processing, and high-pressure nitrogenation (CS, TS). The route is therefore severe but chemically well motivated by the high nitrogen chemical potential demonstrated in the CS high-pressure route. Insufficient evidence from SS directly supports high-pressure synthesis of AlCo₆N, so the structural source is neutral for this condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-pressure N₂ improves nitrogen uptake and was specifically used to achieve high nitrogen content while minimizing disproportionation in Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ (CS). Crushing and sieving the precursor alloy, described in the high-pressure nitrogenation workflow, should improve diffusion relative to dense ingots (CS). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the Co–Al parent structure can rearrange into cubic F-43m rather than forming binary Co–Al plus cobalt nitride phases [Reasonable Inference]. Because high N chemical potential helps anion incorporation but not necessarily cation ordering, the F3 score is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Reactive sputtering avoids arc-melting temperatures and high-pressure autoclaves [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires vacuum equipment and a controlled Ar/N₂ plasma, which qualifies as a controlled atmosphere [Reasonable Inference]. Cobalt target handling has toxicity concerns [Reasonable Inference], but no sealed volatile precursor is required. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly validates the thin-film route, so direct source support is neutral even though the route is synthetically plausible.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Al and Co are accessible as sputtering targets and N₂ can supply nitrogen in a reactive plasma [Reasonable Inference]. The likely need for post-deposition annealing is inferred from bulk analogues, where annealing is needed to improve phase purity and ordering (CS, SS, TS). Composition control is a limiting kinetic/process factor because Co:Al:N must be close to 6:1:1, and Al resputtering or oxygen contamination could shift the film away from the desired phase [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS thin-film analogue directly confirms crystallization of this structure, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s reported structure is relatively simple, with AlCo₁₂ cuboctahedra and NCo₆ octahedra in cubic F-43m, and the description does not show a large unit cell or edge-sharing complexity. The target contains only two cation species, Al and Co, and no large rare-earth/cobalt size mismatch like the RE–Co analogues [Reasonable Inference]. The target does include face-sharing polyhedra, but the specified C3 trigger requires vertex + edge + face sharing coexistence, which is not reported for the target. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives no direct amorphization data for AlCo₆N, so the low-risk assessment is primarily structural extrapolation.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlCo₆N is best viewed as a ground-state-adjacent or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, at the favorable stability threshold. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support Co-rich metallic frameworks, arc-melting/annealing methods, and nitrogenation of Co-rich parent compounds, but none is an exact cubic Al–Co–N match. The most critical enabling factor is the short Co–N bond compatibility: the target Co–N distance of 1.85 Å closely matches Co–N distances of 1.83–1.87 Å in Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ (CS, SS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: AlCo₆N must compete with Co–Al intermetallics, cobalt metal, and possible cobalt nitrides [Reasonable Inference]. Thin-film reactive synthesis is recommended because it can access near-hull metastable phases while avoiding severe bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference].
