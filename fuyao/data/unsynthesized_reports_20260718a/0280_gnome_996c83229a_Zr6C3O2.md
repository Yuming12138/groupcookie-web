### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin and justifies F1 = 98 [target data]. Similar reference materials also include many hull-stable phases, including chemically similar carbides and structurally/thermodynamically similar Zr–C/O compounds (CS: all five have E_hull = 0; SS/TS: ScAlOC, Zr4Pd2O, Zr2PbC, Zr2TlC have E_hull = 0). The remaining uncertainty is that convex-hull stability does not specify the accessible synthesis pathway or anion-ordering kinetics [Reasonable Inference].

Source-specific signal: CS provides strong stability precedent through multiple stable carbides, score 90; SS provides strong structural stability precedent for Zr–C/O-related phases, score 90; TS provides strongest direct thermodynamic signal including Zr–O and Zr–C/O analogues, score 95.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is penalized because arc melting and annealing of Zr–O/Zr–C analogues require high temperature, inert/vacuum handling, repeated remelting, and post-annealing (SS/TS: Zr4Pd2O; SS: Zr4Ni2O; TS: Zr3O). CS carbide analogues also require long high-temperature anneals and inert handling, especially for Sm2Cr2C3 and Sm2Mo2C3 (CS). Insufficient evidence from CS for oxygen-containing arc-melted Zr oxycarbide formation specifically, so CS is neutral-to-supportive rather than decisive for Route 1 severity. F2 = 55 reflects feasible but equipment- and atmosphere-intensive synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Arc melting improves homogenization, as used for Zr4Pd2O/Zr4Ni2O and Zr3O-type materials (SS/TS: Zr4Pd2O; SS: Zr4Ni2O; TS: Zr3O). However, ZrC and ZrO2 are refractory and may leave secondary phases, as Zr4Pd2O reports impurities including ZrO2 and ZrPd (SS/TS). The target requires ordering both C and O in Zr-coordination polyhedra, so structural reconstruction is moderate-to-difficult [Reasonable Inference]. F3 = 47 reflects slow diffusion and likely need for repeated annealing.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr6C3O2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target data]. The analogue set supports synthesis moderately well: CS shows high-temperature monoclinic carbide frameworks can be made, SS/TS show Zr–C and Zr–O octahedral motifs are experimentally accessible, and TS includes stable Zr–O thermodynamic neighbors (CS; SS; TS). The most critical enabling factor is the strong Zr affinity for both C and O and the existence of compatible CZr6/OZr6 motifs in related phases (SS/TS: Zr2PbC, Zr2TlC, Zr4Pd2O; TS: Zr3O). Overall, the material appears synthesizeable but likely requires careful oxygen/carbon activity control and post-synthesis annealing.
