### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls in the highest rubric bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom, giving F1 = 90. TS analogues include synthesized phases with E_hull values around 0.03–0.10 eV/atom, supporting the idea that this metastability range is experimentally accessible. However, CS analogues include chemically similar oxygen-rich phases with much larger E_hull values, so CS gives mixed thermodynamic confidence. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity does not resolve the target’s unusual CaPO6 valence/oxygen bonding.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — High-pressure hydrous/oxide synthesis

SS1 directly supports the severity estimate: 10 GPa is a major penalty, while 450 °C is not thermally extreme. The kinetic score is favorable because SS1 used a gel and H2O, which should improve transport and lower diffusion barriers. Structural transfer is the key reason to prefer this route: octahedral small-cation oxygen coordination is seen in SS1, whereas ordinary phosphate chemistry usually favors PO4 tetrahedra [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because SS1 is Mg–Si–O/H, not Ca–P–O, and its network topology differs from the target.

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Oxidizing sealed-tube / solid-state synthesis

TS3 and TS4 support oxide synthesis at 600–700 °C in silver tubes or controlled sealed environments, so the temperature severity is moderate. Peroxide or oxygen-rich reagents are proposed because the target is oxygen-rich, but those reagents add air-sensitivity and safety penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are less favorable because solid-state synthesis would need complete structural reconstruction into a cubic PO6-bearing phase. The major uncertainty is whether ambient pressure can stabilize octahedral P in this stoichiometry.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal / solvothermal crystallization

CS2, SS2, and SS3 support moderate-temperature autoclave synthesis over several days, while CS4 and SS5 show phosphate salts can crystallize readily from solution. This gives excellent precursor activity and mass transport. The structural score is low because none of these analogues demonstrate a CaPO6 phase with PO6 octahedra; hydrothermal Ca–P chemistry is more likely to produce conventional calcium phosphates [Reasonable Inference]. Thus this route is operationally mild but chemically uncertain.

#### Route 4 F2/F3 — Flux/transport route

CS3 supports high-temperature transport growth of arsenate crystals using TeCl4 and sealed ampoules, so flux/transport is plausible for improving crystal growth. The route is penalized for volatile/corrosive transport agents and controlled sealed conditions. Kinetically, flux growth should reduce diffusion barriers, but phase selection remains weakly supported because no CS/SS/TS example matches cubic CaPO6. This route is best viewed as exploratory crystal growth after establishing phase stability by Route 1 or Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size and charge contrast between Ca and P, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a large unit cell, and the high-symmetry F-43m formula suggests the atom count is likely not >40 [Reasonable Inference]. The target has both corner-sharing and face-sharing motifs, but no edge-sharing, so the full mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium, mainly due to cation mismatch and unusual coordination.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best regarded as a near-hull metastable phase rather than a confidently established ground state, because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom and the chemistry is unusual. CS supports that oxygen-rich pnictate/phosphate-like materials can be crystallized, but CS analogues often involve complex oxygen bonding or conventional PO4 units rather than the target’s PO6 octahedra. SS provides the most important enabling clue: octahedral small-cation oxide frameworks may require high pressure, as in the 10 GPa MgSiO6/MgSi(OH)6 analogue. The most critical enabling factor is high-pressure, hydrous, highly mixed precursor chemistry that can stabilize unusual coordination. The most critical limiting factor is the apparent formal-valence/oxygen-bonding inconsistency of CaPO6 if no O–O bonding is present.
