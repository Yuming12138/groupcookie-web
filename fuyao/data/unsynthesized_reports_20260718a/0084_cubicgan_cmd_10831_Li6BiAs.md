### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the boundary of the most favorable stability bin and therefore earns **90/100**. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies were synthesized: Li₂HgGe at ~0.052 eV/atom (TS1), Na₃As at ~0.043 eV/atom (TS2), and NaBeSb at ~0.048 eV/atom (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that a 50 meV/atom phase may be metastable relative to competing binaries/ternaries, so kinetic access and phase selection still matter [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, matching many analogues made at 650–850 °C under Ar or in sealed ampoules (CS4, CS5, SS5, TS2). Deductions are applied for volatile sealed-ampoule requirements and toxic/air-sensitive precursors because Li and As require inert sealed handling [Reasonable Inference], consistent with glovebox/ampoule use in CS1, SS5, and TS2. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for H₂/NH₃ or high pressure, so those terms are neutral/no deduction.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct elemental routes are widely supported for Li/Na pnictides and related alkali intermetallics (CS4, CS5, SS5, TS2). Kinetics are helped by low-melting Li and Bi [Reasonable Inference], but formation from elements still requires nucleation and ordering of a ternary cubic phase. The score is limited by lack of an exact Li₆BiAs analogue and by CS1’s evidence that Li-rich ternary intermetallics can suffer impurity/peritectic complications.

#### F2 — Route 2

The melt/slow-cooling route uses temperatures below 1000 °C, consistent with CS1 and SS5, and does not require high pressure. It receives additional severity penalties because it involves staged heating, slow cooling, and annealing, which are explicitly seen in CS1 and SS2. Volatile As/Li and toxic/air-sensitive precursor handling remain limiting factors [Reasonable Inference], supported by sealed ampoule use in SS5 and TS2.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt participation should improve reaction kinetics and mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and Li–Pb/Li-rich analogues demonstrate melt-based preparation routes (CS1, CS2, CS3). Slow cooling can help crystallization, as in CS1 and SS2. However, CS1 also reports impurity and incongruent/peritectic phase-control issues, reducing the structural-reconstruction and phase-selection confidence.

#### F2 — Route 3

The Li-rich flux route stays in the same moderate temperature window used for alkali-pnictide synthesis (CS4, CS5, SS5, TS2). It is penalized for sealed-ampoule volatility, toxic/air-sensitive precursors, and multi-step flux growth/removal [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for high pressure, so high-pressure penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 3

Li-rich flux should provide high precursor activity and fast transport [Reasonable Inference], and excess Li was used successfully in Li₁₇Ag₃Sn₆ crystal synthesis (CS1). The kinetic score is therefore highest among the routes. The uncertainty is that excess Li can stabilize competing Li–As or Li–Bi phases [Reasonable Inference], and CS1 demonstrates that Li-rich ternary intermetallics can be difficult to isolate phase-pure.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization check is triggered: the size mismatch between Li and Bi is large if both are treated as cationic/electropositive framework participants [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description gives ordered BiLi₁₂ cuboctahedra and AsLi₆ octahedra in a cubic phase, with corner/face sharing but no reported edge sharing, so C3 is not triggered from the target data. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40-atom cell, so C2 is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆BiAs is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than an obviously deep ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of making alkali-rich pnictides and alkali p-block intermetallics from elements in sealed inert ampoules, especially CS4/CS5, SS5, and TS2. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature sealed-ampoule elemental routes with reactive Li/alkali melts, which should provide adequate mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control: Li-rich ternary systems can form impurities or require slow cooling/annealing, as seen in CS1, and As volatility/toxicity makes stoichiometry control difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed-tube practice and iterative stoichiometry/cooling optimization.
