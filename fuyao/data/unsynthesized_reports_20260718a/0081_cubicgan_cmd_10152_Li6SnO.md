### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the most favorable scoring bin, so F1 = 90 rather than a higher 95–100 [target data]. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values near or above this value have reported syntheses, including KNa₂NiO₂ at 0.0777 eV/atom and Li₂MgIr at 0.0740 eV/atom (TS1, TS2), supporting feasibility of near-hull metastable phases. However, CS and SS do not provide a directly analogous Li–Sn–O phase, so their thermodynamic signal is indirect; insufficient direct Li–Sn–O thermodynamic evidence from CS/SS, scored neutral conceptually at 50 for that specific sub-question.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from many Li-rich intermetallic analogues prepared by direct sealed reactions at moderate-to-high temperature, including Na₆ZnSn₂ at 773 K, Li₁₇Ag₃Sn₆ at 850 °C, and Li₃Pd at 800 °C (CS3, CS4, CS5; SS2). Its condition severity is moderate because it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C, but Li volatility and air sensitivity require sealed ampoules and glovebox handling (CS4, CS5; SS2). Kinetically, molten Li/Sn should help diffusion [Reasonable Inference], but the Li₂O component is likely less reactive and may require prolonged annealing or regrinding [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Li₂O reacting cleanly with Li–Sn melts to form Li₆SnO, so this uncertainty limits F3.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by excess-Li use in Li₈SeN₂/Li₈TeN₂ crystal growth and Li₁₇Ag₃Sn₆ synthesis (CS1, CS2, CS4/SS4). It is more procedurally complex than Route 1 because flux growth requires excess Li, slow cooling, and flux removal [Reasonable Inference], so F2 is lower. Kinetically, excess molten Li should enhance mass transport and buffer Li loss [Reasonable Inference], and slow cooling is proven useful in alkali-rich intermetallic crystal growth (CS3, CS4). The main uncertainty is whether Li₂O dissolves/reacts sufficiently in Li flux rather than persisting as an oxide impurity; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for Li₂O solubility in Li flux.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is a fallback because TS3 demonstrates high-pressure synthesis can stabilize difficult metastable materials at 8 GPa and 873 K. Its F2 is low because >5 GPa high pressure and multi-step capsule assembly are severe (TS3). Kinetically, pressure can improve contact and stabilize dense phases [Reasonable Inference], but no chemically or structurally similar Li–Sn–O analogue was made by high pressure. Therefore, TS3 provides a useful metastability strategy, but insufficient chemical/structural transferability from CS/SS/TS makes this route less attractive.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple high-symmetry cubic F-43m description with one Li, one Sn, and one O site, which disfavors amorphization relative to large complex cells [target structural data]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆SnO is best viewed as a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom [target data]. The CS/SS analogues strongly support handling and crystallizing Li-rich intermetallic frameworks in sealed ampoules, especially Li₁₇Ag₃Sn₆, Li₃Pd, and Li₃Hg (CS4, CS5; SS1, SS2, SS4), but they do not directly prove Li–Sn–O chemistry. The main limiting factor is competition between Li₂O and Li–Sn binary/intermetallic phases because no direct CS/SS/TS analogue demonstrates clean formation of this mixed oxide–stannide chemistry [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS].
