### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is computed on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues include stable MnMoN₂ and MgMoN₂ at E_hull = 0.0 and near-hull MoN/Mo₂N phases, showing that Mo–N frameworks can be thermodynamically competitive (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). CS chalcogenides are also often on the hull, but they are sulfides rather than nitrides, so their thermodynamic signal for nitride stability is weaker; insufficient nitride-specific evidence from CS chalcogenide entries, neutral 50 for that sub-signal. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or cation ordering in the triclinic P1 Mo/W arrangement.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 750 °C flowing NH₃ and no high pressure, which is moderate in severity and directly supported by CrWN₂ synthesis (CS-4, SS-2). The main penalties are controlled NH₃ atmosphere, multistep precursor chemistry, and air/moisture-sensitive chloride precursors, all present or reasonably inferred from the CrWN₂ and MoN precursor routes (CS-4, SS-2, SS-4, TS-3). Insufficient evidence from non-nitride CS sulfides for NH₃ severity is neutral 50, but CrWN₂ is highly relevant. The route is experimentally manageable but requires careful precursor handling.

#### F3 — Route 1

The molecular precursor should give excellent cation mixing because CrWN₂ was made through chelation/hydrolysis/pyrolysis rather than long-range solid diffusion (CS-4, SS-2). However, Mo and W are refractory and cation ordering into MoW₃N₆ may still be sluggish [Reasonable Inference from target composition and SS-4/SS-5 MoN ordering issues]. Structural reconstruction is moderate because CrWN₂ has similar WN₆-based connectivity, but the target’s P1 ordering and W-rich stoichiometry are not directly shown (SS-2; target structure). This gives a moderate F3 rather than a high one.

#### F2 — Route 2

Oxide ammonolysis uses accessible temperatures of 625–800 °C and standard quartz/alumina tube-furnace hardware, as shown for MnMoN₂/FeWN₂ and MnWN₂ (TS-1, SS-3). The penalties are NH₃ atmosphere and multistep mixed-oxide precursor preparation; no GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required (TS-1, SS-3). The narrow phase-purity window reported for TS-1 affects kinetics/phase control more than condition severity. Insufficient evidence from CS chalcogenides for oxide ammonolysis is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Oxide precursors are moderately active and are proven for ternary Mo/W nitride ammonolysis analogues (TS-1, SS-3). The kinetic limitation is large because oxide-to-nitride conversion requires oxygen removal and rearrangement into a nitride network, while TS-1 reports narrow temperature windows and SS-3 reports impurities. Mo/W ordering in a W-rich P1 lattice is not directly demonstrated by oxide ammonolysis, so the structural reconstruction score is low-to-moderate. This route is plausible but likely optimization-intensive.

#### F2 — Route 3

High-pressure routes are the harshest: W₂N₃ synthesis used 5 GPa and up to 2570 K, while MoN ordering used 6 GPa and 1800 K (SS-1, SS-4, TS-3). This triggers penalties for >1000 °C, >1400 °C, >5 GPa, controlled encapsulated conditions, multistep processing, and hazardous chloride/NH₃ precursor chemistry when using the MoN-style route (SS-4, TS-3). The source evidence is strong for high-pressure Mo/W nitride accessibility, but it also indicates high experimental burden. CS provides insufficient high-pressure nitride evidence, neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can accelerate densification and order certain nitride phases, as shown by the disordered-to-ordered MoN treatment (SS-4, TS-3). W₂N₃ synthesis under 5 GPa also supports access to W-rich nitrides that may be difficult at ambient pressure (SS-1). Nevertheless, phase control is reported as difficult, with narrow windows and purification issues (SS-1, SS-4, TS-3). This route may be a rescue strategy if ambient NH₃ routes fail, not the first choice.

#### F2 — Route 4

The azide autoclave route avoids external NH₃ and GPa pressure and uses ~700 °C, as shown for MgMoN₂ (TS-4). Penalties arise from sealed autoclave gas generation, toxic/explosive NaN₃, and post-reaction washing/multistep handling (TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). There is no direct CS or SS evidence for Mo–W azide nitridation, so those source categories are neutral 50 for this route. Condition severity is moderate but safety risk is nontrivial.

#### F3 — Route 4

NaN₃ supplies high nitrogen chemical potential, and TS-4 demonstrates that sealed azide chemistry can produce a ternary molybdenum nitride. However, starting from Mo and W powders creates severe cation diffusion limitations, especially for W [Reasonable Inference from refractory-metal chemistry and TS-4 route type]. The target also requires an ordered Mo/W nitride framework, implying complete structural reconstruction from metals. Thus, F3 is the lowest among ambient/non-GPa routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has similar Mo/W cations, so the radius-mismatch check is not triggered; similar Mo–N and W–N bond lengths are seen in MoN, CrWN₂, and the target description (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; target structure). The described P1 cell contains 10 atoms, so the >40 atom-cell check is not triggered. Mixed face/edge/corner sharing is clearly present in the target and in several analogues, so one amorphization trigger is active (SS-2, SS-4, SS-5; target structure). The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mo(WN₂)₃ is likely a ground-state or very-near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues in Mo–N and ternary Mo nitrides are also stable or near-stable (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CrWN₂, which demonstrates W-containing ternary nitride formation by molecular precursor ammonolysis under NH₃, and from MoN/Mo₂N analogues showing that Mo–N frameworks are accessible (CS-4, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is molecular-scale Mo/W mixing before ammonolysis, because it can bypass long-range diffusion of refractory Mo and W [Reasonable Inference from CS-4/SS-2]. The most critical limiting factor is phase and cation-order control in a W-rich triclinic P1 Mo/W nitride, which is not directly demonstrated in the provided analogues. If ambient-pressure ammonolysis fails, high-pressure annealing is a plausible but much more difficult fallback based on W₂N₃ and MoN high-pressure syntheses (SS-1, SS-4, TS-3).
