### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score of 95. This is reinforced by the closest structural and thermodynamic analogue Rb₂GeS₃, which also has E_hull = 0.0 and the same C2/m topology (SS2, TS2). CS evidence also shows multiple alkali sulfides close to or on the hull, including K₂TiS₃ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is mainly computational: a 0 eV/atom hull prediction does not guarantee a broad synthesis window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires evacuated sealed-tube synthesis because sulfur is volatile and alkali sulfides are air/moisture sensitive; this is directly supported by Rb₂GeS₃ and K₂TiS₃ syntheses (SS2, TS2, CS1). The required temperature, about 750–800 °C, is moderate for chalcogenide solid-state chemistry and below the >1000 °C penalty threshold (SS2, TS2). CS provides a similar but longer 6 d vacuum route for K₂TiS₃, suggesting some possible severity if kinetics are slow (CS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on whether commercial K₂S avoids all precursor-preparation steps, so this route is scored without the multi-step penalty but with uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The exact structural analogue Rb₂GeS₃ forms in 12 h at 1073 K from Rb₂S + Ge + S, which strongly supports reasonable kinetics for a K₂S + Si + S route (SS2, TS2). Si may react more slowly than Ge because of stronger Si–Si bonding and higher melting point [Reasonable Inference], but alkali sulfide/sulfur media should enhance transport relative to oxide solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is high because the target and Rb₂GeS₃ share the same C2/m arrangement of alkali sulfide polyhedra and group-14 sulfide tetrahedra (SS2, TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS alone exists for Si-specific kinetics, so CS is treated as neutral for that subfactor except for confirming alkali sulfide sealed-tube feasibility (CS1).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Elemental synthesis requires handling K metal and sulfur in sealed evacuated graphitized ampoules, which increases severity due to air sensitivity and vapor pressure [Reasonable Inference; SS3, TS3]. The K₃GaS₃ analogue confirms that K + metal + S syntheses can be conducted at 1025 K in evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules (SS3, TS3). The proposed target temperature can likely be kept near 700–780 °C by using stepped pre-reaction and slow heating [Reasonable Inference], but no exact elemental K–Si–S analogue is provided. Insufficient evidence from CS for elemental K + Si + S synthesis specifically is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

K and S are highly reactive, but Si powder is expected to be the kinetic bottleneck [Reasonable Inference]. K₃GaS₃ synthesis from elements is supportive, but Ga is much more fusible than Si, so direct transfer is imperfect (SS3, TS3). The route likely proceeds through in situ K₂S/K₂Sₓ intermediates, which could improve diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural formation is plausible because related K–S ternaries crystallize from sealed high-T reactions, but it is less directly supported than Route 1 (SS3, TS3; CS1).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Reactive flux synthesis requires inert/vacuum sealing, long dwell times, slow cooling, and often post-growth washing, all of which are reported for K/Rb chalcogenide flux analogues (SS1, TS1, SS4, TS4). Alkali polysulfides are reactive and air-sensitive, and sulfur-rich fluxes create sealed-tube vapor pressure concerns [Reasonable Inference]. The temperatures are moderate, around 525–725 °C for K/Eu group-14 chalcogenides and 973 K for Rb/Cs thiophosphates (SS1, TS1, SS4, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of K₂SiS₃ specifically is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth scores well kinetically because alkali polysulfide media improve mass transport and enable crystal growth of complex sulfides (SS1, TS1, SS4, TS4). Long reaction times of 120–150 h are common in the cited flux syntheses, so kinetics are not instantaneous but are manageable (SS1, TS1, SS4, TS4). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: flux conditions may stabilize alternative K–Si–S polysulfide or thiocompound phases rather than the exact K₂SiS₃ C2/m framework [Reasonable Inference]. The exact Rb₂GeS₃ analogue was synthesized directly, not by flux, so Route 3 is a secondary route (SS2, TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has chemically distinct K⁺ and Si⁴⁺ cations with very different sizes and coordination environments, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The likely cell size is small because the isostructural Rb₂GeS₃ analogue is listed as Rb₄Ge₂S₆, i.e., 12 atoms in the formula given for the structure (SS2, TS2). The target contains corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra, but the provided target description does not show simultaneous vertex + edge + face sharing, so the full mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not applied. Remaining uncertainty is whether rapid quenching or off-stoichiometry could produce glassy K–Si–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂SiS₃ is likely a ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic/structural analogues are also on or essentially on the hull (SS2, TS2; SS1, TS1). The synthesis case is strongly supported by the isostructural Rb₂GeS₃ analogue, which has the same C2/m framework and was obtained by a simple evacuated sealed-tube reaction from alkali sulfide, group-14 element, and sulfur (SS2, TS2). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a direct structure-type analogue with very similar bonding topology and feasible synthesis conditions (SS2, TS2). The main limiting factor is chemical handling and phase control in volatile, air-sensitive K–S–Si chemistry, especially maintaining sulfur activity and preventing ampoule attack [Reasonable Inference; CS1, SS3, TS3]. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with Route 1 being the most direct and defensible approach.
