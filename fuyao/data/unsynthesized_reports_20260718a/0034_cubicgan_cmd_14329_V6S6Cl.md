### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable boundary of the specified 90–100 scoring bin. Several similar V–S materials at comparable or even higher E_hull are reported as synthesized: V₃NiS₆ at 0.0514 eV/atom (CS-3), MnCr₂S₄ at 0.0523 eV/atom (CS-5), VS at 0.0877–0.1036 eV/atom in SS/TS listings (SS-2, SS-4, TS-1, TS-2), and TlV₅S₈ at 0.0576 eV/atom (TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic similarity does not prove the cubic chloride-sulfide ordering is a true ground state; insufficient direct Cl-containing evidence is available from CS, SS, and TS.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses standard evacuated sealed-tube conditions similar to CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, and TS-1, which are moderate rather than extreme because no external high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required. Deductions arise from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur/chloride containment, multistep grinding/refiring, and air-sensitive V chloride, with the chloride aspect being [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide strong evidence for sealed-tube V–S synthesis but insufficient direct evidence for V–S–Cl handling.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Direct elemental synthesis has poor-to-moderate kinetics because V metal is refractory and full reconstruction is required, [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-2 show that related sulfides need 800–1000 °C and repeated grinding/refiring, and SS-1/TS-1 indicate V–S phase control can involve long anneals. The V–S framework is plausible, but achieving ordered Cl incorporation from a solid elemental mix is uncertain because CS/SS/TS lack chloride analogues.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 inherits H₂S and sealed-tube requirements directly from CS-3 and CS-4, including toxic dry H₂S, evacuated silica tubes, grinding, sulfur addition, and long anneals. The temperature window of 750–850 °C is moderate and below the >1000 °C penalty threshold, but atmosphere and precursor hazards are significant. **F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Starting from a preformed V–S precursor reduces structural reconstruction compared with elemental synthesis, and CS-3/CS-4 show V-containing sulfides can be produced by sulfidation near 750 °C. It avoids external high pressure and can remain at or below 1000 °C, but it requires volatile sulfur/chloride control, slow heating, gradient annealing, and air-sensitive chloride handling. CS/SS/TS support the sealed-tube vapor/growth environment, but provide insufficient direct chloride-transport evidence.

Sulfur transport is plausible from TS-5, and chloride transport is plausible by [Reasonable Inference], but the target has a narrow Cl content that may make composition control difficult. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V₆S₆Cl is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than an obviously robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The V–S bonding environment is well supported by CS/SS/TS: many V sulfides with V–S distances near 2.3–2.5 Å and similar edge/corner-sharing motifs are synthesized by sealed-tube, H₂S, or vapor-gradient methods (CS-1 to CS-4, SS-1 to SS-4, TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the extensive precedent for forming V–S frameworks at 750–1000 °C in evacuated tubes or H₂S/sulfur-rich conditions. The most critical limiting factor is controlled incorporation of a small amount of Cl into a specific V₆ octahedral site, because no direct V–S–Cl analogue is present in the supplied CS/SS/TS data. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sulfur/chloride activity control and iterative phase analysis.
