#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Thermodynamic analogues with similar or even higher E_hull values are reported as synthesizable, including CuBiSCl2 at 0.037 eV/atom (TS3), BiBr at 0.0387 eV/atom (TS4), and Sn2SbS2I3 at 0.0889 eV/atom (TS5). CS and SS include many synthesized materials at 0–0.04 eV/atom, supporting that near-hull halide/chalcogenide phases can be experimentally accessible (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target may be metastable with respect to binary Sb/Ir/SbClx phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is based on the closest structural analogue, Bi12PtCl12, synthesized by heating Bi, BiCl3, and Pt in quartz ampoules at 1270 K (SS4). The condition severity is significant because the route uses vacuum sealing and volatile/hydrolyzable SbCl3 [Reasonable Inference], analogous to dry halide handling in SS4 and SS5. Kinetically, Ir metal is likely the bottleneck because it is refractory and may react slowly [Reasonable Inference], although SS4 shows that noble-metal/pnictogen chloride cluster phases can form under sealed high-temperature conditions. CS provides only broad support for sealed solid-state methods in heavy p-block chalcogenides and gives insufficient direct evidence for Ir–Sb–Cl chemistry, so CS is treated as neutral for the specific Ir incorporation issue.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by several CVT and vapor-gradient analogues: HfTe4Cl6 at 215–195 °C (CS2), Bi3(TeCl5)2 at 170→100 °C (SS5), BiBr at 272→165 °C (SS3, TS4), and Sn2SbS2I3 at 430/350 °C with iodine transport (TS5). Route 3 receives the best score because a molten SbCl3-rich medium should improve mass transport and simultaneously buffer Sb and Cl activities [Reasonable Inference].
