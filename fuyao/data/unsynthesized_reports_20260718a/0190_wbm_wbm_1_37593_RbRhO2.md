### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Chemically similar RbCuO₂, CsCuO₂, CsCoO₂, RbYO₂, and Rb₂Co₂O₃ are also listed as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar K/Pd/Pt/Ir oxides are likewise listed as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee an accessible kinetic pathway, especially for air-sensitive alkali oxides.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because the best chemical analogue RbCuO₂ forms at only 400 °C for 3–4 d under O₂ (CS-3), and CsCuO₂ forms at 380 °C under O₂ (CS-1). No high pressure or exotic equipment is required in those analogues (CS-1, CS-3). The deductions arise from air/moisture-sensitive alkali oxides and the likely need for precursor preparation/regrinding/annealing, as indicated for RbCuO₂/RbYO₂-type alkali oxide chemistry (CS-3, CS-4). The uncertainty is whether Rh₂O₃ reacts as readily as CuO under the same conditions, which is a **[Reasonable Inference]** rather than directly demonstrated.

#### F3 — Route 1

The reaction kinetics are favorable compared with many refractory oxide syntheses because RbCuO₂ forms in a low-temperature alkali oxide environment (CS-3). The structural reconstruction score is high because the target and RbCuO₂ have the same Cmcm structure motif and analogous Rb/O coordination (CS-3). However, Rh oxide may be less reactive than CuO and Rh–O redox chemistry may require tighter oxygen potential control by **[Reasonable Inference]**. The 3–4 d anneal needed for the cuprates suggests diffusion/order kinetics are not instantaneous (CS-1, CS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because azide/nitrate routes require dried Ar, vacuum drying, ball milling, pellet pressing, controlled ramps, and sealed/closed vessels (CS-2, CS-5). The temperatures are still moderate, 450 °C for Rb₂Co₂O₃ and 833 K for CsCoO₂ (CS-2, CS-5), so no high-temperature penalty applies. The main penalties are hazardous RbN₃/RbNO₃ chemistry and multistep controlled-atmosphere handling (CS-2, CS-5). The uncertainty is whether the azide/nitrate oxygen fugacity favors Rh³⁺ RbRhO₂ instead of other Rb–Rh–O phases.

#### F3 — Route 2

Azide/nitrate precursors are highly active and can produce alkali transition-metal oxides at moderate temperatures (CS-2, CS-5). This improves diffusion relative to dense oxide-only routes, especially through in-situ reactive alkali species (CS-2, CS-5). However, the known azide/nitrate examples are cobaltates, not exact Cmcm RbRhO₂ analogues (CS-2, CS-5). Phase reconstruction and redox selectivity remain uncertain by **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has significant condition severity because structurally similar noble-metal alkali oxides often require sealed Ag/Au/silica containers, glovebox handling, and controlled oxygen pressure (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). Temperatures are moderate, typically 500–700 °C for Pd/Pt-related analogues (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3), so no >1000 °C penalty applies. The main penalties are sealed reactive alkali oxidants, multistep handling, and air-sensitive precursors/products (SS-3, TS-3; CS-4). The uncertainty is that these analogues have different stoichiometries, so the route may stabilize competing Rb–Rh–O phases.

#### F3 — Route 3

The precursor activity is good because alkali peroxides/superoxides such as KO₂/Na₂O₂ successfully form Pd oxides under sealed conditions (SS-3, TS-3). Ion transport should benefit from an alkali-rich reactive environment by **[Reasonable Inference]**, consistent with sealed alkali oxide syntheses at moderate temperature (SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3). Structural support is moderate because Pd/Pt/Ir analogues show square-planar noble-metal oxide coordination and Cmcm-related frameworks (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4). The different stoichiometry and need for oxygen fugacity control limit confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation size contrast between Rb and Rh, supported by the large Rb–O versus transition-metal–O bond-length contrast in RbCuO₂ and target-like analogues (CS-3). Mixed face/edge/corner-sharing connectivity is present in the RbCuO₂ analogue and in the target description, which raises structural complexity and amorphization risk (CS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for a >40-atom target unit cell, so that check is not triggered. The resulting risk is medium rather than high.
