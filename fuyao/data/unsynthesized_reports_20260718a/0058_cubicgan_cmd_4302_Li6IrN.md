### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the most favorable scoring bin and suggests near-hull metastability or marginal ground-state accessibility (target data). Chemically similar Li-rich materials include several reported/synthesized phases with E_hull near zero, such as Li₈TeN₂ at 0.0 eV/atom and Li₇Pb₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS2, CS3). Structurally and thermodynamically similar Li₂MgIr is synthesizable despite E_hull ≈ 0.074 eV/atom, supporting feasibility for a target at 0.05 eV/atom (SS3, TS1). The main uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct Li–Ir–N competing-phase map, so the numerical hull value is weighted more strongly than analogy.

#### F2 and F3: Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because all relevant analogues use sealed inert or vacuum-compatible containers, but not high pressure or >1000 °C synthesis (CS2, SS3, SS5, TS1, TS3). The main F2 deductions are Li volatility/sealed-tube requirement and air-sensitive Li/Li₃N handling, both supported by glovebox/ampoule practices in Li₈TeN₂ and Li₂MgIr (CS2, SS3, SS5, TS1). Kinetically, Li₃N is a favorable active nitride precursor, but Ir is refractory and likely slow to incorporate [Reasonable Inference], consistent with multi-day Li₂MgIr reaction times (SS3, TS1). Structural reconstruction is moderately difficult because F-43m Li₂RhAl supports the cubic ordered motif, but no exact Li₆IrN analogue is provided (TS3).

#### F2 and F3: Route 2

Route 2 has similar nominal condition severity to Route 1 because it can be done below 1000 °C in sealed systems analogous to Li₂MgIr and Li₈TeN₂ (SS3, TS1, CS2, SS5). However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for direct N₂ nitridation of Li–Ir phases, so this route’s feasibility is more inferential. Kinetically, direct N₂ activation and controlled nitrogen uptake introduce additional uncertainty compared with using Li₃N [Reasonable Inference]. The route risks forming Li₃N separately rather than an ordered Li₆IrN phase, so F3 is lower than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 and F3: Route 3

Route 3 is more severe than Route 1 because flux growth adds multi-step processing and residual Li removal, while still requiring sealed air-free handling (CS1, CS2, SS5). Its kinetic score is slightly higher than Route 1 because molten excess Li can enhance mass transport and crystallization [Reasonable Inference], as implied by Li-rich crystal growth in CS1 and excess-Li single-crystal growth in CS2/SS5. Phase control is the main penalty: CS1 reports minor phases and difficulty obtaining a pure crystalline phase, warning that Li-rich flux may generate competing products. The route remains plausible because the target is itself highly Li-rich and may benefit from a Li-rich chemical potential [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ordered cubic description with Li, Ir, and N sites and no evidence for a large unit cell exceeding 40 atoms (target structure). The Li/Ir size and bonding contrast triggers one amorphization-risk factor [Reasonable Inference], but the polyhedral connectivity described includes corner- and face-sharing rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully met (target structure). Structurally similar Li-rich phases crystallize as ordered intermetallics or ternaries, including Li₃Pd, Li₂MgIr, and Li₂RhAl, which argues against severe amorphization tendency (SS1, SS3, TS3). Remaining uncertainty comes from the absence of a direct Li–Ir–N structural analogue in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆IrN is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, at the favorable cutoff but not clearly below it (target data). The analogue support is moderately strong for the Li-rich sealed-tube chemistry: Li₃N-based ternary nitrides are known (CS2, SS5), Li–Ir incorporation in sealed reactions is known through Li₂MgIr (SS3, TS1), and F-43m Li–M ordered phases are known through Li₂RhAl (TS3). The most important enabling factor is the use of Li₃N plus excess Li in a sealed metal ampoule, which supplies high Li and nitride chemical potentials while limiting Li loss [Reasonable Inference] and is supported by CS2/SS5. The main limiting factor is phase competition and slow Ir incorporation, because no direct Li–Ir–N analogue is given and several Li-rich analogues report phase-purity or crystallization challenges (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS5). Overall, the target is plausible but likely requires careful stoichiometry, sealed handling, and annealing optimization.
