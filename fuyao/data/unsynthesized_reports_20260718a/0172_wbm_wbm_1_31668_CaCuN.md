### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target CaCuN has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the provided target thermodynamic data, so it falls in the highest stability scoring band. TS analogues BaCuN, Sr–Cu–N, Ca₂AuN, Ca₆MnN₅, and Sr₈Cu₃In₄N₅ are all also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5), supporting the idea that alkaline-earth transition-metal nitrides can be true ground states. CS gives the strongest chemical confirmation through CaNiN and Ca₂ZnN₂ on the hull (CS-1, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic and synthetic, not thermodynamic.

**F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
The route uses moderate temperatures because CaNiN was synthesized at 800 °C for 20 h under N₂ and Ca₂ZnN₂ at 680 °C in N₂ (CS-1, CS-3). It avoids GPa pressure and highly reducing gases, but it does require air-sensitive Ca nitride handling and several ceramic-processing steps, as seen in Ca₃N₂-based syntheses (CS-3, SS-3). CS provides strong support; SS/TS do not directly document ambient-pressure CaCuN synthesis, so their signal for this specific route is weaker but not contradictory.

**F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to many nitrides because the isostructural CaNiN analogue forms by ordinary sintering at 800 °C (CS-1). Ca₃N₂ + metal reactions are demonstrated for Ca₂ZnN₂ (CS-3), but Cu metal may react more slowly than a preformed Cu nitride source [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic advantage is structural templating from the CaNiN motif (CS-1); the main uncertainty is whether Cu(I) linear nitride units form cleanly without flux or elevated N₂ pressure.

**F2 — Route 2 Na-flux.**  
Route 2 is more severe operationally because BaCuN and Sr–Cu–N flux syntheses use Na, sealed systems, N₂ pressure, slow cooling, and post-reaction washing (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The pressure is far below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, but the sealed reactive flux and hazardous/air-sensitive reagents justify deductions. SS and TS strongly support this route for Cu-containing alkaline-earth nitrides; CS is less direct, because the chemically closest CaNiN was made by solid-state sintering rather than flux (CS-1).

**F3 — Route 2 Na-flux.**  
Flux chemistry should improve kinetics by dissolving/reacting alkaline-earth and Cu species and enabling crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. BaCuN and Sr–Cu–N compounds with linear Cu–N units were made by Na flux under N₂ pressure at ~800 °C (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2), so the Cu–N bonding motif is strongly supported. The uncertainty is structural selectivity: BaCuN is monoclinic and Sr–Cu–N phases include multiple stoichiometries (SS-1, SS-2), so CaCuN may compete with other Ca–Cu–N phases.

**F2 — Route 3 high-pressure dry-N₂.**  
This route is operationally challenging because Ca₂AuN required a high-pressure N₂ vessel, dry nitrogen, glovebox loading, long heating, and regrinding (SS-3, TS-3). It does not trigger the >5 GPa deduction, because the reported pressures are hundreds of bar rather than gigapascals (SS-3/TS-3). The route is justified mainly when nitrogen chemical potential or Ca nitride stability becomes limiting.

**F3 — Route 3 high-pressure dry-N₂.**  
High nitrogen pressure can help maintain nitride stability and drive N incorporation, as shown for Ca₂AuN (SS-3, TS-3). However, the Ca₂AuN structure is not the target P4₂/mmc CaCuN structure, so the structural-reconstruction score is lower than Route 1. The long reaction time and regrinding in the analogue suggest slower bulk kinetics (SS-3/TS-3).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains chemically/size-distinct Ca and Cu cations, so one amorphization trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is not indicated to be large, and the reported connectivity is corner/edge sharing rather than corner/edge/face sharing; the isostructural CaNiN analogue also uses corner/edge-sharing octahedra (CS-1). Therefore amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCuN is likely a ground-state phase because its target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar alkaline-earth transition-metal nitrides are also on the hull (CS-1, CS-3, SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest positive analogue is CaNiN, which is essentially isostructural with the target P4₂/mmc CaCuN and was synthesized by solid-state annealing at 800 °C under N₂ (CS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the compatibility of Ca²⁺ with a linear d¹⁰ Cu⁺–N motif, which is supported by BaCuN and Sr–Cu–N analogues containing linear Cu–N units (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in the Ca–Cu–N system, because Cu nitrides often require high nitrogen chemical potential or flux conditions in the closest Cu-containing analogues (SS-1, SS-2, TS-1, TS-2). A practical strategy is to attempt the simpler Ca₃N₂/Cu solid-state route first, then move to Na-flux/high-N₂ pressure if Cu incorporation or phase purity is inadequate.
