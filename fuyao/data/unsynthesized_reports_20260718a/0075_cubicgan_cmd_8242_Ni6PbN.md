### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable cutoff for 90–100 in the required rubric. TS analogues demonstrate that phases with similar or slightly higher hull energies, such as MgNi₃-related and Ni/P/Pb-containing systems, can be synthesized but may require careful conditions or even high pressure (TS-1–5). CS and SS contain many ground-state or near-ground-state Ni-rich analogues, but CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Ni–Pb–N stability and SS gives only one close nitride analogue, CdNNi₃ (CS-1–5; SS-1). The remaining uncertainty is whether the F-43m Ni₆PbN ordering is a true accessible low-temperature phase or a metastable local minimum [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from the mild 743 K ammonolysis conditions demonstrated for CdNNi₃ (SS-1). The main deductions are NH₃ atmosphere, multistep grinding/sintering, toxicity/reactivity, and likely need to control volatile Pb by excess Pb source, analogous to excess CdO in SS-1. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for nitrogen chemistry because its closest Ni-rich examples are intermetallic melts (CS-1–5). TS gives insufficient direct evidence for this low-temperature ammonolysis route but supports caution regarding metastability and phase control in near-hull metallic systems (TS-1–5).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CdNNi₃ is a strong kinetic analogue because it forms a cubic nitride with Ni–N = 1.91 Å under NH₃ and uses repeated grinding/sintering to complete reaction (SS-1). Fine Ni powder and PbO should be moderately reactive under NH₃, but Ni metal diffusion and Pb chemical-potential control remain limitations [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction burden is relatively low because the target and CdNNi₃ share A-site cuboctahedra and NNi₆ octahedra motifs (SS-1; target). Uncertainty remains because Pb is larger/heavier and may prefer metallic Pb or PbO impurity formation rather than the nitride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the controlled-atmosphere and multi-step nature of arc/induction melting plus annealing used for Ni-rich intermetallics (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-2–5). It also adds an NH₃ nitridation step from SS-1, increasing severity. Pb volatility and toxicity add risk [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS strongly support making Ni-rich alloys, but they provide insufficient evidence that those alloys can be nitrided into Ni₆PbN.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Pre-alloying may improve Ni–Pb homogeneity, consistent with repeated remelting for homogeneous intermetallics in CS/SS examples (CS-1, CS-4; SS-2). However, nitrogen insertion into dense Ni–Pb alloy is likely slower than direct oxide/ammonolysis powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Structural transformation from a Ni–Pb alloy to the F-43m nitride is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS. Therefore kinetics are scored lower than Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high bulk temperatures and high pressure, but it requires vacuum deposition and reactive N₂/NH₃ plasma [Reasonable Inference]. Pb toxicity and volatility create a significant condition-control burden [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly supports thin-film synthesis, so this route is speculative. TS analogues only indirectly motivate it by showing that near-hull metallic phases can be metastable or condition-sensitive (TS-1, TS-3, TS-5).

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Thin-film deposition can bypass long-range bulk diffusion, which is favorable for a slightly metastable compound [Reasonable Inference]. However, simultaneous control of Ni, volatile Pb, and nitrogen incorporation is likely narrow-window chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Lack of direct thin-film analogue evidence keeps the crystallization-pathway score moderate. The CdNNi₃ excess-CdO requirement suggests that volatile A-site control is important, but this is only an indirect analogy (SS-1).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally compact, with simple Ni₆PbN stoichiometry and no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell in the supplied description. The main amorphization concern is the large size/coordination mismatch between Pb and Ni [Reasonable Inference], while the target’s polyhedral connectivity reports corner and face sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face coexistence. SS-1 supports a low-complexity nitride framework analogue, reducing amorphization concern. Remaining uncertainty is that Pb volatility and low-temperature nitridation could yield nanocrystalline or partially amorphous products if crystallization is incomplete [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₆PbN is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, at the upper edge of the most favorable rubric category. The strongest support comes from the structurally similar CdNNi₃ nitride, which shares Ni–N = 1.91 Å and an A-site cuboctahedral nitride framework and is synthesized under NH₃ at moderate temperature (SS-1). Chemically similar Ni-rich intermetallics show that Ni-rich frameworks can be made by melting/annealing, but they do not directly prove nitrogen incorporation into Ni–Pb–N (CS-1–5). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to an experimentally synthesized NH₃-derived nickel nitride perovskite analogue (SS-1). The most critical limiting factor is Pb chemical-potential control: Pb/PbO volatility, toxicity, and possible competing Pb–Ni or PbO-containing phases may narrow the synthetic window [Reasonable Inference].
