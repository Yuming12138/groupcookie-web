### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high-stability/metastability boundary in the supplied rubric. Several TS analogues with E_hull ≈0.048–0.056 eV/atom are reported as experimentally synthesizable, including Na₅WNO₄ at 0.048 eV/atom and Na₃Cu₂SbO₆ at 0.056 eV/atom (TS2, TS5). This supports that ~0.05 eV/atom does not preclude synthesis, although the TS set is not chemically close to Na–Se–O and therefore gives only thermodynamic, not chemical, support.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because it likely needs sealed handling of volatile/reactive SeO₃ and air-sensitive Na₂O [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed Ag/Supremax or silver-bomb methods used for alkali tellurates (CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2). The temperature is not extreme because analogues form at ~700–740 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is that SeO₃ volatility may force lower temperatures or stronger containment than TeO₃ analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because binary alkali oxides and SeO₃ should be more reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], but analogous Te phases require 28–45 d anneals, indicating slow long-range ordering (CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2). The presence of TeO₆ octahedral analogues supports structural transfer (CS3, SS1, SS2), but complete reconstruction from binary oxides remains necessary [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct kinetic evidence from TS exists for this specific Na–Se–O reaction.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has harsher condition severity because peroxide oxidation adds overpressure and redox-control risk [Reasonable Inference], although SS4 demonstrates Na₂O₂ oxidation in a closed silver cylinder at 580 °C. SeO₂ volatility and toxicity add additional risk [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct peroxide-route evidence for selenium oxides, so SS4 is the primary route basis.

#### F3 — Route 2

Peroxide/SeO₂ chemistry should be kinetically active and lower the reaction temperature relative to long sealed oxide anneals [Reasonable Inference], and SS4 shows a sealed peroxide oxidation can form Na-rich oxide crystals. However, Se(IV) must be oxidized to Se(VI), and incomplete oxidation may yield selenites or normal sodium selenates rather than Na₆SeO₆ [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 supports that oxidative high-valent oxide synthesis can require quenching and careful phase control, but it is not chemically specific to Se.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids the most severe high-temperature sealed oxide requirement, and CS1 directly supports molten NaOH/Na-rich oxide synthesis under dry N₂. It still requires hygroscopic/corrosive alkali reagents and likely containment to avoid selenium loss [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give insufficient direct evidence for NaOH-flux synthesis of selenates, so this route is supported primarily by CS1 plus chemical extrapolation.

#### F3 — Route 3

The flux route receives the best kinetic score because molten NaOH/Na₂O media should provide high ion mobility and high sodium chemical potential [Reasonable Inference], and CS1 demonstrates formation of Na₄GeO₄ by reacting sodium germanate with NaOH. Starting from Na₂SeO₄ retains Se(VI)–O connectivity, reducing reconstruction relative to SeO₂ oxidation [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that flux removal and product stability toward moisture may be problematic, as several alkali oxide analogues are air/moisture sensitive (CS3, SS2, SS4, SS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Na⁺ and Se⁶⁺ [Reasonable Inference], and a likely large Fm-3m conventional cell [Reasonable Inference], producing two amorphization-risk triggers. Its connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the most severe mixed-connectivity trigger is not met. The medium F4 score reflects that crystallization should be possible but may require slow cooling or long annealing, as seen for many structurally related Na/K oxides (CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2, SS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆SeO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, and TS analogues near this energy are experimentally reported (TS2, TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically and structurally related alkali oxotellurates containing TeO₆ octahedra prepared by sealed alkali oxide reactions (CS2, CS3, SS1, SS2), while CS1 supports a potentially faster molten-alkali route. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high Na₂O activity, because the target is unusually Na-rich and analogues often use Na₂O/K₂O or NaOH-rich conditions (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The most critical limiting factor is selenium volatility/redox control, since Se(VI) must be retained without reducing to selenite or losing selenium oxide vapor [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require dry, sealed or strongly alkaline conditions and careful phase screening.
