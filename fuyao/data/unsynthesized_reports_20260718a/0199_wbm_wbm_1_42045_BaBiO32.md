### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it directly on the convex hull and justifying a very high F1 score. Multiple close analogues are also on or near the hull, including SrBi₂O₄ (CS1), MgBi₂O₆ (SS2, TS2), NaBiO₃ (SS1, TS1), SrPbO₃ (TS3), and BaPbO₃ near 0 eV/atom (TS4). The main uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for Bi(V) oxides that can decompose or reduce (SS2, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 1 hydrothermal severity

Route 1 uses mild hydrothermal conditions directly supported by MgBi₂O₆ synthesis at 200 °C for 24 h in a Teflon-lined autoclave (SS2, TS2). No GPa pressure, high temperature, or special gas atmosphere is required by the analogue (SS2, TS2). The only severity deduction is precursor hazard, because NaBiO₃ is oxidizing and Ba salts are toxic [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives some Ba hydrothermal precedent but only under much harsher Ba₂Ir₃O₉ conditions at 650 °C/150 MPa (CS5), so CS is a weaker positive signal for this low-temperature route.

#### F3 — Route 1 hydrothermal kinetics

The MgBi₂O₆ analogue demonstrates that ABi₂O₆ Bi(V) frameworks can form at 200 °C in 24 h from NaBiO₃·nH₂O and metal chlorides (SS2, TS2), indicating high precursor activity and solution-mediated ion transport. Structural reconstruction is limited because the target and MgBi₂O₆ share hydrophilite-derived ABi₂O₆ connectivity (target description; SS2, TS2). The remaining uncertainty is Ba²⁺ size mismatch relative to Mg²⁺, which may slow ordering or force a different Ba coordination [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 ion-exchange severity

AgBiO₃ is prepared by ion exchange of NaBiO₃·nH₂O with AgNO₃ at 70 °C for 1 day, with no special atmosphere or pressure (SS1, SS5, TS1). This makes Route 2 very mild by the F2 rubric. The toxic/oxidizing precursor deduction remains because Ba salts and NaBiO₃ are hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Ba²⁺ ion exchange into Bi(V) oxide frameworks, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 ion-exchange kinetics

The NaBiO₃ → AgBiO₃ exchange evidence shows that related Bi(V) frameworks can undergo low-temperature cation exchange (SS1, SS5, TS1). The target formula BaBi₂O₆ is charge-compatible with replacing two Na⁺ by one Ba²⁺ in two formula units of NaBiO₃ [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic uncertainty is significant because Ba²⁺ is larger and divalent, so ion exchange may require vacancies, hydration, or reconstructive rearrangement [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 solid-state severity

Solid-state analogues use accessible furnace conditions: SrBi₂O₄ at 750 °C for 4 days (CS1) and BaPbO₃ at 800–930 °C in air with grinding and annealing (TS4). These conditions avoid GPa pressure and extremely high temperature. However, the multi-step ceramic schedule and hazardous oxidizing precursors justify deductions (TS4; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS MgBi₂O₆ data warn that pentavalent bismuth oxides decompose above 380 °C (SS2, TS2), making severity chemically more problematic than the nominal temperature suggests.

#### F3 — Route 3 solid-state kinetics

CS1 and TS4 show that related alkaline-earth Bi/Pb oxides can be made by solid-state routes, but both require long or repeated high-temperature treatments. Binary oxides/carbonates have lower precursor activity than hydrothermal salts, and forming BaBi₂O₆ from them likely requires complete structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The major kinetic/chemical risk is that Bi(V) stability may be lost during the high-temperature dwell, consistent with the MgBi₂O₆ decomposition warning (SS2, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 4 high-O₂ severity

High-pressure oxygen synthesis is supported by SrPbO₃, which required >3500 atm O₂ at 450 °C (TS3). Although this is below the >5 GPa deduction threshold, high-pressure O₂ is a controlled reactive atmosphere and requires specialized equipment (TS3). The temperature itself is mild, but operational severity is high because of high-pressure oxygen handling [Reasonable Inference based on TS3].

#### F3 — Route 4 high-O₂ kinetics

High-O₂ conditions can stabilize high-valent oxide phases, as shown for SrPbO₃ (TS3), and this is relevant because BaBi₂O₆ requires Bi(V) by charge balance [Reasonable Inference]. However, the SrPbO₃/BaPbO₃ analogues are perovskite-like rather than hydrophilite-derived (TS3, TS4), so structural transfer is weaker than for MgBi₂O₆ (SS2, TS2). Solid-state diffusion at 400–500 °C may also be slower than hydrothermal transport [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Ba–O versus Bi–O bond-length contrast, 2.76 Å vs 2.15 Å, consistent with large cation size mismatch and triggering C1 (target description). The target description reports corner-sharing and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing, so C3 is not fully triggered. No evidence indicates a unit cell atom count above 40, so C2 is not triggered. SS/TS analogues with related BiO₆ frameworks crystallize successfully, especially MgBi₂O₆ and NaBiO₃/AgBiO₃, which supports only low–medium amorphization risk (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaBi₂O₆ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from the hydrophilite-derived MgBi₂O₆ analogue, which is an ABi₂O₆ pentavalent bismuth oxide synthesized hydrothermally at 200 °C (SS2, TS2). Additional support comes from NaBiO₃/AgBiO₃ ion-exchange chemistry showing low-temperature accessibility of Bi(V) oxide frameworks (SS1, SS5, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is use of a reactive Bi(V) precursor such as NaBiO₃·nH₂O under low-temperature oxidative hydrothermal or ion-exchange conditions (SS1, SS2, SS5, TS1, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is stabilizing Bi(V) while accommodating large Ba²⁺ in the hydrophilite-derived framework, especially since pentavalent bismuth oxides can be difficult to prepare and may decompose above 380 °C (SS2, TS2).
