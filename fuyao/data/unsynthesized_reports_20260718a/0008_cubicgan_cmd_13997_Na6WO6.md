### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high-synthesizability cutoff in the provided rubric. CS gives strong support because the closest Na–W–O analogue Na4WO5 has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS2). SS and TS also support near-hull accessibility through KNa3WO5 at 0.0039 eV/atom (SS3) and Na5WNO4 at 0.048 eV/atom (SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that Na6WO6 itself is not reported as synthesized in the references, so this may be a low-energy metastable polymorph rather than a known ground state.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because Na4WO5/KNa3WO5 analogues form at 725–750 °C in sealed containers (CS2, SS3). The main severity penalties are sealed alkali oxide handling, multi-step inert processing, and air-sensitive Na2O, all directly suggested by Na2O-rich analogue syntheses (CS2, SS3, SS4). CS and SS are useful here; TS hydrothermal oxonitride data are less relevant to this oxide-only sealed route, so TS is neutral for Route 1 condition severity, score 50.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetic score is limited by very long analogue dwell times of 28–35 d for Na4WO5/KNa3WO5 (CS2, SS3), implying slow ordering or nucleation. Na2WO4 + Na2O gives a chemically preorganized Na–W–O mixture, which improves precursor activity compared with WO3 + Na2O alone (CS2, SS3). The largest uncertainty is whether cubic Fm-3m Na6WO6 is on the same crystallization pathway as lower-symmetry Na4WO5/KNa3WO5.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 benefits from the Na5OsO6 precedent: high-valent alkali-rich octahedral oxides can form at 750 °C after staged reaction (CS3). Severity is increased by reactive Na2O2 and O2 atmosphere, which are explicitly used in the Os analogue synthesis (CS3). SS and TS do not provide a direct Na–W peroxide route; where they only discuss oxonitrides or sulfide/oxo phases, evidence is insufficient from SS/TS for this specific peroxide route and treated as neutral, score 50.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Na2O2 is more reactive than Na2O and may reduce diffusion barriers, as implied by the relatively short 12 h 750 °C Na5OsO6 tempering step (CS3). However, the target is W-based, and no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates peroxide synthesis of Na6WO6 or a Na–W–O peroxide-derived phase. The route is therefore plausible but more speculative than Route 1.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 uses known high-temperature alkali oxide flux/bomb methods, with Na5TaO5/Na5NbO5 made at 1000 °C for 75 d and Li7TaO6 at 1000 °C for 156 d (CS1, SS4, CS4). The conditions are severe mainly due to very long annealing, sealed Ni-bomb equipment, and air-sensitive alkali oxide fluxes (CS1, SS4, CS4). No high pressure above 5 GPa or >1000 °C condition is required by the cited analogues.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth can enhance ion transport and crystallinity, as demonstrated by single-crystal growth of alkali-rich tantalates/niobates (CS1, SS4). Nevertheless, the extremely long 75–156 d anneals in related systems indicate slow structural ordering and narrow phase windows (CS1, SS4, CS4). The route may produce crystals but may also favor off-stoichiometric or flux-incorporated products, especially if Li2O/K2O modifiers are used.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Na/W cation size and charge contrast, which increases ordering difficulty [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m conventional cell likely exceeds 40 atoms for Na6WO6, giving a second amorphization/complexity trigger [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target description only reports corner and edge sharing, not face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check requiring vertex + edge + face coexistence is not fully triggered. Long crystallization times in CS/SS analogues reinforce a medium kinetic ordering risk (CS2, SS3, CS1, SS4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na6WO6 is likely near-metastable rather than clearly ground-state because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, at the threshold where many oxides can be synthesized but may compete with stable neighboring phases. The strongest support comes from CS/SS Na–W–O analogues Na4WO5 and KNa3WO5, which demonstrate that sodium-rich tungstates can be made in sealed Na2O/Na2WO4 systems (CS2, SS3). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high Na2O chemical potential in a sealed inert metal container, as repeatedly required for alkali-rich oxide analogues (CS1, CS2, SS3, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and slow ordering: analogous phases often require 28–156 d anneals and careful sealed-bomb conditions (CS2, SS3, CS4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine, with the best chance coming from Na2WO4 + excess Na2O in a sealed Ni/Au container.
