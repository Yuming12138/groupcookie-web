### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is within the rubric’s highest stability bin but exactly at its upper limit. Synthesized thermodynamic analogues include Na₃As at E_hull = 0.0425 eV/atom and Li₂HgGe at E_hull ≈ 0.0522 eV/atom, showing that phases in this metastability range can be accessible (TS1, TS2). Chemically similar Li₈N₂Se/Te are calculated on the hull and synthesized in sealed ampoules, which further supports Li-rich nitride chemistry (CS1, CS2). The remaining uncertainty is that no TS entry is the exact Li–As–N composition, so decomposition into Li₃N/Li₃As or related binaries remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses 650–750 °C sealed-ampoule conditions extrapolated from Li₈N₂Se/Te at 1023 K, Na₃As at 700 °C, and KLi₂As at 623–823 K (CS1, CS2, CS4; SS5; TS1). These temperatures are moderate for solid-state synthesis and do not require high pressure or reactive gases (CS1, CS2; SS5). Deductions are mainly for sealed-system volatility control and air/toxic precursor handling, consistent with glovebox and sealed Ta/Nb ampoule use in the analogues (CS1, CS2; SS5). Uncertainty remains over whether Li₃As must be pre-synthesized, which would add operational complexity [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Li₃N is a proven reactive precursor in Li-rich nitrides and nitride halides (CS1, CS2, CS5), and Li₃As is chemically matched to the arsenide sublattice based on KLi₂As synthesis from binary arsenides (SS5). Li-only cation diffusion should be favorable [Reasonable Inference], and analogues form below 800 °C (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS5). The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction because the target cubic F-43m phase is not the same as the tetragonal Li₈N₂Se/Te or orthorhombic KLi₂As analogues (CS1, CS2; SS5). Regrinding may be necessary because Li₄NCl required repeated grinding/heating to achieve phase purity (CS5).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct-element arsenide synthesis is supported by Na₃As and KLi₂As, which use sealed ampoules under Ar at moderate high temperature (CS4; SS3, SS5; TS1). The route avoids high pressure and reactive gas atmospheres, so the severity score remains high. Deductions arise from elemental Li/As volatility and toxicity/air sensitivity, which require sealed handling and glovebox conditions [Reasonable Inference] consistent with the analogues (CS4; SS3, SS5). The uncertainty is that direct elemental reaction may overshoot to Li₃As or other Li–As phases before nitride incorporation [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Elemental alkali arsenide reactions are feasible, as shown by Na₃As and KLi₂As (CS4; SS3, SS5; TS1). However, compared with preformed Li₃As, elemental As requires larger reconstruction and homogenization [Reasonable Inference]. KLi₂As phase-purity problems and residual starting compounds show that alkali arsenide ordering can be sensitive (SS5). Therefore, kinetics are adequate but weaker than Route 1.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The flux route uses temperatures comparable to Li₈N₂Se/Te and Na₃As syntheses (CS1, CS2, CS4; TS1), so there is no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty. It is penalized for sealed volatile Li/As chemistry, air-sensitive/toxic components, and extra flux-growth operations [Reasonable Inference]. The need for sealed Ta/Nb ampoules and glovebox handling is directly supported by Li₈N₂Se/Te and KLi₂As analogues (CS1, CS2; SS4, SS5). The uncertainty is practical flux removal without decomposing or oxidizing Li₆AsN [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Excess Li improved single-crystal growth in Li₈N₂Se/Te analogues, giving the main evidence that a Li-rich melt can enhance transport and crystallization (CS1, CS2; SS4). A Li flux should reduce diffusion limitations and buffer Li chemical potential [Reasonable Inference]. The score is limited by phase competition, because KLi₂As products were not fully free of Li₃As/K₃As starting phases despite optimization (SS5). Thus, flux growth is kinetically attractive but compositionally risky.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only Li as a cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The formula and cubic description suggest a compact crystalline cell rather than a large complex cell, and the target connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not the vertex + edge + face coexist required by the rubric. CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify amorphization tendency for Li₆AsN, so their source signal is insufficient and treated as neutral. The low amorphization-risk score assumes crystalline ordering can be reached if air/moisture and stoichiometry are controlled [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆AsN is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, and synthesized TS analogues occur around 0.04–0.055 eV/atom (TS1–TS3). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: chemically similar Li-rich nitride chalcogenides are made from Li₃N in sealed Ta ampoules (CS1, CS2), and structurally/chemically relevant alkali arsenides are made under sealed inert solid-state conditions (CS4; SS3, SS5; TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of chemically matched reactive binary precursors, especially Li₃N and Li₃As, which should reduce reconstruction compared with elemental synthesis [Reasonable Inference] and is supported by Li₃N/Li₂E and Li₃As/K₃As analogue routes (CS1, CS2; SS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with Li₃N, Li₃As, or other Li–As phases, because KLi₂As analogues showed persistent starting-compound impurities and a narrow temperature window (SS5). Overall, the target appears synthetically plausible but likely requires careful sealed-ampoule stoichiometry and iterative annealing.
