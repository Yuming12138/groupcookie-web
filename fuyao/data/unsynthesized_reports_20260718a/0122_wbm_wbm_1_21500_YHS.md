### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and is scored near the top of the 90–100 range. The closest analogue YHSe and related HoHSe also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS/SS/TS, which supports the plausibility of a stable rare-earth hydride chalcogenide family. The main uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exact hydrogen stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because the closest YHSe/HoHSe analogues are made at 700–850 °C and no high pressure is reported (CS, SS, TS). Deductions are required for sealed Ta/vacuum-style handling, sulfur volatility, multi-step hydride/flux processing, and air-sensitive hydride precursors (CS, SS, TS; sulfur volatility is [Reasonable Inference]). The conditions are specialized but standard for rare-earth hydride chalcogenide synthesis.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to oxide solid-state synthesis because YH2 plus chalcogen in alkali-halide flux is directly demonstrated to form YHSe/HoHSe crystals (CS, SS, TS). The target is isostructural with YHSe, so structural reconstruction should be limited (CS, SS, TS). The 7 d hold and hydrogen-sensitive phase window lower the score because they imply slow ordering or narrow chemical potential conditions (CS, SS, TS).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses similar 750–900 °C sealed hydride chemistry to hydride halides and Tb deuteride bromides, so it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation (CS, SS, TS). It still requires controlled atmosphere/sealing, air-sensitive hydrides, and volatile sulfur containment (CS, SS, TS; sulfur-specific risk is [Reasonable Inference]). Its severity is comparable to Route 1, but without the benefit of a flux.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct solid-state kinetics are less favorable because no direct YHS solid-state synthesis is reported, and dense hydride/sulfide products may limit diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The hydride halide analogues demonstrate that sealed 850–900 °C reactions can form hydride-containing solids (CS, SS, TS), but the YHSe analogue specifically used flux to secure complete and fast reaction (CS, SS, TS). Competing binary Y–S or Y–H phases remain an uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is not temperature-extreme because the YPS CVT analogue uses around 1000 °C (SS, TS). However, sealed ampoules, iodine volatility/toxicity, possible sulfur volatility, and prior preparation of YHS make the route operationally demanding [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for CVT and is treated as neutral 50 for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
YPS shows iodine vapor transport can grow rare-earth sulfide-related crystals (SS, TS), but there is no direct evidence that hydrogenated YHS can be transported or retain hydrogen under iodine transport. Hydrogen loss is the major kinetic/compositional concern because YHSe-type chemistry is explicitly sensitive to hydrogen presence (CS, SS, TS). Thus Route 3 is best viewed as a speculative crystal-growth route after bulk YHS discovery.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The structure is simple in composition and does not contain multiple cations or a large unit cell [Reasonable Inference from target formula and P-6m2 description]. The only triggered risk is mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing SY6 connectivity, which is explicitly described for the target. This suggests low–medium amorphization risk, with the larger practical concern being phase competition rather than glass formation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YHS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is strong: YHSe is chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically the closest match and is experimentally flux-grown in the same P-6m2 structure type (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is use of YH2 plus alkali-halide flux in a sealed Ta capsule, because this directly mirrors the successful YHSe/HoHSe family syntheses (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining the correct hydrogen and sulfur chemical potentials; YHSe-type phases are reported to depend on hydrogen presence, and sulfur is expected to be volatile at synthesis temperature (CS, SS, TS; sulfur volatility is [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, Route 1 should be attempted first, while Route 2 is a reasonable powder-screening route and Route 3 is speculative for crystal improvement.
