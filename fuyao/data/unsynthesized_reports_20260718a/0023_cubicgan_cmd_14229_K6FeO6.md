### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E\_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which lies at the favorable boundary of the requested 90–100 scoring bin. TS analogues show that alkali oxides with E\_hull values of 0.0338, 0.0430, 0.0777, and 0.0831 eV/atom have reported syntheses, so a 0.05 eV/atom target is not thermodynamically implausible (TS #1–#4). CS and SS also contain several E\_hull = 0 K-rich oxides, supporting stability in nearby K–M–O chemistry (CS #1–#5; SS #1, #3–#5). The uncertainty is that the exact cubic K6FeO6 phase is not shown as previously synthesized in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because the closest Fe analogue K3FeO3 forms at 600 °C for 7 d in a closed Fe tube under Ar (CS #2; SS #5). The score is penalized for sealed handling of reactive potassium oxides and likely precursor preparation, as KO\_{1.5} and “FeO” are not trivial shelf-stable reagents (CS #2). Moisture sensitivity is a major concern because related K-rich oxides require dry/inert handling and some decompose rapidly in air (CS #5). The remaining uncertainty is whether the lower-oxidation FeO-based route can reach the high formal Fe oxidation implied by K6FeO6 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate-low because the closest Fe analogue exists but has FeO4 tetrahedra rather than the target FeO6 octahedra, implying substantial structural reconstruction (CS #2; SS #5; Target structure). Alkali oxide media likely assist diffusion because related K-rich oxides crystallize at 580–600 °C, far below temperatures normally required for dense oxide equilibration [Reasonable Inference; CS #1, #2]. Long anneals and phase-control challenges are repeatedly reported in K-rich analogues, lowering confidence in rapid formation (CS #5; SS #3, #4). The principal uncertainty is whether an FeO4-containing intermediate would transform into the cubic FeO6 target or instead remain as K3FeO3.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains moderate in severity because peroxide/superoxide flux synthesis is conducted at 400–650 °C in sealed tubes, not at extreme temperature or high pressure (TS #4; CS #1, #2; SS #3). The peroxide/superoxide route has the best kinetic outlook because K2O2/KO2/K2O can act as reactive fluxes and oxygen buffers [Reasonable Inference], and K–Co–O reactions using K2O/KO2 begin near 400 °C (TS #4). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K6FeO6 is likely near the ground-state/metastability boundary rather than a deeply unstable phase, because its E\_hull is 0.050 eV/atom and synthesized thermodynamic analogues extend into the 0.03–0.08 eV/atom range (TS #1–#4). The CS and SS evidence strongly supports the existence of K-rich oxide solid-state chemistry, especially the direct K–Fe–O analogue K3FeO3 made at 600 °C in a closed Fe tube (CS #2; SS #5). The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive sealed-tube alkali oxide/peroxide flux chemistry, which can provide high K activity, oxygen activity, and enhanced transport at moderate temperature (CS #1, #2; TS #4). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: the known K3FeO3 analogue has FeO4 tetrahedra, whereas the target requires cubic FeO6 octahedra and likely high-valent Fe [Reasonable Inference; CS #2; Target structure]. Overall, the material is plausible but will probably require careful K:Fe:O chemical-potential control rather than simple stoichiometric firing.
