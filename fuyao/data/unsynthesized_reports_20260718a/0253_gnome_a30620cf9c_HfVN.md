### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a very high thermodynamic score. Several structurally and thermodynamically similar nitrides, including HfN, ZrN, Ta₂N, Hf₃AlN, and Th₂CrN₃, are also reported with E_hull = 0 in the reference set, supporting stable nitride chemistry in this region (SS1–SS3, SS5, TS1–TS5). CS phosphides likewise mostly have E_hull ≈ 0, but because P chemistry differs from N chemistry, CS is supportive only for refractory ternary stability, not for nitrogen chemical potential control (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is whether the computed HfVN ground state is experimentally accessible before separating into kinetically favored HfN/VN-like binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Bulk nitridation / solid-state sintering

Condition severity is high because HfN/ZrN analogues require 1500–2200 °C, long homogenization, and controlled He/N₂/Ar atmospheres (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). Ta₂N can be made by direct reaction at 1400 °C under N₂, confirming that direct nitridation is plausible but still severe (SS4). Kinetics are poor because Hf/V are refractory cations and the target requires ternary ordering rather than simple binary nitride formation [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives only indirect support through high-temperature phosphide sintering and long equilibration, so CS nitride-specific evidence is insufficient and treated as neutral where relevant (CS1–CS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Reactive sputter deposition

This route scores best on condition severity because Ta₂N sputtering demonstrates nitride formation at low substrate temperature in Ar/N₂ rather than requiring 1500–2200 °C bulk diffusion (SS3, TS3). The main F2 deduction is the need for controlled vacuum/reactive gas and precise N₂ partial pressure, as explicitly reported for Ta₂N sputtering (SS3, TS3). Kinetically, sputtering bypasses refractory cation diffusion and offers direct Hf/V/N co-deposition [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is ternary P4/nmm crystallization and 1:1 Hf:V ordering, since the sputter analogue is binary Ta₂N rather than HfVN (SS3, TS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Arc melting / melt-assisted nitriding

Arc melting is supported by chemically similar HfNbP and TaCrP routes, which use arc melting, high-temperature annealing, and gettered Ar atmospheres (CS2, CS4). This reduces cation-mixing barriers compared with pure powder diffusion, but it still requires very high temperatures and subsequent nitrogen incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS nitride analogues show that nitrogen activity and high-temperature annealing are critical for nitrides (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS3). The key uncertainty is whether a dense Hf–V alloy can be nitrided uniformly into the layered target rather than forming surface HfN/VN or nitrogen gradients [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Flux-assisted growth

Flux growth is inferred from a chemically similar ternary pnictide prepared with Cu flux in an evacuated quartz capsule (CS5). This route could improve mass transport and crystal growth at lower temperature than bulk sintering [Reasonable Inference]. However, SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of Hf–V nitrides, so route-specific SS/TS support is neutral. The largest uncertainty is flux compatibility with refractory nitrides and whether nitrogen activity can be maintained without forming only binary nitrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one main amorphization trigger: significant cation size/coordination mismatch between Hf and V [Reasonable Inference]. The target does not show face-sharing coexistence; its polyhedra are described as corner- and edge-sharing, similar to HfN/ZrN local connectivity (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so the atom-count check is not triggered. Thin-film growth could still produce amorphous films at low substrate temperature, but post-annealing or heated-substrate deposition should mitigate that risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfVN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple related refractory nitrides in SS/TS are also stable ground-state compounds (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/TS nitride analogues, especially HfN for matching Hf–N local bonding and Ta₂N for reactive sputtering under Ar/N₂ (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). CS analogues support the broader idea that Hf–transition-metal ternary refractory compounds can be made, but they are phosphides, so they do not directly solve nitrogen activity or nitride phase-control issues (CS1–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the excellent thermodynamic stability combined with Hf–N bond-length compatibility to known HfN (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: Hf and V may prefer stable binary nitride environments unless the Hf:V:N ratio and nitrogen chemical potential are tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference].
