### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the boundary of the most favorable scoring bin. Similar or slightly less stable bromide compounds are experimentally known, including BiBr₃ at 0.0617 eV/atom (TS1) and PdSe₂Br₆ at 0.0767 eV/atom (TS2), while several chemically/structurally similar bromide clusters are on or close to the hull (CS1–CS4, SS1, SS5). The remaining uncertainty is whether the exact TlSiBr₆ stoichiometry is chemically charge-balanced and experimentally isolable; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for that exact composition.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from low synthesis temperatures: TlMX₄ analogues form at 150–200 °C in sealed ampoules (CS1), and SeBr₃[AlBr₄] forms at 150 °C in a closed ampoule with a gradient (CS3/SS1). The penalties come from vacuum/inert sealed handling, volatile halide precursors, multi-step ampoule processing, and toxic/moisture-sensitive halides (CS1, CS3/SS1; SiBr₄/Tl hazards [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from TS for this exact low-temperature Tl–Si–Br route is treated as neutral for route-specific confidence.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Halide precursors are highly reactive, and chemically similar Tl–metal halide salts form at only 150–200 °C (CS1). The likely transport/melt/vapor behavior of SiBr₄ and metal bromides reduces diffusion barriers relative to refractory solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], consistent with low-temperature ampoule analogues (CS1, CS3). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target and analogues are zero-dimensional bromide cluster salts (CS1, CS3/SS1), but no direct SiBr₆ analogue is supplied.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature and high pressure, supported by SnBr₆ precipitation from bromide-rich solution (CS5) and immediate Sb/Bi bromide precipitation from acid media (SS4). However, strict dry/inert conditions are needed because related molecular halides are moisture-sensitive (CS3/SS1, SS2/TS4), and SiBr₄/HBr volatility requires controlled addition or sealed apparatus [Reasonable Inference]. Toxic thallium and corrosive bromide media further reduce the F2 score [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Solution precipitation should have favorable kinetics because SnBr₆ cluster salts crystallize readily from bromide-rich media (CS5), and Sb/Bi halide salts form immediately on solution mixing (SS4). Diffusion limitations are minimal in solution [Reasonable Inference], and crystallization can be tuned by cooling or solvent choice as in dry-solution halide crystallizations (SS2/TS4). The largest uncertainty is whether SiBr₄ accepts sufficient bromide to form the target SiBr₆ cluster under nonaqueous conditions; CS/SS/TS do not directly prove this.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures comparable to TS2 and CS2/CS4, but it involves sealed ampoules, volatile Br₂/solvent, and possibly autoclave counterpressure. TS2 explicitly uses sealed silica ampoules under N₂ transferred to a steel autoclave with dry-ice counterpressure, while SS3 uses ~200 bar conditions for TlFeBr₄ synthesis. These hazards keep the route challenging despite the absence of >1000 °C or >5 GPa conditions.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Bromine-assisted solvothermal chemistry provides high chemical activity and has produced molecular bromide clusters (TS2), and CVT methods yield bromide clusters at ~150–180 °C (CS2, CS4). Transport media reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. However, TS2 reports multiphase products requiring manual crystal separation, so kinetic access may not translate to phase-pure bulk TlSiBr₆.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s isolated SiBr₆ clusters suggest low network-frustration risk, consistent with many zero-dimensional bromide cluster analogues (CS1, CS3, CS5). The main amorphization/crystallization risk is the large size and bonding mismatch between Tl and Si-centered bromide clusters [Reasonable Inference], although such size segregation is common in molecular salts (CS1, CS5). The lack of mixed polyhedral connectivity lowers the amorphization risk; TS provides insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior and is neutral for this subfactor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlSiBr₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and no exact Tl–Si–Br analogue is supplied. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of low-dimensional bromide cluster salts using sealed ampoule or solution methods (CS1, CS3, CS5, SS1, SS4), while TS analogues show that bromides near this energy range can be synthesized (TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of molecular bromide precursors and bromide-rich media, which can assemble zero-dimensional clusters at low temperature (CS1, CS3, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is chemical specificity: CS/SS/TS do not directly confirm TlSiBr₆ or SiBr₆ cluster stabilization, and the nominal formula may be charge-balance-challenging under common Tl⁺/Si⁴⁺ assumptions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful anhydrous bromide-rich crystallization and rigorous phase identification.
