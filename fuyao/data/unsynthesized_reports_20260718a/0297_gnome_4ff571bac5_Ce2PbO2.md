### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom (Target), so the thermodynamic score is very high. Ce₂BiO₂ and Ce₂TeO₂ analogues in the same alpha-BiF₃-derived structure are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1, TS3), supporting that this topology can be a ground-state motif for Ce₂XO₂ compositions. The main uncertainty is that DFT thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or retention of volatile Pb [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state

Route 1 is strongly supported by R₂O₂Bi analogues synthesized from rare-earth metal/oxide/Bi in evacuated ampoules with Ar-glovebox handling and multi-step firing (CS2, CS3). Its severity score is reduced by controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile Pb management, multi-step grinding, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents; these issues are directly analogous to sealed ampoule/glovebox requirements in CS2–CS4 and extrapolated for Pb toxicity/volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderate because CeO₂ is refractory and solid-state mixing can be slow, but isostructural Ce₂BiO₂ substantially lowers structural uncertainty (Target, CS1, SS3, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is the lack of demonstrated Pb incorporation in this family.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: high-temperature Ta ampoule

Route 2 is based on RE₂BiO₂ compounds synthesized in sealed Ta ampoules under dynamic vacuum, including high-temperature treatment at 1770 K for 72 h (CS5). Its condition severity is high because the analogue route uses very high temperature, dynamic vacuum, sealed ampoules, and likely air-sensitive intermetallic precursors (CS5). Kinetically, a preformed Ce–Pb intermetallic could improve cation mixing by analogy to REBi use (CS5), but the elevated temperature increases Pb loss risk [Reasonable Inference]. This route is a robust backup for phase formation but less attractive than flux growth.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Pb self-flux

Route 3 receives the best balance because flux growth analogues show that liquid metal or salt flux can enable Ce-containing oxides at lower effective temperatures and with controlled slow cooling (SS1, SS2, TS2). Pb self-flux is an extrapolation, but it directly addresses both Pb supply and mass transport [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are expected to be favorable because liquid Pb reduces diffusion limitations, while the structural target is closely matched to Ce₂BiO₂ (Target, CS1, SS3, TS1). The major uncertainty is whether the correct oxygen activity can be maintained without forming Ce oxides or Pb oxides.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: hydrothermal gold-tube

Route 4 has low nominal thermal severity because Ce₂BiO₂/Ce₂TeO₂ entries list 600 °C, one week, 1000 bar in sealed gold tubes (CS1, SS3, SS4, TS1, TS3). However, those entries contain precursor snippets for RbAlSi₃O₈ gel rather than Ce–Bi–O or Ce–Te–O chemistry, so the useful chemical signal is weak; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for real Ce–Pb–O hydrothermal chemistry. Kinetics could be good if dissolution–reprecipitation occurs [Reasonable Inference], but redox control for Ce/Pb would be challenging. This route is exploratory rather than the primary recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small, simple tetragonal I4/mmm alpha-BiF₃-derived structure and only corner/edge-sharing OCe₄ tetrahedra, not a large mixed-connectivity framework with face sharing (Target). Ce₂BiO₂ has the same structural topology and essentially identical Ce–O/Ce–Bi distances to the target Ce–O/Ce–Pb distances (CS1, SS3, TS1), suggesting low structural frustration. The remaining uncertainty is the unusual electron count/oxidation-state description of Pb in an oxide-like framework [Reasonable Inference], but this is not captured by the amorphization checks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ce₂PbO₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target), and the closest Ce₂BiO₂/Ce₂TeO₂ analogues are also on the hull (TS1, TS3). The structural support is strong: Ce₂PbO₂ is nearly identical to Ce₂BiO₂ in space group, topology, Ce–X distance, and Ce–O distance (Target, CS1, SS3, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly analogous Ce₂BiO₂ alpha-BiF₃-derived phase and broader R₂O₂Bi synthesis precedent in sealed ampoules (CS1–CS5). The most critical limiting factor is Pb volatility/toxicity and the need to maintain a narrow reducing oxygen chemical potential so Ce oxides and Pb oxides do not dominate [Reasonable Inference]. A Pb-rich sealed flux route is the most attractive because it simultaneously buffers Pb activity and accelerates cation transport [Reasonable Inference], while remaining consistent with flux-growth evidence for Ce-containing oxides (SS1, SS2, TS2).
