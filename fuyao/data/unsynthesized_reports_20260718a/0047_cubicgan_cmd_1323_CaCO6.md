### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the boundary of the highest scoring bin in the supplied rubric. Chemically similar CaCO₃ high-pressure polymorphs are much closer to the hull, with E_hull values near 0.001–0.007 eV/atom (CS2–CS4), while the hydrothermal YCO₄ analogue is 0.0716 eV/atom and still reported as synthesizable (CS1). Thermodynamic analogues at ~0.061–0.075 eV/atom are synthesizable but often need hydrothermal or HP conditions (TS2, TS4, TS5), so the thermodynamics are favorable but not decisive.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly because high pressure above 5 GPa is required by analogy to MgSiO₆ synthesis at 10 GPa (SS1) and HP oxide synthesis at 7.5–9 GPa (TS2, TS4). It also requires an oxidizing capsule environment because CaCO₆ is oxygen-rich relative to CaCO₃ [Reasonable Inference supported by TS2/TS4]. The uncertainty is whether oxygen chemical potential plus pressure is sufficient to stabilize C in CO₆ rather than decomposing or reverting to carbonate (CS2–CS4).

#### F3 — Route 1

The precursor activity is moderate because CaCO₃ is directly pressure-transformable in several analogues (CS2–CS4), but CaCO₃ is still a relatively stable carbonate [Reasonable Inference]. Ion diffusion is helped by high pressure and moderate temperature, but SS1 required 205 h at 10 GPa/450 °C, indicating slow ordering can occur. Structural reconstruction is the dominant kinetic penalty because all CaCO₃ analogues contain trigonal planar CO₃ units, while the target contains CO₆ octahedra (CS2–CS4).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses milder hydrothermal temperatures similar to 220–230 °C examples in CS1, SS3, and SS4. It is penalized for sealed aqueous/oxidizing conditions and multi-step preparation/recovery, which are common in hydrothermal syntheses (CS1, TS5). The largest uncertainty is that ordinary hydrothermal pressure may be far too low to stabilize octahedral carbon, since the closest octahedral oxyanion analogue requires 10 GPa (SS1).

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal media give high precursor activity and good mass transport, as shown by carbonate hydroxide formation at 220 °C for 4 days (CS1) and hydrothermal oxide crystals at ~230 °C (SS3, SS4). However, phase selectivity can be poor in hydrothermal oxyanion systems, as TS5 reports multiple by-products. The structural reconstruction score is low because no hydrothermal analogue in CS/SS/TS shows C changing from trigonal CO₃ to octahedral CO₆.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids external high pressure, but it requires sealed oxidizing conditions, hazardous oxidizers, and repeated solid-state processing [Reasonable Inference from SS5 and TS2/TS4]. Ceramic methods can require high temperature and repeated grinding for oxide phase formation (SS5), but the proposed target likely cannot tolerate very high temperature at ambient pressure [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, the conditions are less technically demanding than HP synthesis but chemically riskier for phase stability.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state diffusion at ≤600 °C is likely sluggish for constructing a new Ca–C–O framework [Reasonable Inference]. SS5 shows that oxide ceramic synthesis can need 1300–1400 °C and repeated grinding, which is incompatible with maintaining an oxygen-rich carbonate-derived phase at ambient pressure [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of any ambient-pressure CO₆ carbon analogue in CS/SS/TS makes the structural reconstruction barrier severe.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large size/coordination mismatch between Ca-centered cuboctahedra and carbon-centered octahedra, triggering the cation-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide evidence for a unit cell atom count above 40, so that check is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS. The connectivity includes corner-sharing CaO₁₂–CaO₁₂ and face-sharing CaO₁₂–CO₆ but not the full vertex+edge+face mixture, so amorphization risk is judged low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCO₆ is likely a metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a robust ambient-pressure ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and its CO₆ carbon coordination is unlike normal carbonate analogues (CS2–CS4). The CS evidence strongly supports Ca–C–O chemistry and pressure-induced CaCO₃ polymorphism, but it does not directly support CaCO₆ stoichiometry or octahedral carbon (CS2–CS4). The SS evidence is important because it shows an octahedral light-element oxyanion framework, MgSiO₆, requiring 10 GPa high-pressure synthesis (SS1). The most critical enabling factor is simultaneous high pressure and high oxygen chemical potential, inferred from SS1 and TS2/TS4. The most critical limiting factor is reconstructing stable trigonal carbonate units into CO₆ octahedra without decomposing, reverting, or forming ordinary CaCO₃/CaO₂ phases [Reasonable Inference from CS2–CS4].
