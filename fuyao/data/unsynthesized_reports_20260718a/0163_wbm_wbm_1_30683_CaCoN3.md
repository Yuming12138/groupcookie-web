### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. TS analogues include multiple ground-state alkaline-earth N-rich compounds, including CaN₂, SrN₂, BaN₂, and Sr₃Co(CN)₃ (TS-1–TS-4). This supports the plausibility of a stable Ca–N-rich compound, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that the exact CaCoN₃ phase has been synthesized.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits moderate solid-state conditions from Ca₂PN₃ and LiPN₂, both synthesized around 800 °C under sealed N₂ environments (CS-1, CS-2). The severity increases because the target likely needs high nitrogen chemical potential and moisture-free handling, while Co incorporation is only indirectly supported by Sr₃Co(CN)₃ synthesis under Ar at ~1200 K (TS-4). Insufficient evidence from SS supports this bulk nitride route because SS analogues are mostly molecular/complex azides rather than extended Ca–Co nitrides.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Binary nitrides can react successfully at 800 °C in CS nitride analogues (CS-1, CS-2), but CS-1 required 14 d, indicating slow diffusion/order development. Co metal is less reactive than molecular or salt precursors [Reasonable Inference], although TS-4 shows Co can enter alkaline-earth nitrogen-containing frameworks under high-temperature solid-state conditions. Structural reconstruction is substantial because the target contains both trigonal-planar CoN₃ and N–N units, which are not directly present in Ca₃N₂/Co starting mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized mainly for high pressure, because CaN₂ synthesis specifically uses 12 GPa and 800 °C (TS-3). The azide precursors are hazardous, moisture sensitive, and require careful glovebox and high-pressure capsule handling, as described for alkaline-earth diazenide syntheses (TS-1–TS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS provides high-pressure support, because CS nitride examples use ambient-pressure sealed-tube methods instead.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Azide decomposition gives highly active nitrogen and short N–N-containing products, demonstrated by CaN₂/SrN₂/BaN₂ synthesis from alkaline-earth azides (TS-1–TS-3). The short 15–20 min high-pressure holds in TS-1–TS-3 indicate favorable kinetics once the precursor decomposes under pressure. The main uncertainty is Co ordering into the CaCoN₃ framework, because TS-1–TS-3 do not include transition metals and SS transition-metal azides are structurally similar but chemically distinct (SS-1–SS-5).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from mild solution conditions, as structurally similar azides crystallize at room temperature to mild heating in aqueous/ethanol media (SS-1–SS-5; CS-3–CS-5). It is still penalized for toxic/explosive azide chemistry, possible HN₃ volatility, and the required extra conversion step. Insufficient evidence from TS supports solution synthesis of the final CaCoN₃ phase, because TS N–N phases use high-pressure decomposition rather than solution crystallization.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution crystallization has excellent precursor mobility and avoids solid-state diffusion, consistent with room-temperature precipitation/diffusion routes for Cu, Cd, and Au azides (SS-1–SS-5). However, the likely initial product would be a molecular/ionic Ca–Co azide, not necessarily the target extended CaCoN₃ framework [Reasonable Inference]. The topochemical conversion step is the weak point, and no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates conversion of a Ca–Co double azide into an anhydrous CaCoN₃ lattice.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically different cations, Ca and Co, with a large expected size mismatch, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description reports edge-sharing and corner/edge-sharing motifs, but not face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40-atom unit cell or strong glass-forming tendency, so the amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCoN₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues show that N-rich alkaline-earth compounds with N–N units can be true ground-state phases (TS-1–TS-3). The strongest synthesis support comes from TS high-pressure azide decomposition, especially CaN₂ from Ca(N₃)₂ at 12 GPa and 800 °C (TS-3). CS data supports conventional nitride solid-state synthesis near 800 °C under sealed N₂, but those analogues lack Co and do not directly address N–N retention (CS-1, CS-2). SS data strongly supports azide coordination/crystallization chemistry but mostly for molecular or complex azides, so it is more useful for precursor design than direct CaCoN₃ synthesis (SS-1–SS-5). The key enabling factor is high nitrogen chemical potential from azides or sealed N₂ nitride chemistry; the key limiting factor is controlling hazardous azide decomposition while simultaneously ordering Ca and Co into the target framework.
