### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which places it at the favorable boundary of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. CS analogues include stable or nearly stable refractory compounds such as AuTa₅S and Re₇B₃ (CS-1; CS-5), and SS/TS include Re₂N with E_hull ≈ 0.054 eV/atom (SS-1; TS-1). The score is high but not perfect because 0.05 eV/atom suggests the phase may be metastable rather than a deep ground state [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 ammonolysis.**  
TS-2 is the strongest evidence because it directly demonstrates a ternary nitride made from mixed metal chloride precursors under NH₃ at 950 °C. The route benefits from high precursor activity and molecular-scale mixing, but it carries penalties for NH₃, air-sensitive/volatile chlorides, and multi-step precursor chemistry (TS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). CS gives insufficient evidence from CS for ammonolysis and is neutral, while SS only partially supports Re–N formation through high-pressure binary nitrides (SS-1).

**F2/F3 — Route 2 high-pressure synthesis.**  
SS-1 and TS-1 strongly indicate that Re nitrides can require high-pressure/high-temperature conditions, which supports this route but also makes it severe. Kinetics are limited because elemental Re is refractory and diffusion is slow, and adding Al into an ordered cubic nitride is not directly demonstrated by any source [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence from CS for high-pressure nitridation and is neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 arc-melt + nitridation.**  
CS-5 and SS-2/SS-4 support arc melting for Re-rich refractory phases, including long high-temperature treatments and inert atmospheres. However, none of CS/SS/TS directly proves that nitrogen can be inserted into an Al–Re alloy at ambient pressure, so the nitridation step is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. Slow ordering and phase control are major concerns because Re-rich analogues require high temperatures, long anneals, or difficult phase equilibration (CS-5; SS-4).

**F2/F3 — Route 4 thin film.**  
No source directly reports thin-film synthesis, so CS/SS/TS are neutral for the deposition method. The route is favored by [Reasonable Inference] because near-hull metastable nitrides are often kinetically accessible in thin films, and reactive nitrogen plasma can supply high nitrogen activity without high bulk diffusion distances. The main uncertainty is crystallization of the exact F-43m AlRe₆N phase and tight control of the Al:Re = 1:6 ratio.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple AlRe₆N formula and only one clear amorphization trigger, the size mismatch between Al and Re, which is a [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports corner- and face-sharing polyhedra but not edge-sharing, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face connectivity criterion is not met. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization tendency in this exact target and are therefore neutral for F4 beyond structural analogy.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlRe₆N is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state compound because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom. The strongest direct chemical support comes from TS-2, which shows that ternary metal nitrides with N-centered metal octahedra can be synthesized by ammonolysis, while SS-1/TS-1 show that Re–N bonding is experimentally accessible but may require high pressure. The most critical enabling factor is the favorable thermodynamic proximity to the hull combined with short-diffusion or high-activity synthesis routes such as thin-film deposition or molecular precursor ammonolysis. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing binary Re nitrides, Al–Re intermetallics, and Al loss may prevent formation of the ordered F-43m AlRe₆N structure. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require non-equilibrium control or carefully optimized nitrogen chemical potential.
