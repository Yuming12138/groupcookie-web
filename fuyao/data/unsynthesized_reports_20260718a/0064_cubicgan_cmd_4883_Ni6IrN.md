### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the highest scoring thermodynamic bin and suggests it is either near-ground-state or mildly metastable. SS-1 supports that a closely related Ni nitride motif is low in energy, with Ni₃N at E_hull = 0.0147 eV/atom and the same Ni–N distance of 1.87 Å. TS analogues include materials with E_hull values near 0.056–0.103 eV/atom, showing that phases in this metastability range can be experimentally accessed, although several TS examples require harsh or specialized conditions (TS-1 to TS-4). CS gives stable Ni-rich intermetallic examples but no nitride chemistry, so CS is neutral/insufficient for direct Ni–Ir–N stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because the closest nitride analogue Ni₃N was synthesized at ~10 GPa and ~1800 K in supercritical nitrogen using DAC/YAG laser heating (SS-1). CS supports the preliminary alloying step by arc-melting/annealing under Ar or vacuum, but that adds steps rather than simplifying conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). TS also contains high-pressure examples, but they are not direct nitrides and therefore only generally support pressure-stabilized synthesis, not this exact chemistry. The remaining uncertainty is whether Ir incorporation into the Ni nitride framework lowers or raises the required N pressure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The local NNi₆ octahedron is strongly supported by SS-1, which has Ni–N = 1.87 Å, matching the target. However, the target additionally requires long-range Ni/Ir ordering into IrNi₁₂ cuboctahedra, so structural reconstruction is not trivial [Reasonable Inference]. Ir is refractory and likely slow-diffusing, while CS/SS alloy analogues often require repeated melting and annealing for homogeneity (CS-2, CS-4, SS-2, SS-4). Thus kinetics are moderate-to-poor despite the favorable local nitride motif.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids GPa pressure, which is a major advantage over SS-1, and it uses the well-supported arc-melting/annealing style common to Ni-rich analogues (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-2, SS-4). However, introducing nitrogen at ambient pressure likely requires NH₃, activated N₂, or a reactive nitrogen donor [Reasonable Inference], and NH₃ introduces toxicity/reactive-atmosphere penalties. CS provides strong evidence for making Ni-rich intermetallics but insufficient evidence for nitrogen uptake into those intermetallics. Therefore, the route is less severe than high-pressure nitridation but chemically more uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The Ni–Ir metallic framework can plausibly be homogenized by melting/annealing because many Ni-rich analogues are made this way (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-2). Nitridation of a dense alloy is likely diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], and SS-1 suggests high nitrogen activity may be needed to form Ni nitride motifs. Long anneals in related Ni-rich systems, including one week at 970 K and up to 35 days at 1100 °C, indicate slow equilibration/ordering can be significant (CS-2, CS-5, SS-4). The main uncertainty is whether N can enter the pre-formed Ni–Ir lattice without decomposing to Ni₃N plus Ni–Ir alloys [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is scored as the least severe because it avoids both GPa pressure and sustained >1000 °C bulk heat treatments [Reasonable Inference]. The penalty comes from vacuum/plasma processing and possible NH₃ annealing, which are controlled/reactive atmospheres. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports sputtering of this phase, so this route is an extrapolation rather than evidence-backed processing. The justification is that thin films often stabilize mildly metastable compounds with limited diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Thin-film sputtering can overcome slow bulk diffusion of refractory Ir by atomic-scale mixing during deposition [Reasonable Inference]. N₂ plasma provides activated nitrogen, while SS-1 confirms that Ni–N octahedral chemistry is viable, though only under high-pressure bulk synthesis. The main kinetic risk is crystallizing the desired ordered cubic F-43m structure rather than an amorphous nitride, Ni₃N-like phase, or Ni–Ir alloy [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is also nontrivial because the target requires Ni:Ir:N = 6:1:1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is structurally compact and not a large-complexity cell, so the unit-cell atom count criterion is not triggered. The described connectivity includes corner and face sharing, but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not triggered. The Ni/Ir size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% in a metallic-radius sense [Reasonable Inference], although CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify this. Overall, the crystalline topology appears relatively simple, so amorphization risk is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₆IrN is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and SS/TS analogues show experimentally accessible materials in this low-metastability range (SS-1, TS-1 to TS-4). The strongest positive evidence is structural: Ni₃N has the same NNi₆ octahedral motif and the same Ni–N distance of 1.87 Å as the target (SS-1 and target description). The strongest processing evidence for the metal framework comes from CS/SS Ni-rich intermetallics routinely made by arc-melting and annealing under Ar/vacuum (CS-1 to CS-5, SS-2, SS-4). The most critical enabling factor is achieving high enough nitrogen chemical potential while preserving the Ni–Ir ordered framework [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is competition between Ni–Ir alloy phases and simpler Ni nitrides, especially because CS provides no direct evidence for N incorporation into Ni-rich intermetallics and SS-1 used very high pressure for nitride synthesis.
