### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is maximal under the given rubric (target data). The closest analogues La₃Ta₂N₆ and La₃Nb₂N₆ are also reported as hull-stable with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). This strongly supports Eu₃Ta₂N₆ as a likely ground-state or near-ground-state nitride, although DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Li₃N/Li Flux

The condition severity is moderate-to-high because the closest route uses 1030 °C, sealed Ta/Nb ampoules, long holds, and air-sensitive Li₃N (CS-1, CS-3). This is less severe than the 1600 °C Ga flux route but still requires welded refractory-metal containment and inert handling (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for GPa pressure, so no high-pressure penalty was applied.

#### F3 — Route 1: Li₃N/Li Flux

Kinetics are helped by Li₃N acting as both flux and reactive nitrogen source, as explicitly reported for La₃Ta₂N₆/La₃Nb₂N₆ (CS-1, CS-3). The Ta source is refractory and would be slow in a purely solid-state route [Reasonable Inference], but flux transport lowers the kinetic barrier relative to Ta melting/diffusion limits (CS-1, CS-3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target is essentially an Eu-substituted La₃Ta₂N₆ I4/mmm analogue (target data; CS-1, SS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2: Ga Flux

The Ga flux route is penalized by the 1600 °C synthesis temperature used for La₃Ta₂N₆/La₃Nb₂N₆ (CS-1, CS-3). It also involves controlled Ar/N₂ atmospheres and multiple handling/growth/isolation steps (CS-1, CS-3). No high-pressure penalty is applied because CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for high pressure in Ga flux synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2: Ga Flux

Kinetics benefit from molten Ga flux and from the fact that the exact La₃Ta₂N₆ analogue forms by this route (CS-1, SS-1). However, starting from Eu/Ta metals and N₂ is less chemically activated than Li₃N as nitrogen source [Reasonable Inference]. The very high temperature may improve diffusion but may also worsen Eu volatility/reactivity risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Solid-State Nitride

Solid-state nitride synthesis is supported by SrHfN₂ at 1000 °C for 5 days under Ar and actinide nitrides at 1200–1600 °C in N₂ (CS-2, SS-5, TS-3). The route requires sealed/inert handling, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive nitride precursors (CS-2, SS-5). It avoids flux removal but has harsher kinetic constraints than the flux routes [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Solid-State Nitride

The kinetic score is low because Ta-containing nitride phases are diffusion-limited in bulk solids [Reasonable Inference], and the SrHfN₂ analogue required a 5-day anneal with impurity concerns (CS-2, SS-5). No exact solid-state precedent for Ln₃Ta₂N₆ is provided; the exact analogues are flux-grown instead (CS-1, CS-3). Therefore structural reconstruction from Eu/Ta binary nitrides is expected to be substantial [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4: Alkali-Amide / Ammonothermal

This route avoids >1000 °C temperatures because TS-5 reports 400–800 °C alkali-amide Ta nitride synthesis. However, it requires NH₃ pressure ≥1 kbar, autoclaves, air-sensitive amides, and post-reaction washing (TS-5). The severity is therefore high despite the lower temperature.

#### F3 — Route 4: Alkali-Amide / Ammonothermal

Kinetics may be favorable because alkali amides provide highly reactive nitrogen chemistry and mineralizer-like transport for Ta nitrides (TS-5). However, TS-5 supports NaTaN₂-type products, not Eu₃Ta₂N₆; thus structural selectivity is the major uncertainty. This route is best viewed as an exploratory backup rather than the primary route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very different cation types, Eu and Ta, in distinct coordination environments, satisfying the cation-size/chemistry mismatch risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The structure is nevertheless ordered and relatively simple, and the exact La₃Ta₂N₆ analogue crystallizes in the same I4/mmm structure (CS-1, SS-1). The target description includes corner and edge sharing but not the full vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence criterion, so amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Eu₃Ta₂N₆ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest La₃Ta₂N₆/La₃Nb₂N₆ analogues are also hull-stable (target data; CS-1, CS-3, TS-1, TS-2). The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually strong because La₃Ta₂N₆ is both chemically and structurally very close to the target and has an established flux synthesis route (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of Li₃N or Ga flux chemistry, which supplies nitrogen activity and improves cation transport for refractory Ta nitride formation (CS-1, CS-3). The main limiting factor is Eu handling and phase control: Eu is air-sensitive and potentially volatile/reactive at high temperature [Reasonable Inference], while the analogues report nonstoichiometry and reproducibility challenges in the Ln₃T₂N₆ family (CS-1, CS-3). Overall, the compound is realistically synthesizable, with Li₃N flux in sealed Ta/Nb ampoules as the most credible first attempt.
