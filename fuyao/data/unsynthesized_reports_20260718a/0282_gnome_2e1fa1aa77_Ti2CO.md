### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score from the specified rubric. Chemically related Ti–Al–C phases Ti5Al2C3 and Ti2AlC are also listed with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1, CS-4), and structurally/thermodynamically similar ScAlOC and Nb carbides are stable or near-stable (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5; TS-1, TS-2, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as CS-1 and SS-1 explicitly show mixed/by-product formation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-temperature solid-state reaction

Condition severity is high because refractory carbide analogues often need 1500–2000+ °C or vacuum conditions (CS-1, SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3). The route uses robust but sluggish precursors such as TiC/TiO2/graphite, giving poor precursor activity and slow cation/anion diffusion by analogy to Nb carbide sintering (SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-3). Its main uncertainty is oxygen/carbon ordering into Ti2CO rather than forming TiC plus TiO/TiO2, a concern inferred from phase-control difficulties in CS-1 and CS-4.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: MAX/MXene-derived topochemical route

This route has moderate condition severity because the closest Ti–Al–C topochemical reference uses 1500 °C under Ar (CS-1), and dealloying/controlled oxidation adds multiple steps. Its kinetics are favorable relative to bulk solid-state synthesis because Ti2AlC already contains Ti–C connectivity and Ti–C bond lengths essentially matching the target (CS-4; target structure). The key uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS-1 reports a mixture of Ti5Al2C3, Ti2AlC, and Ti3AlC2, so topochemical conversion may not yield phase-pure Ti2CO without careful oxygen and Al control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Al-flux / melt-growth route

This route is condition-severe due to the 1820 °C melt used for ScAlOC synthesis (SS-1, TS-1), though it avoids high pressure. Kinetically, a molten Al flux should improve mobility and enable mixed oxycarbide crystallization, consistent with ScAlOC formation from Sc/C/excess Al (SS-1, TS-1). The limitation is phase control: ScAlOC was reported as a low-yield/by-product with competing phases (SS-1, TS-1), so the same risk is likely for Ti2CO. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Reactive thin-film deposition

This route scores best on condition severity because it can avoid 1500–2000 °C bulk sintering temperatures. However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for vapor deposition, so the kinetic score is limited by uncertainty in direct crystallization and composition control. The rationale is that thin films reduce diffusion distances and allow Ti:C:O flux tuning, but the target caswellsilverite ordering may require post-annealing and a suitable substrate. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent by target composition. Its described structure is a compact R-3m Ti2CO network of corner/edge-sharing octahedra, with no reported face-sharing coexistence and no indication of a large >40-atom unit cell. CS/SS/TS analogues contain many crystalline refractory carbides and oxycarbides, supporting low inherent amorphization risk, although thin-film deposition could still yield amorphous material if substrate temperature is too low. [Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2CO is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and multiple chemically or structurally related carbides/oxycarbides are also stable or near-stable (CS-1, CS-4, SS-1, TS-1, TS-2). The CS evidence strongly supports Ti–C framework chemistry through Ti2AlC/Ti5Al2C3, while SS/TS evidence supports mixed C/O oxycarbide crystallization through ScAlOC, though no analogue exactly matches Ti2CO. The most enabling factor is the close structural match between Ti2AlC Ti–C bonding and target Ti–C distances, suggesting a topochemical precursor route may preserve useful connectivity (CS-4; target). The most limiting factor is phase control: related Ti–Al–C and Sc–Al–O–C syntheses report mixtures or by-product formation (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Overall, Ti2CO appears synthesizable but will likely require careful oxygen chemical potential and precursor topology control rather than a simple oxide/carbide firing.
