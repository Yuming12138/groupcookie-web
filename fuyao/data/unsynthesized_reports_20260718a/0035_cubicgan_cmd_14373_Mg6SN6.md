### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score boundary in the provided rubric, so F1 is scored 90. Chemically similar Mg nitrides include stable Mg₂PN₃ and Mg₃NF₃ and near-hull Mg₂NF, supporting that Mg nitride frameworks can be thermodynamically accessible (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Structurally similar nitrides are also generally reported at or near hull, including LiMgN at 0.003 eV/atom (SS-5). TS analogues show that materials around 0.05–0.08 eV/atom can be synthesized, but TS is chemically less direct for Mg–S–N, so TS is supportive but secondary (TS-1 to TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses conditions close to Mg nitride analogues: 800 °C for Mg₂PN₃, 900–1000 °C for Mg₂NF, and 900 K for LiMgN (CS-1, CS-2, SS-5). The main deductions are for sealed volatile sulfur/nitrogen components, multi-step glovebox/sealing/grinding workflow, and air-sensitive precursors such as Mg₃N₂ [Reasonable Inference; Mg₃N₂ usage supported by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5]. CS provides strong signal for this route; SS provides moderate support through LiMgN; TS provides insufficient direct route evidence for solid-state Mg–S–N and is treated as neutral, 50, for Route 1 condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Mg₃N₂ is a reasonably reactive nitride precursor and has been used in Mg₂PN₃, Mg₃NF₃, and LiMgN syntheses (CS-1, CS-3, SS-5). Diffusion is not expected to be as severe as refractory oxide systems because Mg-based nitride analogues form below ~1000 °C (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-5). The penalty comes from structural reconstruction: no CS/SS/TS analogue directly contains the target S-centered N₆ unit, and long synthesis times in Mg₂PN₃ and Mg₃NF₃ suggest slow ordering or sluggish completion (CS-1, CS-3). TS gives insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Mg–S–N solid-state formation and is neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits substantial procedural severity from structurally similar nitride flux syntheses: sealed Nb tubes, Ar glovebox loading, NaN₃, Na flux, slow cooling, and Na sublimation/removal (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4). The temperature itself is moderate, since many SS analogues form at 760–900 °C (SS-1 to SS-4). However, reactive/controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile/pressure-generating reagents, multi-step processing, and toxic/air-sensitive Na/NaN₃/Mg₃N₂ drive F2 down. CS supports nitride precursor chemistry but not flux directly; TS gives only indirect chalcogen flux/ceramic relevance and is not strongly weighted.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth improves ion transport and crystallization, and SS analogues demonstrate complex nitrides crystallized from Na/alkali flux at 760–900 °C (SS-1 to SS-4). NaN₃ provides a high-activity nitrogen source, and Na flux enables slow crystal growth and post-growth separation by sublimation (SS-1, SS-2, SS-4). The main kinetic uncertainty is structural and chemical specificity: no SS analogue contains sulfur, and phase mixtures/minor products are reported in related nitride flux syntheses (SS-2, SS-4). CS and TS provide only indirect support for this route, with insufficient direct Mg–S–N flux evidence.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

High-pressure synthesis is penalized heavily because CS-4 reports β-MgSiN₂ synthesis at 27 GPa and >2000 K, and TS-4 reports Mg-rich cubic hydrides synthesized in a high-pressure anvil cell. Even if the proposed target could be attempted at lower 5–15 GPa and 900–1300 °C, specialized pressure equipment, capsule handling, volatile sulfur/nitrogen containment, and air-sensitive precursors make the route severe [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS both provide meaningful high-pressure evidence; SS gives insufficient high-pressure evidence except for one medium-confidence route with only ~36 bar N₂, which is not comparable to GPa pressure (SS-4).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High pressure can enhance densification and may stabilize dense cubic or metastable Mg-rich phases, as shown by β-MgSiN₂ under 27 GPa and cubic Fm-3m Mg₆VH₁₆ under high-pressure anvil synthesis (CS-4, TS-4). This supports a better structural-reconstruction score than ambient solid-state synthesis. However, precursor activity remains only moderate because the route still begins from compacted solids such as Mg₃N₂ and sulfur nitride precursors [Reasonable Inference; Mg₃N₂ supported by CS-1, CS-3, SS-5]. SS provides insufficient direct high-pressure kinetic evidence and is neutral, 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a chemically asymmetric cation set, Mg and high-valent S in nitrogen coordination, so the radius/field-strength mismatch criterion is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m formula Mg₆SN₆ likely produces a conventional cell above 40 atoms if Z = 4, but the prompt does not explicitly list the unit-cell atom count, so this trigger is assigned with uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description states corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed connectivity check as defined is not fully triggered. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct amorphization evidence for Mg–S–N and are treated as neutral, 50, for this subfactor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. CS analogues strongly support formation of Mg–N frameworks at accessible temperatures, especially Mg₂PN₃, Mg₂NF, Mg₃NF₃, and LiMgN (CS-1 to CS-3, SS-5). SS analogues strongly support nitride crystallization using sealed Na/NaN₃ fluxes, but they do not directly validate Mg–S–N chemistry (SS-1 to SS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the good thermodynamic proximity to the hull plus known Mg–N bond-length compatibility with synthesized Mg nitrides (CS-1 to CS-4, SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is the unusual S-centered N₆ octahedral motif and lack of direct Mg–S–N synthetic precedent in CS/SS/TS, making sulfur/nitrogen chemical-potential control the central challenge.
