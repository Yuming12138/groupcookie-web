### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues include TlMnCl₃ with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and Hf/Zr chlorochalcogenide chloride phases with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the idea that low-energy chloride networks are synthetically accessible (TS1–TS4). Structural evidence also supports stable Mn halide frameworks because the closest SS analogue TlMnCl₃ is stable at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1). Remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic access to the specific Cl/F ordered Hydrophilite-derived Pm phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature and high pressure because close analogues use sealed ampoule melting near 490–507 °C or lower-temperature sealed reactions at 260 °C (SS1/TS1, CS3). The penalty comes from requiring evacuated or inert sealed ampoules, dry halide handling, and multiple preparative steps such as drying, grinding, sealing, heating, and cooling (SS1/TS1, CS3). CS gives some support for sealed ampoule oxychloride synthesis but no direct mixed Mn–Cl–F chemistry, so CS is only partially useful and otherwise neutral for F2 transferability. The main uncertainty is whether MnF₂ reacts fully at these temperatures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetic score is moderately high because anhydrous halide salts and partially molten halide systems are generally more reactive than refractory oxide mixtures [Reasonable Inference], and SS3 explicitly reports synthesis by melting anhydrous binary Mn bromides. SS1/TS1 supports Mn–Cl octahedral framework formation from molten chloride mixtures. The limiting kinetic factor is halide ordering: the target contains MnCl₆, MnCl₅F, and MnCl₂F₄ octahedra, while no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates such Mn–Cl/F ordering. Therefore Route 1 is plausible but may require annealing and slow cooling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 because it uses sealed ampoules, dry halides, and controlled cooling, as in SS1/TS1 and TS3. It does not require high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C, which keeps the severity moderate. TS3 supports slow-cooling chloride melt growth in sealed ampoules, while SS1/TS1 supports molten Mn chloride chemistry. The added flux/removal step increases complexity relative to a simple one-step reaction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Route 2 receives the highest F3 score because molten chloride flux should enhance mass transport and promote crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with molten halide routes in SS1/TS1 and slow-cooling melt growth in TS3. The single-cation Mn sublattice reduces cation interdiffusion complexity [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The primary uncertainty is whether F from MnF₂ dissolves sufficiently in the chloride-rich melt to yield the ordered Mn₂Cl₃F phase rather than MnCl₂ + MnF₂ or chloride-rich competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for flux growth of Mn fluorochlorides and is treated as neutral for this kinetic point.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high pressure and very high temperature, consistent with CVT analogues operating at 360–475 °C in evacuated ampoules (SS5/TS2/TS4). However, it requires sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile/corrosive halide species, a temperature gradient, and dry handling, all of which increase severity (SS5/TS2/TS4). CVT analogues also involve air/moisture-sensitive products and difficult handling (SS5/TS2/TS4). No CS/SS/TS example directly confirms Mn₂Cl₃F vapor transport, so the route is more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CVT analogues show that halide-containing crystals can grow by transport in sealed ampoules, but they also report low yields, unreacted halides, sensitivity, and phase-purity problems (SS5/TS2/TS4). The target’s F component is especially uncertain because MnF₂ volatility and transport chemistry are not established in the provided evidence [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is likely narrow because Cl- and F-bearing species will not have identical vapor transport behavior [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 3 is plausible mainly as a crystal-growth exploration, not as the best bulk synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only Mn cations, so the cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target octahedra share corners and edges but no face-sharing connectivity is described, so the mixed vertex+edge+face trigger is not activated. The exact unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, but the formula and Pm description do not clearly indicate >40 atoms per cell, so C2 is treated as not triggered with uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 reports amorphous-to-crystalline transformation for VOCl₃ under cryogenic cooling, but that molecular oxychloride is chemically/structurally unlike the Mn₂Cl₃F network, so CS1 is insufficient evidence for high amorphization risk in the target.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₂Cl₃F is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The best support comes from SS1/TS1, where a Mn–Cl octahedral halide framework is synthesized by sealed-ampoule molten salt methods, and from SS3, where anhydrous binary Mn halides are melted to form a Mn halide compound. The most critical enabling factor is the use of an anhydrous molten halide environment, which should provide high ion mobility and suppress hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1, SS3, TS3]. The most critical limiting factor is achieving the ordered Cl/F distribution in the Hydrophilite-derived Pm structure, because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates Mn–Cl–F mixed-halide ordering. Overall, the target appears moderately favorable to synthesize, with the MnCl₂-rich flux route being the most promising.
