### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which is exactly at the high-stability cutoff and suggests a potentially isolable metastable or near-ground-state phase. CS analogues include related carbonates with E_hull values from about 0.058 to 0.286 eV/atom, showing that metastable carbonate-like compositions can still have reported synthesis routes (CS1–CS5). TS analogues include low-E_hull materials such as KCO3 at 0.0118 eV/atom and oxide/salt analogues near 0.03–0.10 eV/atom, but these are chemically indirect (TS1–TS4). The main uncertainty is that low hull energy does not resolve the unusual valence/coordination implied by CO6 carbon.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, high pressure.**  
The severity score is low because SS1 required 10 GPa, which is a major experimental barrier. The kinetic score is moderate because high pressure can stabilize octahedral small-cation environments, as in MgSiO6 with SiO6 octahedra (SS1), but the target’s CO6 octahedra require a larger reconstruction from ordinary carbonate units seen in CS1–CS5. Remaining uncertainty is whether BaCO6 is a true high-pressure recoverable phase or only a computational local minimum [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, hydrothermal.**  
The condition severity is favorable because CS2–CS4 use only 220 °C hydrothermal conditions for carbonate hydroxides, and SS4 uses 180–330 °C hydrothermal aging. Kinetics are favorable for solution-mediated crystallization, but structural transferability is weak because the CS hydrothermal carbonates all have trigonal planar C–O units rather than CO6 octahedra (CS2–CS4). The uncertainty is whether a peroxide-rich hydrothermal medium can access the target’s O-rich coordination rather than simply forming BaCO3, BaO2, or hydroxide phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, flux growth.**  
CS1 demonstrates that high-temperature flux growth can crystallize chemically similar C–O oxide materials using Li2MoO4/LiVO3 flux and slow cooling. The flux route improves transport and crystallinity, but it has high temperature, slow cooling, flux-removal, and possible oxygen/carbonate retention challenges. The key uncertainty is that flux growth supports crystal formation, not necessarily stabilization of octahedral carbon; insufficient evidence from SS and TS for flux stabilization of BaCO6.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, solid-state ceramic.**  
SS2 supports conventional ceramic synthesis of high-coordinate oxide phases using repeated heating/grinding at 1300–1400 °C, and TS3 supports solid-state oxide formation at 600–700 °C. However, the target likely competes with stable BaCO3/BaO/BaO2 phases and requires unusual CO6 coordination [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics and phase selectivity are poor despite the route being experimentally straightforward.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a strong cation size/coordination mismatch: Ba is 12-coordinate while C is 6-coordinate in the target structure, which increases local strain and disorder risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The structure description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to establish that the unit cell contains more than 40 atoms, so that check is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaCO6 is best interpreted as a near-hull metastable phase rather than a clearly established ground-state compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom but its CO6 octahedral carbon is chemically unusual. Chemically similar carbonates are well supported by hydrothermal and flux routes, but they consistently contain trigonal planar carbonate carbon, not octahedral carbon (CS1–CS5). The most important enabling factor is the low computed hull energy, which suggests the phase is not grossly thermodynamically inaccessible. The most important limiting factor is the structural/valence mismatch between ordinary carbonate chemistry and the target’s cubic CO6 framework. Hydrothermal synthesis is the most practical first screen, but high-pressure synthesis is the more physically motivated backup if the target requires octahedral carbon stabilization.
