### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has Eₕull = 0.050 eV/atom, which is exactly at the requested high-score boundary of ≤0.05 eV/atom. Thermodynamically similar synthesized nitrides such as Nd₂Fe₁₇N₃ and Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ have Eₕull ≈ 0.062–0.063 eV/atom and are reported as synthesizable by high-pressure N₂ methods (TS1, TS2). CS similarly includes synthesized chemically similar Fe-rich nitrides in the 0.062–0.136 eV/atom range (CS1–CS4), supporting that modestly metastable Fe-rich nitrides can be accessed. The main uncertainty is that none of the closest CS/TS entries contains Pt or the cubic F-43m Fe₆PtN structure.

#### F2 — Route 1: High-pressure N₂ nitridation

The route inherits demanding but established conditions: parent alloy preparation, powder activation, and 15 MPa N₂ nitridation at 250–350 °C are reported for Ce/Nd₂Fe₁₇N₃ (CS2, CS4, TS1, TS2). The high-pressure is far below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, but controlled N₂/Ar atmospheres and multiple processing steps justify substantial deductions. Remaining uncertainty is whether a Fe–Pt parent alloy can accept nitrogen into the exact cubic Fe₆PtN arrangement [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: High-pressure N₂ nitridation

Kinetically, this is plausible because CS/TS analogues show nitrogen insertion into preformed Fe-rich intermetallic powders after crushing/sieving or ball milling (CS2–CS4, TS1, TS2, TS4). Dense Fe/Pt metallic precursors and Pt diffusion are likely slow [Reasonable Inference], but interstitial nitrogen diffusion can occur at relatively low temperature in activated powders as shown by analogue nitrides (CS2–CS4, TS1–TS4). The score is limited by the absence of a demonstrated Fe–Pt parent-to-Fe₆PtN topotactic transformation.

#### F2 — Route 2: Ambient-pressure gas-phase N₂ nitridation

Ambient-pressure N₂ nitridation is supported by Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ and Pr₂Fe₁₇N₃ routes at approximately 450–550 °C or 460 °C (CS1, CS3, TS4). It avoids high-pressure equipment, but still requires parent alloy formation, controlled purified N₂, grinding, and multi-step processing (CS1, CS3, TS4). The severity score is similar to Route 1 because the loss of high-pressure equipment is offset by likely longer or less reliable nitridation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Ambient-pressure gas-phase N₂ nitridation

The kinetic concern is nitrogen uptake: CS1 explicitly notes phase-control difficulty from slow nitrogen diffusion, incomplete nitrogenation, and α-Co precipitation in related systems. Pr₂Fe₁₇N₃ synthesis uses fine ball-milled powders and thermogravimetric monitoring, showing that kinetic control is important even when the chemistry is favorable (CS3, TS4). For Fe₆PtN, lower N₂ chemical potential at ambient pressure may make full N incorporation more difficult than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Sealed-tube solid-N-source annealing

Arc melting and sealed annealing are well supported for intermetallic and carbide analogues (CS5, SS1–SS4, TS3), but direct nitride formation from a solid N donor is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS. The route incurs penalties for controlled/sealed atmosphere, possible volatile N release, multi-step processing, and possible hazardous N donors [Reasonable Inference]. It is therefore the most severe and least evidence-backed bulk route.

#### F3 — Route 3: Sealed-tube solid-N-source annealing

The kinetics are weak because formation from metals plus a solid nitride donor likely requires extensive interdiffusion and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS analogues show that Fe-rich interstitial carbide phases often need 20–30 days or longer annealing and still retain impurity phases (SS1–SS4). This suggests slow ordering and a narrow phase window for any analogous bulk Fe–Pt–N formation.

#### F2 — Route 4: Reactive sputtering / PLD

Thin-film synthesis avoids >1000 °C bulk anneals and high-pressure N₂, which gives a favorable condition-severity score [Reasonable Inference]. The penalty comes from reactive/controlled Ar/N₂ atmospheres and likely post-deposition annealing [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS route directly validates thin-film growth, so this low severity is partly speculative.

#### F3 — Route 4: Reactive sputtering / PLD

Fe and Pt are standard sputter targets, and N₂ can act as a reactive gas [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, sputtering bypasses long bulk diffusion distances and can access metastable phases, which is attractive for a 0.050 eV/atom material [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting kinetic factors are crystallization of the cubic ordered phase and maintaining Fe:Pt:N stoichiometry, especially because analogous nitrides show sensitivity to nitrogen content and secondary phases (CS1–CS4, TS1–TS4).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target appears to have a relatively simple Fe₆PtN formula and no evidence for a very large unit cell or edge/face/vertex mixed connectivity all at once; this supports a low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly establish the target’s amorphization tendency, and the structurally similar entries are chemically different rare-earth Fe carbides (SS1–SS5), so their F4 signal is treated as insufficient and neutral. The main uncertainty is that thin films of Fe–Pt–N could still amorphize under low-temperature deposition if crystallization kinetics are poor [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Fe₆PtN is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground state, because its Eₕull is finite at 0.050 eV/atom. The thermodynamic analogue set is encouraging: several synthesized Fe-rich nitrides near 0.06 eV/atom are reported in TS and CS (CS2–CS4, TS1, TS2, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is controlled nitrogen incorporation into an Fe-rich metallic framework, as demonstrated for R₂Fe₁₇N₃ nitrides by high-pressure or purified N₂ nitridation (CS1–CS4, TS1–TS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: no provided analogue contains Pt or the same cubic F-43m Fe₆PtN topology, and related Fe-rich interstitial compounds often show impurities or incomplete anion uptake (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5). Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful N chemical potential, fine particle size or thin-film processing, and tight Fe:Pt stoichiometry control.
