### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the highest stability scoring bin. Similar halides with E_hull near 0.05–0.08 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable, including FeCl₃ at 0.073 eV/atom and NaHgCl₃ at 0.055 eV/atom (TS1, TS2). Stable isolated-octahedra analogues Tl₂SnCl₆ and Tl₂TeCl₆ have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1, CS3), supporting the plausibility of chloride cluster salts. The main uncertainty is that the target stoichiometry TlGeCl₆ is not the conventional charge-balanced A₂MCl₆ stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has the mildest conditions because analogous hexachlorometallates crystallize from concentrated aqueous or HCl solutions with air drying and no high pressure (SS1, CS2, CS3). Kinetics are favorable because solution mixing bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, and isolated MCl₆ octahedra are already demonstrated for Sn, Te, and Pb analogues (SS1, CS2, CS3). The score is reduced for GeCl₄ volatility/hydrolysis and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference supported by SS4 and Tl-containing analogues in CS1/CS3/SS1]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ge forms the desired octahedral GeCl₆ salt at the reported Tl:Ge stoichiometry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by TlAlCl₄/TlGaCl₄ sealed-ampoule syntheses at only 150–200 °C (CS1, CS4). Condition severity is moderate because it requires glove-box/vacuum ampoule handling, volatile GeCl₄ containment, and controlled cooling (CS1, CS4, SS4). Kinetics should be reasonable because molecular halides react at low temperature in the analogues (CS1, CS4), but structural reconstruction from tetrahedral GeCl₄ to octahedral GeCl₆ is nontrivial (SS4 versus target structure). Insufficient direct evidence from CS for Tl–Ge–Cl phase selectivity lowers confidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by sealed-ampoule halide CVT and gradient crystallization examples, including Bi–Te–Cl at 170 → 100 °C and FeCl₃ vapor transport (CS5, TS1). Kinetics are favorable for crystal growth because vapor transport avoids bulk diffusion (CS5, TS1). The condition score is reduced by sealed volatile halide handling, dry atmosphere, and multi-step crystal separation, as also seen in TS5. Insufficient evidence from TS for exact TlGeCl₆ stoichiometry and phase purity makes this more uncertain than Route 1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is mainly justified by TlCuCl₃ melt/Bridgman growth under Ar at ~450 °C (SS2). It may be useful for crystal growth if the target or a related Tl–Ge–Cl phase melts or forms a flux, but this is not directly shown by CS/SS/TS and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference]. Severity is moderate because SS2 requires dehydration, inert handling, controlled soaking, and slow growth. Kinetic and structural scores are lower because SS2 is not an isolated GeCl₆ cluster analogue, and volatile GeCl₄ could cause composition loss.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is 0D with isolated GeCl₆ clusters and no extended mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing network, which reduces amorphization risk (target description). The unit cell content described as four GeCl₆ clusters plus four Tl clusters is below the >40 atom trigger, so complexity is moderate rather than high (target description). One risk trigger is the large cation size/coordination mismatch between Tl and Ge [Reasonable Inference from target coordination environment]. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlGeCl₆ is likely near the boundary between ground-state and mildly metastable behavior because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, while several thermodynamically similar halides near this range are experimentally accessible (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from isolated octahedral chloride cluster analogues such as Tl₂SnCl₆ and Tl₂TeCl₆, which are stable and synthesized by mild precipitation routes (SS1, CS3). The most critical enabling factor is the high lability of chloride solution chemistry, which allows MCl₆ cluster salts to crystallize without high-temperature diffusion (SS1, CS2, CS3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometric/charge-balance ambiguity: isolated Ge(IV)Cl₆ units normally suggest A₂GeCl₆-type chemistry rather than TlGeCl₆ [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, synthesis is plausible, especially as a related Tl–Ge–Cl chloride cluster phase, but exact recovery of the reported TlGeCl₆ structure should be experimentally verified by composition analysis and diffraction.
