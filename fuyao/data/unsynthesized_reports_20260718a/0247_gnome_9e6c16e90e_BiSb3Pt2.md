### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has reported **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is scored near the top of the prescribed ≤0.05 eV/atom range. Many close analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including Pt2TlTe3 (CS1), Hf2GaSb3 (CS4), IrSb3 (SS1; TS1), and filled RuSb3 phases (SS2–SS5; TS2–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing low-temperature ordered variants [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile Sb/Bi management, multistep grinding/annealing, and toxic pnictogen handling, giving substantial severity deductions. Similar evacuated sealed-tube conditions are directly used for IrSb3 and filled RuSb3 antimonides (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). No high pressure is required in the analogues, and temperatures around 850–900 °C remain below the >1000 °C threshold.

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state reaction from elements is feasible because structurally similar transition-metal antimonides form from elemental mixtures in sealed tubes (SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS5). However, Pt is refractory and likely diffuses slowly, while long heating of one month is reported for IrSb3 (SS1; TS1). The target’s monoclinic Bi/Sb ordering adds reconstruction complexity beyond the higher-symmetry skutterudites [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Sb-flux growth still requires a sealed evacuated tube and uses a large excess of toxic, volatile Sb, so F2 remains moderate rather than easy. The 950 °C flux protocol for EuRu4Sb12 uses evacuated carbon-coated quartz tubes and slow cooling (SS3; TS3). No high pressure or >1000 °C setpoint is required by the analogue route (SS3; TS3).

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves kinetics because the Sb-rich melt provides a transport medium for transition-metal antimonide crystallization, as directly shown for EuRu4Sb12 (SS3; TS3). This is the strongest kinetic match among the routes because the target is also Sb-rich and pyrite/skutterudite-derived [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Bi incorporates uniformly rather than segregating into Bi–Sb or Bi-rich secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Arc melting is more severe because local temperatures must exceed the melting/alloying range of Pt-containing mixtures [Reasonable Inference], even though the referenced Hf2GaSb3 route uses arc melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing (CS4). The route also suffers from volatile Bi/Sb loss risk and requires several steps. No high-pressure apparatus is required, consistent with CS4.

#### F3 — Route 3

Arc melting can overcome refractory-metal mixing barriers, as shown for Hf2GaSb3 synthesis from elements (CS4). However, CS4 reports an impurity phase in the product, indicating phase control can remain difficult. For Pt2BiSb3, volatile pnictogen loss during arc melting and subsequent ordering during annealing are significant kinetic and compositional uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Bridgman growth requires vacuum sealing, controlled solidification, volatile-element containment, and multistep presynthesis/growth. Chemically similar Bi-containing materials are grown in sealed ampoules by Bridgman or melt growth (CS2, CS3, CS5). The severity is moderate because no high pressure is used and temperatures are likely near 850–950 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Liquid-phase growth should improve diffusion and crystallinity, as demonstrated for Bi–Te–Pb/Sn and TlBiTe2 compounds (CS2, CS3, CS5). However, those CS analogues are layered tellurides rather than Pt–Sb pyrite/skutterudite derivatives, so structural transferability is weaker than for SS/TS skutterudites. The largest uncertainty is whether Pt2BiSb3 melts congruently or undergoes peritectic decomposition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains chemically distinct Pt, Bi, and Sb sites with different preferred bonding, so one amorphization trigger is assigned for size/bonding mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description reports corner-sharing PtBi/Sb octahedra plus tetrahedral/trigonal-pyramidal pnictogen environments, but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing in the target. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell atom count >40, so that check is not triggered and is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pt2BiSb3 is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar antimonide or heavy-p-block phases are also hull-stable (CS1, CS4, CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from SS/TS skutterudite antimonides, which share transition-metal–Sb octahedral frameworks and are synthesized in evacuated sealed tubes or Sb flux (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the use of Sb-rich flux or melt conditions, which should improve Pt transport and framework crystallization, as demonstrated for EuRu4Sb12 (SS3; TS3). The main limiting factor is composition control involving volatile Sb/Bi and refractory Pt, which can cause secondary phases or incomplete reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with the Sb-flux route preferred over direct sintering or arc melting.
