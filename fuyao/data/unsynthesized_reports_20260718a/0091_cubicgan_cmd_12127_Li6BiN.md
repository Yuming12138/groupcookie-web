### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which places it at the favorable boundary of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin; this supports possible metastable or near-ground-state synthesis. Thermodynamic analogues include experimentally synthesized compounds with E_hull values comparable to or higher than the target, such as Na3As at 0.0425 eV/atom, Li2HgGe at 0.0522 eV/atom, KMnAs at 0.0547 eV/atom, and NaMnSb at 0.0840 eV/atom (TS1–TS3, TS5). CS and SS also contain many stable or near-stable alkali/Li-rich phases, supporting the idea that low-hull Li-rich pnictide/nitride structures can be isolated (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS1–SS3). Remaining uncertainty is that none of the references is exactly Li6BiN or exactly the target F-43m structure.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses temperatures similar to Li8TeN2 synthesis at 1023 K and alkali pnictide/intermetallic syntheses at 650–850 °C, so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty (CS1, CS4, CS5). It requires sealed Ta/Nb ampoules and inert handling because Li-rich analogues use welded/sealed Ta containers and gloveboxes (CS1, CS4, SS1). The main deductions are for volatile/sealed Li chemistry and air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference based on CS1 and CS4]. CS gives strong useful signal, SS gives supportive ampoule/slow-cooling practice, and TS gives general high-temperature inert synthesis support.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Li3N is a demonstrated reactive precursor for Li-rich nitrides (CS4), and preformed Li3Bi should reduce reconstruction compared with elemental Bi [Reasonable Inference]. The low-melting Li/Bi chemistry should reduce diffusion barriers compared with refractory oxide synthesis [Reasonable Inference], consistent with melt-assisted Li/intermetallic routes (CS1–CS3). However, the target structure is not the same as the provided analogues, and slow annealing is likely needed for Li/Bi/N ordering [Reasonable Inference; long annealing in CS1 and SS5]. Insufficient evidence from TS for structural reconstruction kinetics specifically, so TS is neutral for this subfactor.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Direct elemental synthesis is supported for alkali pnictides and Li-rich intermetallics under Ar/sealed conditions (CS1–CS3, CS5, SS2, TS1–TS3). The severity increases because direct nitrogen incorporation likely needs controlled nitrogen activity or use of a nitrogen carrier [Reasonable Inference], while CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for direct Li–Bi–N formation from N2. Sealing is needed to retain Li and maintain stoichiometry, consistent with Ta/quartz sealed analogues (CS1, CS4, SS1). The route is therefore more severe than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Elemental Li and Bi can react readily in a melt-like regime, as Li–Pb and Li-rich intermetallic reactions are performed by melting/heating under Ar (CS1–CS3). The weak point is nitrogen incorporation: N2 is kinetically inert relative to Li3N, and no provided CS/SS/TS example demonstrates direct N2 nitridation of a Li–Bi melt to a ternary nitride. Formation of ordered F-43m Li6BiN from elements requires full reconstruction and anion/cation ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are only moderate despite favorable Li/Bi transport.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The Li-flux route inherits sealed Ta/glovebox requirements from Li17Ag3Sn6 and Li8TeN2 syntheses (CS1, CS4). Temperatures remain below 1000 °C, but multi-stage processing, slow cooling, long annealing, and flux separation increase practical severity, matching the complexity seen in CS1 and SS5. Excess Li improves access to crystals or Li-rich phases in analogues (CS1, CS4), but also raises air-sensitivity and volatility risks [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides useful support for slow-cooling/ampoule methods, while TS gives less direct flux-growth signal and is neutral for this route-specific detail.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A Li-rich melt should provide high precursor activity and rapid mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and excess Li is specifically reported as beneficial for Li-rich analogues/crystals (CS1, CS4). Slow cooling from a melt is also used in structurally similar alkali intermetallics to obtain crystalline phases (SS1, SS5). The limiting kinetic issue is ordering into the target F-43m ternary rather than crystallizing competing Li–Bi or Li–N binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Overall kinetics are the best among the routes, but phase selectivity is less certain than in Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Thin-film synthesis avoids high bulk temperatures but requires UHV/high-vacuum and reactive nitrogen, and SS4 shows that Li-containing deposition used UHV and effusion cells. Li volatility, air sensitivity, and the likely need for overflux/capping add severity [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient useful signal for Li–Bi–N thin-film processing, so they are neutral for this route. This route is plausible but experimentally demanding.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

SS4 supports that Li-containing cubic phases can be deposited under non-equilibrium conditions, but it does not demonstrate nitride incorporation or Li–Bi–N crystallization. Reactive nitrogen incorporation and Li/Bi composition control are expected difficult because Li is volatile and Li6BiN is very Li-rich [Reasonable Inference]. The crystallization path is uncertain and may require post-annealing without losing Li [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the kinetic score is the lowest.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple high-symmetry cubic description and no reported edge-sharing/vertex+edge+face mixed connectivity, reducing amorphization risk from structural complexity. The large chemical/size contrast among Li, Bi, and N introduces one amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS show many ordered Li/alkali pnictides and Li-rich intermetallics crystallize successfully despite size contrast (CS5, SS2, SS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the actual CIF cell size and Li disorder tendency are not experimentally known. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li6BiN is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a strongly stable ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis of Li-rich and alkali pnictide/intermetallic phases under sealed, inert, high-temperature conditions, and TS shows that materials with similar or larger metastability have been synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is using Li3N plus a lithium bismuthide/Li-rich environment, because CS4 directly supports Li3N-based synthesis of Li-rich nitrides and CS1/CS4 support excess Li for Li-rich crystallization. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Li3N, Li3Bi, Li–Bi binaries, Bi metal, or Li-rich flux residues may compete with the target [Reasonable Inference]. A sealed Ta/Nb ampoule and slow annealing/cooling are therefore likely essential for retaining Li/N and allowing ordering.
