### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the computed convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 thermodynamic score range. Many related Sc carbides and ternary carbides in the references are also hull-stable, including Sc₂BC₂, hexagonal ScCrC₂, Sc₃CoC₄, Sc₅Re₂C₇, and Tm₂ReC₂ (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The main uncertainty is not thermodynamic stability but whether the calculated Sc₂CS ground state can be accessed without sulfur loss or competing sulfide/carbide formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because arc melting is a very high-temperature process and analogous Sc carbide routes use arc melting under Ar followed by long annealing (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Evacuated-tube annealing and Ta wrapping are standard in structurally similar carbide syntheses (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3), but adding sulfur requires sealed containment to prevent S loss [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score is therefore low because it combines very high local temperature, controlled atmosphere, sealed sulfur chemistry, and multiple steps.

#### F3 — Route 1

Arc melting improves homogenization and can overcome diffusion barriers, as shown by ScCrC₂ and Sc₅Re₂C₇ syntheses that begin with arc-melted pellets (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2). However, the target requires ordered incorporation of both C and S into ScC₃S₃ octahedra, while analogues mostly contain Sc–C frameworks without sulfur (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2). Long anneals and multiple thermal treatments in analogues indicate slow ordering and phase-control challenges (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, SS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids the extreme local temperatures of arc melting but still requires controlled vacuum/sealed-tube conditions, consistent with Sc carbide synthesis in high vacuum and evacuated anneals for Sc/rare-earth carbides (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3). Sulfur volatility makes sealed containment necessary, but this is inferred because no sulfur-containing analogue is provided [Reasonable Inference]. Multi-step grinding, pressing, sealing, and possible regrinding are justified by the long/repeated annealing patterns in Sc₂BC₂ and Sc carbide analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3).

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state diffusion from Sc, graphite, and Sc₂S₃ is expected to be slow because graphite and metal/sulfide powders are refractory solids [Reasonable Inference]. Sc carbides can form from Sc metal and graphite at 1200 °C in high vacuum, but multiple carbide phases appear under such conditions, indicating phase-control difficulty (SS-3). Long annealing times of two to four weeks in related Sc transition-metal carbides reinforce the moderate-to-slow kinetic assessment (CS-2, CS-3, SS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses hot pressing/SPS, which is less directly evidenced for Sc₂CS but is supported in principle by hot-pressed refractory carbide-like analogues (SS-5, TS-4). The route still requires vacuum/Ar and sulfur-retentive handling, and pre-reacted ScC/ScS-type powders add precursor-preparation complexity [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids >1400 °C in the preferred window, so it is less severe than arc melting but still not easy.

#### F3 — Route 3

Pre-reacted ScC and ScS should reduce the chemical distance to Sc₂CS because they already contain Sc–C and Sc–S bonding motifs [Reasonable Inference]. SPS/hot pressing should improve contact and diffusion relative to a loose solid-state mixture [Reasonable Inference], and hot pressing is a known route for refractory carbide-like phases (SS-5, TS-4). The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry validates Sc–C–S ordering or the H-phase structure under SPS conditions, so the score remains moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from formula]. The H-phase P6₃/mmc formula Sc₂CS suggests a relatively simple cell, while the target description explicitly reports mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing connectivity, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion (target description). The resulting risk is low–medium, with uncertainty in the exact unit-cell atom count because CS/SS/TS do not provide the target CIF details.

Sc₂CS is likely a ground-state phase thermodynamically because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically or thermodynamically similar Sc carbides are also hull-stable (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The analogue support for Sc–C framework synthesis is strong, especially from arc-melted and annealed Sc transition-metal carbides and high-vacuum Sc carbide formation (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3). The analogue support for sulfur incorporation is weak because the CS/SS/TS set contains no direct Sc–C–S synthesis example; sulfur control is therefore the main extrapolated issue [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is the robust thermodynamic driving force combined with known accessibility of Sc–C polyhedral frameworks. The most critical limiting factor is sulfur volatility/stoichiometry control and the risk of competing Sc sulfides or Sc carbides during high-temperature treatment [Reasonable Inference].
