### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin and is scored 100. Thermodynamically similar alkali arsenates/oxoanion salts Cs₂LiAsO₄, Rb₂LiAsO₄, Cs₂NaAsO₄, and Rb₂SeO₄ also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS ranks 1–4), reinforcing that related phases are stable. CS evidence is less consistent because high-pressure silicate analogues include metastable/high-E_hull phases (CS ranks 2–5), so CS is not weighted as strongly for F1. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the predicted orthorhombic Ama2 polymorph is the experimentally selected RbAsO₃ polymorph rather than another RbAsO₃ structure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state synthesis

The route has moderate condition severity because close Rb/Cs arsenate analogues are made at only 500–550 °C, without high pressure, but in sealed Ni-tubes/Ni-bombs and often under Ar (SS ranks 1–3; TS ranks 1–3). The main penalties are air-sensitive/toxic precursors and sealed containment, consistent with Rb₂O/As₂O₅ arsenate syntheses (SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2). Kinetics are moderate: the binary oxides are chemically active, but the 21 d anneals in analogues imply slow crystal growth or ordering (SS ranks 1–3; TS ranks 1–3). Uncertainty is whether Li-free RbAsO₃ forms directly or competes with other rubidium arsenates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 2: Aqueous solution crystallization

Condition severity is low because analogous Cs phosphate and Cs selenate/selenate-acid salts crystallize by room-temperature slow evaporation or cooling near 313 K at ambient pressure (CS rank 4; SS ranks 4–5). The only serious condition penalty is toxic arsenic chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because solution transport avoids solid-state diffusion and oxoanion units can assemble during crystallization [Reasonable Inference supported by CS rank 4 and SS ranks 4–5]. The uncertainty is that the provided solution analogues do not prove direct formation of anhydrous RbAsO₃; hydrates, acid arsenates, or different Rb:As phases may crystallize first [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Hydrothermal synthesis

Hydrothermal conditions are mild compared with high-pressure routes: TS rank 4 reports 493 K for 48 h in a Teflon-lined steel vessel, and CS rank 1 reports low-temperature hydrothermal synthesis for a chemically related oxide. Kinetics should be good because hydrothermal solution transport can dissolve/reprecipitate oxoanion species, reducing diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. The route is less directly supported than the sealed-tube arsenate route because TS rank 4 is a selenate/selenite-type analogue rather than RbAsO₃. The main uncertainty is pH-dependent speciation and possible crystallization of hydrated or acid arsenate phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 Route 4: Alkali-rich flux growth

The flux route avoids very high temperature and high pressure because the thermodynamic flux analogue uses 500–800 °C under Ar (TS rank 5). Severity increases due to inert handling, flux removal, toxic arsenic oxides, and possible volatile arsenic species [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, flux/liquid transport should be favorable and may help grow crystals, consistent with alkali-rich flux synthesis in TS rank 5. Uncertainty is comparatively high because TS rank 5 is a germanate flux system, not an arsenate, and alkali-rich stoichiometry may favor competing rubidium oxides/arsenates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target combines a very large alkali cation environment with small tetrahedral As(V), so the cation size mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives corner-sharing AsO₄ tetrahedra and does not report edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not applied. Unit-cell atom count is not provided, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to determine whether it exceeds 40 atoms; this check is treated as neutral/not triggered. Overall, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high because multiple structurally/thermodynamically similar alkali oxoanion salts crystallize successfully as single crystals (SS ranks 1–5; TS ranks 1–5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbAsO₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and several thermodynamically similar alkali arsenate/oxoanion phases also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS ranks 1–4). The strongest direct synthesis support comes from Rb₂LiAsO₄ and Cs₂LiAsO₄, which use Rb/Cs oxides and As₂O₅ at 550 °C in sealed inert tubes and produce single crystals (SS ranks 1–2; TS ranks 1–2). The most enabling factor is that Rb–As–O chemistry with AsO₄ tetrahedra is already experimentally accessible at moderate temperatures in closely related structures (SS ranks 1–3; TS ranks 1–3). The most limiting factor is phase selectivity: in solution or hydrothermal routes, hydrated/acid arsenates may compete, while in solid-state routes Rb₂O and As₂O₅ require air-free sealed handling [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears highly synthesizable, with solution growth preferred for mildness and sealed-tube solid-state synthesis as the most direct fallback.
