### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 scoring range. This is reinforced by stable zero-hull Caswellsilverite or closely related pnictide/halide analogues: Ca2NBr and Ca2NCl in CS1/CS2, Ca2AsI/Ba2PCl/Eu2PBr in SS1–SS4, and the same thermodynamic neighbors in TS1–TS4. Remaining uncertainty is mostly computational: the target is predicted stable, but experimental phase competition with Ca3P2, CaBr2, Ca3PBr3-type phases, or nonstoichiometric Ca–P–Br phases is not directly measured [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses 740–950 °C sealed-ampoule Ar conditions, directly matching Ca3P2/CaCl2 synthesis of Ca–P–Cl phases (SS5/TS5) and comparable Ca2NBr/Ca2NCl syntheses (CS1/CS2). The main deductions are repeated grinding/firing and air/moisture-sensitive phosphide/halide handling, both indicated by analogue procedures and hydrolysis sensitivity (SS5/TS5; SS2/TS2). No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is required for the powder route based on the closest Ca–P–halide evidence (SS5/TS5).

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The Ca3P2 + CaBr2 route is kinetically favorable because it uses binary precursors that already contain Ca–P and Ca–Br bonds [Reasonable Inference], and the exact stoichiometry maps directly to Ca2PBr [Reasonable Inference]. However, SS5/TS5 reports grinding and reheating, so solid-state diffusion and ordering are not instantaneous. The shared Caswellsilverite/octahedral motif in CS1/CS2 and SS1–SS4 supports structural accessibility.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is supported by Ba2PCl and Eu2PBr/Eu2PI elemental-plus-halide routes under Ar (SS2/TS2; SS3/TS3; SS4/TS4). It is penalized more than Route 1 because red phosphorus introduces volatile-component management and staged heating, as shown by the Ba route heating first to 500 °C before higher-temperature reaction (SS2/TS2). Ca metal also increases air-sensitivity risk [Reasonable Inference supported by inert handling of related syntheses in SS2/TS2 and SS4/TS4].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Elemental Ca is reactive, but the reaction must form Ca–P and Ca–Br networks while controlling P volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Analogues form in the 500–1000 °C range and yield crystalline powders after tempering (SS2/TS2; SS4/TS4), so diffusion is manageable. Repeated heating and grinding in Ba/Eu analogues indicate moderate ordering kinetics rather than facile low-temperature crystallization (SS2/TS2; SS4/TS4).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 is more severe because high-temperature annealing for single crystals in related pnictide halides can require 1250–1400 °C and long durations (SS3/TS3; SS4/TS4). Sealed Mo ampoules and glovebox handling are repeatedly indicated for sensitive Eu/Ca pnictide-halide analogues (SS1/TS1; SS4/TS4). This route is therefore best considered a crystallinity-improvement route, not the lowest-risk first synthesis route [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Starting from preformed Ca2PBr should reduce structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference], and high-T annealing often improves ordering/crystal growth in Caswellsilverite pnictide halides (SS1/TS1; SS4/TS4). Kinetics may be aided by a small CaBr2 flux or Br chemical-potential buffer [Reasonable Inference], but direct flux evidence for Ca2PBr is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. Long annealing times in analogues show that single-crystal growth may still be slow (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS3).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple single-cation Ca sublattice and an ordered Caswellsilverite R-3m framework, matching many crystalline analogues in CS1/CS2, SS1–SS4, and TS1–TS4. The connectivity is corner- and edge-sharing octahedral, without reported face sharing in the target or main analogues, reducing frustration and amorphization risk (CS1/CS2; SS1–SS4). Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a precise C2 check, but all structural analogues suggest a compact, low-complexity cell.
