### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability category and receives 98/100. Thermodynamic analogues LaRh₃Ga₂, MgRh, Ho₃Si₂(Rh₃Sn)₃, Rh₂FeSn, and ScRh are also on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 (TS-1–TS-5), which supports the idea that Rh-rich intermetallics of related thermodynamic character can be ground-state phases. The remaining uncertainty is that convex-hull stability is computed and does not by itself guarantee facile kinetics or absence of competing Mg–Rh/Nb–Rh binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + annealing

Arc-melting is strongly supported for chemically similar ternary intermetallics and Heusler analogues (CS-1–CS-5), and also for Rh-containing structural/thermodynamic analogues LaRh₃Ga₂ and ScRh (SS-1, SS-5 / TS-1, TS-5). The severity score is penalized because arc melting is a high-temperature, multi-step process and Mg is volatile/air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference], even though the cited examples use standard Ar atmospheres rather than extreme pressure (CS-1–CS-5). Kinetics are only moderate because melting helps homogenization, but Nb/Rh are refractory and Mg may be lost before full dissolution/ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Mg₂NbRh₃ arc-melting behavior, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state sintering

This route has the best combined score because it directly matches MgRh synthesis from Mg and Rh powders under Ar with Ta foil and Zr getters (SS-2/TS-2), while also resembling Rh₂FeSn sealed-tube Heusler synthesis with pulverization/reannealing (SS-4/TS-4). Conditions are milder than arc melting and avoid deliberate exposure of Mg to an arc, but the sealed system and Mg handling still impose penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main weakness: SS-2/TS-2 required week-scale sintering and reannealing, and SS-4/TS-4 required pulverization/reannealing, so Nb incorporation is likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for direct Mg–Nb–Rh solid-state formation, so route optimization would be needed.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux/self-flux growth

Flux growth is supported by Rh-rich Ho₃Si₂(Rh₃Sn)₃ growth from Bi flux with slow cooling (SS-3/TS-3), showing that Rh intermetallic single crystals can form by liquid-assisted transport. Kinetically, flux may improve Nb/Rh transport compared with dry powders [Reasonable Inference], but the target-specific Mg-rich flux chemistry is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS. The severity score is low because Mg-rich sealed flux introduces vapor pressure, containment, and composition-control risks [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for Mg self-flux synthesis of Mg₂NbRh₃ makes this route speculative despite plausible kinetic advantages.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple bcc-coordinated, Heusler-like intermetallic description, which argues against high amorphization tendency [target structural description]. One radius-mismatch trigger is assigned because Mg is substantially larger than Rh/Nb [Reasonable Inference], but no evidence indicates a very large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network. CS and SS analogues include many crystalline intermetallics made by standard bulk routes, including Heusler-type ZrNi₂In and HfNi₂In (CS-4, CS-5) and Rh₂FeSn (SS-4/TS-4), supporting crystallization rather than glass formation. The residual uncertainty is that Mg-containing intermetallics can be compositionally sensitive if Mg is lost [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₂NbRh₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS-1–TS-5). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong: chemically similar intermetallics and Heusler analogues are routinely made from elements by arc melting plus annealing (CS-1–CS-5), while the closest Mg–Rh analogue MgRh is made by sealed solid-state sintering (SS-2/TS-2). The most important enabling factor is the existence of robust crystalline Rh intermetallic and Heusler-like analogues synthesized under standard inert/sealed conditions (CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-4 / TS-2, TS-4). The most important limiting factor is the combination of volatile Mg with refractory Nb/Rh, which creates a narrow practical window between Mg retention and sufficient Nb/Rh diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is synthesizable but likely requires careful stoichiometry control and extended annealing.
