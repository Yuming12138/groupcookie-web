### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin supplied in the prompt. Thermodynamic analogues include low-E_hull phases such as TS-2 at 0.035 eV/atom, TS-3 at 0.0118 eV/atom, TS-4 at 0.0324 eV/atom, and TS-5 at 0.0492 eV/atom, showing that phases in this thermodynamic neighborhood can be experimentally accessible. The remaining uncertainty is that low E_hull does not guarantee that the unusual SrCO₆ bonding topology is kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild temperature and no external high pressure, consistent with CS-1/CS-5 hydrothermal carbonate-family syntheses at 220 °C and SS-5 hydrothermal synthesis at 230 °C. Deductions are mainly for sealed-system volatility/gas pressure and multi-step hydrothermal handling [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for the exact oxidizing hydrothermal conditions needed for SrCO₆, so TS is neutral for the oxidizer requirement.

#### F3 — Route 1

Hydrothermal solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths, supported by CS-1, CS-5, SS-5, and TS-3. The major kinetic penalty is structural: all chemically close carbonate analogues show trigonal planar C–O units near 1.28–1.31 Å, not CO₆ octahedra with 1.51 Å C–O bonds (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5, TS-3). Therefore, the route is kinetically favorable for Sr–C–O crystallization but uncertain for the target topology.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized heavily because SS-1 required 10 GPa, which exceeds the >5 GPa threshold. It also needs sealed capsules and reactive oxidizing precursors [Reasonable Inference], and TS-5 confirms that Sr-containing high-pressure hydrothermal chemistry may require specialized belt/high-pressure apparatus. Insufficient evidence from CS for multi-GPa Sr–C–O synthesis, so CS is neutral for pressure feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 2

High pressure is structurally helpful because SS-1 demonstrates SiO₆ octahedra in a high-pressure MgSiO₆ phase. Hydrous high-pressure media in SS-1 and TS-5 support improved transport relative to dry solids, but SS-1 still required 205 h, indicating slow kinetics. The key uncertainty is whether carbon, smaller and typically trigonal in carbonates, can be stabilized as CO₆ under comparable pressure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids multi-GPa pressure and can use temperatures similar to TS-2/TS-4 sealed oxide syntheses at 600–700 °C, giving moderate condition severity. However, sealed oxidizing flux chemistry introduces volatility/internal pressure and reactive precursor hazards [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports flux growth but at 1473 K, which would be harsher than the recommended lower-temperature extrapolated flux route.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux can improve mass transport and crystal growth, as shown by CS-2, and sealed oxide reactions are viable in TS-2/TS-4. However, the target likely requires complete reconstruction from normal carbonate/peroxide species into a cubic SrO₁₂/CO₆ framework [Reasonable Inference]. Because carbonate analogues in CS-2 and TS-3 remain trigonal planar, the kinetic score is low.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a very large Sr-centered 12-coordinate site with a very small C-centered octahedral site, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference based on the target structure]. The target connectivity includes corner-sharing SrO₁₂ and face-sharing SrO₁₂/CO₆ polyhedra, but the prompt does not state edge-sharing, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not supply useful signal for this specific factor; therefore C2 is treated as neutral/not triggered.

SrCO₆ is likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than a clearly established ground-state material because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom and no direct experimental Sr–C–O analogue with CO₆ octahedra is provided [Reasonable Inference]. The CS data support hydrothermal carbonate-family synthesis and Sr oxoanion crystallization, while TS-3 supports easy carbonate crystallization from solution, but these sources mostly contain normal trigonal carbonate units rather than the target CO₆ octahedron. The strongest enabling factor is the favorable calculated thermodynamic proximity to the hull combined with solution/hydrothermal routes that can access low-temperature crystallization. The strongest limiting factor is the unusual carbon coordination and long C–O bond length, which are not matched by chemically similar carbonate analogues. If ambient/autogenous hydrothermal synthesis fails, the SS-1 and TS-5 data suggest that high-pressure hydrothermal synthesis is the most rational escalation path.
