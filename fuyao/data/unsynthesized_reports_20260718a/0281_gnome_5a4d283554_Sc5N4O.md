### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is computed on the hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is high (Target). Similar nitride phases in CS are also reported at E_hull = 0.0, including Ca₄TiN₄, Ca₆GaN₅, Ca₃AlN₃, Ca₆FeN₅, and Ca₆MnN₅ (CS-1 to CS-5). SS/TS also include several E_hull = 0.0 rocksalt or tetragonal nitride analogues, supporting that dense nitride frameworks can be stable ground states (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not directly sample the Sc–N–O ternary chemistry, so oxygen ordering is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses high-temperature solid-state synthesis around 1000–1150 °C, comparable to CS nitride syntheses at 850–1100 °C (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). It avoids high pressure and flux volatility, but it likely requires glovebox handling, repeated grinding, and long annealing because related nitrides often use air-sensitive precursors and extended heating (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1; TS-4). The score is moderate because the equipment is standard for nitride solid-state synthesis, unlike DAC or RF flux methods (SS-3, SS-4; SS-5). Uncertainty remains in whether Sc₂O₃ fully reacts under these conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetic score is limited by refractory ScN/Sc₂O₃ precursors [Reasonable Inference], analogous to refractory nitride systems that need high temperature, long dwell times, or hot pressing (SS-2; TS-4). Structural reconstruction is not extreme if ScN is used because both ScN-like rocksalt octahedra and the target have edge/corner-sharing octahedral networks (Target; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). However, O/N ordering and achieving Sc₅N₄O rather than a ScN + Sc₂O₃ mixture likely require repeated annealing [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Sc oxynitride ordering kinetics, so the kinetic score is conservative.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The Li₃N flux route inherits demanding sealed-ampoule, inert handling, and long slow-cooling requirements from analogous Li₃N flux syntheses (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3, TS-5). Although the temperature is only ~900–1050 °C, Li₃N is highly reactive and moisture-sensitive, and Nb/Ta tube sealing increases operational complexity (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3, TS-5). The route is therefore scored lower in F2 than direct solid-state synthesis. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for handling an oxygen source in Li₃N flux, adding uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Li₃N flux improves precursor activity and mass transport, as shown by ternary nitride growth in sealed Nb/Ta tubes at 900–1050 °C (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3, TS-5). This justifies a higher kinetics score than Route 1 despite harsher handling requirements. Flux growth may also help form ordered crystals rather than polycrystalline mixtures, consistent with flux-grown nitride analogues (CS-1; SS-5; TS-3, TS-5). The main uncertainty is that oxygen incorporation could be suppressed or diverted into Li–Sc–O side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The high-pressure route is penalized heavily because structurally similar Zr₃N₄/Hf₃N₄ syntheses require up to 18 GPa and 3000 K in laser-heated DAC experiments (SS-3, SS-4). Such equipment and conditions are much more severe than the ambient-pressure CS nitride syntheses (CS-1 to CS-5). This route is therefore a fallback rather than the primary recommendation. CS provides insufficient support for needing high pressure in chemically similar Ca nitrides, so this route is mostly extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

High pressure can stabilize and crystallize dense nitride frameworks, as shown for Zr₃N₄ and Hf₃N₄ (SS-3, SS-4). The target’s dense octahedral framework is structurally compatible with pressure-assisted formation (Target; SS-3, SS-4). However, short DAC heating times and limited sample volume can hinder equilibration and scale-up, and high-pressure phases often have narrow stability windows (SS-3, SS-4). Thus kinetics are acceptable locally but not ideal for practical synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Sc, so cation-size mismatch does not trigger amorphization risk (Target). The described framework contains corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered (Target). Rocksalt-like analogues also have simple edge/corner-sharing octahedral connectivity, supporting low amorphization tendency (SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-2). The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given in CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered.
