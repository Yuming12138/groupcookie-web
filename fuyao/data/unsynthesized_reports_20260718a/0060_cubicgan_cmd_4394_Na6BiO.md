### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the top edge of the 90–100 score bin. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values are experimentally synthesized: Na₃As at 0.0425 eV/atom (TS1), Na₆FeS₄ at 0.0466 eV/atom (TS3), and AMnAs phases around 0.055 eV/atom (TS4/TS5). CS and SS reinforce that Na-rich compounds such as Na₃Bi and Na₆ZnSn₂ can be stable ground-state phases (CS1/SS1, CS2/SS2). The uncertainty is that the target is a ternary Na–Bi–O composition not directly represented among CS/SS examples, so decomposition into Na₃Bi + Na₂O or related phases remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to Na₆ZnSn₂ at 773 K and below the highest temperatures used for Na₃Bi-related syntheses (CS2/SS2, CS1/SS1). Its severity comes mainly from sealed ampoule/inert atmosphere requirements, which are common for Na-rich analogues (CS2/SS2, SS4), and from air-sensitive Na/Na₂O handling [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS provide strong support for Na-rich sealed reactions, while TS2 provides the Na₂O handling signal. Insufficient evidence from TS for direct Na–Bi–O formation makes the oxygen incorporation risk neutral-to-uncertain rather than favorable.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderately favorable because molten Na and low-melting Bi can improve transport [Reasonable Inference], and Na₆ZnSn₂ forms by direct fusion in 10 h (CS2/SS2). The Na–Bi structural motif is supported by Na₃Bi, whose Na–Bi distances overlap the target’s 3.48 Å Na–Bi distance (CS1/SS1). However, no CS/SS analogue demonstrates the exact ONa₆ octahedral motif in a Na–Bi compound, and TS2 only shows that Na₂O-based alkali oxide synthesis is feasible. Therefore Route 1 receives a good but not excellent F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids very high temperature and high pressure, consistent with Na-rich fusion/slow-cooling analogues (CS2/SS2, SS4). Its condition score is reduced by the need for excess Na flux, sealed Ta ampoules, inert handling, and likely flux separation [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is supported by Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃Hg₂ syntheses (CS2/SS2, SS4). Insufficient evidence from TS for Na-flux growth of Na–Bi–O phases increases uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Na flux should improve mass transport and crystallization kinetics [Reasonable Inference], and structurally similar Na-rich materials are grown or crystallized by direct fusion/slow cooling (CS2/SS2, SS4). The liquid/flux environment likely lowers diffusion barriers relative to solid-state Na₂O/Bi mixtures [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Na₂O dissolves or reacts sufficiently in Na-rich flux, because CS/SS do not demonstrate oxide solubility in Na flux. TS2 supports Na₂O reactivity in sealed alkali oxide chemistry but not flux solubility.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has relatively mild temperature but poor operational simplicity, because it likely requires preforming Na₃Bi, mixing with Na₂O/Na, and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Long-duration sealed alkali oxide annealing is directly supported by TS2, which used 47 d at 520 °C. Inert/sealed handling is supported by TS2 and Na-rich intermetallic analogues (CS2/SS2). The route is therefore conditionally feasible but more demanding in time and handling.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetics are less favorable because solid Na₂O and preformed Na₃Bi require oxygen redistribution and ordering [Reasonable Inference]. TS2 demonstrates that alkali oxide reactions can proceed but may require very long annealing, indicating slow kinetics. Preforming Na₃Bi is structurally helpful because Na₃Bi is a close chemical/structural analogue (CS1/SS1), but the target’s O-centered Na₆ octahedra require substantial reorganization. Insufficient evidence from SS for an isostructural oxide analogue limits confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple high-symmetry cubic F-43m description and no reported large unit cell, which lowers amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The polyhedral network includes corner and face sharing but not the full vertex+edge+face coexistence trigger. A radius/size mismatch trigger is assigned because Na and Bi differ substantially in size and bonding character [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not directly provide ionic radius data, so that element of the score is uncertain. Overall F4 is low–medium risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆BiO is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, a range where several TS analogues are experimentally accessible (TS1, TS3, TS4/TS5). The CS/SS evidence strongly supports the Na–Bi and Na-rich intermetallic part of the chemistry, especially via Na₃Bi and Na₆ZnSn₂ (CS1/SS1, CS2/SS2). The TS evidence supports that Na-containing oxide chemistry with Na₂O in sealed containers is feasible, but it does not directly demonstrate Na–Bi–O ternary formation (TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed Ta-ampoule Na-rich synthesis methods with slow cooling, as shown for Na₆ZnSn₂ and Na₃Hg₂ (CS2/SS2, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition between binary Na–Bi intermetallics and Na₂O-rich phases, because no provided analogue combines Bi and O in the same Na-rich phase [Reasonable Inference].
