### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The supplied target data gives Ho₂CO an energy above hull of **0.0000 eV/atom**, which strongly supports thermodynamic synthesizability as a predicted ground-state phase. Several similar materials in CS/SS/TS also have E_hull = 0, including Gd₂CBr, Gd₂CCl, ScAlOC, Ho₂SbO₂, and multiple rare-earth oxide analogues (CS-1, CS-4, SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2). The remaining uncertainty is that hull stability is computational and does not ensure accessible kinetics or suppression of competing Ho–C, Ho–O, and Ho₂O₃ phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately demanding because CS-1 and CS-4 require sealed Ta capsules, Ar handling, 1250–1500 K heating, and 1–3 week reactions. The recommended target adaptation uses Ho metal and Ho₂O₃, both requiring inert/dry handling; rare-earth oxide/metal work in gloveboxes is supported by SS-5/TS-2. No high pressure or extreme >1400 °C condition is required, so the route avoids the most severe penalties.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are the main limitation for Route 1 because Ho₂O₃ and graphite are refractory solids and Ho₂CO requires simultaneous C/O ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The strong positive factor is structural transferability: CS-4 is Caswellsilverite Gd₂CCl, and the target is explicitly Caswellsilverite-like. The long 1–3 week sintering in CS-1/CS-4 indicates slow diffusion/order development, justifying a moderate F3 rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has harsher conditions because CS-3 uses arc melting/high-frequency furnace treatment and SS-1/TS-1 uses 1820 °C melt growth for ScAlOC. Rare-earth oxide pnictides also require induction furnaces, Ta tubes, and dynamic vacuum at 1300–1600 °C in SS-2/SS-3 and TS-3/TS-4. These data support severe temperature and atmosphere penalties.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melting or near-melting conditions improve homogenization, as seen in CS-3 for Nd₂BC and SS-1/TS-1 for ScAlOC. However, ScAlOC was obtained as a by-product with phase competition in SS-1/TS-1, and the target Ho₂CO requires precise O/C ordering rather than just elemental mixing [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, kinetics are improved relative to cold solid-state mixing but phase-selective reconstruction remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from lower temperatures because CS-2 demonstrates rare-earth carbide growth in Li flux at 1070 K, and TS-5 shows flux growth of lanthanide oxides near 1000 °C. The penalties come from sealed-tube requirements, Ar handling, Li reactivity, and multi-step flux removal (CS-2, TS-5). No high pressure or >1400 °C step is required.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux transport should improve diffusion and crystal growth, as CS-2 shows for lanthanoid carbides and TS-5 for lanthanide oxides. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates Ho–C–O oxycarbide growth from Li flux, so oxygen solubility and Li–O side reactions are potential issues [Reasonable Inference]. This gives high kinetic potential but lower transferability than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Ho, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description emphasizes corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, but not simultaneous face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met; CS-4 also supports stable corner/edge-sharing Caswellsilverite connectivity. Unit-cell atom count is not clearly established by CS/SS/TS, so that check is treated as untriggered but uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ho₂CO is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest analogue support comes from chemically similar rare-earth carbide halides Gd₂CBr/Gd₂CCl, which are stable and synthesized by sealed Ta solid-state methods (CS-1, CS-4), and from C/O octahedral ScAlOC showing that mixed carbide–oxide frameworks can exist (SS-1/TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the structural match to Caswellsilverite-like rare-earth octahedral networks, especially CS-4. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and stoichiometric control of the C/O anion sublattice while avoiding Ho₂O₃, Ho carbides, residual graphite, or metallic Ho impurities [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires sealed, oxygen-controlled, long-duration annealing.
