### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability threshold specified in the prompt. TS provides useful support because several experimentally reported thermodynamic analogues lie at similar hull energies, including Li2CuO2 at 0.047 eV/atom, Li8TbO6 at 0.0507 eV/atom, and Na3Cu2SbO6 at 0.056 eV/atom (TS). SS also includes stable or near-stable Li-rich oxides such as Li6TeO6 with E_hull = 0 and Li8IrO6 with E_hull ≈ 0.005 eV/atom (SS). CS is less decisive for target thermodynamics because most chemically similar Li phosphates/silicates/arsenates are ground-state tetrahedral oxoanion compounds rather than octahedral Li6SO6; CS is therefore scored as only partially informative, effectively neutral-to-supportive.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the best structural analogue Li6TeO6 requires sealed Ar annealing at 740 °C for 30 d, not extreme temperature or pressure but clearly controlled containment (SS). TS Li8MO6 analogues also require sealed Ag capsules/ampoules and long reaction times, reinforcing the need for dry sealed handling (TS). Kinetically, the route benefits from mobile Li chemistry but is penalized by likely slow ordering and the structural mismatch between trigonal Li6TeO6 and cubic Li6SO6 [Reasonable Inference based on SS]. CS provides insufficient direct signal for this route because the closest chemical analogues are Li3PO4/Li3AsO4 tetrahedral oxoanion phases rather than Li6MO6 octahedral phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best balance because Li2O2-rich TS routes demonstrate that reactive peroxide sources can form Li-rich oxides at 570–700 °C in sealed capsules, which is milder than many conventional ceramic routes (TS). F2 is still penalized because Li2O2/Li2O require dry handling and sealed containment, consistent with sealed Li-rich oxide preparations (TS, SS). F3 is improved relative to Route 1 because peroxide provides a more active Li/O source [Reasonable Inference], but structural reconstruction remains uncertain because no CS/SS/TS entry contains sulfur in the exact octahedral SO6 environment. SS support is structurally useful through Li6TeO6, while CS remains insufficient for octahedral sulfate chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized heavily in F2 because high pressure above 5 GPa is required; TS NaAlO2 used ~110 kbar and specialized Bridgman-anvil equipment (TS). The F3 score is still respectable because TS shows that a metastable/high-pressure oxide can transform rapidly at 900 °C under pressure and be quenched (TS). This route is most justified if the cubic Li6SO6 phase is not accessible at ambient pressure, because the octahedral S(VI) coordination is unusual and may require densification [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure Li–S–O synthesis, so uncertainty is high.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one clear amorphization risk: large mismatch between Li+ and S6+ size/coordination preferences [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure contains both corner- and edge-sharing OLi4S square pyramids, but no face sharing is reported, so the mixed-connectivity criterion as written is not fully triggered. The target formula unit is small, and the prompt gives no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that large-cell complexity is a major amorphization driver. Therefore F4 is low-to-medium risk rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li6SO6 is likely a marginally metastable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which is within the empirical synthesizable range and matches several experimentally realized TS analogues near the same hull energy (TS). The strongest support comes from SS, especially Li6TeO6, which has the same Li6MO6 stoichiometric motif and M–O octahedra prepared by sealed solid-state synthesis (SS). TS further supports the feasibility of Li-rich M–O frameworks using Li2O2/Li2O in sealed capsules at 570–700 °C, making peroxide-assisted synthesis the most attractive route (TS). The critical enabling factor is high Li/O chemical activity under sealed dry conditions, supported by Li2O2-rich Li8MO6 syntheses (TS). The critical limiting factor is the unusual octahedral coordination of small S(VI), for which CS provides no direct analogue and SS support must be extrapolated from larger Te/Sb/rare-earth or transition-metal octahedral oxides (SS; [Reasonable Inference]).
