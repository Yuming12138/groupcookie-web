### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target Y₂Mo₂C₃ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored in the most favorable thermodynamic bin (target data). Multiple close analogues, including Ce₂Mo₂C₃, Sm₂Mo₂C₃, Er₂Mo₂C₃, and Ho₂Mo₂C₃, also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, indicating that Ln₂Mo₂C₃ carbides are thermodynamically stable in the provided dataset (CS-1/2/5; SS-1/2/3; TS-1/2/3). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or single-phase synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because it requires arc-melting, Ar atmosphere, evacuated silica-tube annealing, Ta wrapping, and multiple handling steps (CS-5; SS-1/2; TS-1/2). Although the post-anneal temperature is only 900 °C, the arc-melting step imposes very high local temperatures [Reasonable Inference from arc-melting] (CS-5; SS-1/2; TS-1/2). The route avoids high pressure, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that volatile precursor loss is a major issue, so no volatility deduction is applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by low precursor activity because the route uses elemental rare-earth metal, Mo powder, and graphite, all low-diffusivity solids (CS-5; SS-1/2; TS-1/2). Arc-melting improves initial homogenization, but one-week annealing and impurity reports show that ordering and phase purification are not trivial (CS-5; SS-1/2; TS-1/2). The structural match to Ho₂Mo₂C₃/Er₂Mo₂C₃ is strong, which partially offsets the refractory precursor penalty (SS-1/2; TS-1/2).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is condition-severe because Ce₂Mo₂C₃/Sm₂Mo₂C₃ analogues require arc-melting, 1623 K annealing, dry Ar glove-box handling, weld-sealed Mo crucibles, slow cooling, and quenching (CS-1/2; SS-3; TS-3). It is not penalized for high pressure because the route is ambient-pressure sealed-crucible synthesis (CS-1/2; SS-3; TS-3). Volatility is not explicitly identified in CS/SS/TS, so volatility is treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has the best kinetic score because arc-melting plus high-temperature annealing is a demonstrated pathway for the closest chemical and structural analogues Ce₂Mo₂C₃ and Sm₂Mo₂C₃ (CS-1/2; SS-3; TS-3). The Ce₂Mo₂C₃ structure closely mirrors the target’s Y/Mo/C polyhedral topology, which increases the structural-reconstruction score (CS-1). The main uncertainty is phase competition with Mo₂C and the need to tune carbon stoichiometry, because analogous syntheses report small Mo₂C minority phases (CS-1/2; SS-3; TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for 1673 K annealing, controlled Ar/sealed Mo ampoule conditions, glove-box-style handling, and multi-step processing (CS-3). It avoids arc-melting, but that also removes the melt-homogenization advantage [Reasonable Inference]. It is not penalized for high pressure or volatile precursors because the provided Sm₂Cr₂C₃ route does not involve high pressure and gives no volatility signal (CS-3).

#### F3 — Route 3

The reactive-sintering route has weaker kinetics because elemental Mo and graphite must react largely through solid-state diffusion without a prior melt step [Reasonable Inference]. The Sm₂Cr₂C₃ analogue shows that R₂M₂C₃ carbides can be produced by sealed-ampoule sintering, but it uses Cr rather than Mo and reported a minor Cr₇C₃ impurity (CS-3). Therefore, this route is plausible but less preferred than direct Mo-containing arc-melt routes (CS-1/2/5; SS-1/2/3; TS-1/2/3).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains strongly dissimilar cation environments, with Y–C bonds around 2.45–2.55 Å and Mo–C bonds around 2.07–2.16 Å, so a cation-size/bonding mismatch trigger is assigned (target description; [Reasonable Inference]). The target description shows corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but no face-sharing, so the strongest mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not fully triggered (target description). The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to trigger the >40-atom criterion, so the overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

Y₂Mo₂C₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple Ln₂Mo₂C₃ analogues also sit on the hull (target data; CS-1/2/5; SS-1/2/3; TS-1/2/3). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports synthesis because Ce₂Mo₂C₃, Sm₂Mo₂C₃, Ho₂Mo₂C₃, and Er₂Mo₂C₃ are experimentally accessible using elemental rare-earth/Mo/C routes (CS-1/2/5; SS-1/2/3; TS-1/2/3). The most critical limiting factor is phase purity, especially competition from Mo₂C or residual Mo, because these impurities were reported in close molybdenum carbide analogues (CS-1/2/5; SS-1/2/3; TS-1/2/3). A secondary limitation is the need for stringent inert handling and high-temperature sealed crucible processing (CS-1/2; SS-3; TS-3).
