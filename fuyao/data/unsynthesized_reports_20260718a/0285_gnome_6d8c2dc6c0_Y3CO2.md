### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it at the top of the required thermodynamic stability scale. Several thermodynamic analogues are also at or near the hull, including ScAlOC at 0.0 eV/atom (TS1), Ce₂Zr₂O₇ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS3), and La₃SbO₃ at 0.0 eV/atom (TS5), supporting that related reduced oxide/oxycarbide chemistries can be stable. The main uncertainty is that none of the CS/SS/TS entries is exactly Y₃CO₂, so the convex-hull prediction is strong but not experimentally validated for this composition.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized heavily because high-temperature rare-earth oxy-compounds require 1300–1600 °C, Ta tubes, Ar/dynamic vacuum, and repeated annealing (SS1, SS3, TS5), while ScAlOC requires 1820 °C under Ar (SS5, TS1). Y metal handling under inert atmosphere is a reasonable extension of rare-earth metal glovebox practice in SS1/SS3/TS5. Insufficient evidence from CS suggests no direct low-temperature chemical pathway for Y₃CO₂, so Route 1 remains severe.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The oxide/metal/carbon route uses refractory precursors, and analogous RE oxy-compounds require repeated high-T treatments (SS1, SS3, TS5). ScAlOC shows that C/O mixed solids can form at high T but also that competing phases and low-yield byproduct formation can occur (SS5, TS1). The kinetic score is therefore low-to-moderate, with uncertainty from the lack of direct Y₃CO₂ analogue.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 benefits from reactive carbide/acetylide precedent at low temperature in CS2/CS3 and 553 K in CS5, but yttrium oxide/carbide diffusion is expected to require much higher temperature [Reasonable Inference]. Vacuum/Ar and glovebox handling are required, consistent with CS2/CS3/CS5. The need to pre-synthesize YC₂/Y₂C₃ adds step complexity and air-sensitivity risk.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Preformed Y–C bonds should improve carbon incorporation relative to graphite [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the reactivity of binary acetylides/carbides in CS2/CS3/CS5. However, Y₂O₃ remains refractory, and SS1/SS3/TS5 show that rare-earth oxide frameworks often need high temperatures. The absence of any direct Y–C–O carbide-assisted analogue keeps the score moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux growth is less severe than direct ceramic synthesis because BaLn₂O₄ can be grown at 1000→820 °C under Ar, below traditional ceramic temperatures (SS2, TS2). The route still requires sealed ampoules, air-sensitive flux handling, slow cooling, and flux separation (SS2, TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS exists for a chemically direct flux route to Y₃CO₂, so the route is penalized for uncertainty and multi-step handling.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux media can enhance mass transport and crystallization, as demonstrated for BaLn₂O₄ single crystals across lanthanides/Y-family chemistry (SS2, TS2). This directly supports improved diffusion relative to dense ceramic routes. The limitation is phase selectivity: ScAlOC formation in an Al melt produced oxycarbide material but with byproducts and low-yield behavior (SS5, TS1), so C/O activity and impurity incorporation remain uncertain.

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
The high-pressure route is operationally difficult because TS4 requires belt-type apparatus, BN crucibles, Ta furnace components, and 15–50 kbar for rare-earth monoxides. This route is not directly required by the target’s E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it is treated as a fallback. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates pressure is necessary for Y₃CO₂ specifically.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
Pressure can improve densification and stabilize reduced dense phases [Reasonable Inference], and TS4 confirms pressure can access rare-earth monoxides. However, TS4 also reports difficult phase control, incomplete reactions, and air sensitivity for monoxides.
