### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable boundary of the provided 90–100 scoring bin. Similar metastable materials with E_hull near this range are experimentally accessible: MgCu2O3 at 0.052 eV/atom by flux growth (CS2), BaFe4O7 at 0.070 eV/atom by hydrothermal synthesis (CS5), CaNbO3 at 0.059 eV/atom by high-T synthesis (TS1), and LaTiO3 at 0.068 eV/atom by vacuum sintering (TS2). The remaining uncertainty is that the target’s Mg6PtO6 chemistry has no direct Pt-containing analogue in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: solid-state oxidation

The solid-state route is supported by several oxide syntheses that use MgO or refractory oxides with repeated grinding and high-temperature holds, especially MgTi2O4 at 1080 °C under vacuum (SS1), Be4TeO7 for 10–14 days at 800 °C in air (CS3), and LaTiO3 at 1180–1220 °C in evacuated silica capsules (TS2). Condition severity is moderate-to-high because ceramic diffusion likely requires ≥950–1050 °C and multiple regrinding steps [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are limiting because MgO is refractory and the target structure is not inherited from a known precursor, so complete reconstruction is expected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: flux growth

Flux growth is the best bulk-supported route because MgCu2O3 was grown from MgO + CuO in KCl flux and isolated by dissolving the flux (CS2). This directly addresses the kinetic problem of MgO reactivity, and CS2 explicitly notes that flux growth succeeded where solid-state routes only gave microcrystalline products. The main uncertainty is Pt speciation in chloride flux and possible PtO2 reduction/decomposition, which are not covered by the provided CS/SS/TS data and are therefore [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure oxygen-assisted synthesis

High-pressure synthesis is justified by pressure-stabilized or pressure-transformed oxides: Mg2GeO4 spinel was obtained at 850 °C and 5 kbar with water in a sealed Au tube (CS4), and PrO was made at 15–50 kbar and 600–1000 °C (SS3, TS3). For Mg6PtO6, high oxygen fugacity and pressure may suppress decomposition and stabilize a dense cubic phase [Reasonable Inference]. The difficulty is high because specialized equipment is required and no Mg–Pt–O high-pressure analogue is provided.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: thin-film epitaxy

The thin-film route has the lowest thermal severity because nonequilibrium deposition can operate below bulk sintering temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct thin-film evidence, so the evidence signal for this route is neutral from CS/SS/TS. Its kinetic score is limited by Mg volatility/composition control and by uncertainty about whether the Fm-3m Mg6PtO6 phase crystallizes directly or needs a bulk-like high-temperature reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure has mixed corner- and edge-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers one amorphization-risk factor; similar mixed edge/corner networks appear in MgCu2O3 and MgTi2O4 (CS2, SS1). The Fm-3m conventional unit cell likely exceeds 40 atoms for a 13-atom formula unit, giving a second trigger [Reasonable Inference]. Ionic-radius mismatch is uncertain because CS/SS/TS do not provide direct Mg–Pt radius comparison, so that check is not triggered but remains a source of uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg6PtO6 is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The thermodynamic analogues strongly support possible synthesis of phases in this energy range, including flux-grown MgCu2O3 (CS2), hydrothermal BaFe4O7 (CS5), and high-temperature CaNbO3/LaTiO3 analogues (TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is kinetic assistance: either flux transport, high oxygen fugacity, pressure, or epitaxial stabilization is likely needed to access the ordered Mg–Pt oxide before decomposition or segregation [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is Pt redox/speciation under high-temperature oxide conditions, since no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates an Mg–Pt–O analogue. For bulk synthesis, flux growth is the most evidence-supported route; for maximum score, thin-film epitaxy is attractive but more speculative.
