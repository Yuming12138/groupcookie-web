### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues HfIrSn, HfIrGe, ZrIrSn, ZrIrSi, and ThRhSb all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, giving strong TS support for stable Hf/Zr/Th–Ir/Rh–p-block intermetallic phases (TS1–TS5). CS evidence is mostly favorable because CeIrSb and PrPtSb are E_hull = 0.0, but HfRhSb has a conflicting listed E_hull = 0.484 eV/atom despite E_above_ground_state = 0.0, so CS is not uniformly clean (CS1–CS3). The remaining uncertainty is whether competing Hf–Ir, Hf–Sb, or Ir–Sb binaries could form under non-equilibrium Sb-deficient conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + vacuum anneal

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the direct analogues HfRhSb and ThRhSb require arc melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing at 800 °C for 2–3 weeks (CS1; SS5; TS5). The main deductions are high-temperature melting, vacuum annealing, Sb volatility, and Sb toxicity; Sb vaporization is specifically highlighted indirectly by the use of AuSb₂ to minimize Sb loss in LnAuSb synthesis (CS5). Kinetically, arc melting improves homogenization, and repeated remelting is used for CeIrSb and ZrIrSn analogues (CS2; SS3; TS3). The uncertainty is whether HfIrSb orders into the half-Heusler arrangement as readily as HfRhSb/ThRhSb or instead forms a hexagonal/orthorhombic TiNiSi-type competitor like HfIrSn/HfIrGe analogues (SS1; SS2; TS1; TS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state sintering

Route 2 is more severe kinetically because it lacks a full melt-homogenization step; CS3 shows that related antimonides require 950–1100 °C, 2–4 weeks, and intermediate grinding. HfIrSn is reported as accessible by solid-phase reaction from elements, so the route is plausible, but the SS/TS entries provide insufficient detailed temperature/time/equipment evidence for precise optimization and are therefore partially neutral for detailed process design (SS1; TS1). The expected kinetic bottleneck is diffusion through Hf/Ir-rich solids, because both Hf and Ir are refractory metals [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is high for phase purity because direct solid-state reaction may leave binary Hf–Sb or Ir–Sb impurities if mixing is incomplete [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Induction/levitation melting + anneal

Route 3 is supported by HfIrGe, which was prepared by induction levitation melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing at 950 °C for 1–2 weeks (SS2; TS2). Condition severity is similar to Route 1 because melting refractory Hf/Ir-containing charges requires very high temperature [Reasonable Inference], and vacuum annealing is still needed (SS2; TS2). Kinetically, the melt route is favorable for homogenization, but HfIrGe is not half-Heusler; it is orthorhombic Pnma, so its structural transferability to HfIrSb is only partial (SS2; TS2). The major uncertainty is Sb volatility during induction melting, which is not directly addressed by HfIrGe data because Ge is less volatile than Sb under analogous conditions [Reasonable Inference; CS5].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Pre-reacted Ir–Sb precursor + arc melting

Route 4 intentionally addresses Sb volatility by analogy to LnAuSb synthesis, where AuSb₂ was used as a starting material to minimize Sb vaporization during arc melting (CS5). The route has worse F2 severity because it adds a binary precursor synthesis step before arc melting and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Its F3 score is modestly better than direct solid-state reaction because a preformed Ir–Sb precursor should improve local Ir–Sb contact and Sb retention [Reasonable Inference; CS5]. Remaining uncertainty is whether IrSb/IrSb₂ reacts cleanly with Hf to the half-Heusler phase without trapping binary residues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The half-Heusler analogue set suggests low intrinsic glass-forming/amorphization tendency because HfRhSb and ThRhSb are simple ordered F-43m ternaries obtained by conventional arc melting plus anneal (CS1; SS5; TS5). One risk factor is the size/coordination contrast between Hf and Ir, inferred from distinct target bond lengths and analogous ThRhSb coordination distances [Reasonable Inference; SS5; TS5]. The unit cell is not large, and the half-Heusler topology is comparatively simple, so no large-cell penalty is applied (CS1; SS5; TS5). The uncertainty is that disorder between Ir/Sb or antisite defects could occur if annealing is insufficient [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfIrSb is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from HfRhSb and ThRhSb, both half-Heusler F-43m antimonides prepared by arc melting followed by long vacuum annealing (CS1; SS5; TS5). The most important enabling factor is the direct structural and procedural match to known half-Heusler antimonide syntheses (CS1; SS5; TS5). The most important limiting factor is maintaining Sb stoichiometry during high-temperature melting, since Sb vaporization is important enough in LnAuSb chemistry that AuSb₂ precursor strategies are used to reduce loss (CS5). A secondary limitation is slow ordering/homogenization, reflected by repeated remelting or multi-week annealing in related intermetallic systems (CS1; CS2; SS3; TS3; SS5; TS5).
