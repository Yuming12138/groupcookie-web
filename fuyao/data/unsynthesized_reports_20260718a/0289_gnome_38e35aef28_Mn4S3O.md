### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the highest scoring thermodynamic bin. Thermodynamically similar Mn chalcogenides include multiple stable or nearly stable compounds, including Na₂Mn₂S₃, Na₂Mn₂Se₃, Cs₂Mn₃S₄, and Cs₂Mn₃Se₄ with E_hull = 0.0, and MnSe with E_hull ≈0.0035 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5). Chemically similar sulfides also show low hull energies from 0 to ~0.08 eV/atom (CS-1–CS-5), but CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Mn–O–S oxysulfide stability. Remaining uncertainty is mainly that the computed ground state may depend on O/S ordering and magnetic configuration [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses high but standard chalcogenide temperatures below 1000 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty was applied [Reasonable Inference]. Vacuum sealed-tube synthesis and sulfur volatility are directly supported by sulfide analogues using evacuated quartz/silica tubes and elemental sulfur (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). The multistep deduction is justified because comparable direct sulfide syntheses required quenching, homogenization, annealing, slow cooling, or repeated recycling to reach single phase (CS-1, CS-4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically confirms sealed-tube Mn oxysulfide synthesis, so the route remains moderately severe.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The MnS/MnO powder route has moderate-to-slow kinetics because binary sulfide/oxide powders are less reactive than solution or flux precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Analogous solid-state sulfides often require 1000 °C or repeated heat treatments (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4), supporting a low structural-reconstruction score. The single-cation nature of Mn₄S₃O helps cation diffusion, but O/S ordering across tetrahedral sites has no direct CS/SS/TS precedent. Therefore the kinetic score is only moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 benefits from lower temperatures because Na₂Mn₂S₃ was synthesized from a Na₂CO₃/S/Mn melt at 870 K under Ar (SS-2, TS-2). The route still requires sulfur-rich flux handling, slow cooling, extraction/washing, and drying, all consistent with the reported flux method (SS-2, TS-2). Alkali sulfide/polysulfide fluxes are inferred to be moisture-sensitive and can generate H₂S on hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference], and related alkali manganese chalcogenides are air/moisture sensitive (SS-4, SS-5, TS-4, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of Mn oxysulfides is treated as neutral rather than negative.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because molten alkali polysulfide/carbonate media can enhance diffusion, and structurally similar Na₂Mn₂S₃ was grown by a flux/melt route (SS-2, TS-2). Related MnS₄ tetrahedral distances and networks in Na₂Mn₂S₃ and Cs₂Mn₃S₄ support structural transferability to a tetrahedral Mn–S/O target (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether oxygen can be incorporated in an ordered way rather than remaining as MnO or dissolving into the flux [Reasonable Inference]. This uncertainty prevents a very high F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses a reactive sulfurizing atmosphere, supported by chalcogen-loaded H₂ flow synthesis of related Mn chalcogenides (SS-5, TS-5). The use of H₂/H₂S or sulfur vapor imposes safety and redox-control penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Temperature can remain below 1000 °C in the proposed route, but related chalcogenide syntheses reach 950–1050 °C (SS-5, TS-5), so thermal margin is modest. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact Mn₄S₃O gas-solid formation makes this route challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Gas sulfurization can provide high sulfur activity and faster anion exchange than purely solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, MnO may either remain unreacted or be fully sulfurized to MnS depending on sulfur and oxygen potentials [Reasonable Inference]. Chalcogen-loaded H₂ synthesis is evidenced for alkali manganese selenides/tellurides, not Mn oxysulfides (SS-5, TS-5), so transferability is weaker than for flux synthesis. The route therefore receives intermediate kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Mn as the cation, so the cation-size-mismatch trigger is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description emphasizes corner-sharing tetrahedra and does not report simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity, unlike some SS/TS analogues that contain edge and corner sharing (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4). The target formula has only 8 atoms per formula unit, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom cell; CS/SS/TS are insufficient for exact target cell size and are neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low, although flux quenching could trap disorder if cooling is too rapid [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₄S₃O is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Mn chalcogenides are also on or near the hull (TS-1–TS-5). The analogues support synthesis of Mn chalcogenide tetrahedral frameworks, especially MnS₄-containing phases made under Ar flux or high-temperature solid-state conditions (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4). The strongest enabling factor is the simple single-cation Mn chemistry combined with a stable predicted hull position and Mn–S bond lengths consistent with known Mn sulfide tetrahedra (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-4). The main limiting factor is the lack of direct CS/SS/TS evidence for ordered Mn–O–S oxysulfide formation, so O/S chemical potential and site ordering are uncertain. The best practical route is an alkali polysulfide/carbonate flux because it lowers diffusion barriers and has direct precedent for crystallizing Mn sulfide tetrahedral phases (SS-2, TS-2).
