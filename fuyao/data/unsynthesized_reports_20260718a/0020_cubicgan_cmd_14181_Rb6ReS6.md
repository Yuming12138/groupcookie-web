### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which lies at the high-stability threshold in the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues near or above this value are reported as synthesizable, including K₂CoS₂ at 0.0506 eV/atom, Rb₂CoS₂ at 0.0476 eV/atom, Cs₂Se at 0.0751 eV/atom, and Rb₂S at 0.0876 eV/atom (TS2, TS4, TS1, TS3). The remaining uncertainty is that E_hull alone does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct Re oxidation state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, consistent with Rb₆HgS₄ synthesis at 360–380 °C in sealed ampoules and Rb₂S synthesis at 350 °C in evacuated quartz (CS1, SS4, TS3). The deductions come from controlled Ar/vacuum handling, volatile sulfur in a sealed system, and air-sensitive Rb sulfide chemistry (CS1, SS4, TS3). The main uncertainty is whether Re sulfide reacts sufficiently at 400–550 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is limited by Re incorporation and the change from tetrahedral HgS₄ analogue chemistry to octahedral ReS₆ target chemistry (CS1, SS4; target structure). Rb₂S and sulfur are chemically active and directly relevant, but long anneals of 7–10 d in Rb₆HgS₄ analogues indicate slow ordering or diffusion (CS1, SS4). The uncertainty is the lack of a direct Rb–Re–S analogue in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses no extreme pressure and temperatures below 1000 °C, consistent with Co sulfide analogues at 1030 K and Bi selenide analogues at 850 °C (TS2, TS4, SS2, CS5). Deductions arise from H₂/S reactive gas, volatile chalcogen control, and air-sensitive chalcogenide products (TS2, TS4, CS5). The key concern is that H₂ may oppose the high Re oxidation state implied by charge balance in Rb₆ReS₆ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Carbonates and metal powders are less active than binary sulfides, even though they are proven precursors for Co and Bi chalcogenides under chalcogen-charged H₂ (TS2, TS4, CS5). Re metal or oxide likely requires substantial sulfiding and structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The method has good precedent for alkali transition-metal chalcogenides but weak direct precedent for Rb₆ReS₆ composition.

#### F2 — Route 3

The azide route operates at moderate temperature around 500 °C and uses sealed ampoules, which is supported by Cs₄In₂S₅ synthesis (CS3). The score is reduced because RbN₃ handling, pressure generation from N₂, multistep preparation, and air-sensitive products increase severity (CS3). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty applies.

#### F3 — Route 3

The azide route gives high precursor activity because alkali azides are specifically used to introduce reactive heavier alkali metals without elemental Rb/Cs handling (CS3). However, Re transport and formation of a ReS₆ octahedral phase remain uncertain because CS3 involves In/Al sulfides rather than Re sulfides. Long anneals of 8–15 d in CS3 indicate that ordering can still be slow despite reactive precursors.

#### F2 — Route 4

Ammonothermal routes use moderate temperature but require 300–3000 bar NH₃ and special autoclaves, as shown for Cs₂S/Cs₂Se analogues (SS1, SS3, TS1). The rubric only penalizes >5 GPa pressure, so the formal high-pressure deduction is not triggered, but controlled NH₃ atmosphere, sealed volatile medium, multistep handling, and air sensitivity all apply (SS1, SS3, TS1). The practical difficulty is higher than the rubric captures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

High-pressure ammonia can enhance transport for binary alkali chalcogenides and promote crystal growth with slow cooling (SS1, SS3, TS1). However, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Re sulfide species dissolve or transport under these conditions. The structural similarity evidence is mostly binary and therefore weak for a ternary Rb₆ReS₆ phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Rb/Re cation size and charge contrast likely increases local ordering difficulty [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s Fm-3m symmetry and 13-atom formula likely imply a conventional cell above 40 atoms, giving a second amorphization/disorder trigger [Reasonable Inference]. The target has corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but no reported face sharing, so the most severe connectivity trigger is not fully met.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₆ReS₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues near 0.05–0.09 eV/atom are experimentally reported (TS2, TS3, TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from Rb₆HgS₄, which demonstrates Rb-rich sulfide synthesis from Rb₂S and metal sulfide precursors in sealed dry-Ar ampoules (CS1, SS4). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of reactive Rb₂S/sulfur chemistry at moderate temperature, which is supported by Rb₆HgS₄ and Rb₂S syntheses (CS1, SS4, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is Re redox/coordination control, because the target requires ReS₆ octahedra while the closest Rb₆MS₄ analogue contains tetrahedral HgS₄ units (CS1, SS4; target structure). Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine, with the best chance coming from sealed-ampoule sulfide chemistry rather than gas-flow or ammonothermal screening.
