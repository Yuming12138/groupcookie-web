### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the boundary of the 90–100 scoring bin specified by the prompt. TS analogues with positive hull energies near this range are experimentally synthesized, including K₃NiO₂ at 0.035 eV/atom (TS3), Cs₂Se at 0.075 eV/atom (TS1), and Rb₂S at 0.088 eV/atom (TS2). CS and SS provide many E_hull = 0 Cs-rich oxides, but they do not directly prove the Cs–S–O phase; CS/SS are supportive but not definitive. Remaining uncertainty is the unusual SO₆ octahedron rather than bulk thermodynamic instability.  

Source signals: CS supportive = 85; SS supportive = 85; TS strongly supportive for small positive E_hull phases = 90.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses temperatures similar to Cs₆[Al₂O₆] at 680 °C for 6 months (CS5), Cs₆[In₂O₆] at 650 °C for 14 d (CS4), and Cs₄IrO₄ at 740 °C for 86 d (CS1), so it avoids >1000 °C penalties. It is penalized for sealed Cs oxide handling, multi-step preparation/sealing/annealing, and air-sensitive precursors, all supported by CS1/CS5 and SS3. TS provides insufficient direct signal for sealed Cs–S–O oxide synthesis and is treated as neutral 50 for this route’s condition transferability. The resulting F2 = 70 reflects moderate temperature but demanding containment and handling.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The route has good Cs mobility because analogous Cs-rich oxides form at 300–740 °C (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS3). However, solid-state reaction from Cs₂SO₄ likely requires major sulfate-to-SO₆ structural reconstruction, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing in analogues, e.g., 86 d (CS1) and 6 months (CS5), indicates slow ordering can be severe. Thus the kinetic score is limited to 62.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains below 1000 °C and is supported by Cs-rich flux/melt syntheses at 500–800 °C (CS3) and Cs-rich suboxometallate formation at 300 °C (SS3). It still requires sealed containment and glovebox handling because Cs metal/Cs₂O/CsOₓ are air-sensitive, as shown by CS3 and SS3. It is also multi-step because flux synthesis requires loading, heating, slow cooling, and flux separation, as in CS3. TS gives insufficient direct support for oxide-flux synthesis and is neutral 50 for this condition factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux/melt chemistry improves precursor activity and cation mobility; CS3 explicitly reports A-rich melts forming Cs-rich germanates at 500–650 °C, and SS3 reports Cs₉AlO₄-type phases at only 300 °C. The structural reconstruction remains uncertain because no CS/SS/TS example shows octahedral sulfur in a Cs₆SO₆-type oxide. Phase competition is likely because CS3 reports multiple Cs-rich germanate/aluminate side phases. The F3 = 72 reflects better kinetics than Route 1 but persistent structural uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is inspired mainly by TS1, where Cs₂Se-II requires 1000–2000 bar ammonia conditions, but TS1 is a chalcogenide and gives weak chemical transferability to Cs–S–O oxides. The route avoids >5 GPa pressure but incurs penalties for controlled/reactive atmosphere, sealed volatile/reactive Cs oxides, multi-step pressure handling, and air-sensitive oxidants. CS1 and CS2 support the use of CsOₓ oxidants, but they do not require the high-pressure route. Therefore this route is feasible only as a secondary exploratory method.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Reactive CsO₂/CsO₁.₈ precursors are supported by CS2 and CS1, so precursor activity is acceptable. Pressure and oxygen activity may help stabilize a dense/high-coordination S–O environment, but this is mostly [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for high-pressure synthesis of Cs₆SO₆ or any octahedral sulfate analogue, so structural reconstruction remains the main kinetic uncertainty. The F3 = 65 reflects good chemical driving force but weak precedent.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Cs⁺ coordination environments with small S–O octahedra, giving a large cation size/coordination mismatch; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure. The conventional Fm-3m cell likely contains 52 atoms if Z = 4 for Cs₆SO₆, triggering the >40 atom count criterion. The target description does not establish mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for that check, so that item is not triggered. The medium F4 score of 55 captures possible ordering/amorphization risk in a large, Cs-rich, unusual oxyanion lattice.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₆SO₆ is likely near-ground-state or weakly metastable because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, and TS shows experimentally accessible alkali phases with comparable positive hull energies (TS1–TS4). The strongest support comes from CS and SS analogues showing that very Cs-rich oxides can be synthesized under sealed, dry Ar conditions using Cs₂O/CsOₓ, often at 300–740 °C (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS3). The most critical enabling factor is high Cs/oxide mobility in Cs-rich oxide flux or melt environments, supported by CS3 and SS3. The most critical limiting factor is the chemically unusual octahedral S–O coordination, because typical S(VI) oxides are sulfate-like and none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates Cs₆SO₆-type SO₆ formation. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful Cs activity control, sealed handling, and slow crystallization.
