### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the prompt’s ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability threshold. CS and SS analogues are mostly reported as hull-stable, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that alkali-rich oxides in this chemical/structural neighborhood can be thermodynamically viable (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). TS analogues include reported phases with E_hull values around 0.035–0.088 eV/atom, showing that materials at similar metastability can still be synthesized (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target’s Ir(VI) oxidation state is not directly demonstrated by the provided Ir analogues.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa conditions, consistent with many analogues synthesized at 450–740 °C in sealed capsules (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1, TS5). The penalties arise from air-sensitive Rb oxides, sealed-vessel requirements, and multistep precursor/capsule handling, all well documented in alkali oxide syntheses (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS5). The main uncertainty is whether an Ar-sealed alkali oxide mixture supplies enough oxygen chemical potential for Ir(VI) [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are mixed: Rb oxides are reactive, but IrO2 is refractory and Cs4IrO4 required 86 d even with CsO1.8 (SS1). The closest Ir structural analogues contain square-planar IrO4, whereas the target contains octahedral IrO6, implying substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from target structure and SS1/SS5]. The long annealing times in SS1 and SS5 support a moderate-to-slow kinetic assessment.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses similar temperatures to Route 1 but adds a more reactive oxygen-buffered environment, increasing severity. Oxygen-rich alkali reagents are supported by SS1, SS5, CS2, CS5, and TS1, while controlled O2 synthesis is supported by CS3. The route is penalized for controlled oxygen activity, sealed volatile/air-sensitive Rb oxidants, and multistep handling.

#### F3 — Route 2

Oxidizing alkali flux chemistry should improve precursor activity and possibly Ir oxidation [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the use of CsO1.8 and KO0.51 in alkali iridate synthesis (SS1, SS5). However, structural reconstruction remains a major uncertainty because no provided analogue has the exact cubic Rb6IrO6 motif or octahedral Ir(VI) environment. Thus Route 2 has slightly better kinetic/chemical plausibility than Route 1 but worse condition severity.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is exploratory and inherits the same air-sensitive and sealed-vessel penalties as Routes 1–2. CS3 supports oxygen-atmosphere sealed synthesis for high-valent alkali oxides, but no provided source demonstrates high-pressure O2 synthesis for Rb–Ir–O. TS2 supports high-pressure stabilization only generically and is chemically dissimilar, so its signal for this target is weak.

#### F3 — Route 3

A post-oxidation route could reduce reconstruction if a suitable Rb–Ir–O precursor is found [Reasonable Inference]. However, such a precursor is not reported in CS/SS/TS, and diffusion-limited oxidation is likely for dense alkali iridate particles [Reasonable Inference]. Long durations in CS3, TS1, and TS5 support the expectation of slow kinetics.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb6IrO6 is probably a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a strongly robust ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS and SS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of sealed alkali-rich oxide synthesis, especially using Rb/Cs oxides and IrO2-type precursors in Ag/Pt capsules under Ar (CS1–CS5, SS1, SS5). The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and fluxing/oxygen-buffering ability of Rb oxides or Rb peroxides, which is repeatedly exploited in related alkali-rich oxides (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS5, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is achieving and retaining formal Ir(VI) in an octahedral IrO6 environment, because the closest Ir analogues show square-planar IrO4 rather than the target octahedral geometry (SS1, SS5). Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require careful oxygen activity, sealed handling, and long annealing.
