### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and supporting a ground-state assignment from the supplied thermodynamic data. Many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar alkali oxometallates are also reported as E_hull = 0, including Na4WO5/KNa3WO5-related phases, K2WO4, K3SbO4, K3LiIrO4, Cs4IrO4, and K4GeO4 (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to hydration/CO2 reaction, and several analogues require long sealed-tube anneals (CS1, SS2, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is conditionally severe because it uses air-sensitive alkali oxides and sealed containers, directly mirroring K-rich alkali oxide syntheses in Au/Ag/Ni tubes (CS1, CS5, SS3, TS5). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, which improves the score relative to refractory ceramic syntheses. The main uncertainty is Re2O7 volatility and oxidizing behavior, which is [Reasonable Inference] not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics benefit from highly reactive K2O/KO2/Re(VII) precursors [Reasonable Inference], and K-rich analogues form at 600–750 °C rather than requiring extreme temperatures (CS1, CS5, TS5). However, the repeated long annealing times of 28–86 d in structurally/thermodynamically similar materials indicate slow ordering and narrow crystallization windows (SS2, SS3, TS5). Structural transferability is moderate because K3SbO4 and K4Na2TeO6 provide octahedral high-valent oxide analogues, but none contains Re (CS5, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less severe than Route 1 because it can start from KReO4 and K2CO3/K2O rather than volatile Re2O7 [Reasonable Inference], and it stays below 1000 °C as supported by CS4/CS5/TS5. It still requires dry handling and likely regrinding/annealing cycles, similar to K2WO4 and K3XO4 syntheses (CS4, SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct KReO4-to-K5ReO6 conversion, so the condition score is moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 2

KReO4 plus K2O/K2CO3 should be kinetically more active than refractory oxide mixtures [Reasonable Inference], and alkali carbonate/oxide ceramic routes are proven for K/Rb molybdates/tungstates (CS3, CS4). The limiting kinetic issue is structural reconstruction from ReO4 tetrahedra to ReO6 octahedra [Reasonable Inference], whereas CS3/CS4 analogues retain tetrahedral MO4 units. Long-duration sealed K-rich analogues suggest slow cation/anion ordering and possible need for repeated annealing (CS1, SS3, TS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild nominal temperature and pressure, supported only by CS2 for solution growth of Rb2CrO4. However, the target is not a simple aqueous oxoanion salt, and insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports solution crystallization of K5ReO6. Dry highly alkaline flux handling adds chemical severity [Reasonable Inference], so it is not scored as an easy route despite low temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Ion transport in solution or molten hydroxide would be favorable [Reasonable Inference], and CS2 shows that alkali oxoanion salts can crystallize from solution. The major weakness is structural: CS2 concerns tetrahedral CrO4 chemistry, while target requires an anhydrous K-rich ReO6 environment; insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports such a pathway. Therefore Route 3 is best treated as an exploratory crystal-growth approach, not the primary synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has strongly size-mismatched cations, K and Re, which triggers the ionic-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula and described local structure do not clearly indicate a >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS resolves those checks. Analogues with K-rich frameworks crystallize successfully under slow annealing, suggesting amorphization is not the dominant risk if dry sealed conditions are used (CS5, SS3, TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K5ReO6 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related alkali oxometallates are also calculated or reported at E_hull ≈ 0 (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support dry, sealed, alkali-rich solid-state synthesis, especially for K-rich high-valent oxide frameworks requiring K2O/KOx and long anneals (CS1, CS5, SS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high potassium oxide activity while preserving Re(VII), likely via K2O/KO2/KReO4 chemistry in a sealed dry environment [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with simpler perrhenate-like ReO4 salts and possible volatility/reactivity of Re(VII) oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but experimentally demanding, with the best strategy being a dry sealed-tube K-rich ceramic/flux route rather than aqueous crystallization.
