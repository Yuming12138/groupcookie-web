### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues include multiple hull-stable Ca–Pb/Ca–tetrel-related phases, including Ca₂Pb, Ca₃GeN, CaSrPb, Ca₅Sn₃H, and Ca₅Ge₃H (TS-1 to TS-5). Structural analogues also include hull-stable Ca₂Pb, Ca₃GeN, and CaSrPb, supporting the plausibility of stable Ca–Pb frameworks and Ca–nitride motifs (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5). The main uncertainty is that one chemically close reported compound, Ca₃PbN, is listed with a high computed E_hull despite reported synthesis, so CS thermodynamic evidence is contradictory and was not weighted as strongly (CS-1).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because it likely requires 1100–1300 °C, sealed Nb/Ta containment, and air-free handling, matching the conditions reported for Pb/Ge/Sn nitride analogues (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2). The need to avoid material loss at high temperature is explicitly noted for the sealed Nb-tube tetrel nitride route (CS-1, SS-4). The route is chemically well supported but operationally demanding due to Ca/Ca₃N₂ sensitivity and Pb toxicity/volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because Ca₃N₂ is an active nitrogen precursor and is used in known Ca–tetrel nitride syntheses (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2). Diffusion should be less severe than for refractory oxide systems because Ca–Pb intermetallics form by melting and Pb is highly mobile at high temperature (SS-1, TS-1; [Reasonable Inference]). However, the target is orthorhombic Cmmm rather than cubic Pm-3m Ca₃PbN, so structural reconstruction and ordering are nontrivial (CS-1; target structure).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids sealed high-pressure equipment but still requires reactive Ca, toxic Pb, dry Ar/N₂ control, and likely 950–1100 °C temperatures. Ca₃AuN was made from elements under Ar then flowing N₂ at 950 °C (CS-2), and Sr₃BiN-type phases were made from melt beads under ambient-pressure N₂ (CS-3). The key uncertainty is whether flowing N₂ provides sufficient nitrogen chemical potential for the more complex Ca₇Pb₃N₂ phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The alloy/melt pathway is kinetically attractive for Ca/Pb mixing, since Ca₂Pb forms by melting elemental mixtures (SS-1, TS-1). Direct nitridation from elements is documented for Ca₃AuN and melt-bead alkaline-earth pnictide nitrides (CS-2, CS-3). The limiting kinetic step is nitrogen incorporation and ordering into the Cmmm Ca–Pb framework rather than cation diffusion [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has a lower synthesis temperature, around 820–900 °C, based on Ca₂AuN, but requires a high-pressure dry-N₂ vessel and careful regrinding/reannealing (CS-4). The pressure is not >5 GPa, so it is not penalized as extreme high pressure, but the controlled dry-N₂ condition and air-sensitive precursors are still significant constraints (CS-4). This route is operationally challenging but less thermally harsh than the 1300 °C sealed Nb route (CS-1, SS-4).

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetic score is the best among the routes because Ca₃N₂ provides activated nitrogen and high N₂ pressure helps maintain nitrogen activity during annealing (CS-4). Ca₂AuN was reproducibly formed at 820 °C using Ca₃N₂ and a metal foil under dry N₂ pressure, with a second heat treatment (CS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that Pb differs from Au in bonding/volatility, so phase selection for Ca₇Pb₃N₂ is inferred rather than directly demonstrated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is severe because it combines intermetallic pre-synthesis with a second sealed nitridation step, making it explicitly multi-step [Reasonable Inference]. CaSrPb analogues used welded Nb tubes, fused silica jackets, quenching, long equilibration, and slow cooling (SS-5, TS-3). The approach is feasible but time-consuming and sensitive to stoichiometry.

#### F3 — Route 4

Pre-forming a Ca–Pb alloy should improve cation homogeneity and reduce Ca/Pb diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], consistent with Ca₂Pb and CaSrPb formation from elemental melting or sealed-tube reactions (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-3). The structural match to the target’s Ca–Pb framework is favorable because target Ca–Pb distances overlap with Ca₂Pb/CaSrPb distances (SS-1, SS-5). The main kinetic difficulty is nitrogen insertion into an already formed intermetallic framework [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains multiple cation environments and mixed polyhedral connectivity, including corner-, edge-, and face-sharing PbCa₁₀Pb₂, PbCa₁₂, and NCa₆ units, which increases the likelihood of disorder or glassy/poorly crystalline intermediates [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Similar Ca–Pb frameworks and mixed sharing are documented in Ca₂Pb and Ca₂AuN analogues (SS-1, CS-4). Unit-cell atom count cannot be confirmed from CS/SS/TS evidence, so that check is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is medium rather than high because many related Ca–Pb and Ca–nitride phases crystallize under solid-state conditions (CS-1 to CS-4, SS-1, SS-4, SS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₇Pb₃N₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues in Ca–Pb/Ca–tetrel systems are hull-stable (TS-1 to TS-5). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis moderately well: Ca₃PbN/Ca₃GeN demonstrate Ca–tetrel nitride formation from Ca₃N₂ at high temperature (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2), while Ca₂Pb/CaSrPb demonstrate accessible Ca–Pb frameworks (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficient nitrogen chemical potential while retaining volatile/toxic Pb, which is best addressed by the moderate-pressure dry-N₂ route (CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition between Ca–Pb binaries, Ca₃PbN-like anti-perovskites, and the target Cmmm ordered phase [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful atmosphere, stoichiometry, and phase-control optimization.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Air/moisture sensitivity of Ca and Ca₃N₂ | High | Use Ar/He glovebox; Ca₃PbN/Ca₃GeN analogues use Ar-filled glovebox and sealed Nb tubes (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2). |
| 2 | All | Pb toxicity and possible Pb loss at high temperature | High | Use welded Nb/Ta tubes or pressure vessels; material-loss prevention by sealed Nb tubes is explicitly used for Pb/Ge/Sn nitride analogues (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2). |
| 3 | All | Competing Ca–Pb binary formation, especially Ca₂Pb/CaPb-like phases | Medium–High | Control stoichiometry and use nitrogen-rich conditions; Ca₂Pb is a stable/synthesizable Ca–Pb phase, so it is a likely competitor (SS-1, TS-1). |
| 4 | All | Competing Ca₃PbN or anti-perovskite nitride formation | Medium–High | Vary Ca:Pb:N ratio and annealing temperature; Ca₃PbN is a known chemically close nitride formed at high temperature (CS-1). |
| 5 | Route 1 | Very high annealing temperature needed for Pb/tetrel nitride formation | High | Use sealed Nb/Ta tubes and small batch sizes; Ge/Sn/Pb analogues required 1300 °C and sealed Nb tubes (CS-1, SS-4, TS-2). |
| 6 | Route 2 | Incomplete nitrogen incorporation from flowing N₂ | Medium | Use pre-alloyed melt beads and prolonged nitridation; melt-bead nitridation produced Sr₃BiN-type phases under ambient N₂ (CS-3), and Ca₃AuN formed under flowing N₂ at 950 °C (CS-2). |
| 7 | Route 3 | Pressure-vessel complexity and dry-N₂ control | Medium–High | Follow Ca₂AuN-type high-pressure dry-N₂ protocol with 100–200 bar N₂, long hold, and reanneal (CS-4). |
| 8 | Route 4 | Slow ordering kinetics in multicomponent Cmmm phase | Medium | Use prealloying, regrinding, and slow cooling/equilibration; CaSrPb analogues used quench, 10-day equilibration at 550 °C, and slow cooling (SS-5, TS-3). |
| 9 | All | Crystal growth difficulty for single-crystal verification | Medium | Slow cooling or long equilibration may help; Ca₂Pb yielded only a few suitable crystals despite successful synthesis (SS-1, TS-1). |
| 10 | All | Stoichiometry sensitivity in Ca-rich nitrides | High | Use small sealed batches, monitor mass loss, and test slight Ca/Pb excesses [Reasonable Inference]; related nitride syntheses required careful sealed conditions and sample-size limits due to nitrogen pressure/material loss (SS-4, TS-2). |
