### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. This is reinforced by several synthesized related nitrides with E_hull = 0, including SrTiN₂, Ba₂NbN₃, Ba₅(TiN₃)₂, and Ba₂TaN₃ (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). The main uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as SrTiN₂ and other alkaline-earth nitrides still report impurities and practical synthesis difficulty (SS1/TS1; CS1).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 980 °C and flowing N₂ based on Ba₁₀[Ti₄N₁₂], avoiding high pressure and >1000 °C temperature penalties (SS3/TS3). The severity comes from controlled N₂ atmosphere, air-sensitive Ba₃N₂, and multi-step glovebox/heating/grinding procedures (SS3/TS3; CS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that a sealed volatile precursor is mandatory for this exact route, so that item is scored neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Ba₃N₂ is chemically active, but TiN is refractory and analogous Ba–Ti nitride syntheses require long anneals, so precursor activity is moderate (SS3/TS3). Diffusion is not prohibitive because Ba–Ti and Sr–Ti nitrides form at ~980–1030 °C, but long dwell times show slow ordering (SS1/TS1; SS3/TS3). Structural reconstruction is less favorable than Route 2 because the known Ba–Ti product Ba₅(TiN₃)₂ is not the same structure as BaTiN₂ (SS3/TS3).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits the SrTiN₂ conditions: 1030 °C, 5 days, Ar, Mo foil, sealed stainless crucible, and glovebox handling (SS1/TS1). The penalties arise from T >1000 °C, sealed-container operation, multiple steps, and air-sensitive alkaline-earth nitride precursors (SS1/TS1; CS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports using high pressure, so no high-pressure deduction is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

This route has the strongest structural kinetic argument because SrTiN₂ is isostructural with the BaTiN₂ target and has nearly identical Ti–N connectivity (SS1/TS1). TiN remains a refractory precursor and the reported 5-day dwell implies slow solid-state diffusion (SS1/TS1). The larger Ba cation may alter phase stability and favor other Ba–Ti nitrides [Reasonable Inference] from the existence of Ba₅(TiN₃)₂ (SS3/TS3), so the kinetic score is high-moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is based indirectly on Ba₂TaN₃ crystal growth from Li/Ba under N₂ at 1000 °C (SS4/TS4). It avoids high pressure but uses reactive metals, controlled N₂, likely volatile/reactive flux components, and specialized Ta/Nb containment [Reasonable Inference] from SS4/TS4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that BaTiN₂ specifically can be flux-grown, so the method remains more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A Ba/Li flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth compared with dry TiN/Ba₃N₂ pellets [Reasonable Inference], and Ba₂TaN₃ single crystals were formed using a Li/Ba/N₂ route (SS4/TS4). However, no CS/SS/TS example shows BaTiN₂ itself or SrTiN₂ grown by flux, so structural selectivity is uncertain. Competing Ba–Ti nitride compositions are plausible because Ba₅(TiN₃)₂ is a known Ba–Ti nitride formed from Ba₃N₂/TiN under N₂ (SS3/TS3).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Ba–Ti size/coordination contrast, as shown by Ba–N distances of 2.85–2.97 Å and Ti–N distances of 1.83–2.11 Å in the target description, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check. The structure is small and ordered, with the closest SrTiN₂ analogue listed as Sr₂Ti₂N₄ in P4/nmm, so the >40 atom check is not triggered (SS1/TS1). Corner and edge sharing occur, but no face sharing is described for either the target or SrTiN₂, so the mixed connectivity check is not triggered (SS1/TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaTiN₂ is likely a ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several closely related nitrides are also reported on the hull (SS1–SS4; TS1–TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from SrTiN₂, which is isostructural P4/nmm and synthesized from alkaline-earth nitride plus TiN at 1030 °C (SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the direct structural transferability from SrTiN₂ to BaTiN₂, especially the conserved TiN₅/Ti–N framework (SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in Ba–Ti–N, because Ba₅(TiN₃)₂ is a known competing Ba–Ti nitride formed from Ba₃N₂ and TiN under N₂ (SS3/TS3). Overall, BaTiN₂ appears synthetically plausible but likely requires careful nitrogen activity, Ba stoichiometry, and air-free high-temperature processing.
