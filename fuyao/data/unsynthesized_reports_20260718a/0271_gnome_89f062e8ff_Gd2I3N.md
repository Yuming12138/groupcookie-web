### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is explicitly 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom thermodynamic bracket. TS analogues also include several E_hull = 0 iodide/interstitial phases, including Gd₃CI₃, Tb₇FeI₁₂, Y₆RuI₁₀, and Sc₇CI₁₂, indicating that reduced rare-earth iodide frameworks can be thermodynamically stable (TS ranks 1, 3–5). CS and SS reinforce that the closest Gd–I interstitial analogue is also on the hull (CS rank 1; SS rank 1). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to moisture/iodine loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 inherits the sealed Ta, high-temperature, anhydrous handling requirements of Gd₃CI₃, which is made at 1250 K for 20 h and quenched (CS rank 1; SS rank 1; TS rank 1). The severity is not extreme because the temperature is below or near 1000 °C and no high pressure is needed, consistent with most rare-earth iodide analogues (CS ranks 1–5; SS ranks 1, 3–5; TS ranks 1, 3–5). Deductions are mainly for controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile iodide chemistry, multistep handling, and air-sensitive precursors. Insufficient CS/SS/TS evidence exists for whether GdN specifically requires harsher conditions, so that part is treated neutrally.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic support is that Gd + GdI₃ + C forms a closely related Gd–I interstitial compound after only 20 h at 1250 K (CS rank 1; SS rank 1; TS rank 1). However, replacing C with N and using GdN likely lowers precursor activity because GdN is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. The local Gd–I bond-length overlap between target Gd–I distances and Gd₃CI₃ Gd–I distances supports structural transferability (CS rank 1; SS rank 1; TS rank 1). The main uncertainty is whether the NGd₄ tetrahedral motif forms directly or competes with binary GdN [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 uses the same sealed rare-earth iodide reaction logic as Gd₃CI₃ and other rare-earth iodide cluster phases, so controlled atmosphere, volatile iodide, multistep, and air-sensitive precursor penalties apply (CS rank 1; SS ranks 3–5; TS ranks 3–5). Its temperature can plausibly remain below 1000 °C because La/C/I and Sc/C/I analogues form near 800–900 °C (CS ranks 2–3; SS rank 5; TS rank 5). No high-pressure penalty is applied because CS/SS/TS analogues use sealed ampoules, not GPa pressure. The uncertainty is the reactivity and safety of the nitrogen donor, especially if azides or ammonia-derived sources are used [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The Gd metal + GdI₃ pair is supported by the closest Gd₃CI₃ analogue (CS rank 1; SS rank 1; TS rank 1). However, the nitrogen incorporation pathway is unsupported by CS/SS/TS; insufficient direct evidence from all three sources is scored neutral 50 for nitride-specific kinetics. Reactive nitrogen donors could improve activity, but they may introduce gas pressure or impurities [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is more demanding than Route 1 because all components must assemble into the ternary framework from separate metal/halide/nitrogen sources [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 remains a sealed iodide process, so atmosphere, volatility, and air-sensitivity deductions remain supported by the rare-earth iodide analogues (CS ranks 1–5; SS ranks 1–5; TS ranks 1–5). It may operate at somewhat lower temperatures because molten iodide flux or transport could assist mass transfer [Reasonable Inference]. The ThI₄ CVT analogue shows iodide vapor/transport chemistry at about 500–550 °C, but it is not chemically identical to Gd₂NI₃ (SS rank 2; TS rank 2). The lack of direct Gd–N–I flux evidence is the main reason this route is not rated easy.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux or self-flux iodide conditions could improve mass transport and crystal growth compared with dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The CVT/transport analogue ThI₄ supports the general feasibility of iodide transport in sealed tubes (SS rank 2; TS rank 2). However, La/C/I and Y/Ru/I analogues report competing phases and sensitivity to time/temperature, which lowers confidence in clean phase formation (CS ranks 2–3; SS rank 4; TS rank 4). The route receives the best kinetic score but also an expert penalty because its direct chemical evidence is weaker than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Gd, so cation radius mismatch does not apply [Reasonable Inference]. The reported structure contains a limited set of crystallographic sites and edge-sharing NGd₄ tetrahedra; no evidence is provided for a very large unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity in the target description. CS/SS/TS analogues include complex cluster phases, but that complexity is not directly transferable to this target, so those sources are neutral for F4. The amorphization risk is therefore low, though air/moisture sensitivity remains a separate handling issue [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Gd₂NI₃ is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple rare-earth iodide/interstitial analogues are also on the hull (TS ranks 1, 3–5). The CS/SS/TS evidence strongly supports sealed-tube rare-earth iodide chemistry, especially through the very close Gd₃CI₃ analogue made from Gd + GdI₃ + C in sealed Ta (CS rank 1; SS rank 1; TS rank 1). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of anhydrous GdI₃-based sealed Ta chemistry, which provides iodine activity and rare-earth halide transport (CS ranks 1–5; SS ranks 1, 3–5; TS ranks 1, 3–5). The most critical limiting factor is the absence of direct nitride analogue evidence: CS/SS/TS do not demonstrate formation of a Gd–N–I phase, so nitrogen incorporation and competition with binary GdN remain uncertain. Overall, the target is moderately promising but requires careful stoichiometry, dry handling, and likely exploratory optimization.
