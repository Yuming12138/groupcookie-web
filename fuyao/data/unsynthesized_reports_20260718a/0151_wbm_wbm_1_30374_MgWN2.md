### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
MgWN2 has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability score band. The strongest supporting evidence is MgMoN2, which is H-phase P6₃/mmc like the target and also has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS; TS). CS contains W nitrides such as MnWN2 and CrWN2 with higher hull energies around 0.148–0.149 eV/atom (CS; SS), so CS is supportive of W–N chemistry but less supportive thermodynamically than SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The MgMoN2 analogue demonstrates a moderate-temperature sealed-autoclave route at 700 °C for 10 h from Mg, Mo, and NaN₃ (SS; TS), so condition severity is moderate rather than extreme. The main penalties are NaN₃ toxicity/gas generation and Mg handling [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are mixed: identical structure to MgMoN2 strongly helps structural confidence (SS; TS), but W is more refractory than Mg and long-range W diffusion may be limiting [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides no direct Mg–W azide synthesis, so CS is a weaker/neutral source for this route-specific factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
MnWN2 synthesis by ammonolysis of MnWO4 at 800 °C in dry NH₃ supports the chemical feasibility of converting tungstates to W nitrides (CS; SS). The route is penalized for reactive NH₃, multiple precursor-processing steps, and reported phase-purity difficulty in MnWN2 (CS; SS). Kinetically, a fine MgWO4 precursor should improve mixing [Reasonable Inference], but oxide-to-nitride conversion requires substantial reconstruction and oxygen removal. SS/CS show this route can work for W nitrides, but the MnWN2 analogue has a different structure and impurity issues (CS; SS).

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
CrWN2 was made by a multi-step metal-organic precursor route followed by ammonolysis at 750 °C under NH₃ (SS), showing that homogeneous W-containing ternary nitride precursors can crystallize under accessible conditions. This route scores better kinetically than oxide ammonolysis because molecular mixing reduces cation diffusion length [Reasonable Inference]. It is still challenging because no direct Mg–W H-phase polymeric-precursor example is provided; insufficient evidence from CS and TS for this exact MgWN2 route gives a neutral signal. The likely risks are Mg loss, residual oxygen/carbon, and crystallization into competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
TS demonstrates that high-pressure nitrogen environments can stabilize or transform dense nitrides, as in TaN at 20–100 kbar and 800–960 °C (TS), while Li2Ta3N5 synthesis used 0.6 GPa NH₃ for 5 days (CS; SS; TS). This route has high condition severity because it may involve multi-GPa equipment, reactive gases, and encapsulation. Kinetically, high nitrogen chemical potential may help nitride formation [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS example directly shows high-pressure Mg–W nitride formation. Therefore, it is best viewed as a fallback route if ambient-pressure synthesis fails.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target structure has mixed face, edge, and corner sharing, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion from the target description and from the MgMoN2 analogue (SS; TS). The target is a simple ternary formula in an H-phase structure, and no evidence indicates a large >40-atom unit cell. Mg²⁺ and W⁴⁺ radii are not extremely mismatched under the charge-balanced Mg²⁺/W⁴⁺/N³⁻ assignment [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that mixed connectivity can increase the number of competing defective or amorphous intermediates during low-temperature ammonolysis.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MgWN2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the isostructural MgMoN2 analogue is also stable on the hull (SS; TS). The analogue support is unusually strong: MgMoN2 has the same H-phase P6₃/mmc structure, nearly identical Mg–N and group-6-metal–N bond lengths, and a demonstrated sealed-autoclave synthesis from Mg/Mo/NaN₃ (SS; TS). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of this direct MgMoN2 structural/thermodynamic/synthetic template (SS; TS). The main limiting factor is kinetic: W is refractory and may diffuse or nitride more slowly than Mo, so phase formation may require longer hold times or finer W powder [Reasonable Inference]. Secondary limitations include Mg volatility, NaN₃ handling hazards, and possible competing Mg3N2/WN/W phases [Reasonable Inference].
