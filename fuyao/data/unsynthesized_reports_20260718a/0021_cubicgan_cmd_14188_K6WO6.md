### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, which sits at the top of the most favorable rubric range and supports likely accessibility as either a ground-state-borderline or low-metastability phase. CS provides strong support because several chemically similar K/Rb/Na alkali oxides are reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and high-confidence syntheses. TS also supports tolerance of modest metastability because similar compounds with E_hull ≈ 0.035–0.088 eV/atom have reported syntheses. SS is useful for showing structurally similar materials can exist, but insufficient evidence from SS for exact K₆WO₆ thermodynamic stability; SS contribution is therefore less direct.

#### F2: Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C, high pressure, and exotic equipment, consistent with K/Rb gallate and cadmate solid-state syntheses at 400–750 °C in sealed ampoules or crucibles (SS). Deductions arise because K₂O/KO₂-type precursors and K-rich products are air/moisture-sensitive, as repeatedly noted for alkali oxide analogues in CS/SS/TS. The remaining uncertainty is whether dry solid-state diffusion can form the cubic K₆WO₆ phase rather than more common potassium tungstates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 1

Dry oxide solid-state kinetics are limited by refractory WO₃ and by the need to reorganize into the target cubic K₆WO₆ topology [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues frequently require multi-day to multi-week anneals, which supports a moderate-to-slow kinetic score. CS/SS provide evidence that K-rich oxide frameworks can form under these conditions, but no source confirms the exact WO₆ octahedral K₆WO₆ arrangement.

#### F2: Route 2

Route 2 uses high but not extreme temperatures, matching 1000 °C flux synthesis of K₂ZrO₃ and Na₅TaO₅ (CS). It still requires sealed or controlled dry vessels because K₂O-rich melts/fluxes are reactive and moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed Ni-bomb and dry N₂/Ar routes in CS. The severity is therefore moderate rather than severe.

#### F3: Route 2

Flux growth should improve precursor activity and mass transport relative to dry powders because K₂ZrO₃ was produced by dissolution in excess K₂O flux from KNO₃ (CS). The main kinetic limitation is not transport alone but phase selection: K₂WO₄ or other potassium tungstates may compete with K₆WO₆ unless K₂O activity is sufficiently high [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in CS flux examples justify retaining a moderate reconstruction/ordering penalty.

#### F2: Route 3

Route 3 benefits from lower temperatures because Na–W oxonitride analogues form at 350–750 °C in autoclaves (SS, TS). However, amide/autoclave chemistry requires reactive controlled media, sealed pressure vessels, moisture-sensitive reagents, and likely post-conversion steps, all supported by SS/TS descriptions. Thus the condition severity is worse than Routes 1–2 despite the lower temperature.

#### F3: Route 3

Autoclave amide chemistry gives high chemical activity, as WO₃ reacts with NaNH₂ to form Na₅WNO₄ and related phases (SS, TS). The limitation is structural and chemical transferability: the closest W analogues are oxonitrides with WNO₄ trigonal bipyramids, whereas K₆WO₆ requires pure oxide W-centered octahedra [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore kinetics may be favorable, but the pathway risks producing N-containing or off-stoichiometric products.

#### F4: Amorphization Tendency

The target has a very large cation size/charge mismatch between K⁺ and W⁶⁺ [Reasonable Inference], and the likely Fm-3m conventional cell contains more than 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference]. The target description includes corner- and edge-sharing OK₄W square pyramids but does not report face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. CS/SS/TS show many related alkali oxides require long anneals or flux growth, implying ordering challenges rather than outright amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₆WO₆ is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline-stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which is favorable but not unambiguously on the hull. CS provides strong chemical support from many stable K/Rb/Na-rich oxides, SS provides structural support from K-rich alkali oxide frameworks with similar alkali–oxygen environments, and TS shows that related moderately metastable compounds can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high K₂O activity, best achieved by K₂O-rich flux or sealed K₂O-containing reaction conditions. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition against more conventional potassium tungstates and loss/hydrolysis of potassium oxide species. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed-vessel flux chemistry and stringent dry handling.
