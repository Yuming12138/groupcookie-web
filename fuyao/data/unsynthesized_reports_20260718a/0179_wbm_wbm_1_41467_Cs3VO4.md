### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target Cs₃VO₄ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided target thermodynamic data. Chemically similar Cs oxides such as Cs₂ZrO₃, Cs₂MoO₄, Cs₂CrO₄, and Cs₃AlO₃ analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5). Structurally/thermodynamically similar Cs₂NaVO₄ and Cs₂LiVO₄ are also on the hull (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2), supporting high thermodynamic feasibility. Remaining uncertainty is kinetic and phase-selection related, not thermodynamic.

**F2/F3 — Route 1 sealed solid-state.**  
Route 1 inherits strong support from Cs₂NaVO₄ and Cs₂LiVO₄, which were made from CsO₀.₅-containing oxide mixtures in sealed Ni tubes under Ar at 750 °C (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The severity score is reduced by sealed inert atmosphere, volatile/air-sensitive Cs oxide precursors, and toxic/air-sensitive handling requirements; this is consistent with Cs oxide synthesis needing glovebox/sealed-tube methods (CS1, CS5). Kinetics are moderate because the analogues require long anneals of 25–59 d, but the presence of reactive Cs oxides and the same-structure K₃VO₄ analogue improve confidence (SS1, SS2, SS3).

**F2/F3 — Route 2 carbonate-derived route.**  
Route 2 is attractive because Cs₂CO₃ is easier to handle, and Cs₂CO₃ + oxide decarbonation routes are demonstrated for Cs molybdate synthesis (CS3), while K₂CO₃ + V₂O₅ routes are demonstrated for potassium vanadates (SS5, TS4). The condition score is lower than a simple carbonate route because final sealed/inert annealing is recommended to avoid Cs loss and because multiple decarbonation/regrinding/annealing steps are likely [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are fair but less direct than Route 1 because carbonate decomposition and possible intermediate condensed vanadates can complicate formation of the isolated VO₄³⁻ orthovanadate [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3 Cs-rich flux.**  
Route 3 is supported by Cs-rich alkali flux growth of high-alkali germanates at 500–650 °C (CS2) and by alkali-rich conditions used to obtain Cs₂NaVO₄/Cs₂LiVO₄ single crystals (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The condition severity is significant because Cs metal/CsO₂/Cs₂O fluxes require glovebox handling and sealed corrosion-resistant containers (CS1, CS2). The kinetic score is highest among credible bulk routes because liquid/flux-mediated transport should overcome slow diffusion and promote crystallization [Reasonable Inference], consistent with flux growth evidence (CS2).

**F2/F3 — Route 4 solution/evaporation.**  
Slow evaporation is mild and is demonstrated for Cs₂CrO₄ crystals from aqueous solution (CS4). However, insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports solution crystallization of anhydrous Cs₃VO₄; all close Cs–V–O structural/thermodynamic analogues use sealed solid-state routes (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The route is therefore scored as kinetically easy but chemically uncertain, with a high risk of hydrates, carbonates, or alternate vanadate speciation [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large size/charge contrast between Cs⁺ and V⁵⁺, reflected by long Cs–O bonds near 2.92–3.08 Å and short V–O bonds near 1.76 Å in the target and analogous VO₄ phases (SS1, SS2, SS3). The target description reports only corner-sharing polyhedra, unlike some analogues with edge sharing (SS1, SS2), so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not applied. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, so this check is treated as neutral/not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃VO₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is strongly supported by the same-structure K₃VO₄ analogue in tetragonal I-42m (SS3) and by closely related Cs–V–O orthovanadate analogues Cs₂NaVO₄ and Cs₂LiVO₄ synthesized under sealed solid-state conditions (SS1, SS2, TS1, TS2). The most important enabling factor is the stability of isolated VO₄ tetrahedra in alkali-rich environments, shown by V–O distances near 1.75–1.76 Å in Cs₂NaVO₄/Cs₂LiVO₄ and the target (SS1, SS2). The main limiting factor is Cs chemical-potential control: Cs oxide/metal precursors are reactive, air-sensitive, and likely volatile under high-temperature conditions [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed-tube/glovebox requirements in Cs-rich analogues (CS1, CS2, CS5). Overall, Cs-rich flux or sealed Cs oxide solid-state synthesis should be feasible but requires careful dry, sealed handling.
