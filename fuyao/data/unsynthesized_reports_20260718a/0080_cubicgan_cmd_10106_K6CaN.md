### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-score threshold specified in the rubric. TS data support syntheses of materials in the same metastability range: K3NiO2 at 0.035 eV/atom, CsMnAs at 0.0556 eV/atom, Cs2Se at 0.0751 eV/atom, and Rb2S at 0.0876 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). CS/SS evidence is contradictory: K3P is on-hull and conventionally synthesized (CS-1), but K3N is much higher in hull energy and requires LT-ABD (CS-3/SS-1). The score is therefore high but not perfect because the closest K–N analogue is metastable and difficult.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, consistent with K3P/K-rich pnictide synthesis at 150–700 °C and Rb2S at ~350 °C (CS-1, TS-5). The penalties come from sealed vacuum/Ar handling, volatile K, air-sensitive reagents/products, and possible pre-synthesis of K3N, all supported by CS-1, CS-3/SS-1, and TS-5. Insufficient evidence from TS specifically proves K–Ca–N compatibility in sealed-tube synthesis, so the route remains challenging.

#### F3 — Route 1

K metal and possibly molten alkali flux should provide high chemical activity and fast transport, consistent with excess alkali-metal syntheses in CS-1 and low-temperature alkali chalcogenide formation in TS-5. K3N offers a close K–N local motif because its K–N distance is 2.73 Å and it contains NK6 octahedra, closely matching the target K–N distance of 2.75 Å and NK6 coordination (CS-3/SS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is reconstructing from a hexagonal/ribbon K3N-like motif to cubic F-43m K6CaN, for which direct evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

The deposition route avoids high temperature and high pressure, but it requires UHV, cryogenic deposition, activated nitrogen, and specialized equipment exactly as reported for K3N LT-ABD (CS-3/SS-1). K volatility and air/moisture sensitivity are major penalties, also directly indicated for K/N samples in CS-3/SS-1. Insufficient evidence from TS contributes no useful signal for vapor deposition severity, so TS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2

The closest K–N analogue was made by LT-ABD, so vapor delivery of K and activated N is feasible (CS-3/SS-1). However, the analogue is metastable, decomposes below 263 K, and has poor crystallinity according to the provided data, which lowers the crystallization score (CS-3/SS-1). Ca incorporation and the exact K6:Ca:N stoichiometry are extrapolated [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS data demonstrate a wide composition window.

#### F2 — Route 3

The ammonothermal route is based on TS-1, where Cs2Se required 100–300 °C but 300–2000 bar NH3, specialized autoclaves, Ar-box loading, and vacuum post-treatment. These conditions create penalties for controlled atmosphere, sealed/volatile pressure medium, multi-step operation, and air-sensitive alkali handling (TS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS supports ammonothermal K–Ca–N synthesis specifically, so this route is more speculative.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solvothermal transport can improve kinetics, and TS-1 demonstrates that high-pressure NH3 can produce alkali-rich phases and new modifications. However, the thermodynamic analogue is a Cs–Se compound, not a nitride or Ca-containing phase, so structural reconstruction and chemistry transfer are weak. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes whether K6CaN is soluble, transportable, or nucleates under NH3.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The principal amorphization/order risk is the large K/Ca cation size and charge mismatch [Reasonable Inference], which can frustrate long-range cation ordering. The target description does not indicate a large >40-atom unit cell, and the stated connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing. Therefore, amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K6CaN is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline stable phase rather than a deeply unstable compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values are experimentally reported (TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from K-rich solid-state chemistry and the close K–N local geometry in K3N, whose NK6 octahedra and K–N distance closely match the target (CS-3/SS-1). The most important enabling factor is the probable high mobility/reactivity of molten or excess K under sealed conditions, consistent with alkali-rich direct syntheses (CS-1, TS-5). The most important limiting factor is maintaining K-rich stoichiometry while incorporating Ca and N without forming K3N, Ca3N2, metallic Ca/K, or decomposed air-sensitive products [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require stringent containment, oxygen/moisture exclusion, and careful temperature control.
