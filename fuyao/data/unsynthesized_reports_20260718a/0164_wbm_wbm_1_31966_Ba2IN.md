### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV atom⁻¹, placing it on the convex hull and giving a high thermodynamic score. Thermodynamic analogues Ba₂NBr, Ba₂NCl, and Sr₂NI are also E_hull = 0.0 eV atom⁻¹, so TS strongly supports stability of the alkaline-earth nitride halide Caswellsilverite family (TS1–TS3). CS is favorable but less direct because Ba₂PCl and CsLnS₂ analogues are chemically adjacent but not Ba–N–I systems; CS therefore supports general Caswellsilverite stability but is weaker than SS/TS for this target (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly DFT/temperature-related, because finite-temperature halide volatility and entropy are not captured directly by E_hull [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 725–850 °C, 5-day, Ar-sealed solid-state conditions, which are demanding but not extreme compared with high-pressure or >1000 °C syntheses (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The main penalties come from sealed halide handling, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive Ba₂N/BaI₂ handling (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). CS gives insufficient direct route evidence for Ba₂N + BaI₂, neutral 50, because CS1 involves Ba/red P/BaX₂ rather than nitride-halide synthesis (CS1). The resulting F2 = 70 reflects moderate-to-challenging but standard solid-state chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Ba₂N + BaI₂ should have reasonable kinetics because Ba₂NBr/Ba₂NCl are formed from Ba₂N + BaX₂, and Sr₂NI is formed from Sr₂N + SrI₂-type chemistry (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The same Caswellsilverite structure type minimizes reconstructive barriers relative to unrelated structure formation (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The 5-day reaction time and slow cooling indicate ordering/diffusion is not instantaneous, so the structural-reconstruction component is penalized (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Remaining uncertainty is whether the larger iodide in Ba₂NI slows ordering or promotes BaI₂-rich secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 retains the same air-free sealed solid-state environment as Route 1, so condition severity is similar (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Ba₃N₂ is not directly reported in the provided nitride-halide syntheses, so its use is a [Reasonable Inference] and not directly supported by CS/SS/TS as a preferred precursor. The route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures by extrapolating from 725–850 °C analogue syntheses (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The score remains 70 because practical handling and sealing burdens are comparable to Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Kinetics are less certain than Route 1 because the demonstrated precursor is Ba₂N/Sr₂N rather than Ba₃N₂/Sr₃N₂ (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Ba₃N₂ may require more structural rearrangement to form the layered Caswellsilverite Ba₂NI topology, so the precursor activity and structural reconstruction scores are reduced [Reasonable Inference]. Diffusion remains favorable relative to refractory multi-cation oxides because only Ba cations are involved and analogue syntheses occur below 900 °C (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The final F3 = 70 reflects plausible but less validated kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses sealed Nb/Ta ampoule or vacuum methods analogous to Ba₃I₂Si flux-like synthesis, so it receives deductions for controlled/vacuum atmosphere and sealed volatile halide containment (SS4, TS4). It also uses air-sensitive Ba metal and Ba nitrides, requiring glovebox handling (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). Although the temperature remains below 1000 °C, flux loading, slow cooling, and flux separation add procedural complexity [Reasonable Inference]. F2 = 55 captures a more demanding experimental setup than the dry solid-state route.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A BaI₂-rich flux should improve ion mobility and crystal growth, supported indirectly by Ba₃I₂Si formation at 1170 K for only 8 h in sealed Nb ampoules (SS4, TS4). Compared with the 5-day dry Ba₂NX/Sr₂NX reactions, the flux route likely lowers diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference from SS1–SS4/TS1–TS4]. The uncertainty is nitride incorporation into a halide-rich melt and possible off-stoichiometry, since no exact Ba₂NI flux synthesis is reported (SS4, TS4). Thus F3 = 80 is kinetically favorable but experimentally less certain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple single-cation Ba nitride iodide, and structurally similar Ba₂NBr/Ba₂NCl/Sr₂NI are ordered crystalline Caswellsilverite phases, not amorphous products (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The target has no mixed-cation radius mismatch, no evidence for a large >40-atom cell, and no face-sharing/edge-sharing/vertex-sharing coexistence trigger in the target description; analogous structures show simple octahedral networks (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₂NI is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV atom⁻¹ and the closest TS analogues Ba₂NBr, Ba₂NCl, and Sr₂NI are also on the hull (TS1–TS3). The synthesis is strongly supported by SS/TS because Ba₂NBr and Ba₂NCl share the same Ba₂NX Caswellsilverite structure and Sr₂NI provides an exact iodide nitride analogue with the same structure type (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The most important enabling factor is the direct structural and chemical continuity across Ba₂NCl/Ba₂NBr/Sr₂NI to Ba₂NI, making a Ba₂N + BaI₂ route highly plausible (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The main limiting factor is experimental handling: Ba nitrides and anhydrous barium iodide are moisture-sensitive, and the synthesis likely requires welded sealed crucibles, Mo foil, and slow cooling (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Overall, the target is highly synthesizable for an air-free solid-state laboratory but not trivial for routine open-atmosphere ceramic synthesis.
