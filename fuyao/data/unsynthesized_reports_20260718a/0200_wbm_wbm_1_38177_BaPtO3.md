### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target BaPtO₃ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the provided rubric. Several similar Pt-containing or Ba-based analogues are also on or near the hull, including Ca₂Pt₃O₈ at E_hull = 0 (SS-1, TS-1), Ba₆Ru₂PtCl₂O₁₂ at E_hull = 0 (SS-2, TS-2), and Ba₂PrPtO₆ at E_hull = 0 (TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or the correct P6₃mc polytype.

**F2/F3 — Route 1: solid-state.**  
Solid-state synthesis is supported by BaPbO₃ from BaCO₃/PbO under O₂ flow at 800–950 °C with repeated grinding/sintering (CS-4, CS-5) and Ba₂PrPtO₆ from BaCO₃/PtO₂·nH₂O at ~980 °C (TS-5). The condition severity is moderate because it uses ordinary furnaces and ambient pressure, but repeated grinding/annealing is likely needed, as in Ba₃MIr₂O₉ analogues (SS-4, SS-5, TS-3, TS-4). Kinetics are weaker because constructing a face-sharing hexagonal PtO₆ network from carbonate/oxide powders is a substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2: BaCl₂ flux.**  
Flux growth has strong support from BaIrO₃ and Ba–Ru–Pt analogues at ~1000 °C using BaCl₂ (CS-1, CS-3, SS-2, TS-2). Its severity is moderate: it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, but it requires toxic BaCl₂, long holds, flux washing, and careful flux management (SS-2, TS-2). Kinetics are better than solid-state because flux transport improves cation mobility [Reasonable Inference], and the Ba–Ru–Pt analogue already contains PtO₆ octahedra with Pt–O = 2.04 Å (SS-2, TS-2).

**F2/F3 — Route 3: high pressure.**  
High-pressure synthesis is directly supported for dense Ba transition-metal oxide polytypes: BaIrO₃ at 5.0 GPa and 1000 °C (CS-2), and BaRhO₃ at 60–65 kbar and 1175 °C (SS-3). The severity score is low because multi-GPa equipment and quenching are required. The kinetics/structure score is reasonably strong because the target’s face-sharing hexagonal PtO₆ motif is conceptually close to high-pressure-stabilized hexagonal/perovskite polytypes (CS-2, SS-3), but transferability from Ir/Rh to Pt is [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4: hydrothermal.**  
Hydrothermal synthesis is supported for Ca₂Pt₃O₈ from CaPt(OH)₆ at 800 K and 170 MPa (SS-1, TS-1). Condition severity is acceptable relative to multi-GPa synthesis, but autoclave/tube handling and corrosive post-treatment are nontrivial (SS-1, TS-1). The main uncertainty is structural: Ca₂Pt₃O₈ is not a BaPtO₃ perovskite, and the analogue reports Pt impurities and low crystallinity, so the route is exploratory rather than preferred (SS-1, TS-1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**
