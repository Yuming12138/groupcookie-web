### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the provided target data, so it scores near the maximum under the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues reinforce this: Cs2NaVO4, Rb2LiVO4, Cs2LiVO4, K4V2O7, and K2LiVO4 all have E_hull = 0.0 in TS (TS1–TS5), while the exact structural/formula analogue K3VO4 is only 0.0215 eV/atom above hull (SS2). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or resistance to hydration/carbonation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 650–750 °C, directly supported by Rb2LiVO4 at 750 °C for 25 d and Cs2NaVO4 at 750 °C for 59 d (SS3, TS2; SS1, TS1). The main penalties are sealed containment and air-sensitive Rb oxide precursors, consistent with Ni-tube/Ar synthesis of Rb-containing vanadates (SS3, TS2) and moisture-sensitive Rb2TiO3 handling (CS1). Insufficient evidence from CS for exact Rb3VO4 conditions, so CS is neutral except for Rb oxide handling and moisture sensitivity.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is that K3VO4 already adopts the same I-42m structure type as target Rb3VO4 (SS2), reducing structural reconstruction. However, the long durations in Cs2NaVO4 and Rb2LiVO4 syntheses, 59 d and 25 d respectively, indicate slow diffusion/crystal-growth kinetics in sealed alkali oxide media (SS1, SS3, TS1–TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS for alkali vanadate kinetics specifically, so CS is neutral for this subfactor except as general Rb oxide solid-state precedent (CS1).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 can operate at lower temperatures, because A-rich alkali melts form products at 500–800 °C and high-alkali Rb/Cs phases at 500–650 °C (CS3). The penalties are from reactive air-sensitive alkali-rich media, flux removal, and sealed/inert handling, all consistent with CS3 and the sealed Rb2LiVO4 analogue (SS3, TS2). Insufficient evidence from TS for flux removal details, so TS is neutral for those operational steps.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux/self-flux conditions should improve ion transport compared with dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], supported by K2ZrO3 growth from excess K2O flux (CS2) and A-rich melt synthesis of high-alkali oxometallates (CS3). The target’s isolated VO4 units are compatible with alkali vanadate chemistry found in K3VO4 and Rb2LiVO4 (SS2, SS3, TS2). The uncertainty is selectivity: CS3 explicitly emphasizes stoichiometry/oxygen-content control in A-rich melts, and multiple alkali vanadate phases can plausibly compete [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Carbonate/oxide ceramic synthesis uses accessible precursors and ambient-pressure equipment, supported by K2CO3/V2O5 synthesis of K4V2O7 and alkali carbonate/oxide ceramic preparation of molybdates (SS5, TS4; CS4). The severity increases if vacuum annealing is required, as in K4V2O7 (SS5, TS4), and if sealed conditions are needed to retain Rb [Reasonable Inference] based on sealed alkali vanadate syntheses (SS2, SS3). Insufficient evidence from TS for carbonate synthesis of Rb3VO4 specifically, so TS is neutral beyond the K4V2O7 precedent.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Carbonates can be more reactive than dense oxides because decomposition produces fine alkali oxide/carbonate intermediates [Reasonable Inference], and K4V2O7 formation from K2CO3/V2O5 supports this pathway (SS5, TS4). However, full conversion to Rb3VO4 may be slowed by CO2 removal, Rb loss, and phase competition [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS4 noting purity difficulties for simple ceramic alkali molybdate preparations and SS5/TS4 reporting impurities in K4V2O7. Insufficient evidence from CS for rubidium vanadate carbonate synthesis specifically, so CS is only a moderate indirect signal.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very dissimilar cations, Rb+ and V5+, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is simple and corner-sharing, with target description reporting RbO4/VO4 corner-sharing only, while the exact K3VO4 analogue also has a simple corner-sharing I-42m framework (SS2). The remaining uncertainty is that amorphization is less relevant for long high-temperature bulk routes than for rapid quench or thin-film routes [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3VO4 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many alkali vanadate analogues in TS are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strong because K3VO4 has the exact same I-42m structure type (SS2), and Rb-containing vanadates such as Rb2LiVO4 are experimentally synthesized from Rb oxide/V2O5 mixtures in sealed Ni tubes (SS3, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the use of dry, sealed, alkali-rich oxide conditions, which maintain Rb stoichiometry and promote VO4-based phase formation (SS2, SS3, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is slow kinetics and air/moisture sensitivity, evidenced by long heating times of 25–59 d and glovebox/sealed-tube requirements in close analogues (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS2). Overall, the target is quite synthesizable, but likely not by casual open-air ceramic firing without careful Rb and moisture control [Reasonable Inference].
