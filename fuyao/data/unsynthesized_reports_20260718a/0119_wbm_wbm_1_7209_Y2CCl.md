### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest stability range. The closest analogue Gd2CCl is also reported with E_hull = 0.0 and the same Caswellsilverite R-3m structure (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 requires high temperature near 1050–1150 °C, sealed Ta ampoules, inert handling, and long reaction times, all consistent with Gd2CCl and rare-earth carbon chloride analogues (CS4 / SS1 / TS1; CS1, CS3, CS5). No high pressure or exotic reactive gas is required, which moderates severity (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Y2CCl requires a slightly different temperature than Gd2CCl due to smaller Y size [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The kinetics are limited by dense rare-earth metal and graphite, and related reactions require days to weeks, indicating slow diffusion/order formation (CS4 / SS1 / TS1; CS1, CS5). However, the exact Gd2CCl structure is accessible by the same type of reaction, so structural reconstruction is favorable (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). The uncertainty is whether YCl3-mediated transport is sufficient to overcome Y/graphite sluggishness [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses the same sealed Ta/inert rare-earth chloride chemistry as Ln–C–Cl analogues and therefore inherits air/moisture sensitivity and sealed-system requirements (CS1, CS3, CS5; TS3, TS5). It may operate at similar or slightly lower temperature if halide-rich transport is effective [Reasonable Inference], but there is insufficient direct SS/TS evidence for a Y2CCl flux route. The route remains challenging because excess halide and flux handling add processing steps [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
A halide-rich environment could improve diffusion and crystal growth relative to a purely stoichiometric powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], and many related Ln–C–Cl phases form in chloride-containing sealed systems (CS1, CS3, CS5). The only explicit Y flux example is Y3AlC3 in Li flux, which supports Y–C crystallization but provides insufficient evidence for Cl-containing Y2CCl (CS2; insufficient evidence from SS/TS). The main uncertainty is whether flux improves Y2CCl formation or instead stabilizes competing chloride-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Y2CCl has a simple single-cation Caswellsilverite structure, and the exact Gd2CCl analogue shows an ordered crystalline R-3m phase rather than a glassy/complex framework (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). The target has edge/corner-sharing octahedra but not the simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing criterion for high amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is low because the analogous structure type is experimentally realized (CS4 / SS1 / TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y2CCl is likely a **ground-state or very near-ground-state phase** because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest Gd2CCl analogue is also on-hull (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). The CS/SS/TS evidence is unusually strong because Gd2CCl has the same formula type, same Caswellsilverite R-3m structure, and an established sealed-Ta synthesis route (CS4 / SS1 / TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the proven accessibility of rare-earth carbon chlorides from **Ln metal + LnCl3/LnX3 + graphite** in sealed Ta ampoules (CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase-pure stoichiometry control, since related Ln–C–Cl systems show multiple competing phases, low yields, moisture sensitivity, and long annealing requirements (CS1, CS5; SS4; TS5). Overall, the target is substantially synthesizeable but requires rigorous air-free, high-temperature solid-state practice.
