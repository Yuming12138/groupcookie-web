### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range by the required rubric. Similar stable or near-stable materials include CuRh₂S₄ with E_hull = 0.0 (SS2, TS1), RuP₄ with E_hull = 0.0–0.016 eV/atom (CS2, CS3), and Rh₂S₃ with E_hull ≈ 0.001 eV/atom (SS4). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or avoidance of sulfur-rich competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube direct solid-state sulfurization

The severity score is moderately favorable because CuRh₂S₄ and Rh₂S₃ were synthesized in evacuated sealed quartz tubes at 800–900 °C without high pressure (SS2, SS4, TS1). The main penalties are vacuum/sealed-tube handling and sulfur volatility [Reasonable Inference from sealed chalcogenide reactions in SS2/SS4/TS1]. Kinetics are less favorable because Rh is refractory and diffusion from elemental Rh is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], although successful Rh sulfide analogues at 800–900 °C support feasibility (SS2, SS4, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Rh–S sealed-tube kinetics, so CS is treated as neutral for this specific route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: sulfur-rich flux growth

Flux synthesis is supported by chemically similar RuP₄, where RuP₄ crystals were obtained using Sn flux and slow cooling (CS2, CS3), and by chalcogen-rich AuCuSe₄ flux growth using K₂Se/Se (TS4). Severity is penalized for sealed vacuum, multi-step processing, flux removal, and possible air-sensitive alkali polysulfide handling [Reasonable Inference from TS4]. Kinetically, the flux should improve Rh transport and reduce the impact of refractory Rh [Reasonable Inference], and RuP₄ provides a close M:X = 1:4 analogue with metal octahedra and P–P bonding (CS2, CS3). Insufficient evidence from SS for Sn/polysulfide flux specifically in RhS₄, so SS contributes mainly structural transferability rather than route validation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure sulfurization

High-pressure chalcogenide analogues include pyrite-like CuS₂ and NiSe₂-related synthesis under GPa/kbar conditions (SS1, SS3, TS2, TS3), and high-pressure As₂Se₃ transformations at 3–7 GPa (SS5). This route is penalized heavily for high pressure, encapsulation, multi-step handling, and volatile sulfur containment [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics can be acceptable for dense octahedral chalcogenide networks because high pressure can promote compact phases [Reasonable Inference from SS1/SS3/SS5], but no direct RhS₄ high-pressure synthesis is provided. CS gives insufficient evidence for high-pressure synthesis and is scored neutral for that source signal.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: iodine-assisted vapor transport

The route has weak but plausible support from RuP₄/OsP₄ preparation in the presence of iodine (CS3), while direct evidence for RhS₄ chemical vapor transport is insufficient from SS and TS. Severity is moderate-to-high because iodine and sulfur are volatile and the method requires evacuated gradient ampoules [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, vapor transport could bypass slow Rh bulk diffusion by using volatile transport species [Reasonable Inference], but composition control for sulfur-rich RhS₄ may be difficult. Because no source reports a directly analogous Rh–S CVT route, uncertainty remains high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a single cation, Rh, so cation radius mismatch is not expected [Reasonable Inference], and no mixed-cation disorder is suggested by CS/SS/TS. The target structure is described as edge-sharing RhS₆ octahedra with two inequivalent S sites and S–S bonds, not as a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network. SS4 shows that Rh₂S₃ can have more complex mixed polyhedral sharing, but that feature is not reported for the target. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given in CS/SS/TS, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2; it is treated as non-triggered based on the simple RhS₄ formula and site count [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RhS₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related Rh/Ru/chalcogenide or pnictide analogues are also stable or near-stable (CS2, CS3, SS2, SS4, TS1). The strongest synthesis support comes from Rh–S octahedral sulfides such as CuRh₂S₄ and Rh₂S₃, which form by conventional sealed-tube methods (SS2, SS4, TS1), and from RuP₄ flux growth, which closely matches the 1:4 stoichiometry and X–X bonding motif (CS2, CS3). The most important enabling factor is the compatibility of Rh with octahedral sulfide coordination, demonstrated by RhS₆ units in CuRh₂S₄ and Rh₂S₃ (SS2, SS4, TS1). The most important limiting factor is maintaining sufficiently high sulfur chemical potential without forming lower sulfides such as Rh₂S₃ or losing sulfur to the vapor phase [Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth is therefore recommended because it combines high chalcogen activity with improved mass transport, as supported by RuP₄ and AuCuSe₄ flux analogues (CS2, CS3, TS4).

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sulfur volatility and pressure buildup in sealed ampoules. | High | Use evacuated sealed tubes as in CuRh₂S₄ and Rh₂S₃ syntheses (SS2, SS4, TS1); use staged low-temperature prereaction before high-T hold [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | All | Competing lower Rh sulfides such as Rh₂S₃ may form instead of sulfur-rich RhS₄. | High | Maintain sulfur-rich conditions; Rh₂S₃ forms from Rh + S at 900 °C (SS4), so excess sulfur/flux buffering is recommended [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Slow reaction kinetics of refractory Rh metal. | Medium–High | Use fine Rh powder, pelletizing, intermediate grinding, and long holds; elemental Rh sulfides formed after 48 h to ≥1 d in analogues (SS2, SS4, TS1). |
