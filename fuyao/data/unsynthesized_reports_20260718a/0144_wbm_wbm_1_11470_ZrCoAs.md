### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the calculated convex hull and supporting ground-state stability. CS/SS/TS analogues also include many hull-stable compounds, including ZrPdAs, ZrCuGe, HfCoGe, TiRuAs, HfRuAs, and Th(CoAs)₂ (CS1/CS2; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the specific Pnma polymorph will be the experimentally accessed polymorph, especially in arsenides with possible competing binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because the closest arsenide analogue ZrPdAs requires 800–1100 °C treatment, Ar atmosphere, sealed quartz ampoules, grinding, and long annealing (CS1; SS3; TS3). The volatile/toxic As component makes sealed handling necessary [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed ampoule use in CS1/SS3/TS3]. No high-pressure requirement is indicated in CS/SS/TS, which keeps the severity from becoming high.

#### F3 — Route 1

Solid-state kinetics are limited by refractory Zr and Co metal diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS1/SS3/TS3 explicitly report inhomogeneous products requiring grinding and 50–150 h tempering. However, close Pnma analogues ZrCuGe and HfCoGe reportedly form easily near 1000 °C from stoichiometric powders (CS2; SS2; TS2), which supports feasible ordering. The main uncertainty is whether Co–As binary formation competes strongly with ternary ZrCoAs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc/induction melting is more severe because CS4/CS5 use high-temperature melting equipment under gettered Ar, vacuum, or dynamic Ar. For an arsenide target, As volatility during melting is a major additional penalty [Reasonable Inference], even though CS5 shows a mitigation strategy by using preformed ZrSb₂ for antimonide synthesis. No high pressure is required, but the atmosphere and volatility constraints make this route operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 2

Melt processing improves homogenization and diffusion relative to dry powder sintering [Reasonable Inference], and Pnma ZrPdGe and ZrNiSb analogues are accessible by arc/induction or arc-melting plus annealing (CS4; CS5). Annealing after melting is still needed for crystallinity/ordering in these analogues (CS4; CS5), so kinetics are good but not trivial. The largest uncertainty is whether stoichiometric As can be retained during melt processing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth avoids the highest temperatures because TS5 reports 850–950 °C sealed ampoule/flux conditions for related Co–As compounds, and SS1/TS1 show flux growth in evacuated quartz capsules. It still requires vacuum-sealed ampoules, volatile/toxic pnictogen handling, and multi-step flux removal (SS1; TS1; TS5). Thus its condition severity is moderate, not easy.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux should enhance mass transport and crystal growth for refractory Zr-containing intermetallics [Reasonable Inference], and related pnictide crystals are obtained from fluxes or tin melts (SS1; SS4; TS1; TS4; TS5). However, the closest Pnma Zr/Hf–Co/Ge structural analogues are reported by sintering rather than flux growth (CS2; SS2; TS2), so the pathway to the exact Pnma ZrCoAs phase is less directly validated. Flux incorporation or stabilization of competing Co–As phases remains an uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic arsenide with simple 1:1:1 stoichiometry and Pnma symmetry, and close Pnma analogues crystallize readily according to CS2/SS2/TS2. Only one amorphization risk is triggered: the large Zr–Co size contrast [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide explicit mixed polyhedral connectivity, and CS/SS/TS do not give sufficient target-specific connectivity evidence, so C3 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrCoAs is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable. The synthesis evidence is strong at the family level: ZrPdAs arsenide solid-state synthesis is reported (CS1; SS3; TS3), and Pnma 1:1:1 ZrCuGe/HfCoGe analogues form near 1000 °C (CS2; SS2; TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the robust high-coordinate 1:1:1 intermetallic framework that tolerates Zr/Hf and late-transition-metal substitution [Reasonable Inference from CS2/SS2/TS2]. The most critical limiting factor is arsenic volatility/toxicity and consequent stoichiometry control during high-temperature synthesis [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed ampoule use in CS1/SS3/TS3 and TS5]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with moderate practical difficulty.
