### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. TS data support stable related alkaline-earth N-rich compounds: BaN₂ and SrN₂ have E_hull = 0 (TS1, TS2), and Sr₃Co(CN)₃ also has E_hull = 0 (TS3). CS evidence is mixed because some chemically similar azide/amidometallate entries have high calculated E_hull, such as KSnN₃ and RbYN₄ (CS1, CS2), while Ca₂PN₃ and CaN₂ are hull-stable (CS4, CS5). SS evidence is less thermodynamically supportive because many structurally similar complex azides are above hull by ~0.27–0.53 eV/atom (SS1–SS4), so insufficient evidence from SS for strong ground-state thermodynamic support; SS is treated as neutral-to-cautionary for F1.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Solid-state nitridation

The solid-state route has moderate severity because it avoids GPa pressure but requires air-sensitive nitrides and long high-temperature treatment; this is supported by Ca₂PN₃ requiring 800 °C/14 d under N₂ (CS4) and Sr₃[Co(CN)₃] requiring ~1200 K under Ar with sensitive precursors (TS3). Kinetics are limited by reconstructing a ternary Sr–Co–N framework from binary nitrides/metals, and long sintering in CS4 suggests sluggish ordering. TS3 supports Sr–Co-containing nitrogen chemistry, but the cyanide ligand differs from the target nitride/azide-like N framework, so transferability is incomplete. SS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-temperature Sr–Co nitride solid-state formation and is scored neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure azide decomposition

This route is severe because SrN₂ synthesis uses 9 GPa and 550 °C from Sr(N₃)₂ (TS2), and the alkaline-earth diazenide family requires 3–12 GPa with specialized multianvil equipment (CS5, TS1). Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state nitridation because azide decomposition gives reactive nitrogen and short synthesis times of 15–20 min in CS5/TS1/TS2. The key uncertainty is Co incorporation: TS1/TS2/CS5 demonstrate alkaline-earth diazenides, but not ternary Sr–Co–N frameworks. SS evidence supports azide-like N–N motifs but not high-pressure Co incorporation, so SS is neutral-to-supportive only for the N–N bonding motif.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Ammonothermal / supercritical NH₃

Condition severity is moderate-to-high because CS2 requires 6 kbar supercritical NH₃ for 22 d, and CS1 requires liquid NH₃/inert handling. Kinetics are comparatively favorable because liquid/supercritical NH₃ provides solvated, reactive nitrogen species and cation transport, reducing diffusion limitations relative to solid-state synthesis; this is supported by CS1 and CS2. The uncertainty is whether an anhydrous SrCoN₃ phase, rather than amide or ammoniate products, will crystallize; CS2 explicitly notes strong molar-ratio dependence and low yield for some products. TS provides indirect support via stable Sr–N and Sr–Co–N/C thermodynamics (TS2, TS3), while SS provides insufficient direct evidence for ammonothermal synthesis and is scored neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Azide metathesis / crystallization

This route is mild in temperature and pressure because CS3 and SS1–SS4 show room-temperature crystallization or precipitation of azide-containing compounds. Kinetics of initial crystallization should be excellent, but conversion to an anhydrous SrCoN₃ phase is the weak point because the references produce complex azides/azide halides, not a direct Sr–Co nitride phase. The target’s N–N distance of 1.18 Å is strongly consistent with azide-like N–N distances in CS3 and SS1–SS4, supporting the use of azide precursors. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for low-temperature azide metathesis to SrCoN₃ and is treated as neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a large electropositive Sr site with a much smaller transition-metal Co site, reflected directly in long Sr–N bonds of 2.58–3.06 Å and short Co–N bonds of 1.70–1.81 Å in the target structure. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing NSr₃Co₂ trigonal bipyramids, which increases topological complexity and raises the risk of disorder or amorphous products. CS4 shows that mixed edge/corner-sharing nitride frameworks can be synthesized, but only after long solid-state annealing. Exact target unit-cell atom count is not provided, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2, so that check is treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCoN₃ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and TS analogues include several hull-stable Sr/alkaline-earth nitrogen-rich compounds (TS1–TS3). The strongest structural support comes from azide/diazenide-like N–N distances in CS3, CS5, TS1, TS2, and SS1–SS4, which match the target N–N distance of 1.18 Å. The most plausible enabling factor is maintaining high nitrogen chemical potential while allowing Sr and Co to assemble into an ordered framework, best addressed by ammonothermal NH₃ chemistry or controlled azide decomposition [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Sr–N, Co–N, azide, amide, cyanide-like, and elemental Co side phases are all plausible competitors, as suggested by phase-control issues in CS2, TS3, and TS4. Overall, the target is moderately synthesizable but likely requires specialized inert, N-rich synthesis rather than ordinary oxide-style ceramic processing.
