### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom from the supplied target data, which warrants the maximum thermodynamic score. Multiple related phosphides in TS are also on the hull, including HfMoP and TiCrP, supporting that this family contains stable ternary phosphides rather than only metastable phases (TS-1, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee accessible kinetics or phase purity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state sintering  
The route uses moderate nominal temperature, 1070 K, but requires vacuum sealing, volatile P management, multi-step processing, and extremely long homogenization, all supported by HfMoP and related phosphide syntheses (SS-1, TS-1; SS-3, TS-3). Kinetics are the main weakness because Mo is refractory [Reasonable Inference] and the closest analogue required 1000 h at 1070 K, indicating slow diffusion (SS-1, TS-1). The strong structural match to HfMoP improves the structural-reconstruction component (SS-1, TS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: arc melting plus evacuated anneal  
The arc-melt route is directly supported by TiCrP synthesis from Ti, Cr, and red P powders under Ar, followed by evacuated-tube annealing at 1070 K for 800 h and quenching (CS-1, TS-5). Its severity score is reduced because arc melting implies very high local temperature and because P volatility requires careful containment [Reasonable Inference], with related phosphide syntheses explicitly using excess P to compensate mass loss (CS-4). Kinetically, arc melting improves initial mixing [Reasonable Inference], but long annealing in TiCrP shows ordering/homogenization remains slow (CS-1, TS-5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Cu/P flux growth  
Flux growth is supported by a related ternary phosphide route using a master alloy, P, and Cu in an evacuated quartz capsule, with recovery of crystallites after CuP flux dissolution (CS-5). The flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to direct solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference], giving the highest F3 among the routes. The main uncertainty is that CS-5 is not a direct TiMoP example, and the required temperature/time/composition window is not fully specified in CS-5.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency  
The P-62m ternary phosphide analogues are compact, crystalline intermetallic-type structures with small cells, not large disordered framework materials (SS-1, TS-1; TS-5). No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates glass formation, mixed polyhedral amorphization risk, or very large cell complexity. The remaining uncertainty is that high-coordinate metallic descriptions do not map cleanly onto oxide-style polyhedral amorphization criteria, so the low-risk score is partly [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiMoP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close analogues such as HfMoP and TiCrP are also reported at E_hull = 0 in the provided TS set (TS-1, TS-5). The synthesis case is strongly supported structurally by HfMoP, which has the same P-62m topology and Mo/P framework, and chemically by TiCrP/TiRuP-type Ti–transition-metal phosphides (SS-1, TS-1; TS-5; CS-5). The most important enabling factor is the existence of multiple P-62m ternary phosphide analogues accessible by conventional sealed-tube, arc-melt, or flux methods (SS-1, CS-1, CS-5). The most important limiting factor is slow homogenization and P volatility, evidenced by 800–1000 h anneals, sealed ampoules, slow heating, and P-loss compensation in related syntheses (SS-1, TS-1; CS-1, CS-4; SS-3, TS-3). Overall, the material appears synthesizable but likely requires careful sealed-system phosphide practice and prolonged thermal treatment.
