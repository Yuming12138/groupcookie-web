### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target NaMgBi has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it on the convex hull and strongly favoring synthesizability [Target]. The closest analogues KMgBi, NaMgSb, Na₂MgSn, Na₃Bi, and multiple chemically related compounds are also reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom [CS#1–CS#5, SS#1–SS#4, TS#1–TS#4]. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of low-temperature competing polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is moderately severe because exact/near analogues use sealed Ar ampoules and temperatures around 1150 K to 1150 °C, but no high pressure is required [CS#3, SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3]. The largest penalties come from Na/Mg volatility and air sensitivity, supported by sealed-tube/glovebox precedent in Na–Mg analogues [SS#2, SS#3, TS#2, TS#3]. CS/SS/TS do not indicate a need for H₂, NH₃, or high pressure, so those deductions are not applied [CS#3, SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because the target shares the Matlockite structure with KMgBi and NaMgSb, reducing reconstructive burden [Target, CS#3, SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3]. However, elemental Mg and Bi are not highly activated precursors, and the analogue syntheses require long heating or tempering, so the kinetic score is not maximal [SS#3, CS#3, TS#3]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on actual NaMgBi diffusion coefficients, so diffusion scoring uses [Reasonable Inference] from melting/reactivity trends.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has lower thermal severity because Na₂MgSn and Na₂CdSn analogues form at 350–750 °C ranges in sealed Ar systems [SS#2, SS#5, TS#2, TS#5]. It still requires sealed containment due to volatile/reactive Na and possibly Mg, and the proposed staged prereaction/anneal/cooling sequence counts as multistep [SS#2, TS#2; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether NaMgBi Matlockite forms cleanly at this lower temperature, since NaMgSb’s reported synthesis used 1150 K [SS#3, TS#3].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics may benefit from molten or highly mobile Na-containing reaction media, supported by Na₂MgSn and Na₂CdSn sealed-tube syntheses at moderate temperatures [SS#2, SS#5, TS#2, TS#5]. The structural match is weaker than Route 1 because Na₂MgSn/Na₂CdSn are hexagonal P6₃/mmc rather than Matlockite P4/nmm [SS#2, SS#5, TS#2, TS#5]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that NaMgBi specifically crystallizes by this lower-temperature route, so part of the score is [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth avoids >1000 °C conditions because CS flux examples use 900 °C, but it is operationally more severe due to vacuum/ sealed Nb–silica containment, controlled cooling, annealing, quenching, and flux removal [CS#1, CS#5]. Pb flux introduces toxicity and possible contamination risk [CS#1, CS#5; Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS direct analogues do not require flux, so flux is not the lowest-severity route [SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux should improve kinetics by providing liquid-phase mass transport and crystal growth, consistent with Pb-flux growth of chemically similar bismuthide/pnictide phases [CS#1, CS#5]. The limiting kinetic/phase issue is that CS flux examples explicitly report phase-purity problems and mixtures for some related compounds [CS#1, CS#5]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS that NaMgBi itself grows from flux, so this is a plausible but less certain extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple crystalline Matlockite P4/nmm phase with a small formula and same-structure crystalline analogues, lowering amorphization risk [Target, CS#3, SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3]. One risk trigger is the large Na/Mg size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but the structure accommodates distinct Na and Mg coordination sites in analogues [Target, SS#3]. No face-sharing or large unit cell is reported, so amorphization risk remains low-to-medium [Target].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaMgBi is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [Target]. The synthesis evidence is strong: the target shares the Matlockite P4/nmm structure with KMgBi and the Na–Mg–pnictide analogue NaMgSb, both supported by direct elemental synthesis routes [CS#3, SS#1, SS#3, TS#1, TS#3]. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of same-topology alkali–Mg–pnictide analogues with MgPn₄ tetrahedra and 5-coordinate alkali sites [Target, CS#3, SS#1, SS#3]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Na/Mg volatility and air sensitivity during high-temperature reaction, as sealed systems and glovebox handling are repeatedly used for Na/Mg analogues [SS#2, SS#3, TS#2, TS#3]. Overall, direct sealed-ampoule synthesis is the best-supported route, while flux growth is more useful for crystals than for phase-pure bulk [CS#1, CS#5].
