### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues Zr₂InN, ZrN, HfN, Zr₃AlN, and Ce₃(BN₂)₂ are also reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in TS1–TS5, so TS evidence supports stable nitride-type chemistry. The remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature stability, especially with respect to hydrogen loss, which is not resolved by E_hull alone [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C processing by using 850–950 °C, consistent with Zr₂InN annealing at 500–900 °C and hydride analogues at 850–900 °C (SS1/TS1, CS2–CS4). The severity score is reduced because hydride-containing analogues require sealed Ta capsules and controlled atmospheres (CS2–CS5). The main uncertainty is whether ZrH₂ provides the correct H chemical potential without decomposing or forming binary zirconium hydrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory ZrN and Zr metal, consistent with high-temperature requirements for ZrN/HfN analogues (SS2/TS2, SS3/TS3). Zr₂InN forms at 500–900 °C but requires 750–850 h and can contain mononitride impurity, showing slow ordering and phase-purity limitations (SS1/TS1). The structural match to Zr₂InN and ZrN supports partial transferability, but H ordering into tetrahedral sites is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures, 750–850 °C, directly supported by YHSe and CeHSe flux syntheses at 700–850 °C (CS2, CS5). The score is penalized for sealed Ta handling, hydride precursor sensitivity, and multi-step flux processing, all of which are present in hydride analogue routes (CS2, CS5). The uncertainty is whether halide fluxes are chemically compatible with Zr₂NH without forming Zr chlorides or oxychlorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux-assisted transport improves kinetics relative to dry solid-state synthesis because alkali chlorides “secure complete and fast reactions” and single crystals in hydride chalcogenide analogues (CS5). This is beneficial for a refractory Zr–N framework, where dry nitride analogues often require high temperatures or long anneals (SS1–SS3/TS1–TS3). The uncertainty is whether chloride flux can dissolve or mobilize enough Zr/N species at 750–850 °C [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition avoids bulk high-temperature diffusion and does not require sealed hydride powders [Reasonable Inference]. However, it requires reactive N₂/H₂ or plasma/vacuum conditions, which triggers a controlled-atmosphere penalty [Reasonable Inference]. Direct thin-film evidence is insufficient from CS, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS, so this route is lower-confidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Short diffusion lengths in thin films could help crystallize complex hydride nitrides at lower temperature [Reasonable Inference]. However, direct evidence for vapor-deposited Zr₂NH is absent, and hydride analogues show strong dependence on H content (CS2, CS3). Therefore, the kinetic score is kept near neutral-to-low because crystallization and composition control are unproven by CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so cation radius mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference]. The described structure contains ordered NZr₆ octahedra and HZr₄ tetrahedra with corner/edge sharing, and a close Zr₂InN analogue has an ordered NZr₆ octahedral framework (SS1/TS1). The lack of face-sharing and apparent small formula complexity reduce amorphization risk, though hydrogen disorder remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₂HN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and multiple thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from structural/thermodynamic similarity to Zr₂InN and ZrN, which reproduce the Zr–N bond-length scale and NZr₆ octahedral motifs (SS1/SS2/TS1/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the close match between the target Zr–N framework and known stable Zr nitride/intermetallic nitride frameworks (SS1, SS2). The most critical limiting factor is hydrogen chemical-potential control, because hydride analogues require sealed Ta conditions and show phase sensitivity to H content (CS2, CS3, CS5). Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require sealed, oxygen-free processing and careful screening of H loading.
