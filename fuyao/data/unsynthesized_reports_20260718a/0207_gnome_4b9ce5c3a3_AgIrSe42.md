### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin. Many analogues are also on or very near the hull: AuCuSe₄ in CS-1/TS-5, TaSe₄I in CS-3, NiSe₂ in SS-1/TS-1, Rh₂Se₃ in SS-3/TS-2, and PtSe₂ in TS-3/TS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any decomposition tendency for the exact Ag–Ir–Se composition; the main uncertainty is whether the computed ground state is experimentally accessible rather than thermodynamically stable.

#### F2 — Route 1

The sealed-tube solid-state route uses standard evacuated quartz/silica ampoules, which is strongly supported by CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-2. Its penalty comes from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, repeated firings/grinding, and toxic/volatile chalcogen handling; these are consistent with the analogue procedures in SS-2 and SS-5. The route avoids high pressure and can likely avoid >1000 °C by using the 850–900 °C range supported by CS-3 and SS-3/TS-2, but Ir diffusion remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The route has poor-to-moderate kinetics because elemental Ir is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference], even though elemental solid-state reactions are common for selenides in CS-3, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-2. SS-2 and SS-5 explicitly use multiple heating/grinding steps, supporting a kinetic penalty for homogenization and phase formation. Structural reconstruction is plausible because SS/TS analogues contain MSe₆ octahedra, but no direct AgIr₂Se₈ analogue is provided.

#### F2 — Route 2

The flux route operates at much lower temperature than solid-state synthesis, following AuCuSe₄ formation at 310 °C in CS-1/TS-5. However, it requires glovebox handling, vacuum sealing, air-sensitive K₂Se/K₂Seₓ, and post-synthesis flux removal, all documented in CS-1/TS-5. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports the exact flux route for Ir phases; the transfer to Ir is an extrapolation.

#### F3 — Route 2

K₂Seₓ/Se flux should greatly improve precursor activity compared with dry diffusion, as AuCuSe₄ crystallizes at only 310 °C in CS-1/TS-5. The target’s Se–Se bonds are chemically similar to those in AuCuSe₄ (CS-1/TS-5), supporting polyselenide flux transferability. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Ir metal dissolves or converts fast enough under low-temperature flux conditions; using preformed Ir selenide is therefore recommended [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The high-pressure route is penalized heavily because SS-1/TS-1 uses 1000 °C and 50 kbar in a belt apparatus, i.e., specialized high-pressure equipment. It also requires sealed/capsuled handling of volatile Se and likely a prereaction step [Reasonable Inference based on SS-1/TS-1]. Although structurally relevant, its practical condition severity is the highest of the three routes.

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure can favor dense octahedral MSe₆/Se–Se frameworks, as shown by the NiSe₂ pyrite analogue in SS-1/TS-1. Structural reconstruction is therefore comparatively favorable, but precursor activity and cation diffusion are still limited by Ir-containing solids [Reasonable Inference]. The short high-pressure dwell in SS-1/TS-1 suggests kinetics can be acceptable once a suitable precursor pellet is prepared, but exact Ag/Ir ordering remains uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: the Ag/Ir cation size mismatch in octahedral coordination is large [Reasonable Inference], while the target description does not support a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing. The target’s structure is ordered but low-symmetry triclinic P-1, which may increase crystallization sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues show that octahedral selenide frameworks crystallize readily, but the ternary Ag/Ir ordering adds uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ag(IrSe₄)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar selenides are also stable or near-stable (CS-1, CS-3, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from the combination of polyselenide flux chemistry for noble-metal Se–Se compounds in CS-1/TS-5 and octahedral transition-metal selenide formation in sealed tubes from SS-2, SS-3, SS-5, and TS-2. The most critical enabling factor is the presence of Se–Se polyselenide units, because reactive Se/K₂Seₓ flux can directly supply this bonding motif (CS-1/TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is Ir activation and ordering into the low-symmetry Ag/Ir octahedral framework, since Ir is refractory and no direct Ag–Ir–Se analogue is provided [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful flux composition, Se chemical potential control, and slow cooling.
