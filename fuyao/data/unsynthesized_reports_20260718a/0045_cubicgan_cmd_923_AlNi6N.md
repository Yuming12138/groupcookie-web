### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold in the rubric, so it receives 90/100. TS analogues include phases with comparable mild metastability, such as Mn3Ni20P6 at 0.0568 eV/atom (TS-2) and ZrNi4Sn at 0.0328 eV/atom (TS-3), supporting that phases in this energy range can be experimentally accessible. The remaining uncertainty is that E_hull does not encode nitrogen chemical potential or finite-temperature entropy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is supported most directly by SS-1, where a cubic Ni nitride analogue with NNi6 octahedra was made under NH3 at 743 K with regrinding and a second sintering. Its severity is moderate because NH3 is reactive/toxic and the process is multi-step, but it avoids high pressure and >1000 °C conditions. Kinetically, the shared Ni–N octahedral motif from SS-1 helps, but replacing CdNi3N/Pm-3m with AlNi6N/F-43m means the exact framework is not proven.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is supported by many Ni-rich intermetallic syntheses using arc melting plus annealing, including CS-4 and SS-2 to SS-5. Its condition severity is worse because arc melting exceeds 1000–1400 °C and the route also needs controlled Ar/vacuum plus NH3 nitridation. The kinetic benefit is strong cation homogenization by melting, but the limiting step is nitrogen insertion into a dense Al–Ni alloy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 draws on TS-1 and TS-5, which show high-pressure methods can access dense or metastable compounds under GPa/kbar-class conditions. The severity score is low because high pressure, reactive internal sources, multistep capsule work, and quenching are required. The kinetic score is also modest because there is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for high-pressure Al–Ni nitride formation.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 has favorable condition severity because it avoids bulk diffusion and high temperatures, but no provided CS/SS/TS analogue directly supports thin-film growth. Its kinetic score is moderate: Al/Ni targets and reactive nitrogen are plausible [Reasonable Inference], yet crystallization and stoichiometric N incorporation are uncertain. This route scores best numerically but receives an expert penalty for the lack of direct reference precedent.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple ordered composition and no evidence of a >40-atom unit cell, which lowers amorphization risk. The target connectivity includes corner-sharing AlNi12 cuboctahedra and face-sharing with NNi6 octahedra, but not the full vertex+edge+face coexistence that would trigger the highest risk. A modest penalty is assigned because the Al/Ni size mismatch is near or slightly above 15% [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AlNi6N is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, at the boundary of the most favorable scoring region. The strongest analogue support comes from SS-1, which demonstrates a cubic Ni nitride with NNi6 octahedra and A–Ni12 cuboctahedra can be synthesized under NH3. CS and SS intermetallic analogues also show Ni-rich metallic frameworks can be made by arc melting and long annealing, but they do not directly prove nitrogen incorporation into AlNi6N. The key enabling factor is the close local structural match to known Ni nitride coordination in SS-1. The main limiting factor is controlling nitrogen chemical potential and avoiding competing Al–Ni intermetallic or AlN/Ni nitride phases [Reasonable Inference].
