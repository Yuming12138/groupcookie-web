### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable threshold in the provided rubric and supports metastable synthesizability. However, the exact-composition BaNiF6 analogue in trigonal R-3m is reported at E_hull = 0, indicating that the cubic F-43m target is likely a metastable polymorph relative to the known ground-state structure (CS1, SS1). The main uncertainty is whether the cubic polymorph can be kinetically trapped before relaxation or transformation to the trigonal phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is strongly supported compositionally because BaNiF6 itself was obtained from BaF₂/Monel under high-pressure F₂ at 450–600 °C for 10 d (CS1, SS1). Its F2 score is limited by the need for hot, compressed F₂ and a Monel autoclave, even though the pressure is below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty (CS1, SS1). Its F3 score is moderate-low because dense BaF₂/Ni sources and 10 d reaction time imply slow fluorination/diffusion, and the reported product is trigonal rather than the cubic target (CS1, SS1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits kinetically from reactive molecular/complex precursors and lower temperature, as SrNiF6/BaNiF6-related phases were fluorinated at ~280 °C and pF ≈ 140 atm for 12 h (CS4, SS2). The F2 score is reduced by F₂/N₂ pressure fluorination, cyanide precursor handling, and multi-step precursor/fluorination operations (CS4, SS2). The key uncertainty is phase purity, because the analogue report explicitly notes that Guinier-pure samples are extremely difficult and products are air/moisture sensitive (CS4, SS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has the best balance because cubic K₂NiF6 demonstrates that Ni(IV) fluoride octahedra can form by direct F₂ fluorination of chloride salts at ~275 °C (SS5). Its F3 score is high because halide precursors and F₂ enable rapid chemical conversion relative to refractory oxide/solid-state routes, and the target’s Ni–F distance and cuboctahedral large-cation environment closely resemble the cubic K₂NiF6 local motif (target description; SS5). The main uncertainty is that BaNiF6 stoichiometry differs from K₂NiF6 and known BaNiF6 is trigonal, so cubic phase selection is not guaranteed (CS1, SS1, SS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Photochemical HF/F₂ synthesis is supported by structurally similar ACrF6 phases made at ambient temperature under UV irradiation in anhydrous HF/F₂ for 10–20 d (SS3, SS4). This gives excellent kinetic activation but severe handling penalties because both HF and F₂ are hazardous and require FEP/PTFE equipment (SS3, SS4). The uncertainty is high because the precedent is Cr-based rather than Ni-based, and Ba solubility/reactivity in HF may be limiting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a very large Ba-site cation and a much smaller octahedral Ni cation, so the cation size mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference] from the target’s BaF12 and NiF6 coordination. The atom-count check is not triggered because no evidence indicates a >40 atom unit cell, and the simple BaNiF6 formula/high-symmetry cubic description suggests a small crystallographic cell [Reasonable Inference]. The mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered because the target description includes corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, reducing amorphization risk relative to highly mixed networks.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The cubic BaNiF6 target is most likely a metastable polymorph rather than the ground state, because the exact-composition trigonal BaNiF6 analogue is on the hull while the cubic target is 0.05 eV/atom above hull (CS1, SS1; target data). The CS/SS evidence strongly supports the existence of Ba/Sr NiF6 high-valent fluoride chemistry, but it also shows that these phases often require F₂ pressure, specialized autoclaves, and difficult phase control (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2). The most critical enabling factor is access to sufficiently high fluorine chemical potential to oxidize Ni to Ni(IV) in NiF6 octahedra, as shown by BaNiF6/SrNiF6 and K₂NiF6 fluorination precedents (CS1, CS4, SS5). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph control: known BaNiF6 prefers trigonal R-3m, while the target is cubic F-43m (CS1, SS1; target description). A direct F₂ fluorination route adapted from cubic K₂NiF6 offers the best practical starting point, but pressure escalation or quenching may be needed to trap the cubic phase [Reasonable Inference].
