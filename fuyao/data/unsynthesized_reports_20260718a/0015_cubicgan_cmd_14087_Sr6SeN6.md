### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary of the requested ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. CS and SS analogues are mostly calculated on-hull or near-hull, including Sr₃GaN₃, Sr₃CrN₃, Sr₃MnN₃, Sr₅Ge₂N₆, Sr₂ZnN₂, LiSrN, and SrN (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). TS analogues are chemically indirect, but they show experimentally known materials with E_hull around 0.055–0.088 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), supporting that 0.05 eV/atom is not prohibitive. The uncertainty is that thermodynamic proximity alone does not validate the unusual SeN₆ coordination environment [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for controlled atmosphere, hazardous/air-sensitive reagents, sealed volatile flux, and multi-step handling. The deductions are supported by NaN₃/Na/Sr use, welded Nb/Ta tubes, Ar glovebox loading, vacuum sealing, slow cooling, and dynamic-vacuum Na removal in Sr nitride flux syntheses (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1–SS3). The temperature is relatively moderate, ~750–760 °C, and no high-pressure apparatus is required (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1–SS3). Remaining uncertainty is Se compatibility with Na flux and whether Se forms target Se–N bonding rather than SrSe or other selenides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Flux growth gives the best kinetics because molten Na and NaN₃ enable Sr-rich nitrides at temperatures well below many direct ceramic reactions (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1–SS3). Sr–N bond distances in the target, 2.62 Å, match the 2.55–2.94 Å range in Sr nitride analogues (CS1, CS2; SS1–SS4). The main kinetic/structural penalty is that no analogue directly contains SeN₆ octahedra, so nucleation of the exact cubic phase may require nontrivial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is favorable but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses more conventional bulk solid-state conditions but still requires sealed/inert handling. CS3 demonstrates 1050 °C for 4 d in sealed stainless-steel crucibles under Ar for Sr₃CrN₃, while CS4 uses 850 °C under Ar/N₂ for Sr₃MnN₃. The need for sealed operation is increased by Se volatility and air sensitivity of Sr nitrides/Sr selenides [Reasonable Inference]. The lack of flux lowers procedural complexity but not the atmosphere/stoichiometry risk.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state diffusion is slower than flux-mediated growth, as indicated by long high-temperature treatments such as 4 d at 1050 °C for Sr₃CrN₃ (CS3). Sr₃MnN₃ formation at 850 °C for 24 h suggests that Sr-rich nitrides can form without flux, but still require controlled high-temperature conditions (CS4). The target likely requires rearrangement between nitride and selenide sublattices, and no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates this exact chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the kinetics are moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C temperature if modeled on SrN/SrN₂ pressure synthesis at 920 K, but it requires specialized N₂-pressure equipment (SS5). The requested F2 rubric only penalizes high pressure above 5 GPa, so the 400–5500 bar range from SS5 does not trigger the −25 high-pressure deduction, but it remains practically challenging. Controlled N₂ pressure, air-sensitive nitride/selenide precursors, and likely sealed volatile Se chemistry create significant severity (SS5; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and TS provide insufficient direct route-specific signal, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

High nitrogen chemical potential can stabilize Sr nitrides, as SrN and SrN₂ were obtained from Sr₂N under 400–5500 bar N₂ for 72 h (SS5). This supports N-rich phase formation but not specifically Se–N octahedra. Because the route likely starts from solid Sr₂N/Sr₃N₂ and SrSe, diffusion and reconstruction remain moderate-to-slow [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is similar to Route 2 rather than flux growth.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target likely has a moderately complex Fm-3m cell if the conventional cell contains multiple Sr₆SeN₆ formula units, giving a likely atom count above 40 [Reasonable Inference]. The structure also mixes corner- and edge-sharing NSr₄Se square pyramids, but not face sharing according to the target description. A large Sr-vs-Se cation/network-former mismatch is expected [Reasonable Inference], though CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify ionic-radius mismatch for this exact oxidation-state assignment. The resulting F4 score reflects medium risk, not severe amorphization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₆SeN₆ is likely a near-hull metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the favorable threshold but not clearly below it. CS and SS strongly support the synthesis of Sr-rich nitrides with similar Sr–N bond lengths and polyhedral environments, especially by Na-flux/NaN₃ methods (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1–SS4). TS supports the general idea that phases with comparable metastability can be synthesized, but TS is chemically weak for Sr–Se–N specifically (TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is use of a molten alkali-metal flux to enhance Sr/N mobility and allow low-temperature crystallization (CS1, CS2, CS5; SS1–SS3). The most critical limiting factor is the lack of direct precedent for SeN₆ octahedral coordination in a Sr nitride lattice, making competing Sr nitrides, SrSe, or Se-rich phases likely [Reasonable Inference].
