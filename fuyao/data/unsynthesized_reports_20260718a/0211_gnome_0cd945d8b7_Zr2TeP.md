### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied thermodynamic data. This is strongly favorable because many successful analogues are also on the hull, including Ti2SbP in CS1, ZrSiTe in CS3, Zr7P4 in SS1/TS1, Zr5Sb3P in SS3/TS3, and Zr5AgSb3 in TS5. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of off-stoichiometric disorder [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2: Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderate because the closest H-phase analogue was synthesized at only 800 °C for 48 h in quartz tubes, without high pressure (CS1). However, the target contains P and Te, so sealed-tube handling is needed to manage volatile anions, consistent with sealed chalcogenide/phosphide procedures in CS3 and SS4. Insufficient evidence from CS1 on exact atmosphere was treated cautiously by applying a controlled/sealed-system penalty.

#### F3: Route 1 reaction kinetics

The same H-phase/Caswellsilverite topology in CS1 gives a strong structural-kinetic advantage because the target requires similar mixed octahedral connectivity. Kinetics are still limited by refractory Zr diffusion, as Zr-rich phosphides in SS1/TS1 require high-temperature treatment and ZrSiTe in CS3 requires long annealing. The best practical mitigation is repeated grinding/sintering, directly supported by CS1 and CS3.

#### F2: Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because it requires making or obtaining binary precursors such as ZrP and ZrTe2, then annealing the ternary product, analogous to SS1/TS1 and SS4. CS3 shows that Zr–chalcogenide solid-state syntheses may require pre-annealing, grinding, pelletizing, and annealing up to several weeks. The route avoids arc temperatures but pays penalties for multi-step sealed-tube processing and volatile components.

#### F3: Route 2 reaction kinetics

Prereacted binaries should reduce uncontrolled exothermic reaction and volatile loss relative to raw elements [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1 and SS4]. However, the target P6₃/mmc phase must reconstruct from ZrP/ZrTe2-type building blocks, and CS3 indicates long annealing can be needed for Zr–chalcogenide phase formation. Thus kinetics are moderate but not as favorable as the direct same-structure CS1 route.

#### F2: Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is challenging because analogous Zr/Hf phosphides require arc melting or induction annealing near 1580 °C or 1673 K (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS4). These temperatures strongly increase P/Te volatility risk, so prereacted binaries and sealed post-annealing are needed [Reasonable Inference]. The method uses specialized arc/induction equipment, as explicitly noted in SS1/TS1 and SS5/TS4.

#### F3: Route 3 reaction kinetics

Arc melting improves homogenization of refractory Zr-containing charges, as seen in SS1/TS1 and CS5. However, volatilization of P/Te and possible quenching into disordered or competing phases can undermine ordering into the target Caswellsilverite phase [Reasonable Inference]. Because the structurally closest analogue CS1 does not require arc melting, this route is less kinetically targeted.

#### F2: Route 4 condition severity

Flux synthesis operates at 900–1000 °C for Zr-rich pnictide/chalcogenide-like frameworks in SS2/SS3/TS2/TS3/TS5, which is milder than arc melting. It still requires sealed Ar or evacuated ampoules and post-reaction flux removal, as in SS2/SS3/TS2/TS3 and CS2. The presence of volatile Zn/Sn flux and P/Te gives moderate condition severity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3: Route 4 reaction kinetics

Flux improves mass transport and can grow crystals at temperatures well below the melting point of Zr, supported by Zn-fluxed Zr5Sb3X syntheses in SS2/SS3/TS2/TS3/TS5. The structural match is not as direct as CS1 because the flux analogues are P6₃/mcm Zr5Sb3X rather than P6₃/mmc Zr2TeP. The main uncertainty is whether Zn/Sn stabilizes competing phases or enters the product [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Zr, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from formula]. The target is a compact ordered intermetallic/pnictide/chalcogenide phase rather than a large complex cell, and analogues such as CS1 are simple P6₃/mmc phases. The one amorphization/kinetic-risk trigger is mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing connectivity, explicitly stated for the target and also present in CS1.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr2TeP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis case is unusually strong because CS1 provides a direct same-structure H-phase/Caswellsilverite-like analogue synthesized by ordinary sealed-tube sintering, while CS3, SS1/TS1, and SS4 independently show that Zr–P and Zr–Te bonding networks are experimentally accessible. The most critical enabling factor is the exact structural transferability from Ti2SbP-type P6₃/mmc chemistry in CS1 to the Zr2TeP target. The main limiting factor is maintaining P/Te stoichiometry during high-temperature treatment, because P and Te are volatile and competing Zr phosphides/tellurides are plausible [Reasonable Inference from CS3, SS1/TS1, and SS4]. Overall, the target appears highly synthesizable by careful sealed-tube solid-state methods, with flux and binary-precursor routes as useful backups.
