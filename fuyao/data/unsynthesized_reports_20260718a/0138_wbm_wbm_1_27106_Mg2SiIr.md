### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and earns a high thermodynamic score. The closest Mg₂PtSi analogue and the top SS/TS analogues also have **E_hull = 0.0**, supporting that related intermetallic compositions can be thermodynamic ground states (CS/SS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or ambient-pressure stability after quench [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 high-pressure synthesis.**  
The closest analogue Mg₂PtSi requires **1675 K** and **40 kbar**, with slow cooling and quenching, making conditions severe despite the strong structural match (CS/SS/TS). The pressure is below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, but it still requires specialized high-pressure apparatus, which is a practical difficulty described in the analogue data (CS/SS/TS). The main uncertainty is whether Ir substitution changes the pressure/temperature window [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 high-pressure synthesis.**  
Kinetics are helped by the fact that Mg₂PtSi forms in only **30 min** under high pressure and high temperature, suggesting rapid reaction once Mg loss is suppressed (CS/SS/TS). The target is isostructural with the Mg₂PtSi analogue, so structural reconstruction is expected to be low [Reasonable Inference from CS/SS/TS]. The limiting kinetic factor is refractory Ir diffusion, which may be slower than Pt depending on particle size and temperature [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 sealed-tube sintering.**  
SS/TS show that Ir-containing intermetallics can be made from high-purity elements in sealed Ar ampoules at **650–900 °C**, giving moderate condition severity. However, Mg₂PtSi evidence explicitly states that normal-pressure experiments suffer Mg vaporization losses, so sealed containment and Mg excess are required [Reasonable Inference from CS/SS/TS]. The conflict is that SS/TS support ambient sealed synthesis for Heuslers, while CS/SS/TS Mg₂PtSi specifically favors high pressure for Mg₂TMSi chemistry.

**F3 — Route 2 sealed-tube sintering.**  
Solid-state diffusion into Ir at <1000 °C is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], and no SS/TS analogue demonstrates ambient-pressure synthesis of the exact Mg₂PtSi-type framework. Mg mobility above its melting point could improve kinetics [Reasonable Inference], but the exact structural analogue required high pressure, lowering confidence (CS/SS/TS). Regrinding/reannealing may be necessary, increasing kinetic difficulty [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 arc melting plus annealing.**  
CS supports arc melting plus annealing for chemically similar ternary silicides, but those examples are Th–T–Si rather than Mg–Ir–Si and do not address Mg volatility directly. Arc melting imposes very high local temperatures, and Mg loss is expected to be severe at normal pressure [Reasonable Inference from Mg vaporization warning in CS/SS/TS]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Mg-containing arc-melted analogues and are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 3 arc melting plus annealing.**  
Arc melting can overcome diffusion limits by forming a melt or prealloy [Reasonable Inference], and CS shows arc melting works for some ternary silicides. However, Mg volatility and possible formation of Ir–Si or Mg–Si binaries can prevent the exact Mg₂IrSi stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Because the CS arc-melted analogues have a different tetragonal structure, structural transferability is only moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a relatively simple ordered intermetallic structure with small formula content, so amorphization risk is not high [Reasonable Inference from target/CS structural descriptions]. One risk trigger is size/chemistry mismatch among Mg, Ir, and Si, but the successful crystalline Mg₂PtSi analogue suggests this framework can crystallize cleanly under suitable conditions (CS/SS/TS). SS/TS Heusler analogues provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for the Mg₂IrSi target and are treated as neutral for that subfactor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₂IrSi is likely a **ground-state** phase because its calculated hull energy is **0.0000 eV/atom**. The strongest support comes from Mg₂PtSi, which is essentially the same P6₃/mmc structural type and was synthesized from elements under high pressure (CS/SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is **suppression of Mg volatility and preservation of stoichiometry**, which the high-pressure Mg₂PtSi route explicitly accomplishes (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is the need for specialized high-pressure synthesis and the uncertainty of whether ambient-pressure sealed-tube routes can access the same Mg₂TMSi framework. Overall, synthesis is feasible but most credible via high-pressure elemental reaction rather than ordinary ambient-pressure sintering.
