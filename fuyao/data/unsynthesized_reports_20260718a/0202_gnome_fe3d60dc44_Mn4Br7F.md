### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high score. TS strongly supports this because close thermodynamic analogues include TlMnCl₃ with E_hull = 0.0 and MnInBr₃ with E_hull ≈ 0.004 eV/atom (TS-1, TS-2). SS also supports stability because structurally similar Mn halides are on or very near the hull (SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). CS is partly contradictory because one chemically similar Mo bromide oxide has E_hull ≈ 0.58 eV/atom (CS-1), but the more relevant Mn bromide analogue MnInBr₃ is near hull (CS-2), so CS is weighted less than TS/SS for F1.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 450–500 °C, supported by MnInBr₃ ampoule synthesis at 450 °C and TlMnCl₃ halide melt synthesis near 490–507 °C (CS-2/SS-1/TS-2; SS-2/TS-1). It avoids high external pressure, unlike the high-pressure Mo bromide oxide route in CS-1, so CS-1 is not used as a primary route model and is effectively neutral for this target route. The main severity penalties are evacuated sealed ampoule conditions, volatile halide containment, and dry/inert handling, all supported by related halide syntheses (CS-2, CS-4, SS-1, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether MnF₂ fully reacts under these relatively low-temperature conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable compared with oxide ceramic synthesis because anhydrous halides can melt or form high-activity halide media, as shown by Li₂MnBr₄ from melted anhydrous binary bromides and MnInBr₃ from molten ampoule synthesis (SS-3; CS-2/SS-1/TS-2). The target’s Mn–Br octahedral framework is structurally close to MnInBr₃ and Li₂MnBr₄, reducing reconstruction burden (CS-2/SS-1; SS-3). The main uncertainty is F incorporation/order, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates MnBr₅F octahedral ordering.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 also uses moderate halide-melt temperatures, based on MnInBr₃ and TlMnCl₃ molten halide syntheses (CS-2/SS-1/TS-2; SS-2/TS-1). It receives a larger penalty than Route 1 because flux growth adds more operations: flux choice, drying, loading, slow cooling, and product separation [Reasonable Inference]. Air/moisture sensitivity and sealed-ampoule handling are supported by MnInBr₃/CrInBr₃ descriptions (CS-2, CS-4, SS-1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms an appropriate Br/F flux, so that part is treated as extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth gives the best kinetic score because liquid halide media should improve diffusion and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported by quantitative MnInBr₃ synthesis in molten salt conditions and by Li₂MnBr₄ formation through melting anhydrous binary bromides (CS-2/SS-1/TS-2; SS-3). However, phase control may be nontrivial because Li₂MnBr₄ reports difficulty obtaining single crystals (SS-3), and TlMnCl₃ analogues show phase transformations/metastability (SS-2/TS-1). The uncertainty is whether flux cations such as Li⁺/K⁺ remain excluded from the target lattice [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate CVT temperatures and sealed evacuated ampoules, supported by CVT analogues at 360–475 °C for 5 days with temperature gradients (SS-5, TS-3, TS-5). It receives penalties for vacuum, volatile transport chemistry, multi-step setup, and toxic/corrosive halide additives [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 supports that halide vapor reactions can work at moderate temperature, but it is chloride/aluminate chemistry rather than Mn–Br–F, so its transferability is limited. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms CVT of Mn₄Br₇F specifically.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT can overcome bulk diffusion limitations by vapor transport, as shown for related halide/chalcogenide systems (SS-5, TS-3, TS-5). The score is limited because MnBr₂ and MnF₂ likely have very different transport behavior, making Br:F control difficult [Reasonable Inference]. CVT analogues can also have low yields and impurity mixtures, indicating phase-control risk (SS-5, TS-3, TS-5). Therefore Route 3 is plausible mainly for crystal growth screening, not as the primary synthesis route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Mn, so cation-radius mismatch is not an amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference from target composition]. The described structure is dominated by edge-sharing octahedra, with no evidence of mixed vertex/edge/face sharing coexistence; analogous Mn halides also contain edge-sharing MnBr₆ or MnCl₆ motifs (CS-2/SS-1; SS-2/TS-1; SS-3). The exact crystallographic cell size is not given in CS/SS/TS; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom target cell, so that check is treated as not triggered but uncertain. Overall amorphization tendency is low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mn₄Br₇F is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamically similar Mn halides are also on or near the hull (TS-1, TS-2). The strongest support comes from MnInBr₃ and Li₂MnBr₄, which demonstrate that edge-sharing MnBr₆ octahedral bromide frameworks can be synthesized from anhydrous halide melts or sealed ampoules (CS-2/SS-1/TS-2; SS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature halide melt chemistry, which avoids refractory high-temperature ceramic diffusion limitations (CS-2/SS-1/TS-2; SS-3). The most critical limiting factor is achieving and retaining ordered Br:F = 7:1 stoichiometry, because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates mixed Mn bromide fluoride ordering. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with sealed-ampoule halide reaction as the best first attempt.
