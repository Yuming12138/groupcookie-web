### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has **E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom**, giving a high thermodynamic score by the provided rubric. Multiple close analogues are also calculated on or near the hull: Zr3AlN has E_hull = 0.0 in CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2, Zr2InN has E_hull = 0.0 in SS-1 / TS-1, and Zr5Sn3N has E_hull = 0.0 in CS-5. The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee a practical kinetic pathway, especially with volatile As **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is strongly supported structurally because Zr3AlN is the same Cmcm-type analogue and was made by melting stoichiometric AlN + Zr under pure Ar followed by slow cooling in CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2. The severity score is reduced because a Zr-rich melt likely requires high temperature, and As introduces volatility/toxicity not present in the Al analogue **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state sintering because melting improves homogenization, but ZrN/ZrAs may still resist reaction and As loss may shift stoichiometry **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Sealed-tube sintering is supported by Zr2InN formation in quartz ampoules at 500–900 °C for very long times in SS-1 / TS-1 and by Zr5Sn3N derivative synthesis at 1000–1350 °C for 3–14 days in CS-5. Its condition severity is moderate-to-high because evacuated/sealed containment is needed, and As volatility/toxicity requires additional control **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are the main weakness: long reported sintering times and mononitride impurity in Zr2InN from SS-1 / TS-1 indicate slow diffusion and incomplete conversion risk.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best overall score because CS-2 / CS-3 show Zr–pnictide/metalloid compounds can form in Zn flux at only 900–1000 °C for about a week. The liquid flux is expected to improve mass transport and reduce the need for extreme Zr-rich melting **[Reasonable Inference]**. The limitation is that CS-2 / CS-3 are not exact Cmcm Zr3MN analogues and do not demonstrate nitrogen incorporation in the same phase, so composition and structure selectivity remain uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

High-temperature arc/induction routes are well supported for Zr–P and related refractory pnictides in SS-3 / TS-3 and SS-5 / TS-4. However, the severity score is low because these precedents use 1450–1580 °C, controlled Ar/vacuum, and multi-step precursor preparation. Kinetics may be acceptable due to melting and annealing, but target Zr3AsN could lose As or N under such high-temperature conditions **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is an ordered crystalline intermetallic nitride with a simple Zr-only metal sublattice and no reported face-sharing/edge-sharing/corner-sharing coexistence; the target specifically reports corner- and edge-sharing NZr6 octahedra. Zr3AlN has the same corner/edge-sharing NZr6 motif in CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2, indicating that such connectivity is compatible with crystallization rather than amorphization. The main uncertainty is the exact conventional cell size, but the formula and close analogue suggest it is not a very large-cell compound **[Reasonable Inference]**.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr3AsN is likely a **ground-state or near-ground-state phase** because its reported hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support is the exact structural analogue Zr3AlN, which shares the Cmcm framework and was synthesized by melt growth under Ar in CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Zr–N octahedral framework, which appears repeatedly in Zr nitride/metalloid analogues such as Zr3AlN and Zr2InN in CS-1 / SS-2 / TS-2 and SS-1 / TS-1. The main limiting factor is As volatility/toxicity and the possibility of competing ZrAs, ZrN, or Zr-rich pnictide phases during high-temperature treatment **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with flux-assisted or carefully contained melt/anneal routes most promising.
