### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Ti3PtN is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score (target data). Stable analogues include Ti3Pt with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-1, TS-1), Zr3AlN/Hf3AlN with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-4, CS-5), and Ti2AlN/Ti2GaN with E_hull = 0.0 (SS-3, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). CS is partly contradictory because V3AuN has very high E_hull = 2.8877 eV/atom (CS-2), but that entry is less directly Ti–Pt-like than the Ti3Pt and Cr3PtN/Ti3AlN analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the calculated 0 K hull phase remains accessible under finite-temperature nitrogen chemical potential and competing TiN/Ti3Pt equilibria [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state ampoule reaction

The severity score is limited by controlled vacuum/sealed-tube conditions and long multi-step annealing, both supported by Cr3PtN-type synthesis in sealed ampoules with multiple anneals (CS-3) and Ti3AlN sintering under vacuum/Ar for up to hundreds of hours (SS-5). The kinetic score is modest because Ti/Pt/TiN are dense refractory solids [Reasonable Inference], and analogous systems report long annealing or homogeneity challenges (CS-3, SS-5, TS-2). The favorable aspect is structural match: Ti3AlN and Cr3PtN share the target Pm-3m antiperovskite connectivity (SS-5, CS-3). The main uncertainty is whether Pt diffusion and N ordering can proceed at 900–1100 °C without leaving Ti3Pt or TiN impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Arc-melt prealloy + nitriding

This route has higher condition severity because arc melting exceeds 1400 °C and requires inert atmosphere control, consistent with Ti3Pt preparation under high-purity helium (SS-1, TS-1). Kinetically, prealloying should improve Ti–Pt homogeneity [Reasonable Inference], but N insertion from TiN into a Ti–Pt framework remains slow and requires structural rearrangement from Pm-3n Ti3Pt to Pm-3m Ti3PtN (SS-1/TS-1; target). The route is chemically plausible because Ti3Pt has PtTi12 cuboctahedral motifs similar to the target PtTi12 environment (SS-1; target). The uncertainty is high because CS/SS/TS do not directly report nitriding Ti3Pt to Ti3PtN; that conversion is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Reactive melt / induction melting

This route is severe thermally because it uses a melt/arc step above 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], but it benefits kinetically from liquid-state mixing. Melt/slow-cool nitride synthesis is supported by Zr3AlN/Hf3AlN analogues prepared from nitride/metal powders in a high-frequency furnace under pure Ar on a water-cooled support (CS-4, CS-5), and Ti3Pt formation by arc melting supports Ti–Pt framework compatibility (SS-1, TS-1). The kinetic score is higher than solid-state routes because melt homogenization reduces cation diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is nitrogen loss or persistence of TiN, because CS-4/CS-5 do not establish that Ti–Pt–N has the same nitrogen retention behavior.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film deposition

This route scores well on condition severity because it avoids bulk melting and high pressure [Reasonable Inference], but it lacks direct CS/SS/TS precedent; insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS gives neutral direct support for thin-film feasibility. Kinetically, sputtering can deliver Ti, Pt, and N at atomic scale [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization into ordered cubic Pm-3m Ti3PtN is unvalidated by the provided analogues. Composition control is a concern because TiN and Ti3Pt are plausible competing phases [Reasonable Inference supported by TiN/Ti3Pt analogue stability in SS-1/SS-5]. Therefore, although the raw score is high, the recommended evidence-supported route remains the reactive melt route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple cubic Pm-3m antiperovskite with only Ti, Pt, and N in ordered high-symmetry sites, and the reported structure has no octahedral tilting (target data). The unit cell is small and does not trigger the >40 atom criterion (target data). The connectivity includes corner-sharing NTi6 octahedra and face-sharing with PtTi12 cuboctahedra, but the required vertex + edge + face coexistence is not present (target data). Remaining uncertainty is that thin films could amorphize under low-temperature deposition, but bulk equilibrium routes should have low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti3PtN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest analogue support comes from same-structure Ti3AlN in cubic Pm-3m (SS-5), same noble-metal nitride antiperovskite Cr3PtN (CS-3), and stable arc-melted Ti3Pt with closely related Ti–Pt coordination (SS-1, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the simple, untilted cubic antiperovskite framework with short Ti–N bonds closely matching known Ti–Al–N analogues (target; SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: Ti, Pt, and TiN are refractory, diffusion is slow, and analogous solid-state routes often require long anneals or repeated homogenization (CS-3, SS-5, TS-2). Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but likely requires either a melt-assisted route or extensive annealing to avoid Ti3Pt/TiN secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].
