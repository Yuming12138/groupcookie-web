### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin by the requested rubric. Several close Pd oxide analogues are also calculated on the hull, including Ba₂Hg₃Pd₇O₁₄, BiPd₂O₄, PdO, TlPd₃O₄, and NaPd₃O₄ (SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1–TS-5). Chemically similar Cd oxides Cd₂Ru₂O₇, Cd₂SnO₄, and CdRh₂O₄ are also hull-stable (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic/oxygen-potential stability rather than 0 K thermodynamics [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is strongly supported by BiPd₂O₄, which contains mixed square-planar and octahedral Pd and was made at 6 GPa/1500 K in Au capsules with PdO/KClO₄ (SS-3, TS-2). The severity score is low because the route uses >5 GPa pressure, high temperature, sealed capsules, toxic CdO, and oxidizers [Reasonable Inference from SS-3/TS-2]. Kinetics are moderate because high T and short 2 h holds help diffusion, but bulk oxide reaction and ordering of Cd/Pd/O sublattices remain nontrivial (SS-3, TS-2). CS provides only indirect support through Cd oxide precursor use and high-pressure Cd₂Ru₂O₇, while SS/TS provide the main route evidence (CS-2, SS-3, TS-2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 has the best structural/thermodynamic basis because Ba₂Hg₃(PdO₂)₇ is both the top SS and TS analogue and was synthesized specifically under 3900 bar O₂ at 630 °C for 12 days (SS-1, TS-1). F2 is relatively high because the temperature is moderate and pressure is below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, though high-pressure O₂ apparatus and toxic CdO remain concerns (SS-1, TS-1; CS-4/CS-5). F3 is limited by slow diffusion at 630 °C and the 12-day analogue reaction time (SS-1, TS-1). CS gives useful CdO precursor evidence but insufficient direct signal for high-valent Pd stabilization, so SS/TS are weighted more heavily for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Sealed peroxide/oxidizer routes are supported by NaPd₃O₄ synthesis from Na₂O₂/KO₂/PdO in sealed silica tubes at 600–700 °C and by TlPd₃O₄ synthesis at 550–600 °C from PdO/Tl₂O₃ (TS-5, SS-5, TS-4). This route has moderate condition severity because it avoids GPa pressure but requires sealed oxidizing media, moisture-sensitive peroxides/superoxides, and multi-step handling [Reasonable Inference from TS-5]. Kinetics are helped by reactive oxidizers and possible molten flux behavior, but the structural match is weaker than Route 2 because Na/TlPd₃O₄ are not Cd Hausmannite-derived phases (TS-5, SS-5). CS gives insufficient direct signal for peroxide/sealed-tube Pd oxide synthesis and is treated as neutral for this route.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Conventional ceramic synthesis is supported for Cd mixed oxides by Cd₂SnO₄ at 1050 °C in air and CdRh₂O₄ at 850 °C for 48 h in air (CS-4, CS-5). However, closest Pd mixed-valent analogues generally required high oxygen pressure or GPa pressure, so the route is less targeted for Cd(PdO₂)₃ (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2). Condition severity is modest, but kinetics and phase selectivity are weaker due to oxide precursor sluggishness and possible PdO decomposition/Pd metal formation [Reasonable Inference supported by Pd metal removal noted for TlPd₃O₄, SS-5/TS-4]. SS/TS therefore conflict with the simpler CS ceramic evidence, and SS/TS are weighted more heavily because they contain Pd-rich/high-valent Pd chemistry.

The target combines large CdO₈ polyhedra with PdO₄ square-planar and PdO₆ octahedral units, giving a substantial cation size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target also has mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity, which tends to increase crystallization/ordering difficulty relative to a simple single-polyhedron framework [Reasonable Inference; similar mixed connectivity appears in CS-2 and SS-1/TS-1]. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cd(PdO₂)₃ is likely a true ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Pd-rich oxide analogues are also on the hull (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2, TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from Ba₂Hg₃(PdO₂)₇ and BiPd₂O₄, which demonstrate that Pd–O networks with square-planar and/or octahedral Pd can be stabilized using high oxygen pressure or high-pressure oxidizing synthesis (SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high oxygen chemical potential to prevent reduction to PdO/Pd metal and to stabilize the high average Pd oxidation state [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/TS-1 and SS-5/TS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Cd–Pd–O system, especially Cd volatility/toxicity and possible competing CdO, PdO, Pd metal, or alternate Pd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference from CS-4/CS-5 and SS-5/TS-4]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires controlled oxygen pressure rather than a simple air-fired ceramic route.
