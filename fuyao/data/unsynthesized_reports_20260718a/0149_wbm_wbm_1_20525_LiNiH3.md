### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
LiNiH₃ is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum F1 score according to the provided rubric. Thermodynamic analogues Mg₂RuH₆, Mg₂CoH₅, Li₄RuH₆, Li₂PtH₆, and Li₄OsH₆ are also at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS), and chemically similar CaMgNiH₄ and Li₄RuH₆ are hull-stable (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or room-temperature persistence [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1, autoclave H₂ hydrogenation.**  
The severity score is reduced mainly by H₂ pressure, multi-step autoclave handling, and air-sensitive LiH, all of which are present in analogous hydride syntheses (CS/SS/TS). Kinetically, this route is plausible because LiH + transition-metal powder reactions form Li₄RuH₆ under H₂ (CS/SS/TS), and Ni–H bonding is demonstrated in CaMgNiH₄ (CS). The main uncertainty is slow conversion and impurity formation, because CaMgNiH₄ and Mg₂RuH₆-type syntheses report impurities or variable yields (CS/SS/TS).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, high-pressure multi-anvil.**  
The severity score is low because Li₂PtH₆ required >8 GPa, a multi-anvil assembly, inert handling, and a solid hydrogen source (SS/TS). Kinetically, high pressure is attractive because Li₂PtH₆ has very close LiH₁₂/MH₆ geometry and similar M–H distance to LiNiH₃ (SS/TS). The uncertainty is whether Ni stabilizes the same high-pressure hydride topology and whether the LiNiH₃ stoichiometry survives decompression [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, mechanochemical activation + H₂ anneal.**  
This route scores best because it avoids GPa pressure while using precedented Ar milling/H₂ or D₂ annealing conditions for perovskite-derived hydrides (CS). Mechanochemical activation should improve precursor activity and diffusion relative to simple LiH/Ni pellets [Reasonable Inference], and H₂ annealing is consistent with Li₄RuH₆ and Mg₂RuH₆ formation conditions (CS/SS/TS). The main uncertainty is that no direct mechanochemical Li–Ni–H perovskite analogue is provided, so crystallization into cubic Pm-3m LiNiH₃ may require optimization [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target structure is simple, cubic, and small-cell, so the >40 atom-cell check is not triggered. The target has corner and face sharing but no edge sharing in the provided description, so the strict mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. The only trigger is the large Li/Ni cation size and coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference], giving a low–medium amorphization risk; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence and are neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiNiH₃ is predicted as a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamically similar hydrides are also hull-stable (TS). The synthesis case is supported by chemically similar Ni–H hydrides such as CaMgNiH₄ (CS), Li–transition-metal hydrides such as Li₄RuH₆ (CS/SS/TS), and structurally similar hydride perovskite/double-perovskite frameworks such as Mg₂RuH₆ and Li₂PtH₆ (SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high hydrogen chemical potential, since most successful analogues use H₂/D₂ pressure or internal hydrogen sources (CS/SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in the Li–Ni–H system, because analogous complex hydride syntheses often report impurities, incomplete conversion, or pressure sensitivity (CS/SS/TS). Overall, the best route is mechanochemical activation followed by H₂ annealing, with high-pressure synthesis reserved as a fallback if the cubic hydride requires pressure stabilization [Reasonable Inference].
