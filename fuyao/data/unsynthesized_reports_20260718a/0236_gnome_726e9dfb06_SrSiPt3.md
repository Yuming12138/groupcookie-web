### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target SrPt₃Si has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest stability bin by the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues include several on-hull Pt-rich intermetallics such as Tb₂(Pt₃Ge)₃ and REPt₄In phases (TS1–TS5), which supports the plausibility of a stable Pt-rich ternary framework. CS evidence also includes on-hull Eu–Pt–B, CePt₃B, EuPt₄B, and DyPt₂Si analogues (CS1–CS4). Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee the P4mm polymorph is the experimentally accessible low-temperature phase, but the exact same P4mm topology in CePt₃B is encouraging (CS2).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized heavily because arc melting imposes very high local temperatures and because Sr retention likely requires sealed handling after melting [Reasonable Inference]. The route is nevertheless supported by many analogues that use arc melting and annealing under Ar or evacuated tubes (CS1, CS3–CS4, SS1–SS2, TS1). Phase and crystallinity control commonly require remelting and annealing, as reported for Eu–Pt–B and RE–Pt–Ge analogues (CS1, CS3, SS1–SS2, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct Sr volatility management in this exact composition, so that penalty is based on [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Dense elemental Pt and Si give only moderate precursor activity, consistent with elemental solid-state/intermetallic synthesis rather than reactive molecular precursors (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS2, TS1). Arc melting improves kinetics by forming a homogenized alloy button, and annealing at 900–1000 °C for about a week is sufficient for related Pt–Ge phases (SS1–SS2, TS1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because a same-topology P4mm analogue exists in CePt₃B (CS2), but Route 1 still requires long annealing and repeated remelting in several analogues (CS1, CS3, SS1–SS2). The main uncertainty is whether SrPt₃Si orders cleanly or competes with binary Sr–Si/Pt–Si phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from the closest direct analogue: CePt₃B was made by high-frequency melting under Ar followed by annealing at 880 °C for 14 days (CS2). It avoids explicitly evacuated high-temperature transport chemistry and does not require high pressure, so the severity is moderate rather than severe (CS2). Penalties remain for high melt temperature and air-sensitive Sr handling [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for exact P4mm Sr–Pt–Si melt-growth conditions, so SS/TS contribute mainly general support for elemental Pt intermetallic melting routes (SS4–SS5, TS2–TS5).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Kinetic confidence is highest for Route 2 because CS2 has the same P4mm structure type and 1:3:1 composition pattern. High-frequency melting gives liquid-state mixing, reducing the diffusion limitation that would be severe for refractory Pt [Reasonable Inference supported by CS2]. The 880 °C/14 d anneal in CS2 suggests ordering can proceed at accessible temperatures after melting. The uncertainty is that B-to-Si and Ce-to-Sr substitutions change size and bonding, so transferability is strong but not exact [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route is based mainly on CS5, where CeAu₄Si₂ was grown from Au–Si flux at 1050 °C with slow cooling over two weeks. It avoids the highest temperatures of full Pt melting, but it introduces sealed ampoule handling, flux preparation, and a multi-step growth/decanting protocol (CS5). Sr compatibility with silica and flux is not addressed by CS5, so a refractory containment recommendation is [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports flux growth for the target-like P4mm structure, so SS/TS are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth should improve mass transport compared with solid-state diffusion because the reactants can dissolve and recrystallize from a liquid medium [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 shows that a noble-metal–silicon flux can grow Ce–Au–Si crystals, supporting the general approach for silicide-type intermetallics. However, no provided analogue demonstrates Sr–Pt–Si growth from Pt–Si flux, so the structural and compositional control scores are lower than for Route 2. The main uncertainty is whether a Pt–Si or Si-rich flux has a useful solubility window for SrPt₃Si without stabilizing competing silicides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small ordered intermetallic P4mm cell analogous to CePt₃B, so the unit-cell-size amorphization risk is low (CS2; target structure). A radius mismatch trigger is assigned because Sr is substantially larger than Pt/Si, which can frustrate amorphous-to-crystalline ordering if quenched too fast [Reasonable Inference]. However, intermetallic analogues with high-coordinate rare-earth/alkaline-earth environments crystallize readily under melt/anneal conditions (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS2, TS1–TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for explicit mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity in SrPt₃Si, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPt₃Si is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and many thermodynamically similar Pt-rich intermetallics are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis evidence is the same-structure CePt₃B P4mm analogue made by high-frequency melting under Ar and annealing at 880 °C for 14 days (CS2). The most important enabling factor is liquid-state homogenization followed by long annealing, which is repeatedly successful for Pt-rich intermetallics (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS2, TS1). The most important limiting factor is Sr handling: Sr is air-sensitive and likely volatile/reactive during high-temperature melting, requiring careful stoichiometry and containment [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible in bulk, with Route 2 giving the best balance of structural transferability and practical severity.
