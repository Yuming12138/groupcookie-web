### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is high by the required rubric (Target). Similar fluorides SrNiF6, BaNiF6, KCrF6, RbCrF6, and CrF5 also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in CS/SS/TS, which supports stability of related high-valent fluoride frameworks (CS; SS; TS). The remaining uncertainty is chemical rather than hull-based: the target contains formal Cu(V), and the provided analogues demonstrate Ni(IV) or Cr(V), not Cu(V) [Reasonable Inference; CS; SS; TS].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because SrNiF6 and BaNiF6 require F2 pressure fluorination in Monel/Monell autoclaves, with SrNiF6 at 140 atm and BaNiF6 at >3500 atm F2 (CS; SS; TS). The temperatures are not extreme by the rubric, because the relevant analogues use 280 °C or 450–600 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS; SS; TS). The main uncertainty is how much F2 pressure KCuF6 needs, since KCrF6 is accessible photochemically but BaNiF6 required very high pressure (SS; TS).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 receives a favorable kinetics score because the closest R-3m analogues SrNiF6 and BaNiF6 already possess the same AF12/MF6 structural motif as KCuF6 (CS; SS; TS; Target). Gas-phase F2 provides strong chemical driving force at relatively low temperature, which can reduce the need for long-range diffusion [Reasonable Inference; CS; SS; TS]. The uncertainty is whether copper can be oxidized into the same framework before competing Cu fluoride phases form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature and high mechanical pressure because KCrF6/RbCrF6 form at ambient temperature in anhydrous HF/F2 under UV irradiation (SS; TS). However, it is still severe because HF and F2 are volatile, corrosive, toxic, and require sealed fluoropolymer equipment (SS; TS). Insufficient evidence from CS alone exists for a photochemical Cu analogue, so CS is treated as neutral, 50, for this specific route-transfer factor.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 receives the best kinetics score because the anhydrous HF/F2/UV medium is solution-mediated and directly demonstrated for KCrF6, a K-containing AF6 analogue with very similar K coordination (SS; TS). Ambient-temperature formation over 10–20 d indicates that the route bypasses conventional solid-state diffusion limitations (SS; TS). The main uncertainty is whether Cu(V) fluoride chemistry is compatible with HF/F2 photochemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized similarly to Route 1 because CrF5 direct synthesis requires elemental F2, passivated Monel autoclave equipment, staged heating, vacuum-line operations, and drybox handling (SS; TS). Its temperature is moderate, 260–300 °C, so no high-temperature deduction applies (SS; TS). Insufficient evidence from CS exists for direct K–Cu fluorination, so CS is neutral, 50, for this route-specific feasibility.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 has lower kinetics than Route 2 because direct solid fluorination is heterogeneous and lacks a demonstrated KCuF6 or KMF6 product from binary KF/MF precursors [Reasonable Inference; SS; TS]. CrF5 shows that high-valent metal fluorides can form under F2 at 260–300 °C, but it does not demonstrate ternary K–Cu framework ordering (SS; TS). Competing binary copper fluorides and incomplete K/Cu mixing are the main kinetic uncertainties [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a large K-centered 12-coordinate cuboctahedron with a small high-valent CuF6 octahedron, so the cation-size mismatch check is triggered [Reasonable Inference; Target]. The target also has corner, edge, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers the mixed-connectivity check (Target). The atom-count check is not triggered because no >40-atom cell is indicated by the target or close R-3m analogues; exact cell multiplicity remains an uncertainty [Reasonable Inference; Target; CS; SS; TS].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KCuF6 is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The analogue support is strong structurally and thermodynamically: SrNiF6/BaNiF6 share the R-3m AF12/MF6 framework, and KCrF6/RbCrF6 show that alkali-metal hexafluorometallates with 12-coordinate A sites are experimentally accessible (CS; SS; TS). The most critical enabling factor is very high fluorine chemical potential supplied by F2, either in anhydrous HF/UV photochemistry or in a pressure fluorination autoclave (CS; SS; TS). The most critical limiting factor is the formal Cu(V) oxidation state, which is more demanding than the demonstrated Ni(IV) and Cr(V) analogues and may narrow the phase-stability window [Reasonable Inference; CS; SS; TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires specialized fluorine chemistry rather than conventional ceramic processing.
