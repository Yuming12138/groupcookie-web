### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. CS/SS/TS analogues also include multiple stable Caswellsilverite-like or derived sodium/alkali noble-metal oxides, including Na₂PtO₃, NaRhO₂, Na₃Cd₂IrO₆, and Na₅OsO₆ with E_hull = 0 in the cited entries (SS1/TS1, CS5/SS4/TS3, SS3/TS2, SS5/TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct oxygen chemical potential during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 has mild conditions because Na₂PtO₃ forms at 500 °C for 2 d under O₂-free Ar without high pressure or sealed flux (SS1/TS1). Its kinetic score is limited by possible slow Rh metal/oxide conversion and phase-control difficulty, as Na₂PtO₃ analogues note unexpectedly difficult phase purity despite simple conditions (SS1/TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Rh substitution in the Na₂PtO₃ route; this chemical transfer is [Reasonable Inference] and therefore not scored as fully certain.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The high-O₂ route is harsher experimentally because the chemically related Na–Ru–O synthesis uses 220 MPa oxygen, gold crucibles, glovebox handling, and a 4 d reaction at 600 °C (CS1). Kinetically, mineralizer/high oxygen pressure should improve reaction and stabilize higher transition-metal oxidation states [Reasonable Inference], but the autoclave route remains specialized. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for high-pressure oxygen necessity in Na₂RhO₃ specifically; SS/TS mostly support ambient-pressure Na₂PtO₃/flux routes.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux growth is supported by NaRhO₂ growth in a Pd bomb at 950 °C for 18 d and Na₃Cd₂IrO₆ growth in closed Ag tubes at 800 °C for 10 d (CS5/SS4/TS3, SS3/TS2). It earns a higher F3 than dry solid-state routes because molten alkali peroxide/oxide flux can accelerate transport [Reasonable Inference], but F2 is reduced by sealed vessels, long schedules, and air-sensitive fluxes. The main uncertainty is whether Na-rich flux will select Na₂RhO₃ over NaRhO₂, since NaRhO₂ is an experimentally demonstrated competing Rh phase (CS5/SS4/TS3).

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Conventional carbonate/oxide synthesis is operationally simple and supported for Li₂RuO₃ and Li₂ZrO₃ Caswellsilverite-like analogues in air at 950–1000 °C with grinding/pelletizing (CS3, CS4). Its kinetic score is lower because Na₂CO₃/Rh₂O₃ lacks the oxidizing/reactive benefits of peroxide flux and may suffer Na loss or incomplete Rh oxidation [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for direct carbonate synthesis of sodium rhodates; SS/TS favor peroxide/flux routes for Na-containing noble-metal oxides.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is an ordered crystalline octahedral framework with corner- and edge-sharing NaO₆/RhO₆ octahedra, matching many crystalline analogues (CS1, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS5). Only the large Na–Rh cation size contrast triggers the amorphization check [Reasonable Inference]; no face sharing is described in the target, and exact cell size evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS. The risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

Na₂RhO₃ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable but likely not by a completely uncontrolled ceramic route.
