### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic-stability score (target data). Thermodynamic analogues HfC, Nb6C5, Ti2GaN, Ti2AlN, and Ti5Al2C3 are also reported at E_hull = 0 or near-zero, indicating that refractory carbide/nitride frameworks in this similarity space can be true ground-state phases (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is kinetic rather than thermodynamic, because a hull-stable ordered carbonitride may still be difficult to realize if C/N/vacancy ordering is slow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because it likely requires >1400 °C, controlled Ar/vacuum, repeated grinding/annealing, and air-sensitive Ti powder [Reasonable Inference]. These penalties are consistent with Nb6C5 requiring 2300 K vacuum sintering and Ti5Al2C3 requiring 1500 °C in Ar (SS2, TS2, CS1, SS5, TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Ti6C2N3 synthesis conditions, so CS contributes mainly general Ti carbide precedent and is otherwise neutral for exact condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Ti/TiC/TiN powder diffusion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the high synthesis temperatures reported for Nb6C5 and Ti5Al2C3 (SS2, TS2, CS1). The structural relationship to Nb6C5 improves feasibility because both have C2/m square-pyramidal metal sites and M6 anion octahedra (SS2, TS2; target description). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ti6C2N3 orders directly or first forms disordered Ti(C,N) [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less thermally severe than Route 1 because ampoule syntheses of Ti2GaN/Ti2GaC-like phases occur at 500–900 °C, and V–P–C analogues occur near 1000 °C (CS5, SS3, TS3, CS3, CS4). It is still penalized for controlled sealed conditions, repeated annealing, and air-sensitive Ti powder [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS for a sealed-ampoule route specifically to Ti6C2N3, so TS is neutral for exact ampoule transferability beyond analogous thermodynamic similarity.

#### F3 — Route 2

Lower temperature and refractory precursors make ordering slow, as shown by 500–900 h anneals in Ti2GaN/Ti2GaC analogues and second homogenization anneals in V–P–C analogues (CS5, SS3, TS3, CS3, CS4). The absence of Al/Ga/metametal liquid or soft sublattice in the target may reduce diffusion compared with H-phase analogues [Reasonable Inference]. The route is plausible but kinetics are likely the main weakness [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 requires high temperature and controlled atmosphere, following hot-press refractory-carbide precedent at ~2000 °C and high-temperature Nb6C5/Ti5Al2C3 syntheses (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2, CS1). It avoids high pressure >5 GPa, because hot pressing/SPS operates at much lower mechanical pressures [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for specialized equipment, multistep processing, and Ti powder handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Hot pressing/SPS improves particle contact and shortens diffusion pathways relative to conventional sintering [Reasonable Inference], which is valuable for refractory TiC/TiN-type powders. The route is supported by HfC hot pressing and Nb6C5 high-temperature sintering analogues (SS1, TS1, SS2, TS2). The main uncertainty is whether rapid densification allows enough time for C/N/vacancy ordering, hence the recommended post-anneal [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only Ti as the cation, so there is no mixed-cation ionic-radius mismatch trigger (target description). The target description reports corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but not face sharing, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered (target description). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS gives the exact target unit-cell atom count, but the simple 11-atom formula and close ordered-carbide analogue Nb6C5 argue against high amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference; SS2, TS2].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti6C2N3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest supporting analogue is Nb6C5, which shares monoclinic C2/m symmetry, metal-centered square pyramids, anion-centered M6 octahedra, and similar 5–7° octahedral tilts (SS2, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural match to an experimentally synthesized ordered refractory carbide, combined with strong thermodynamic stability (SS2, TS2; target data). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: TiC/TiN/Ti powders are refractory, and C/N/vacancy ordering may require high temperature and/or long annealing [Reasonable Inference], consistent with high-temperature or long-duration analogues (SS2, TS2, CS5, SS3, TS3). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but not trivial, with hot pressing/SPS plus post-anneal the best balance of kinetic activation and phase control [Reasonable Inference].
