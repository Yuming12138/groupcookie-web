### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic score bracket. Thermodynamic analogues ScS, YS, TmS, DyS, and DyTe are all on the hull (TS1–TS5), and chemically/structurally relevant ScTe and SrSc₂Te₄ are near the hull (CS3/SS4, CS4). This strongly supports a ground-state or near-ground-state target, although DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing ordered variants [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C if the reaction can be completed near 850–950 °C, consistent with SrSc₂Te₄-type synthesis at 800–900 °C and YTe-type synthesis at 650 °C (CS4, SS2/TS2). However, evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile S/Te, grinding/reannealing, and air-sensitive Sc handling impose significant deductions [Reasonable Inference; sealed and multistep practice supported by CS4, CS5, SS2/TS2]. The main uncertainty is whether Sc₂TeS forms at these moderate temperatures or requires the higher ScTe-like anneal (CS3/SS4).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Sc is not a highly reactive low-temperature precursor, so reaction kinetics are expected to be diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing is supported by SrSc₂Te₄ and β-ScTe examples that require 200 h-scale treatments (CS4, CS3/SS4). The local bonding match to ScS and ScTe helps, but anion ordering between S and Te may be slow [Reasonable Inference; local bonding similarity from SS1/TS1 and CS3/SS4].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 directly follows the high-temperature ScTe precedent, which used arc-melting under Ar, Ta containers, vacuum furnace annealing, and 1573 K for 200 h (CS3/SS4). These conditions are severe because they require specialized equipment, controlled atmosphere, sealed refractory containers, and control of volatile chalcogens [Reasonable Inference; equipment and anneal from CS3/SS4]. The route is credible but operationally more demanding than sealed-tube synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Arc-melting improves homogenization and can overcome the poor solid-state reactivity of Sc metal [Reasonable Inference]. The closest structural analogue, ScTe, is P6₃/mmc with ScTe₆ octahedra and similar Sc–Te distances (CS3/SS4), so structural reconstruction is relatively favorable. The uncertainty is that sulfur incorporation may be lost during arc-melting or segregate into Sc sulfides unless binary sulfide precursors and sealed annealing are used [Reasonable Inference; Sc sulfide nonstoichiometry concerns from SS1/TS1].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 can operate near 950 °C, matching ScS CVT with I₂ (SS1/TS1), and iodine traces are also reported as a mineralizer for scandium telluride crystal growth (CS4). It still requires evacuated ampoules, toxic/corrosive I₂, volatile chalcogen management, and a pre-synthesized charge [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is inherently required by the analogues, so the severity is moderate rather than extreme.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CVT improves mass transport relative to bulk diffusion, which is why this route receives the best kinetics score [Reasonable Inference; ScS CVT evidence from SS1/TS1]. It is especially attractive because ScS can be transported at 950 °C and iodine mineralization appears in related scandium telluride crystal growth (SS1/TS1, CS4). The major uncertainty is possible incongruent transport of Sc, S, and Te, since no source directly shows ternary Sc–S–Te CVT.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size-disorder amorphization is not expected [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is described as having mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing octahedra, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion; similar mixed connectivity occurs in ScTe (CS3/SS4). Because the cell is likely small and crystalline analogues with similar local motifs exist, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high [Reasonable Inference; structural support from CS3/SS4 and SS1/TS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂TeS is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom and many thermodynamic analogues are also hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from ScTe, which shares P6₃/mmc symmetry and Sc-centered chalcogen octahedra (CS3/SS4), and from ScS, which provides a close Sc–S bond-length analogue and an I₂-CVT precedent (SS1/TS1). The most important enabling factor is the excellent local bond-length and coordination match: target Sc–Te = 2.96 Å matches ScTe 2.90–2.99 Å, and target Sc–S = 2.59 Å matches ScS 2.61 Å (CS3/SS4, SS1/TS1). The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of S/Te stoichiometry and ordering under sealed high-temperature conditions, because S and Te are volatile and Sc sulfides/tellurides can form competing binary phases [Reasonable Inference; sealed/long anneal practices from CS3/SS4, CS4, SS1/TS1]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sealed-tube or transport-assisted processing rather than a simple open crucible reaction.
