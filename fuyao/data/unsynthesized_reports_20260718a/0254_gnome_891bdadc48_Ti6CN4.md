### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives a high F1 score. Thermodynamically similar nitride/MAX-like references ZrN, Ti2GaN, Ti2AlN, HfN, and Ti2InN also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which supports the plausibility of stable Ti–N-rich refractory phases (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing TiN/TiC ordering variants [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Synthesis condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because comparable ampoule syntheses use 500–1000 °C but often require long holds and reannealing (CS-2, CS-5, SS-2, TS-2, TS-5). The sealed/vacuum environment and repeated homogenization lower the F2 score, but no high pressure or toxic volatile precursors are required for Ti/TiC/TiN [Reasonable Inference].  

Route 2 is more severe because refractory nitride analogues use 1500–2200 °C hot pressing or pressure sintering under controlled atmospheres (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). This adds high-temperature and equipment burdens, though not >5 GPa pressure [Reasonable Inference].  

Route 3 is penalized by reactive N2/NH3 atmospheres and possible toxic NH3 handling, with gas-phase nitrogen incorporation supported indirectly by ZrN/NbN analogues (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1). The route is chemically simple but atmosphere-sensitive [Reasonable Inference].  

Route 4 is penalized by reactive gases and volatile/corrosive metal precursor handling; Nb nitride CVD uses NbCl5/NH3/H2 at 950–1000 °C, demonstrating feasibility but also gas-handling complexity (SS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS for thin-film Ti–C–N deposition is therefore treated as neutral for CS-specific support.

#### F3 — Reaction kinetics

Route 1 has moderate-to-slow kinetics because Ti/TiC/TiN are refractory powders, and analogues require long anneals or repeated grinding/homogenization (CS-2, CS-5, SS-3, TS-3). However, mononitride/monocarbide powder routes are directly used for related H-phase nitrides at 500–900 °C, supporting kinetic accessibility with long duration (SS-2, TS-2, TS-5).  

Route 2 gains densification and diffusion benefits from hot pressing, as used for refractory ZrN/HfN analogues (SS-1, SS-4, TS-1, TS-4). Its kinetic advantage is partly offset by the risk that high temperature favors simpler TiN/TiC solid solutions instead of ordered Ti6CN4 [Reasonable Inference].  

Route 3 has the weakest kinetics because it must form both Ti–C and Ti–N networks from elemental/gas precursors, making binary TiC/TiN trapping likely [Reasonable Inference]. Nitride analogues explicitly note phase-window and N-loss issues, supporting this concern (SS-1, TS-1).  

Route 4 has the best kinetic score because vapor deposition bypasses bulk diffusion, and NbNx films can form at 950–1000 °C in CVD (SS-5). Its uncertainty is composition and ordering control, since NbNx phase selection is sensitive to N/Nb ratio and Ti6CN4 also requires precise C/N incorporation (SS-5; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ti, so cation-size disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The reported polyhedral network contains corner and edge sharing but no face-sharing coexistence in the target description, lowering frustration/amorphization risk. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for that check, so that component is treated neutrally rather than as a high-risk trigger.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti6CN4 is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues in the Ti/Zr/Hf nitride and Ti MAX-like families also sit on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for sealed-ampoule or evacuated-tube solid-state processing, since chemically and structurally related carbides/nitrides are made from powders, mononitrides, and monocarbides with long anneals (CS-2, CS-5, SS-2, SS-3, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of stoichiometrically matched Ti/TiC/TiN precursors that already contain the desired Ti–C and Ti–N bonds [Reasonable Inference; SS-2/TS-2 use mononitride/monocarbide precursors]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with TiN, TiC, or disordered Ti carbonitride solid solutions, consistent with phase-control issues in refractory nitride/carbonitride analogues (SS-1, TS-1, SS-5). Overall, bulk synthesis is plausible but likely requires long homogenization and careful stoichiometry control.
