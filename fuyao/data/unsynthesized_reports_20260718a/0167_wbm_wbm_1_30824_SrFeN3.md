### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the required rubric. Structurally and thermodynamically similar compounds SrN₂, CaN₂, BaN₂, and LiSr₂Fe₂N₃ are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom or very close to hull, supporting thermodynamic plausibility (SS1, SS3–SS5, TS1–TS4). The main uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or ambient-pressure stability, particularly for diazenide motifs that in analogues often require high pressure (SS1, TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is relatively mild because the closest Sr–Fe–N analogues were synthesized at 750 °C, 1 atm N₂, and 36 h, without GPa pressure (SS2, SS3, TS2). The main severity penalty is air/moisture sensitivity of nitride precursors and inert handling, consistent with glovebox/inert procedures in nitride syntheses (SS3, CS3). CS evidence from Ca₂PN₃ supports similar high-temperature binary-nitride synthesis, but it uses longer 14 d firing and sealed crucibles, so the exact time requirement remains uncertain (CS3).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The Fe/Sr local coordination in LiSr₂Fe₂N₃ closely matches the target Fe–N and Sr–N distances, which supports feasible local bonding formation (SS3, target). However, the target’s N–N bond is absent from the Sr–Fe nitride analogue and must be generated or preserved from a diazenide precursor, lowering the structural-reconstruction score [Reasonable Inference based on SS1/SS3]. Residual Fe impurity in analogous Sr–Fe nitride synthesis suggests Fe incorporation may be kinetically incomplete (SS3, TS2).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is severe because the most relevant N–N analogues, especially SrN₂, require multianvil high-pressure synthesis at 9 GPa and 550 °C (SS1, TS1). The azide precursors are reactive and require careful glovebox handling, which adds safety and operational difficulty (SS1, SS4, SS5). The relatively short reaction times of 15–20 min reduce thermal burden but do not offset the pressure/equipment penalty (SS1, SS4, TS1).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Azide decomposition is highly active for forming diazenides, and the SrN₂ analogue directly matches the target N–N bond length of 1.25 Å (SS1, TS1, target). High pressure improves reaction driving force and can rapidly produce dense diazenide phases, as shown by 15–20 min syntheses of alkaline-earth diazenides (SS1, SS4, SS5). The uncertainty is Fe incorporation: none of the high-pressure diazenide references demonstrate a mixed Sr–Fe diazenide/nitridoferrate framework, so this is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperatures but requires sealed high-pressure ammonia, which is operationally challenging and receives deductions for NH₃ and volatile sealed conditions (CS2, CS5). Ammonothermal analogues use 250 °C/5000 atm for 7 d or ~500 K/6 kbar for 22 d, showing that long high-pressure treatments are typical (CS2, CS5). Additional post-treatment to remove hydrogen and form an anhydrous nitride is not directly demonstrated and is an added uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Supercritical ammonia can provide high precursor mobility and crystal growth, as demonstrated by Y/alkali amide crystals and KSnN₃-related liquid-ammonia chemistry (CS1, CS2, CS5). This benefits cation mixing relative to dense solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The major kinetic/chemical risk is that ammonia routes preferentially form amides or N–H-containing compounds, whereas the target is anhydrous SrFeN₃ with a diazenide-like N–N bond (CS2, CS5, target).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The Sr/Fe size and coordination mismatch is significant, which can promote disorder or amorphous intermediates during low-temperature synthesis [Reasonable Inference from target Sr 10-coordinate and Fe tetrahedral sites]. The target description does not report a very large cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing Fe polyhedra, so only one amorphization check is triggered (target). The remaining uncertainty is that the exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so C2 is treated conservatively as not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrFeN₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close SS/TS analogues are also on hull (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: Sr–Fe–N frameworks are experimentally accessible at 750 °C under N₂, while Sr diazenide N–N units are experimentally accessible under high pressure (SS3, TS2, SS1, TS1). The most important enabling factor is the close match between target Fe–N/Sr–N distances and the LiSr₂Fe₂N₃ analogue, which supports feasible local bonding in an ambient-pressure solid-state route (SS3, target). The most important limiting factor is combining FeN₄ tetrahedra with a diazenide-like N–N unit in one phase, because the strongest Fe-containing analogue lacks N–N bonding and the strongest N–N analogue lacks Fe (SS3, SS1, target). Overall, the target appears synthesizable but may require careful nitrogen chemical potential control or a diazenide-containing precursor.
