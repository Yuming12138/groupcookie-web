### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the highest-stability boundary in the requested rubric and therefore receives 90/100 (target data). Thermodynamic analogues with similar E_hull values, including K₂CoS₂ at 0.0506 eV/atom and Rb₂CoS₂ at 0.0476 eV/atom, are reported as synthesizable under controlled solid-state conditions (TS-2, TS-5). However, CS and SS provide insufficient evidence that the specific Li–N–Cl/N–Cl bonded framework is a common ground-state motif, so the structural-chemical uniqueness introduces uncertainty despite favorable hull energy.

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed solid-state

Route 1 is penalized because Li₃BN₂ analogues require high temperatures up to 1300 K and controlled N₂/glovebox conditions (CS-3, CS-4), while Li oxide chloride synthesis uses 1300 K and arc-welded Nb containers under purified Ar (CS-2). The use of Li₃N and possible LiN₃/N₂ reservoirs requires air-free sealed handling, which adds severity [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature and equipment are feasible but demanding, so the severity score is low-moderate at 45/100.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed solid-state

Kinetically, Li₃N is a reactive precursor and forms Li–N frameworks in CS-3 and CS-4, so precursor activity is favorable. Li diffusion should be relatively facile compared with refractory cation systems [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS source indicates a refractory diffusion bottleneck for Li nitride analogues. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction: the target requires N–Cl bonding and cubic ordering not directly shown by Li₃BN₂ or Li₅OCl₃ analogues (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2: ammonothermal

Route 2 avoids high temperature because SS ammonothermal analogues form at 420–470 K or 430–450 K (SS-2, SS-3). However, NH₃ autoclaves, 50–500 bar or up to 4.5 kbar pressure, and drybox handling are significant operational burdens (SS-2, SS-3). Since the rubric only penalizes pressures >5 GPa, the main deductions are NH₃ controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile solvent, multi-step handling, and air-sensitive/toxic reagents.

#### F3 — Route 2: ammonothermal

Ammonothermal conditions can improve precursor activity and transport because reactive Li–N species may dissolve or recrystallize in NH₃ [Reasonable Inference], and SS-2/SS-3 demonstrate alkali compound crystallization in NH₃ autoclaves. The low-temperature environment may help preserve nitrogen-rich compositions [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates Li–N–Cl N–Cl framework assembly under ammonothermal conditions, limiting the structural reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3: LiCl-rich flux

Route 3 has moderate condition severity because a LiCl-rich melt/flux can plausibly operate below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], and flux growth analogues use moderate temperatures and sealed tubes (SS-5). Nevertheless, sealed/inert handling, volatile/reactive nitrogen sources, multi-step flux processing, and Li₃N/LiNH₂ air sensitivity remain significant deductions (CS-3, CS-4, SS-5). The route is therefore operationally demanding but less thermally severe than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3: LiCl-rich flux

The flux route receives the best kinetics score because molten/flux media can reduce diffusion barriers and aid crystallization [Reasonable Inference], consistent with slow-cooling crystal growth in CS-3 and flux growth in SS-5. Li₃N supplies reactive Li–N chemistry, while LiCl supplies both Cl and a possible transport medium [Reasonable Inference; CS-3, CS-4, SS-5]. The remaining kinetic risk is that the N–Cl-bonded cubic framework is not directly represented among the CS/SS/TS analogues.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Li, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered (target formula/structure). The structure description includes corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face-sharing criterion is not fully triggered (target description). A possible conventional-cell atom count above 40 is likely for Fm-3m Li₆N₆Cl if Z ≥ 4, but this is inferred rather than explicitly provided; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific evidence, so the F4 score is moderately favorable at 75/100.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₆N₆Cl is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, and TS analogues around 0.035–0.051 eV/atom are experimentally synthesizable (target data; TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). The chemical analogues strongly support Li–N solid-state chemistry and air-free high-temperature processing through Li₃BN₂ examples (CS-3, CS-4), while Li–Cl-containing lithium oxychloride chemistry is supported by CS-2. The strongest enabling factor is the high mobility/reactivity of Li nitride chemistry and the possibility of flux-assisted crystallization [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-3 and SS-5]. The strongest limiting factor is the absence of a direct CS/SS/TS analogue with N–Cl bonds in a Li-rich nitride chloride framework, making the target’s N–Cl connectivity a major extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful nitrogen chemical potential and sealed, dry handling.
