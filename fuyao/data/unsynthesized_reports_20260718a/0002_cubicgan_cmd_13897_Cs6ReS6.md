### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable boundary for likely synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are reported as synthesizable, including K₂CoS₂ at 0.0506 eV/atom (TS-2), Rb₂CoS₂ at 0.0476 eV/atom (TS-4), and Cs₂Se at 0.0751 eV/atom (TS-1). CS also supports stable Cs-rich sulfides/chalcogenides at E_hull = 0, including Cs₄In₂S₅, Cs₃BS₃, and Rb₆HgS₄ (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that Re(VI) sulfide chemistry is not directly represented in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed ampoule sulfide route avoids extreme temperatures and pressures, consistent with CS-3, CS-5, and TS-3. Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur in sealed systems, possible precursor preparation, and air-sensitive alkali sulfides/products, all supported by CS-3, CS-5, SS-3, and TS-3. The main uncertainty is whether ReS₂/Re reacts fully at 550–700 °C [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Cs₂S/S are chemically active sulfide reagents, supported by their use in Cs₃BS₃ and Rb₆HgS₄ syntheses (CS-3, CS-5). However, Re incorporation is kinetically limiting because no Re analogue is given and dense refractory Re would diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is likely substantial because the analogues are tetrahedral or binary chalcogenides rather than cubic octahedral ReS₆ (CS-3, CS-5, SS-3).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The azide route is supported by CS-1 at only ~500 °C, so temperature severity is moderate. It is penalized for vacuum/inert sealed-ampoule operation, azide decomposition/gas evolution, multi-step handling, and hazardous/air-sensitive reagents, all directly indicated by CS-1. The method is synthetically powerful but operationally more hazardous than ordinary ampoule synthesis.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
CS-1 states that azides provide highly reactive heavy alkali metals without using pure Cs/Rb, so precursor activity is high. Re remains the kinetic bottleneck because CS-1 involves In sulfides, not ReS₆ octahedra; this is a [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing and homogenization in CS-1 suggest ordering may require days.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The carbonate/H₂/chalcogen route is experimentally simple and uses standard corundum boats and tube-furnace conditions at 1030 K for 10 h, supported by CS-4, SS-4, TS-2, and TS-4. It is penalized for H₂/chalcogen controlled atmosphere, sulfur volatility, and air-sensitive products. The major issue is redox mismatch: H₂ conditions that work for Co sulfides may over-reduce Re relative to the Re(VI)-like target [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Co analogues form quickly in H₂/chalcogen streams, showing favorable kinetics for some alkali transition-metal sulfides (CS-4, TS-2, TS-4). But Re is not represented, and the carbonate-to-Cs₆ReS₆ transformation is less direct than sulfide/polysulfide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The structural mismatch between Ibam Co sulfides and cubic Cs₆ReS₆ lowers the reconstruction score (CS-4, SS-4, TS-2).

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Ammonothermal synthesis uses modest temperature but high NH₃ pressure, specialized autoclaves, and high-vacuum post-treatment in SS-1/TS-1 and SS-3. The rubric does not penalize pressure below 5 GPa, but reactive NH₃, sealed volatile media, multi-step operation, and air-sensitive products justify substantial deductions. This route is operationally challenging even though thermal severity is low.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
SS-1/TS-1 and SS-3 show that NH₃ under pressure can grow alkali chalcogenide phases and reduce solid-state diffusion limitations through solvothermal transport. Slow cooling and temperature gradients support crystallization rather than amorphous precipitation (SS-1, TS-1). The uncertainty is Re solubility/speciation and whether NH₃/polysulfide chemistry can stabilize an octahedral ReS₆ unit [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains very different cation environments, with Cs–S at 3.39 Å and Re–S at 2.42 Å, implying a large cation-size/field-strength mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The likely conventional Fm-3m unit cell has >40 atoms if Z ≥4, which increases ordering complexity [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for the target’s polyhedral sharing, so C3 was treated as not triggered/uncertain rather than assumed.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₆ReS₆ is likely a borderline stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, and TS analogues near 0.05–0.08 eV/atom have reported syntheses (TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The CS and TS data strongly support the general feasibility of Cs/Rb-rich chalcogenides, while SS/TS data show that high-pressure NH₃ can stabilize alkali chalcogenide frameworks (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur activity and mobile Cs-containing sulfide/polysulfide species, as inferred from sulfide/ampoule and ammonothermal analogues (CS-3, CS-5, SS-3). The most critical limiting factor is Re chemistry: none of the provided analogues directly demonstrate formation of octahedral ReS₆ in a Cs₆MS₆ lattice, so Re oxidation state, diffusion, and phase competition remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine, with the best chance coming from a transport-enabled ammonothermal or reactive sulfide/azide route.
