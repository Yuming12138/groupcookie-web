### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score by the requested rubric. Thermodynamically similar analogues also include several E_hull = 0 phases, including Ba2Ru7O18, PtO2, NaIrO3, Cd2Os2O7, and Ba2Ir3O9 (TS1–TS5). CS data supports the existence of stable or near-stable heavy transition-metal oxides, but many are Ru/Os/Bi/W rather than Ni/Ir, so the chemical transfer is supportive but imperfect (CS1–CS5). SS data includes structurally similar HP phases with positive metastability around 0.06–0.08 eV/atom, so SS is less favorable but still indicates quenchable related structures (SS1–SS3).

#### F2 — Route 1: oxidizing multi-anvil high pressure

Route 1 is severe because several similar Ir/Ru oxide structures require 4.5–20 GPa and high temperature in multi-anvil assemblies (SS1–SS3, TS3). The required Au/Pt capsule, oxidizer, quench, and decompression are directly supported by NaIrO3 and CaCu3Ir4O12 procedures (TS3, SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS for NiIr3O8 specifically at multi-anvil conditions, so CS is neutral for the detailed pressure choice. The score is penalized mostly by >5 GPa pressure, controlled oxidizing environment, and multi-step capsule processing.

#### F3 — Route 1: oxidizing multi-anvil high pressure

Kinetics are only moderate because IrO2/NiO/Ir are refractory precursors, while the closest high-pressure analogues rely on strong P–T conditions rather than inherently reactive precursors (SS1–SS3, TS3). Structural reconstruction is helped by IrO6 octahedral starting motifs and similar Ir–O distances in IrO2/NaIrO3/Ba2Ir3O9 (SS1, TS3, TS5). The uncertainty is Ni/Ir cation ordering in a hydrophilite-derived ternary lattice, which is not directly demonstrated by SS or TS.

#### F2 — Route 2: high-oxygen-pressure cold-seal synthesis

Route 2 avoids GPa pressure and >1000 °C heating, giving a better severity score than multi-anvil synthesis. The main penalties are high-pO2 controlled atmosphere and sealed pressure-bomb operation, exactly as used for hydrophilite PtO2 at up to 3500 atm O2 (TS2). CS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-pO2 Ni–Ir oxides, so CS is neutral here. The route is challenging but experimentally less severe than multi-anvil methods.

#### F3 — Route 2: high-oxygen-pressure cold-seal synthesis

The structural-kinetic match is strong because TS2 is hydrophilite PtO2, and the target is hydrophilite-derived with edge/corner-sharing octahedra. Precursor activity and ion diffusion remain limiting because NiO and IrO2 are dense refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and TS2 still requires long high-pO2 heating. SS1 supports IrO6 bond-length compatibility, but it does not prove Ni incorporation, so uncertainty remains.

#### F2 — Route 3: oxidizing hydrothermal synthesis

Route 3 uses demanding but not GPa-level conditions: 650 °C and 1500 atm in Au capsules/autoclaves, as shown for Ba2Ir3O9 and Ba2Ru7O18 (TS5, TS1). The controlled oxidizing hydrothermal environment and strong oxidizers/mineralizers add severity. CS evidence is insufficient for hydrothermal Ni–Ir hydrophilite phases, so CS is neutral for this route. The major practical burden is specialized autoclave/Au capsule operation, not extreme temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3: oxidizing hydrothermal synthesis

Hydrothermal transport can improve kinetics relative to dry solid-state reaction, as Ir/Ru oxide phases crystallize at 650 °C under hydrothermal conditions (TS5, TS1). However, Ba2Ru7O18 is reported with coexisting BaRuO3 impurity and small crystals, indicating phase-control and nucleation challenges (TS1). The target lacks Ba and may not benefit from the same peroxide-alkaline chemistry, so transferability is moderate rather than high [Reasonable Inference]. This gives the best F3 among the routes but not the best total score.

#### F2 — Route 4: ambient solid-state oxidation

Ambient-pressure solid-state synthesis is operationally simpler and is supported for Bi2WO6 using Bi2O3/WO3 at 750–850 °C with regrinding (CS2, CS3). However, Ir high oxidation likely requires controlled O2 or an internal oxidizer [Reasonable Inference], which adds severity. SS/TS data often points toward HP or high-pO2 stabilization for related Ir/Ru/Pt oxides, so the ambient route is not strongly supported (SS1–SS3, TS2, TS3). The score is moderate by rubric but confidence is low.

#### F3 — Route 4: ambient solid-state oxidation

Repeated grinding/firing is a standard kinetic mitigation in CS2–CS4, but NiO and IrO2 are less reactive than Bi2O3/WO3 and may not reach the high Ir oxidation state at ambient pressure [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction from IrO2 to a ternary ordered NiIr3O8 phase is substantial. Insufficient evidence from TS for ambient-pressure formation of Ni–Ir hydrophilite-derived phases, so TS is effectively cautionary rather than supportive. This route has the weakest kinetic score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has ordered octahedral Ni/Ir sites and only corner/edge sharing, which lowers amorphization risk relative to frameworks with simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing; hydrophilite PtO2 similarly has edge/corner-sharing octahedra (TS2). The main risk is cation size/valence mismatch, suggested by longer Ni–O bonds of 2.03–2.06 Å versus Ir–O bonds of 1.94–2.01 Å in the target structure [Reasonable Inference from target data]. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct atom-count evidence for the target cell, so C2 is treated as not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ni/Ir ordering survives rapid quench or instead yields mixed IrO2/NiO secondary phases.
