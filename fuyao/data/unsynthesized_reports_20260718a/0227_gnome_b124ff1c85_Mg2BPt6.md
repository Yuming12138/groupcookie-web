### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 band by the provided rubric. This is consistent with many related Pt-rich borides/intermetallics also lying on the hull, including Zr2Ir6B, CePt4B, PrPt4B, CePt3B, LiPt3B, EuPt4B, NaPt3B, and Pt8In2As (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5; TS1–TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or exact Mg retention [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.** The arc-melting route is directly supported by the isostructural Zr2Ir6B synthesis, which produced single-phase powder and crystals under Ar with repeated remelting (CS2). Ce/Pr/Eu Pt borides show that elemental Pt/B ternaries tolerate arc melting plus long annealing near 1020–1070 K (CS1, CS3, SS4, TS2). The main severity and kinetic penalties come from Mg volatility/air sensitivity and the need for sealed post-annealing, which are target-specific extrapolations rather than directly shown in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.** Sealed Ta-tube solid-state synthesis is supported by LiPt3B and NaPt3B, which were made from elements under Ar at 1000 °C with homogenization and a second anneal (SS2, TS1, TS4). This route avoids open Mg loss better than arc melting [Reasonable Inference], but has slower diffusion because Pt and B remain refractory solids for much of the process [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control concerns are supported by homogeneity ranges, poor crystal quality, and need for stoichiometry deviations in Li/Na Pt borides (SS2, TS1, TS4).

**F2/F3 — Route 3.** CVT is supported only by structurally similar Pt-rich Al–Pt intermetallics, where iodine-assisted transport at 400–600 °C can produce Pt-rich phases (SS1, SS5, TS5). However, CS provides insufficient evidence for Mg–Pt–B CVT, and TS provides insufficient direct evidence for B incorporation under halide transport. The lower score reflects likely narrow composition control, Mg/halide volatility, B transport difficulty, and documented phase sensitivity to halide partial pressures in Pt–Al CVT (SS1, SS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has a simple cubic Fm-3m framework with MgPt12 cuboctahedra and BPt6 octahedra, and the isostructural Zr2Ir6B analogue has the same corner/face-sharing topology (CS2). There is no evidence for >40 atoms per cell or vertex+edge+face sharing coexistence in the provided structure description or CS2. Radius mismatch is not clearly above the threshold under metallic bonding assumptions, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct ionic-radius data, so this remains a minor uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

Mg2BPt6 is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close Pt-rich boride/intermetallic analogues are also on or very near the hull (CS1–CS3, CS5; TS1–TS5). Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with the best chance coming from rapid arc homogenization followed by sealed Mg-retentive annealing.
