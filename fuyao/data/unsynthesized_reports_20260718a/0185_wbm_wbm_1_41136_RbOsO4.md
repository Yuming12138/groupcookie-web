### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin (target data). Chemically similar RbOs₂O₆, KOs₂O₆, and Ca₂Os₂O₇ are also listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, indicating stable alkali/alkaline-earth osmium oxide chemistry (CS). Structurally and thermodynamically similar tetrahedral oxyanion salts, including CsOsNO₃, Rb₂RuO₄, and CsTcO₄, are also at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee an easy kinetic path to the specific tetragonal I4₁/a polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the most chemically direct evidence: RbOs₂O₆ synthesis from RbO₂ + OsO₂ + AgO at 600 °C generated RbOsO₄ as an impurity (CS). The route is penalized for sealed oxidizing atmosphere, multi-step ampoule handling, and reactive/toxic precursors (CS). Kinetically, it is less favorable because OsO₂ must oxidize and reconstruct into isolated OsO₄ tetrahedra; nevertheless, the observed RbOsO₄ impurity proves the pathway is accessible at least in minor yield (CS). SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for this exact Rb–Os sealed-tube route, so they are treated as neutral for route-specific severity.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by Rb₂RuO₄/K₂RuO₄ analogues that contain isolated tetrahedral RuO₄ groups and require sealed ampoules, alkali peroxide/superoxide reagents, and long reaction durations (SS, TS). The severity is moderate because temperatures are below 1000 °C, but controlled sealed environments and reactive alkali oxides are required (SS, TS). Kinetics are slow because analogues required up to 62 days, but structural reconstruction is less severe than Route 1 because the target and analogues both use isolated tetrahedral MO₄ units (SS, TS). CS supports Rb–Os–O precursor selection but does not directly validate the long-duration perruthenate-style route, so part of the transfer is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest physical conditions because CsOsNO₃ and CsTcO₄ analogues were prepared by aqueous slow evaporation rather than high-temperature sintering (SS, TS). Kinetics are favorable because soluble ions/preformed tetraoxo anions avoid refractory solid-state diffusion and preserve tetrahedral oxyanion geometry (SS, TS, [Reasonable Inference]). The major penalty is chemical hazard and uncertainty: if OsO₄ or unstable high-valent osmium intermediates are required, volatility and toxicity become critical (CS, [Reasonable Inference]). CS provides insufficient direct aqueous evidence for RbOsO₄, so route transfer relies mainly on SS/TS analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is based on KOs₂O₆ high-pressure synthesis from KO₂ + OsO₂ + AgO at 900 °C and 30 kbar (CS). It is chemically plausible for oxidized alkali osmium oxides, but it is not strongly indicated for a target with E_hull = 0 and known low-pressure impurity formation (target data, CS). Kinetics may improve at 900 °C and pressure, but the product structure differs from the high-pressure KOs₂O₆ framework, so structural transfer is limited (CS, [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that high pressure is needed for tetrahedral alkali oxyosmates, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size and bonding contrast between large 8-coordinate Rb and short-bonded tetrahedral OsO₄, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check (target data, [Reasonable Inference]). The structure description does not indicate a large complex unit cell or mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing framework, so C2 and C3 are not triggered (target data). Structurally similar isolated tetraoxo salts crystallize readily as single crystals by evaporation or long solid-state treatment, which argues against severe amorphization (SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether the tetragonal I4₁/a polymorph competes with other alkali osmate polymorphs during crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbOsO₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest chemical evidence is that RbOsO₄ was already observed as an impurity during oxidizing RbOs₂O₆ synthesis, showing that the composition can form experimentally under accessible conditions (CS). Structural and thermodynamic analogues strongly support isolated tetrahedral alkali oxyanion chemistry, especially CsOsNO₃, CsTcO₄, Rb₂RuO₄, and K₂RuO₄ (SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high oxygen chemical potential or using a preformed high-valent osmium tetraoxo anion (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is safe and selective control of high-valent osmium chemistry, especially avoiding OsO₄ volatilization and competing Rb–Os oxide phases such as RbOs₂O₆ (CS, [Reasonable Inference]).
