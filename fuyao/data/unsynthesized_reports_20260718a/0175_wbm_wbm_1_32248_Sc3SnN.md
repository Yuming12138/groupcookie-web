### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the computed hull, so it receives a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues Lu₃InN, Tm₃InN, Tb₃InN, Zr₂InN, and Sc₂Sb are also on-hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), supporting a stable nitride/intermetallic phase field. The main uncertainty is that CS1 lists synthesized Ca₃SnN with a very high computed hull energy, so CS evidence is chemically encouraging but thermodynamically inconsistent with the target prediction.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits its condition profile from R₃InN syntheses using RN + R₂In, arc fusion, sealed ampoules, and 1220 K week-long annealing (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Its severity is penalized because it requires precursor synthesis, sealed ampoules/vacuum, air-sensitive Sc-bearing materials, and long annealing. Kinetically, the same anti-perovskite structure in R₃InN analogues helps structural formation (SS1–SS3), but dense ScN and Sc₂Sn are expected to diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from the direct Sc₃InB structural analogue, which was synthesized by arc melting and homogenization at 800 °C for In/Tl/Sn/Pb systems (CS3). Condition severity is moderate because arc melting is specialized but not high-pressure, and the sustained anneal can remain below 1000 °C. The main kinetic advantage is rapid homogenization through melting, but CS3 warns that component losses produced two- and three-phase alloys, making Sn/N stoichiometry the key uncertainty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is chemically motivated by Ca₃SnN, where the Sn-containing cubic phase required 1300 °C under N₂ and sealed Nb tubes (CS1), and by Ca₄In₂N synthesis under nitrogen at 1420 K (CS2). Its condition severity is higher because it uses high temperature, reactive Sc, and sealed refractory containment for Sn. Kinetically, direct nitridation is likely slower and less controllable than using ScN or prealloy precursors [Reasonable Inference], and competing ScN formation is a likely limitation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple 5-atom cubic Pm-3m cell, so the large-unit-cell amorphization criterion is not triggered. The structure contains corner- and face-sharing motifs but no edge-sharing coexistence in the target description, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not fully triggered. A cation size/role mismatch between Sc and Sn is treated as one trigger [Reasonable Inference], yielding a low–medium amorphization risk; direct CS/SS/TS amorphization evidence is insufficient and therefore neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₃SnN is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamically similar nitrides/intermetallics are also on-hull (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from same-structure cubic Pm-3m R₃InN analogues (SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3) and the same-Sc-sublattice anti-perovskite Sc₃InB analogue (CS3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of several experimentally synthesized anti-perovskite-like nitrides using sealed ampoule or arc-melt/homogenization methods (CS1, CS3, SS1–SS3). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of Sn volatility and nitrogen content, because component losses produced multiphase Sc₃AB alloys in CS3 and Sn-containing nitride phases required high-temperature sealed-tube methods in CS1. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed handling, mass-loss control, and repeated annealing.
