### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. Several SS/TS Os oxide analogues, including KOs₂O₆, LiOsO₃, RbOs₂O₆, Ca₂Os₂O₇, and Cd₂Os₂O₇, also have E_hull ≈ 0, supporting the stability of Os octahedral oxide frameworks. CS analogues include stable or near-stable oxides, but CS gives insufficient evidence for Os–N/O mixed-anion stability, so CS is only partially supportive and otherwise neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized heavily because high-pressure equipment and sealed capsules are required, matching HP KOs₂O₆ and LiOsO₃ evidence (SS/TS). The likely need for buffered O/N chemical potential and hazardous nitrogen sources is **[Reasonable Inference]** because no CS/SS/TS material contains nitrogen. The route is feasible but severe because HP Os oxide analogues require specialized capsules, multi-anvil/DAC equipment, and careful redox control (SS/TS).

#### F3 — Route 1

The target’s Os octahedral network is structurally close to KOs₂O₆/LiOsO₃-type OsO₆ frameworks, which improves the structural-reconstruction score (SS/TS). However, OsO₂/Os precursors are refractory, and no analogue proves facile nitrogen incorporation, lowering precursor activity and diffusion scores. The kinetic score is therefore moderate-low despite strong structural similarity.

#### F2 — Route 2

The sealed-ampoule route avoids external high pressure, improving severity relative to Route 1. However, SS/TS Os oxide syntheses using OsO₄ require vacuum-sealed ampoules, liquid-nitrogen cooling during sealing, and repeated firings, so volatile/toxic precursor and multi-step deductions apply. Adding a nitrogen source in a sealed system is **[Reasonable Inference]** and increases safety uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 2

Bulk ampoule synthesis is diffusion-limited because SS/TS analogues such as Cd₂Os₂O₇ require heating, opening, regrinding, resealing, and reheating. The target additionally requires N/O ordering, which is not evidenced in CS/SS/TS. This makes Route 2 kinetically less favorable than topochemical nitridation.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses ambient pressure and moderate temperatures, so its condition severity is better than HP synthesis. The main deductions come from reactive NH₃ atmosphere, multiple processing steps, and Os/NH₃ handling risks; NH₃ use is **[Reasonable Inference]**, while repeated precursor processing is supported by SS/TS Os oxide syntheses. CS provides insufficient evidence for ammonolysis of Os oxides and is neutral for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 3

Topochemical nitridation is kinetically attractive because it can preserve the Os octahedral framework rather than requiring full cation redistribution **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS analogues show that OsO₆ networks with similar bond lengths and tilt angles are stable, supporting a precursor-based route. The uncertainty is whether N can substitute into the robust Os–O framework without decomposition or over-reduction.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film growth avoids bulk diffusion and high pressure, but it requires controlled reactive O/N plasma or gas chemistry **[Reasonable Inference]**. No CS/SS/TS source reports thin-film growth, so direct evidence is insufficient from all three source classes and the route is scored cautiously. Os target preparation and possible Os oxide volatility/toxicity remain limiting factors, consistent with Os handling concerns in SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 4

Reactive thin-film growth can improve kinetic access to metastable or narrow-window phases **[Reasonable Inference]**. However, crystallization of this exact hydrophilite-derived P2₁ Os₂NO₃ phase is not supported by CS/SS/TS, and N/O stoichiometry control is likely difficult. The route receives a moderate kinetic score but lower confidence than Route 3.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Os, so the cation-size-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered. The structure has mixed corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, which triggers one amorphization/complex-connectivity risk; this connectivity is also present in SS/TS Os oxide analogues. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve that uncertainty, so C2 is treated as non-triggering/neutral.

Os₂NO₃ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from SS/TS analogues: several stable Os oxide frameworks contain OsO₆ octahedra with similar bond lengths, octahedral tilts, and edge/corner-sharing connectivity. The most critical enabling factor is the apparent thermodynamic stability of an Os-centered octahedral framework, reinforced by multiple E_hull = 0 Os oxide analogues. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will require careful control of oxygen and nitrogen chemical potentials.
