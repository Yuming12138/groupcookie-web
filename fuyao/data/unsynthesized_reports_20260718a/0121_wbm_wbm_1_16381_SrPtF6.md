### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.** SrPtF6 is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score (Target). This is reinforced by stable structurally similar CaPdF6 and CdPtF6, both E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and by near-stable Cs2PtF6 at ~0.0009 eV/atom (SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2, TS4). CS evidence is mixed because SrPbF6 is 0.162 eV/atom above hull while RbBiF6 and Ba(AuF4)2 are stable, so CS supports fluoride accessibility but is less definitive for thermodynamic stability (CS1, CS2, CS5).

**F2 — Route 1.** The main severity penalty is the need for elemental F₂, which is explicitly used in CaPdF6, SrPbF6, and RbBiF6 analogue syntheses (SS1/TS1, CS1, CS2). Temperature is comparatively mild because the best structural analogue CaPdF6 was made by direct fluorination around 100 °C (SS1/TS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Pt rather than Pd requires a higher fluorination temperature or longer hold, which is inferred from Pt-fluoride routes but not directly demonstrated for SrPtF6 [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2 and TS4].

**F3 — Route 1.** Kinetics are favorable because reactive fluorination can convert carbonate/chloride precursors at low temperature in CaPdF6 analogues (SS1/TS1). Structural reconstruction is strongly favorable because the target, CaPdF6, and CdPtF6 share corner-sharing octahedral R-3 Upper-Bainite frameworks with very similar octahedral tilt angles (Target, SS1, SS3/TS2). The uncertainty is that Sr2+ is larger than Ca2+/Cd2+, which may alter tilt stability or require annealing [Reasonable Inference from Target vs SS1/SS3 bond lengths].

**F2 — Route 2.** This route is more severe because Pt-tube routes for CdPtF6-related and Cs2PtF6 materials require high-temperature anneals of 700–800 °C for weeks after fluorination (SS3/TS2, TS4). F₂, dry Ar, sealed Pt tubes, and slow cooling all add operational complexity (SS3/TS2, TS4). The source evidence is strong for Pt fluoride crystallization but not specific to SrPtF6, so the transfer is plausible but uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.** Kinetics are slower than Route 1 because similar Pt fluoride crystal-growth routes required multi-week annealing and slow cooling (SS3/TS2, TS4). However, pre-fluorinated powders are chemically close to the target and should reduce the redox burden [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2 and TS4]. The likely benefit is improved crystallinity, but the cost is greater risk of competing phases or volatile Pt fluoride loss [Reasonable Inference; volatility concern supported indirectly by noble-metal fluoride handling in CS5].

**F2 — Route 3.** Autoclave fluorination avoids >1000 °C conditions but introduces pressurized F₂/Ar, which is a major handling hazard (SS4/TS3). The stated pressures up to 300 atm are high but below the scoring threshold of >5 GPa, so no high-pressure deduction is applied (SS4/TS3). Evidence for this exact target is indirect, so the route is ranked below Route 1 despite similar F2 score.

**F3 — Route 3.** Pressurized F₂/Ar can improve fluorination of less reactive oxide/carbonate mixtures, as shown for CaCrF6-type fluorides and Pt-fluoride autoclave steps (SS4/TS3, SS3/TS2). Kinetic uncertainty remains because the exact R-3 SrPtF6 product has not been demonstrated by autoclave synthesis, and subsequent annealing may be needed [Reasonable Inference]. The route is most useful if ambient-flow fluorination leaves incomplete Pt oxidation or mixed SrF2/Pt fluoride phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.** The target has only corner-sharing SrF6/PtF6 octahedra, which lowers glass/amorphization risk relative to mixed edge/face/corner networks (Target). One risk trigger is the large Sr–Pt cation-size mismatch, reflected in Sr–F 2.46 Å vs Pt–F 1.97 Å and standard ionic-size expectations [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The compact R-3 analogue family and simple connectivity argue against severe amorphization, but low-temperature fluorination could still yield poorly crystalline powders before tempering [Reasonable Inference from SS1/TS1].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrPtF6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (Target). The analogue support is strong: CaPdF6 and CdPtF6 share the same Upper-Bainite-derived R-3 corner-sharing octahedral motif, and CdPtF6 contains PtF6 octahedra with Pt–F distances close to the target (SS1, SS3/TS2, Target). The most important enabling factor is access to high-fluorine-chemical-potential conditions, since many relevant fluorides are made under F₂ streams or F₂/Ar mixtures (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2). The main limiting factor is practical rather than thermodynamic: safe, controlled fluorination without hydrolysis, incomplete fluorination, or Pt fluoride volatility (CS2, SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2; volatility partly [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, SrPtF6 appears synthetically plausible but requires specialized fluorine chemistry facilities.
