### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the upper edge of the prompt’s highest stability bin, so a high F1 score is justified. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable or worse hull energies are experimentally synthesized: TS2 has 0.0481 eV/atom, TS1 has 0.0777 eV/atom, TS3 has 0.0865 eV/atom, and TS5 has 0.0353 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is that TS analogues are not K–Os–O compounds, so they support metastability tolerance rather than direct chemical transferability.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because oxidative sealed-ampoule synthesis requires controlled oxygen or oxygen-buffered conditions, consistent with K4PbO4 being prepared under O2 in sealed Ag-Bombchen (CS1). Long sealed ampoule methods and air-sensitive alkali oxide precursors are common in CS1, CS2, SS1, and SS2. The main uncertainty is whether oxidizing conditions stabilize K6OsO6 or instead generate volatile Os oxides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The reactive KOx/K2O2 precursor set should have reasonable activity, supported by oxidized alkali oxide precursors in SS1 and SS2. However, SS1, SS2, CS1, and CS2 all require very long anneals, indicating slow cation ordering and reconstruction in alkali-rich oxometallates. The kinetics are therefore moderate-to-poor despite favorable reactivity.

#### F2 — Route 2

The inert sealed-tube route avoids explicit reactive gas penalties because Ar-sealed syntheses are common for alkali oxides in CS4 and SS2–SS5. It is still penalized for sealed volatile/air-sensitive alkali oxide handling, multi-step precursor preparation, and toxic/air-sensitive materials, consistent with CS2–CS4 and SS2–SS5. The major uncertainty is whether Os reaches +6 under nominally inert conditions; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly resolves this.

#### F3 — Route 2

Dry solid-state diffusion is the slowest route because binary oxide analogues often require 20–71 d at 570–800 °C, as shown by CS4 and SS2–SS4. The Os precursor is likely refractory and slow to reorganize [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction from K2O/OsO2 to cubic K6OsO6 is substantial, so F3 is lower than for the flux route.

#### F2 — Route 3

The flux route has the least severe condition profile because CS5 demonstrates K2O flux growth from KNO3 at 1000 °C under dry N2 without high pressure. It is penalized for volatile/corrosive K2O flux and toxic Os chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS specifically supports nitrate-flux synthesis of Os oxides, so uncertainty remains.

#### F3 — Route 3

KNO3-derived K2O flux gives high precursor activity and improved mass transport, as CS5 shows dissolution of ZrO2 in excess K2O at 1000 °C. Flux conditions should reduce the diffusion barrier compared with dry solid-state annealing [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS5 also notes phase-control difficulty in K2O flux systems, so structural selection and Os redox control remain significant uncertainties.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large size/charge mismatch between K+ and Os6+ triggers a radius-difference risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly contains mixed corner- and edge-sharing OK4Os units, triggering a connectivity risk. The unit cell atom count is not provided; based only on the formula and Fm-3m description, there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to trigger the >40 atom criterion.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K6OsO6 is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, and TS analogues show synthesized phases at similar or higher energies. The CS and SS data strongly support that very K-rich alkali oxometallates can be synthesized, usually by sealed-tube or flux methods with long anneals. The most critical enabling factor is the high K2O/KOx chemical activity, which can promote formation of unusual alkali-rich oxide frameworks, as supported by CS1–CS5 and SS1–SS4. The most critical limiting factor is Os redox/volatility control because none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates the K–Os–O system or an Os(VI) cubic K6MO6 analogue. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful oxygen buffering, sealed or covered containment, and extensive phase screening.
