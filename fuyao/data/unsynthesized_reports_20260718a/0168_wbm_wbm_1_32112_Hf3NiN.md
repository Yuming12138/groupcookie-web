### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated at **E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom**, so it receives a high thermodynamic score under the provided rubric. This is reinforced by the closest Cmcm analogue Hf3AlN and Zr3AlN, both reported at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). Hf–Ni-containing analogues Hf2NiP and Hf6NiSb2 are also reported stable at E_hull = 0.0, supporting compatibility of Hf–Ni bonding in stable ternaries (CS2 / SS3 / SS4 / TS3 / TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of lower-temperature competing phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires high-temperature melting under pure Ar, matching Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN analogue synthesis by high-frequency furnace and slow cooling (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). The supplied data do not give a temperature for that melt route, so the >1400 °C penalty is a **[Reasonable Inference]** based on melting a Hf-containing refractory mixture. No high pressure or reactive gas is required according to the analogues (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). The main severity uncertainty is whether HfN fully dissolves/reacts during melting without N loss **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from a liquid/high-temperature pathway, as the closest Hf3AlN/Zr3AlN analogues are obtained by melting and slow cooling (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). The structural reconstruction burden is low because the target and Hf3AlN share the same Cmcm-type arrangement with Hf/N octahedral networks (CS1 / SS1 / TS1). The main penalty is low precursor activity and refractory Hf/HfN diffusion before or during melting **[Reasonable Inference]**. Thus F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 inherits demanding conditions from Hf2NiP synthesis: arc melting, 1400 °C annealing, dynamic vacuum of 10^-6 bar, and in some cases Ta-tube annealing up to 6 days (CS2 / SS4 / TS4). Hf6NiSb2 also uses arc melting under Ar on a water-cooled copper hearth (SS3 / TS3). These data justify penalties for high temperature, controlled atmosphere/vacuum, and multi-step processing. The target avoids volatile P/Sb precursors, so no volatile-precursor penalty is applied for Hf3NiN **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Arc melting improves cation mixing and is proven for Hf–Ni ternary frameworks such as Hf2NiP and Hf6NiSb2 (CS2 / SS3 / SS4 / TS3 / TS4). However, the structural analogues are not Cmcm Hf3MN-type phases, so the reaction may crystallize competing Hf–Ni binaries or nitrides before reaching Hf3NiN **[Reasonable Inference]**. Annealing at 1400 °C helps homogenization, as shown for Hf2NiP single-crystal growth (CS2 / SS4 / TS4). The score remains moderate-low because refractory Hf/HfN kinetics and phase selection are limiting **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses lower nominal temperatures than the melt routes, supported by Zr2InN-type synthesis at 500–900 °C and rare-earth nitridoindide annealing at 1220 K (SS5 / TS5 / CS5). It nevertheless requires sealed ampoules, long annealing, and likely repeated grinding/reannealing, as analogues report long durations and phase-purity issues (SS5 / TS5). No high pressure is indicated in the supplied data (CS5 / SS5 / TS5). The main uncertainty is whether quartz is compatible with Hf/Ni/HfN at the needed temperature, so a Ta/Nb liner is recommended **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solid-state reaction from HfN + Hf + Ni is likely diffusion-limited because HfN and Hf are refractory **[Reasonable Inference]**. The analogous Zr2InN route requires 750–850 h and can leave mononitride impurity, directly indicating slow reaction and phase-purity risk (SS5 / TS5). The structural pathway is also less favorable because Zr2InN is hexagonal H-phase rather than the target Cmcm structure (SS5 / TS5). Therefore Route 3 receives the lowest F3 score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: large Hf/Ni cation size mismatch **[Reasonable Inference]**. The unit cell does not appear large based on the closest analogues Hf6Al2N2/Zr6Al2N2, and the target connectivity is corner + edge sharing without stated face sharing (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). Structurally similar Cmcm analogues crystallize successfully rather than amorphizing under melt growth (CS1 / SS1 / SS2 / TS1 / TS2). The remaining uncertainty is whether rapid quenching from melt could trap disorder; slow cooling should mitigate this **[Reasonable Inference]**.
