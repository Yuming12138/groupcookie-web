### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase and receives a high F1 score (target data). Several chemically similar Y or rare-earth transition-metal p-block compounds are also reported at E_hull = 0.0, including Y2Pd2In, Sr2Pd2In, YRhSn, and YPdSn (CS1, CS3, CS4, CS5). The closest same-structure Pr2Co2I analogue is also at E_hull = 0.0 (SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee experimental accessibility if Se volatility or competing Y–Co/Y–Se binaries dominate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids temperatures above 1000 °C because the closest Pr2Co2I analogue forms at 950 °C, which reduces thermal severity (SS1, TS1). The deductions come from required Ar glovebox/sealed Ta-tube handling, long multi-step thermal processing, and volatile/toxic Se management [Reasonable Inference; SS1, TS1]. CS data provide insufficient direct evidence for a Se-containing Y2Co2Se sealed-tube synthesis, so CS is treated as neutral for this route-specific volatility issue. TS reinforces Route 1 because the same Pr2Co2I analogue is the top thermodynamic match (TS1).

#### F3 — Route 1

The strongest kinetic advantage is that the same P6₃/mmc R2Co2X structure type exists experimentally for Pr2Co2I, so structural reconstruction should be feasible (SS1, TS1). The kinetic limitation is slow solid-state diffusion, as the analogue requires 5 days at 950 °C and 10 days annealing at 600 °C (SS1, TS1). CS provides only indirect support from Y intermetallics and insufficient evidence for the exact P6₃/mmc selenide kinetics, so CS is weighted less for Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because arc/induction melting and high-temperature sealed annealing are used in related Y-containing and Zr–Co 2:2:1 intermetallics (CS2, CS5, SS5, TS3). The need for high-purity Ar, evacuated/sealed ampoules, and remelting/annealing increases operational complexity (CS2, CS5, SS5, TS3). The main uncertainty is that CS analogues use In/Sn rather than Se, and those p-block elements are less problematic for melt retention than Se [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Melting improves homogenization and diffusion, as multiple analogues use arc or induction melting followed by annealing to form intermetallic phases (CS1, CS2, CS5, SS5, TS3). However, the exact target structure is not demonstrated by the CS melting examples, so the pathway may require post-melt ordering and may compete with Y–Co or Y–Se binaries [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides support that thermodynamically similar metallic systems can be prepared by arc melting and annealing, but insufficient direct evidence for Se retention during melting is available from TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has similar containment demands to Route 1 but may reduce free Se vapor by using Y₂Se₃/YSe rather than elemental Se [Reasonable Inference]. The closest analogue uses PrI3 + Pr + Co in a sealed Ta tube, which supports using a rare-earth anion-bearing precursor rather than a fully elemental mixture (SS1, TS1). CS data provide insufficient direct evidence for binary selenide precursor use, so CS is neutral for this route-specific precursor strategy.

#### F3 — Route 3

The binary selenide route improves selenium chemical potential control but still requires solid-state incorporation of Co into the P6₃/mmc framework, so kinetics remain moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals are justified by Pr2Co2I and Zr2Co2In, both of which require extended heating or annealing for phase formation/homogeneity (SS1, TS1, SS5, TS3). The route is judged slightly better than Route 1 overall because it reduces elemental Se loss risk while preserving the close sealed-tube structural analogy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a small, ordered hexagonal structure related to Pr2Co2I, whose formula in the database is Pr4Co4I2, suggesting a small cell rather than a >40-atom complex cell (SS1). The target description only reports edge-sharing SeY6 polyhedra and does not indicate mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral complexity, so the structural-topology amorphization trigger is not activated (target structure). One radius-mismatch trigger is applied because Y and Co are very different in size, but this is common in rare-earth cobalt intermetallics that are experimentally accessible (SS2, SS4; [Reasonable Inference]). Remaining uncertainty is that melt quenching or poor Se control could still produce amorphous or multiphase material, especially in Route 2 [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y2Co2Se is likely a **ground-state phase** because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The best support comes from the same-structure P6₃/mmc Pr2Co2I analogue, which is both structurally and thermodynamically similar and experimentally synthesized in sealed Ta tubes (SS1, TS1). Chemically similar Y-containing intermetallics show that Y–transition-metal–p-block phases can be made by arc/induction melting and annealing, but these analogues involve In/Sn rather than volatile Se (CS1, CS2, CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the exact R2Co2X structural precedent from Pr2Co2I (SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is selenium chemical-potential control during high-temperature synthesis, especially avoiding Se loss and competing Y–Se/Y–Co phases [Reasonable Inference].
