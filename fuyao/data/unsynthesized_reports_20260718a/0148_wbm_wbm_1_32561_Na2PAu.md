### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic Stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly places it in the highest stability bracket. Many close analogues are also on the hull and experimentally synthesized, including Li2AuSn, Na2MgPb, Na2AgSb, Na3AuS2, Na2CdSn, and Na3AuIn2 (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Na–P/Au–P phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because the closest Na analogues form at 600–700 °C in sealed Ar systems (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS3). Deductions are applied for volatile/sealed-system requirements and air-sensitive/toxic handling due to Na/P [Reasonable Inference], consistent with glovebox/sealed ampoule handling in Na3As and Na2CdSn analogues (SS3, SS4/TS3). Insufficient evidence from CS3 supports using high-pressure nitride conditions for Na2AuP, so CS3 is treated as neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Elemental synthesis is kinetically plausible because Na2AgSb, Na3As, Na2CdSn, and Na3AuIn2 were made from elements under sealed inert conditions (SS1/TS1, SS3, SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4). The main kinetic penalty is dense Au, although direct Au use is successful in Na3AuS2 and Na3AuIn2 (SS2/TS2, SS5/TS4). Structural reconstruction is aided by the Cmcm match to Na2AgSb (SS1/TS1), but slow cooling/annealing suggests ordering control is still needed.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The binary phosphide route stays in the moderate 600–650 °C range, inferred from Na3AuS2 at 600 °C and Na3As/Na2AgSb near 700 °C (SS2/TS2, SS3, SS1/TS1). It is penalized for being multi-step if Na3P must be prepared and for air-sensitive Na3P handling [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports Na3P + Au + P to Na2AuP, so that specific precursor combination carries extrapolation uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Na3P should improve Na–P chemical activity relative to separate elemental P [Reasonable Inference], analogous to Na2S acting as a reactive sodium chalcogenide precursor in Na3AuS2 (SS2/TS2). However, the target Cmcm lattice must be reconstructed from binary Na3P plus Au/P, so structural kinetics are less favorable than direct formation from a melt-like elemental mixture [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for binary phosphide-assisted Au chemistry is treated as neutral beyond the Na2S analogy.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The Na-rich flux route uses moderate temperatures but requires sealed handling, excess reactive Na, and multiple operations such as slow cooling and flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling and sealed Ta containment are supported by Na3AuS2 and Na3AuIn2 syntheses (SS2/TS2, SS5/TS4). Phase-control complexity is supported by quench/slow-cool sensitivity in Na3AuIn2 and homogeneity issues in Na2CdSn analogues (SS5/TS4, SS4/TS3).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten Na should strongly enhance transport and reaction with Au/P [Reasonable Inference], and melt/slow-cooling crystallization is experimentally supported in Na3AuS2 (SS2/TS2). The high F3 reflects liquid-assisted diffusion, but it is limited by likely competing Na-rich phosphides and cooling-rate-sensitive crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest direct support is SS2/TS2 and SS5/TS4; CS evidence is less specific but confirms alkali–Au intermetallic phases can be synthesized from elements under Ar (CS1, CS5).

**F4 — Amorphization Tendency.**  
The target has a relatively simple Cmcm coordination description with one Na, Au, and P site, and no evidence for a large complex cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network in the target description. A size/coordination mismatch between Na–P and Au–P bonding is present [Reasonable Inference], giving one amorphization trigger. Remaining uncertainty is that the actual conventional cell size is not explicitly given in the prompt, but the structural description does not suggest a >40-atom complex framework.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na2AuP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close Na/alkali–Au or Na–pnictide analogues with E_hull = 0 are experimentally synthesized (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS3). The analogue support is strong for sealed, inert, moderate-temperature synthesis, especially from Na2AgSb in Cmcm and Na3AuS2/Na3AuIn2 containing Na–Au–anion chemistry (SS1/TS1, SS2/TS2, SS5/TS4). The most critical enabling factor is sealed Ta/quartz inert containment, which allows volatile/reactive Na and P chemistry while preventing oxidation and loss [Reasonable Inference], as seen broadly in Na analogues (SS1/TS1, SS3, SS4/TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, because Na-rich systems can require excess alkali, quenching, or precise cooling to avoid competing phases (CS2, SS4/TS3, SS5/TS4). Overall, the target should be synthesizable by conventional solid-state methods, but it will likely require careful ampoule design and air-free handling.
