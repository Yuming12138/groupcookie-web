### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored very high. This is reinforced by multiple chemically similar stable intermetallics with E_hull = 0.0, including Hf₅Re₂₄, WIr₃, UIr₃, and HfPt₃ (CS-1 to CS-4), and by stable structurally/thermodynamically similar Ta-containing phases (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee that the specific ordered Pm-3n Ta₃Ir phase is easily accessed kinetically.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, arc-melting + anneal.**  
Condition severity is moderate because analogous refractory intermetallics require high temperature, repeated melting, and annealing, but no high pressure or volatile component is required (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5; SS-3/TS-3). Kinetics are the main limitation: Ta and Ir are refractory metals [Reasonable Inference], so diffusion and ordering can be slow, but arc melting and repeated remelts directly address homogeneity as shown in CS-5 and SS-3/TS-3. This is the best-supported route because it matches the chemistry and processing style of TaIrSi and WIr₃ analogues (CS-2; SS-3/TS-3).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, vacuum/inert sintering.**  
The conditions are more severe than simple solid-state synthesis because vacuum/high vacuum and ≥1400 °C temperatures are likely needed, consistent with CS-4 and SS-1/TS-1. Kinetically, this route is weak because dense Ta and Ir particles must interdiffuse without a liquid phase [Reasonable Inference], and analogous sintering data reports heterogeneity/metastability or need for additional annealing (SS-1/TS-1; CS-4). It is plausible but less attractive than arc melting.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, melt growth / controlled cooling.**  
This route inherits high-temperature and multi-step penalties from the controlled-cooling melt-growth analogue at 1500 °C (SS-5/TS-5). It may improve crystallinity and ordering relative to sintering, but the need for slow cooling and impurity control is a real concern because the analogous Ta₃Al₂CoC route reports impurity formation and crystal-growth difficulty (SS-5/TS-5). Its feasibility depends on the Ta–Ir liquidus/solidification behavior, which is not provided by CS/SS/TS.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, thin film + anneal.**  
This route has lower thermal severity by design, but there is insufficient direct thin-film evidence from CS, SS, and TS, so the evidence base is neutral. The kinetic score is relatively favorable only by [Reasonable Inference] because nanoscale multilayers reduce diffusion distances, while crystallization into the ordered Pm-3n phase remains unconfirmed. It is a useful exploratory route but not the primary recommendation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple binary metallic compound in cubic Pm-3n with edge/face-sharing IrTa₁₂ cuboctahedra, not a large, complex mixed-anion framework. The radius mismatch and unit-cell-size checks are not triggered by [Reasonable Inference], and the target description does not show simultaneous vertex+edge+face sharing. Therefore amorphization risk is low, although rapid quenching from arc melting could still produce disorder rather than full A15 ordering [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₃Ir is likely a ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally similar references are also hull-stable (CS-1 to CS-4; SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The CS evidence is especially supportive because refractory/noble-metal intermetallics such as WIr₃ and Hf–Re phases are synthesized by arc melting and annealing (CS-1, CS-2), while SS/TS add Ta–Ir-containing arc-melt precedent through TaIrSi (SS-3/TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the absence of volatile anions and the ability to homogenize the binary alloy by arc melting, as shown in multiple metallic analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is slow ordering/homogenization in refractory Ta–Ir alloys, requiring repeated remelting and annealing, analogous to the remelting and phase-control issues in CS-2, CS-5, and SS-5/TS-5. Overall, bulk synthesis is feasible but likely requires careful metallurgical processing rather than a simple powder reaction.
