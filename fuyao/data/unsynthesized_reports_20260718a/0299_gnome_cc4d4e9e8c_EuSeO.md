### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is scored at 98/100 under the provided rubric (target data). CS and SS analogues include stable or near-stable Eu/rare-earth oxyhalides and oxyselenides, including EuCuOSe at 0.044 eV/atom and several Matlockite oxyhalides at 0.0–0.064 eV/atom, supporting that nearby Eu–O–anion frameworks are energetically plausible (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4). TS analogues are stable Se-rich compounds but are chemically distant from EuSeO; therefore TS is only indirect support and insufficient evidence from TS for target-specific Eu–O–Se stability. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the calculated orthorhombic Matlockite-like EuSeO competes experimentally with EuO, Eu₂O₃, EuSe, or EuSe₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 750–850 °C sealed-ampoule conditions, which are comparable to EuCuOSe at 1123 K for 10 days and M₄O₄Se[Se₂] at 750 °C for 168 h (SS1, CS2/CS3). The main deductions are for vacuum/inert sealed handling, Se volatility, and Eu/Se hazards, all consistent with glovebox and sealed-ampoule use in analogues (SS1, CS2/CS3). TS analogues support the need for sealed tubes in Se-rich reactions but do not provide Eu–O–Se-specific condition optimization, so TS is partially useful but not decisive (TS1–TS5). The route avoids high pressure and >1000 °C operation, keeping severity moderate.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetic penalty comes from Eu₂O₃ being a relatively refractory oxide and from the long solid-state holds needed in EuCuOSe and rare-earth oxyselenide analogues (SS1, CS2/CS3). Eu metal and Se should improve reactivity relative to an all-oxide route [Reasonable Inference], and the target’s Eu–O/Eu–Se local coordination resembles EuCuOSe, reducing structural mismatch (SS1). CS2/CS3 show related Se–Se oxyselenides form at 750 °C, but CS3 also reports failed Eu/Gd attempts in that family, indicating uncertain Eu-specific kinetics/phase selection. TS provides general evidence that Se–Se networks can crystallize in sealed ampoules but insufficient evidence for EuSeO-specific kinetics (TS1–TS5).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more operationally complex because it uses a flux, sealed ampoule, slow cooling, and likely flux removal, as seen in CsI-assisted rare-earth oxyselenides and rare-earth oxybromide flux growth (CS2–CS5). It remains below 1000 °C, so there is no high-temperature deduction beyond controlled-atmosphere and sealed-system requirements (CS2/CS3/CS5). Se and SeO₂ introduce volatility/toxicity risks, and Eu metal requires inert handling [Reasonable Inference supported by Ar loading in CS2/CS3]. SS gives only indirect support through structurally related phases and does not directly validate a EuSeO flux route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux should improve mass transport and crystallinity, supported by CsI use in rare-earth oxyselenides and bromide flux growth of Matlockite oxybromides (CS2–CS5). The kinetic score is limited because the closest CS oxyselenide family is M₄O₄Se[Se₂], not EuSeO, and Eu/Gd attempts reportedly failed in that family (CS3). SS supports the layered Eu–O–anion motif but does not show flux growth of EuSeO itself, so SS is structurally helpful but kinetically incomplete (SS1–SS4). TS provides only generic sealed-flux/selenide behavior and is insufficient evidence from TS for this Eu oxyselenide flux chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate EuOCl/EuOBr precursor temperatures, with EuOCl made at 450/750 °C under N₂ and EuOBr made near 350 °C in dry air (SS4, SS2). The conversion step requires sealed/inert selenization and multiple operations, so the severity score is reduced despite the moderate temperature [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides related oxyhalide flux chemistry but not direct topochemical selenization, so CS is only indirect support (CS4/CS5). TS is insufficient evidence for halide-to-selenide topochemical conversion in Eu–O layers.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

This route has attractive kinetics because EuOBr/EuOCl already contain the Matlockite Eu–O layer and OEu₄ tetrahedra, closely matching the target Eu–O substructure (SS2, SS4). The major uncertainty is whether Br⁻/Cl⁻ sites can be replaced by a Se-bearing sublattice with a short Se–Se bond, which is not shown in the supplied references [Reasonable Inference]. CS oxybromide flux growth supports structural compatibility of rare-earth oxyhalides, but not the required conversion chemistry (CS4/CS5). Therefore the kinetic score is good but capped by lack of direct evidence.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Route 4 requires high vacuum or sealed-tube transport, similar to EuIO sublimation at 800 °C under high vacuum (CS1, SS3). Se volatility and potential halogen transport chemistry add safety and stoichiometry challenges [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed Se chemistry in SS1/CS2/CS3/TS1–TS5]. The route remains below 1000 °C and does not require high pressure, but it is multi-step and more equipment-sensitive than Route 1. TS supports sealed high-temperature Se reactions but does not confirm vapor transport of Eu–O–Se species.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

Vapor transport can bypass bulk diffusion limitations, and EuIO demonstrates that Eu oxyiodide-like material can be sublimed/grown under high vacuum (CS1, SS3). However, EuSeO-specific volatile species are not demonstrated, and Se loss or conversion to Eu oxyhalides is a significant risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS and CS support the broader Eu–O–anion structural family, but not direct transport of EuSeO. Thus this route is best viewed as a crystal-growth follow-up after Route 1 establishes phase formation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Eu, so cation-size mismatch does not apply (target data). The reported structure is a simple EuSeO formula with corner- and edge-sharing OEu₄ tetrahedra; no face-sharing or large unit-cell complexity is reported, so no amorphization trigger is activated (target data). CS/SS analogues are crystalline layered oxyhalides/oxyselenides, supporting that related Eu/rare-earth O–anion frameworks crystallize rather than vitrify (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS4). The remaining uncertainty is the exact Cmce cell size from the CIF, but the provided description gives insufficient evidence for a >40-atom cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

EuSeO is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The strongest support comes from SS1, where EuCuOSe shares Eu–O and Eu–Se coordination motifs with the target, and from CS2/CS3, where rare-earth oxyselenides with Se–Se bonds form in sealed ampoules. The most critical enabling factor is sealed-ampoule control of Se chemical potential, because Se-bearing analogues repeatedly use sealed evacuated or inert ampoules (SS1, CS2/CS3, TS1–TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition/redox control for Eu, especially because CS3 reports failed Eu/Gd attempts in a related rare-earth oxyselenide family. Overall, synthesis is plausible but not trivial, with best odds from a carefully buffered Eu/Eu₂O₃/Se sealed-tube solid-state route.
