### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it exactly at the high-score boundary specified in the prompt. Thermodynamic analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally synthesized or reported, including CeSi5 at 0.058 eV/atom (TS1), Zr2CuSi4 at 0.0587 eV/atom (TS4), and YSi2 at 0.0626 eV/atom (TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that many close Si-rich rare-earth silicides are metastable high-pressure phases despite moderate hull energies (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2).

#### F2 — Route 1, high-pressure multi-anvil

This route is severe because close analogues require 8–15 GPa and high temperatures, with quenching before decompression in CeSi5-type systems (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2). The precursors and steps are manageable because binary silicides plus Si are established for CeSi5/YSi3/LaSi5 analogues (CS2–CS4; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2). Uncertainty remains over whether Zr incorporation stabilizes the phase enough to avoid the highest-pressure conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1, high-pressure multi-anvil

Reaction kinetics are moderate: powder silicides and Si are better than massive elements but still require solid-state diffusion and Si-network reconstruction (CS2–CS4; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS2). High pressure helps stabilize dense Si-rich connectivities, as shown by CeSi5, YSi3, LaSi5, and EuSi6 (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2). The main uncertainty is the formation of an ordered ternary Y/Zr sublattice rather than binary Y–Si or Zr–Si phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2, arc melting + anneal

This route avoids high pressure and is therefore less severe than Route 1, with direct ambient-pressure precedent in BaRh2Si9 and Zr/Hf silicide analogues (CS1; SS4; TS3–TS4). It still requires high temperature, inert atmosphere, repeated melting, and long annealing, as BaRh2Si9 needed eight weeks at 950 °C and Zr/Hf analogues needed 14 days at 800–1100 °C (CS1; SS4; TS3–TS4). The uncertainty is whether the target is an ambient-pressure phase or a high-pressure metastable phase like several closest Si-rich rare-earth silicides (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2).

#### F3 — Route 2, arc melting + anneal

Kinetics are limiting because the closest arc-melt analogue BaRh2Si9 initially formed multiple phases and required long annealing for near-single-phase material (CS1; SS4). Zr/Hf silicide analogues also require prolonged annealing for homogenization (TS3–TS4), indicating slow diffusion/order formation. The target’s cubic YZrSi6 framework likely requires reconstructive ordering from a melt-solidified microstructure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3, thin-film co-deposition

Thin-film processing has lower thermal severity because TS5 reports rare-earth silicide film formation/annealing at 423–773 K under vacuum. However, vacuum deposition, multi-step processing, and air-sensitive Y sources impose moderate severity (TS5; [Reasonable Inference]). CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for thin-film processing of close structural analogues, so that part of the route-specific evidence is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3, thin-film co-deposition

Vapor availability is acceptable because Y, Zr, and Si can be sputtered or evaporated [Reasonable Inference], and TS5 demonstrates rare-earth/Si film processing. Crystallization and composition control are uncertain because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates ternary YZrSi6 film crystallization, and a 1:1:6 ternary phase may have a narrow composition window [Reasonable Inference]. Thin-film nonequilibrium processing may help access metastable phases, but that is an extrapolation rather than directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one likely amorphization trigger: a significant Y/Zr cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description does not show an atom count above 40 or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so the score remains relatively favorable. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YZrSi6 is likely **near-metastable rather than a robust ground-state phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and many close Si-rich rare-earth silicides require high-pressure stabilization and quenching (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2). The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: high-pressure Si-rich RE silicides are strongly represented, and Zr-containing silicides are thermodynamically comparable, though not identical in structure (TS3–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the dense Si-rich framework compatibility, with Y–Si and Si–Si distances comparable to YSi3/CeSi5/EuSi6 analogues (CS2–CS5; SS1–SS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and ordering kinetics, as chemically similar arc products can require very long annealing and high-pressure analogues are metastable (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5; TS1–TS2). Overall, the material is experimentally plausible but likely requires either high-pressure quenching or carefully optimized nonequilibrium/long-anneal processing.
