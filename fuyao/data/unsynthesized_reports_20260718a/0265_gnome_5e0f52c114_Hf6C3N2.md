### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic score bin. Several close Hf–C analogues, including Hf2InC, HfC, Hf2SnC, Hf2TlC, and Hf2PbC, are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in SS/TS, supporting stability of related Hf carbide bonding motifs (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). However, CS/SS/TS do not include a direct Hf–C–N analogue, so the stability of ordered C/N sublattices is supported primarily by the target hull value rather than experimental analogue evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: solid-state annealing

The solid-state route is supported by Hf2InC/Hf2SnC analogues synthesized in sealed ampoules by long annealing, grinding, pressing, and re-annealing (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4, CS5). Its condition severity is high because the target likely needs >1400 °C to overcome refractory HfC/HfN diffusion, while analogues already require long anneals of 150–350 h even for simpler H-phases (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS4). Kinetics are the main weakness because HfC is rock-salt and refractory (SS2, TS2), and C/N ordering into monoclinic Hf6C3N2 requires more reconstruction than retaining a simple HfC-like lattice [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct nitridation or carbonitride ordering, so that uncertainty is treated as neutral 50 where source-specific support is absent.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: arc-melting + annealing

Arc-melting is strongly supported for refractory carbide systems: Pu–W–C and Th–Ru–C analogues were made by arc-melting under Ar and annealing at high temperature (CS2–CS4). This route scores better kinetically because melting/remelting improves homogeneity and bypasses some solid-state diffusion barriers, as implied by successful arc-melted carbide formation in CS2/CS3. The limiting uncertainty is nitrogen retention and ordering, because CS2–CS4 are carbides rather than carbonitrides; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Hf–C–N arc synthesis is therefore neutral 50 for that specific factor. The route remains best overall because it balances severe but feasible conditions with better mass transport than sealed-pellet annealing.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: hot pressing / SPS

Hot pressing is supported by HfC and Hf-containing H-phase analogues synthesized at 1200–2000 °C with hot pressing and/or sealed-tube homogenization (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS5). The pressure is not extreme by the scoring definition because analogue pressures are ~100 atm or conventional hot-press conditions, far below >5 GPa (SS3, SS5, TS3, TS5). Kinetics are better than simple annealing because of enhanced contact and densification, but the short hot-press dwell may not allow C/N ordering without a post-anneal [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is again the absence of direct nitrogen-containing analogues in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: molten-salt / flux

Flux synthesis is supported by Ta MAX-like carbides made at 1400–1500 °C using excess Al or Al/Sn melt under Ar, followed by flux removal (CS1). This suggests that a flux can lower effective transport barriers for refractory carbide growth, so its kinetics score is competitive [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target contains nitrogen and Hf, and CS1 does not demonstrate nitrogen transport or Hf carbonitride formation, so evidence for N retention is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and scored neutral 50 for that aspect. Flux compatibility with Hf and HfN is the main unresolved risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Hf, so cation-size disorder is absent by composition. The structure description shows corner- and edge-sharing HfC3N2 pyramids and CHf6/NHf6 octahedra, but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the specified mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met. The formula Hf6C3N2 is not intrinsically large, and no >40-atom unit cell is provided; thus the unit-cell trigger is not applied [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that low-symmetry monoclinic C2 ordering could still slow crystallization even if it does not meet the formal amorphization triggers.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf6C3N2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The analogue support is moderate: CS/SS/TS strongly support synthesis of refractory Hf carbides and related metal carbides, but they do not directly demonstrate Hf carbonitride formation or C/N ordering. The most critical enabling factor is the strong local compatibility of Hf–C bonding, since HfC and Hf-containing H-phases have Hf–C distances close to the target Hf–C distances (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: refractory HfC/HfN precursors and monoclinic C/N ordering may require high temperature and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical route is arc-melting followed by high-temperature annealing because it offers the strongest mass transport among the supported bulk routes (CS2–CS4).
