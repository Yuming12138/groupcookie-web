### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom from the supplied target data, placing it in the highest thermodynamic stability category. Thermodynamic analogues include several E_hull = 0 compounds, such as Hf8FeTe6, Ti5CuSb3, Nb3AsTe3, Ti2P, and Ti3Te4 (TS1–TS5). This strongly suggests the target is not merely a high-energy hypothetical phase, although finite-temperature entropy, off-stoichiometry, and DFT hull limitations remain uncertainties [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS also provide positive but less direct signals, with Nb3AsTe3 at E_hull = 0 (CS1) and Hf8FeTe6/Ti5CuSb3 at E_hull = 0 among structurally similar entries (SS2, SS4).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a moderate severity score because all key analogues can be made in sealed evacuated ampoules without high pressure (CS1/TS3, SS1, TS5). The deductions come from the need for high vacuum, sealed Te containment, repeated grinding/heating, and Te toxicity/volatility [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed-tube use in CS1/TS3, SS1, TS5]. CS provides strong support for Nb–Te sealed synthesis (CS1), SS provides strong support for Ti–Te sealed synthesis (SS1), and TS provides strong support for Ti3Te4 phase formation but also warns of thermal-history sensitivity (TS5). Insufficient evidence from CS2/CS3/CS4 for Ti–Nb–Te specifically is treated as neutral rather than negative.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are the main weakness for Route 1 because elemental Ti and Nb are refractory and likely diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference]. Ti–Te analogues require staged heating, grinding, long treatments, quenching, or composition-dependent protocols (SS1, TS5), which supports a low-to-moderate kinetics score. Nb3AsTe3 synthesis at 1273 K for 10 days shows Nb–Te bonding is accessible but still requires prolonged sealed heating (CS1/TS3). The remaining uncertainty is whether Ti4NbTe4 nucleates readily or is outcompeted by TiTe/Ti3Te4/Nb–Te binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because arc-melting introduces very high local temperatures and requires purified/gettered Ar plus a subsequent sealed anneal (SS5, CS5). Arc-melting is well established for Ti-containing intermetallics and antimonides (SS5, CS5), and Hf–Fe–Te uses related high-temperature arc/capsule processing (SS2/TS1). However, Te is more volatile than Sb under arc conditions [Reasonable Inference], so Te loss is a serious penalty even though Sb loss compensation is explicitly discussed in CS5. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for open arc-melting of Ti–Nb–Te itself increases uncertainty but does not make the route impossible.

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc-melting improves kinetics by bypassing slow Ti/Nb solid-state diffusion and producing a homogenized ingot [Reasonable Inference], consistent with arc-melt/anneal methods for Ti5RhSb2 and related phases (SS5). Subsequent annealing at ~800 °C for 10 days is used to homogenize arc-melted samples (SS5), supporting moderate crystallization kinetics. Phase-purity evidence is mixed: some arc-melted analogues are near single phase, while others retain impurities or require special handling (SS5, CS5, SS2/TS1). The main uncertainty is whether Te loss during melting shifts the sample away from Ti4NbTe4 stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has intermediate severity because it requires pre-synthesized binary tellurides and a sealed refractory capsule but avoids open arc-melting and extreme pressure. The route is supported by ZrTe formation from ZrTe2 + Zr in an Ar-filled Ta tube inside an evacuated outer tube (SS3), and by Hf–Fe–Te processing in Nb capsules inside silica ampoules (SS2/TS1). The temperature is high but below the most severe Mo chalcogenide range of 1300–1500 °C (CS2), and the sealed capsule is a standard high-temperature telluride strategy in the provided evidence (SS3, SS2/TS1). Te volatility and toxicity still require deductions [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed Te syntheses in SS1/TS5].

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 receives the best kinetics among sealed routes because Ti–Te local bonding is pre-formed in TiTe/Ti3Te4, and binary chalcogenide precursors are proven useful in related telluride syntheses (SS1, TS5, SS3, CS4). The target Ti–Te distances of 2.80–2.81 Å closely match TiTe and Ti3Te4 Ti–Te distances, supporting local structural compatibility (SS1, TS5). Nb incorporation remains the kinetic bottleneck because Nb is refractory and must enter an ordered Ti/Nb/Te framework [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control uncertainty persists because Ti–Te analogues show narrow composition ranges and strong thermal-history dependence (TS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target appears to have a relatively low amorphization tendency because Ti and Nb are chemically similar early transition metals with comparable cation sizes [Reasonable Inference], and the reported target structure is ordered tetragonal I4/m with only one Ti, one Nb, and one Te site in the supplied description. Structurally similar analogues sometimes contain mixed polyhedral connectivity (SS1, SS4, TS5), but this is not sufficient to trigger C3 for the target itself. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on exact target cell size is treated neutrally, not as a penalty.
