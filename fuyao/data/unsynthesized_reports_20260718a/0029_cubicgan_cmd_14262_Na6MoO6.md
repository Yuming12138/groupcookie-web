### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly within the highest rubric bin of ≤0.05 eV/atom, so a score of 90 is appropriate. CS analogues are mostly reported at E_hull = 0.0, including Na₅NbO₅, Na₄TiO₄, Li₄MoO₅, Na₄GeO₄, and Na₄SnO₃ (CS1–CS5). SS/TS analogues show synthesizable materials with small positive hull energies, such as Na₅MoNO₄ at 0.012 eV/atom (SS1) and Na₃MoO₃N at 0.032 eV/atom (TS5). The uncertainty is that the target is at the stability threshold and may be metastable rather than a true ground state.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, consistent with Na₄TiO₄ at 750 °C (CS2), Li₄MoO₅ at 950 °C (CS3), and Na₄WO₅/KNa₃WO₅ at 725–750 °C (SS4). The main deductions are sealed-system requirement, multi-step handling/sealing/annealing, and air-sensitive alkali oxides, all supported by CS2, CS3, and SS4. Insufficient evidence from TS for a direct pure-oxide sealed Na₆MoO₆ route, so TS is neutral for this route-specific severity signal.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Dry oxide/molybdate diffusion is expected to be slow because analogous materials require 28–52 d anneals (CS2, CS3, SS4). Alkali oxide excess may provide some fluxing and improve diffusion, [Reasonable Inference] from Na₂WO₄ + Na₂O and Li₂O + MoO₃ reactions (SS4, CS3). The structural reconstruction score is limited because no analogue reports the exact cubic Fm-3m Na₆MoO₆ topology; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for direct structural inheritance.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Flux growth is temperature-intensive but not beyond the rubric’s >1000 °C threshold if held at 950–1000 °C, as in CS1 and CS3. It requires sealed Ni/Au/quartz hardware, dry Ar, long heat treatments, and air-sensitive alkali oxides (CS1, CS3). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Li/Na oxide flux growth of pure Na₆MoO₆, so their source score is neutral for this specific route.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Flux/self-flux conditions improve precursor activity and diffusion compared with dry sintering, supported by Na₅NbO₅-type flux growth (CS1) and Li₄MoO₅ formation from Li₂O/MoO₃ (CS3). However, the very long 28–75 d durations in CS1 and CS3 indicate slow nucleation and ordering. The major uncertainty is possible Li incorporation or competing Li–Mo–O phases if Li₂O is used, [Reasonable Inference] from Li-containing flux chemistry in CS1/CS3.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 is lower temperature, based on Na₅MoO₄N at 500 °C (SS1) and Na₃MoO₃N at 650 °C (TS5). It still requires reactive/air-sensitive sodium reagents, sealed containers, vacuum drying, grinding/pelletizing, and controlled heating (SS1, TS5). CS gives insufficient evidence for peroxide/azide activation of pure Na-rich molybdates, so CS is neutral for this route-specific signal.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Reactive Na₂O₂/NaN₃ chemistry gives high precursor activity, as Na–Mo frameworks form at 500–650 °C in SS1 and TS5. Kinetic uncertainty is high because SS1 and TS5 form oxynitrides rather than a pure oxide, so nitrogen-free Na₆MoO₆ may require modified oxygen activity and post-annealing, [Reasonable Inference]. The route scores best kinetically but is less directly proven structurally.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has distinct Na and Mo coordination environments and a likely 52-atom conventional Fm-3m cell, [Reasonable Inference] from the formula and F-centered cubic symmetry, which triggers two amorphization checks. The reported target connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered. CS/SS/TS analogues provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization of this exact phase, so they are neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₆MoO₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than an unambiguously robust ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports synthesis of Na/alkali-rich oxides using sealed alkali-oxide methods, and the SS/TS evidence supports reactive Na–Mo chemistry at moderate temperatures, but no analogue directly confirms cubic Fm-3m Na₆MoO₆. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of highly basic, Na-rich sealed-tube chemistry that can stabilize unusual alkali-rich oxometallates, supported by CS1–CS3 and SS4. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against stable Na₂MoO₄/MoO₃-derived side phases and possible structural relaxation to lower-symmetry Na–Mo–O frameworks, [Reasonable Inference] because analogues often need long anneals and careful stoichiometry (CS3, SS4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require iterative sealed-tube optimization rather than a one-shot ceramic reaction.
