### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic synthesizability. Thermodynamic analogues include stable MnMoN₂ and MgMoN₂ at E_hull = 0 (TS-1, TS-4), and near-hull Mo nitrides (TS-2, TS-3, TS-5), supporting that Mo/W nitrides can be thermodynamically competitive. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct nitrogen chemical potential during synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, NH₃ ammonolysis.**  
Condition severity is moderate because FeWN₂/MnMoN₂ and CrWN₂ analogues form at 625–750 °C under NH₃ without high pressure (SS-2, SS-3, TS-1). Kinetics are the main penalty: Mo and W are refractory, and the target requires ordered Mo/W distribution, while MnMoN₂/FeWN₂ reports narrow temperature windows for pure products (SS-2, TS-1). The route is nevertheless the most evidence-backed bulk route because several structurally/thermodynamically similar nitrides are made by ammonolysis (SS-2 to SS-4, TS-1, TS-2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2, high-pressure synthesis.**  
Condition severity is high because related W₂N₃ and MoN require 5–6 GPa and high temperatures up to 1800 K or higher (SS-1, SS-5, TS-3). Kinetics improve through high-temperature densification and possible ordering, as shown by ordered MoN obtained after HP annealing (SS-5, TS-3). The uncertainty is whether Mo₃WN₆ is truly a pressure-stabilized phase or merely an ambient-pressure ground state that does not require HP treatment.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, azide autoclave.**  
Severity is moderate because MgMoN₂ was synthesized at 700 °C in a sealed autoclave using NaN₃, avoiding GPa pressure (TS-4). Kinetics are poor for the target because direct reaction of Mo/W metals with nitrogen from NaN₃ requires diffusion through refractory metals and ordering of two similar cations [Reasonable Inference]. This route is attractive as a high-nitrogen-chemical-potential method but has weaker transferability than Route 1.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, thin film.**  
The numerical route score is high because thin films reduce diffusion length and avoid bulk high-pressure equipment [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS entry reports thin-film crystallization of this structure, so the evidence basis is weak and the route receives an expert penalty. It may be useful for screening but is less reliable for bulk phase confirmation.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing Mo/W nitride polyhedra, which triggers one amorphization/kinetic-frustration criterion from the provided target structure and is also common in similar nitrides (SS-2, SS-3, SS-4, TS-1, TS-2). Mo and W have similar chemistry, so cation size mismatch is not expected to trigger amorphization [Reasonable Inference]. The conventional cell atom count is not given, so C2 is uncertain and treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mo₃WN₆ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Mo-containing nitrides in the TS set are stable or near-stable (TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest synthetic support comes from SS/TS ammonolysis analogues, especially FeWN₂/MnMoN₂ and CrWN₂, which show that W/Mo-containing ternary nitrides can be made under flowing NH₃ at 625–750 °C (SS-2, SS-3, TS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high nitrogen chemical potential from NH₃ or NaN₃, because nitride formation rather than oxide/chalcogenide formation is the central chemical requirement (SS-2, SS-3, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is slow Mo/W cation ordering and phase selectivity, since analogues report narrow temperature windows or require high-pressure annealing for ordered nitride phases (SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-3). Overall, the material appears synthesizable, but likely requires careful precursor homogenization and temperature-window optimization.
