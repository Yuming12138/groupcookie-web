### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the top boundary of the 90–100 scoring range specified in the prompt. Thermodynamic analogues CeSi₅ and NaSi₆ have comparable E_hull values of 0.058 and 0.062 eV/atom and are reported as synthesizable high-pressure phases (TS). BaRh₂Si₉ is an on-hull chemically/structurally similar analogue synthesized by arc melting and annealing (CS, SS). The main uncertainty is whether the cubic CaRuSi₆ polymorph is the experimentally selected phase rather than a nearby competing Ca–Si/Ru–Si assemblage [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids multigigapascal pressure and is supported by the closest BaRh₂Si₉ analogue, which used arc melting plus 950 °C annealing under Ar (CS, SS). Its penalties come from multi-step processing, sealed/inert handling, and expected Ca/Ca-silicide air sensitivity or volatility [Reasonable Inference]. TS also supports the general arc-melt/anneal approach for a ternary silicide, Hf₂CuSi₄, at 800–1100 °C for 14 days (TS). The uncertainty is that Ba is less volatile/reactive than Ca under some synthesis conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Arc melting should improve reaction kinetics by pre-alloying the components, as used for BaRh₂Si₉ and Hf₂CuSi₄ analogues (CS, SS, TS). However, BaRh₂Si₉ required eight weeks to reach nearly single phase after multiphase as-cast products, indicating slow ordering and competing phases (CS, SS). Ru is refractory and likely slows equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 1 has moderate rather than high kinetic feasibility.

#### F2 — Route 2

High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported by many Si-rich silicide analogues: SrSi₆, NaSi₆, EuSi₆, CeSi₅, LaSi₅, and YSi₃ all require 2–15 GPa and high temperatures (CS, SS, TS). The severe F2 penalty is therefore unavoidable because pressure >5 GPa dominates the practical difficulty. Capsule loading, quenching, and inert handling are also common in the analogues (CS, SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether CaRuSi₆ actually requires high pressure; the closest ternary Rh analogue does not (CS, SS).

#### F3 — Route 2

High pressure is favorable for forming dense or unusual Si-rich networks, as demonstrated by SrSi₆, NaSi₆, EuSi₆, CeSi₅, LaSi₅, and YSi₃ (CS, SS, TS). Kinetics can still be limited by refractory Ru diffusion unless Ru silicide precursors are used [Reasonable Inference]. The analogues show that short high-pressure holds can be sufficient, e.g. EuSi₆ at 15 min and SrSi₆ at at least 60 min (SS, CS/TS). The uncertainty is the lack of a direct Ca–Ru–Si high-pressure precedent in the provided data.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses ambient pressure and moderate annealing temperatures, so condition severity is lower than Route 2. Its penalties arise from sealed handling, Ca-containing precursor sensitivity, and multiple anneal/regrind steps [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals are consistent with BaRh₂Si₉ and Hf₂CuSi₄ analogues (CS, SS, TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a directly demonstrated CaRuSi₆ solid-state binary-silicide route, so this route is less certain.

#### F3 — Route 3

Preformed Ca–Si and Ru–Si precursors could reduce reaction enthalpy and shorten diffusion paths [Reasonable Inference], and binary silicide + Si routes are common among SrSi₆, LaSi₅, CeSi₅, and YSi₃ analogues (CS, SS, TS). However, without a melt or high pressure, reconstructing the cubic CaRuSi₆ network is kinetically difficult [Reasonable Inference]. BaRh₂Si₉ needed arc melting plus an eight-week anneal, implying solid-state ordering alone may be slow (CS, SS). This is therefore the weakest kinetic route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines very different Ca and Ru coordination environments, CaSi₁₂ cuboctahedra and RuSi₆ octahedra, which supports one amorphization-risk trigger through cation size/environment mismatch (target description; [Reasonable Inference]). The provided structure reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully met. The provided data do not state the unit-cell atom count; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS, treated neutrally. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaRuSi₆ is likely a near-metastable or barely stable phase rather than a robust deep ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and many thermodynamically similar Si-rich silicides are metastable high-pressure phases (TS). The synthesis case is nevertheless reasonably strong because the closest chemical and structural analogue BaRh₂Si₉ is experimentally accessible by arc melting and long annealing under Ar (CS, SS). The most important enabling factor is the existence of stable or synthesizable alkaline-earth/late-transition-metal/silicon frameworks with similar M–Si and Si–Si distances, especially BaRh₂Si₉ (CS, SS). The most important limiting factor is phase control: analogous systems often require long anneals, high pressure, quenching, or careful stoichiometry to suppress competing silicides (CS, SS, TS). The best practical route is therefore ambient-pressure arc melting followed by sealed long annealing, with high-pressure synthesis as a backup if the cubic phase does not form.
