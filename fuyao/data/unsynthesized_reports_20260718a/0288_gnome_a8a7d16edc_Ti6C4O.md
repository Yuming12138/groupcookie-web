### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, giving the maximum thermodynamic score. Several analogues are also on or very near the hull: Ti5Al2C3 is 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1), Nb6C5 is 0.0 eV/atom (SS-2, TS-2), HfC is 0.0 eV/atom (SS-3, TS-3), and Sc3RuC4/Sc3RhC4 are only slightly above hull (CS-3, CS-4). The main uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS do not explicitly contain a Ti–C–O ordered oxycarbide analogue, so competing TiC/TiO/TiOx phases remain insufficiently constrained by the provided sources.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Solid-state sintering is severe because the closest structural analogue Nb6C5 required 2300 K for 20 h in 0.01 Pa vacuum (SS-2, TS-2). Ti–Al–C analogues can form at 1500 °C in Ar (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1), but this is still above 1400 °C and phase purity was difficult for Ti5Al2C3 (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Oxygen-source behavior is not directly evidenced in CS/SS/TS, so that uncertainty is neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Ti/TiC diffusion and the need to order C/O/vacancy-like sites [Reasonable Inference]. The Nb6C5 structural match lowers the reconstruction penalty because both phases use monoclinic C2/m metal square pyramids and anion-centered M6 octahedra (SS-2, TS-2). However, Nb6C5 required high temperature, vacuum, and long holds, indicating slow ordering kinetics (SS-2, TS-2).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Arc melting is high-temperature but avoids long initial diffusion through solid powders and is well supported for several carbide analogues under Ar (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). The route is still multistep because the analogues commonly use cold pressing, remelting, and annealing (CS-3, SS-5, TS-5). Direct oxygen-containing arc-melt evidence is absent; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for oxygen retention during arc melting, neutral 50.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Arc melting improves homogenization, as shown by Sc3TC4-type carbide syntheses using repeated melting and annealing to obtain well-crystallized samples (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). The structural formation from melt is a complete crystallization process, so the structural reconstruction score is not as high as for a topotactic route [Reasonable Inference]. The largest kinetic uncertainty is whether the ordered Ti6C4O structure forms instead of TiC/TiO solid solutions or multiphase products [Reasonable Inference], not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Hot pressing/SPS remains severe because refractory carbide analogues such as HfC require approximately 2000 °C hot pressing (SS-3, TS-3). The route uses controlled atmosphere/vacuum and multiple powder-processing steps [Reasonable Inference]. The pressure is not counted as high pressure because typical hot pressing/SPS uses MPa-scale pressure, not >5 GPa [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Pressure-assisted consolidation should improve particle contact and shorten diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. HfC hot pressing and Nb6C5 sintering show that refractory carbide frameworks are compatible with high-temperature powder consolidation (SS-3, TS-3, SS-2, TS-2). Direct evidence for Ti6C4O ordering by SPS/hot pressing is absent, so the ordering aspect remains uncertain and is scored neutrally where source evidence is lacking.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Ti, so cation radius mismatch is not a meaningful amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure has corner and edge sharing but no reported face-sharing coexistence, reducing topological frustration; the analogous Nb6C5 also uses edge/corner-sharing C-centered octahedra in C2/m (SS-2, TS-2). Exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so the unit-size check is inferred from the reduced formula and related simple carbide structures; insufficient direct cell-size evidence from CS/SS/TS is neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti6C4O is likely a ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically or structurally related refractory carbides are also stable or nearly stable on the hull (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2, SS-3, TS-3). The strongest support is structural: Nb6C5 has the same monoclinic C2/m motif with metal–anion square pyramids and anion-centered metal octahedra, suggesting the target topology is chemically plausible (SS-2, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ti–C/refractory-carbide bonding network, supported by Ti–Al–C and transition-metal carbide syntheses (CS-1, CS-2–5). The most critical limiting factor is oxygen stoichiometry/order, because no provided CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates synthesis of an ordered Ti–C–O phase with this composition. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful control of oxygen activity and high-temperature homogenization.
