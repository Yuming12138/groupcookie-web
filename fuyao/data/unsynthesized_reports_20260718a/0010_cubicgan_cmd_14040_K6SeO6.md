### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability/metastability boundary in the supplied scoring rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with similar or slightly higher E_hull are synthesized, including Na5WNO4 at 0.048 eV/atom and KNa2NiO2 at 0.078 eV/atom (TS-4, TS-1). TS therefore supports accessibility of phases in this energy range, although TS analogues are not chemically close to K6SeO6 and give insufficient direct chemical evidence for selenium oxide stability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C temperatures and high pressure because related K-rich oxides and TeO6 oxides form at 500–720 °C in sealed Ag or silver-bomb systems (CS-1, CS-4, SS-1, SS-2). Deductions are assigned for sealed volatile selenium oxide handling [Reasonable Inference], multi-step dry loading/sealing/annealing, and air-sensitive alkali oxides documented in related syntheses (CS-3, SS-2, SS-5). TS gives insufficient route-specific evidence for this oxide sealed-tube route and is treated as neutral for Route 1 condition severity.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
K2O/KOx and SeO3 should be chemically reactive but are still bulk solid-state reagents, and analogues often require long anneals of 28–45 d, implying slow ordering (CS-1, SS-1, SS-2). Structural transfer is plausible because TeO6 octahedra occur in the closest structural analogues (SS-1, SS-2), but no analogue has the exact cubic K6SeO6 topology. The main uncertainty is whether a simple alkali oxide route can stabilize isolated octahedral SeO6 rather than lower selenates or K2SeO4-like phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The K/KO2 route receives a better severity score because the strongest analogue, K3SbO4, uses a relatively direct heating and cooling protocol at 975 K under Ar without autoclave pressure (CS-5). Deductions remain for K/KO2 air sensitivity and selenium oxide volatility in a sealed container (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). SS provides structural support through TeO6 analogues but insufficient direct evidence for K/KO2 selenium chemistry, so SS is neutral for this route’s condition severity.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
K metal and KO2 provide high precursor activity, and CS-5 demonstrates formation of an alkali-rich high-valent octahedral oxide from such a reactive mixture. The SbO6 environment in K3SbO4 is the closest chemical/coordination precedent among CS entries, supporting high-valent octahedral oxoanion formation (CS-5). The uncertainty is structural: K3SbO4 is monoclinic, whereas the target is cubic Fm-3m, so nucleation of the predicted cubic phase may need careful cooling or seeding [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The amide/autoclave route is penalized for reactive NH3/amide conditions, autoclave containment, multi-step handling, and moisture-sensitive precursors (CS-2, TS-4, TS-5). Temperatures remain moderate, 250–750 °C, and no >5 GPa high-pressure requirement is indicated, even though kbar ammonia pressure is reported for related systems (CS-2). CS and TS support feasibility of amide flux chemistry but also show increased operational difficulty.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Amide melts provide strong kinetic assistance, as K6Ge2O7 and Na/W/Mo oxometallate or oxonitride phases form in autoclave amide media (CS-2, TS-4, TS-5). However, TS-4 and TS-5 explicitly show competing oxonitride/side phases and hydrolysis sensitivity, reducing confidence in selective formation of pure K6SeO6. This route is kinetically attractive but chemically less selective for a pure oxide target.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The large K⁺/Se⁶⁺ cation size contrast triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference], and the Fm-3m conventional cell likely exceeds 40 atoms if it contains multiple K6SeO6 formula units [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not specify mixed edge/face/vertex sharing, so C3 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for exact target polyhedral connectivity. The score reflects medium risk: crystallization is plausible, but long anneals and slow cooling may be needed, as in CS-1 and SS-1/SS-2.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K6SeO6 is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-stability threshold, and thermodynamic analogues near 0.05–0.09 eV/atom are synthesized (TS-1, TS-4). CS and SS evidence strongly support alkali-rich oxide synthesis in sealed inert containers, especially for K-rich oxides and octahedral Te/Sb oxides (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5, SS-1, SS-2). The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive alkali oxide or K/KO2 chemistry that can stabilize high-valent oxoanion frameworks under moderate temperatures (CS-5, SS-1). The main limiting factor is selenium volatility/redox control, because no direct K–Se–O synthesis analogue is provided and use of SeO2/SeO3 is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. Phase selectivity is also a concern because many related alkali-rich oxides require long sealed anneals or show side phases and moisture sensitivity (SS-2, SS-5, TS-4, TS-5).
