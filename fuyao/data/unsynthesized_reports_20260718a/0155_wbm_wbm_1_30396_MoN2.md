### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target thermodynamics. This is reinforced by stable or near-stable Mo nitride analogues: MgMoN₂ has E_hull = 0.0 (SS3/TS1), MoMnN₂ has E_hull = 0.0 (TS4), and MoN has E_hull = 0.0108 eV/atom (SS5/TS3). CS is mixed: MoS₂ and MoP₂ include stable compounds (CS2/CS5), but TcN₂ is highly above hull despite high-pressure synthesis (CS1), so CS thermodynamic evidence is not uniformly predictive. Remaining uncertainty is whether DFT correctly captures the N–N bonded MoN₂ phase relative to MoN/Mo₂N + N₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1, flowing-NH₃ ammonolysis

The temperature severity is moderate because related ammonolysis syntheses occur at 400–800 °C rather than >1000 °C (SS5/TS3, SS2, TS4). The main penalties are the reactive/toxic NH₃ atmosphere and likely multi-step optimization, since CrWN₂ uses controlled ramps and NH₃ flow (SS1), while MnMoN₂ requires a narrow temperature window (TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS for ammonolysis of binary MoN₂ specifically; CS mainly supports high-pressure TcN₂ and sulfide/phosphide routes (CS1–CS5). The remaining uncertainty is whether NH₃ at ambient pressure provides enough nitrogen chemical potential to stabilize MoN₂ rather than lower nitrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1, flowing-NH₃ ammonolysis

Kinetics are moderate-to-poor because oxide-to-nitride conversion requires anion exchange and structural reconstruction, as seen in MnMoN₂ and MnWN₂ oxide ammonolysis routes (TS4, SS2). Mo–N bond lengths in the target are close to MgMoN₂, MoMnN₂, and MoN analogues, supporting formation of Mo–N frameworks (SS3/TS1, TS4, SS5/TS3). However, SS2 and TS4 report impurity or narrow phase-control issues, and SS5/TS3 reports multiple Mo nitride products, so competing phases are a major kinetic risk. The score is therefore limited by reconstruction and phase-selection uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2, sealed autoclave azide-assisted nitridation

The temperature is favorable because MgMoN₂ forms at 700 °C for 10 h in a sealed autoclave (SS3/TS1). Severity arises from NaN₃ toxicity/energetic decomposition and the need for a sealed pressure vessel, both directly tied to the NaN₃ autoclave chemistry in SS3/TS1. Insufficient evidence from CS for azide synthesis; CS provides no azide-based nitride route and is neutral for this factor. The route is attractive for nitrogen chemical potential but less operationally benign than flowing NH₃.

#### F3 — Route 2, sealed autoclave azide-assisted nitridation

The MgMoN₂ precedent shows that Mo–N frameworks can form from Mo + NaN₃ at 700 °C (SS3/TS1), which supports kinetic feasibility. However, direct binary MoN₂ formation from Mo + NaN₃ requires full construction of the target phase without Mg stabilization, which is a [Reasonable Inference] risk. Mo metal is refractory and diffuses slowly, lowering the ion-diffusion score [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Na-containing intermediates or lower Mo nitrides dominate instead of MoN₂.

#### F2 — Route 3, high-pressure direct nitridation

High-pressure synthesis is strongly supported by chemically similar TcN₂ at 5.5 GPa and 1773 K (CS1) and structurally similar P-6m2 TaN at 20–100 kbar and 800–960 °C (SS4/TS2). The deductions are severe because this route requires multi-GPa pressure, high temperature, air exclusion or encapsulation, and specialized equipment (CS1, SS4/TS2). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS that MoN₂ specifically requires high pressure; the inference comes from nitrogen-rich TcN₂ and high-pressure P-6m2 TaN analogues (CS1, SS4/TS2). This route is experimentally challenging but may best stabilize the N–N bonded stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3, high-pressure direct nitridation

High pressure can overcome thermodynamic and packing barriers, as shown by TcN₂ single-crystal growth at 5.5 GPa (CS1) and β-TaN conversion under 20–100 kbar (SS4/TS2). Kinetics remain limited by refractory Mo and N₂ activation, requiring high T and possibly reactive nitrogen sources [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction may be more favorable if starting from MoN/Mo₂N, but this is not directly demonstrated in the provided sources. The route is therefore scored similarly to the azide route kinetically, but worse in condition severity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, so cation-radius mismatch does not drive amorphization; crystalline binary nitride analogues such as TaN and MoN are reported (SS4/TS2, SS5/TS3). The described P-6m2 MoN₂ structure appears small and ordered, unlike large multicomponent cells [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The target connectivity is described primarily as edge-sharing MoN₆ units, whereas mixed edge/corner/face-sharing complexity is explicitly present in some analogues such as CrWN₂ and TaN (SS1, SS4/TS2). The main amorphization uncertainty is not topological complexity but possible kinetic trapping during low-temperature nitridation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

MoN₂ is predicted as a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Mo nitride analogues are stable or near-stable, including MgMoN₂ and MoMnN₂ (SS3/TS1, TS4). The best synthesis support comes from ammonolysis and azide routes that already form Mo–N frameworks at 400–800 °C (SS5/TS3, SS3/TS1, TS4), while high-pressure evidence from TcN₂ and P-6m2 TaN provides a backup path for dense or nitrogen-rich nitride stabilization (CS1, SS4/TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural match in Mo–N bond lengths between target MoN₂ at 2.10 Å and Mo nitride analogues at about 2.12–2.14 Å (SS3/TS1, TS4, SS5/TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because related ammonolysis systems show impurities, narrow temperature windows, and multiple possible Mo nitride products (SS2, TS4, SS5/TS3). Overall, the material is moderately synthesizable, with the best practical starting point being NH₃ ammonolysis followed by high-pressure or azide approaches if nitrogen content is insufficient.
