### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is calculated at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, placing it in the 90–100 range for thermodynamic stability by the required rubric. TS analogues include several on-hull or near-hull materials, including TaAs, V₅Se₄, Zr₅Sb₃S, and VAs (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). CS evidence is more mixed because CS1 has high E_hull = 0.907 eV/atom, while CS3 and CS5 are on the hull; therefore CS provides only partial support. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee retention of hydrogen during synthesis **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 uses directly supported arsenide sealed-tube conditions: TaAs-type arsenides at 1000–1200 °C in evacuated silica tubes and VAs at 850 °C in evacuated quartz ampoules (SS2, TS1, TS5). Its F2 is low because it involves vacuum, volatile/toxic As, and a hydride source, while its F3 is limited by refractory Nb diffusion and lack of hydride analogue evidence. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for Nb–As–H hydrogen incorporation, so that part is neutral-to-negative and treated as **[Reasonable Inference]**. The route is chemically plausible but kinetically demanding.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 receives the best F2 because the flux precedent operates around 900–1000 °C rather than >1200–1500 °C (TS4). Its F3 is better than direct solid-state synthesis because flux should improve mass transport **[Reasonable Inference]**, and TS4 demonstrates a related metal-pnictide/chalcogenide flux synthesis. However, CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for flux synthesis of Nb–As–H, and TS4 is not a hydride. The main uncertainty is whether H can be retained and ordered in a molten-metal flux environment **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

High-pressure synthesis is supported by the Nb₅Se₄ analogue made at 3–5 GPa and 1500 °C with quenching (SS1, TS3). F2 is penalized by high temperature and specialized containment, though the required pressure can be kept near the reported 3–5 GPa range and not necessarily exceed the rubric’s >5 GPa threshold. F3 remains moderate-to-low because high pressure helps densification and hydrogen retention **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the analogue is a chalcogenide, not an arsenide hydride. CS gives insufficient high-pressure signal and is neutral for this route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Arc-melting and long annealing are common in chemically similar transition-metal borides and phase-equilibrated systems (CS1, CS3, CS5). F2 is poor because the route is multi-step, high-temperature, atmosphere-controlled, and uses volatile/toxic As plus hydrogenation conditions. F3 benefits from pre-alloying, which can reduce Nb–As diffusion distances **[Reasonable Inference]**, but hydrogen insertion into an ordered Nb₃H environment is not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS. This route is best viewed as a fallback if direct flux or sealed-tube routes fail.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Nb, so cation-radius mismatch is not a meaningful amorphization driver **[Reasonable Inference]**. The structure description is relatively simple, with two Nb sites, one As site, and one H site in Imm2, and it does not explicitly report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity; therefore no amorphization trigger is clearly activated from the target description. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence on amorphization for Nb₂As₂H specifically, so their signal is neutral. The remaining uncertainty is the exact crystallographic cell multiplicity and whether local H disorder could induce partial amorphization **[Reasonable Inference]**.

Nb₂As₂H is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). The arsenide part of the chemistry is well supported by sealed-tube group-5/transition-metal arsenide syntheses, especially TaAs and VAs analogues (SS2, TS1, TS5). The most enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with known accessibility of Nb/Ta/V arsenide frameworks under sealed high-temperature conditions (SS2, TS1, TS5). The most limiting factor is hydrogen control: no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates a Nb–As–H hydride or the trigonal-planar Nb₃H motif, so all hydride routes require **[Reasonable Inference]**. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful tuning of As vapor pressure, hydrogen chemical potential, and post-growth annealing.
