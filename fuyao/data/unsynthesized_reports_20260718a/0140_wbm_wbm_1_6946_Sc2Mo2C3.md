### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin. This is reinforced by multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar carbides also being on or essentially on the hull: Ce₂Mo₂C₃, Er₂Mo₂C₃, Ho₂Mo₂C₃, Sm₂Mo₂C₃, ScCrC₂, and Sc₅Re₂C₇ (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, especially where Mo₂C impurities are reported in analogues (CS3, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4).

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses arc-melting under Ar and annealing in evacuated silica tubes at 850–950 °C, matching ScCrC₂, Er₂Mo₂C₃, Ho₂Mo₂C₃, and Sc₅Re₂C₇ precedents (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). The conditions are not high pressure and the annealing temperature is below 1000 °C, but the route requires controlled atmosphere and several operations: pressing, arc-melting, Ta wrapping, sealing, and annealing (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is whether Sc₂Mo₂C₃ needs the higher-temperature Ln₂Mo₂C₃ protocol rather than the 900 °C anneal [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Elemental Sc/Mo/graphite precursors are low-activity compared with salts or molecular routes, and Mo is refractory [Reasonable Inference], so intrinsic diffusion is difficult. However, analogues are made by arc-melting before annealing, which likely homogenizes the elements and enables formation at 780–900 °C (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS3, TS1–TS3). Structural kinetics are favorable because the same C2/m M₂Mo₂C₃ network exists in Er₂Mo₂C₃, Ho₂Mo₂C₃, Ce₂Mo₂C₃, and Sm₂Mo₂C₃, but impurity reports indicate that full ordering may be slow (CS3, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is more severe because Ce₂Mo₂C₃ and Sm₂Mo₂C₃ analogues use 1623 K/72 h heat treatments, weld-sealed Mo crucibles, Ar atmosphere, slow cooling, quenching, and glovebox-level dryness (CS3, SS4, TS4). No high pressure is required, and the temperature is below 1400 °C, but the equipment and atmosphere control are demanding (CS3, SS4, TS4). The route is therefore scored lower in severity despite stronger structural transferability.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The high-temperature Mo-crucible route improves diffusion relative to Route 1 and is directly demonstrated for M₂Mo₂C₃ molybdenum carbides (CS3, SS4, TS4). Precursor activity remains modest because it still starts from elemental metals and graphite (CS3, SS4, TS4). Kinetic uncertainty remains in phase purity because analogous syntheses report Mo₂C minority phases and no broad homogeneity ranges (CS3, SS4, TS4).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Direct reactive sintering is supported by Sm₂Cr₂C₃ at 1673 K for 72 h in weld-sealed Mo ampoules under Ar (CS4). It avoids arc-melting but still uses high temperature, sealed ampoules, inert handling, and multiple operations (CS4). The CS/SS/TS data provide insufficient evidence that direct sintering is preferred for Sc₂Mo₂C₃ specifically, so the route is plausible but not optimal.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The lack of an arc-melt homogenization step makes diffusion through Sc/Mo/C powders the main limitation [Reasonable Inference]. The closest sintering precedent required 72 h and still gave minor impurities, indicating nontrivial kinetic barriers (CS4). Transferability is partial because Sm₂Cr₂C₃ is chemically different from Sc₂Mo₂C₃, while the strongest Mo-based analogues use arc-melting and/or high-temperature Mo crucibles (CS3, SS4, TS4).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has distinct Sc and Mo coordination environments and a meaningful cation-size/chemistry mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure], triggering one amorphization-risk criterion. However, the target framework is not excessively complex by formula count, and the supplied target description reports corner and edge sharing but not simultaneous corner/edge/face sharing. The repeated crystallization of closely related C2/m M₂Mo₂C₃ analogues argues against a high amorphization tendency (CS3, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂Mo₂C₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and many close carbide analogues are also on the hull (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The synthesis case is strongly supported by C2/m Ln₂Mo₂C₃ analogues with nearly identical Mo–C coordination motifs and by Sc-containing carbides made from elemental Sc, transition-metal powders, and graphite (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The most important enabling factor is the well-established arc-melting plus annealing chemistry for refractory ternary carbides, especially M₂Mo₂C₃ phases (CS3, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4). The main limiting factor is phase purity: Mo₂C, residual Mo, and competing ternary carbides appear in closely related syntheses, requiring stoichiometry and annealing optimization (CS3, SS1, SS2, SS4, TS1, TS2, TS4). Overall, the target appears realistically synthesizable, but likely as a bulk ceramic/intermetallic carbide requiring careful inert-atmosphere high-temperature processing rather than a low-temperature route.
