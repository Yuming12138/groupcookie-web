### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
Lu₂CO is predicted on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom from the target data, so the thermodynamic score is high. Several chemically similar rare-earth carbide halides are also on the hull (CS1, CS2, CS4), and the closest oxycarbide ScAlOC analogue is on/near hull (SS1, SS2, TS1). The remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct C/O ordering **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, sealed-Ta solid-state reaction.**  
The condition severity is moderate because rare-earth carbide halides require sealed Ta capsules, Ar, high temperature, and long times, but not high pressure (CS1, CS2, CS4). Kinetics are limiting because dense Lu/Lu₂O₃/graphite powders require solid-state diffusion; analogous reactions require 1–3 weeks or 18 d (CS1, CS2, CS4). The structural match to Caswellsilverite Gd₂CCl supports transferability, but replacing halide by oxygen is an extrapolation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, reactive Li-flux growth.**  
The Li-flux route has lower thermal severity because lanthanide carbides were prepared at 1070 K, much lower than the high-T solid-state routes (CS3). Kinetics are better because the flux provides liquid-mediated transport and slow cooling, as demonstrated for lanthanide carbide crystals (CS3). The main uncertainty is whether Lu₂O₃ dissolves/reacts sufficiently in Li flux and whether an O-containing Lu₂CO phase forms rather than binary carbides/oxides; insufficient direct evidence from CS for Li-flux rare-earth oxycarbide synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, high-temperature oxide–carbide melt/flux synthesis.**  
This route is severe because ScAlOC oxycarbide analogues require 1600–1820 °C, controlled cooling, and specialized high-temperature equipment (SS1, SS2, TS1). Kinetics are improved by melting/fluxing but phase control is poor, since ScAlOC can appear as a by-product with other carbide phases (SS1, TS1). Transferability is structurally reasonable for mixed C/O coordination but weak for the exact Lu₂CO composition **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation species, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern based on Lu₂CO stoichiometry. The target network is reported as corner- and edge-sharing LuC₃O₃ / CLu₆ / OLu₆ octahedra, not vertex+edge+face coexistence, so the connectivity-based amorphization trigger is not met. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization measurements, so the low-risk assessment is based on structural simplicity and should be treated with moderate uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Lu₂CO is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its predicted E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and several chemically/thermodynamically related references are also on the hull (CS1, CS2, CS4, TS1–TS3). The analogue support is good for rare-earth carbide formation and mixed C/O oxycarbide coordination, but no reference directly demonstrates Lu₂CO, so the synthesis assessment relies partly on **[Reasonable Inference]**. The most important enabling factor is the strong predicted thermodynamic stability combined with the existence of rare-earth carbide/oxycarbide high-temperature routes (CS1–CS4, SS1, SS2, TS1). The most important limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: Lu₂O₃ and graphite are refractory, and analogues often need long annealing, flux transport, or high temperature (CS1–CS4, SS1, SS2). Overall, Li-flux growth is the best first attempt because it reduces diffusion barriers relative to direct solid-state synthesis (CS3).
