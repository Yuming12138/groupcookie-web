### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability edge of the provided 90–100 band. Closest BaNiF6 and SrNiF6 analogues are calculated on the hull and are experimentally accessible by fluorination, supporting the idea that AMF6 fluoride frameworks can be real phases (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2). The most relevant Cu thermodynamic analogue, CsCuF4, has E_hull ≈ 0.048 eV/atom and was synthesized by high-pressure fluorination, supporting near-hull Cu fluoride accessibility (TS1). Remaining uncertainty is that TS2–TS5 are not direct Ba–Cu–F analogues, so their direct stability signal for BaCuF6 is insufficient and treated neutrally.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for F2 atmosphere, volatile/reactive gas pressure, and hazardous air/moisture-sensitive handling, all directly supported by high-pressure fluorination analogues (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2, TS1). It is not penalized for >1000 °C or >5 GPa because the cited syntheses use 280–600 °C and gas pressures from ~140 bar to >3500 atm, not multi-GPa conditions (CS1, CS4, TS1). The severity remains high because Monel autoclave fluorination is specialized and dangerous, but it is the most chemically transferable route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

High-pressure F2 improves reaction kinetics by providing a strongly oxidizing fluorinating environment, as shown by BaNiF6/SrNiF6 and CsCuF4 formation at moderate temperatures (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2, TS1). Structural similarity is favorable because the target has BaF12 and CuF6 motifs analogous to BaNiF6/SrNiF6 Ba/SrF12 and NiF6 units (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is whether cubic F-43m BaCuF6 is the kinetic product rather than a distorted/trigonal or decomposed Cu fluoride phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Sealed-tube fluoride routes are supported for BaAlF5 and Ba2Ni3F10 at 600–950 °C in noble-metal tubes, so the temperature demand is moderate rather than extreme (CS2, CS3, CS5). For BaCuF6, an oxidizing fluorination component is likely needed because Cu must be stabilized in a high-valent fluoride environment [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS4/TS1]. Thus, the route receives the same F2 hazard/sealed-system penalties as Route 1, although its pressure may be lower.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

BaF2-based sealed-tube reactions can form complex barium fluorides, but CS5 indicates long heating at 950 °C for Ba2Ni3F10, implying diffusion and ordering can be slow. Unlike Route 1, Route 2 lacks direct evidence for producing high-valent BaMF6 from BaF2 + MF2 binaries, so structural reconstruction is scored lower. Insufficient evidence from TS supports a direct sealed-tube Ba–Cu–F route, increasing uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Photochemical ACrF6 synthesis uses ambient temperature, so it avoids high-temperature penalties (SS3, SS4). However, it still requires anhydrous HF, elemental F2, UV irradiation, and specialized FEP/PTFE apparatus, so reactive atmosphere, volatile sealed-system, and toxic precursor penalties apply (SS3, SS4). Insufficient CS and TS evidence exists for BaCuF6 photochemistry, so the route is considered plausible but less direct.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

HF/F2/UV chemistry provides high precursor activity and bypasses bulk diffusion, as demonstrated by ACrF6 synthesis at ambient temperature over 10–20 days (SS3, SS4). The structural match is reasonable because ACrF6 phases have large A-site fluorine coordination plus MF6 octahedra similar to the target topology (SS3, SS4). The major kinetic uncertainty is nucleating BaCuF6 rather than soluble/insoluble BaF2, CuF2, or lower-valent Cu fluoride products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large Ba–Cu cation size mismatch triggers one amorphization risk check, consistent with the target’s very different BaF12 and CuF6 coordination environments [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description does not indicate a >40-atom cell, and it does not report edge sharing in addition to vertex and face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. Structurally similar AMF6 analogues crystallize successfully rather than amorphizing, which supports a low–medium amorphization risk (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaCuF6 is likely a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase rather than a deeply stable ground-state phase, because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom. The CS/SS evidence is strong for AMF6 frameworks containing large A-site cations and MF6 octahedra, especially BaNiF6 and SrNiF6, but the exact Cu analogue is not directly reported (CS1, CS4, SS1, SS2). The TS evidence is most useful in showing that near-hull Cu fluorides such as CsCuF4 can be synthesized under high-pressure F2 (TS1). The critical enabling factor is access to high fluorine chemical potential to stabilize high-valent Cu–F coordination. The critical limiting factor is phase/oxidation-state control: Cu may form competing lower-valent fluorides or decompose under moisture/air exposure [Reasonable Inference from CS4 and TS1 sensitivity reports].
