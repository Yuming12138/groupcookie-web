### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the high-synthesizability boundary of the scoring rubric. Thermodynamic analogues include near-hull examples at 0.0306 and 0.0414 eV/atom (TS2, TS5), supporting plausibility for metastable or near-ground-state synthesis. Chemical analogues are mixed: CS5 is very low in hull, while CS1 is high at 0.521 eV/atom, so CS provides both positive and cautionary evidence. SS gives insufficient direct thermodynamic signal for BaSO₆ and is treated as neutral for F1 beyond demonstrating related oxyanion crystal chemistry.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Oxidative aqueous precipitation + slow evaporation

Condition severity is low because CS1 and CS3 use room-temperature aqueous methods, and SS1/TS5 use slow evaporation near room temperature. The main deductions are procedural complexity and hazardous Ba/oxidant chemistry [Reasonable Inference], not thermal or pressure severity. Kinetics are favorable because soluble precursors avoid long-range solid diffusion, as seen in aqueous crystallization analogues (CS1, CS3, SS1, TS5). The uncertainty is structural: the closest Ba analogues contain trigonal or tetrahedral oxyanions rather than target SO₆ octahedra (CS1, CS3, CS5).

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal oxidative crystallization

Hydrothermal methods are moderately severe because they require sealed autoclaves, although CS2 and CS4 imply standard hydrothermal rather than extreme pressure. Kinetics are strong because hydrothermal fluids promote dissolution/reprecipitation and single-crystal formation in CS2/CS4. The route may better stabilize metastable oxygen-rich phases than open beaker precipitation [Reasonable Inference], but sealed oxidizer use adds safety and stoichiometry risk. Exact hydrothermal parameters are not given in CS2/CS4, so the proposed 120–200 °C range is extrapolated.

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Gel diffusion/crystallization

Gel crystallization is mild in temperature and pressure, as TS3 achieved single crystals at 22 °C. The kinetic advantage is controlled slow diffusion, which can reduce immediate precipitation and improve crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. However, TS3 explicitly reports competing unidentified periodate phases at high NaOH concentration, so phase selectivity is a real risk. Chemical transferability is indirect because TS3 is Na–I–O rather than Ba–S–O.

#### Route 4 F2/F3 — Low-temperature solid-state oxidative/dehydration

Bulk oxidative/dehydration synthesis is more severe because it may require controlled O₂/vacuum or sealed conditions, and CS5 shows that Ba halate dehydration/crystallization can require vacuum, Soxhlet extraction, and glove-box handling. Kinetics are weaker than solution routes because solid-state reconstruction is needed; TS4 required 600–700 °C for 48 h for a related oxide powder. For BaSO₆, high-temperature treatment risks oxygen loss or collapse to BaSO₄-like phases [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 4 is a backup route, not the preferred synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large Ba/S size and bonding mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is high-symmetry and relatively simple in formula, and the provided target description does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell. Its polyhedral connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not all three corner/edge/face sharing modes, so C3 is not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for amorphization behavior of BaSO₆ itself and are neutral for that specific factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSO₆ is most likely a near-hull metastable phase rather than an unambiguous ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support mild aqueous crystallization of related oxyanion salts, especially Ba-containing examples in CS1, CS3, and CS5, but they do not directly demonstrate octahedral sulfur in BaSO₆. The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated reactivity: aqueous precipitation/slow evaporation avoids solid-state diffusion and is repeatedly successful for oxyanion salts (CS1, CS3, SS1, TS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against ordinary BaSO₄, BaCO₃, or lower-oxygen Ba–S–O phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, but likely requires careful oxidant and supersaturation control.
