### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and justifying a high but not perfect score (target data). Thermodynamic analogues with comparable E_hull values are experimentally associated with synthesis routes: CeSi₅ at 0.058 eV/atom by high pressure, Zr₂CuSi₄ at 0.0587 eV/atom by arc melting/annealing, Hf₂CuSi₄ at 0.0656 eV/atom by solid-state/arc methods, and YSi₂ at 0.0626 eV/atom by thin-film evaporation/annealing (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is a distinct cubic F-43m Zr–Ru silicide, so thermodynamic proximity does not guarantee phase selectivity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because arc melting and 900–1100 °C annealing are standard for intermetallic silicides, and analogues used Ar atmospheres without high pressure (CS1, SS4, SS5, TS2, TS3). The major deductions are high-temperature exposure above 1000 °C in the Zr/Hf–Cu–Si analogue range and multi-step processing involving pressing, remelting, annealing, and possible quenching (TS2, TS3). The uncertainty is whether ZrRuSi₆ can be homogenized at 950 °C like BaRh₂Si₉ or needs the higher 1000–1100 °C window used for Zr/Hf–Cu–Si phases (CS1, TS2, TS3).

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are the limiting factor for Route 1 because Zr and Ru are refractory, and forming an ordered 1:1:6 cubic phase from a melt requires substantial atomic rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. Analogue evidence shows slow ordering: BaRh₂Si₉ needed eight weeks to become nearly single phase, and Zr/Hf–Cu–Si systems report peritectic/slow-equilibration phase-control difficulties (CS1, SS1, TS2, TS3). Arc melting provides initial homogenization, but the long anneal requirement keeps the kinetic score low (CS1, TS2, TS3).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is condition-severe because high-pressure analogues require 10–15 GPa and high temperatures, using Walker/Kawai-type multianvil equipment (CS2, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS4). The high-pressure deduction dominates, and the process also includes multiple steps such as precursor preparation, loading, compression, heating, quenching, and decompression (CS2, SS2, TS1). The benefit is that high-pressure runs are short, typically several hours for CeSi₅-type products (CS2, TS1).

#### F3 — Route 2

The high-pressure route improves framework formation by starting from binary silicides plus Si, as in CeSi₅/LaSi₅/YSi₃ analogues (CS2, CS4, SS2, SS3, TS1, TS4). The kinetic score remains moderate because diffusion of Zr/Ru in dense silicide precursors is still challenging [Reasonable Inference], but pressure and quenching can stabilize Si-rich metastable frameworks (CS2, SS2, TS1). The uncertainty is whether Ru incorporation into the cubic F-43m network is favored under the same pressure regime as rare-earth silicides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids high pressure and very high temperatures, with TS5 demonstrating silicide thin-film formation under vacuum and annealing at 423–773 K. It receives deductions for vacuum processing and multi-step deposition/annealing, but these are less severe than multianvil synthesis (TS5; CS2/SS2 for contrast). The main uncertainty is that CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for ternary Zr–Ru–Si thin-film synthesis, so the route is extrapolative.

#### F3 — Route 3

Thin-film kinetics are favorable because co-deposition can mix Zr, Ru, and Si at nanometer length scales, reducing bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference]. TS5 supports deposition plus annealing as a route to silicide films, but it also indicates that crystallization depends on annealing and structural matching, so a post-anneal crystallization score rather than direct-crystallization score is appropriate (TS5). Composition control is a significant uncertainty because a 1:1:6 ternary phase likely competes with Zr silicides, Ru silicides, and elemental Si [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: a significant size mismatch between Zr and Ru cations/metals [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description does not show edge-sharing together with vertex and face sharing, only corner/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not applied (target data). The exact unit-cell atom count is not directly given by CS/SS/TS, so that evidence is neutral; the target formula and F-43m description suggest a moderate-size ordered cell rather than a >40-atom cell [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrRuSi₆ is likely a near-threshold metastable phase rather than a deep ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, right at the usual metastability cutoff (target data). The analogue support is meaningful: Si-rich silicides with similar thermodynamic energies have been synthesized by high pressure, arc melting/annealing, and thin-film annealing (CS1–CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong precedent for Si-rich frameworks and metal–Si coordination environments, including Zr–Si, Ru/Rh/Ir–Si, and Si–Si bonding motifs (CS1, SS4, SS5, TS2, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: analogues often require long anneals, high pressure, quenching, or yield mixtures/impurities, indicating competing silicide phases and slow ordering kinetics (CS1, CS2, CS4, TS1–TS4). The best practical first attempt is thin-film co-deposition if phase discovery is the goal, while arc melting plus long anneal is the best bulk route if milligram-scale powder is required [Reasonable Inference].
