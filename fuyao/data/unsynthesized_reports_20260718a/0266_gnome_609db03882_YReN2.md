### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is reported exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a very high thermodynamic score. Close chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogues are also frequently on hull: La₃Nb₂N₆, La₃Ta₂N₆, La₃Cr₂N₆, U₂MnN₃, and Th₂MnN₃ are reported at E_hull = 0 in the provided data (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee accessible kinetics or resistance to off-stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1, flux.**  
Flux analogues require 1030–1600 °C, sealed Ta/Nb ampoules, inert handling, and flux removal, so the condition-severity score is low-moderate (CS2, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3). Li₃N/Ga flux chemistry is well supported for rare-earth transition-metal nitrides, but it is operationally demanding (SS1, SS5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Re-specific flux volatility or solubility, so that point remains neutral-to-risky [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1, flux.**  
Flux improves precursor activity and can enable synthesis below refractory transition-metal melting points, as shown by La₃Nb₂N₆/La₃Ta₂N₆ syntheses (SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3). However, Re is more refractory than Nb/Ta/Cr and its dissolution/transport in Li/Ga flux is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Structural analogues strongly support the N-centered rare-earth/transition-metal octahedral motif, but not the exact YReN₂ P4/mmm ordering (SS1, SS4, SS5).

**F2 — Route 2, solid state.**  
Solid-state actinide analogues were made by simple mixing, pressing, and equilibration under 1 atm N₂ at 1200–1600 °C, which is procedurally simple but thermally harsh (CS1, CS4, TS5). The route avoids high pressure and complex flux removal, giving a better F2 than flux. The main severity penalty is the high temperature and air-sensitive nitride handling [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2, solid state.**  
Kinetics are the major weakness because YN and Re are refractory, low-activity precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The U/Th analogues show that square-planar transition-metal nitride motifs can form by sintering (CS1, CS4, TS5), but Re diffusion and cation ordering are likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for successful direct solid-state synthesis of any Re-containing ternary nitride of this structure, so the kinetics score is conservative.

**F2 — Route 3, ammonothermal.**  
The Li₂Ta₃N₅ analogue demonstrates lower-temperature nitride formation at 550 °C, but requires 0.6 GPa NH₃, glovebox loading, and high-pressure autoclaves (CS3, SS3). Because the pressure is below the >5 GPa penalty threshold, the main deductions come from NH₃, sealed systems, and air-sensitive reagents. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for ammonothermal synthesis of the closest thermodynamic analogues, so TS is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 3, ammonothermal.**  
Reactive NH₃/LiNH₂ media can enhance nitrogen activity and transport, as shown for Li₂Ta₃N₅ (CS3, SS3). Still, formation of a Y–Re ordered ternary from refractory Re under ammonothermal conditions is not demonstrated in the provided data [Reasonable Inference]. The score is moderate-low because structural reconstruction and Re chemistry are uncertain.

**F2 — Route 4, CVD.**  
Nb₄N₅ CVD occurs at 950–1000 °C using NbCl₅/NH₃/H₂ at atmospheric pressure, avoiding the very high temperatures of bulk synthesis (SS2). The route is penalized for toxic/reactive gases, volatile chloride delivery, and multi-step deposition control (SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS and TS directly supports ternary Y–Re CVD, so CS/TS are neutral for route-specific severity.

**F3 — Route 4, CVD.**  
CVD bypasses bulk diffusion and can crystallize refractory nitrides, as shown for Nb₄N₅ (SS2). However, ternary composition control for Y:Re:N is likely narrow, and the Nb–N analogue already reports phase-control difficulty in a simpler binary system (SS2). Thus, CVD receives the best numerical weighted score but with an expert penalty due to weak ternary analogue support.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing NY₄Re₂ octahedra by the given structure description, triggering a connectivity-related ordering risk. Similar mixed networks exist in stable analogues such as La₃Nb₂N₆, La₃Ta₂N₆, La₃Cr₂N₆, and U/Th transition-metal nitrides (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1–TS3, TS5), which mitigates but does not eliminate the risk. The large Y/Re size and coordination mismatch is a [Reasonable Inference], not directly quantified by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YReN₂ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically/structurally related nitrides are also on the hull (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strongest for rare-earth/refractory-transition-metal nitride frameworks made by Li₃N/Ga flux and for actinide transition-metal nitride motifs made by solid-state sintering (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1, SS5, TS1, TS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is high nitrogen chemical potential combined with enhanced transport—either through flux, NH₃, or vapor deposition—because Re/Y ordering is likely kinetically limited [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is incorporation and ordering of refractory Re into the P4/mmm YReN₂ framework without forming Re metal, Re nitrides, YN, or off-stoichiometric phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausible but not easy; it is best approached through thin-film CVD screening or Li₃N/Ga flux bulk synthesis.
