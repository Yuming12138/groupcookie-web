### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. The closest analogue CuGaI4 is also on the hull and shares the same tetragonal I-4 corner-sharing tetrahedral framework (CS1, SS1, TS1). CsFeI4 is also reported at E_hull = 0.0 and contains FeI4 tetrahedra, supporting the thermodynamic plausibility of Fe(III) iodide tetrahedra (SS5, TS4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or air stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild in temperature because the direct analogue CuGaI4 was crystallized by cooling from 573 K / ~300 °C, far below high-temperature ceramic synthesis regimes (CS1, SS1, TS1). The route still requires a sealed ampoule and strict moisture exclusion, as CuGaI4 was prepared in sealed Pyrex under strict exclusion of moisture (CS1, SS1, TS1). Fe iodide chemistry likely increases air/redox sensitivity relative to GaI3 [Reasonable Inference], supported by the extreme air/moisture sensitivity reported for Fe iodide tetraiodoferrates (SS5, TS4). Thus the route is moderate rather than easy.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because CuGaI4 forms directly from a melt of CuI and GaI3 in one step (CS1, SS1, TS1). The target’s Fe–I 2.57 Å and Cu–I 2.62 Å bonds closely match the analogue’s Ga–I 2.58 Å and Cu–I 2.61 Å bonds, implying minimal structural reconstruction if FeI3 can substitute for GaI3 [Reasonable Inference] based on target and CuGaI4 structures (CS1, SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is Fe(III) iodide stability and possible competing FeI2/CuI/I2 equilibria [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses conditions similar to CsFeI4 synthesis: 300 °C, closed quartz ampoule, Ar glovebox handling, and slow cooling (SS5, TS4). It is more severe than Route 1 because iodine vapor pressure at 300 °C was reported near 9.7 bar in the CsFeI4 synthesis (SS5, TS4). The Fe iodide products are described as extremely air and moisture sensitive, increasing handling difficulty (SS5, TS4). There is no evidence for GPa pressure or >1000 °C treatment, so those penalties do not apply.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Formation of FeI4 tetrahedra from Fe + I2 is directly supported by CsFeI4 synthesis (SS5, TS4), and formation of CuI4 tetrahedra is supported by CuGaI4 (CS1, SS1, TS1). However, elemental Fe is a less reactive precursor than preformed FeI3 [Reasonable Inference], so reaction kinetics and phase homogenization are less favorable than Route 1. The need to assemble an ordered Fe/Cu I-4 framework from separate Fe, CuI, and iodine sources adds structural reconstruction uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT/vapor-transport-like routes use moderate temperatures, with iodine-rich analogues prepared at 150–350 °C (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). They require vacuum or Ar sealed ampoules, temperature gradients, and volatile iodine handling, which triggers atmosphere, volatility, and multi-step penalties (SS2, SS3, TS2, TS3). Air/moisture sensitivity is also a concern because related iodide crystals are reported as air or moisture sensitive (SS2, SS3, SS5, TS2, TS3, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS specifically supports CVT for FeCuI4, so CS is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Vapor transport can reduce bulk diffusion barriers, as GaTeI7 forms from Te, I2, and Ga/In in sealed ampoules with controlled heating (SS3, TS3). The method is plausible for FeCuI4 because CsFeI4 demonstrates Fe iodide formation in iodine vapor at 300 °C (SS5, TS4). However, no provided analogue shows Fe–Cu ordering by CVT, so composition control and selective crystallization remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. This makes the kinetic score moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only corner-sharing FeI4/CuI4 tetrahedra, and no mixed edge/face-sharing network is reported (target description). CuGaI4 has the same simple corner-sharing tetrahedral framework, supporting a low structural-frustration tendency (CS1, SS1, TS1). One risk trigger is the Fe/Cu tetrahedral cation size mismatch exceeding 15% by Shannon radii [Reasonable Inference]. The small, ordered framework and lack of mixed connectivity keep the amorphization risk low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

FeCuI4 is likely a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest CuGaI4 analogue is also on the hull in the same tetragonal I-4 corner-sharing tetrahedral structure (target; CS1, SS1, TS1). The analogue support is unusually strong structurally: CuGaI4 has Cu–I and Ga–I distances nearly identical to the target Cu–I and Fe–I distances (target; CS1, SS1, TS1). The most important enabling factor is the existence of a directly transferable CuI + M(III)I3 melt-growth route for CuGaI4 (CS1, SS1, TS1). The main limiting factor is Fe(III) iodide redox and moisture sensitivity, because Fe iodide tetraiodoferrates are reported as extremely air/moisture sensitive and require sealed ampoule/glovebox handling (SS5, TS4). Overall, the target appears realistically synthesizable, with the best chance via dry sealed-ampoule melt growth using CuI and an Fe(III) iodide source.
