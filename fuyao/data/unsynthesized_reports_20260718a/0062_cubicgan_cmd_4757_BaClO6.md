### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls in the top scoring bin in the prompt, so F1 is scored 90/100. Thermodynamic analogues near this hull energy are reported as experimentally accessible, including TS4 at E_hull ≈0.0502 eV/atom by hydrothermal synthesis and TS5 at E_hull ≈0.0520 eV/atom by slow evaporation. The uncertainty is that E_hull does not validate the unusual ClO₆ octahedron, and the closest Ba–Cl–O source reports synthetic difficulty for Ba(ClO₃)₂ crystals (CS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Aqueous precipitation / slow evaporation

Condition severity is low because CS2, SS1, and TS5 all show room-temperature or mild aqueous crystallization without high pressure or special atmosphere. Deductions are applied for multiple steps because CS2 uses precipitation, washing, dissolution, and crystallization, and for hazardous oxidizing/soluble Ba chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because solution-phase Ba oxyhalate precipitation is demonstrated (CS2), but structural reconstruction is penalized because CS2 and CS3 contain trigonal XO₃ groups rather than octahedral ClO₆.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-temperature dehydration / anhydrous crystallization

CS3 is the most chemically direct evidence for Ba–Cl–O, but it explicitly reports that Ba(ClO₃)₂ single crystals could not be prepared by the standard slow-evaporation method and required vacuum dehydration, Soxhlet extraction, ~20 days, and glove-box handling. This increases F2 severity via vacuum and multi-step processing. Kinetically, a hydrated Ba chlorate precursor is chemically active and close to the target, but F3 is limited because CS3 does not show ClO₆ formation and indicates crystallization difficulty.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Mild hydrothermal oxidative synthesis

Hydrothermal analogues use moderate temperatures around 220–230 °C or 493–495 K in Teflon-lined autoclaves (SS3, SS5, TS1, TS4), so condition severity is acceptable and far below solid-state ceramic temperatures. Kinetics benefit from solution-mediated transport and crystal growth, as shown for Te/Cu/K oxoanion frameworks (SS3, SS5) and selenite/chromate hydrothermal phases (TS1, TS4). The major F3 penalty is structural: none of the CS/SS/TS analogues demonstrates cubic BaClO₆ with octahedral ClO₆, so target nucleation remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Oxidative solid-state / sealed-tube

There is insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS for a solid-state route to BaClO₆, so this route is mostly extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is penalized because sealed oxidizing systems with chlorates/perchlorates/peroxides pose volatility, pressure, and safety concerns [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is poor because low-temperature solid-state diffusion and reconstruction into a cubic BaO₁₂/ClO₆ framework are not supported by the provided analogues.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

One amorphization trigger is assigned because the target combines a very large Ba cation site with a much smaller Cl-centered oxoanion site [Reasonable Inference], and the target description explicitly gives BaO₁₂ and ClO₆ coordination. The unit-cell atom count is not given and the formula has only 8 atoms, so C2 is not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports a >40 atom cell. The connectivity includes corner-sharing BaO₁₂ and face-sharing BaO₁₂/ClO₆, but no edge sharing is reported, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaClO₆ at E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom is best viewed as a near-hull metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground-state compound, because it lies exactly at the high-stability cutoff but not at zero hull energy. The CS data support Ba oxyhalate solution chemistry and directly include Ba–Cl–O chlorate handling, but the closest Ba–Cl–O source also reports crystallization difficulty and rehydration sensitivity (CS3). The SS and TS data support mild solution or hydrothermal synthesis of high-oxygen oxoanion frameworks, especially in Teflon-lined autoclaves at ~220–230 °C or ~493–495 K (SS3, SS5, TS1, TS4). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution/hydrothermal transport, which avoids high-temperature decomposition and diffusion limits. The most critical limiting factor is the target’s unusual octahedral ClO₆ coordination, which is not directly supported by the provided Ba chlorate/bromate analogues that instead show trigonal non-coplanar XO₃ units (CS2, CS3).
