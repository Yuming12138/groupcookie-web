### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the most favorable boundary in the provided scoring rubric. Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull values of ~0.053–0.073 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by arc melting and annealing, supporting that phases in this metastability range can be accessible (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). Chemically similar nitride analogues also include Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ at 0.0624 eV/atom and Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ at 0.1363 eV/atom, both reported as synthesizable nitrides (CS1, CS3). The remaining uncertainty is that none of the TS examples is a Ru-containing nitride, so thermodynamic similarity supports metastability tolerance but not target-specific phase formation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melted Co–Ru alloy + gas nitridation

Route 1 is strongly supported in process logic by R₂Co₁₇ parent alloy nitridation to Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ under flowing N₂ at 450–550 °C (CS3, SS1) and by alloy preparation/annealing protocols for Co-rich intermetallics at ~900 °C for 125 h (CS2, CS5, TS1, TS2, TS5). The condition severity is substantial because the Co–Ru alloy precursor likely requires arc melting and high-temperature homogenization [Reasonable Inference], while the references show multi-step melting, annealing, milling, and nitridation workflows (CS3, CS4, SS1). Kinetically, nitrogen insertion into Co frameworks is plausible because Co–N bond lengths in the target, 1.86 Å, match Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ and Co₂N Co–N distances (CS3, SS1, SS4). The main uncertainty is whether a Co₆Ru metallic parent framework exists and can accept nitrogen without decomposing; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Co–Ru parent nitridation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure direct nitridation

Route 2 has direct evidence for forming Co₂N from Co and N₂ at ~10 GPa and ~1800 K in a DAC/laser-heating environment (SS4). This gives strong support for nitrogen incorporation into dense Co frameworks but imposes the harshest F2 penalties because the source explicitly uses GPa pressure, high temperature, supercritical nitrogen, and specialized DAC/YAG laser equipment (SS4). Kinetics may be enhanced by high nitrogen activity and high temperature, as SS4 reports nitride formation after 30 min, but ordered ternary Co/Ru cation arrangement remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient useful signal for GPa Co–Ru–N synthesis and are therefore treated as neutral for this route-specific factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Reactive sputtering/PLD

Route 3 scores best on condition severity because it avoids bulk melting and GPa pressure; the proposed temperature window is [Reasonable Inference] guided by 450–550 °C nitridation of Co-rich nitride analogues (CS3, SS1). Thin-film deposition is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so the route relies on kinetic stabilization logic for a phase at 0.050 eV/atom above hull [Reasonable Inference]. The route’s kinetic score is moderate because Co and Ru targets are available [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization of the cubic F-43m phase and exact Co:Ru:N stoichiometry are unproven by the sources. The major uncertainty is that CS/SS/TS support nitrogen chemistry and metastability tolerance, but insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS exists for direct vapor deposition of this structure type.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target structure is compact and simple: Ru is in RuCo₁₂ cuboctahedra and N is in NCo₆ octahedra, with reported corner and face sharing but no edge-sharing coexistence in the target description. The Co/Ru cation size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% [Reasonable Inference], and the formula Co₆RuN contains only eight atoms per formula unit, so no large-cell complexity trigger is apparent. This gives a low amorphization tendency and a high F4 score. The uncertainty is that thin-film deposition can still yield amorphous Co–Ru–N if substrate temperature is too low or nitrogen content is excessive [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Co₆RuN is best viewed as a borderline low-energy metastable phase rather than an assured ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom from the target data. The CS and SS analogues support the chemical feasibility of Co–N bonding and nitrogen insertion into Co-rich metallic frameworks, especially Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ and Co₂N (CS3, SS1, SS4), while the TS analogues show that nearby metastable Co-rich intermetallics around 0.05–0.07 eV/atom can be synthesized (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). The most important enabling factor is the close match between the target Co–N bond length of 1.86 Å and Co–N distances in known Co nitrides, 1.83–1.88 Å (CS3, SS1, SS4). The most important limiting factor is absence of a direct Co–Ru–N analogue in CS/SS/TS, making cation ordering and phase competition uncertain. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable, with thin-film reactive deposition offering the best balance between kinetic trapping and manageable conditions [Reasonable Inference].
