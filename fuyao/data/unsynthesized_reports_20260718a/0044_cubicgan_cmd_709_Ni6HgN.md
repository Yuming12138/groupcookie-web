### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the stated ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability cutoff, so it receives 90/100 by the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues include compounds with E_hull values around 0.056–0.103 eV/atom that are still experimentally synthesized but often require severe or controlled conditions (TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that a low hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or Hg/N retention during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

The condition severity is moderate because CdNNi₃ forms at only 743 K under NH₃ using a tube furnace and alumina boats, without high pressure (SS-3). Deductions are mainly due to NH₃, multiple grinding/sintering steps, and volatile/toxic Hg handling; the Hg volatility penalty is [Reasonable Inference], while Cd volatility compensation is explicitly supported by 20% excess CdO in SS-3. Kinetically, this is the best route because the target’s Ni–N bond length and NNi₆ octahedra match the CdNNi₃ analogue closely (SS-3; target data), though dense Ni powder and HgO decomposition may slow or complicate reaction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Ni-rich intermetallic analogues are commonly prepared by arc-melting or induction melting under Ar, with remelting and annealing for homogeneity (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-1, SS-4, SS-5). However, these CS/SS sources provide insufficient evidence for nitrogen uptake, so the nitridation step must be inferred from CdNNi₃ (SS-3). Kinetics are less favorable than Route 1 because a prealloyed Ni–Hg phase would still require substantial structural reconstruction to incorporate N into Ni₆ octahedra [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Thin-film synthesis is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS and is therefore entirely [Reasonable Inference]. Its condition severity is not extreme because it avoids bulk high pressure and high temperature, but it requires vacuum/reactive plasma and toxic/volatile Hg control [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics and composition control are the main weaknesses, since Hg loss during crystallization and the fixed Ni₆HgN stoichiometry likely create a narrow growth window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

Only one amorphization trigger is clearly activated: large Ni–Hg cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a large >40-atom cell, and its reported polyhedral connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not simultaneous corner/edge/face sharing (target data). The low-to-medium amorphization risk is consistent with the simple cubic motif and close structural relationship to crystalline CdNNi₃ (SS-3), but exact unit-cell contents are not available in the prompt.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ni₆HgN is likely a near-ground-state or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which lies at the high-synthesizability cutoff. The strongest support comes from the structurally similar CdNNi₃ nitride, which has the same essential large-cation Ni₁₂ cuboctahedron / NNi₆ octahedron topology and nearly identical Ni–N bond length (SS-3; target data). Chemically similar Ni-rich intermetallics show that Ni-rich metallic frameworks are accessible by standard metallurgical routes, but they do not directly validate nitrogen incorporation (CS-1, CS-3 to CS-5). The most important enabling factor is the close CdNNi₃ structural analogue formed under moderate NH₃ conditions (SS-3). The most important limiting factor is retaining Hg while simultaneously nitriding the Ni framework, because Hg is volatile/toxic and no supplied source directly demonstrates Hg–Ni–N synthesis [Reasonable Inference].
