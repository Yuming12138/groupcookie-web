### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 scoring range. Many close analogues in CS/SS/TS, including Ba(IrSi)₂, EuPt₂Si₂, Eu₂Pt₃Si₅, HoPt₂Si₂, PuPt₂Si₂, and Pt₆Si₅, also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS/SS/TS). This supports the idea that Pt–Si intermetallic frameworks with electropositive spacer atoms can be true ground-state phases. The remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially with volatile Cs [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + sealed anneal

Arc melting is strongly supported for HoPt₂Si₂ and PuPt₂Si₂, which used Ar atmospheres and repeated melting or annealing for homogeneity (SS/TS). The severity score is reduced because arc melting locally exceeds very high temperatures and because Cs must be retained in a sealed secondary step [Reasonable Inference]. Inert Ar/He atmospheres are standard and not unusually severe, as shown by EuPt₂Si₂ and HoPt₂Si₂ preparations (CS/SS/TS). The major uncertainty is whether Cs can be introduced without loss or reaction during post-annealing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + sealed anneal

Elemental Pt and Si are low-activity, refractory precursors, but arc melting gives good initial homogenization, as repeated remelting improved PuPt₂Si₂ samples (SS/TS). Pt–Si bond distances in the target overlap with those in HoPt₂Si₂, EuPt₂Si₂, and Pt₆Si₅, supporting Pt–Si network formation (SS/TS). However, the target I4 Cs(PtSi)₄ topology is not directly represented, so structural reorganization after melting/annealing may be significant. The route is kinetically feasible but not optimal because Cs incorporation after high-T alloying is difficult [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed solid-state reaction

The Ba–Ir–Si analogue was made by direct reaction of elements at 1100 °C for 50 h in corundum crucibles sealed in quartz under Ar (CS). This supports a moderate-condition solid-state approach, but Cs requires sealed containment and air-free handling [Reasonable Inference]. Multiple grind/anneal cycles may be necessary because Pt and Si diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS specifically supports direct Cs–Pt–Si solid-state synthesis, so the route is not scored higher.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed solid-state reaction

The kinetics are limited by dense elemental Pt and Si, consistent with the long 50 h thermal treatment used in Ba–Ir–Si synthesis (CS). Pt–Si intermetallics are well represented among structural and thermodynamic analogues, so the target bonding is plausible (SS/TS). However, direct solid-state reaction may favor binary Pt silicides before Cs insertion, similar to phase-control issues noted for EuPt₂Si₂ where extra lines likely from Pt silicides were observed (CS/SS/TS). This route is feasible but may require repeated annealing and careful stoichiometry.

#### F2 — Route 3: Flux growth

Eu₂Pt₃Si₅ was successfully grown from In flux with heating to 1273 K, annealing at 1123 K, slow cooling, centrifugation, and washing (CS/SS/TS). These multiple steps and vacuum sealing increase condition severity, but the maximum temperature is lower than arc melting. Cs air sensitivity and volatility add additional constraints [Reasonable Inference]. The route receives the best overall score because flux growth directly addresses diffusion and phase-selection problems.

#### F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

Flux growth improves precursor activity by dissolving Pt and Si and enabling transport, as demonstrated for Eu₂Pt₃Si₅ where direct heating produced impurities but flux growth gave pure single crystals (CS/SS/TS). This strongly supports improved kinetics relative to bulk solid-state reaction. The target is not isostructural with Eu₂Pt₃Si₅, so structural reconstruction remains only partially supported. The main uncertainty is whether Cs remains in the melt and incorporates into the target instead of forming Cs-rich side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4: Thin-film co-deposition

Pt₆Si₅ was synthesized by Pt/Si e-beam deposition followed by 200–750 °C vacuum annealing, which avoids very high temperatures (SS/TS). The need for high vacuum is a controlled-atmosphere penalty, and Cs volatility likely requires capping or low-temperature processing [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS data directly demonstrates Cs-containing thin-film silicide formation, so transferability is uncertain. The route is plausible for exploratory phase screening but less attractive for bulk synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 4: Thin-film co-deposition

Pt and Si vapor sources are directly supported by the Pt₆Si₅ thin-film analogue (SS/TS). Cs vapor availability is not the issue; rather, maintaining the correct Cs:Pt:Si ratio during annealing is difficult because Cs is volatile [Reasonable Inference]. Pt–Si thin films required careful annealing to select specific silicide stoichiometries, indicating narrow phase control (SS/TS). Therefore the kinetic score is moderate despite low-temperature processing.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large size mismatch between Cs and the Pt–Si framework increases structural strain and amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide a unit-cell atom count or explicit polyhedral sharing topology, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for C2 and C3; these are treated as not triggered but uncertain. Crystalline analogues with large electropositive cations and Pt–Si networks exist, including EuPt₂Si₂ and HoPt₂Si₂ (CS/SS/TS), which lowers the concern. The assigned score reflects low–medium amorphization risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs(PtSi)₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because its reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar Pt–Si intermetallics are also hull-stable (CS/SS/TS). The analogue support is strongest for Pt–Si framework formation and for high-temperature inert-atmosphere or flux methods, but it is weaker for direct Cs incorporation because no provided analogue contains Cs. The most critical enabling factor is the robust Pt–Si bonding network, with target Pt–Si distances similar to known Pt–Si silicides and rare-earth Pt silicides (SS/TS). The most critical limiting factor is retention and stoichiometric control of volatile, air-sensitive Cs during high-temperature synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth is the preferred route because it reduces diffusion barriers and has precedent for producing pure complex Pt–Si crystals when direct heating gives impurities (CS/SS/TS).
