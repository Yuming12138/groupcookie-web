### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score under the supplied rubric. Multiple chemically similar nitrides and Sc oxychalcogenide references are also at or near the hull, including CS1, CS3, CS4, and CS5, supporting that low-hull nitrides/oxycompounds can be isolable. TS similarly contains E_hull = 0 examples such as CeO, HfN, BaNbO₃, and EuNbO₃ (TS1–TS4). SS is less uniformly supportive because several structurally similar oxides lie ~0.025–0.069 eV/atom above hull, so SS gives only moderate thermodynamic signal rather than decisive confirmation (SS1–SS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct high-temperature solid-state reaction

The severity score is penalized because Sc₂SO₂ synthesis requires 1500 °C and additional high-temperature processing (CS4), while refractory HfN analogues require 1500–2100 °C and long homogenization (TS2). Ambient-pressure solid-state nitride syntheses are feasible in CS2, CS3, and CS5, but they often require controlled N₂ atmospheres, glovebox handling, and careful phase control. The kinetics score is limited by refractory Sc₂O₃/ScN/Sc feedstocks and the likely need to reconstruct into ordered mixed-anion ScN₃O₃ octahedra [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is substantial because no CS/SS/TS source reports direct synthesis of Sc₂NO or a close Sc–N–O Caswellsilverite analogue.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Alkali-metal flux / azide-assisted crystal growth

The flux route receives the best severity score because CS1 demonstrates nitride crystal growth at 760 °C in Na-containing flux, and TS5 demonstrates oxide flux growth at ≤1000 °C with slow cooling. It is penalized for sealed-tube volatile/reactive Na, NaN₃ toxicity, and air-sensitive handling, all directly implied by CS1 and TS5. Kinetically, flux growth is favorable because liquid-phase transport bypasses slow solid-state diffusion, as shown by CS1 and TS5. The major uncertainty is whether Sc₂O₃/ScN dissolve sufficiently in Na flux and whether the flux maintains the narrow N/O/redox window required for Sc₂NO [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-pressure high-temperature stabilization

The high-pressure route is penalized heavily for pressure because similar monoxides and perovskites require 15–80 kbar equipment in SS1, TS1, and TS3. Kinetics are only moderate because dense octahedral phases are favored under high pressure in SS1/TS1/TS3, but precursor diffusion remains limited by refractory Sc compounds [Reasonable Inference]. This route is most justified as a contingency if ambient-pressure synthesis yields ScN + Sc₂O₃ or other competing phases. The uncertainty is that the target’s E_hull = 0 does not itself indicate a need for high pressure, so HP synthesis may be unnecessary.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Sc, so cation-radius mismatch is absent by formula and target structure. The structure is compositionally simple, so a large unit-cell amorphization penalty is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. Mixed corner- and edge-sharing octahedral connectivity is explicitly described for ScN₃O₃, NSc₆, and OSc₆ polyhedra in the target data, so one amorphization-risk check is triggered. The risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc₂NO is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several CS/TS analogues are also reported as hull-stable phases (CS1, CS3–CS5, TS1–TS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar nitride/oxycompound routes: Na-flux nitride growth in sealed Nb tubes (CS1), direct nitride solid-state synthesis under N₂ (CS2, CS3, CS5), and high-temperature Sc₂SO₂ synthesis from Sc₂O₃/Sc₂S₃ (CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the simple single-cation Sc framework, which avoids cation-site disorder and radius mismatch [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous control of nitrogen activity, oxygen activity, and Sc redox state, because chemically similar nitrides often show phase-purity challenges (CS1–CS3) and Sc oxycompound ordering requires careful thermal treatment (CS4). Overall, flux growth is the best first attempt because it reduces diffusion barriers and is directly supported by nitride and oxide flux analogues (CS1, TS5).
