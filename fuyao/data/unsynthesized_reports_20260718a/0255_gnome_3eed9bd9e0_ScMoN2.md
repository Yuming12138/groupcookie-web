### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is predicted to lie exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a high thermodynamic score. Stable nitride analogues include MnMoN₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1) and MgMoN₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (TS-2), supporting the plausibility of stable Mo-containing ternary nitrides. The main uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as MnMoN₂ and MnWN₂ still required narrow phase control despite favorable stability (CS-1, SS-4).

#### F2 — Route 1: Mixed-oxide ammonolysis

The route uses moderate temperatures, because MnMoN₂ and MnWN₂ were synthesized at 625–800 °C under NH₃ (CS-1, SS-4). The major severity penalties are the reactive/toxic NH₃ atmosphere and the need for precursor preparation plus ammonolysis, consistent with CS-1 and SS-4. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for an exact Sc–Mo oxide precursor, so precursor selection is extrapolated.

#### F3 — Route 1: Mixed-oxide ammonolysis

Kinetics are moderate to poor because oxide-to-nitride conversion requires significant reconstruction [Reasonable Inference], and SS-4 reports impurity phases and kinetic sensitivity for MnWN₂. The favorable point is that chemically related MnMoN₂ forms at only 625 °C under NH₃ (CS-1), suggesting Mo–N frameworks can form without extreme temperatures. Remaining uncertainty is whether Sc enters the targeted layered P-3m1 arrangement rather than forming ScN plus Mo nitrides.

#### F2 — Route 2: Azide autoclave

The severity is moderate because MgMoN₂ was made at only 700 °C for 10 h in a sealed autoclave (TS-2). Penalties arise from NaN₃ hazard, sealed-system pressure, and likely washing/post-processing as in TS-2. Insufficient evidence from TS indicates no high-pressure >5 GPa requirement, so no high-pressure penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2: Azide autoclave

The kinetics are limited by using elemental Sc and Mo powders, especially refractory Mo [Reasonable Inference]. TS-2 supports that NaN₃ can drive formation of a stable Mo ternary nitride at 700 °C, but the MgMoN₂ analogue does not prove Sc:Mo = 1:2 ordering. Structural reconstruction from metals to layered ScMo₂N₂ is therefore a major uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3: Molecular precursor ammonolysis

The route inherits moderate temperature conditions from CrWN₂ synthesis at 750 °C in NH₃ (SS-3). It is penalized for multi-step processing because SS-3 explicitly uses precursor formation, hydrolysis, and ammonolysis. It is also penalized for hazardous/moisture-sensitive chloride and NH₃ chemistry, consistent with SS-3’s glovebox and NH₃-flow setup.

#### F3 — Route 3: Molecular precursor ammonolysis

This route has the best bulk kinetics because molecular-level mixing reduces diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], and SS-3 demonstrates successful formation of a ternary nitride from such a precursor. The remaining kinetic barrier is crystallizing the correct Sc/Mo-ordered P-3m1 layered phase rather than binary nitrides or disordered ternary nitride. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for Sc–Mo chloride precursor chemistry specifically.

#### F2 — Route 4: Reactive thin-film deposition

The route scores well on severity because it avoids bulk diffusion and does not require sealed autoclave or >1000 °C processing [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2 supports nitride film formation under reactive gas chemistry, but it used NbCl₅/NH₃/H₂ CVD, not Sc–Mo sputtering. The main severity penalty is the controlled reactive nitrogen atmosphere.

#### F3 — Route 4: Reactive thin-film deposition

Thin-film kinetics benefit from short diffusion lengths and independent Sc/Mo flux control [Reasonable Inference]. However, crystallization of the specific layered MAX-like P-3m1 phase is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS, and SS-2 shows transition-metal nitride phases can be composition-sensitive. Thus, despite a favorable numerical F2, the route has higher uncertainty than Route 3.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple layered P-3m1 description and no evidence of a large unit cell, so C2 is not triggered. The target reports corner and edge sharing but no face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered by the specified criterion. The Sc–N and Mo–N bond lengths are close, suggesting limited cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], but exact ionic-radius comparison is uncertain because Mo coordination/oxidation state is unusual.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc(MoN)₂ is likely a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically related Mo ternary nitrides made by ammonolysis or azide-assisted solid-state routes, especially MnMoN₂ (CS-1) and MgMoN₂ (TS-2), and from structurally related ternary nitride ammonolysis routes such as CrWN₂ and MnWN₂ (SS-3, SS-4). The most important enabling factor is the existence of multiple Mo–N ternary nitride analogues with similar Mo–N bond lengths near 2.12–2.14 Å (CS-1, TS-2, SS-3). The most important limiting factor is phase-pure cation ordering in the Sc:Mo = 1:2 layered P-3m1 structure, because analogous ammonolysis systems often show narrow phase windows or impurity formation (CS-1, SS-4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful nitrogen chemical potential, precursor homogeneity, and stoichiometry control.
