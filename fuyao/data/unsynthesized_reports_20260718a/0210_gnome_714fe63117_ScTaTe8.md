### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin by the requested rubric. This is reinforced by many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar tellurides also being on the hull: TiGeTe6, TaPtTe5, TaNiTe5, NbNiTe5, ZrGeTe4, HfGeTe4, and TaTe4I all report E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS #1–#5; SS #1–#5; TS #1–#5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity, as impurity phases are reported for TaPtTe5, Ta4Pd3Te16, and NbNiTe5 syntheses (CS #2, CS #4, CS #5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for vacuum sealing, volatile Te in a sealed system, and multistep staged heating/cooling, all of which are directly reflected in Te-rich telluride syntheses (CS #1, CS #2, CS #4). It is not penalized for >1000 °C because the proposed range is ≤950–1000 °C and analogues form at 900–1000 °C (CS #1, CS #2, CS #4). The main uncertainty is whether ScTaTe8 needs a temperature above the analogue window; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly addresses Sc–Ta–Te reaction temperatures.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by refractory Ta and elemental-powder diffusion [Reasonable Inference], although Te excess and Te-rich melts are beneficial in analogous syntheses (CS #1, CS #2). The target’s 1D ribbon character is encouraging because TiGeTe6 is also 1D and is obtained by Te-rich sintering (CS #1; SS #3; TS #3). However, long heating and slow cooling in the analogue indicate slow ordering, and the lack of Sc analogues leaves substantial uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has lower thermal severity because structurally and thermodynamically similar ZrGeTe4/HfGeTe4 flux syntheses operate at 973 K, about 700 °C (SS #1, SS #2; TS #1, TS #2). It still requires vacuum sealing, volatile Te containment, and multiple operations including flux washing (SS #1, SS #2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates whether Sc metal requires additional special atmosphere beyond standard sealed-tube handling.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves kinetics relative to direct sintering because KCl flux analogues crystallize low-dimensional Te-rich phases at ~700 °C with controlled cooling (SS #1, SS #2; TS #1, TS #2). The flux route is only partially structurally matched because the strongest flux analogues are Zr/Hf–Ge–Te layered phases, whereas ScTaTe8 is a 1D Sc/Ta telluride [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-control uncertainty remains because ZrGeTe4 work notes sensitivity to ratios/nonstoichiometric phases (SS #1; TS #1).

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT is more severe than flux in handling complexity because it uses volatile/toxic I2, requires vacuum sealing, and needs a temperature gradient for many days (CS #3, CS #5). The absolute temperature is moderate, typically 675–750 °C in Ta/Nb telluride CVT analogues (CS #3, CS #5; SS #4; TS #4). The key uncertainty is whether iodine assists ScTaTe8 growth or instead diverts chemistry into iodotellurides, as TaTe4I is a stable related phase (SS #5; TS #5).

#### F3 — Route 3

I2 can greatly improve mass transport, and one Ta/Pd/Te analogue states iodine is necessary for crystal growth (CS #3). This supports a higher kinetic score than direct bulk sintering, but Sc transport chemistry is not evidenced by CS/SS/TS and remains unknown. Iodine also introduces a competing structural pathway because TaTe4I is synthesized from Ta/Te/I under sealed conditions (SS #5; TS #5), making phase purity less predictable.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple stated local structure—Sc and Ta are each 8-coordinate to Te, and Te is 2-coordinate—so there is no direct evidence for highly mixed polyhedral connectivity in the target description. A cation-size mismatch check is triggered by expected Sc/Ta radius differences [Reasonable Inference], while unit-cell size >40 atoms is not supported by the provided target text; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS addresses the exact target cell size. The amorphization risk is therefore low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScTaTe8 is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and many close Te-rich analogues are also calculated on the hull (CS #1–#5; SS #1–#5; TS #1–#5). The analogue support is strong for Te-rich low-dimensional tellurides, especially 8-coordinate Ta–Te frameworks and 1D/2D telluride motifs (CS #1–#4; SS #1–#5; TS #1–#5), but no reference directly demonstrates Sc–Ta–Te synthesis, so Sc/Ta ordering is extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is a Te-rich sealed environment or flux, which compensates Te volatility and improves transport, as shown by excess-Te and flux syntheses (CS #1, CS #2; SS #1, SS #2). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition: Ta telluride analogues frequently report binary/ternary impurities or nonstoichiometry (CS #2, CS #4, CS #5). Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful stoichiometry, slow cooling, and iterative phase optimization.
