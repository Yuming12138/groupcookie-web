### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the top of the 90–100 bin specified by the rubric. Thermodynamic analogues at comparable hull energies are reported synthesized, including Li2CuO2 at 0.047 eV/atom (TS1), Na3Cu2SbO6 at 0.056 eV/atom (TS2), Li8TbO6 at 0.0507 eV/atom (TS4), and LiGaO2 at 0.066 eV/atom under high pressure (TS5). CS and SS also provide stable Li-rich oxide analogues, especially Li6TeO6 with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, SS2). The remaining uncertainty is that a 0.05 eV/atom cubic polymorph may be metastable relative to a lower-symmetry Li–Se–O phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the closest Li6TeO6 synthesis required sealed Ag bombs inside Ar-filled glass ampoules for 30 d at 740 °C (CS1, SS2). Li-rich rare-earth oxides similarly used sealed Ag capsules in evacuated Supremax glass ampoules (TS3, TS4), supporting the need for controlled containment. Selenium precursors add a volatile/toxic component risk [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly confirms Se precursor stability under these exact conditions, so this part carries uncertainty.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderately favorable because Li-rich oxides form at relatively low temperatures, e.g., Li8SnO6 at 600 °C (SS3), Li8PbO6 at 600 °C (SS5), and Li6TeO6 at 740 °C (CS1, SS2). However, Li6TeO6 required 30 d and crystal growth was reported as very difficult (CS1, SS2), implying slow ordering and phase-selection issues. Structural transfer is strong chemically but imperfect crystallographically because Li6TeO6 is trigonal, while the target is cubic Fm-3m (CS1, SS2; target description). Thus F3 is good but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses conventional ceramic conditions supported by Li5SbO5 in dry O2 at 900 °C/24 h (CS2, SS4) and Li3AsO4 in air with repeated grinding/heating to 1273 K (CS3). These conditions avoid high pressure and extreme temperature, so the rubric severity is relatively low. The major penalties are inferred Se oxide volatility and toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates a selenium analogue by open ceramic firing, so sealed or covered crucibles are recommended [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Ceramic kinetics are acceptable because Li carbonate/oxide plus p-block oxide routes are successful for Li5SbO5 and Li3AsO4 (CS2, CS3). The method likely requires repeated grinding/calcining because Li3AsO4 used alternate grinding and heating phases before final heating (CS3). Structural reconstruction is more demanding than hydrothermal crystallization because carbonate/oxide precursors must transform into a highly ordered Li6SeO6 framework [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Se(VI) survives without volatilization or reduction during firing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 scores well under the provided deduction rubric because 700 °C and 1500 bar hydrothermal conditions do not exceed the >5 GPa high-pressure penalty (CS5). It is nevertheless experimentally specialized, since Li4TeO5 required a high-pressure hydrothermal apparatus (CS5). The main deduction is toxic/corrosive Se(VI) chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms Li6SeO6 hydrothermal formation specifically, but CS5 gives the closest process analogue.

#### F3 — Route 3

Hydrothermal kinetics are favorable because LiOH·H2O and Te(OH)6 produced crystalline Li4TeO5 in 48 h at 700 °C/1500 bar (CS5). Solution-mediated transport should reduce diffusion limitations relative to solid-state routes [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is phase selectivity: Li4TeO5 differs from Li6SeO6 in stoichiometry and structure, and the exact Li6MO6 analogue Li6TeO6 was made by sealed solid-state rather than hydrothermal synthesis (CS1, CS5). Therefore Route 3 is best for kinetic access but not guaranteed for the target polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains Li and high-valent Se in distinct coordination environments, and large cation-size/charge contrast can promote local ordering frustration [Reasonable Inference]. The target description explicitly includes corner and edge sharing of OLi4Se square pyramids, while Li6TeO6 and Li5SbO5 analogues also show mixed edge/corner-sharing polyhedral networks (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms target face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The likely conventional Fm-3m cell size triggers C2 only by crystallographic inference, not by direct CS/SS/TS evidence [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li6SeO6 is likely a near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, and TS analogues with similar hull energies are experimentally accessible (TS1, TS2, TS4, TS5). The strongest support comes from Li6TeO6, which shares the Li6MO6 chemical motif and MO6 octahedral framework, but Li6TeO6 is trigonal and difficult to crystallize, so it does not prove the cubic target directly (CS1, SS2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of many Li-rich oxide analogues synthesized between 600 and 900 °C or hydrothermally at 700 °C, showing that Li-rich oxide frameworks are accessible (CS1, CS2, CS5, SS3, SS5). The most critical limiting factor is selenium chemistry: maintaining Se(VI), preventing volatile Se loss, and avoiding competing lithium selenates/selenites are all inferred risks [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but likely requires sealed or hydrothermal conditions rather than a simple open crucible synthesis.
