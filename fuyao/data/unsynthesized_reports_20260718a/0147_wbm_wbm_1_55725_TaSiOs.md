### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin by the rubric. Thermodynamically similar TaRuSi, TaIrSi, HfOsSi, TaRhSi, and TaReSi also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so TS provides strong supporting evidence for ground-state-like stability (TS-1–TS-5). CS and SS do not contradict this; the closest chemical and structural analogues are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that computed E_hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase-field width [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is not severe by high-pressure or highly reactive-atmosphere criteria because the analogue TaIrSi was made under Ar, not high pressure, and annealed at 1200 K / 927 °C (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). It does require a multi-step arc-melt/remelt/anneal workflow, which is explicitly reported for TaIrSi/NbIrSi-type analogues (CS-1, CS-2). The main added penalty is Os handling, because elemental Os is used in HfOsSi-type synthesis but must be protected from oxidation to toxic OsO₄ [Os use from SS-3/TS-3; toxicity is Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that sealed synthesis is required specifically for volatility in Route 1, so that factor is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetically, Route 1 is limited by refractory elemental precursors, especially Ta and Os, so precursor activity and solid-state diffusion scores are low [Reasonable Inference]. Arc-melting mitigates this because close analogues are homogenized by repeated remelting and then annealed, indicating that the liquid/alloy stage can overcome slow diffusion (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Structural reconstruction is favorable because TaIrSi has the same Pnma structure and nearly identical coordination motifs, which strongly reduces the structural search burden (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is whether Os stabilizes the same Pnma polymorph rather than an Ima2 variant, as HfOsSi is reported in Ima2 (SS-3, TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because long evacuated-ampoule annealing is explicitly used for TaRuSi/TaReSi analogues at 900 °C for 7–10 days (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5). Some related Pnma silicides require 1200 °C anneals for several days, so a >1000 °C penalty is plausible during optimization (CS-5, SS-4, TS-4). The route also includes multiple steps—arc-melting, crushing, grinding, pelletizing, sealing, and annealing—as reported for TaRuSi/TaReSi analogues (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves volatile precursor loss as the reason for sealing, so the volatility factor is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Route 2 uses the same low-activity elemental precursors, so precursor activity remains poor relative to nitrate/sol-gel routes [Reasonable Inference; elemental route from SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3]. Long annealing after crushing and pelletizing improves equilibration and ordering, as used for TaRuSi/TaReSi (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-5). However, several close long-anneal analogues are Ima2 rather than target Pnma, so structural reconstruction/phase selection is less direct than for TaIrSi (SS-1, SS-3, SS-5). The remaining uncertainty is whether long annealing favors the desired Pnma phase or a competing Os-containing polymorph [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target belongs to a compact ordered intermetallic silicide family rather than a large, complex multication oxide/network solid, and the closest Pnma analogue has only 12 atoms per conventional cell as Ta₄Si₄Ir₄ (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient explicit polyhedral-connectivity information for C3, so that check is neutral rather than penalizing. CS/SS/TS also provide insufficient ionic-radius data for C1, but Ta/Os metallic radii are expected to be reasonably close [Reasonable Inference]. The low amorphization risk is further supported by multiple crystalline analogues synthesized by arc-melting rather than glass-forming methods (CS-1–CS-5, SS-1–SS-5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TaSiOs is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom and the closest thermodynamic analogues also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1–TS-5). The synthesis case is strong because TaIrSi is an almost direct isostructural Pnma analogue made by arc-melting plus annealing, while HfOsSi confirms that Os-containing ternary silicides can be accessed by arc-melting under Ar (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2, SS-3, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a robust arc-melted Ta–late-transition-metal–Si structural family with similar coordination and bond lengths (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-4). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic homogenization of refractory Ta/Os and maintaining exact 1:1:1 composition while avoiding oxygen/Os oxidation [Reasonable Inference supported by repeated remelting/annealing requirements in CS-1, SS-1, TS-1]. Overall, the material is quite synthesizable as a bulk polycrystalline intermetallic, with Route 1 preferred.
