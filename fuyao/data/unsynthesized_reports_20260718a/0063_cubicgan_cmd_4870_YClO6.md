### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0500\) eV/atom falls exactly at the boundary of the highest stability scoring band, so it is treated as marginally metastable but accessible. Thermodynamic analogues with comparable hull energies are reported as synthesizable, including AgCu₂(SeO₅)₂ at 0.04796 eV/atom and Tl(CuSeO₅)₂ at 0.07813 eV/atom (TS3, TS5). CS and SS also show that some similar halate/oxysalt phases are on the hull and experimentally crystallized, such as Sr(O₃Cl)₂ and Pb(O₃Cl)₂ (CS3, SS1). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not validate the unusual ClO₆ local chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 scores highly because the closest chlorate analogues were crystallized from aqueous solution under mild conditions, including room-temperature saturated-solution crystallization for Sr chlorate (CS3, CS4). SS1 shows that some related halates require slow evaporation at 120–130 °C, vacuum dehydration, or glove-box handling, but those are still much milder than high-temperature solid-state synthesis (SS1). The only major condition penalty is the hazardous oxidizing nature of HClO₃/chlorate chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS3]. There is insufficient evidence from TS for low-temperature chlorate crystallization, so TS is neutral for this specific route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solution crystallization should have excellent precursor activity and ion transport because Sr/Pb chlorates were obtained from aqueous solution rather than high-temperature diffusion (CS3, CS4, SS1). The kinetic weakness is structural: all close chlorate analogues contain ClO₃ trigonal units, while the target has ClO₆ octahedra and YO₁₂ cuboctahedra [Reasonable Inference from target structure versus CS3/CS4/SS1]. Thus, nucleation of the predicted cubic phase may be much less favorable than formation of conventional hydrated or anhydrous Y chlorate phases [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for any experimentally known YClO₆-type crystallization pathway.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Hydrothermal analogues use moderate temperatures of about 220–250 °C and standard Teflon-lined autoclaves, as shown for Sc oxysalts and Cu/Au/Tl selenite/selenate compounds (CS2, CS5, SS2, SS3, SS5). These conditions are not extreme, and no external reactive atmosphere is required in those analogues (CS2, CS5, SS2). The penalties come from sealed heating of oxychlorine oxidizers and corrosive oxidizing acids, which are safety and volatility concerns [Reasonable Inference]. TS hydrothermal analogues support that hydrothermal oxysalts can form under standard conditions, but they do not directly address oxychlorine volatility (TS1, TS3, TS5).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis provides high precursor mobility and strong mineralization, as demonstrated by Sc and Cu/Tl/Au oxysalts forming single crystals at 423–523 K ranges (CS2, CS5, SS2, SS3, SS5). Nitrate/acid media are especially relevant because Sc(NO₃)₃, H₂SeO₄, and HNO₃ were used in CS2, and pH-controlled acidic hydrothermal synthesis was used in CS5. However, the target requires a local ClO₆ motif absent from the hydrothermal analogues, so the structural-reconstruction score remains limited [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS that hydrothermal conditions stabilize octahedral oxychlorine.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized heavily because TS4 used high pressures between 15 and 70 kbar in a tetrahedral-anvil apparatus, and the proposed target route may need the upper part of that pressure range [Reasonable Inference from TS4]. High-pressure equipment is specialized and less scalable than solution or hydrothermal routes (TS4). Additional penalties arise from sealed oxychlorine oxidizers and moisture-sensitive chlorides [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure Y–Cl–O synthesis, so their route-specific signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High pressure can enable dense oxysalt phases, as shown by ZnSeO₃ and related compounds in TS4, but the method still relies on solid-state reorganization of refractory oxide precursors. Y₂O₃ is expected to react sluggishly compared with nitrate/aqueous precursors [Reasonable Inference], and no direct analogue demonstrates fast Y–Cl–O ordering. Pressure may help stabilize the dense ClO₆/YO₁₂ topology [Reasonable Inference from TS4], but it does not eliminate the need for phase screening. Therefore Route 3 is a backup strategy rather than the recommended first attempt.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a very large Y-centered 12-coordinate polyhedron and a much smaller Cl-centered octahedron, giving a strong cation-size/coordination mismatch that triggers C1 [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description includes corner sharing and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. The formula is small, and the supplied description does not prove a >40-atom unit cell; CS/SS/TS give insufficient evidence for that trigger. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is best viewed as a borderline metastable phase because its energy above hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which is commonly accessible but not strongly ground-state-stable [Reasonable Inference; TS3/TS5 show comparable metastable oxysalts can be synthesized]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically close chlorate analogues that crystallize from aqueous solution under mild conditions (CS3, CS4, SS1), while hydrothermal oxysalt analogues support a backup route for complex oxide frameworks (CS2, CS5, SS2, SS5). The most important enabling factor is solution mobility: aqueous or hydrothermal media avoid slow solid-state diffusion and are proven for halate/oxysalt crystallization (CS3, CS4, CS2, CS5). The most important limiting factor is the target’s unusual ClO₆ octahedral coordination, which is not represented in the chlorate analogues, where Cl is trigonal ClO₃ (CS3, CS4, SS1). Therefore, synthesis is plausible as a metastable crystallization target, but phase identification should explicitly check for conventional Y chlorates/perchlorates/hydrates as competing products [Reasonable Inference].
