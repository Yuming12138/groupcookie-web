### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

NaMgP is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom from the target data. The strongest thermodynamic analogue, KMgP, is also E_hull = 0.0 and is identical in Matlockite/P4/nmm topology except K replaces Na (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Additional hull-stable Na-containing analogues include NaLiS, NaAlSi, NaSrP, and K₂NaInP₂ (CS-4, CS-5, SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is experimental rather than thermodynamic, mainly volatility/phase competition.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits high-temperature severity from KMgP, which used 1150 °C followed by several days at 800 °C (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). Sealed refractory containment is justified because KMgP used Ta crucibles/iron ampoule under Ar and NaAlSi used Ta under Ar (CS-1, CS-5). The route is not high-pressure and does not require H₂/NH₃, so those deductions are not applied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any need for high pressure. The limiting condition is handling volatile Na/P in a sealed system [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The exact structural match to KMgP strongly favors crystallization into the target Matlockite structure (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). However, direct elemental reactions require complete ternary phase formation from Na, Mg, and P, and KMgP required a high-temperature step plus multi-day anneal (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The low cation melting points of Na and Mg should help mass transport [Reasonable Inference], but Mg–P framework ordering may still be slow.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C operation because related binary-precursor/sealed-ampoule syntheses occur near 870–950 K (CS-4, SS-5, TS-5). It is penalized for evacuated/sealed operation because K₂NaInP₂ uses evacuated sealed silica tubes (SS-5, TS-5). It is also penalized for air-sensitive phosphide precursors and likely multi-step precursor preparation [Reasonable Inference], while insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any high-pressure requirement.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Na₃P and Mg₃P₂ should be more chemically matched to the target than separated elements because they already contain alkali/phosphide and Mg/phosphide bonding [Reasonable Inference]. Binary precursor use is supported by NaLiS from Li₂S/Na₂S and K₂NaInP₂ from Na/KP/InP (CS-4, SS-5, TS-5). The drawback is structural reconstruction from binary phosphides into the layered Matlockite phase, and NaLiS required 7 d tempering (CS-4).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth lowers the required temperature relative to direct KMgP synthesis because NaSrP was grown at 600 °C for 120 h using Na/Sn or related fluxes (SS-2, TS-2). The route is penalized for vacuum/sealed operation, volatile Na/P, air-sensitive handling, and multi-step flux removal, all reflected in the NaSrP procedure (SS-2, TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for high pressure or >1000 °C in the flux route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux conditions should provide the best kinetics through liquid-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference], and NaSrP single crystals were observed from Na/Sn metallic alloy or Li₃N flux conditions (SS-2, TS-2). The low synthesis temperature and long dwell are favorable for crystallization rather than rapid solid-state reconstruction (SS-2, TS-2). The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the exact Matlockite NaMgP structure is supported by KMgP and Na-containing Matlockites (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5), but not directly by a flux-grown NaMgP report.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple Matlockite/P4/nmm structure with Na and Mg on distinct crystallographic sites, matching stable crystalline analogues KMgP, NaLiS, and NaAlSi (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). One amorphization risk is the substantial Na/Mg size/charge contrast [Reasonable Inference], but this is mitigated by the ordered 5-coordinate Na and tetrahedral Mg sites reported for the target and KMgP analogue (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). No evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a large unit cell or face-sharing/edge/corner coexistence that would strongly frustrate crystallization.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaMgP is likely a ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the exact K analogue KMgP is also hull-stable and experimentally synthesized in the same Matlockite/P4/nmm structure (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The analogue support is strong: KMgP provides direct Mg–P tetrahedral and alkali-site structural evidence, while NaLiS and NaAlSi demonstrate Na-containing Matlockite phases (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the very close structural and thermodynamic match to KMgP (CS-1, SS-1, TS-1). The most critical limiting factor is controlling Na and P volatility/reactivity during sealed high-temperature synthesis or flux growth [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the repeated use of sealed refractory ampoules in alkali phosphide analogues (CS-1, SS-2, SS-5, TS-2, TS-5). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but requires careful air-free sealed-tube technique.
