### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is computed exactly on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility according to the target data. The closest SS/TS analogues Ti2InN, Ti2GaN, Zr2InN, and Ti2AlN are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and adopt related H-phase or Ti–N framework structures (SS1–SS3, SS5; TS1–TS4). CS is less decisive because it contains U/Fe/P/Pt/Co systems rather than Ti–Hg–N; CS gives general evidence that intermetallic ternaries can be stable and synthesized, but insufficient evidence for Hg nitride chemistry, so CS is effectively neutral-to-weak for this factor.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The proposed route uses temperatures comparable to 500–900 °C H-phase nitride syntheses in sealed quartz ampoules, avoiding the >1000 °C penalty (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The major severity penalties are the controlled sealed environment and volatile/toxic Hg, both required by [Reasonable Inference] because Hg would not be retained in an open high-temperature reaction. Remaining uncertainty is whether quartz is compatible with Hg/Ti at the necessary temperature, so a metal inner capsule is recommended [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Structurally, this is the best-supported route because the target is an H-phase Ti2MN-type nitride matching Ti2InN/Ti2GaN/Ti2AlN topology and Ti–N distances (SS1, SS2, SS5; TS1, TS2, TS4). Kinetically, long anneals of 500–900 h in analogues indicate slow ordering/diffusion, especially from TiN/Ti precursors (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The main uncertainty is Hg incorporation into the Ti–N framework, since no SS/TS analogue contains Hg.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth is supported in chemically similar intermetallic/phosphide systems at 850–900 °C for about a week in evacuated ampoules, with slow cooling and post-treatment (CS1, CS5). For Ti2HgN, the route must be modified to lower temperature and sealed Hg-rich conditions because Hg is volatile/toxic [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for flux growth of H-phase nitrides specifically, so flux transferability from SS/TS is neutral.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
A Hg-rich self-flux could improve mass transport relative to dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the flux-assisted growth behavior in CS1/CS5. However, no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates Hg-flux synthesis of Ti2MN H-phases, and flux chemistry could instead stabilize Ti–Hg binaries or residual TiN [Reasonable Inference]. The score is slightly higher than Route 1 for kinetics but lower in condition severity.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Thin-film deposition avoids high bulk-reaction temperatures [Reasonable Inference], but it requires vacuum/reactive N₂, Hg flux control, and likely post-annealing under Hg overpressure [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source provides thin-film evidence, so CS, SS, and TS are all insufficient for this route and treated as neutral, 50. The penalty is driven mainly by process complexity and Hg handling.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The thin-film route could bypass bulk diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization into the ordered H-phase is unproven. Hg incorporation is likely difficult because of high vapor pressure and narrow stoichiometry tolerance [Reasonable Inference]. Since all CS/SS/TS evidence is absent for vapor deposition, this route receives the lowest kinetic confidence.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple H-phase ordered structure, and the structurally similar H-phase nitrides are crystalline products under long annealing (SS1–SS3, SS5; TS1–TS4). The main amorphization/disorder risk is the large Ti–Hg cation size/chemical mismatch [Reasonable Inference], while the small cell and absence of face-sharing coexistence reduce glass-forming complexity. Remaining uncertainty is whether Hg positional disorder or partial occupancy occurs under real synthesis conditions [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti2HgN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and closely related H-phase Ti2MN nitrides are also reported as hull-stable in SS/TS analogues (SS1–SS3, SS5; TS1–TS4). The structural support is strong: Ti2InN and Ti2GaN match the same H-phase P6₃/mmc framework and have very similar Ti–N distances to the target (SS1, SS2; TS1, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated robustness of the Ti2MN H-phase nitride topology under sealed-tube solid-state synthesis (SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The most critical limiting factor is Hg volatility/toxicity and retention at annealing temperature, which is not directly addressed by any CS/SS/TS analogue and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference] risk. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but experimentally demanding, with success most likely in sealed capsules using long low-to-moderate-temperature annealing.
