### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, giving a high thermodynamic score by the required rubric. Similar alkali-rich oxides in CS, SS, and TS also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so the evidence consistently supports ground-state accessibility rather than a high-energy metastable phase (CS, SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a wide experimental phase field, especially for alkali-rich and oxygen-sensitive compounds [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures, 600–700 °C, which are not severe by solid-state standards and match K–Pd oxide synthesis (SS, TS). The main penalties are controlled O₂, sealed-tube handling, and air-sensitive alkali peroxide/superoxide precursors, all consistent with the sealed KO2/PdO synthesis and alkali oxide sensitivity in analogues (CS, SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether Rb2PdO2 prefers the same oxygen chemical potential as K3Pd2O4; this is an extrapolation from K to Rb [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The KO2/PdO route indicates that reactive alkali oxidants can form square-planar Pd oxides without extreme temperatures (SS, TS). Structural kinetics are favorable because the target and K3Pd2O4 both contain square-planar PdO4 units, although the target has different stoichiometry and Rb rather than K (SS, TS). Remaining uncertainty is competition between Rb–Pd–O phases and possible PdO decomposition/reduction under non-optimal oxygen potential [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids controlled oxygen pressure and uses Ar-filled sealed tubes similar to Rb2NiO2 and Rb/K/Co/O syntheses (CS, SS, TS). Its severity is reduced by moderate temperatures, but air-sensitive Rb2O/RbO0.5 and sealed metal tubes are still necessary (CS, SS, TS). Insufficient evidence from CS for Pd-specific behavior in this route; CS mainly supports Rb2MO2-type chemistry and handling conditions, so the Pd transfer is partly [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary oxide reactions are kinetically slower than peroxide-assisted routes, and analogues often require 20–48 d anneals, indicating slow ordering in alkali-rich oxides (CS, SS, TS). The route is plausible because Rb2NiO2 and Rb/K/Co/O compounds form under similar sealed-tube conditions, but no exact Rb–Pd oxide analogue is given for this Ar route (CS, SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether Pd²⁺ square-planar coordination forms under inert conditions without an oxygen buffer [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures but requires sealed Ag/Au bombs, air-sensitive alkali oxide flux, and long multi-stage thermal programs, as seen for Rb6Al2O6 and Cs4IrO4 analogues (CS, SS, TS). The method is experimentally more cumbersome than Route 1 despite similar temperature because flux separation and container compatibility add complexity [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a direct Rb–Pd flux product, so the route is less directly validated.

#### F3 — Route 3

Alkali-rich flux should improve mass transport and crystal growth, and long alkali-oxide bomb syntheses have produced single crystals of related Rb/Cs oxides (CS, SS, TS). However, analogues also show very long anneals, up to 86–120 d, which implies narrow nucleation/growth windows and slow equilibration (CS, SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether Rb2PdO2 crystallizes before competing Rb–Pd–O phases or Pd metal/PdO impurities form [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Rb/Pd cation size contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives a simple Immm structure with one Rb, one Pd, and one O site, so there is no direct evidence for a >40 atom cell or complex mixed-connectivity network; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to trigger C2 or C3. The residual uncertainty is that SS K3Pd2O4 has mixed OK4Pd2 octahedral sharing, but the target description does not state equivalent connectivity, so that feature was not transferred automatically (SS, TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb2PdO2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally/thermodynamically similar alkali oxides are also on the hull (CS, SS, TS). The strongest support comes from K3Pd2O4, which is a synthesized Pd oxide containing square-planar PdO4 units and made by a practical sealed-tube KO2/PdO method (SS, TS). The most important enabling factor is the strong structural match of square-planar Pd–O coordination combined with moderate-temperature alkali oxide chemistry (SS, TS). The most important limiting factor is chemical handling and phase control: Rb oxides/peroxides are air-sensitive, volatile/reactive, and many related alkali-rich oxides require sealed bombs or long anneals (CS, SS, TS). Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will likely require careful oxygen-potential and Rb stoichiometry control.
