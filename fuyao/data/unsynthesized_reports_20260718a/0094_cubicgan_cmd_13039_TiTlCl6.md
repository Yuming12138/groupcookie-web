### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which is at the upper edge of the most favorable bin and therefore receives 90/100 by the provided rubric. Synthesized chemical analogues include several near-hull chlorides, such as CS 2 at 0.0 eV/atom and CS 1/CS 4/CS 5 at about 0.009–0.023 eV/atom. Structurally similar analogues also include stable or nearly stable MCl₆-containing materials, including SS 1–2 at 0.0 eV/atom and SS 5 near 0 eV/atom. TS analogues show that halides at 0.055–0.081 eV/atom can be crystallized, so the target’s 0.05 eV/atom hull energy is plausible but likely metastable rather than robustly ground-state (TS 1–5).

#### F2/F3: Route 1

Route 1 has moderate severity because it avoids high temperature and high pressure, but requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile chlorides, and air-sensitive/toxic reagents; these requirements are repeatedly present in CS 1, CS 2, CS 4, CS 5, SS 1–2, and TS 2. Its kinetics are favorable because many analogues crystallize by vapor transport or temperature-gradient methods over days, and isolated MCl₆ motifs are directly present in SS 1–2 and SS 5. The main uncertainty is that none of CS/SS/TS directly demonstrates Ti–Tl–Cl chemistry, so Ti/Tl redox compatibility and ordering are extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from TS exists for the exact target composition, so TS contributes mainly a neutral-to-supportive thermodynamic and vapor-transport signal rather than direct chemical validation.

#### F2/F3: Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because a direct sealed-tube reaction may require multiple preparatory steps, controlled Cl₂/vacuum, and toxic/air-sensitive chlorides, consistent with difficulties reported for CS 3 and TS 4. Kinetically, chloride precursors are reactive, but solid-state ordering may be slow; CS 3 reports an extreme six-month sealed reaction and narrow phase control. SS 5 supports direct halogenation to an isolated WCl₆ molecular solid, but insufficient evidence from SS exists for direct formation of a mixed Ti/Tl chloride. Thus Route 2 is plausible but less attractive than CVT.

#### F2/F3: Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because chloride-flux crystallization can occur at low temperature, as in SS 3, but still needs sealed, dry, volatile, moisture-sensitive reagents. Kinetics should be favorable in a Lewis-acidic chloride medium because diffusion is liquid/vapor assisted [Reasonable Inference], and SS 3 demonstrates crystal growth near 175 °C. However, transferability is indirect: TS 1 demonstrates chloride-rich solution crystallization, but aqueous solution is not suitable for Ti chloride chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS exists for a Ti/Tl chloride flux specifically, so this route remains more speculative than Route 1.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target structure is zero-dimensional with isolated TiCl₆ clusters and no mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing polyhedral network, which lowers amorphization risk according to the target structural description. The unit cell described by four TiCl₆ clusters and four Tl clusters is below 40 atoms, so the cell-size trigger is not activated. The one activated trigger is the large Ti/Tl cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that disorder on the Tl sublattice or molecular-cluster packing could still occur, but CS/SS analogues show many low-dimensional halides can crystallize under controlled gradients (CS 1–5, SS 1–5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiTlCl₆ is most likely a metastable or marginally stable phase rather than a deep ground-state phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.05 eV/atom and TS analogues show crystallizable halides slightly above the hull (TS 1–5). The CS and SS datasets strongly support the general feasibility of crystallizing low-dimensional chloride materials containing isolated MCl₆ octahedra by sealed-ampoule vapor transport (CS 1–5, SS 1–2, SS 5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature, high-chloride-activity vapor/flux chemistry that can assemble discrete TiCl₆ units without requiring high-temperature solid diffusion (CS 1, CS 2, CS 4, CS 5, SS 3). The most critical limiting factor is the specific Ti–Tl redox/stoichiometry problem, because plausible formal oxidation-state assignments may involve unstable or mutually reactive Ti/Tl chloride states [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is feasible enough to justify sealed-ampoule screening, but phase purity and reproducibility are likely nontrivial.
