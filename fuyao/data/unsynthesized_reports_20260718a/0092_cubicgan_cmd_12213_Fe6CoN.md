### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the high end of the “≤0.05 eV/atom” 90–100 scoring bin from the requested rubric. Thermodynamic analogues near this metastability range are reported as synthesized, including TS1 at 0.059 eV/atom and TS2–TS4 at ~0.062–0.063 eV/atom, which supports experimental accessibility despite not being ground-state phases. The remaining uncertainty is that these analogues are mostly rare-earth Fe phases, while the target is rare-earth-free cubic Fe₆CoN.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 requires controlled Ar/N₂ atmospheres, high-pressure N₂ at 15 MPa, alloy preparation, milling, and nitridation, as in CS3/TS2/TS3. It does not require >5 GPa pressure, so the pressure penalty in the rubric is not triggered, but the controlled atmosphere and multi-step nature are significant. Co powder toxicity is an additional practical penalty [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
High-pressure nitridation analogues show that nitrogen can be inserted into Fe-rich hosts at only 250–350 °C after alloy preparation (CS3; TS2; TS3), which improves the kinetic outlook. However, the Fe–Co host must be pre-alloyed and milled, and analogues warn about disproportionation/α-Fe formation if nitridation is not controlled (CS3; TS2; TS3). The structural change is likely partial/interstitial rather than a complete rebuild [Reasonable Inference], but the cubic target structure is not directly demonstrated.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 uses ambient-pressure purified N₂ but still requires high-temperature alloy preparation, ball milling, and controlled nitridation, matching the multi-step workflow in CS5/TS4. It avoids autoclave pressure, but the controlled N₂ atmosphere and long precursor preparation remain nontrivial. The route is not easier enough to score much higher than Route 1 because the lower N chemical potential may require stricter kinetic control [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
CS5/TS4 show that ball milling to ~5 µm and thermogravimetric monitoring can produce Pr₂Fe₁₇N₃ within hours at ~460 °C. This supports reasonable nitrogen-insertion kinetics, but the lower pressure than CS3/TS2/TS3 increases the risk of incomplete N uptake [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is substantial because Fe₆CoN may require a different N chemical potential than R₂Fe₁₇N₃.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-tube annealing is common in CS1/CS2/CS4/TS1 and SS1–SS4, so the equipment severity is moderate rather than exotic. The penalties arise from vacuum/sealed ampoule handling, possible nitrogen pressure from nitride decomposition, and multiple grind/anneal steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate Fe₆CoN formation by this route, so the score is conservative.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
A solid nitride donor could increase nitrogen activity relative to N₂ gas [Reasonable Inference], but solid-state Fe/Co diffusion at 500–800 °C is likely slow. Multi-day annealing and impurity formation are recurring themes in sealed-tube/arc-melt analogues (CS1; CS2; CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1). The lack of direct Fe–Co–N sealed-tube precedent is the dominant uncertainty.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Reactive sputtering has mild thermal severity and avoids high-pressure bulk processing [Reasonable Inference]. The main severity is controlled Ar/N₂ plasma chemistry, with no CS/SS/TS examples available for direct validation. Therefore, although the numerical F2 is high, its evidence confidence is low.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Fe and Co targets and N₂ plasma are readily available [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization of the specific cubic F-43m Fe₆CoN phase is unproven in CS/SS/TS. Composition control is likely narrow because both Fe:Co ordering and nitrogen content must be tuned [Reasonable Inference]. This is why the route receives a moderate kinetic score despite favorable thermal conditions.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has chemically similar Fe and Co cations, a simple Fe₆CoN formula, and no explicit edge-sharing in the target connectivity, so none of the three amorphization checks is strongly triggered. SS analogues show complex corner/face-sharing R-3m frameworks, but they are not the same cubic structure and therefore provide only weak amorphization evidence (SS1–SS5). The low amorphization risk is partly offset by possible metastability and N-content sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Fe₆CoN at E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom is best interpreted as a low-energy metastable phase rather than a clearly stable ground state, although it lies at a commonly accessible metastability threshold. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS/TS nitridation analogues showing Fe-rich nitrides can be made by high-pressure or monitored N₂ uptake (CS3; CS5; TS2–TS4), while SS evidence is weaker because the closest structural entries are rare-earth Fe carbides rather than cubic Fe–Co nitrides (SS1–SS5). The most important enabling factor is the short Fe–N bonding motif, since analogue Fe–N distances of ~1.90–1.93 Å closely match the target Fe–N distance of 1.88 Å (CS3; CS5; TS2–TS4; target structure). The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity: Fe–Co metallic phases, α-Fe/Co segregation, and off-stoichiometric nitrides may compete during nitrogen insertion [Reasonable Inference], consistent with impurity/disproportionation concerns in nitride and carbide analogues (CS3; CS5; SS1–SS4; TS2–TS4). Overall, bulk synthesis is plausible but likely requires careful nitrogen chemical-potential control.
