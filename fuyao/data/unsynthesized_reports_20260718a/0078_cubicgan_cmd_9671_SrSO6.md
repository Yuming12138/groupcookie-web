### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom from the provided target data, which falls at the high-score threshold for synthesizability. CS evidence is mixed: chemically similar CaSeO4 phases are near the hull or stable (CS4, CS5), while BaS2O8 and NaAsO6 analogues are much higher in hull energy (CS1, CS2). TS evidence is moderately supportive because several thermodynamic neighbors have low E_hull values near 0.03–0.16 eV/atom (TS2–TS5), but SS analogues include many higher-hull phases (SS1–SS5), leaving uncertainty about whether the exact cubic phase is robust.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 scores high because CS1, SS1, and SS3 all demonstrate aqueous oxyanion crystallization under room-temperature or near-room-temperature conditions without high pressure or special atmosphere. The only deduction is for multiple operations, since CS1 uses evaporation and successive crystallizations and the proposed route requires dissolution, filtration, and crystallization. TS gives insufficient direct route-specific severity evidence for aqueous SrSO6, so it is neutral for Route 1 severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because solution-phase reaction avoids long-range solid diffusion, as evidenced by room-temperature crystallization in CS1, SS1, and SS3. The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction: the exact cubic SrSO6 topology is not present among the listed analogues, although octahedral oxyanion units occur in SS1 and TS5. Thus F3 is strong but not maximal.

#### F2 — Route 2

Hydrothermal synthesis has moderate severity because CS3–CS5 use 70–230 °C hydrothermal conditions and standard Teflon-lined autoclaves, which are not extreme. Deductions come from sealed-vessel oxidant hazards, multistep handling, and hazardous oxidizing acid/peroxide chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides insufficient direct hydrothermal evidence for this target and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Hydrothermal media should provide good precursor mobility and crystallization kinetics, supported by CS3–CS5 forming oxyanion crystals under low-hydrothermal conditions. However, the target’s presumed oxidized/peroxo sulfate chemistry may be less stable at 120–220 °C than selenate chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore kinetically plausible but less reliable than room-temperature evaporation.

#### F2 — Route 3

Gel crystallization is mild because TS5 demonstrates crystal growth at 22 °C in test tubes, and no high pressure or special atmosphere is required. The route loses points for multiple steps, since TS5 explicitly involves gel preparation, aging, overlaying with solution, and long growth. CS provides insufficient gel-specific evidence and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Diffusion-controlled gel growth gives high ion mobility while suppressing uncontrolled precipitation [Reasonable Inference], and TS5 directly supports gel growth of an octahedral IO6 oxyanion material. The structural uncertainty remains substantial because no source shows SO6 octahedra in an alkaline-earth sulfate analogue. Therefore, F3 is slightly lower than Route 1 but still favorable.

#### F2 — Route 4

Solid-state oxidation is more severe because it likely requires sealed or controlled oxidizing conditions, thermally unstable oxidizers, and repeated handling [Reasonable Inference]. TS4 supports solid-state oxide synthesis at 600–700 °C, but preserving oxygen-rich sulfate/peroxo units likely requires lower temperature than TS4 [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS provide insufficient direct evidence for this solid-state route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4

The solid-state route has poorer kinetics because SrSO4-like precursors are expected to be poorly reactive and because full reconstruction into cubic SrSO6 would be required [Reasonable Inference]. TS4 demonstrates powder formation by sintering but also notes single-crystal growth difficulty, which supports concern over crystallinity and phase control. The route is exploratory rather than recommended.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation environments: large 12-coordinate Sr and small S-centered SO6 octahedra, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. The cell-size criterion cannot be confirmed as >40 atoms from the target text, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact cell size; the resulting amorphization risk is low–medium.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a marginally metastable phase rather than a deeply stable ground state because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom from the target data. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis most strongly through low-temperature aqueous crystallization of oxyanion salts: CS1 shows sulfur oxyanion crystallization, SS1/SS3 show slow evaporation of related oxyanion salts, and TS5 shows room-temperature crystallization of an octahedral IO6 analogue. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of solution-mediated crystallization, which avoids high-temperature decomposition and slow solid-state diffusion, as supported by CS1, SS1, SS3, and TS5. The most critical limiting factor is the unusual SrSO6 chemistry and the absence of a direct Sr–S–O6 analogue in CS/SS/TS; ordinary SrSO4 or other sulfate/peroxosulfate phases may compete [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is plausible but should be targeted by low-temperature oxidative crystallization rather than high-temperature ceramic synthesis.
