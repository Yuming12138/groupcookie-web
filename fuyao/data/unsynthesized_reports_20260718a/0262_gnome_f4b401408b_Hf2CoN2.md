### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a high thermodynamic score. Several analogous nitrides and nitride-derived compounds in CS/SS/TS are also on or near the hull, including Hf₂N₂S, HfN, ZrN, and Th₂CrN₃, showing that chemically related refractory nitride frameworks can be stable phases (CS, SS, TS). The remaining uncertainty is that database hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing Hf–N/Co–N phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 bulk solid-state

Bulk synthesis is penalized mainly because HfN/ZrN analogues require 1500–2200 °C, hot pressing, and long homogenization treatments (SS, TS). CS actinide transition-metal nitrides show that mononitride + transition-metal powder equilibration under N₂ is feasible, but still at 1200–1600 °C (CS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates a need for vacuum, NH₃, H₂, or volatile sealed precursors in this route, so those penalties were not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1 bulk solid-state

Kinetics are poor because HfN is refractory and Hf–N analogues require long high-temperature treatments to reach equilibrium (SS, TS). The local Hf–N bond distance match between HfN and target helps, but incorporation and ordering of Co into a distinct linear Co–N environment requires substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference from target and SS/TS]. The uncertainty is whether a transient liquid/Co-rich phase could enhance diffusion; no CS/SS/TS evidence directly confirms that.

#### F2 — Route 2 topochemical / metathesis

The topochemical route avoids >1000 °C conditions because Hf₂N₂S forms at 750 °C from α-HfNBr + Na₂S (CS). However, it requires vacuum sealing, prior α-HfNBr synthesis, air-sensitive/reactive nitride or halide reagents, and washing/separation steps, similar in complexity to sealed metathesis routes (CS, TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS confirms that Co can replace S in this exact chemistry, so transferability is plausible but uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 2 topochemical / metathesis

The reactive layered precursor α-HfNBr gives this route better kinetics than bulk sintering, since Hf₂N₂S forms at 750 °C rather than at refractory diffusion temperatures (CS). Metathesis chemistry can provide mobile salt phases and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference from TS]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether Co can be inserted in the required linear N–Co–N site without forming Co metal, Co nitrides, or halide side products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 thin-film deposition

Thin-film synthesis avoids extreme bulk temperatures and high pressure, with a directly relevant nitride CVD analogue operating at 950–1000 °C (SS). Penalties come from reactive nitrogen chemistry, possible volatile halide precursors, and toxic/flammable gases such as NH₃/H₂ in CVD (SS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS proves that post-annealing is mandatory, so no multi-step severity penalty was applied.

#### F3 — Route 3 thin-film deposition

Kinetically, thin films benefit from atomic-scale mixing and short diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference]. SS provides evidence that refractory transition-metal nitride films can crystallize by CVD, but it also warns that Nb–N phase control is sensitive to N/metal ratio and phase windows (SS). The principal uncertainty is achieving the exact Hf:Co:N = 2:1:2 stoichiometry and long-range I4/mmm cation/nitrogen ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 high pressure

High-pressure synthesis is heavily penalized because TaN analogues require 20–100 kbar with specialized pressure apparatus (SS, TS). Although the proposed pressure range is plausible, the target is already thermodynamically stable at ambient-pressure hull conditions, so high pressure is not clearly required [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports high pressure specifically for Hf₂CoN₂, making this a fallback route.

#### F3 — Route 4 high pressure

High pressure can enhance nitrogen chemical potential and stabilize dense nitride arrangements, as in TaN polymorph conversion (SS, TS). However, HfN + Co still have slow solid-state diffusion, and high-pressure capsule volumes limit homogenization [Reasonable Inference]. The pathway is therefore kinetically less attractive than thin-film or topochemical methods unless the target is only accessible under high nitrogen chemical potential [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a notable Hf/Co size and bonding mismatch, reflected by Hf–N distances of 2.23–2.24 Å and Co–N distances of 1.96 Å in the target description, so one amorphization trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The structure is not described as a large-cell material and shows corner/edge sharing but no face-sharing coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization for Hf₂CoN₂, so the F4 score is based primarily on target structural features.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂CoN₂ is predicted to be a ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS evidence strongly supports Hf₂N₂ backbone chemistry through topochemical Hf₂N₂S synthesis, while SS/TS evidence supports the feasibility of refractory Hf–N nitride formation but also highlights slow diffusion and difficult phase control. The most critical enabling factor is the close Hf–N bond-length compatibility between target HfN₅ units and known HfN, plus the on-hull thermodynamic stability (SS, TS; target data). The most critical limiting factor is ordering Co into the target’s linear Co–N coordination without forming Co metal, cobalt nitrides, or HfN-rich competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with thin-film deposition and topochemical/metathesis routes more promising than direct bulk equilibration.
