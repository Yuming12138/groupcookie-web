### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic stability category according to the required rubric. Thermodynamic analogues ZrIrSi, HfIrGe, TiIrSi, TaIrSi, and Th(IrSi)₂ are also on the hull, supporting the stability of nearby Ir–tetrelide structures (TS1–TS5). Chemically similar HfPtSi, ZrIrSi, and ZrPtSi are also on the hull, while HfOsSi appears at either 0 or only 0.0125 eV/atom above hull, so the broader Hf/Zr–late-transition-metal–Si family is energetically favorable (CS1–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly computational/phase-diagram completeness, not an obvious thermodynamic red flag.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses arc melting and annealing at 950–1000 °C, which is moderate for refractory intermetallics and directly supported for MIrSi compounds including the Hf member family (CS4, SS1, TS1). The main deductions are controlled atmosphere/vacuum and the multi-step sequence of pelletizing, melting, and annealing, as described for ZrIrSi/TiIrSi analogues (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). No high pressure or chemically aggressive gas is required, which keeps the severity moderate rather than challenging. The uncertainty is whether a slight Si excess is needed to compensate melt loss; this is a [Reasonable Inference] not directly specified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are limited by refractory Hf and Ir and by the need to order a ternary intermetallic phase [Reasonable Inference]. However, arc melting substantially improves homogenization, and analogues with the same Pnma structure are successfully prepared after annealing (CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The direct statement that MIrSi with M = Hf is made by this route strongly reduces structural-reconstruction risk (CS4, SS1, TS1). The long 10-day anneal indicates that ordering/homogeneity is not instantaneous, so F3 remains moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Solid-state sintering near 950–1000 °C is not extreme and related E-phases are reported to form by sintering powdered components around 1000 °C (CS5). Vacuum or Ar control is recommended because related silicides are annealed under vacuum or purified Ar (CS2, CS4, SS1, TS1). The process is multi-step if grinding, pressing, annealing, and possible regrinding are used [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS supports pure sintering specifically for HfIrSi, so this route is less certain than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The main kinetic penalty is the absence of a melt-homogenization step, which makes diffusion between Hf, Ir, and Si powders slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 states that related E-phases form easily by sintering powdered components near 1000 °C, but that evidence is for ZrPtSi-like chemistry rather than HfIrSi specifically. CS2 supports direct reaction/annealing for HfOsSi-type silicides, but still uses melting plus annealing rather than only low-temperature sintering. Therefore, F3 is lower for Route 2 because phase formation may require repeated grinding or partial melting.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Induction levitation melting plus annealing is supported by HfIrGe, a close Pnma Hf–Ir–tetrelide analogue (SS2, TS2). The 950 °C anneal is moderate, but the melting step requires specialized high-temperature equipment and controlled purified Ar [Reasonable Inference from SS2/TS2]. Vacuum annealing for 1–2 weeks adds controlled-atmosphere severity (SS2, TS2). No high pressure or hazardous reactive gas is required.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Levitation melting should overcome much of the refractory diffusion barrier by forming a melt [Reasonable Inference], and HfIrGe demonstrates that Hf–Ir–tetrel Pnma compounds can be made by melt + anneal processing (SS2, TS2). The structural match is strong for HfIrGe, but the anion/tetrel substitution Ge → Si is indirect and may shift phase equilibria [Reasonable Inference]. The required 1–2 week anneal in the analogue implies ordering/homogenization still limits kinetics (SS2, TS2). Thus, kinetics are moderate, slightly below Route 1 because Route 1 has more direct HfIrSi/MIrSi support.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with relatively simple Pnma 1:1:1 ordering, and the closest analogues are reported as crystalline Pnma phases rather than glasses (CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The unit-cell size is small in the analogues, with 12-atom formula listings such as Zr₄Si₄Ir₄ and Ti₄Si₄Ir₄, so the large-cell amorphization criterion is not triggered (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The radius mismatch criterion is triggered by Hf/Ir/Si size differences [Reasonable Inference], but the high coordination environments in the analogues show this mismatch is crystallographically tolerated (CS4, SS1, TS1). Polyhedral-sharing information is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so that sub-check is treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

HfIrSi is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom and many close Ir–silicide/tetrelide analogues are also on the hull (TS1–TS5). The analogue support is unusually strong: ZrIrSi and TiIrSi have the same Pnma structure, and the reported MIrSi synthesis series explicitly includes M = Hf under conventional arc-melting/annealing conditions (CS4, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is melt homogenization followed by long annealing, which allows refractory Hf/Ir/Si to reach the ordered TiNiSi-type Pnma phase (CS4, SS1, TS1). The most critical limiting factor is slow diffusion/order development in refractory elemental mixtures, especially if attempting a purely solid-state route [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis should be feasible in a standard intermetallic synthesis laboratory with arc-melting capability.
