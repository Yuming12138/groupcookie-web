### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it falls at the top of the provided stability scoring range. Thermodynamic analogues FeCl₂, FeBr₂, and CsFeCl₃ are also listed with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that Fe halide frameworks in this chemical neighborhood are ground-state-accessible (TS1, TS2, TS3). CS also contains multiple stable or near-stable halide salts, including CuAlCl₄, Mg(AlCl₄)₂, and VAl₂Cl₈, which supports general halide stability but is less specific to Fe–Cl–F chemistry (CS2, CS4, CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee easy Cl/F ordering or kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures and no high pressure, consistent with halide melt/Bridgman analogues that operate from ~250 °C to 900 °C (CS1, CS4, SS4). The score is reduced because sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile halides, dry handling, and controlled cooling are repeatedly required for related halides (CS2, CS5, TS4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for the exact melting behavior of Fe₄Cl₇F, so the temperature program is extrapolated from FeCl₂ and halide melt analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because halide melts and self-fluxes generally enhance mass transport relative to oxide solid-state reactions [Reasonable Inference], and CS analogues show halide salts crystallizing from melts or ampoules at moderate temperature (CS1, CS2, CS4). Structural reconstruction should be limited because FeCl₂ already has edge-sharing FeCl₆ octahedra similar to the target’s edge-sharing FeCl₆/FeCl₅F network (SS1, TS1). The main uncertainty is whether F distributes into the FeCl₂-like framework without forming FeF₂ or F-poor FeCl₂ domains [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high pressure and can use a 650–800 °C range comparable to CsFeCl₃ synthesis at 750 °C in sealed Ta capsules under Ar (SS5, TS3). Deductions arise from sealed volatile halide conditions, optional multistep regrinding/annealing, and air-sensitive anhydrous halide handling [Reasonable Inference supported by sealed/glovebox ampoule practices in CS5 and SS5/TS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS for Fe–F mixed-halide capsule synthesis increases uncertainty but does not make conditions severe.

#### F3 — Route 2

The FeCl₂/FeF₂ binary halide precursors should be more reactive than refractory oxide precursors [Reasonable Inference], and halide analogues are synthesized successfully in sealed capsules/ampoules (SS5, TS3, TS4). The FeCl₂ structural analogue reduces the structural reconstruction burden because it already contains edge-sharing FeCl₆ octahedra (SS1, TS1). Kinetics are less certain than pure FeCl₂ growth because Cl/F ordering and FeF₂ dissolution into a chloride-rich lattice are not directly demonstrated; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for FeCl₂–FeF₂ solid solution behavior.

#### F2 — Route 3

Vapor transport uses moderate temperatures, supported by FeBr₂ sublimation at 773 K under Ar and VAl₂Cl₈ CVT in evacuated ampoules (SS2, TS2, CS5). The score is penalized for vacuum/Ar sealed tubes, volatile halide transport, multistep two-zone control, and air-sensitive volatile transport agents [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for Fe₄Cl₇F congruent vapor growth makes this route more severe in practice despite moderate nominal temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Fe-halide vapor growth is supported by FeBr₂ sublimation yielding single crystals (SS2, TS2), and chloride CVT is supported by VAl₂Cl₈ synthesis using gaseous AlCl₃ (CS5). However, FeF₂ volatility and mixed Cl/F vapor activity control are not demonstrated; this creates a likely composition-control bottleneck [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the kinetic score is significantly lower than for sealed melt or self-flux routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Fe as the cation, so cation-radius mismatch is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described structure is built from edge-sharing FeCl₆/FeCl₅F octahedra rather than a highly mixed vertex/edge/face network, and the formula has only 12 atoms per Fe₄Cl₇F unit [Reasonable Inference from target description]. FeCl₂, the closest SS/TS analogue, is a simple crystalline edge-sharing FeCl₆ framework, which supports low glass-forming/amorphization risk (SS1, TS1). The uncertainty is the actual crystallographic cell size of the R3m model, but no evidence indicates a >40 atom unit cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Fe₄Cl₇F is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0 eV/atom, and the closest TS analogues FeCl₂, FeBr₂, and CsFeCl₃ are also hull-stable (TS1–TS3). The strongest synthesis support comes from the structural match to FeCl₂, which has edge-sharing FeCl₆ octahedra and closely matching Fe–Cl distances (SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the use of FeCl₂-rich sealed halide chemistry, because chloride melts/capsules repeatedly produce crystalline halides under moderate conditions (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5, SS5, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is controlled incorporation and ordering of a small amount of F into a chloride-rich Fe²⁺ framework, because no CS/SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates Fe₄Cl₇F or congruent Fe–Cl–F mixed-halide synthesis. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with the best route being sealed Ta-capsule solid-state/self-flux reaction under dry Ar.
