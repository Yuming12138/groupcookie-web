### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls exactly at the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-synthesizability threshold specified by the scoring rubric. Thermodynamically similar materials with E_hull values around 0.053–0.073 eV/atom are reported as synthesized by arc/anneal methods, including Sm₂Co₁₅Ga₂, Pr₂Co₁₅Ga₂, and Ce₂Co₁₅Ga₂ (TS, CS). The main uncertainty is that the target is a nitride with Rh rather than a rare-earth Co–Ga intermetallic, so similar hull energy does not guarantee the same phase field [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, flowing-N₂ nitridation

The severity score is reduced because the route requires alloy synthesis, annealing, grinding, and nitridation, matching multistep processing seen in SS and CS nitride routes (CS, SS). Flowing N₂ nitridation is experimentally supported for Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ at 450–550 °C, but SS explicitly reports slow nitrogen diffusion and α-Co precipitation risks, which lowers the kinetic score (SS). The major uncertainty is whether a Co₆Rh parent alloy can rearrange or order into cubic F-43m RhCo₆N rather than forming Co/Rh solid solution plus surface nitride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, high-pressure N₂ nitridation

The high-pressure N₂ route is not penalized as “high pressure >5 GPa” because CS uses only 15 MPa, but it still requires controlled gas-pressure equipment and multistep alloy preparation (CS). Kinetically, this route is better than Route 1 because CS reports high-pressure N₂ nitridation as a way to improve nitrogen uptake and minimize disproportionation in Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃-like materials (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that CS evidence is Fe-rich/rare-earth nitride chemistry, while RhCo₆N has no rare-earth sublattice and a different cubic framework [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, NH₃/N₂ annealing

NH₃ is not directly supported by CS/SS/TS for this family, so the route is explicitly extrapolated from general nitridation chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Its severity score is lowered by the toxic/reactive gas and controlled-flow requirements [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, NH₃ may improve nitrogen activity, but it may also favor surface nitrides or binary cobalt nitrides instead of ordered RhCo₆N, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS prevents a higher score.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, reactive sputtering / PLD

Route 4 receives the best severity score because it avoids bulk arc-melting diffusion temperatures and can operate at moderate substrate temperatures under vacuum/plasma [Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetic score is moderate because thin-film growth can bypass slow bulk nitrogen diffusion, but CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct thin-film evidence, so crystallization and stoichiometry control remain uncertain. This route is favored for a borderline-metastable target because non-equilibrium deposition can stabilize phases that are difficult to isolate in bulk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple Co/Rh cation set with modest size mismatch, so the cation-radius amorphization trigger is not met [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports corner and face sharing but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully met. The uncertainty is the exact crystallographic atom count from the CIF, but the formula and F-43m description do not indicate a large complex cell exceeding the threshold [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RhCo₆N is best viewed as a borderline-stable or mildly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. CS and SS strongly support the feasibility of forming Co–N bonds in Co-rich intermetallic frameworks, especially since the target Co–N distance of 1.87 Å matches Co–N distances in Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ (SS). TS provides a useful cubic F-43m intermetallic precedent through UCo₄Sn, but that analogue lacks nitrogen and Rh, so structural transfer is incomplete (TS). The most critical enabling factor is controlled nitrogen insertion into a pre-organized Co/Rh metallic framework or direct incorporation during reactive thin-film growth [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and nitrogen diffusion, because SS reports incomplete nitrogenation and α-Co precipitation in Co-rich nitrides (SS).
