### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target ZrIrP has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it scores in the top thermodynamic-stability bin by the provided criterion. Similar compounds HfRuP, ZrOsP, HfOsP, TaRuP, and ZrRuP also have E_hull = 0.0 or near-zero in CS/SS/TS, which supports that this 1:1:1 phosphide family contains stable phases (CS1–CS4; SS1–SS4; TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or single-phase synthesis, especially for Ir-containing samples [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Cu–P Flux Growth

Flux growth requires evacuated sealed quartz capsules, volatile P, multiple operations, and hot nitric acid flux removal, all of which lower the severity score (CS1–CS4; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3, TS5). The references do not specify exact maximum temperatures, so the T >1000 °C and T >1400 °C penalties are not applied; this is a conservative neutral treatment due to insufficient exact temperature evidence from CS/SS/TS. The method remains moderate rather than severe because no high pressure or exotic atmosphere beyond vacuum sealing is reported (CS1; TS5).

#### F3 — Route 1: Cu–P Flux Growth

The Cu–P liquid flux should improve mass transport and nucleation relative to direct solid-state reaction, consistent with analogue crystallite growth from CuP flux (CS1; TS5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because Pnma ZrOsP and ZrRuP share the same Zr–M–P coordination motifs as the target (CS2; SS3; TS3, TS5). The main uncertainty is whether Ir dissolves and transports in the Cu–P flux as effectively as Ru/Os [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Arc-Melting / Sealed-Tube Annealing

Arc melting and annealing near 1000 °C are supported for thermodynamically similar phosphides, with excess P and sealed tubes (TS4). The severity is higher because arc melting implies very high local temperatures and because volatile P must be controlled [Reasonable Inference from TS4]. The method avoids high pressure, but phase control and multi-step processing are significant penalties (TS4).

#### F3 — Route 2: Arc-Melting / Sealed-Tube Annealing

Dense elemental metals/master alloys give relatively poor precursor activity, and the need for arc melting plus annealing reflects kinetic barriers (TS4). Ir is expected to diffuse more slowly than Ru because of its refractory nature [Reasonable Inference], lowering the diffusion score. The structural family is accessible by Pnma analogues, but TS4 reports phase-control challenges for related phosphides, limiting confidence.

#### F2 — Route 3: Solid-State Sintering

The solid-state route uses standard sealed-ampoule and furnace equipment similar to SS5, but long sintering and possible annealing up to 1200 °C increase severity. Phosphorus volatility requires sealed conditions by analogy to phosphide routes (CS1–CS4; TS4–TS5), even though the direct SS5 analogue is arsenide. This route is less temperature-severe than arc melting but more kinetically demanding than flux growth [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Solid-State Sintering

SS5 demonstrates that related Zr–late-metal–pnictide frameworks can form by master-alloy grinding, pelletization, and long annealing. However, the route relies on solid-state diffusion through refractory metals, and SS5’s 7-day sintering plus regrinding/annealing indicates slow kinetics. Transfer from arsenide to phosphide is plausible but less direct than the flux phosphide evidence [Reasonable Inference from SS5 versus CS/TS phosphide routes].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains mixed face-, edge-, and corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which is directly described for ZrIrP and also appears in analogous Ru/Os phosphides (target description; CS1, CS3–CS4; SS1–SS2, SS4; TS1–TS2). The conventional Pnma analogues have small unit cells, so the atom-count amorphization trigger is not met (CS2; SS3; TS3, TS5). A cation-size mismatch trigger is assigned based on Zr versus Ir size difference [Reasonable Inference], but exact ionic-radius data are not provided in CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrIrP is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple related Zr/Hf/Ta–Ru/Os phosphides are also stable in CS/SS/TS (CS1–CS4; TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong: Pnma ZrOsP and Pnma ZrRuP have very similar Zr coordination, P coordination, and 1:1:1 stoichiometry to the target (CS2; SS3; TS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the Cu–P flux route, which provides liquid-phase transport and has been demonstrated for close phosphide analogues (CS1; TS5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining phosphorus chemical potential while achieving homogeneous Zr–Ir incorporation, since P is volatile and Ir is refractory [Reasonable Inference from sealed-ampoule phosphide routes in CS/TS]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with flux growth being the most promising route.
