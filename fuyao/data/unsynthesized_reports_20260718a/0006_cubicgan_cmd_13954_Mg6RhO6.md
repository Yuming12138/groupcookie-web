### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-synthesizability threshold specified by the rubric, so a score of 90 is assigned. CS analogues include synthesized phases at E_hull = 0.0–0.052 eV/atom, including MgCu2O3 at ~0.052 eV/atom (CS-2), supporting that similar near-hull oxides can be made. TS is especially supportive because several thermodynamic analogues are synthesized at E_hull values around 0.043–0.095 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-5). SS provides insufficient direct thermodynamic transferability because structurally similar phases include high-pressure monoxides and a metastable MgO polymorph; SS is treated as neutral 50 for this factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1, conventional ceramic synthesis

Condition severity is moderate because TS-1 uses ordinary furnace heating at 900–1000 °C with intermediate grinding, but target synthesis likely needs controlled oxygen potential by analogy to reduced ferrites and vacuum spinels (CS-4, CS-5; TS-4). Kinetics are limiting because MgO is refractory and TS-1 requires repeated firings, while CS-3 requires 1100 °C for Mg2GeO4 formation. The target topology is not the same as the spinel analogues in TS-1/CS-3, so structural reconstruction from binary oxides is likely substantial [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is high because the Rh oxidation state required by Mg6RhO6 is not clear from the provided analogues.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2, flux-assisted crystallization

Route 2 has similar atmosphere and multistep penalties, but kinetics improve because CS-2 shows MgCu2O3 can be crystallized from KCl flux during controlled cooling and isolated by dissolving the flux. Flux growth can reduce diffusion limitations and promote crystal nucleation from dissolved species [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 also shows melt growth can succeed where solid-state methods are problematic for a chemically similar oxide family. Uncertainty remains because no provided analogue is specifically Mg–Rh–O in chloride flux.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3, high-pressure synthesis

High-pressure severity is high because SS-4 and SS-5 require 15–50 kbar and specialized belt-type equipment for cubic Fm-3m monoxides. Kinetically, pressure may stabilize dense cubic coordination, but high-pressure solid-state reaction of refractory oxides/metals remains diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. The structural analogy to Fm-3m PrO/CeO is useful, but chemical similarity to Mg6RhO6 is weak, so SS is only partially transferable. The route is best considered a fallback if ambient-pressure methods fail.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4, thin-film deposition

Direct evidence for thin-film synthesis is insufficient from CS, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS. The route is proposed by [Reasonable Inference] because non-equilibrium deposition can trap near-hull metastable oxides and because the target’s 0.05 eV/atom hull value is not prohibitively high. F2 appears less severe than high-pressure synthesis, but F3 is limited by Mg volatility, Mg-rich 6:1 composition control, and uncertain crystallization of the Fm-3m phase [Reasonable Inference]. Because it lacks direct analogue support, it is not selected as the best route despite a competitive numerical score.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner- and edge-sharing OMg4Rh square pyramids, but the target description does not mention face sharing, so the strict C3 criterion is not fully triggered. Mg–O distances in the target, 2.14 Å, are close to Mg–O distances in CS-2, CS-3, and TS-1, and late-transition-metal–O distances in TS-2/TS-3 are near Rh–O = 2.05 Å, so severe cation-size mismatch is not clearly indicated. The exact conventional Fm-3m cell atom count is not supplied; using the 13-atom formula unit gives low atom-count risk, but the conventional cell could be larger [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining risk is local-coordination unusualness, especially square-planar Mg, which may promote disorder or competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg6RhO6 is best viewed as a near-hull, potentially metastable oxide rather than an unequivocal ground-state phase, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom and its valence chemistry is unusual [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support comes from TS-1, where Mg/Rh-containing oxide spinels are synthesized by straightforward oxide heating, and CS-2, where a Mg–transition-metal oxide is grown successfully from KCl flux. SS analogues support the possibility of cubic Fm-3m stabilization but mostly under high pressure and with chemically different rare-earth monoxides (SS-4, SS-5), so they are secondary evidence. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty combined with flux-assisted crystallization that can overcome MgO/Rh oxide diffusion barriers. The most critical limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control, because standard Mg2+ and O2− charge balance makes the formal Rh valence ambiguous or unusually low [Reasonable Inference].
