### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the highly favorable cutoff in the rubric, so it receives 92/100 (target data). Chemically similar Th₂MnN₃ and Th₂CrN₃ are stable on the hull and experimentally synthesized by solid-state sintering (CS1; CS2), which supports the plausibility of ternary early-metal/transition-metal nitrides. Thermodynamic analogues Hf₃N₄ and Zr₃N₄ are slightly higher above hull, 0.069 and 0.061 eV/atom, yet synthesized under high pressure (TS2; TS4), suggesting that 0.050 eV/atom is accessible but may correspond to metastability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires high temperatures because Th₂MnN₃ is prepared at 1200–1600 °C under N₂ (CS1; SS1), while HfN-related materials require 1500–2100 °C and long homogenization (SS4). Ambient pressure and N₂ atmosphere are manageable compared with DAC or NH₃ autoclave methods, so the main deductions are temperature and multistep regrinding/annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Evidence from CS and SS is strong for high-temperature nitride synthesis, but insufficient from TS to specify a bulk Hf–Mn–N phase window.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

HfN and Hf-containing nitride precursors are refractory, and SS4 explicitly reports high-temperature hot pressing and 60–150 h homogenization for Hf–N samples. This implies slow cation diffusion and slow ordering kinetics for Hf₆MnN₆ [Reasonable Inference]. The shared cubic Fm-3m motif between HfN and the target improves structural transferability (SS4; target data), but Mn ordering into a 6:1 Hf:Mn framework is still a major kinetic barrier [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized heavily because TS2 and TS4 require pressures up to 18 GPa and temperatures up to 3000 K in laser-heated DAC experiments. These are far more severe than ambient N₂ sintering in CS1/SS1. The benefit is that high pressure can stabilize dense nitrides near or above the hull, but equipment and sample size are limiting (TS2; TS4).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High pressure and laser heating can rapidly form dense cubic nitrides, as shown for Hf₃N₄ and Zr₃N₄ (TS2; TS4). However, the reaction uses refractory Hf/HfN-type precursors, and the target requires ternary cation ordering rather than a binary nitride framework [Reasonable Inference]. The short heating times in TS2/TS4 may be insufficient for long-range Hf/Mn ordering unless multiple heating cycles are used [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux/ampoule methods reduce temperature relative to direct HfN sintering, as Li₃N flux routes can operate near 1030 °C and NH₃ autoclave routes near 550 °C (CS5; TS5). The penalties arise from sealed reactive systems, air-sensitive Li₃N/LiNH₂, and multistep handling/flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. CS3/CS4 also show that sealed-tube Hf nitride/chalcogenide topochemical syntheses can be very sensitive to heating protocol.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Fluxes improve precursor activity and mass transport, supported by complex nitride formation using Li₃N or NH₃ routes (CS5; TS5). Nevertheless, no provided source directly demonstrates Hf–Mn–N flux synthesis, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity remain uncertain. Hf₂N₂S examples show that heating rate and temperature can select different polymorphs or mixed phases (CS3; CS4), which lowers confidence for clean Hf₆MnN₆ crystallization.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

Thin-film deposition avoids the >1000 °C and high-pressure penalties that dominate bulk and DAC routes [Reasonable Inference]. The route still requires controlled reactive N₂/NH₃ plasma and likely post-annealing, so it receives moderate deductions [Reasonable Inference]. Direct evidence is insufficient from CS, SS, and TS for this specific processing route.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

Vapor-phase deposition can bypass bulk diffusion limitations by mixing Hf, Mn, and N atomically during growth [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is crystallization into the ordered cubic Fm-3m target rather than amorphous Hf–Mn–N or HfN-rich films [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is nontrivial because the target has a dilute Mn fraction, Hf:Mn = 6:1, and nitrogen stoichiometry must be maintained [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s conventional Fm-3m cell may exceed 40 atoms if it contains four formula units, which triggers C2 [Reasonable Inference]. The target reports corner- and edge-sharing NHf₄Mn square pyramids but not face sharing, so C3 is not triggered (target data). Cation size mismatch is uncertain because formal ionic oxidation states are ambiguous in this metallic nitride; using metallic radii gives a difference below 15%, so C1 is not counted [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₆MnN₆ is likely a low-energy metastable phase rather than an unequivocal ground state, because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom rather than 0.0 eV/atom (target data). The synthesis is supported by chemically and structurally similar Th₂MnN₃, which is experimentally made by high-temperature N₂ sintering (CS1; SS1), and by HfN, which shares cubic Fm-3m Hf–N chemistry (SS4). Thermodynamic analogues show that nearby metastable nitrides can be synthesized, but sometimes only under high pressure or specialized nitrogen-rich conditions (TS2; TS4; TS5). The most important enabling factor is the close Hf–N bond-length/structure compatibility with cubic HfN and the low E_hull (SS4; target data). The most important limiting factor is sluggish Hf/Mn ordering and competition with binary HfN plus manganese nitrides or metallic Mn [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires either non-equilibrium thin-film methods or careful high-temperature/flux screening.
