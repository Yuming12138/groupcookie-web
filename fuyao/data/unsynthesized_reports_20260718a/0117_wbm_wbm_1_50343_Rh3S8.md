### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, directly indicating predicted ground-state stability [target]. TS analogues RuS₂, Rh₃S₄, IrS₂, Rh₂S₃, and NiPS are all on or near the hull, supporting that related chalcogenide frameworks are thermodynamically accessible [TS1–TS5]. CS gives useful support mainly through stable RuS₂ and RuP₃ analogues [CS1; CS5], while halide CS entries provide limited relevance to Rh–S thermodynamics; insufficient evidence from CS2–CS4 for sulfide phase stability beyond generic low-hull precedent, treated as neutral where chemistry is mismatched. SS also supports stability because the closest structural analogues Rh₂S₃, RuS₂, Rh₃S₄, and IrS₂ are stable or nearly stable [SS1–SS4].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses 850–950 °C, evacuated sealed tubes, and sulfur-containing elemental charges, matching Rh₂S₃ and IrS₂ syntheses [SS1; SS4; TS3; TS4]. The main severity deductions are vacuum/sealed-tube operation and volatile sulfur containment [SS1; SS4]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for elemental Rh₃S₈ sintering, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity assessment. Remaining uncertainty is whether sulfur-rich Rh₃S₈ can be reached before lower-sulfur Rh₂S₃/Rh₃S₄-type phases form [Reasonable Inference from SS3/TS2].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are limited by refractory Rh metal and solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], even though Rh₂S₃ and IrS₂ formation at 900–960 °C shows the reaction is feasible [SS1; SS4; TS3; TS4]. IrS₂ required long heat treatment and possible grinding/refiring for homogeneity, which penalizes kinetics [SS4; TS3]. SS and TS provide useful signals for slow but workable diffusion; CS gives insufficient direct signal for Rh elemental sintering and is treated as neutral. The target’s S–S-dimerized sulfur-rich framework likely requires more reconstruction than forming a simple Rh₂S₃-type network [target; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits sealed evacuated quartz tubes, sulfur volatility, multi-step flux growth, and Te handling from RuS₂ flux synthesis [CS1; SS2; TS1]. Temperatures are high but generally not above 1000 °C, so no >1000 °C deduction is applied if the experiment is capped at 1000 °C [CS1; SS2; TS1]. SS and TS strongly support the same RuS₂ flux precedent because it appears in all three evidence pools [CS1; SS2; TS1]. The main uncertainty is whether Te flux dissolves Rh sufficiently without stabilizing Rh–Te impurities [Reasonable Inference based on RuTe₂ concern in CS1/SS2/TS1].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves reaction kinetics by providing liquid-mediated transport, which is supported by successful RuS₂ growth in Te flux [CS1; SS2; TS1]. The structural match is strong because both target Rh₃S₈ and RuS₂ feature transition-metal sulfide octahedra, S-centered tetrahedra, and S–S bonding with similar bond lengths/tilt angles [target; CS1; SS2; TS1]. Phase control is still penalized because RuS₂ flux growth required precise sulfur content and could form RuTe₂ [CS1; SS2; TS1]. Insufficient evidence from CS2–CS5 for Te-flux Rh–S growth specifically, so those entries are neutral for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT requires >1000 °C, evacuated ampoules, volatile sulfur/chloride transport, and anhydrous RhCl₃ handling [SS3; TS2]. SS3/TS2 explicitly indicate Rh₃S₄ was prepared at 1320 K → 1290 K and used Rh/RhCl₃/S in evacuated quartz glass ampoules. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for chloride CVT of Rh–S phases and is scored neutral for this route. The route is therefore harsher than solid-state sintering or flux growth.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport improves crystal growth kinetics relative to purely solid-state diffusion, and Rh₃S₄ crystal growth by CVT is directly demonstrated [SS3; TS2]. However, composition control is a major weakness because Rh₃S₄ could not be obtained phase-pure without Rh₁₇S₁₅, indicating narrow Rh–S stability windows [SS3; TS2]. Transferring CVT to sulfur-richer Rh₃S₈ is plausible but unproven [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS and SS entries other than SS3 for Rh₃S₈-specific CVT growth is treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is strongly penalized because the analogous pyrite-like IrS₂ route used 60 kbar and 1400–1500 °C [SS5]. SS5 provides the direct severity evidence; CS provides insufficient evidence for high-pressure Rh sulfide synthesis and is neutral. TS provides insufficient evidence that high pressure is required for Rh₃S₈, especially because the target is on the hull at 0 eV/atom [target; insufficient evidence from TS for high-pressure necessity]. This route is thus technically challenging despite being chemically plausible.

#### F3 — Route 4

High pressure and high temperature can overcome diffusion barriers and stabilize dense sulfide structures, as shown for pyrite-like IrS₂ [SS5]. The structural analogy is useful but imperfect because Rh₃S₈ is sulfur richer and not exactly IrS₂ [target; SS5]. The absence of a direct Rh₃S₈ high-pressure precedent leaves significant uncertainty; insufficient evidence from CS/TS for this exact route is treated as neutral. Kinetics may be acceptable once at P–T, but sample size and optimization burden are limiting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only Rh as the cation, so cation size mismatch is not a driver for amorphization [target]. The provided target description does not report edge/face/corner coexistence; it reports corner-sharing RhS₆ octahedra and S-centered tetrahedra, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low [target]. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the target’s cell size is treated neutrally. Overall, the ordered low-hull sulfide network suggests low amorphization tendency [target; TS1–TS4].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rh₃S₈ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom [target]. The synthesis evidence is fairly strong: same-binary Rh₂S₃ and Rh₃S₄ are experimentally accessible [SS1; SS3; TS2; TS4], and the pyrite-like RuS₂ analogue closely matches the target’s octahedral sulfide/S–S-bonded topology [CS1; SS2; TS1]. The most important enabling factor is the combination of thermodynamic stability and structural analogy to known S–S-dimerized transition-metal sulfides [target; CS1; TS1]. The most important limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Rh–S system, because Rh₃S₄ synthesis shows competing Rh₁₇S₁₅ and narrow stoichiometry/temperature windows [SS3; TS2]. A Te-flux route is the best first attempt because it improves mass transport and has the closest structural precedent, but careful sulfur chemical potential control is essential [CS1; SS2; TS1].

| 3 | Route 1 | Slow diffusion from refractory Rh metal | Medium | Long holds, fine Rh powder, pressing, regrinding/refiring; IrS₂ analogue used long annealing and grinding/refiring for homogeneity [SS4; TS3]. |
