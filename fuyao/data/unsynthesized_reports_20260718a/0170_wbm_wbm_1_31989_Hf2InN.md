### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target Hf2InN is reported with E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it on the computed convex hull and supports a high thermodynamic score. The closest analogue Zr2InN is also E_hull = 0.0 and is both structurally and thermodynamically similar (SS1, TS1), while Ti2InN and Hf3AlN also have E_hull = 0.0 in the provided data (SS4, TS3; CS1, TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase purity; Zr2InN still showed mononitride impurity despite being on-hull (SS1, TS1).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids the very high temperatures used in HfN hot pressing or Hf phosphide arc-melting routes because the direct H-phase In nitride analogues form at 500–900 °C (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The severity penalty comes mainly from sealed-ampoule handling, long processing, and Hf/In handling risks [Reasonable Inference], not from pressure or extreme temperature. Insufficient evidence from CS for this exact Hf2InN solid-state route; CS mainly supports broader Hf nitride/ternary chemistry rather than the exact H-phase In nitride.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The reaction kinetics are only moderate because HfN and Hf are refractory, and the direct analogues require 500–850 h anneals, indicating slow diffusion/order formation (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The structural reconstruction penalty is reduced because Zr2InN is essentially isostructural with the target, including P6₃/mmc H-phase topology and similar M–In/M–N bond lengths (SS1, TS1; target description). The main uncertainty is whether Hf substitutes for Zr without increasing diffusion barriers or stabilizing competing HfN-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Arc melting/induction annealing is much more severe because Hf2CoP and Hf7P4 analogues require arc melting and/or 1400 °C annealing under Ar/vacuum (CS3, SS3). Dynamic vacuum and specialized high-temperature equipment are explicitly reported for Hf2CoP and Hf7P4-type routes (CS3, SS3). The target also contains In and N, so In loss and N retention are additional risks not directly solved by the cited arc-melting analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt processing can overcome slow solid diffusion, as Hf-containing analogues were homogenized by arc melting and annealing (CS3, SS3). However, the cited arc-melt structures are not the same H-phase, so crystallization into Hf2InN may require substantial ordering during annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest uncertainty is whether In and N can be retained at the high temperatures used for Hf melt processing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Flux growth can operate near 900 °C, as Ce2BiN/Pr2BiN were synthesized in flux at 900 °C for 7 d (CS2, CS4), making it less thermally severe than arc melting. However, sealed tubes/capsules, flux removal, and volatile/melt containment introduce condition penalties; Cu-flux recovery required acid dissolution in a related phosphide flux route (CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for exact Hf2InN flux growth, so this route is more speculative than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

The flux route likely improves kinetics through liquid-phase transport, consistent with flux growth of related nitride/pnictide crystals (CS2, CS4, CS5) and Fe-flux-assisted formation of Hf7P4 (SS3). The target’s H-phase topology is supported by Zr2InN/Ti2InN analogues, but those were made by long solid-state annealing rather than flux (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The uncertainty is whether an In-rich melt stabilizes Hf2InN rather than binary HfN plus In-rich intermetallics [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small, ordered H-phase with simple corner- and edge-sharing InHf6/NHf6 connectivity and no face-sharing coexistence in the target description. The closest Zr2InN analogue has the same corner/edge-sharing H-phase topology and was synthesized as a crystalline phase, supporting low amorphization risk (SS1, TS1). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for quantitative cation-radius disorder, so the radius check is treated as not triggered based on standard chemical size reasoning [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf2InN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and the closest H-phase analogue Zr2InN is also on the hull (SS1, TS1). The synthesis outlook is strongly supported by the existence of isostructural Zr2InN and Ti2InN made by sealed-ampoule solid-state routes at 500–900 °C (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The most important enabling factor is structural transferability: Zr2InN has the same P6₃/mmc H-phase framework, similar M–In/M–N distances, and identical polyhedral connectivity (SS1, TS1; target description). The main limiting factor is kinetic/phase-purity control, because the closest Zr analogue required 750–850 h and still showed mononitride impurity (SS1, TS1). Overall, the material is quite synthesizable, but likely requires patient sealed-ampoule annealing rather than rapid high-temperature melting.
