### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the high-scoring threshold in the provided rubric. TS-1 reports a synthesizable compound with E_hull = 0.0568 eV/atom, and TS-5 reports a synthesizable compound with E_hull = 0.0621 eV/atom, so the target’s thermodynamic penalty is within a range where experimental synthesis is plausible. CS analogues are mostly near-hull or stable Ni-rich intermetallics/phosphides (CS-1 to CS-5), but they do not directly establish nitride stability; insufficient direct nitride thermodynamic evidence from CS, neutral 50 for nitride-specific inference. Remaining uncertainty is the ternary Co–Ni–N phase competition, especially against binary Ni nitrides or metallic Co–Ni phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because SS-1 uses ~10 GPa and ~1800 K with DAC/YAG laser heating in supercritical nitrogen. These conditions trigger high-pressure, >1000 °C, >1400 °C, and controlled/reactive atmosphere deductions. CS provides insufficient evidence for comparable high-pressure nitride synthesis and is neutral 50 for this route; TS provides general high-pressure analogues (TS-2, TS-4) but not Co–Ni nitrides. The main uncertainty is whether Co/Ni ordering can occur during a short high-pressure laser-heating step [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, SS-1 shows that 3d metal nitrides can form rapidly under high pressure/high temperature, supporting nitrogen uptake and NNi₆ octahedral formation. However, the starting metals are dense solids and the target requires ternary ordering, so precursor activity and structural ordering are not ideal [Reasonable Inference]. The shared NNi₆ octahedra and very similar Ni–N distance between Ni₃N and the target improve the structural-reconstruction score (SS-1; target). CS is insufficient for nitride kinetics and neutral 50; TS is only indirectly relevant through high-pressure synthesis examples (TS-2, TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses well-established arc melting and annealing protocols for Ni-rich and Co-containing intermetallics, supported by CS-3 to CS-5, SS-2 to SS-5, and TS-5. It avoids >5 GPa pressure and sustained >1000 °C reaction holds, but it requires controlled Ar/vacuum/N₂/NH₃ atmospheres and multiple steps. NH₃ use is an extrapolated nitriding adaptation and adds toxicity/handling penalty [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that ambient-pressure nitridation of this exact Co–Ni framework is not demonstrated in CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

The alloy-preparation portion has strong kinetic precedent: repeated arc melting and annealing improve homogeneity in CS-3, CS-4, CS-5, SS-2 to SS-5, and TS-5. However, nitrogen diffusion into a dense Co–Ni alloy is likely slow, and many analogues require long anneals or show impurity phases, implying moderate phase-control difficulty (CS-4; SS-3 to SS-5; TS-5). The structural motif is plausible because Ni-rich cuboctahedral metal frameworks occur in several analogues (CS-5; SS-2; TS-5), but inserting N into ordered NNi₆ octahedra is a substantial reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 supports nitride formation in principle but only under high pressure.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has milder nominal temperature and pressure because sputtering can be done at low substrate temperature or with moderate post-annealing [Reasonable Inference]. It still incurs deductions for controlled/reactive N₂ plasma and multi-step deposition/annealing. CS, SS, and TS contain no direct thin-film evidence, so each source is insufficient for direct route validation and neutral 50. The route’s score is therefore penalized by expert adjustment despite favorable processing severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Co and Ni are readily available as sputtering targets, so vapor availability is favorable [Reasonable Inference]. The weak point is crystallization: no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms direct formation of the target phase by deposition, and post-annealing may either crystallize the phase or expel nitrogen [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is also challenging because the exact Co:Ni:N ratio must be maintained, and reactive sputtering commonly has narrow nitrogen windows [Reasonable Inference]. The route is plausible for metastable stabilization but weakly evidenced by the supplied data.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only chemically similar cations, Co and Ni, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described formula and structure are compact, and the target description does not indicate a large >40-atom unit cell. The target has corner and face sharing, but the criterion specifically requires vertex + edge + face coexistence; edge sharing is not reported in the target description. Thus the amorphization risk is low, although thin-film deposition could still yield amorphous material if annealing is insufficient [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CoNi₆N is likely a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly at the supplied high-synthesizability cutoff. The strongest structural support comes from Ni₃N, which contains NNi₆ octahedra and very similar Ni–N bond lengths, but Ni₃N required ~10 GPa high-pressure nitridation in SS-1. The strongest process support for the metallic framework comes from many Ni-rich and Co-containing intermetallics made by arc melting and annealing in CS-3 to CS-5, SS-2 to SS-5, and TS-5. The most critical enabling factor is the low thermodynamic penalty combined with a known NNi₆ nitride motif. The most critical limiting factor is nitrogen incorporation and ordering into the CoNi₆N F-43m structure without forming binary Ni₃N, Co nitrides, or remaining as Co–Ni metal [Reasonable Inference].
