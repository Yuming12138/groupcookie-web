### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.050 eV/atom, placing it at the upper edge of the most favorable scoring bin, so a score of 90 is justified by the provided rubric. Experimentally known analogues at E_hull = 0 include BaGeF6, CaPdF6, Rb2PdF6, Ba(AuF4)2, and related noble-metal fluorides (CS-1, SS-1, SS-2, SS-3). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull near 0.05 eV/atom are synthesized, including K2CrOF5 and CsCuF4 (TS-1, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is not exactly on the hull, so it may be metastable rather than a strict ground-state phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized mainly for F2 atmosphere and toxic/reactive handling, because CaPdF6 and Rb2PdF6 analogues require direct fluorination in F2 or diluted F2 (SS-1, SS-3). The temperature is mild, around 100–300 °C in Pd fluoride analogues and 350 °C in Ru fluoride analogues, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (SS-1, SS-3, CS-2, CS-3). Multi-step handling is included because SS-1 describes pre-fluorination, through-fluorination, tempering, and cooling in F2. Uncertainty remains in whether BaPdF6 forms directly or competes with BaF2/PdF2/PdF4 phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable relative to conventional solid-state diffusion because CaPdF6 forms by direct fluorination at only ~100 °C, indicating high chemical driving force and reactive intermediates (SS-1). Structural similarity is strong at the PdF6 level: CaPdF6 has Pd–F = 1.92 Å, and the target has Pd–F = 1.94 Å (SS-1; target description). However, Ba coordination in the target is 12-coordinate cuboctahedral, whereas CaPdF6 uses CaF6 octahedra, so some structural reconstruction is expected [Reasonable Inference]. This gives a moderate-high F3 rather than an excellent one.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits penalties from both F2 fluorination and sealed noble-metal tube annealing, as fluoroaurate and Cs2PtF6 syntheses require F2/N2, dry Ar, Au/Pt tubes, and long heat treatments (SS-2, SS-4). BaAlF5 also uses sealed Au/Pt tubes at 600–850 °C under Ar and can require quenching/polymorph control (CS-4, CS-5). No >1000 °C or high-pressure penalty is applied because the cited routes remain below 1000 °C and do not use >5 GPa pressure (SS-2, SS-4, CS-4). The main uncertainty is Pd(IV) thermal stability at the temperatures useful for crystallization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are slower for Route 2 because fluoride precursors such as BaF2 are relatively stable and BaAlF5 synthesis requires 15 h to several days at 600–850 °C (CS-4, CS-5). Noble-metal fluoride single-crystal growth can require 30–40 days or 35 days of annealing, indicating slow crystal growth/order development (SS-2, SS-4). The route may improve crystallinity but may also promote decomposition or competing phases if Pd(IV) fluoride stability is limited [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore F3 is moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the mildest temperature profile because BaGeF6 can be obtained by slow evaporation and Ba/Sr manganese fluorides can precipitate at room temperature in aqueous HF, while hydrothermal fluoride phases form at 175–225 °C (CS-1, SS-5, TS-4). It is penalized for HF and oxidant hazards, but not for high temperature, high pressure >5 GPa, or long solid-state processing. The main contradiction is that Pd fluoride analogues use F2 gas rather than aqueous routes, so the benign condition score may overestimate chemical practicality for Pd(IV) [Reasonable Inference from SS-1 and SS-3]. Thus an expert −5 adjustment is applied to the final score.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solution and hydrothermal routes avoid long-range solid-state diffusion, giving high precursor activity and ion mobility, as shown by room-temperature precipitation in SS-5 and hydrothermal crystallization in TS-4. BaGeF6 formation by slow evaporation also supports mild crystallization of Ba-containing fluoride frameworks (CS-1). The structural-reconstruction component is lower because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates aqueous Ba–Pd(IV) fluoride crystallization; this is the largest uncertainty. The route is kinetically attractive but chemically speculative for Pd(IV) [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation environments, BaF12 cuboctahedra and PdF6 octahedra, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination mismatch check [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description does not indicate a large >40 atom cell, and its connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not explicit coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so only one amorphization trigger is counted. Similar fluorides with BaF12/PdF6 or A-site cuboctahedra are crystalline in CS-1 and SS-3, supporting only low-to-medium amorphization risk. Remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic F-43m target is a dynamically stable ordered phase or a generated high-symmetry variant [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaPdF6 is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline ground-state-accessible phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, and experimentally known thermodynamic analogues exist around this energy range (TS-1, TS-2). The strongest support comes from Pd fluoride structural analogues, especially CaPdF6 and Rb2PdF6, which demonstrate that PdF6 octahedral fluoride chemistry is experimentally accessible under F2 (SS-1, SS-3). The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride chemical potential plus oxidizing conditions to stabilize Pd(IV), directly supported by F2 fluorination in Pd and Ru fluoride analogues (SS-1, SS-3, CS-2, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase control: Ba size favors high coordination and possibly different stoichiometries, while Pd(IV) fluoride phases may be moisture- and thermally sensitive [Reasonable Inference from SS-3 and SS-4]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with direct F2 fluorination the most evidence-backed route and fluoride-rich oxidative solution growth the most attractive but more speculative route.
