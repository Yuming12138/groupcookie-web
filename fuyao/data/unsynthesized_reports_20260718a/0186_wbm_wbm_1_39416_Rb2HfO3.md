### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly indicates a predicted ground-state phase. The closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue Cs₂ZrO₃ is also on the hull and has the same Cmcm framework (CS1/SS1/TS1). K₂ZrO₃, Rb₂TiO₃, and RbNaTiO₃ analogues are also reported as E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, reinforcing that alkali early-transition-metal oxides of this family can be thermodynamically stable (SS2–SS5; TS2–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is computational/experimental: E_hull = 0 does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or air stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures comparable to Cs₂ZrO₃ synthesis at 650–700 °C and does not require high pressure (CS1/SS1/TS1). The penalties come from sealed-tube operation, air-sensitive alkali oxide precursors, and multistep heating/grinding/annealing, all explicitly present in the Cs₂ZrO₃ route (CS1/SS1/TS1). CS data strongly support this route because Cs₂ZrO₃ is chemically and structurally closest; SS and TS provide the same strong signal through the identical analogue (CS1/SS1/TS1). The remaining uncertainty is whether Rb₂HfO₃ forms as cleanly as Cs₂ZrO₃, since the provided data do not report the exact Rb–Hf compound.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because HfO₂ is expected to be refractory, while alkali oxides are reactive [Reasonable Inference]. The best evidence for feasibility is that ZrO₂ reacts with Cs₂O-derived media to form Cs₂ZrO₃ at 650–700 °C over 3–7 days (CS1/SS1/TS1). The structural reconstruction burden is low because the target and Cs₂ZrO₃ share the same Cmcm topology and nearly identical BO₅ local geometry (CS1/SS1/TS1). The kinetic uncertainty is that HfO₂ can be less reactive than more labile oxide precursors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high pressure and can use a standard dry N₂/Ar furnace approach, as K₂ZrO₃ was grown at 1000 °C under dry nitrogen (SS2/TS2). It is penalized for multistep flux handling, Rb-containing volatile/corrosive flux, and moisture-sensitive alkali oxide chemistry [Reasonable Inference supported by SS2/TS2 and CS2–CS5]. SS and TS give strong support because K₂ZrO₃ is a close structural/thermodynamic analogue; CS gives less direct signal because the closest chemical analogue Cs₂ZrO₃ was not flux-grown in the provided data, so CS is partially useful but not route-defining (CS1; SS2/TS2). The uncertainty is whether Rb₂O flux stabilizes Cmcm Rb₂HfO₃ rather than other rubidium hafnates.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because nitrate-derived K₂O dissolved ZrO₂ and produced K₂ZrO₃ crystals at 1000 °C (SS2/TS2). This suggests that Rb₂O flux could bypass the slow solid-state diffusion barrier of HfO₂ [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is favorable because K₂ZrO₃ and Cs₂ZrO₃ are close A₂ZrO₃ analogues with five-coordinate Zr units (SS2/TS2; CS1/SS1/TS1). The main uncertainty is composition control, because excess alkali flux and Rb volatility can shift phase equilibria [Reasonable Inference from phase-control issues in SS2/TS2].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 operates in the 520–780 °C range used for several Rb oxide syntheses, so temperature severity is not high (CS3–CS5; SS3/TS3; SS5/TS5). It is penalized for RbOₓ precursor preparation, sealed capsule handling, O₂-free/dry Ar requirements, and air-sensitive materials (CS3–CS5; SS3/TS3). CS provides useful evidence for Rb oxide precursor chemistry; SS/TS provide useful evidence from Rb₂TiO₃ and RbNaTiO₃ capsule syntheses (CS3–CS5; SS3/TS3; SS5/TS5). The uncertainty is that these analogues are not direct hafnates.

#### F3 — Route 3

RbOₓ precursors are reactive and have enabled several Rb oxide phases, but the Rb₂TiO₃ analogue required 41 days at 780 °C, indicating potentially slow kinetics (SS3/TS3). HfO₂ is likely even more diffusion-limited than Ti oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Structural compatibility is reasonable because the target has Rb–O distances similar to other Rb oxides and the closest A₂BO₃ framework exists for Cs₂ZrO₃ (CS1; CS3–CS5; SS3/TS3). The main uncertainty is whether non-stoichiometric RbOₓ will provide the correct oxide chemical potential without forming Rb-rich or HfO₂-rich impurity phases.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large size/charge contrast between Rb⁺ and Hf⁴⁺, which triggers the cation-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference supported by large alkali–O vs Zr/Hf–O bond-length separation in CS1/SS1/TS1]. However, the structure is simple A₂BO₃ and the closest Cmcm analogue Cs₂ZrO₃ is not a large, complex mixed-polyhedra structure (CS1/SS1/TS1). More complex mixed-sharing networks appear in K₂ZrO₃ and K₃NaTh₂O₆, but those are not the exact target topology (SS2/TS2; SS4/TS4). Thus amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂HfO₃ is likely a **ground-state** phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and multiple close A₂BO₃ analogues are also on the convex hull (CS1; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). The strongest support comes from Cs₂ZrO₃, which has the same Cmcm structure type and nearly identical five-coordinate B-site oxide geometry (CS1/SS1/TS1). The most enabling factor is the existence of directly analogous alkali zirconates synthesized by conventional sealed-tube or flux methods (CS1/SS1/TS1; SS2/TS2). The most limiting factor is practical handling and phase control of Rb₂O/RbOₓ, which is air-sensitive, volatile/corrosive, and often requires sealed Ag/Ni capsules or rigorously dry inert atmospheres (CS2–CS5; SS3/TS3). Overall, the target appears experimentally accessible, with flux growth or sealed alkali-oxide synthesis being the most plausible approaches.
