### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold, so the thermodynamic score is high. The closest chemical/structural analogue Cs4IrO4 is computed on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS1, SS1). Thermodynamic analogues with similar positive hull energies, such as E_hull ≈0.048–0.088 eV/atom, are reported as synthesized, supporting possible metastable accessibility (TS1, TS4, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target’s high-valent IrO6 environment is not directly demonstrated by any CS/SS/TS example.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because Cs4IrO4 was made with 900 °C initial heating and 740 °C long anneal in an Ag-bomb (CS1, SS1). The penalties come from volatile/air-sensitive Cs oxide precursors, sealed-container requirements, and multiple handling/heating steps, all explicitly present in the Cs4IrO4 route (CS1, SS1). The main uncertainty is whether the same conditions stabilize octahedral Ir(VI) rather than a lower-valent Cs–Ir–O phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are limited by IrO2 reactivity and the very long 86 d annealing required for the closest analogue (CS1, SS1). Alkali oxide media likely aid transport, but the structural conversion from square-planar IrO4 in Cs4IrO4 to octahedral IrO6 in the target is significant [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS1]. Thus, F3 is moderate-low despite strong chemical relevance.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses lower temperatures similar to Cs-rich oxometallate syntheses at 500–700 °C (CS2, CS3), but it introduces an internally oxidizing sealed environment to reach Ir(VI) [Reasonable Inference]. The severity is higher than Route 1 because peroxide/superoxide oxygen release creates pressure/redox-control risk, and CS/SS do not directly demonstrate this exact Ir(VI) chemistry. Insufficient direct evidence from TS for synthesis conditions, so TS is neutral for this route’s F2.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reactive Cs peroxide/superoxide-type precursors should improve oxygen activity and possibly help form IrO6 octahedra [Reasonable Inference], and CsO1.8 has already reacted with IrO2 to make Cs4IrO4 (CS1, SS1). However, Ir diffusion remains slow and no analogue directly shows Cs6IrO6 crystallization. F3 is slightly better than Route 1 structurally but still limited by unproven redox control.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from low-temperature sealed-container precedents for Cs-rich oxometallates, including Cs9AlO4 at 180–300 °C in sealed Ta crucibles (SS3). It still uses highly air-sensitive Cs metal/Cs2O and a sealed system, so it is not easy. Insufficient TS evidence supports Cs–Ir–O redox chemistry; TS analogues mainly show that sealed long-duration reactions can isolate metastable materials (TS2, TS3).

#### F3 — Route 3

Cs metal/Cs oxide mixtures are highly reactive and could improve transport, as in suboxometallate synthesis (SS3). The key kinetic/chemical weakness is that this route is reducing, whereas the target likely requires high-valent Ir(VI) [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, structural/redox reconstruction is poor even though precursor activity is high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large size/field-strength contrast between Cs and Ir triggers cation-radius mismatch risk [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the complex low-symmetry Cs-rich oxometallates in CS/SS datasets (CS2–CS5, SS3–SS5). The likely conventional Fm-3m cell exceeds 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference], adding ordering complexity. Mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity is not established for the target, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific evidence, so that check is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs6IrO6 is likely a marginally stable or weakly metastable phase because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom, near the typical experimental accessibility threshold. The best support comes from the direct Cs–Ir–O analogue Cs4IrO4, which is both chemically and structurally similar and experimentally synthesized in a sealed Ag-bomb (CS1, SS1). The most important enabling factor is the availability of reactive cesium oxide media, which can serve both as Cs source and oxygen/redox buffer in sealed containers (CS1–CS3). The most important limiting factor is redox/phase control: the target likely requires octahedral high-valent Ir, whereas the closest known Cs–Ir–O analogue contains square-planar Ir coordination (CS1, SS1; target structure). Long annealing and moisture-sensitive Cs oxide handling further reduce practical ease (CS1, CS5).
