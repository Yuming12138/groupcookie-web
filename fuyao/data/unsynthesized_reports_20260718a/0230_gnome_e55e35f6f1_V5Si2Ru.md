### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum thermodynamic score under the provided rubric. Chemically similar transition-metal silicides have low hull energies, including Nb8Co2Si2 at 0.0128 eV/atom and Nb8Fe2Si2 at 0.0479 eV/atom (CS #1, CS #2). Thermodynamic analogues are also mostly ground-state phases with E_hull = 0.0, including V2P, V3Sb, V4CP2, and V5Ge3 (TS #1–#4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee facile kinetic access or absence of competing ternary silicide phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because arc melting implies temperatures above 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], while analogous routes require Ar atmosphere and evacuated annealing (CS #3, CS #5, TS #1). The route is also multi-step because analogues use pressing or melting followed by annealing and sometimes quenching (CS #3, CS #5, TS #1). No high pressure is required, and insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates no mandatory volatile precursor compensation for Si, unlike Sb in CS #3.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental arc-melting improves homogenization by creating an alloy button, as seen in CS #3 and CS #5. However, purity improves only after long annealing in CS #3 and CS #5, indicating that ordering and phase equilibration remain kinetically limited. The target is not structurally identical to the cited arc-melted analogues, so some structural reconstruction is expected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 follows the chemically closest evidence: fusion under Ar and annealing at 1100 °C for 90 h or 800 °C for 750 h in evacuated ampoules (CS #1, CS #2). It requires controlled atmosphere/vacuum and multiple processing steps, so deductions are applied. The additional >1400 °C deduction is conservative because the target contains refractory V and Ru and may require high-temperature fusion for complete alloying [Reasonable Inference], although CS #1 and CS #2 do not specify the fusion temperature.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The closest silicide analogues were obtained by fusion plus very long annealing, which supports feasibility but also indicates slow homogenization (CS #1, CS #2). Elemental precursors are stable and easy to handle but not chemically activated, so precursor activity is modest (CS #1, CS #2). Structural transfer is only partial because the closest chemical analogues are P4/mcc Khatyrkite-derived phases, whereas the target is P-4m2 [Reasonable Inference from CS #1, CS #2 and target structure].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Hot-pressing/high-temperature V–Si processing can require 1500–2000 °C and hydrogen or controlled atmosphere (SS #1), while V–Ge analogues use Ar at 900–1250 °C (TS #4). These data support significant temperature and atmosphere deductions. No high-pressure deduction is applied because hot pressing/cold pressing in SS #1 and TS #4 is not >5 GPa.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Powder hot pressing improves contact area [Reasonable Inference], but SS #1 and TS #4 still indicate that high-temperature homogenization is needed for V-rich silicide/germanide phases. SS #1 also reports contamination/phase-control issues in related silicide processing, lowering the structural-reconstruction score. This route is therefore feasible but kinetically less attractive than fusion/arc-melting routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a relatively simple ordered intermetallic formula and no evidence of a >40 atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered. The target description reports face- and edge-sharing cuboctahedra but not vertex sharing, so the full mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered. A modest radius/size mismatch involving Si relative to V/Ru is treated as one amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference], giving a low–medium amorphization risk. Insufficient direct radius evidence is available from CS/SS/TS, so this part is extrapolated.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

V5RuSi2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the supplied target hull energy is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is moderately strong: chemically similar transition-metal silicides are made by elemental fusion and long evacuated annealing (CS #1, CS #2), while V-containing analogues support arc-melting, hot pressing, and sealed-ampoule annealing approaches (CS #3, SS #1, TS #4). The most critical enabling factor is the very favorable computed thermodynamic stability combined with multiple successful high-temperature intermetallic synthesis precedents (CS #1–#3, TS #1–#4). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic equilibration among refractory V/Ru/Si components, because analogous systems require long annealing or homogenization (CS #1, CS #2, CS #3, CS #5). Phase competition with nearby V–Si or Ru–Si binaries is also a likely concern [Reasonable Inference].
