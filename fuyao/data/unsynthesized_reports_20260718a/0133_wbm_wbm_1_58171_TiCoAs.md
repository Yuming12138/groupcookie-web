### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, which directly supports a near-maximum thermodynamic score. TS gives strong corroboration because the closest thermodynamic analogues TiRuAs, TiRuP, NiPTi, TiRhGe, and CoMoP are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom. SS also supports stability because several structurally related Ti–pnictide/germanide analogues lie on the hull, while CS gives mostly positive evidence but includes some mildly metastable compounds such as CoCrGe at 0.0645 eV/atom and VMnAs at 0.101 eV/atom. Remaining uncertainty is not thermodynamic hull stability, but whether the calculated Pnma TiCoAs phase is the experimentally preferred polymorph at accessible temperatures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-tube solid-state synthesis

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the required 800–1000 °C range is comparable to arsenide solid-state routes at 750–850 °C (CS) and Pnma sintering near 1000 °C (CS), but sealed vacuum handling is needed due to As volatility [Reasonable Inference; TS sealed tubes]. Kinetics are moderate: elemental Ti/Co powders are not highly reactive [Reasonable Inference], but related arsenides form by solid-state diffusion after long treatment (CS). The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: the target Pnma coordination pattern is closely mirrored by NiPTi and CoMoP analogues (SS, TS). The uncertainty is whether Ti–Co–As competing binaries slow equilibration or trap off-stoichiometric phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Arc/induction melting plus annealing

Route 2 has high severity because melt analogues use purified Ar, evacuated anneals, and sometimes multiple high-temperature anneals up to 1275 °C (SS), while elemental As introduces volatility and toxicity concerns [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are relatively favorable because melting/prealloying removes much of the diffusion limitation, matching melt-growth TiRhGe/TiPdGe analogues (CS, SS, TS). However, phase control is a concern because TiRhGe/TiPdGe-related systems show allotropic or narrow-temperature stability behavior (CS, SS, TS). The main uncertainty is whether As can be retained quantitatively during melt processing even if pre-reacted arsenides are used [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Metal-flux growth

Route 3 has moderate-to-high condition severity because flux analogues require evacuated capsules, high-temperature heating, slow cooling, and flux removal, as shown for TiRuAs/TiRuP-related growth (SS, TS). Kinetically, this is the most favorable route because a liquid flux improves mass transport and can grow better-ordered crystals below the melting points of refractory elements (SS, TS). The limitation is phase selectivity: SS/TS analogues mention variations between structural forms and impurities, so the Pnma TiCoAs phase may require careful flux, cooling-rate, and composition optimization. Insufficient evidence from CS directly supports flux growth of TiCoAs-like Co arsenides, so CS is neutral for this route’s phase-selectivity estimate.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Iodine-assisted CVT

Route 4 has moderate severity because CoCrGe is grown in closed quartz ampoules with I₂ at elevated temperature (CS), but I₂/As handling and pre-synthesis of charge add hazards and steps [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics can be good due to vapor-phase transport, as demonstrated for CoCrGe CVT (CS). The structural transferability is weaker because the CVT analogue is chemically similar but not the same Pnma structure type, and no SS/TS source directly confirms CVT for TiCoAs-like Pnma pnictides. Therefore this route is best treated as a crystal-growth/purification option after bulk synthesis rather than the primary discovery route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic/arsenide with dense coordination, and Pnma analogues such as NiPTi and CoMoP are ordered crystalline phases (SS, TS), which argues against severe amorphization. Two rubric triggers are counted: Ti/Co size mismatch exceeds ~15% [Reasonable Inference], and the Pnma CoMoP analogue shows mixed face/edge/corner polyhedral sharing (TS). The unit-cell atom count is not a trigger because Pnma 1:1:1 analogues have 12-atom conventional cells (SS, TS), and the target is inferred to share that small-cell motif [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is that CS does not provide direct amorphization data, so CS is insufficient and neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TiCoAs is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is exactly 0.0000 eV/atom, and thermodynamic analogues in TS are also frequently on the hull. The synthesis evidence is fairly strong: CS supports ternary arsenide formation by long solid-state diffusion, SS/TS support closely related Pnma 1:1:1 frameworks, and TS supports direct sealed-tube preparation for an isostructural phosphide analogue. The most critical enabling factor is the structural match to known Pnma 1:1:1 pnictide/germanide phases, especially NiPTi and CoMoP-like coordination environments (SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is arsenic volatility/toxicity combined with slow Ti/Co solid-state diffusion, requiring sealed tubes, prereaction, and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, bulk synthesis should be feasible, with direct sealed-tube annealing being the best first attempt and flux growth as the best route for single crystals.
