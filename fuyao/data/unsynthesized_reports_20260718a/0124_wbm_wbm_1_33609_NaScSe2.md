### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is exactly on the calculated convex hull, E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. The most relevant analogues, including NaErSe2, LiYSe2, LiHoSe2, LiTbSe2, Na2ZrSe3, and LiScS2, are also listed with E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, CS2, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). This strongly supports a ground-state or very low-driving-force phase, although calculated hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for competing Na–Sc–Se phases specifically, so phase competition within the exact ternary remains uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because the analogues require sealed evacuated/inert tubes, staged heating, long annealing, and intermediate grinding (CS2, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4). Na and Se volatility/reactivity make sealed-tube work necessary [Reasonable Inference], consistent with Na–Se syntheses in evacuated ampoules (CS1, CS3). No high pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required based on the analogues (CS1–CS3). Insufficient evidence from TS for direct elemental NaScSe2 conditions means TS is neutral for exact condition severity, but TS analogues support sealed 850–900 °C processing (TS1–TS5).

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because elemental Sc must react and diffuse into an ordered layered selenide [Reasonable Inference]. The R-3m product topology is strongly supported by LiYSe2/LiHoSe2/LiTbSe2 analogues (CS2, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4), but those examples use long multi-step anneals, indicating slow ordering. The direct elemental Na–Ga–Se route demonstrates elemental Na/Se reactions are feasible in evacuated ampoules (CS3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for elemental Sc + Na + Se reacting cleanly to NaScSe2, so uncertainty remains.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids elemental Sc-only chemistry but still requires air-sensitive Na2Se and sealed vacuum tubes, as in Na2ZrSe3 (CS1/SS5/TS5). The temperature is favorable, because the closest Na2ZrSe3 route works at 800 °C for 3 d (CS1/SS5/TS5), and Sc sulfide analogues can be made from binary sulfides (CS4). The route is multi-step if Na2Se and Sc2Se3 must be prepared separately, which is supported by the precursor-complexity discussion for Na2Se and Sc2S3 analogues (CS1, CS4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for Sc2Se3 specifically, so that precursor choice is partially inferential.

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary selenides should have better chemical activity and local bonding compatibility than elemental Sc [Reasonable Inference]. Na2ZrSe3 formation from Na2Se + metal + Se supports a similar Na–selenide route (CS1/SS5/TS5), and LiScS2/NaScS2 synthesis from binary sulfides supports binary Sc-chalcogenide chemistry (CS4). Structural reconstruction remains nontrivial because NaScSe2 requires long-range Na/Sc ordering in octahedral layers [Reasonable Inference]. Phase purity remains uncertain because Na2ZrSe3 products were described as crystals in a metallic matrix, indicating phase-control issues (CS1/SS5/TS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because NaErSe2 forms at 850 °C in sealed Nb ampoules with NaCl flux, avoiding >1000 °C and high pressure (SS1, TS1). It still requires specialized sealed metal ampoules and prepared selenide/chloride precursors (SS1, TS1). The route is less penalized for volatile free Se if Sc2Se3/ScCl3 are used instead of elemental Se [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS for flux growth of Sc analogues means CS is neutral for this route’s exact condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth should improve kinetics by enhancing mass transport [Reasonable Inference], and the strongest NaMSe2 analogue, NaErSe2, forms in NaCl flux (SS1, TS1). Structural transferability is high because NaErSe2 and NaScSe2 share NaMSe2 stoichiometry, R-3m symmetry, NaSe6/MSe6 octahedra, and nearly identical Na–Se distances (SS1, TS1; target description). The main kinetic uncertainty is that Sc3+ is smaller than Er3+, so local bonding and competing Sc–Se/Sc–Cl phases may differ [Reasonable Inference]. The NaErSe2 report also indicates phase competition because NaErSe2 formed instead of the intended ErSeCl (SS1, TS1).

#### F2 — Route 4

The exchange step can likely be performed below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], and cation exchange in alkali manganese selenides uses sealed/inert conditions rather than high pressure (CS5). However, the overall route is multi-step because it requires parent LiScSe2 synthesis plus ion exchange and washing (CS5; parent synthesis inferred from CS2/SS2/TS2 and CS4). Air/moisture sensitivity is expected for alkali scandium selenides [Reasonable Inference], and CS5 explicitly uses deoxygenated washing under nitrogen. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for LiScSe2 → NaScSe2 exchange specifically, so SS/TS are neutral for the exact exchange step.

#### F3 — Route 4

The route receives the best kinetic score because topotactic Na/Li exchange can preserve an already-formed ScSe2 octahedral framework [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 demonstrates that alkali cation exchange can access difficult Na selenides, and LiScS2 plus LiYSe2/LnSe2 analogues support the plausibility of a layered LiMCh2 parent (CS4, CS2, SS2–SS4, TS2–TS4). The major uncertainty is lack of a directly reported LiScSe2 parent or NaScSe2 exchange product in CS/SS/TS. Incomplete Li/Na exchange and framework collapse are possible limiting factors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple R-3m layered octahedral compound rather than a large complex unit-cell structure, and close analogues share the same compact Caswellsilverite structure (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The Na–Se and Sc–Se octahedra have significantly different bond lengths, reflecting a sizeable cation-size mismatch (target description; ionic-radius interpretation [Reasonable Inference]). The structure has corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no reported face-sharing, reducing amorphization risk relative to mixed face/edge/corner networks (target description). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for amorphous NaScSe2 formation, so this assessment is based on structural descriptors and analogues.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaScSe2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close Na/Li trivalent selenide analogues are also on the hull (CS1, CS2, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong structurally: NaErSe2, LiYSe2, LiHoSe2, and LiTbSe2 share the R-3m Caswellsilverite framework of edge- and corner-sharing octahedra (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of multiple experimentally accessible alkali–trivalent–selenide Caswellsilverite phases under sealed-tube or flux conditions (CS2, SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control under Na/Se volatility and air-sensitive precursor conditions, as shown by sealed ampoule requirements and mixed/competing phase issues in Na2ZrSe3 and NaErSe2 analogues (CS1/SS5/TS5; SS1/TS1). Overall, synthesis should be feasible, with flux growth or binary-selenide routes being the most experimentally defensible starting points.
