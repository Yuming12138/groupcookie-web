### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the supplied target thermodynamic data. Thermodynamic analogues reinforce that Zr pnictide frameworks can be stable: Zr₂NiAs₂, Zr₅Sb₃P, Zr₅GeSb₃, ZrAs₂, and Zr₅Sb₃S all have E_hull = 0.0 in TS-1 to TS-5. CS is less clean because chemically similar entries include both stable compounds and an unstable one at E_hull ≈ 0.907 eV/atom (CS-5), so CS is treated as only secondary support. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or H occupancy control.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits sealed-tube/vacuum severity from ZrAs₂ synthesis, where evacuated fused-silica tubes and 850–1050 °C heating are used (SS-5, TS-4). Zr₂NiAs₂-related arsenides also require glovebox handling under purified Ar and evacuated quartz ampoules at about 1100 °C for Zr compounds (SS-1, TS-1). As volatility/toxicity and the need for regrinding/reannealing penalize the route, while the absence of high pressure avoids a larger penalty. H incorporation has insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS, so the ZrH₂ adaptation is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are limited by refractory Zr and solid-state diffusion, but Zr–As analogues demonstrate that Zr–As bonds and frameworks can form in sealed tubes at 850–1100 °C (SS-1, SS-5, TS-1, TS-4). The target’s mixed AsZr₆ polyhedral connectivity implies nontrivial ordering, and analogues sometimes require long holds, grinding, reheating, or failed phase attempts (SS-5, TS-4). ZrH₂ may improve H chemical activity relative to H₂ gas, but that is [Reasonable Inference] because no hydride analogue is present. The score is therefore moderate-low.

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting adds severe temperature demands, with related intermetallic/boride syntheses using arc melting or temperatures up to 1482–1500 °C (CS-3, CS-4, SS-3). Zr–Sb analogues require purified, Ti-gettered Ar, evacuated ampoule annealing, long times, and quenching (SS-3). This route is penalized for high temperature, controlled atmosphere, volatility of As, and multiple processing steps. The advantage is homogenization, but H must be added after melting because direct H retention during arc melting is unsupported by CS/SS/TS and is unlikely [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc melting should improve cation mixing for refractory Zr–As compositions, consistent with arc-melted Zr–Sb and transition-metal boride analogues (SS-3, CS-3, CS-5). However, long post-anneals are still needed in related systems, including 1400 h annealing for a Zr–Sb analogue and 500 h annealing for a boride analogue (SS-3, CS-5). Hydride uptake after alloy formation introduces an unproven diffusion/insertion step, and CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct hydride-kinetics evidence. Thus F3 is slightly better than direct solid state but still only moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C synthesis by using Zn flux at 900–1000 °C, directly supported by Zr₅Sb₃X analogues (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). It still requires Ar-sealed ampoules, volatile Zn/As handling, multiple steps, and toxic As, so the severity is not low. Compared with Route 1 and Route 2, the absence of arc-melting temperatures is a major benefit. CS provides insufficient specific evidence for flux synthesis of Zr–As–H and is treated as neutral for this route’s hydride component.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux-mediated synthesis is kinetically favorable because Zn flux gives quantitative or improved Zr₅Sb₃X products at 900–1000 °C and mitigates inhomogeneity compared with arc melting (SS-2, SS-4, TS-2, TS-3, TS-5). Liquid flux should improve Zr/As transport and lower diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference] consistent with those analogues. The limiting kinetic step is still H insertion into the final Imm2 phase, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence. Therefore Route 3 receives the best F3 but not a near-perfect score.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one metal cation, Zr, so cation-radius mismatch is not a strong amorphization driver from the target composition. The formula is small, but the exact crystallographic cell size is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not directly establish the target atom count, so C2 is not triggered. The target explicitly contains mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing AsZr₆ polyhedra, and related Zr pnictides also show complex polyhedral sharing (SS-3, SS-4, TS-3), so one amorphization/complexity trigger is assigned. The result is a low–medium amorphization risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₂As₂H is likely a thermodynamic ground-state candidate because the supplied target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several thermodynamic Zr pnictide analogues are also on the hull (TS-1 to TS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from SS/TS, especially Zr₂NiAs₂ and ZrAs₂ sealed-tube chemistry and Zr₅Sb₃X Zn-flux synthesis (SS-1, SS-2, SS-5, TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). The most important enabling factor is the robust Zr–pnictide framework chemistry under sealed Ar/vacuum or flux conditions, demonstrated repeatedly in the structural and thermodynamic analogues (SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The most important limiting factor is H incorporation: none of CS/SS/TS provides a direct Zr–As–H hydride analogue, so all hydride-source choices and post-anneal steps are [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will require careful stoichiometry, sealed containment, and iterative phase analysis.
