### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is direct evidence for a predicted ground-state phase. The CS/SS/TS sets strongly reinforce this because many close analogues are also E_hull = 0, including ZrRuSi, ZrPdSi, HfRhSi, ZrIrSi, ZrPtSi, and NbRhSi (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3, SS-4, SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not guarantee the hexagonal P-62m polymorph is the kinetically selected product under all lab conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

This route has moderate severity because the closest same-structure ZrRuSi analogue uses arc/induction melting and annealing at 800–1100 °C under gettered Ar/vacuum in ampoules (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3). The deductions come from temperatures exceeding 1000 °C in parts of the analogue window, controlled atmosphere/vacuum, and multi-step melting/annealing/cooling (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3). The route is not extreme because no high pressure or exotic precursor chemistry is reported (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3).

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

Kinetics are helped by the melt step, which should homogenize dense Zr/Rh/Si elements before solid-state ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest kinetic support is that ZrRuSi already forms in the same P-62m structure and similar coordination environment after arc/induction melting and annealing (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3). The main uncertainty is slow long-range ordering among refractory metals, as multi-day annealing is standard in the analogues (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct solid-state sintering

This route scores best on condition severity because related E-phases are described as forming easily by sintering powdered components near 1000 °C at 1:1:1 stoichiometry, with no special atmosphere or pressure specified (CS-1, SS-5, TS-4). Because atmosphere is not specified in those sources, evidence is insufficient from CS-1/SS-5/TS-4 for requiring Ar or vacuum, so no controlled-atmosphere deduction was applied. Remaining uncertainty is that best practice for Zr-containing powders likely still favors inert handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct solid-state sintering

Kinetics are the weakness of this route because dense elemental Zr and Rh powders are expected to diffuse slowly at ~1000 °C [Reasonable Inference]. The sintering references state related E-phases form easily, which is favorable (CS-1, SS-5, TS-4), but the closest sintered ZrPdSi/ZrPtSi analogues are orthorhombic Pnma rather than the target hexagonal P-62m (CS-1, SS-5, TS-4). Thus, phase selection may require repeated grinding, extended sintering, or a melt-assisted route if Pnma/binary impurities appear [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Induction/levitation melting + vacuum anneal

This route has moderate severity because HfRhGe is prepared by induction levitation melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing at 950 °C for one or two weeks (SS-2). The controlled atmosphere/vacuum and multi-step process drive the F2 deductions (SS-2). No high-pressure condition is reported, and the annealing temperature is below or around 1000 °C, so it is less severe than the highest-temperature arc-melt/anneal variants (SS-2; CS-2/SS-4/TS-3).

#### F3 — Route 3: Induction/levitation melting + vacuum anneal

Kinetics benefit from full melting, which should improve compositional homogeneity before annealing [Reasonable Inference]. The same-structure HfRhGe P-62m analogue supports the crystallization pathway, but the Hf/Ge chemistry is not identical to Zr/Si, so the transferability is slightly weaker than ZrRuSi (SS-2 versus CS-2/SS-4/TS-3). The long one- to two-week anneal indicates ordering or polymorph equilibration may be slow (SS-2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a compact ternary intermetallic rather than a large complex oxide or framework, and close analogues crystallize readily as ordered intermetallic phases (CS-2, SS-2, SS-4, TS-3). One amorphization risk trigger is assigned because Zr and Rh have substantially different metallic radii [Reasonable Inference], but the unit cell complexity and polyhedral-connectivity criteria are not clearly triggered by the provided structural descriptions. Remaining uncertainty is that amorphization data are not explicitly reported in CS/SS/TS, so F4 relies partly on structural simplicity and successful crystalline analogues.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrRhSi is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many close CS/SS/TS analogues are also hull-stable. The strongest synthesis support comes from ZrRuSi, which has the same hexagonal P-62m structure and is made by arc/induction melting plus annealing under controlled atmosphere (CS-2, SS-4, TS-3). The most enabling factor is the dense cluster of stable, experimentally synthesized equiatomic RTX/RTSi-type analogues containing Zr/Hf/Nb, Rh/Ru/Ir/Pt/Pd, and Si/Ge (CS-1 to CS-5; SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5). The most limiting factor is phase selection between hexagonal P-62m and orthorhombic Pnma relatives, since several close Zr–T–Si analogues are Pnma while ZrRuSi/HfRhGe are P-62m (CS-1, CS-2, SS-2, SS-4, SS-5). Overall, the material should be synthesizable with conventional intermetallic methods, with direct sintering as the lowest-severity route and arc melting plus anneal as the most robust backup.
