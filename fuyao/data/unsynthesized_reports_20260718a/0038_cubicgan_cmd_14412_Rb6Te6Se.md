### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has an explicitly reported E_hull of 0.0500 eV/atom, placing it at the favorable edge of the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. Chemically and structurally nearby reference compounds are often on the hull, including Rb₂ZrTe₃, K₂ZrTe₃, K₃SbTe₃, RbYTe₂, Rb₂Te₃, and Rb₃SbSe₃/Rb₃BiSe₃ analogs (CS 1–5; SS 1–5). TS is particularly relevant because several thermodynamic analogs around 0.05–0.08 eV/atom are reported as synthesizable, such as RbMnTe₂ and Cs₂Se (TS 1, TS 2). Remaining uncertainty is that E_hull = 0.05 eV/atom may correspond to a metastable rather than true ground-state phase.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses standard sealed solid-state conditions similar to Rb₂ZrTe₃/K₂ZrTe₃ and K₃SbTe₃ syntheses (CS 1–3; SS 5). The severity deductions come from vacuum/inert sealed conditions, volatile Se/Te/Rb-containing reagents, and air-sensitive alkali chalcogenides [Reasonable Inference supported by CS 1–3 and TS 1]. It avoids multi-GPa pressure and can likely avoid temperatures above 1000 °C by using reactive binaries [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic phase requires quenching or a narrower temperature window.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Pre-synthesized Rb₂Te/Rb₂Se should be more reactive than elemental refractory powders, and related Rb/K tellurides form in ampoules without exotic activation (CS 1–3). Diffusion should be manageable because Rb/Se/Te are relatively mobile compared with refractory cation systems [Reasonable Inference], and analogs crystallize at 973 K–1000 °C (CS 1–3). Structural reconstruction is moderate rather than easy because none of the direct solid-state analogs has the exact Fm-3m Rb₆Te₆Se topology. The main uncertainty is whether mixed Te/Se ordering equilibrates within practical annealing times.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is chemically attractive but operationally severe because Rb₂Te₃ and Cs₂Se ammonothermal syntheses require hypercritical/high-pressure ammonia and special autoclaves (SS 1; TS 2). Although the pressure is far below the scoring threshold of 5 GPa, 1000–2000 bar NH₃ is still a major practical burden (SS 1; TS 2). The route also requires air-free loading, pressure reaction, slow cooling, and ammonia removal, motivating a multi-step and hazardous-atmosphere penalty [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient evidence for NH₃ autoclave synthesis and is neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Ammonothermal transport should strongly improve kinetics by dissolving or mobilizing polychalcogenide species, as shown for Rb₂Te₃ in NH₃ (SS 1) and Cs₂Se in NH₃ (TS 2). Slow cooling and temperature gradients support crystallization rather than simple precipitation (SS 1; TS 2). Structural transferability is plausible because the target is an alkali mixed chalcogenide, but the exact Te/Se ordered Rb₆Te₆Se phase is not demonstrated [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Se substitutes/order-selects into the desired single Se site rather than producing Rb₂Te₃/Rb₂Se mixtures.

Route 3 uses a flux or melt pathway in the 750–900 °C range, supported by RbBr flux synthesis of RbYTe₂ at 900 °C (CS 5) and Rb–Se melt/gas routes at 750–850 °C (SS 2–4). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₆Te₆Se is likely a **near-ground-state or mildly metastable phase**, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom. The synthesis outlook is favorable because chemically similar Rb/K tellurides and structurally related Rb/K selenide/telluride frameworks are experimentally accessible by ampoule, flux, melt, and ammonothermal routes (CS 1–5; SS 1–5). The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and flux/ammonothermal transportability of alkali chalcogenide species, supported by Rb₂Te₃ ammonothermal synthesis and RbBr-flux RbYTe₂ growth (SS 1; CS 5). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: competing Rb–Te and Rb–Se binary/polychalcogenide phases are plausible, and related systems report impurity/by-product formation or narrow windows (CS 1, CS 2, CS 5, TS 1). Overall, the best practical route is a dry RbBr/KBr flux or chalcogenide-flux synthesis under sealed inert conditions, followed by careful composition and cooling-rate optimization.
