### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. TS analogues include several E_hull = 0.0 Re/Os oxide frameworks, including SbRe₂O₆ and multiple Os oxides (TS-1 to TS-5), supporting the general stability of heavy 5d transition-metal oxide networks. CS evidence is mixed but still favorable: TaON has a stable monoclinic polymorph at E_hull = 0.0 and another slightly metastable polymorph at 0.0386 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that none of TS directly demonstrates ReNO oxynitride thermodynamics beyond the computed target hull value.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate-low F2 because NH₃ or NH₄Cl nitridation requires controlled/reactive atmosphere, and CS-1 explicitly uses Ar, evacuated quartz ampoules, liquid ammonia chemistry, and 900–1000 °C synthesis. CS-2 also reports a narrow phase window for TaON nitridation, indicating nontrivial condition control. The temperature is not extreme compared with high-pressure routes, but volatile/reactive nitrogen sources and possible multi-step precursor preparation lower the score. Insufficient direct evidence from SS and TS for ammonolysis of ReNO specifically.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetically, Route 1 benefits from chemically active nitrogenating precursors, analogous to metal oxide amides and TaOCl₃/NH₄Cl chemistry in CS-1. However, Re oxides/metals are likely less reactive than molecular or sol-gel precursors [Reasonable Inference], and formation of the hydrophilite-derived framework likely requires significant reconstruction. CS-2 reports that TaON phase control can be difficult, supporting only moderate kinetic confidence. Insufficient direct kinetic evidence from SS/TS for nitrogen diffusion into Re oxide frameworks.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has better structural targeting but severe conditions. SS-4 uses 5 GPa and 1350 °C in a Girdle–Belt apparatus with graphite furnace and platinum capsule, while SS-5/TS-4 use 3 GPa and 900 °C for a high-pressure Os oxide. Because the scoring table penalizes pressures strictly greater than 5 GPa, the pressure penalty is not applied at exactly 5 GPa, but specialized equipment remains a practical challenge. Insufficient CS evidence for high-pressure Re oxynitride synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has only moderate kinetic scoring because refractory Re oxide/nitride solids likely diffuse slowly [Reasonable Inference]. The key positive is structural reconstruction: SS-4 is a hydrophilite-derived monoclinic P2₁/c Re oxide with edge/corner-sharing octahedra and tilt angles close to the target, so the structural pathway is plausible. However, the need to substitute half the anion sublattice with nitrogen is not demonstrated by SS-4, so anion ordering and nitrogen retention remain uncertain. TS-4 supports that high-pressure Os oxide frameworks can crystallize in short holds, but it does not address N incorporation.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids multi-GPa pressure, but it requires evacuated sealed tubes and volatile/internal nitrogen chemistry. TS-5 shows Re oxide synthesis at 500 °C for 3 days in evacuated sealed silica tubes, while CS-1 shows oxynitride formation from TaOCl₃ + NH₄Cl in quartz ampoules at 900–1000 °C. The need to manage pressure, NH₄Cl decomposition, and possible oxyhalide volatility makes this route challenging. Insufficient SS evidence for sealed-tube hydrophilite ReNO formation.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 has the lowest F3 because Re metal plus Re oxide reactions can be slow; TS-5 requires 3 days even for SbRe₂O₆ formation. NH₄Cl/oxychloride chemistry can improve reactivity by analogy to TaON (CS-1), but the exact ReNO phase is not structurally templated. The route may require repeated grinding or transport-like conditions, and TS-5 notes additional chemical transport for single crystals. Therefore, kinetics and phase selection are less favorable than in the high-pressure structural route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The amorphization risk appears low because the target has a single cation, a compact formula, and a regular octahedral ReN₃O₃ framework rather than a highly mixed-cation glass-forming network. The target reports corner- and edge-sharing octahedra, and SS-4 shows that a related hydrophilite-derived Re oxide framework with similar corner/edge sharing can crystallize. No face-sharing is reported, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not fully triggered. Remaining uncertainty is the actual unit-cell size from the CIF, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct target-specific atom count.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ReNO is likely a ground-state phase on the computed convex hull because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest structural support comes from hydrophilite-derived ScRe₂O₆, which shares monoclinic P2₁/c symmetry and edge/corner-sharing Re-centered octahedral connectivity (SS-4). The strongest chemical support comes from TaON oxynitrides, which show that dense transition-metal oxynitrides can be synthesized by thermal ammonolysis or oxychloride/NH₄Cl routes (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the combination of thermodynamic stability and a close hydrophilite-derived Re oxide structural analogue. The most critical limiting factor is controlled nitrogen incorporation into a Re(V) oxynitride without forming Re oxides, Re metal, or Re nitrides; no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates ReNO synthesis.
