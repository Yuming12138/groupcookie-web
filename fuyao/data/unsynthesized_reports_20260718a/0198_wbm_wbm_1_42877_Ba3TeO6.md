### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the 90–100 score band (target data). Multiple close analogues also sit on the convex hull, including Ba2SrTeO6, Ba2CaTeO6, Ba3SrSb2O9, and Ba3CaSb2O9 (SS-1; SS-2; SS-3; SS-4; TS-1; TS-2; TS-3; TS-4). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee easy phase selection under laboratory oxygen chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The ceramic route is supported by direct Ba–Te oxide synthesis at 750–800 °C in flowing O2 (CS-2) and by very close Ba2SrTeO6/Ba2CaTeO6 analogues requiring 600 °C preheat and 1100–1300 °C sintering in air (CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The >1000 °C and multi-step deductions are justified by the perovskite-derived Ba2MTeO6 ceramic protocols (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). Toxicity is assigned because tellurium compounds require careful handling [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
BaCO3/TeO2 precursors are accessible but relatively low-activity ceramic reagents, consistent with the need for prolonged heating and regrinding in Ba2SrTeO6/Ba2CaTeO6 (CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). Structural reconstruction is helped by the close R-3 perovskite-derived Ba2SrTeO6 analogue with TeO6 octahedra (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). The key uncertainty is whether Ba can fully occupy the divalent octahedral site without forming Ba3Te2O9 or other Ba–tellurate competitors [Reasonable Inference from CS-2].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The precipitation route is milder thermally because Ba5I2O12 was calcined at 700 °C after wet precursor formation (CS-1). The route is multi-step, since CS-1 includes dissolution, precipitation, decanting/washing, drying, and heating. No high pressure or sealed volatility control is required by CS-1, but caustic Ba(OH)2 and toxic Te chemistry justify a handling deduction [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Solution precipitation should give higher precursor homogeneity than carbonate mixing [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 demonstrates that a Ba/high-valent oxoanion precipitate can be converted to a crystalline oxide at 700 °C. The high kinetic score is moderated because CS-1 reports intermediate phases and use of Rb2O to improve crystallite size, indicating nontrivial crystallization control. The largest uncertainty is that CS-1 is an iodate analogue, not a tellurate, so exact transferability is inferred rather than demonstrated.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Hydroxide flux growth operates at 700 °C for Ba3NaSbO6-family single crystals (SS-5; TS-5), so no high-temperature deduction applies. The route is multi-step and uses caustic hydroxide fluxes plus specialized Ag/alumina or sealed silver vessels (SS-5; TS-5). No high pressure, H2/NH3/vacuum, or volatile sealed precursor condition is reported (SS-5; TS-5).

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux growth improves ion mobility and can crystallize structurally related Ba-rich A3A′MO6 oxides at 700 °C (SS-5; TS-5). However, Ba3NaSbO6 contains Na, and the target Ba3TeO6 lacks an alkali cation site, so alkali incorporation and competing flux-derived phases are realistic risks [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic score is therefore high but slightly below Route 2 for target phase selectivity.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has large Ba/Te cation size contrast and distinct BaO6/BaO9 and TeO6 environments, triggering the radius-mismatch check (target; [Reasonable Inference]). The target description does not state a >40-atom unit cell, and it reports corner-sharing octahedra rather than coexistence of vertex/edge/face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered from the provided structural data (target). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the exact target unit-cell atom count, so that check is treated conservatively.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba3TeO6 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The synthesis outlook is strongly supported by direct Ba–Te oxide chemistry from BaCO3/TeO2 in O2 (CS-2) and by extremely close Ba2SrTeO6/Ba2CaTeO6 perovskite-derived analogues that are experimentally synthesized by standard ceramic routes (CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The most important enabling factor is the robust TeO6 octahedral framework chemistry, with Te–O distances in the target matching Ba2SrTeO6/Ba2CaTeO6 very closely at ~1.96–1.97 Å (target; CS-5; SS-1; SS-2; TS-1; TS-2). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: Ba–Te–O contains other stable Ba-rich tellurates such as Ba3Te2O9, and Te oxidation state control is required when starting from TeO2 (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, the target should be synthetically accessible, with the wet hydroxide/precipitation route giving the best balance of homogeneity, moderate temperature, and oxidation control [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and CS-2].
