### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Li₂GePt is predicted exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so it receives a near-maximum thermodynamic score. Stable analogues include Li₂PdSb, Li₂PtGa, Li₂PtIn, Li₂PtAl, and LiMgPtSn with E_hull = 0 in the provided data (CS3, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The main uncertainty is that computed ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of off-stoichiometric competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses the same general sealed Ar ampoule/crucible solid-state conditions as Li₂PdSb and Li–Pt–X analogues (CS3, CS4, SS1, TS1). The maximum proposed temperature is below 1000 °C, and no high pressure or reactive gas is required (CS3, CS4, SS1). Deductions arise from Li volatility/air sensitivity and the need for sealed containment [Reasonable Inference based on sealed Li syntheses in CS3/CS4].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The closest structural precedent, Li₂PdSb, has the same F-43m Li₂M X Zintl-derived pattern as the target, which supports a favorable structural reconstruction score (CS3). Kinetics are limited because Pt is refractory and elemental Pt powders can react slowly [Reasonable Inference], although Li–Pt–X analogues form at 650–900 °C (CS4, SS1, TS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ge/Pt ordering competes with binary Pt–Ge or Li–Ge phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is directly inspired by Nb-tube synthesis of Li-rich intermetallics using 900 °C heating, slow cooling, and long annealing (CS2). It avoids high pressure and extreme temperature, but it requires arc-welding and multiple processing steps (CS2). The severity is therefore lower than Route 1 because the containment and thermal schedule are more elaborate (CS2).

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Long annealing and slow cooling should improve atomic ordering and homogeneity [Reasonable Inference], and such schedules are used successfully for Li₃HoSb₂ (CS2). Structural analogues in the Li–Pt–X family show that the cubic F-43m framework is accessible by bulk synthesis (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The uncertainty is that prolonged annealing can also allow competing phases to equilibrate if the target has a narrow stability field [Reasonable Inference; phase-purity challenges are noted in CS2/SS3].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 remains below 1000 °C and avoids high pressure, consistent with the 650–900 °C analogue synthesis window (CS4, SS1, TS1). However, it is penalized for Li excess, sealed flux containment, and multi-step flux separation [Reasonable Inference]. Flux removal is a known complication in related Li intermetallic work, as CS2 explicitly mentions Pb flux and elaborate removal.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A Li-rich or Li–Ge-rich melt should enhance mass transport and reduce the diffusion limitation from Pt [Reasonable Inference]. This is why Route 3 receives the best F3 score, despite being less directly evidenced than Route 1. The major uncertainty is phase selectivity: flux chemistry could promote Li–Ge binaries or Pt–Ge phases unless cooling rate and stoichiometry are carefully optimized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion and is also seen in the Li₂PdSb-like structural description (CS3). A large size/chemistry contrast among Li, Ge, and Pt triggers another criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The small ordered intermetallic cell implied by F-43m analogues avoids the >40-atom-cell penalty (CS3, SS1, SS3), so the amorphization risk is medium rather than high.

Li₂GePt is likely a ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and many close Li–Pt–X or Li₂M X analogues are also on the hull (CS3, CS4, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). Overall, synthesis appears favorable but will likely require careful sealed-tube handling and possibly Li-excess or flux-assisted transport.
