### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold in the provided rubric (Target). Similar synthesized Co-rich materials have E_hull values in the 0.053–0.073 eV/atom range, including Pr₂Co₁₅Ga₂, Nd₂Co₁₅Ga₂, Sm₂Co₁₅Ga₂, and Ce₂Co₁₅Ga₂ (TS1–TS4). Chemically similar nitrides such as Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃/Nd₂Fe₁₇N₃ have E_hull around 0.062–0.063 eV/atom and reported synthesis routes (CS2; CS5), while Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ is synthesized despite E_hull = 0.136 eV/atom (CS1). The remaining uncertainty is that Co₆IrN is a different ternary nitride and may be stabilized only metastably [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits demanding high-temperature alloying from arc-melted Co-rich intermetallic analogues (CS4; TS1–TS5) and multistep nitrogenation from Sm₂Co₁₇N₃/Dy₂Co₁₇ analogues (CS1; SS1; SS5). The flowing-N₂ nitrogenation itself is moderate in temperature, 450–550 °C, but the parent-alloy preparation and powdering steps increase severity (CS1; SS5). The main uncertainty is that CS/SS analogues use rare-earth Co frameworks, not Ir-containing cubic Co₆IrN (Target; CS1; SS1).

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is low because dense Co–Ir alloy particles are expected to be less reactive than molecular or nanoscale precursors [Reasonable Inference], and analogous Co-rich nitrides report slow nitrogen diffusion (CS1; SS5). Local Co–N bonding is favorable because Co–N = 1.87 Å in the target matches Co–N = 1.87 Å in Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ (Target; CS1; SS1). The uncertainty is whether N can occupy the correct octahedral site in the cubic F-43m framework without forming binary cobalt nitrides or α-Co [Reasonable Inference; CS1; SS5].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses specialized but not extreme 15 MPa N₂ autoclave conditions, as reported for Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ and Nd₂Fe₁₇N₃ (CS2; CS5). The pressure does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty, but prior alloy synthesis, powdering, and high-pressure gas handling make the route multistep and controlled-atmosphere intensive (CS2; CS5). The main uncertainty is whether 15 MPa N₂ is sufficient for Ir–Co nitride formation rather than only interstitial nitrogen uptake [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

High-pressure N₂ improves nitrogen uptake and helps minimize disproportionation in R₂Fe₁₇N₃ analogues (CS2; CS5), so Route 2 receives a better kinetic score than ambient flowing N₂. Cation diffusion remains difficult because the Co–Ir alloy must first be homogenized, and Ir is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. Structural uncertainty remains high because the high-pressure analogues are trigonal R-3m rare-earth Fe nitrides, not cubic F-43m Co₆IrN (Target; CS2; CS5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for reactive NH₃/activated-N₂ atmosphere and toxic gas handling [Reasonable Inference]. The need for prior alloying and powdering follows the same parent-alloy logic as R₂Co₁₇N₃ nitrogenation (CS1; SS1; SS5). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for NH₃ use in these analogues, so this route is plausible but less evidence-backed.

#### F3 — Route 3

NH₃ or activated N₂ should raise nitrogen chemical potential relative to molecular N₂ [Reasonable Inference], but the analogues warn that even flowing N₂ routes suffer from slow diffusion and competing α-Co precipitation (CS1; SS5). The local Co–N geometry remains favorable based on the target and Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ bond lengths (Target; CS1; SS1). The uncertainty is over-nitridation or decomposition under more reactive nitrogen atmospheres [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 avoids bulk melting and long annealing if the film can crystallize directly or after a low-temperature anneal [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for controlled reactive N₂ plasma, but it avoids the high-temperature and high-pressure penalties of bulk routes [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sputtered Co₆IrN, so the high F2 reflects physical accessibility of the route rather than literature confirmation.

#### F3 — Route 4

Thin-film synthesis can bypass bulk diffusion limitations by co-depositing Co, Ir, and N at near-atomic mixing length scales [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s small cubic F-43m framework and E_hull = 0.05 eV/atom are favorable for metastable trapping [Reasonable Inference from Target], and TS5 shows a Co-rich cubic F-43m framework is experimentally accessible. The principal uncertainty is composition control, especially the 6:1 Co:Ir ratio and N occupancy, because CS/SS/TS provide no direct sputtering window.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target description indicates a compact cubic F-43m structure with IrCo₁₂ cuboctahedra and NCo₆ octahedra, not a large complex framework (Target). The connectivity includes corner and face sharing, but the target description does not report simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not fully met (Target). Radius mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% for Co and Ir metallic radii [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not directly provide this metric.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Co₆IrN is likely a low-energy metastable or near-threshold phase rather than an unambiguous ground state, because its E_hull is exactly 0.0500 eV/atom (Target). The CS/SS evidence strongly supports transition-metal-rich nitride formation by post-alloy nitrogenation, especially through Co–N environments with bond lengths matching the target (CS1; SS1; Target). The TS evidence supports that Co-rich intermetallic frameworks near 0.05–0.07 eV/atom can be experimentally accessed by high-temperature metallurgical routes (TS1–TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the favorable local Co–N octahedral bonding, since target Co–N = 1.87 Å matches Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ Co–N = 1.87 Å (Target; CS1; SS1). The most critical limiting factor is nitrogen incorporation and phase purity, because analogous Co-rich nitrides report slow N diffusion, incomplete nitrogenation, and α-Co precipitation (CS1; SS5).
