### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic stability bin. Thermodynamic analogues IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃ are also stable or very close to stable, with IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃ reported at E_hull = 0 in TS-1 and TS-2. CS analogues such as AuPS₄, ThP₂S₆, and Pb₂GeS₄ are also reported at E_hull = 0, showing that ternary sulfide/phosphosulfide chemistry can be thermodynamically stable, although CS is less specific to Ir–P–S than TS. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee a wide finite-temperature phase field.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct Elemental Solid-State Reaction

Direct ampoule synthesis is strongly supported for AuPS₄, ThP₂S₆, IrS₂, and Ir₂S₃ analogues (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1/TS-1, SS-3/TS-2). The severity score is reduced because vacuum sealing and volatile P/S containment are necessary, and IrS₂ required long high-temperature annealing plus possible regrinding/refiring (SS-1/TS-1). Kinetics are the main weakness because Ir is refractory [Reasonable Inference], and Ir sulfide analogues required 900–960 °C and long holds (SS-1/TS-1). The route is plausible but may yield incomplete reaction or IrS₂/P–S side phases without staged prereaction [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Staged Sulfidation / Phosphosulfidation

This route combines low-temperature prereaction practice from CS-5 with high-temperature Ir sulfide annealing from SS-1/TS-1 and SS-3/TS-2. Its severity remains moderate because it still requires an evacuated sealed ampoule and multiple processing steps, but it avoids high pressure and extreme temperatures. Kinetically, staged formation of Ir–S and P–S intermediates should improve reaction uniformity [Reasonable Inference], while repeated grinding/refiring is supported as useful for homogeneity in IrS₂ and related sulfides (SS-1/TS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The largest uncertainty is whether the PIr₃S tetrahedral motif forms before competing binary Ir sulfides or P–S phases.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux / Self-Flux Growth

Flux growth is supported by RuS₂ single-crystal growth in Te flux using evacuated quartz tubes, high temperature, and slow cooling (SS-4/TS-3). Severity is higher than direct solid-state synthesis because flux handling, possible Te toxicity, and flux removal add complexity (SS-4/TS-3). Kinetically, a flux can enhance mass transport and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference], but phase control may be difficult because RuS₂ flux growth showed sensitivity to sulfur content and possible RuTe₂ competition (SS-4/TS-3). The uncertainty is whether Ir–P–S dissolves and crystallizes congruently without Te incorporation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Halide-Assisted CVT

CVT/mineralizer growth is supported by Rh₃S₄ synthesis using Rh, RhCl₃, and S in an evacuated temperature-gradient ampoule (SS-5/TS-4). Severity is high because the route requires halide chemistry, a temperature gradient, sealed ampoule operation, and temperatures near or above 1000 °C (SS-5/TS-4). Kinetically, vapor transport can bypass slow Ir diffusion [Reasonable Inference], but Rh₃S₄ suffered from narrow phase stability and unavoidable neighboring phases (SS-5/TS-4). For PIr₃S₇, the additional volatility of P makes composition control even more uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: High-Pressure High-Temperature

High-pressure synthesis is directly supported for pyrite-like IrS₂ at 60 kbar and 1400–1500 °C (SS-2). The condition severity is poor because high pressure and high temperature require specialized apparatus (SS-2). Kinetics improve through high temperature and dense-phase stabilization [Reasonable Inference], but the apparatus scale is small and volatile P/S containment is nontrivial [Reasonable Inference]. This route is best viewed as a fallback if ambient-pressure routes fail or if the target proves to be a dense high-pressure polymorph.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has chemically very different cation centers, Ir in octahedral IrPS₅ coordination and P in tetrahedral PIr₃S coordination, which triggers a cation mismatch risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, the target description reports mainly corner-sharing octahedra/tetrahedra and does not report simultaneous edge and face sharing, so the connectivity check is not triggered. The formula is compact, and exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a >40-atom cell, so that check is treated as nontriggering/neutral. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

PIr₃S₇ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several Ir–S thermodynamic analogues are also stable (TS-1, TS-2). The strongest support comes from SS/TS: IrS₂ and Ir₂S₃ share Ir–S octahedral frameworks with bond lengths and S-based tetrahedral motifs similar to the target (SS-1/TS-1, SS-3/TS-2). CS supports the feasibility of sealed-ampoule thiophosphate and sulfide chemistry but is less directly predictive for Ir–P–S because the closest CS examples involve Au, Th, Pb, Ge, or Bi rather than Ir (CS-1 to CS-5). The key enabling factor is the strong structural/thermodynamic analogy to stable Ir sulfides combined with target hull stability. The key limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: refractory Ir reacts slowly, while P and S are volatile and may favor binary IrS₂ or P–S side products [Reasonable Inference] despite sealed-ampoule precedent.
