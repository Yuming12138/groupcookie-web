### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is reported at E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted as a ground-state phase rather than merely metastable. Chemically similar ternary nitrides and nitride-derived phases such as SrTiN₂, MnMoN₂, and SrZrN₂ are also on or very near the hull (CS #1, CS #4, CS #5). Structurally/thermodynamically similar ZrN and HfN are stable nitrides with E_hull = 0 (SS #1–#2; TS #1–#2). The main uncertainty is not thermodynamic stability but whether the ordered Sc/Nb H-phase can be reached kinetically.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Ammonolysis

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because flowing NH₃ is reactive/toxic, but the temperature is below 1000 °C and no high pressure is required; this follows the MnMoN₂/FeWN₂ ammonolysis precedent (CS #4). Kinetics are only moderate because converting ScNbO₄ to ScNbN₂ requires oxide-to-nitride reconstruction, and CS #4 notes narrow temperature windows and phase-purity difficulty. The route is nevertheless attractive because it directly produced a P6₃/mmc Caswellsilverite-like nitride analogue, MnMoN₂ (CS #4). Remaining uncertainty is whether Sc–O bonds in ScNbO₄ are too stable to fully ammonolyze without forming ScN/NbN mixtures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct nitride sintering

Route 2 has a relatively good F2 score because it can use Ar/N₂ and avoids NH₃, but it likely requires >1000 °C and long multistep annealing, as seen for SrTiN₂/SrZrN₂ at 1000–1030 °C for 5 days (CS #1, CS #5). Kinetics are weak because ScN and NbN are refractory, and analogous ZrN/HfN synthesis requires very high-temperature sintering or long homogenization (SS #1–#2; TS #1–#2). The local octahedral nitride motif helps, but long-range Sc/Nb ordering is the limiting step [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether dry nitride diffusion can produce a single ordered H-phase before binary nitrides persist.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Li₃N flux

Route 3 is conditionally more severe because Li₃N is air-sensitive and sealed ampoule processing is required; these handling requirements are consistent with Li₃N flux syntheses in ternary nitrides (TS #4, TS #5). Kinetically, it is promising because flux growth of Ce₂CrN₃ and La₃Nb₂N₆ occurs at 900–1030 °C and can overcome refractory nitride diffusion limitations (TS #4, TS #5). The route may improve crystallinity and cation ordering, but it could introduce Li-containing side phases [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS for Li₃N flux specifically for ScNbN₂, so that part remains uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film route

Route 4 has significant condition penalties because CVD uses reactive NH₃/H₂ and moisture-sensitive halides, as in Nb₄N₅ CVD from NbCl₅/NH₃/H₂ (SS #3). Its kinetic score is relatively good for film formation because vapor/plasma routes bypass bulk diffusion, and Nb nitrides can crystallize at 950–1000 °C (SS #3). However, no CS/SS/TS evidence demonstrates ScNbN₂ H-phase film growth, so crystallization and cation ordering are speculative [Reasonable Inference]. The route is best considered exploratory for epitaxial or metastable thin films rather than first-choice bulk synthesis.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, which triggers one amorphization risk; similar mixed connectivity appears in MnMoN₂ and related nitride structures (CS #4). The cation size mismatch is not expected to exceed 15% for plausible octahedral Sc/Nb states [Reasonable Inference], and the H-phase/P6₃/mmc cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms. Thus the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high. Remaining uncertainty is whether local Sc/Nb disorder could freeze in during rapid ammonolysis or thin-film deposition.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScNbN₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the reported energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is fairly strong because a very close P6₃/mmc Caswellsilverite-like ternary nitride, MnMoN₂, is experimentally accessible by ammonolysis of an oxide precursor (CS #4), and related ternary nitrides are accessible by solid-state nitride reactions or flux routes (CS #1, CS #5; TS #4, TS #5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable octahedral nitride frameworks with mixed cations, supported by MnMoN₂/SrZrN₂ analogues (CS #4, CS #5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic: Sc–Nb ordering and full oxide-to-nitride conversion may be slow, and Nb–N chemistry has multiple competing nitride phases (SS #3). Overall, the target is plausible but will likely require careful control of nitridation temperature, precursor homogeneity, and phase-purity screening.
