### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable thermodynamic category. Many close analogues are also hull-stable or near-hull, including PdCdF₆ and PdZnF₆ in CS-2/CS-4, PtNiF₆ and CoPtF₆ in SS-2/SS-3 and TS-1/TS-2, and PdF₃ near hull in SS-1/TS-5. The main uncertainty is that DFT ground-state stability does not ensure kinetic accessibility or resistance to over-fluorination, which is a [Reasonable Inference] from the reactive F₂/HF conditions in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 350–425 °C, supported by CS-1 and SS-1/TS-5, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. However, dry F₂/Ar or F₂/N₂ is required, and CS-1 shows specialized Monel/Ni/PFA equipment and cold trapping for volatile oxyfluoride products. The route is therefore penalized mainly for toxic/reactive atmosphere, multi-step fluorination, and possible volatility.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Gas-phase fluorination is kinetically powerful because CS-1 converts oxide to oxyfluoride at 400 °C and SS-1/TS-5 prepare RhF₃/PdF₃ under dry F₂ near 400 °C. The target’s corner-sharing RhOF₅ octahedra are close to the OsOF₅/RuOF₅ motif in CS-1, supporting a moderate-to-good structural reconstruction score. Remaining uncertainty comes from controlling partial replacement of O by F rather than complete fluorination to RhF₃/RhF₄/RhF₅-type phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is supported by SS-2/SS-3 and TS-1/TS-2, which use 350–400 atm F₂ at 390–450 °C with pre-fluorination and sometimes sealed Au-tube annealing. Although this pressure is far below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, it is still experimentally demanding because F₂ is toxic, corrosive, and oxidizing. The multi-step protocol and noble-metal sealed containers justify a challenging but not impossible severity score.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The high-pressure F₂ route has strong kinetic support for forming Upper-Bainite-derived octahedral fluorides, as seen for PtNiF₆, CoPtF₆, and CdPtF₆ in SS-2/SS-4 and TS-1/TS-3. Pre-fluorination improves precursor activation, and multi-day F₂ exposure should enhance fluorination completeness. The main kinetic risk is that the target is an oxyfluoride, whereas the strongest structural analogues are pure fluorides, so O/F ordering must be retained [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids extreme temperature and >5 GPa pressure, using 300–450 °C conditions supported by CS-5 and SS-5/TS-4. The severity remains substantial because HF is volatile, toxic, and corrosive, and both CS-5 and SS-5/TS-4 require sealed autoclave-compatible materials. Compared with F₂ pressure autoclaves, the condition severity is slightly lower, but still challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

HF solvothermal/hydrothermal chemistry offers better mass transport than dry solid-state routes, as indicated by single-crystal or crystalline fluoride/oxyfluoride formation in CS-5 and SS-5/TS-4. The 300–450 °C temperature range is compatible with fluoride framework crystallization while limiting thermal decomposition risk. The uncertainty is that no exact Rh₂OF₅ analogue is reported, and an additional oxidizing component may be needed to stabilize average Rh +3.5 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation species, Rh, so cation size mismatch is not a concern. The target description reports corner-sharing RhOF₅ octahedra, with no mixed edge/face-sharing connectivity, which lowers amorphization risk. Exact unit-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS, so that sub-check has insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and is treated neutrally for source confidence, but the formula and simple C2 description suggest low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rh₂OF₅ is likely a true thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stable phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The synthesis evidence is good but indirect: CS-1 strongly supports oxyfluoride RhOF₅-like octahedral chemistry by analogy to Os/Ru oxyfluorides, while SS/TS strongly support Upper-Bainite-derived corner-sharing fluoride frameworks. The most enabling factor is the availability of high-fluorine-activity routes—F₂ gas, high-pressure F₂, and HF solvothermal media—that successfully form related noble/transition-metal fluorides and oxyfluorides. The most limiting factor is precise O/F and Rh oxidation-state control, because over-fluorination could drive Rh fluoride phases while under-fluorination could leave Rh oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires specialized fluorine/HF infrastructure and careful phase-control optimization.
