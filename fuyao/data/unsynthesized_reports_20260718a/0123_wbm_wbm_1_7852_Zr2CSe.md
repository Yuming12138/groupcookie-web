### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase by the provided thermodynamic data. This is reinforced by structurally and thermodynamically similar H-phase Zr2PbC and Zr2TlC, which also have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). CS evidence gives stable carbide/MAX-like analogues but no direct Zr–C–Se experimental analogue, so the chemical-specific evidence remains incomplete (CS1–CS5). The main uncertainty is whether DFT ground-state stability survives finite-temperature Se volatility and competing binary Zr–Se/Zr–C phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Hot-pressing/sealed-tube synthesis is demanding because Zr2PbC/Zr2TlC analogues require high temperature, pressure, inert handling, and long annealing (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). Se introduces an additional volatile/toxic component requiring sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for hot-pressing Zr–C–Se specifically, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity assessment.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory Zr/ZrC diffusion, consistent with long homogenization times in Zr/Hf–A–C analogues (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). The H-phase structural match is favorable because the target, Zr2PbC, and Zr2TlC all share P6₃/mmc-type layered carbide topology (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). The uncertainty is whether Se reacts too readily with Zr to form stable binary selenides before H-phase ordering occurs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The low-temperature sealed-ampoule route avoids >1000 °C operation, which reduces condition severity relative to Route 1 [Reasonable Inference from CS5]. However, sealed inert/vacuum conditions and Se volatility remain unavoidable [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 supports sealed-ampoule H-phase synthesis, while SS/TS provide only indirect support through sealed-tube Zr/Hf–A–C processing (CS5, SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are likely slow because ZrC and Zr are refractory, and Ti2InC required 350 h even with a low-melting In component (CS5). Structural similarity to H-phase Ti2InC is favorable, but the target contains Se rather than In, which changes chemical reactivity and vapor pressure [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues warn that Zr/Hf–A–C phases may require long homogenization, supporting a conservative kinetics score (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Flux synthesis operates at moderate-to-high temperatures and requires sealed inert conditions, similar to Zn-flux Zr5CSb3 at 900–1000 °C (SS2, TS3). Se and possibly Zn are volatile, so sealed ampoules and careful loading are required [Reasonable Inference]. Compared with hot pressing, equipment severity is lower, but chemical containment severity is significant.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux routes improve mass transport, supported by quantitative Zn-flux syntheses of Zr5CSb3 and molten-metal MAX-like carbide growth (SS2, TS3, SS4, TS4). This is advantageous for refractory Zr/C chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is nucleation of the exact H-phase Zr2CSe rather than binary Zr–Se or residual ZrC phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
Topochemical selenization can plausibly use lower temperatures than bulk solid-state synthesis, following Nb2S2C topochemical chemistry in which Cu was extracted without oxidizing the carbide/chalcogenide lattice (CS2, CS3). Nevertheless, it requires dry inert/vacuum handling, Se containment, and a preformed layered precursor [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for topochemical Zr–C–Se synthesis, so their route-specific signal is neutral.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
Topochemical pathways are kinetically attractive because the framework is partly retained, as shown for Nb2S2C formation from Cu-containing Nb–S–C precursors (CS2, CS3). This may reduce long-range Zr/C diffusion relative to direct reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is high because no provided source demonstrates Zr2CSe or an H-phase Zr–C–Se compound via topochemistry.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple H-phase formula and only one metal cation, so cation radius mismatch and very large unit-cell complexity are not indicated [Reasonable Inference from target description and H-phase analogues SS1/SS3/CS5]. However, the target description explicitly reports mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing octahedra, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion. This gives a low-to-medium amorphization risk rather than a high one.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr2CSe is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several close Zr2AC H-phase analogues are also stable on the hull (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). The structural evidence is strong: Zr2PbC and Zr2TlC share the same H-phase P6₃/mmc layered carbide motif and very similar Zr–C bond lengths to the target (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS2). The most important enabling factor is the structural compatibility of Zr–C octahedral slabs with layered A-site chemistry, supported by H-phase Ti2InC and Zr2PbC/Zr2TlC analogues (CS5, SS1, SS3). The most important limiting factor is Se volatility and chalcogen chemical potential control, because excessive Se may favor binary zirconium selenides while insufficient Se may leave ZrC or Zr-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require sealed inert processing, flux/topochemical assistance, or long annealing to overcome diffusion and phase-selection barriers.
