### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is 0.0500 eV/atom, which falls at the favorable cutoff for the 90–100 range in the rubric. Closely related SrNiF6 and BaNiF6 are predicted/reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2), and the Cu fluoride analogue CsCuF4 has E_hull ≈ 0.048 eV/atom and is experimentally accessible under high-pressure F2 (TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is that SrCuF6 may be metastable with respect to lower-valent Cu fluorides unless high fluorine chemical potential is maintained [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
High-pressure oxidative fluorination is strongly supported by SrNiF6 and CsCuF4, but the conditions are severe because they require F2 pressure, Monel equipment, and strict exclusion of air/moisture (CS-1, TS-3). The kinetic score is high because analogous phases form at 280–400 °C, suggesting fluorination chemistry can overcome slow solid-state diffusion (CS-1, TS-3). The main uncertainty is whether Cu can be stabilized in the exact SrCuF6 cubic framework rather than forming other Cu fluoride phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Photochemical HF/F2 synthesis has low thermal severity but high chemical hazard because ACrF6 analogues require anhydrous HF, F2, UV irradiation, and specialized FEP/PTFE equipment (SS-3, SS-4). Kinetics are favorable because the reaction occurs at ambient temperature in a highly reactive fluorinating medium (SS-3, SS-4). Transferability is uncertain because the demonstrated systems are alkali Cr fluorides rather than divalent Sr/Cu fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Direct fluorination benefits from relatively mild temperatures, with CaPdF6 at ~100 °C and K2NiF6 at 275 °C (CS-3, SS-5). However, F2 handling remains severe, and K2NiF6 reports contamination/stoichiometry issues, indicating phase-control risk (SS-5). The route may be kinetically accessible but may not provide enough fluorine chemical potential for phase-pure SrCuF6 without pressure [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and TS-3].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Sealed-tube fluoride synthesis is well established for Ba2Ni7F18 and CaFeF5 using component fluorides at 850–1000 °C for long durations (CS-4, CS-5). For SrCuF6, however, the need for high-valent Cu makes a simple sealed fluoride route less convincing, and insufficient evidence from TS supports this exact approach. Kinetic limitations are expected because the cited sealed-tube examples require long high-temperature treatments (CS-4, CS-5).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one clear amorphization risk: large cation-size/coordination mismatch between 12-coordinate Sr and 6-coordinate Cu [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target does not explicitly show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing; only corner and face sharing are described (target description). ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCuF6 is likely a low-energy metastable or near-ground-state phase rather than a deeply unstable hypothetical compound, because its E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom and related fluorides occur near the hull (CS-1, CS-2, TS-3). The analogues strongly support synthesis of A–M–F6 frameworks under aggressive fluorination, especially SrNiF6 and CsCuF4 (CS-1, SS-1, TS-3). The most important enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, which stabilizes high-valent transition-metal fluoride octahedra in the relevant analogues (CS-1, TS-3). The most important limiting factor is Cu oxidation/phase control, because Cu fluoride products such as CsCuF4 are highly reactive and decompose in air or moisture (TS-3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but should be attempted with pressure fluorination or carefully controlled direct fluorination rather than ordinary solid-state sintering.
