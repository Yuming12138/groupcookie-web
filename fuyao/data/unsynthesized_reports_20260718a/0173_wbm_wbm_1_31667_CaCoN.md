### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target CaCoN has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state thermodynamic stability. This is reinforced by CaNiN, Ca₃MnN₃, and Sr₃MnN₃ analogues all being reported on the hull in the provided data (CS-1, SS-1, SS-2, SS-4, TS-1, TS-2, TS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or phase selectivity [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate solid-state temperatures because the closest analogue CaNiN was annealed at 800 °C under N₂ for 20 h, and Ca₂ZnN₂ was made at 680 °C in N₂ (CS-1, CS-3). The main severity penalties are air-sensitive Ca₃N₂ handling and a likely multi-step procedure if Ca₃N₂ must be prepared or if regrinding is needed (CS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports any need for high pressure or NH₃/vacuum in this route, so those are scored as non-applicable/neutral.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The strongest kinetic advantage is structural continuity with CaNiN: same P4₂/mmc topology, very similar Ca–N and M–N distances, and same linear M–N coordination (CS-1, target). The limiting factor is diffusion through Co metal and nitride pellets, but analogue formation within 20 h at 800 °C suggests manageable kinetics (CS-1). The score remains below 80 because direct Ca₃N₂ + Co has not been explicitly shown for CaCoN in the evidence.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Li-assisted elemental nitridation is supported by Ca₃MnN₃ at 900 °C and Sr₃MnN₃ at 850 °C under Ar/N₂, so the temperature and pressure severity are moderate rather than extreme (SS-1, SS-4). The penalties arise from reactive Ca/Li handling and multi-step flux loading/removal [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS proves that Li volatility mandates a sealed system for CaCoN, so that item is treated as neutral.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Li flux likely enhances precursor activity and cation transport [Reasonable Inference], and analogous Ca/Sr transition-metal nitrides are successfully prepared at 850–900 °C (SS-1, SS-4). However, these analogues are not the exact CaCoN structure, so structural reconstruction and phase selectivity are less favorable than Route 1 (SS-1, SS-4). The route is plausible but may produce Ca-rich nitridocobaltates instead of 1:1:1 CaCoN [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Ammonolysis avoids very high temperatures because CdNCo₃ was made at 743 K under NH₃ (SS-3, TS-3). It receives significant penalties for NH₃ atmosphere, toxic/corrosive gas handling, and repeated processing, all directly reflected in the CdNCo₃ procedure with repeated NH₃ refilling, regrinding, pressing, and sintering (SS-3). Insufficient CS evidence supports ammonolysis for CaCoN specifically, so transferability remains uncertain.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
NH₃ provides reactive nitrogen and can form Co–N bonding, as shown for CdNCo₃ (SS-3, TS-3). The major kinetic drawback is that the CdNCo₃ analogue has a different cubic perovskite-type structure, so forming P4₂/mmc CaCoN likely requires complete reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Calcium oxide or hydride starting materials may also introduce stable side phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Azide-assisted sealed ampoule synthesis is more severe because the closest analogue Sr₈Mn₂FeN₈ used 1050 °C, 48 h, sealed Fe ampoules, glovebox handling, and ~20 bar pressure (SS-5, TS-5). Although the pressure is far below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty, sealed volatile/azide chemistry, air-sensitive precursors, and multi-step ampoule preparation add difficulty (SS-5, TS-5). This is a fallback route if ambient N₂ synthesis fails.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
NaN₃ can raise nitrogen chemical potential and helped synthesize a complex alkaline-earth transition-metal nitride in the analogue data (SS-5, TS-5). High temperature improves diffusion, but the 48 h hold and complex ampoule procedure suggest slow ordering and phase-control challenges (SS-5, TS-5). The structural match to CaCoN is weaker than CaNiN, so the kinetics score is moderate.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple ternary composition and only corner/edge-sharing octahedra, not the vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence that would strongly promote amorphization under the rubric (target; CS-1). The main trigger is the large Ca–Co cation size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference], although the existence of ordered CaNiN with very similar geometry mitigates this risk (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). Remaining uncertainty is that Co-specific electronic preferences could destabilize the ordered P4₂/mmc arrangement despite the favorable hull [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaCoN is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and several related Ca/Sr transition-metal nitrides are also hull-stable (target; CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The strongest support comes from CaNiN, which is experimentally synthesized, hull-stable, and essentially isostructural with the target, including the same P4₂/mmc symmetry and linear transition-metal nitride unit (CS-1, SS-2, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the near-perfect structural analogy to CaNiN, making a Ca₃N₂ + Co solid-state route highly plausible (CS-1, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity in the Ca–Co–N system, because Co may form competing cobalt nitrides, Ca-rich nitridocobaltates, or remain unreacted if diffusion is poor [Reasonable Inference; Co–N alternative structure supported by SS-3/TS-3]. Overall, the material appears quite synthesizable by conventional air-free nitride solid-state chemistry.
