### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which is the strongest thermodynamic indicator for synthesizability (target data). Thermodynamic analogues in TS are also on the hull, including Cs₃BS₃, CsLi₂BS₃, Cs₄In₂S₅, K₃GaS₃, and Cs₂LiNbS₄ (TS-1 to TS-5). CS further supports stable Cs–M–S phases, with CsFeS₂ and Cs₂Zn₃S₄ at E_hull = 0 and Cs₂Ni₃S₄ near the hull or on the hull depending on polymorph (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is finite-temperature phase competition among Cs–Al–S compositions, for which CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild-to-moderate because analogous Cs–M–S materials form at 800 °C to 1000–1200 K under Ar or dry N₂, without high pressure (CS-1/2/3/5). The main severity penalty is sulfur volatility and possible need for sealed ampoules, supported by sealed-ampoule use in CsFeS₂ synthesis (CS-1) and by the use of sulfur in all chemically similar routes (CS-1/2/3/5). CS gives useful condition evidence; SS/TS are less directly useful for this carbonate route, so SS/TS are treated as neutral for this specific route condition score.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because carbonate/metal/sulfur mixtures have repeatedly produced Cs transition-metal sulfides in short holds of about 3–4 h (CS-2/3/5). The strongest structural-kinetic support is CsFeS₂, which has 8-coordinate Cs and edge-sharing FeS₄ tetrahedra analogous to the target’s 8-coordinate Cs and edge-sharing AlS₄ tetrahedra (CS-1; target description). The main uncertainty is the Al metal oxide shell and whether Al enters sulfide tetrahedra as readily as Fe/Co/Zn/Ni under carbonate fusion conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The elemental route uses evacuated graphitized ampoules similar to K₃GaS₃ synthesis at 1025 K (SS-4, TS-4). It is penalized for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, sulfur volatility, and elemental Cs reactivity; the need to avoid pure Cs metal is explicitly highlighted as a motivation for azide routes (SS-3, TS-3). CS provides limited direct support for elemental Cs use, so CS is neutral for this route’s precursor-handling severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

Direct elemental reaction should have good mass transport because Cs and S are highly reactive and mobile at the proposed temperatures [Reasonable Inference from SS-4/TS-4]. However, structural assembly is less certain because K₃GaS₃ is not isostructural with CsAlS₂ and has different stoichiometry (SS-4/TS-4; target description). Therefore the kinetics are good but not as directly supported as Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3

The azide route operates at only ~500 °C, which is milder thermally than carbonate fusion, but it requires vacuum/inert ampoules, azide decomposition control, long annealing, and reactive azide precursors (SS-3, TS-3). This route is penalized for being multistep and for handling CsN₃, which is specifically used to avoid pure Cs metal but introduces its own hazards (SS-3, TS-3). CS and SS/TS do not contradict here; SS-3 and TS-3 are the primary evidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

CsN₃ decomposition provides reactive Cs species and has been shown to enable alkali sulfide synthesis including Al-containing systems (SS-3, TS-3). Long 8–15 day anneals indicate slow crystallization or ordering may be required, lowering the structural reconstruction score (SS-3, TS-3). The target’s simpler CsAlS₂ stoichiometry may reduce complexity relative to Cs₄In₂S₅/Rb₄Al₂S₅-type systems, but that is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Sulfide/flux sealed-tube routes use moderate temperatures, typically 650–720 °C or 773 K, but require glovebox loading, sealed tubes, and often pre-synthesized moisture-sensitive alkali sulfides or A₂S₃ fluxes (SS-1/2/5, TS-1/2/5). Carbon-coated or fused-silica tubes are needed in analogues, which adds operational complexity (SS-1/2/5, TS-1/2/5). The route is therefore chemically attractive but experimentally more demanding than carbonate fusion.

#### F3 — Route 4

Preformed sulfides should lower the kinetic barrier to AlS₄ formation because sulfide anions and Cs–S environments are already present [Reasonable Inference from SS-1/2/5]. Flux growth evidence shows controlled transport and crystallization in related alkali sulfide frameworks at moderate temperatures (SS-5, TS-5). The uncertainty is phase selectivity: the closest SS/TS analogues are B, In, Nb, and Li-containing sulfides, not binary-cation CsAlS₂ (SS-1/2/3/5, TS-1/2/3/5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size contrast between Cs and Al, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from standard ionic-radius trends and target composition]. The target description gives a relatively simple connectivity, specifically edge-sharing AlS₄ tetrahedra, so mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence is not triggered (target description). The unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not directly determine it for the target, so that check is treated as not triggered with uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsAlS₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because the target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom (target data). The synthesis case is strongly supported by CS because CsFeS₂ is chemically similar and structurally analogous, with 8-coordinate Cs and edge-sharing MS₄ tetrahedra (CS-1). SS/TS also support the broader feasibility of alkali thiometallate synthesis in sealed tubes, fluxes, and azide routes, although they are less directly matched to the exact CsAlS₂ stoichiometry (SS-1 to SS-5, TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with the known stability of Cs–S frameworks containing tetrahedral metal sulfide units (target data; CS-1; TS-1 to TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is controlling volatile sulfur/cesium sulfide chemistry and preventing competing Cs–Al–S or Cs₂S/Al₂S₃ side phases during heating [Reasonable Inference from sealed/inert requirements in CS/SS/TS].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Sulfur volatility and possible pressure buildup or sulfur deficiency | High | Use sealed ampoules or slight S excess; sealed ampoules and chalcogen-rich conditions are supported by CS-1 and CS-4, and sealed-tube sulfide routes are supported by SS-1/2/5 and TS-1/2/5. |
| 2 | All | Moisture/oxygen sensitivity of Cs sulfide products or intermediates | Medium–High | Handle under dry Ar/N₂; analogous syntheses use dry oxygen-free N₂, Ar flow, gloveboxes, and sealed tubes (CS-1/2/3/5; SS-1/2/3/5). |
| 3 | Route 1 | Al metal surface oxide may slow sulfiding and cause Al₂O₃ impurities | Medium | Use fine Al powder/grit, pre-dry Cs₂CO₃, and consider repeated grinding/heating [Reasonable Inference]; simple mixing/heating is supported for metal powders in CS-1/2/3/5. |
| 4 | Route 1 | Competing Cs₂S, Al₂S₃, Cs polysulfides, or carbonate residues | Medium | Tune Cs₂CO₃:Al:S stoichiometry and use post-reaction washing only after confirming product stability; washing/drying practices are reported in CS-2/3/4. |
| 5 | Route 2 | Elemental Cs handling and ampoule attack | High | Prefer Route 1 or Route 3 if possible; if direct elements are used, load in Ar glovebox and use graphitized/carbon-coated ampoules as in SS-4/TS-4 and SS-1/2. |
| 6 | Route 3 | Azide decomposition and N₂ pressure management | High | Use small batches, staged heating through 270–350 °C, and vacuum/inert ampoule protocols as in SS-3/TS-3. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Long annealing may be needed for crystallinity and ordering | Medium | Follow 8–15 day annealing practice from azide analogues and include intermediate homogenization if needed (SS-3/TS-3). |
| 8 | Route 4 | Cs₂S / Al₂S₃ moisture sensitivity and prior synthesis burden | Medium–High | Prepare/load in glovebox and use sealed carbon-coated or fused-silica tubes; alkali sulfide sealed-tube handling is supported by SS-1/2 and TS-1/2. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Flux inclusion or incomplete flux removal | Medium | Use controlled slow cooling and wash flux residues with appropriate dry solvent only after product stability testing; flux washing is reported for related sulfides in SS-5/TS-5. |
| 10 | All | Crystal growth difficulty for structure verification | Medium | Use slow cooling from sulfide/carbonate melt or flux; slow-cooling programs are used in SS-1/2/5 and TS-1/2/5. |
