### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E\(_\mathrm{hull}\) = 0.0500 eV/atom, exactly within the highest scoring bin boundary, so it receives 90/100 rather than a higher value because it is borderline rather than clearly on-hull. Chemically similar Cs oxometallates in CS1–CS5 are all reported at E\(_\mathrm{hull}\) = 0.0 eV/atom, which supports thermodynamic plausibility in the Cs–metal–O chemical family. Thermodynamic analogues include experimentally synthesized compounds around 0.035–0.088 eV/atom (TS1–TS5), suggesting that a 0.05 eV/atom phase is experimentally accessible. Remaining uncertainty is that TS analogues are not chemically close to Cs–Ru–O, so they support metastability tolerance rather than specific phase competition.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because closest analogues use 650–740 °C sealed Ag/Pt/Fe containers under Ar (CS1–CS5; SS1, SS4–SS5). The main penalties are air-sensitive Cs oxides, sealed-system requirements, and multi-step handling, all directly reflected in CsO1.8/CsO0.57/Cs2O syntheses (CS1–CS4). TS gives insufficient route-specific severity evidence for this Cs–Ru–O solid-state route and is treated as neutral. The score is moderate because equipment is specialized but not extreme.

#### F3 — Route 1

The CsO\(_x\) precursor is reactive, as shown by Cs4IrO4 and Cs3BiO3 formation from oxidized cesium oxides (CS1, CS5), but RuO2 is expected to be a dense oxide requiring oxidation and reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing times in close analogues—86 d for Cs4IrO4 and 6 months for Cs6[Al2O6]—indicate slow ordering kinetics (CS1, CS3). Structural reconstruction is substantial because the target has octahedral RuO6 while the closest Ir analogue has square-planar IrO4 (CS1, SS1). Thus kinetics are the limiting feature for Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from low temperatures, since SS3 reports Cs-rich A9MO4 formation at 180–300 °C and SS2 reports Cs3O formation at 170 °C. However, it still requires sealed Ta/steel or capillary equipment and strict Ar handling (SS2, SS3). Air-sensitive Cs metal/oxide precursors trigger a severity penalty (SS2, SS3). CS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this exact Cs–Ru–O flux route, so their route-specific severity signal is neutral except for general sealed-container support from CS4 and TS2/TS4.

#### F3 — Route 2

Alkali-metal/alkali-oxide fluxes are kinetically favorable because SS3 forms Cs-rich oxides at only 300 °C. Diffusion should be enhanced by the reactive Cs-rich medium [Reasonable Inference], and SS2/SS3 show short Cs–O environments similar to the target. The main kinetic uncertainty is not diffusion but redox: a Cs-metal-rich flux may be too reducing for a charge-balanced Ru(VI) phase [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore the raw kinetic score is good, but it is penalized by expert adjustment.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 operates at moderate temperatures comparable to CS1 and CS5, but controlled oxygen activity and peroxide/superoxide handling make conditions more severe [Reasonable Inference]. Oxidized CsO1.8 and Cs1.99O were used in sealed Ag containers for Cs4IrO4 and Cs3BiO3 (CS1, CS5), supporting the need for sealed oxidizing cesium oxide chemistry. The possible autogenous O2 pressure is not GPa-level, so no high-pressure penalty is applied [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score is lower than Route 1 because oxygen-pressure and oxidizer safety add risk.

#### F3 — Route 3

This route has better redox compatibility because target Cs6RuO6 implies high-valent Ru if Cs is +1 and O is −2 [Reasonable Inference]. Oxidized Cs oxide reagents successfully access high-valent Cs oxometallates such as Cs4IrO4 and Cs3BiO3 (CS1, CS5). Kinetics remain uncertain because RuO2 must reconstruct into isolated RuO6 octahedra and no provided analogue has the same cubic Fm-3m RuO6 topology; SS gives insufficient direct evidence for exact structural replication. Overall, Route 3 is chemically plausible but experimentally delicate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very dissimilar cations, Cs and Ru, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The Fm-3m conventional unit cell likely exceeds 40 atoms if multiple Cs6RuO6 formula units are present, but the provided data do not state Z, so this trigger is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not specify mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral networks, so C3 is not triggered from available evidence. CS/SS analogues often require slow cooling and long anneals for crystallinity (CS1–CS4), consistent with medium crystallization difficulty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs6RuO6 is likely a low-energy metastable or borderline stable phase rather than an obviously unstable compound, because its E\(_\mathrm{hull}\) is 0.0500 eV/atom and TS analogues show experimental accessibility in the 0.035–0.088 eV/atom range (TS1–TS5). The CS/SS evidence strongly supports the broader feasibility of Cs-rich oxometallates made in sealed inert capsules, especially from reactive cesium oxides (CS1–CS5; SS1–SS5). The most critical enabling factor is a reactive Cs oxide or Cs oxide flux that supplies both Cs and mobile oxide species, as demonstrated in Cs4IrO4, Cs6[In2O6], Cs6[Al2O6], and Cs3BiO3 (CS1–CS3, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is redox/phase control: charge balance suggests Ru must be highly oxidized [Reasonable Inference], while too reducing a Cs-metal flux could stabilize lower-valent Ru phases. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require sealed-capsule optimization of Cs excess, oxygen activity, and long annealing.
