### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is calculated exactly on the convex hull, E_hull = 0.000 eV/atom, which strongly supports ground-state accessibility. Close structural and thermodynamic analogues Sc3GaC, Sc3InC, Tb3InN, Sc3TlC, and Lu3InN are also hull-stable in SS/TS, and chemically similar Ca4In2N and Ca3GeN are hull-stable in CS (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5; CS4–CS5). Remaining uncertainty is experimental: a 0 K hull result does not guarantee easy kinetic formation or phase purity.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is condition-severe because analogues require arc melting, inert/vacuum handling, sealed ampoules, and long anneals (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). It avoids high pressure and can likely remain below 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], but Sc/ScN handling and Ga retention impose penalties. The main uncertainty is whether Sc3GaN equilibrates at 1000–1250 °C or requires the higher Sc-related temperatures noted for R3InN analogues (SS3; TS3).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetically, this is the best-supported route because the Sc–Ga framework has a direct Pm-3m carbon analogue, Sc3GaC, and rare-earth nitride analogues use RN + R2In-type precursors (SS1, SS3, SS5; TS1, TS3, TS5). Arc melting improves cation mixing, but ScN is refractory and long anneals are common in the evidence base (CS1; SS1–SS2). The uncertainty is nitrogen ordering and whether residual ScN persists.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 inherits the high-temperature, sealed-tube, controlled-atmosphere burden seen in Ti3AlN and Ca3GeN nitride solid-state routes (CS1, CS5). It is not high-pressure and uses simple stoichiometry, but the process requires air-free mixing, pelletizing, sealing, prolonged annealing, and likely regrinding. The sealed system is also important for Ga retention [Reasonable Inference], supported indirectly by CS5.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Solid-state diffusion from ScN + Sc + Ga is slower than arc-melted alloy routes because refractory nitrides and metallic Sc require long-range diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 reports very long sintering times for Ti–Al–N equilibrium, and SS/TS analogues report difficulty reaching single-phase materials. The structural target is well supported, but kinetic access is less favorable than Route 1.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Reactive nitridation under N₂ has a milder formal atmosphere score than vacuum/NH₃ routes, and Ca–In nitrides form under N₂ at 1420 K (CS4). However, this route remains high-temperature and may require prealloying, sealed containment, and air-free Sc handling [Reasonable Inference]. If NH₃ is needed, the severity would increase due to reactive/toxic gas handling.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The route has weaker kinetic evidence because CS supports nitridation in Ca/Sr–In systems, but SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Sc–Ga alloy nitridation. Nitrogen diffusion into a Sc–Ga intermetallic may be sluggish or may form binary ScN before the ordered ternary phase [Reasonable Inference]. Its advantage is potentially avoiding refractory ScN powder as a starting material.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple cubic Pm-3m 5-atom cell, and analogous phases share the same uncomplicated corner/face-sharing topology without large complex cells (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The main amorphization risk is cation size/environment mismatch between Sc framework and Ga cuboctahedral site [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high, because the structure is simple and many crystalline analogues exist.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc3GaN is most likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.000 eV/atom and several close Pm-3m nitride/carbide analogues are also hull-stable (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). The analogue support is strong structurally: Sc3GaC is nearly identical except C→N, and R3InN phases demonstrate that nitrides of this antiperovskite family can be made by RN + intermetallic routes (SS1, SS3, SS5; TS1, TS3, TS5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a preorganized Sc–Ga alloy plus a nitride source, which should reduce cation diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference] and is supported by master-alloy/nitride strategies in CS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase purity: multiple analogues explicitly report difficulty reaching single-phase products, incomplete equilibrium, nitrogen nonstoichiometry, or need for repeated anneals (CS1; SS1–SS3; TS1–TS3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require careful sealed-ampoule annealing and iterative phase analysis.
