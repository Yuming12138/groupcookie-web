### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

BaPdO₃ has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability scoring bracket. This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues with E_hull = 0.0, including BaMnO₃ 9R (TS1), Ca₂Pt₃O₈ (TS2), Ba₂Hg₃Pd₇O₁₄ (TS3), and SrMnO₃ (TS5). The uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct oxygen chemical potential during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Multi-anvil high-pressure synthesis

Route 1 is strongly supported structurally and chemically by BaRhO₃ and BaRuO₃ analogues, both hexagonal perovskite-like Ba–transition-metal oxides with face-sharing octahedral motifs (CS1–CS3; SS5). Its F2 is low because BaRhO₃ required 1175 °C, 60–65 kbar, and quenching (CS1; SS5), and BaRuO₃ required high-pressure sintering up to 5 GPa (CS2). Its F3 is moderate-to-good because high pressure can directly stabilize the desired dense hexagonal framework, but the need for specialized equipment and Pd oxidation control remains a major uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High oxygen-pressure ceramic synthesis

Route 2 is the most chemically direct Pd-based route because Ba₂Hg₃Pd₇O₁₄ was synthesized from BaO/HgO/PdO under 3900 bar O₂ at 630 °C and contains Pd–O bonds close to those in the target (SS4; TS3). The formal F2 score is high under the given rubric because the pressure is far below 5 GPa and the temperature is below 1000 °C, although in practice high-O₂ equipment is still specialized [Reasonable Inference]. The F3 score is moderate because the 12-day reaction time in the analogue indicates slow kinetics, and the structural match is chemical rather than topological (SS4; TS3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux growth

Flux growth is supported by ambient-pressure BaRuO₃ formation in BaCl₂ flux (CS3), BaMnO₃-related growth in Bi₂O₃ flux (SS2; TS1), and RE RhO₃ perovskite growth in K₂CO₃ flux (CS4). Its F2 is moderate because it requires >1000 °C and multiple heating/cooling/leaching steps (CS3; SS2; TS1). Its F3 is moderate because flux transport helps overcome diffusion, but CS3 explicitly reports phase competition between 4H and 9R BaRuO₃, implying a narrow phase window.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Hydrothermal oxidative synthesis

Hydrothermal synthesis is supported structurally by hexagonal BaMnO₃ prepared at 275 °C and 50 atm (SS1) and chemically by Ca₂Pt₃O₈ hydrothermal conversion from a noble-metal hydroxide precursor at 800 K/170 MPa (SS3; TS2). Its F2 is favorable because temperatures and pressures are much lower than multi-anvil synthesis (SS1; SS3; TS2). Its F3 is high on precursor activity and solution-mediated transport, but the route is speculative for BaPdO₃ because no Ba–Pd hydrothermal perovskite analogue is provided, and Ca₂Pt₃O₈ showed impurity/crystallinity issues (SS3; TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaPdO₃ is predicted to be a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The CS/SS/TS analogues support synthesis reasonably well: BaRhO₃/BaRuO₃ show that hexagonal Ba–late-transition-metal oxide perovskites can be made under high pressure (CS1–CS3; SS5), while Ba₂Hg₃Pd₇O₁₄ shows that Ba–Pd oxides can be stabilized under high oxygen pressure (SS4; TS3). The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential, because charge-balanced BaPdO₃ implies Pd(IV) [Reasonable Inference] and the closest Ba–Pd analogue required high-pressure O₂ (SS4; TS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among hexagonal perovskite polytypes and competing PdO/Pd-metal-containing products, as BaRuO₃ flux synthesis showed polytype competition (CS3) and Ca₂Pt₃O₈ hydrothermal synthesis showed noble-metal impurity issues (SS3; TS2). Overall, BaPdO₃ is plausibly synthesizable but likely requires either oxygen-pressure ceramic conditions or high-pressure oxidizing synthesis rather than a simple ambient-pressure solid-state route.
