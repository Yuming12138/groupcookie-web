### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, which is at the high-score threshold in the provided rubric. Thermodynamically similar Co–Ga phases that are experimentally synthesized have E_hull values from ~0.053 to 0.073 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-4), so the target’s calculated metastability is not unusually high. Structurally similar InCo₃N is even closer to the hull at 0.0225 eV/atom (SS-5), supporting the viability of Co–N antiperovskite-like frameworks. Remaining uncertainty is that the exact GaCo₆N phase is not directly reported in CS/SS/TS, so the 0.050 eV/atom phase may require kinetic stabilization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high F2 score because SS-5 demonstrates ambient-pressure synthesis at only 600–650 °C using a tube furnace and alumina boats. The main severity penalty is NH₃, which is a reactive/controlled atmosphere explicitly used in SS-5. CS and TS do not provide useful direct evidence for this ammonolysis condition; insufficient evidence from CS and TS for Route 1 severity, neutral signal 50. The uncertainty is whether Ga₂O₃ behaves as cleanly as In₂O₃ under NH₃ for this Co-rich target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is moderate-good because SS-5 shows that an oxide/metal powder mixture can form a related Co–N antiperovskite framework at 600–650 °C. The target’s GaCo₁₂/NCo₆ polyhedral motif resembles the InCo₁₂/NCo₆ motif in SS-5, reducing structural reconstruction risk. However, the target has GaCo₆N rather than InCo₃N stoichiometry, so the reaction may have a narrower phase window [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS do not directly address ammonolysis kinetics; insufficient evidence from CS/TS for this route, neutral signal 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is penalized because Co–Ga alloy analogues require arc melting, remelting, and 900 °C / 125 h annealing in evacuated silica capsules (CS-1, CS-3; TS-1 to TS-4). Nitrogenation of Sm₂Co₁₇N₃ then requires flowing N₂ at 450–550 °C for hours to days (CS-5; SS-1), making the route clearly multi-step. Ga volatility and sealed containment are inferred from the use of sealed capsules for Ga-containing analogues [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-3]. The route is feasible but more severe than direct NH₃ ammonolysis.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are the limiting factor for Route 2 because metallic parent alloys have low precursor activity and Co–Ga analogues need long anneals for phase homogenization (CS-1, CS-3). Nitrogenation analogues report slow diffusion, incomplete nitrogenation, and possible α-Co precipitation in Co-rich nitrides (CS-5; SS-1). The advantage is that nitrogen insertion into a preformed metallic framework may require less reconstruction than forming the whole lattice from oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The exact Co–Ga parent structure needed for GaCo₆N is not identified in CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because CS-4 uses 15 MPa N₂, which requires an autoclave but is below the rubric’s >5 GPa high-pressure penalty. The parent alloy preparation still requires high-temperature alloying/annealing under Ar or sealed conditions, supported by CS-4 and Co–Ga alloy analogues (CS-1, CS-3). The process is multi-step and needs controlled gas pressure. TS provides thermodynamic similarity but not specific high-pressure conditions; insufficient evidence from TS for pressure-route conditions, neutral signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

High-pressure N₂ improves the nitrogen chemical potential and enabled Ce₂Fe₁₇N₃ formation at 250–350 °C in CS-4, which supports better nitrogen uptake than simple flowing N₂. Crushing/sieving of the parent alloy in CS-4 addresses diffusion limitations, so the ion-diffusion score is higher than Route 2. Structural reconstruction remains only partially supported because CS-4 is an Fe–rare-earth nitride, not a Ga–Co nitride. The key uncertainty is whether Co–Ga accepts nitrogen without decomposing or forming simpler cobalt nitrides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a small-cell ordered intermetallic nitride with simple GaCo₁₂ cuboctahedra and NCo₆ octahedra, and the description does not show all three vertex/edge/face sharing modes simultaneously (target structure). Similar Co–Ga intermetallic frameworks are crystalline after arc melting/annealing (CS-1, CS-3; TS-1 to TS-4), and structurally similar InCo₃N is crystalline after solid–gas synthesis (SS-5). Therefore, amorphization risk is low. The remaining uncertainty is nanoscale disorder or partial N occupancy, which is common in nitride antiperovskites but not directly specified in the provided sources [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

GaCo₆N is best viewed as a near-hull metastable or borderline-stable phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable threshold, but no provided source reports the exact compound. The strongest synthesis support comes from the structural analogue InCo₃N, which forms by NH₃ solid–gas reaction and shares the key ACo₁₂/NCo₆ antiperovskite framework motif (SS-5). Chemical and thermodynamic analogues strongly support Co–Ga metallic bonding and accessible near-hull Co–Ga phases, but mostly in rare-earth Co–Ga intermetallics rather than Ga–Co nitrides (CS-1, CS-3; TS-1 to TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is reactive nitrogen incorporation at moderate temperature, especially NH₃ ammonolysis as demonstrated for InCo₃N (SS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target is Co-rich and may compete with Co metal, Co–Ga intermetallics, cobalt nitrides, or Ga-containing oxide residues [Reasonable Inference from CS-5/SS-1 phase-control issues and SS-5 oxide route].
