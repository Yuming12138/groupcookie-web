### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is exactly 0.050 eV/atom, which falls at the high-stability/metastability threshold and justifies a score of 90. Multiple close chemical/structural analogues such as BaNiF6, RbRuF6, RbBiF6, CaPdF6, and BaCrF6 are reported as E_hull = 0 phases (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS2, SS4). Thermodynamic analogues with E_hull near 0.05 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by several methods, supporting that this metastability level is not prohibitive (TS1–TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that the exact BaRhF6 cubic polymorph is not experimentally demonstrated in the references.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
High-pressure fluorination is severe because it uses corrosive F2 under hundreds to thousands of atmospheres in Monel/Monell autoclaves (CS1, SS4). The temperature itself is moderate, 300–600 °C, and below the rubric’s high-temperature penalty (CS1, SS4). The main penalty is safety/equipment complexity rather than thermal severity. TS provides insufficient direct evidence for high-pressure BaRhF6 synthesis and is neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are aided by high fluorine chemical potential, as BaNi/BaCr fluoride analogues form at only 300–600 °C (CS1, SS4). Structural reconstruction is moderate because BaCrF6 and BaNiF6 already contain BaF12 and MF6 motifs similar to target BaF12/RhF6 (CS1, SS4). The uncertainty is whether Rh will form the correct Rh(IV) fluoride rather than RhF3/RhF5-related impurities [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Ambient-pressure direct fluorination avoids the high-pressure penalty and is supported by RbRuF6 at 350 °C and CaPdF6 at low fluorination temperatures (CS5, SS2). It still requires elemental F2, giving a significant reactive-atmosphere and toxicity penalty (CS4, CS5, SS2). The route is operationally simpler than Route 1 but still specialized due to fluorine handling.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to binary-fluoride sintering because RuCl3-type chloride precursors fluorinate readily in F2 (CS5), and intimate mixtures can convert under diluted F2 (SS2). The target’s Ba/Rh ordering and cubic polymorph selection remain uncertain because the direct analogue is RbRuF6, not BaRhF6 (CS5). Thus F3 is moderate-high but not excellent.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-tube synthesis uses less extreme equipment than F2 autoclaves, and BaAlF5-type fluorides are made in sealed Au/Pt tubes under Ar (CS2, CS3). However, for BaRhF6 this likely requires RhF4 or a strong fluoride oxidant, which introduces volatile/toxic/air-sensitive precursor penalties [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient SS evidence that sealed inert fluoride synthesis can stabilize Rh(IV), so SS is neutral-to-weak for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Binary fluoride diffusion is expected to be slower than reactive F2 fluorination [Reasonable Inference]. BaAlF5 examples require controlled multistep polymorph transformations and quenching, showing that sealed fluoride reactions can have difficult phase selection (CS2, CS3). TS4 indicates sealed gold-tube halide crystallization can be easy for some Ba–transition-metal halides, but that structure/chemistry is not a direct BaRhF6 analogue.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Ba/Rh cation size and coordination contrast, triggering one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference from the target’s BaF12 and RhF6 coordination]. The target description does not indicate a very large unit cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity, so only one check is triggered. CS and SS analogues show that crystalline BaF12/MF6 frameworks can be obtained despite similar size mismatch (CS1, SS4), reducing concern.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaRhF6 is best viewed as a near-threshold metastable or marginally stable phase because its E_hull is 0.050 eV/atom, exactly at the favorable cutoff. The strongest support comes from chemically and structurally related BaMF6/RbMF6 fluorides with MF6 octahedra and high-valent transition metals, especially BaNiF6, BaCrF6, and RbRuF6 (CS1, SS4, CS5). The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, which stabilizes high-valent octahedral transition-metal fluorides in the analogues (CS1, CS4, CS5, SS4). The most critical limiting factor is Rh oxidation/phase control, because no supplied reference directly demonstrates BaRhF6 or a Rh-containing BaMF6 analogue. Route 2 is recommended first because it balances strong fluorination power with lower equipment burden than high-pressure F2.
