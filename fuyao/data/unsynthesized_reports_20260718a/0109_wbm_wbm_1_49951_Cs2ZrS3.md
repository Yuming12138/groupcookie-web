### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be a ground-state phase. This is reinforced by multiple chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar Cs sulfides with E_hull ≈ 0, including Cs₄In₂S₅, Cs₂LiNbS₄, K₂TiS₃, Cs₃BS₃, and CsPrS₂ (CS/SS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is that calculated ground-state stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing polymorphs (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature and high pressure because the closest K₂TiS₃ analogue was made at 1070 K, about 797 °C, in a sealed evacuated ampoule (SS/TS). The penalties come from vacuum sealing, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive alkali sulfides, and multi-step ampoule processing (SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]). Overall severity is moderate but not prohibitive.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The closest structural analogue, K₂TiS₃, strongly supports formation of the same A₂MS₃ framework type with edge-sharing MS₅ trigonal bipyramids (SS/TS). Kinetics are limited by refractory Zr and long-range solid-state diffusion, but sulfide chemistry and long annealing can overcome this, as shown by the 6 d K₂TiS₃ synthesis (SS/TS; Zr extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]). This gives the best kinetic score among the routes.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The flux route operates at lower temperature, about 773 K for Cs₂LiNbS₄, which is less severe thermally (SS/TS). However, it still requires glovebox loading, sealed evacuated tubes, sulfur/polysulfide volatility control, and post-synthesis flux removal (SS/TS). Therefore the route is moderate in equipment and handling severity.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The Cs-polysulfide flux should improve mass transport and reduce diffusion barriers, as Cs₂LiNbS₄ crystallized from A₂S₃/Li₂S/S/metal mixtures at 773 K (SS/TS). The limitation is structural mismatch: Cs₂LiNbS₄ contains tetrahedral NbS₄/LiS₄ units, whereas the target contains ZrS₅ trigonal bipyramids (SS/TS). Thus kinetics are favorable for crystallization but less specifically directed toward the target structure.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The H₂S route uses 1000 °C and toxic/reactive H₂S, as shown for CsLnS₂ synthesis from Cs₂CO₃ and rare-earth oxides (CS/SS/TS). It avoids sealed volatile sulfur ampoules, which improves that part of the severity score (CS/SS/TS). The main severity is gas-handling safety and atmosphere switching.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The H₂S route has poor-to-moderate kinetics for Cs₂ZrS₃ because ZrO₂ is refractory and difficult to convert fully to sulfide [Reasonable Inference]. The references demonstrate rare-earth oxide sulfidation, not ZrO₂ conversion into A₂ZrS₃ (CS/SS/TS). Also, the CsLnS₂ structures are not close to the target ZrS₅ trigonal-bipyramidal framework, implying substantial reconstruction (CS/SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

The azide route operates near 500 °C, which is thermally mild, and was used to synthesize Cs₄In₂S₅ (CS). Its severity comes from azide handling, N₂ pressure evolution, sealed ampoules, sulfur volatility, and long annealing (CS). The route is chemically powerful but operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 4 kinetics

CsN₃ decomposition provides highly reactive Cs and can promote low-temperature formation of Cs sulfides (CS). However, the demonstrated product Cs₄In₂S₅ contains InS₄ tetrahedra, not ZrS₅ trigonal bipyramids, so structural targeting is uncertain (CS). Zr incorporation may require higher temperature or longer annealing than the In case [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines very large Cs⁺ with much smaller Zr⁴⁺, and related data show long Cs–S bonds versus much shorter transition-metal–S bonds (CS/SS/TS), which increases local structural mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target also has mixed edge/face/corner-sharing connectivity, which raises crystallization complexity; the close K₂TiS₃ analogue shows similar mixed connectivity but was still synthesized (SS/TS). Unit-cell size is not established from the provided data, so that check is treated as not triggered due to insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₂ZrS₃ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state phase because its calculated energy above hull is 0.0000 eV/atom, and many related Cs sulfides are also hull-stable or nearly hull-stable (CS/SS/TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from the K₂TiS₃ structural analogue, which shares the A₂MS₃ composition pattern and distorted edge-sharing MS₅ trigonal-bipyramidal motif with the target (SS/TS). The most important enabling factor is the availability of sealed sulfide solid-state and flux routes that stabilize air-sensitive Cs sulfide frameworks under controlled sulfur chemical potential (SS/TS). The main limiting factor is kinetic and phase-control difficulty associated with refractory Zr, volatile sulfur/Cs sulfide chemistry, and possible competing Cs–Zr–S phases [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control issues in CS/SS/TS]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but requires careful ampoule handling, slow cooling, and likely iterative optimization.
