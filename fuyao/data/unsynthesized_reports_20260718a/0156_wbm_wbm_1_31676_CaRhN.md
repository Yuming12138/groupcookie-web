### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target is exactly on the convex hull with E_hull = 0.0 from the provided target data, which places it in the 90–100 range. This is reinforced by CaNiN, the closest chemical/structural analogue, also having E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2), and by multiple thermodynamic neighbors with E_hull = 0.0 (TS-1–TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing experimentally stabilized polymorphs [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Direct solid-state synthesis is not severe in temperature because the closest analogue CaNiN is made at 800 °C for 20 h under N₂ (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2), and related Ca/Sr nitrides form at 850–950 °C (SS-1, SS-4, SS-5; TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The main severity penalty is air/moisture-sensitive Ca/Ca₃N₂ handling [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the inert-atmosphere handling used in related nitrides (SS-5; TS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Rh volatility or sealed-system necessity, so that item was not penalized.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are limited by Rh metal diffusion and nitridation, since direct solid-state reactions using dense metals commonly require long anneals [Reasonable Inference], and analogues use 20–24 h holds (CS-1; SS-1; TS-1). The structural reconstruction burden is low because CaNiN has the same P4₂/mmc framework and very similar coordination (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2). The uncertainty is whether Rh forms the same linear nitride unit as readily as Ni in CaNiN [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Li-assisted nitridation uses temperatures of 850–900 °C, supported by Li/Ca/Sr/Mn nitride syntheses (SS-1, SS-4; TS-1, TS-4). Penalties arise from volatile/reactive Li chemistry and air-sensitive Ca/Li handling; Li-containing components were explicitly noted to evaporate or be lost above 800 °C in Ca[(Ni,Li)N] synthesis (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for exact Li compatibility with Rh, so compatibility is treated as an extrapolation.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Li flux/mineralization should improve mass transport compared with dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference], and Li-assisted analogues successfully form crystalline alkaline-earth transition-metal nitrides at 850–900 °C (SS-1, SS-4; TS-1, TS-4). Structural targeting is supported indirectly by the CaNiN P4₂/mmc analogue (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2), but the Li-assisted Mn nitrides are not isostructural, so phase selection remains uncertain. The route earns the best balance of moderate conditions and improved kinetics.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The sodium/azide flux route is more operationally severe because CS-3 uses glovebox loading, sealed Nb tubes, vacuum-sealed quartz jackets, medium N₂ pressure, sodium flux, and NaN₃. Although the temperature is only ~800 °C (CS-3), the sealed reactive flux/azide pressure system creates significant safety and handling burden. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports sodium flux for the exact target, so CS-3 is the main source and transferability is moderate.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux chemistry improves precursor activity and ion transport, as demonstrated by BaCuN formation in sodium flux at ~800 °C (CS-3). However, BaCuN is only partially structurally similar: it contains linear Cu–N and alkaline-earth/transition-metal nitride octahedral motifs but is monoclinic rather than P4₂/mmc (CS-3). The main uncertainty is Rh solubility and whether sodium flux stabilizes the CaRhN phase instead of competing Ca/Rh nitrides or intermetallics [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed corner- and edge-sharing connectivity, and this motif is also present in the CaNiN analogue (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2), which increases reconstruction complexity relative to a simple binary nitride. The likely Ca/Rh cation size mismatch exceeds 15% [Reasonable Inference], but CS/SS/TS do not provide ionic radii, so those sources are neutral for that check. The small expected P4₂/mmc cell, inferred from Ca₂Ni₂N₂ in the same structure family, reduces amorphization risk relative to large complex structures (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2).

CaRhN is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is 0.0 and thermodynamic analogues are also on the hull (TS-1–TS-5). The best support comes from CaNiN, which is chemically and structurally very close: same P4₂/mmc framework, linear transition-metal–N coordination, and NCa₄M₂ octahedral connectivity (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly analogous CaNiN phase synthesized under accessible N₂ solid-state conditions (CS-1; SS-2; TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is the lack of direct Rh nitride analogue evidence and the likely slow nitridation/diffusion of Rh metal [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is quite promising, but successful synthesis will likely require careful nitrogen activity, inert handling, and possibly flux/mineralizer assistance.
