### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = 0.0000 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on the convex hull and receives 98/100. CS analogues include several E_hull ≈ 0 rare-earth-rich compounds, such as Y3RuI3 and Pr3I3Ru (CS1, CS4), supporting that metal-rich rare-earth compounds can be thermodynamically stable. SS and TS also include stable rare-earth chalcogenides such as PrSe, YS, and LaS with E_hull = 0 (SS1/TS1, SS3/TS2, SS5/TS3). Remaining uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or absence of competing low-temperature phases.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses evacuated sealed ampoules, which are supported by YSe/YTe synthesis and Y2CdSe4 synthesis (SS3/TS2, CS2). The main deductions come from vacuum sealing, volatile Se, multistep grinding/annealing, and air-sensitive Y/selenide handling [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 supports evacuated quartz chalcogenide synthesis but does not directly address metal-rich Y–Ga–Se phase stability, so that part has insufficient evidence from CS2 and is treated cautiously. SS/TS provide strong evidence for Y–Se ampoule chemistry but insufficient evidence for Ga incorporation.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Y, Ga, and Se are accessible but not as kinetically active as solution-derived precursors [Reasonable Inference]. SS3/TS2 show that YSe/YTe formation from elements can require long heating and regrinding, indicating nontrivial diffusion and phase-purity issues. CS2 shows direct elemental synthesis of ternary selenides, but it also reports repeated regrinding/reheating for complete reaction in a related chalcogenide family. The target’s Caswellsilverite-like cation ordering requires more reconstruction than binary rocksalt YSe, adding kinetic uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits demanding sealed refractory-tube conditions from metal-rich rare-earth analogues, where Ta or Nb tubes and 850–975 °C holds for weeks are reported (CS1/CS3/CS4). The use of YSe reduces free Se volatility relative to elemental Se [Reasonable Inference], but sealed containment is still needed because selenides and Se-bearing compositions can generate vapor at high temperature [Reasonable Inference]. SS3/TS2 support the feasibility of preparing YSe, but they also note impurity issues in YSe samples. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for the final Y–Ga–Se metal-rich ordering step.

#### F3 — Route 2

Preforming YSe should improve chemical homogeneity because Y–Se bonds are already present [Reasonable Inference], and YSe synthesis is documented (SS3/TS2). The route still requires diffusion of Y and Ga into a new ordered metal-rich structure, and CS rare-earth-rich analogues often need 3–4 week anneals (CS1/CS4). CS3 reports narrow temperature/composition control for rare-earth-rich iodides, which suggests phase selection may be difficult by analogy. The route is kinetically better than direct elements but not easy.

#### F2 — Route 3

CVT avoids very high temperatures if operated near the Dy3Se4 transport window of 1150 → 1070 K or slightly lower [Reasonable Inference based on SS4]. It still requires evacuated sealed ampoules, volatile Se, and toxic/corrosive transport agents such as I2 or SeCl4 [Reasonable Inference]. SS4 supports rare-earth selenide transport from elements, but CS provides insufficient direct evidence for CVT of Y–Ga–Se and is scored neutral for that sub-factor. The absence of high pressure and the potential for crystal growth make severity moderate rather than extreme.

#### F3 — Route 3

SS4 directly supports chemical transportation of a rare-earth selenide from elements, which helps the crystallization-pathway score. However, the target’s Y:Ga:Se = 5:1:4 composition is more complex than binary Dy3Se4, and differential transport of Y, Ga, and Se is a major uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. Composition control is therefore the weakest kinetic aspect. Insufficient evidence from CS and TS exists for transport chemistry of Ga-containing rare-earth selenides, so this route remains plausible but experimentally risky.

#### F2 — Route 4

Melt/anneal routes are supported by high-temperature rare-earth chalcogenide analogues at 1700 °C (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS3) and YS synthesis at 1700 °C (SS3/TS2). The proposed Ga–Se flux variant lowers the temperature to ~1000–1150 °C by exploiting Ga/Se liquid formation [Reasonable Inference], but sealed refractory containment is still required. Volatile Se and air-sensitive Y drive major F2 deductions. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for Ga–Se flux growth of the target and is treated neutral on that point.

#### F3 — Route 4

A liquid Ga–Se-rich phase can improve mass transport [Reasonable Inference], which partially offsets the slow diffusion expected for refractory Y. Melt-growth analogues show that rare-earth chalcogenides can be crystallized from high-temperature melts (SS1/TS1, SS5/TS3), but those analogues are simpler binary/solid-solution systems. The target’s ordered metal-rich tetragonal phase may not nucleate cleanly from a flux, and competing Y–Ga or Ga–Se phases are plausible [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: large cation size/chemistry mismatch between Y and Ga [Reasonable Inference]. The target description shows corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity C3 criterion is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS to determine whether it exceeds 40 atoms; this subcheck is treated neutrally. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y5GaSe4 is likely a predicted ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is 0.0000 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from Y–Se sealed-ampoule chemistry (SS3/TS2), ternary selenide direct synthesis in evacuated quartz (CS2), rare-earth-rich long-anneal Ta/Nb tube chemistry (CS1/CS3/CS4), and rare-earth selenide CVT growth (SS4). The most important enabling factor is the very favorable thermodynamic stability combined with multiple high-temperature sealed-vessel precedents. The main limiting factor is not thermodynamics but phase selection: the target is metal-rich, contains Y–Ga contacts, and may compete with YSe/Y2Se3, Ga2Se3, and Y–Ga intermetallics [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but likely requires careful sealed-tube or transport-assisted optimization.
