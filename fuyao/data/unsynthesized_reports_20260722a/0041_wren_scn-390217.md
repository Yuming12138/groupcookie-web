### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull = −0.0131494 eV/atom places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin, so the formal F1 score is high. CS-2, CS-4, and CS-5 include fluorides at E_hull = 0.0, demonstrating that fluoride phases in chemically adjacent spaces can be thermodynamically stable. However, CS-1 has E_hull ≈ 0.102 eV/atom yet is experimentally synthesized, showing that moderate positive hull values do not preclude fluorination products. The main uncertainty is that KF₆ has unusual charge balance as a binary K–F phase [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS entry confirms the exact composition.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a low F2 score because CS-1 uses anhydrous HF, F₂ gas, fluoropolymer hardware, and a vacuum line, all of which are hazardous and specialized. Its F3 score is comparatively favorable because CS-1 demonstrates that a chemically very close K–F-rich analogue can form at room temperature within 72 h, implying rapid kinetics in HF/F₂ media. The remaining uncertainty is structural: CS-1 contains an HF/KF framework description and does not establish the target’s R-3 KF₁₂ cuboctahedral network. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has severe atmosphere and pressure demands because CS-2 and CS-5 both use F₂ pressure in Monel/Monell autoclaves. The F3 score is moderate because CS-2 and CS-5 contain AF₁₂ cuboctahedra similar to the target’s KF₁₂ motif, but their structures also include NiF₆ octahedra, so the structural transfer is incomplete. CS-2 explicitly notes that Guinier-pure samples are extremely difficult, and CS-5 reports difficult phase control, which lowers confidence. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for K–F high-pressure fluorination and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for sealed volatile fluorinating agents and multi-step handling, consistent with the sealed-ampoule complexity seen in CS-4 and the HF/F₂ hazards indicated by CS-1. Kinetics are less favorable than Route 1 because dry KF is expected to be less reactive than aHF solution chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports the general idea that sealed fluoride solid-state reactions can yield crystalline fluorides, but it required 860 °C for 12 d and involved CaF₂/FeF₃ rather than K–F, so transferability is weak. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion is not triggered. The target description reports edge-sharing KF₁₂ cuboctahedra and does not report mixed corner/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is not triggered. CS-2 and CS-5 do show mixed connectivity in SrNiF₆/BaNiF₆, but that feature depends on NiF₆ octahedra absent from the target. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, so this check carries some uncertainty, but the simple formula and high symmetry suggest low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically stable by the reported negative E_hull, so it should be treated as a likely ground-state candidate by the computational criterion. The chemical evidence is strongest from CS-1 because it is an extremely close K–F-rich analogue synthesized under anhydrous HF/F₂ conditions, while the structural evidence is strongest from CS-2 and CS-5 because they contain AF₁₂ cuboctahedra in trigonal fluoride frameworks. The most important enabling factor is access to very high fluorine chemical potential using HF/F₂ or high-pressure F₂, as demonstrated by CS-1, CS-2, and CS-5. The most important limiting factor is the formal chemical plausibility of binary KF₆ without F–F, HF₂⁻, or other polyfluoride/HF-derived units [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the best practical strategy is to screen low-temperature aHF/F₂ chemistry first, while carefully checking whether the isolated phase is truly binary KF₆ rather than an HF-containing or polyfluoride compound.
