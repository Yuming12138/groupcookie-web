### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.2024 eV/atom, which places it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin and gives F1 = 100 (target data). Chemically close analogues Cs₂TcF₆ and Cs₂NbF₆ both have E_hull = 0.0 and the same A₂MF₆ structure type, reinforcing the stability expectation from CS (CS1, CS2). SS and TS provide insufficient useful chemical signal for Cs₂MoF₆ because the listed entries are unrelated chemistries, so they are treated as neutral = 50 for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild in temperature and pressure because Cs₂TcF₆ crystallizes at room temperature from aqueous HF by evaporation, so no high-temperature or high-pressure deductions apply (CS1). The deductions come from the multistep crystallization sequence and hazardous HF/fluoride handling, which are explicit in CS1 and extended to Mo by [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and remain neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are highly favorable because solution metathesis in HF bypasses solid-state diffusion and directly crystallizes an isostructural A₂MF₆ phase (CS1; target data). The structural match is unusually strong: same P-3m1 space group, same 17° tilt, and same corner/face-sharing CsF₁₂/MF₆ framework (CS1; target data). The main uncertainty is whether Mo(IV) remains stable in the chosen HF medium without oxidation or hydrolysis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures because the Cs₂NbF₆ analogue is made at 520 °C in sealed copper tubes under nitrogen (CS2). The severity is moderate because sealed fluoride containment and air/moisture-sensitive MoF₄/CsF handling are required [Reasonable Inference] based on CS2. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

The structural reconstruction score is high because Cs₂NbF₆ is isostructural with Cs₂MoF₆ and has the same M–F bond length of 2.01 Å (CS2; target data). The diffusion score is lower under the provided rubric because Mo has a very high elemental melting point, although reactive fluoride chemistry and the low 520 °C analogue synthesis partly mitigate this [Reasonable Inference] from CS2. Remaining uncertainty is the availability and stability of MoF₄ under sealed-tube heating [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because CS3/CS4 explicitly require carefully dried Ar and diluted fluorine gas at 300–500 °C for Cs transition-metal fluoride synthesis. F₂ gas handling, multistep intermediate preparation, and moisture-sensitive fluorides cause the main deductions (CS3, CS4). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Fluorination provides high chemical activity, but CS3/CS4 also indicate long 1–5 day treatments and specialized handling, implying slower ordering and phase-control challenges. The target A₂MoF₆ stoichiometry is not the same as the A₃MF₇ phases in CS3/CS4, so structural reconstruction and competing phase risk are less favorable [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is maintaining Mo in the +4 state under fluorinating conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Cs/Mo cation-size contrast, as reflected by long Cs–F distances of 3.20–3.39 Å versus Mo–F = 2.01 Å, triggering one rubric point (target data). The structure is simple A₂MF₆ and not a >40 atom cell, and it has corner and face sharing but no reported edge sharing, so only one amorphization trigger applies (target data). CS reduces concern because crystalline isostructural Cs₂TcF₆ and Cs₂NbF₆ are reported (CS1, CS2), while SS/TS are insufficient and neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₂MoF₆ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is strongly below the stability threshold and close analogues Cs₂TcF₆ and Cs₂NbF₆ are on the hull (target data; CS1, CS2). The chemical-similarity evidence is very strong because two A₂MF₆ cesium transition-metal fluorides share the same P-3m1 framework, and Cs₂TcF₆ is nearly a one-for-one structural analogue (CS1, CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the apparent compatibility of large Cs⁺ with isolated/face-contacted MF₆ octahedra in the P-3m1 A₂MF₆ lattice (CS1, CS2; target data). The most critical limiting factor is Mo(IV) fluoride chemistry: avoiding hydrolysis, oxide contamination, or over-fluorination/oxidation requires fluoride-rich, dry, HF-compatible conditions [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not materially improve confidence because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated, so they are treated as neutral evidence.
