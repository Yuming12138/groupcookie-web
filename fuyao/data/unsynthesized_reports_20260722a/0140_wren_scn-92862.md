### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.03826218 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable stability category and suggests it is at or below the computed convex hull. Several chemically similar F-43m intermetallics, including ScNi4Cd, ScCu4In, and AgCu4Zr, are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS2–CS4), reinforcing that this structure family is thermodynamically plausible. TS examples are stable/near-stable but chemically unrelated, so TS gives insufficient useful target-specific signal and is treated neutral 50 for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting

Arc melting is directly supported by ScCu4In and AgCu4Zr syntheses under Ar with repeated remelting (CS3, CS4). The severity penalty comes from very high local arc temperatures, multiple melting/remelting/annealing steps, and Y oxidation sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence that high pressure or reactive gases are required; CS3 and CS4 use standard Ar atmospheres.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting

Elemental metals are relatively low-activity precursors compared with salts or sol-gel reagents, but arc melting provides high mobility and has succeeded for closely related F-43m intermetallics (CS3, CS4). Repeated remelting in CS3/CS4 indicates that homogenization is important, so kinetics are not trivial. The structural reconstruction score is favorable because the desired cubic F-43m cuboctahedral motif is already demonstrated in CS1, CS3, and CS4.

#### F2 — Route 2: Melt growth / self-flux

The main condition penalty is the >1000 °C melt-growth temperature, since the closest analogue CaCu4In was grown at 1450 K / 1177 °C (CS1). The route avoids high pressure, reactive gases, and complex multi-step processing, which keeps condition severity moderate. Y oxidation/reactivity remains a real handling issue [Reasonable Inference], motivating sealed or inert processing.

#### F3 — Route 2: Melt growth / self-flux

Melt growth has better kinetics than solid-state diffusion because a liquid phase can equilibrate the Y–Cu–Ag composition [Reasonable Inference]. This is strongly supported by the extremely close CaCu4In analogue grown directly from elements and by the matching F-43m bonding geometry (CS1). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ag stabilizes the same site as In without forming Ag–Cu or Y–Ag competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Two-step precursor route

The two-step route is modeled on ScNi4Cd synthesis, where a rare-earth/Ni binary precursor is arc-melted and then reacted with the third metal in sealed Ta under Ar (CS2). It is more complex than direct melt growth because it requires precursor formation, grinding/mixing, sealed annealing, and slow cooling. Its benefit is better control of Y–Cu pre-reaction before Ag addition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Two-step precursor route

Pre-alloying Y–Cu may reduce reactivity mismatch relative to mixing all elements at once [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the binary precursor strategy in CS2. However, Ag ordering and diffusion into a preformed Y–Cu matrix may be slower than crystallization from a homogeneous melt [Reasonable Inference]. Structural support remains strong because CS1–CS4 all validate closely related F-43m intermetallic motifs.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, explicitly described for CuY3Cu6Ag3 units, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion. A second risk comes from the large size mismatch between Y and Cu [Reasonable Inference], although the small F-43m unit and simple intermetallic topology reduce glass-forming risk. The medium F4 score reflects that Cu–Ag–rare-earth alloys can have competing metallic phases or glassy tendencies under rapid quenching [Reasonable Inference], so slow cooling/annealing is preferred.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YCu4Ag is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stable phase because its reported E_hull is negative, −0.03826218 eV/atom, and chemically similar F-43m intermetallics are stable on the hull (CS2–CS4). The strongest support comes from CS1 and CS4: CS1 has nearly identical F-43m geometry and bond lengths, while CS4 directly contains Ag–Cu plus an early transition metal in the same structure type. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of melt/arc routes that create high atomic mobility and allow the ordered cuboctahedral intermetallic framework to form (CS1, CS3, CS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase/composition control, especially avoiding Y oxidation, Ag/Cu segregation, and competing Y–Cu or Ag–Cu-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS examples do not materially improve route confidence because they are chemically unrelated, so the assessment is dominated by CS evidence.
