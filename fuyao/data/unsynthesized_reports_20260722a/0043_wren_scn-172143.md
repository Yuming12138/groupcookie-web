### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.146389752 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifies a high F1 score. Several chemically similar fluorides are stable or near-stable, including BaCrF6 with E_hull = 0 (CS-1), BaCrF5 with E_hull = 0 (CS-3), SrSbF5 with E_hull = 0 (CS-5), and SmZrF7/PrZr2F11 near the hull (CS-2, CS-4). SS/TS analogues are not chemically similar fluorides, so they provide insufficient chemistry-specific evidence and are treated as neutral where relevant.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct sealed-tube route avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa, consistent with CS-3/CS-4 style sealed fluoride syntheses. Deductions come from the need for sealed dry conditions, volatile W fluoride precursors, and toxic/air-sensitive fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether WF5 can be handled without disproportionation or volatilization during the reaction [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluoride reactions are experimentally credible because CS-2, CS-3, and CS-4 form ternary fluorides directly from fluoride precursors. However, bulk diffusion and cation ordering may be slow, as CS-3 required 65 h sintering for phase-pure BaCrF5. The route is kinetically plausible but not fast, especially because the target requires ordered alternating NaF6/WF6 octahedra [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The autoclave fluorination route uses only moderate temperature, as CS-1 reports 300 °C, but the F2/Ar atmosphere and up to 300 atm pressure are severe from a practical safety standpoint. It is not “high pressure” by the rubric’s >5 GPa criterion, but it requires specialized Monell fluorination equipment (CS-1). The score is reduced for reactive gas, toxicity, volatile fluoride risk, and multi-step handling.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
F2/Ar fluorination is highly chemically active and can form highly fluorinated phases at low temperature, as shown for BaCrF6 (CS-1). The kinetic weakness is phase control: CS-1 reports difficulty obtaining analytically pure chromium fluoride products due to volatility/losses, and analogous W fluoride volatility/redox issues are likely [Reasonable Inference]. This route may form Na–W fluorides rapidly but may not selectively yield the ordered NaWF6 phase.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The flux route uses sealed dry ampoules and volatile/reactive fluoride precursors, so it carries the same atmosphere and handling penalties as Route 1. Its temperature can likely be kept below the 1150 °C used for BaCrF5 crystal growth in CS-3 by using flux or self-flux conditions [Reasonable Inference]. It remains experimentally specialized but less hazardous than the F2 autoclave route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux or molten-fluoride media should improve mass transport relative to dry solid-state reaction, consistent with CS-3 where melt/slow cooling produced millimeter-scale BaCrF5 crystals and CS-5 where slow cooling aided Sr/BaSbF5 crystallization. The method should help Na/W ordering in the corner-sharing octahedral framework [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the flux composition or high temperature shifts W(V) to volatile or competing W fluoride species [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has simple corner-sharing octahedral connectivity with no mixed edge/face-sharing described, which lowers amorphization risk. The main amorphization trigger is the large Na⁺/W⁵⁺ size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The compact R-3 description with one Na, one W, and one F site suggests the unit cell is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference from target structure].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaWF6 is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is substantially below the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability threshold. The chemically similar fluoride references strongly support that ternary fluorides with metal-fluoride polyhedra can be synthesized by sealed-tube, melt, or fluorination routes (CS-1 to CS-5), while SS/TS analogues are largely chemically irrelevant and are neutral for route design. The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic driving force combined with the structural simplicity of corner-sharing NaF6/WF6 octahedra. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining W(V) and preventing loss or over-fluorination of volatile tungsten fluoride species [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical route is a sealed molten-fluoride/self-flux synthesis because it balances enhanced diffusion against manageable severity.
