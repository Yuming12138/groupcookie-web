### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.039011784 eV/atom, so it lies in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring band and is predicted to be thermodynamically stable within normal computational uncertainty (target data). Chemically similar A₂MO₄ oxides including Cs₂RuO₄, Ba₂TiO₄, Ba₂ZrO₄, and Rb₂WO₄ are reported at or essentially on the hull, reinforcing that this stoichiometry family can be stable (CS1–CS4). SS gives insufficient chemically relevant evidence for this factor because the listed compounds are not oxides of Rb/Os; neutral source score 50. TS gives weak generic support because the thermodynamic analogues are near-hull but chemically unrelated; neutral-to-mild score 60 (TS1–TS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild in temperature because the closest isostructural analogue Rb₂WO₄ was synthesized at 900 K, about 627 °C, in air for 24 h (CS4). It avoids high pressure and specialized atmosphere, following the Rb₂WO₄ precedent (CS4). The main severity penalty is the toxicity and volatility risk associated with Os chemistry, especially possible OsO₄ formation [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are treated as neutral 50 for condition transferability.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are aided by the exact structural match to Rb₂WO₄, since both target and analogue have C2/m symmetry, two 8-coordinate Rb sites, and isolated tetrahedral MO₄ groups (CS4; target data). However, using OsO₂ introduces a refractory, redox-active precursor that may react more slowly than WO₃ in Rb₂WO₄ synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. Intermediate grinding and longer holds are therefore likely needed beyond the 24 h Rb₂WO₄ benchmark if OsO₂ conversion is incomplete [Reasonable Inference; CS4].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 inherits moderate condition severity from Cs₂RuO₄ synthesis, which required 800 °C, 31 days, dry Ar, and sealed Pd-tube containment (CS1). The temperature is not extreme, but sealed noble-metal tube work and reactive alkali peroxide/oxide handling increase operational difficulty (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The route also carries toxic Os and possible OsO₄ hazards [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give insufficient evidence for this specific route and are scored neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Peroxide/oxide chemistry should provide higher oxygen activity and better access to high-valent Os(VI), by analogy to Cs₂O₂ + RuO₂ forming Cs₂RuO₄ (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The long 31-day Cs₂RuO₄ synthesis indicates that even chemically suitable alkali tetraoxo phases can have slow solid-state ordering kinetics (CS1). Structural reconstruction is plausible because the product motif is the same isolated MO₄ tetrahedron family as Cs₂RuO₄ and Rb₂WO₄ (CS1, CS4; target data), but the long anneal justifies a kinetic penalty.

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux synthesis is plausible because Ba₂TiO₄, an A₂MO₄ tetrahedral oxide, was grown from a BaCl₂/BaCO₃/TiO₂ melt in a Pt crucible (CS2). For Rb₂OsO₄, a lower-temperature RbCl/Rb₂CO₃ flux is inferred rather than the 1340 °C Ba₂TiO₄ temperature, because Rb salts can form lower-temperature flux media [Reasonable Inference]. The route has penalties for multistep processing, possible sealed containment, and Os toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS again provide insufficient direct route evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux should improve kinetics by providing a liquid transport medium, consistent with the successful flux growth of Ba₂TiO₄ crystals (CS2). The target’s exact structural analogue Rb₂WO₄ supports that the desired framework is accessible once composition and oxidation state are controlled (CS4). Remaining uncertainty is substantial because the flux may dissolve or decompose Rb₂OsO₄, and Os redox/volatility could narrow the crystallization window [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Rb⁺ and high-valent tetrahedral Os(VI), so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference]. However, the structure is relatively simple, with isolated OsO₄ tetrahedra and Rb–O coordination rather than a complex mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing transition-metal framework (target data). The close crystalline precedent Rb₂WO₄ with the same C2/m topology reduces concern about glass formation for this structure type (CS4). SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence on amorphization tendency for this oxide and are treated as neutral 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂OsO₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.039 eV/atom and the closest chemical/structural analogues include stable A₂MO₄ oxides on the hull (target data; CS1–CS4). The strongest synthesis support comes from Rb₂WO₄, which is isostructural in C2/m and was made by a simple air solid-state route at 900 K (CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the excellent structural match between Rb₂OsO₄ and Rb₂WO₄, including nearly identical Rb coordination and isolated tetrahedral MO₄ units (CS4; target data). The most critical limiting factor is Os redox/volatility control, because reaching Os(VI) without forming volatile, highly toxic OsO₄ requires careful oxygen chemical potential and containment [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but should be attempted at small scale with rigorous Os safety protocols [Reasonable Inference].
