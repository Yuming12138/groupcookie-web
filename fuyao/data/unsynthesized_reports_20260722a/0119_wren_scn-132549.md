### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is given as −0.0261 eV/atom, which is effectively on-hull within numerical tolerance and earns the maximum thermodynamic score. CS1 and CS2 report isostructural or closely related alkali Mo selenide analogues with E_hull = 0.0, supporting the stability of this structure family. However, SS and TS entries are chemically unrelated to K–Mn–Se, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for target-specific phase stability; those sources are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits sealed-tube, dry-inert handling from CS3, which used dry Ar glovebox preparation and evacuated silica tubes for elemental alkali molybdenum selenides. The target route avoids the 1000–1200 °C anneal used in CS3 by **[Reasonable Inference]** because K/Se volatility and Mn–Se chemistry make lower-temperature sealed reactions safer. The severity remains significant because volatile/toxic Se and air-sensitive K/K₂Se require sealed, inert processing **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS3 shows that direct reactions can make M₂Mo₆Se₆ analogues, but also that high-temperature treatment is needed for single-phase samples. For K(MnSe)₃, starting from elements or binaries likely requires substantial structural reconstruction into a P6₃/m chain framework **[Reasonable Inference]**. Kinetics are therefore only moderate despite favorable thermodynamics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CS1 and CS2 establish relatively mild temperatures, 500–680 °C or ~550 °C, under vacuum with slow heating near the salt melting point. This avoids very high-temperature penalties but still requires controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoules, and dry handling of salts, as stated in CS1/CS2. The need for a preformed Mn–Se framework precursor is not demonstrated and is a **[Reasonable Inference]**, making the route multi-step and moderately severe.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 receives the best kinetic score because CS1 gives the exact K(MoSe)₃ structure type and CS2 demonstrates ion exchange into related M₂Mo₆X₆ frameworks. Molten halide salts improve transport and can preserve chain topology, which should reduce reconstruction barriers **[Reasonable Inference]**. The major uncertainty is whether an Mn-based chain precursor can be made or whether MnSe binaries dominate.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CS5 supports sealed-ampoule melt/Bridgman growth of alkali transition-metal selenides at ~850–947 °C with slow cooling. Applying this to K–Mn–Se requires sealed growth because of K and Se volatility **[Reasonable Inference]**. It is not harsher than Route 1 in the scoring rubric, but crystal-growth handling and stoichiometry control make it experimentally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CS5 shows that melt growth can overcome diffusion limitations in alkali transition-metal selenides. However, CS5’s RbFe₂Se₃ structure is orthorhombic and not the P6₃/m K(MoSe)₃ type, so structural transferability is weaker than Route 2. This route may be useful for crystals but has a higher risk of crystallizing competing K–Mn–Se phases **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple high-symmetry P6₃/m description similar to CS1, which lowers amorphization concern. The large K/Mn cation size contrast triggers one amorphization-risk criterion by **[Reasonable Inference]**, but the unit-cell complexity and mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral connectivity are not supported by the provided target description. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for amorphization behavior in K–Mn–Se and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(MnSe)₃ is likely to be a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is −0.0261 eV/atom, and the isostructural K(MoSe)₃ analogue in CS1 is on the hull. The CS evidence is strong structurally and chemically for the alkali selenide chain framework, especially CS1 and CS2, but the Mn-for-Mo substitution is not directly demonstrated and remains the principal extrapolation. SS and TS provide little useful target-specific evidence because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated, so they are treated as neutral. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of an isostructural K(MoSe)₃ molten-salt product in CS1. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition from simple Mn–Se binaries and the need to control volatile K/Se activity in sealed reactions **[Reasonable Inference]**.
