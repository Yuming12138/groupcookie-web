### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported \(E_{\text{hull}}\) is negative, so it is scored as effectively thermodynamically stable; this directly supports a high F1 score. Chemically close phosphides CaP, K₄P₃, and NaP are all reported on the hull, indicating that binary metal phosphides in this chemical region can be stable crystalline phases (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). TS analogues are near-hull or stable but chemically unrelated, so there is insufficient evidence from TS for Ca–P-specific stability, neutral source score 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The direct route uses vacuum-sealed tubes, staged reaction, pressing/pelletizing, and high-temperature annealing up to 900 °C, all of which are directly supported by CaP synthesis (CS-1). The severity deductions arise from vacuum, volatile P in a sealed system, multi-step processing, and air/moisture-sensitive Ca/phosphide handling [Reasonable Inference; CS-1 notes moist-air sensitivity]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this Ca–P route, neutral source score 50 for route-specific condition transfer.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Ca and red P are reactive elemental precursors, but CS-1 still required several days and pelletized annealing, supporting moderate kinetic difficulty. Low Ca melting point and moderate synthesis temperatures reduce diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], but the target’s low-symmetry Ca₈P₅ network requires complete reconstruction from elements. Therefore the kinetics are workable but not rapid.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
NaP/KP sealed-ampoule synthesis at 725–765 K with iodine and Ar provides a lower-temperature model for phosphide formation (CS-5). Deductions are assigned for sealed volatile P/I₂ chemistry, multiple operational steps, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents [Reasonable Inference]. Direct Ca–P iodine transport is not shown in CS, so this transfer carries uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Iodine-assisted transport is expected to improve precursor activity and reduce long-range bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference based on CS-5 use of iodine transport]. However, CS-5 also reports impurity/explosion issues for related KP, indicating narrow conditions and nontrivial stoichiometry control. The F3 score is therefore the highest among routes but not near-perfect.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The Ca-rich flux route is inferred from excess alkali being required for K₄P₃ and from sealed high-temperature CaP annealing (CS-4, CS-1). Its temperature is moderate, but sealed volatile P, multi-step flux separation, and highly air-sensitive excess Ca impose safety and handling penalties [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient Ca-rich flux evidence, neutral source score 50.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Molten Ca should enhance mass transport and precursor activity for a Ca-rich phase [Reasonable Inference], and excess-metal strategies are validated for K phosphide synthesis (CS-4). The limiting factor is crystallographic ordering into the monoclinic Pm Ca₈P₅ structure, which has many inequivalent Ca/P sites and mixed polyhedral sharing [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Related CaP and K₄P₃ syntheses show phase-purity sensitivity, reducing the structural-reconstruction score (CS-1, CS-4).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Ca, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from composition]. The structural description contains extensive corner-, edge-, and face-sharing among CaP₄/CaP₅/PCa₇ polyhedra, triggering the mixed-connectivity amorphization/ordering risk. The unit-cell atom count is not clearly above 40 from the supplied description, so insufficient evidence from SS/TS supports a larger-cell amorphization penalty, neutral source score 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₈P₅ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported \(E_{\text{hull}}\) is negative and chemically related binary phosphides such as CaP, K₄P₃, and NaP are stable/on-hull phases (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemical similarity rather than structural or thermodynamic analogues: CS directly demonstrates sealed-tube and sealed-ampoule synthesis of metal phosphides, while SS/TS are mostly chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct Ca–P transferability. The most critical enabling factor is the high reactivity and mobility of Ca/P under sealed moderate-temperature conditions, especially if iodine transport or Ca-rich flux is used [Reasonable Inference; supported by CS-1 and CS-5]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among multiple possible Ca–P compositions and the ordering of a low-symmetry, mixed-connectivity Ca₈P₅ framework [Reasonable Inference; phase-control issues noted in CS-1, CS-4, CS-5]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but will require careful ampoule design, staged prereaction, and strict air-free handling.
