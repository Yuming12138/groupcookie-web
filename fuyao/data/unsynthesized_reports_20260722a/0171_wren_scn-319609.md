### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as −0.017529963 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within numerical tolerance and warrants a high thermodynamic score by the provided rubric. Chemically similar pnictides also include stable or near-stable phases, such as CeNi₂As₂ at E_hull = 0.0 or 0.0186 eV/atom and Ca₂Pd₃Sb₄ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The TS set contains near-hull materials, but they are chemically dissimilar amide/boride/phosphate/halide/nitride examples, so there is insufficient evidence from TS for Na–Pd–As phase stability beyond generic near-hull precedent; TS is treated as neutral 50 for chemical transfer.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

The sealed-tube route inherits high-temperature, long-time, vacuum/inert ampoule requirements from CS arsenides, which were annealed at 660–1000 °C for 1–2 weeks in evacuated sealed silica tubes (CS-1, CS-2). It does not require >1000 °C or high pressure if kept near 700–900 °C [Reasonable Inference], but it does require controlled atmosphere, sealed containment for volatile As/Na, and toxic/air-sensitive handling [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide useful chemically relevant severity data for Na–Pd–As, so they are neutral/insufficient for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental solid-state reaction is plausible because CS arsenides were made from elemental mixtures with grinding/pelletizing and long anneals (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetics are only moderate because Pd is a dense refractory metal and the target structure differs from the tetragonal CS analogues [Reasonable Inference]. The long annealing and repeated grinding in CS imply slow diffusion/ordering rather than rapid reaction (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The flux route remains controlled-atmosphere and sealed because Na–As chemistry is volatile/reactive [Reasonable Inference], but it can operate below 1000 °C, consistent with the 700 °C Pb-flux preparation of ScPdSb (CS-3). It is multi-step because Na–As prereaction, flux growth, slow cooling, and flux removal/decanting are required [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS flux guidance is chemically insufficient for Na–Pd–As and is treated neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux or self-flux should improve kinetics by providing liquid-mediated transport and better Pd dissolution than dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. This inference is supported by the Pd pnictide/antimonide CS example where ScPdSb single crystals were grown in Pb flux at 700 °C (CS-3). The structural reconstruction score remains moderate because no provided analogue shares the exact orthorhombic Imm2 NaPd₃As₂ structure, and CS arsenides report competing structural modifications and phase-control difficulty (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The prereacted Pd–As route is more severe because it adds a separate high-temperature binary synthesis or melting step before Na incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-2 mention optional melting in a high-frequency furnace and quenching for arsenides, supporting feasibility but also increased procedural complexity. Na and As still require sealed inert handling and toxic/air-sensitive precautions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Prereacting Pd–As may reduce uncontrolled As volatility but risks making dense binary phases that resist Na diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS example directly supports topotactic Na insertion into a Pd–As framework, so the structural reconstruction burden is uncertain and scored conservatively. The repeated annealing/grinding practices in CS arsenides support the expectation of slow ordering kinetics (CS-1, CS-2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an intermetallic arsenide with ordered high-coordination Na/Pd/As sites and a small formula unit, so amorphization risk is not intrinsically high [Reasonable Inference]. One risk trigger is assigned for the large Na–Pd size/coordination mismatch, reflected by long Na–Pd/Na–As contacts around 3.24–3.47 Å versus shorter Pd–As bonds around 2.51–2.73 Å in the target description. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target has >40 atoms per unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity, so those checks are not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaPd₃As₂ is likely thermodynamically stable or very near the ground state because its reported E_hull is −0.017529963 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar ternary pnictides and arsenides prepared by sealed-tube solid-state reactions and from a Pd pnictide/antimonide flux example (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The most important enabling factor is that many related pnictide intermetallics form from elemental mixtures or fluxes at accessible temperatures below or near 1000 °C (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The most important limiting factor is control of volatile/toxic As and volatile/air-sensitive Na under sealed high-temperature conditions [Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limitation is phase competition, because CS arsenides report coexistence of related structure modifications and difficult phase control (CS-1, CS-2).
