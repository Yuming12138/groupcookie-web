### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.1453 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability bin and gives F1 = 98. Chemically similar fluorides with E_hull near zero are well represented: CaF₂ fluorite is on the hull (CS2), NaNiF₃ Pnma is on the hull (CS3), NaCuF₃ is on the hull (CS5), and high-pressure NaNiF₃ is near the hull (CS4). However, SS and TS do not provide chemically relevant Na–F binary evidence; therefore there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for this factor beyond neutral thermodynamic similarity.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is condition-severe because high-pressure fluoride synthesis in CS4 uses 12 GPa and 1273 K, and CS1 uses 8.6 GPa and 1100 °C for a dense CaF₂ polymorph. The proposed NaF₂ route avoids >1000 °C by [Reasonable Inference], but still needs >5 GPa, sealed reactive fluorination, multiple handling steps, and hazardous fluorinating reagents. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure Na–F binary fluorination, so the severity estimate is mainly CS-based.

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary fluoride precursors show good reactivity in CS3 and CS5, and high pressure can enable dense fluoride structures in CS1 and CS4. The kinetic penalty is the lack of a structurally matched pyrite NaF₂ analogue in CS/SS/TS, implying complete reconstruction from NaF plus fluorinating agent [Reasonable Inference]. This gives moderate kinetics despite severe conditions.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from avoiding GPa pressure and very high temperature, unlike CS1 and CS4. It still requires sealed/vacuum or inert handling, analogous to CS5, and anhydrous controlled conditions, as emphasized in CS3. Toxic/air-sensitive fluorinating agents are [Reasonable Inference] for NaF₂ and impose a significant safety and containment deduction.

#### F3 — Route 2

The precursor activity is moderately high because NaF reacts with binary transition-metal fluorides in CS3 and CS5 under dry conditions. Diffusion is acceptable at 150–300 °C only if the fluorinating agent is highly reactive [Reasonable Inference]; otherwise NaF may remain kinetically inert. The largest uncertainty is structural reconstruction, because no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates binary pyrite-type NaF₂.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses the NaF-flux/quench precedent from CS2 and, optionally, high-pressure flux logic from CS1. The ambient sealed variant avoids the GPa penalty, but the high operating temperature near molten NaF, sealed fluorinating chemistry, volatile/reactive components, and multiple processing steps make it challenging. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for NaF-flux stabilization of this target.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux synthesis receives the best kinetic score because CS2 directly shows a NaF-rich flux/melt-quench route, and flux growth is also effective for a high-pressure fluoride polymorph in CS1. A molten flux should enhance ion transport [Reasonable Inference], improving diffusion relative to purely solid-state fluorination. The structural-reconstruction score remains limited because the target pyrite topology is unsupported by CS/SS/TS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Na, so cation-size disorder is absent [Reasonable Inference]. The pyrite-type Pa-3 AB₂ framework is expected to have a small atom count below the >40-atom amorphization trigger [Reasonable Inference], and the target description reports corner-sharing NaF₆ octahedra rather than mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity. CS4 and CS5 include more complex mixed connectivity, but those are chemically related ternary fluorides rather than the target structure.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable by its reported negative E_hull, so it appears ground-state-like in the calculation rather than metastable by the scoring rubric. The chemical analogues support fluoride synthesis broadly, especially NaF-based solid-state reactions, sealed ampoule reactions, NaF flux/quench methods, and high-pressure fluoride stabilization (CS1–CS5). The most important enabling factor is the availability of NaF-rich flux or binary-fluoride reaction chemistry that can provide high fluoride mobility and dry conditions, as shown in CS2, CS3, and CS5. The most important limiting factor is the absence of any direct CS/SS/TS precedent for binary NaF₂ or pyrite-type NaF₂, making the required fluorine chemical potential and charge-balance chemistry uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the material is synthetically plausible on thermodynamic grounds but experimentally high-risk because phase identity may depend on trapping an unusual Na–F bonding state.
