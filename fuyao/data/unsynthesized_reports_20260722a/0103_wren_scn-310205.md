### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.002116 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull, so it receives a high stability score. Synthesized analogues in CS and TS often have E_hull near 0, including CS-3, TS-4, and TS-5, supporting that low-hull chloride phases are experimentally accessible. The remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee resistance to hydrolysis, volatilization, or kinetic decomposition during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures similar to complex chloride ampoule syntheses in CS-4/5 and lower than CS-3. The main severity penalties are sealed/vacuum operation and moisture-sensitive/volatile metal chlorides, consistent with sealed-tube and glovebox requirements in SS-1, SS-5, and TS-5. Uncertainty remains because the vapor pressure and decomposition behavior of ReCl₅ under the proposed conditions are not directly supplied by CS/SS/TS; that part is [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chlorides provide much higher precursor activity than refractory oxides/metals, supported by chloride reactions in CS-3/4/5 and TS-5. The target shares KCl₁₂ and MCl₆ motifs with K₂MoCl₆ in TS-5, which improves structural reconstruction likelihood. However, solid diffusion and stoichiometric reordering into KReCl₆ may be slower than in a flux, and CS-3/TS-4 show that annealing or phase control can be time-consuming.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has mild thermal severity because TS-5 forms K₂MoCl₆ at 150 °C in a sealed tube. It is penalized for sealed-tube chemistry, volatile ICl, and air-sensitive chlorides, all consistent with TS-5 and the broader chloride handling requirements in SS-1/SS-5. The uncertainty is whether ICl will stabilize the correct Re(V) chloride chemistry rather than overchlorinating or dissolving/transporting Re species away [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The ICl-assisted route should have strong chemical activity because TS-5 reports KCl + MoCl₅ + ICl producing K₂MoCl₆ at low temperature. The KCl₁₂/MCl₆ structural analogy to the target is strong, but the K:Re ratio and oxidation state differ from K₂MoCl₆, lowering the structural score. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether low-temperature metathesis provides enough mobility for the lower-symmetry monoclinic KReCl₆ arrangement [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 requires sealed ampoules, volatile chlorides, and multi-step flux handling, so its condition severity is lower than Routes 1–2. Flux and melt routes in CS-1, CS-4, CS-5, and SS-5 routinely use evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, and controlled temperature gradients. The temperature remains moderate, but the handling burden and post-growth separation increase practical difficulty.

#### F3 — Route 3

Route 3 scores best kinetically because molten chloride flux provides liquid-phase transport, which bypasses slow diffusion expected for Re-containing solids [Reasonable Inference]. Flux/melt success for complex chlorides is supported by CS-4/5 and TS-4, while SS-5 shows temperature-gradient chloride transport can generate complex halide crystals. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: polymorphism and competing phases are documented in CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, and TS-1.

#### F2 — Route 4

Direct chlorination is thermally feasible by analogy to WCl₆ and OsCl₄ formation in SS-2/TS-2 and SS-4/TS-3. It is penalized for chlorine pressure, sealed apparatus, volatile chloride species, and toxic reagents. CS provides insufficient direct support for applying elemental chlorination to KReCl₆, so CS is neutral for this route-specific severity assessment.

#### F3 — Route 4

Route 4 has the weakest kinetics because Re metal is refractory and must be fully chlorinated and incorporated into a K chlorometallate lattice [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2/TS-2 and SS-4/TS-3 show that direct chlorination can form binary metal chlorides, but not necessarily ternary K–Re–Cl phases. The main uncertainty is whether volatile Re chloride transport will react with KCl productively or instead form separate Re chloride phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The large size/coordination contrast between KCl₁₂ and ReCl₆ polyhedra triggers the ionic-size criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target also has mixed corner, edge, and face sharing, explicitly stated in the target description and echoed by mixed-connectivity chlorides such as CS-3 and TS-5. The atom-count trigger is not supported by direct CS/SS/TS evidence and is treated as neutral/false based on likely conventional-cell size [Reasonable Inference], leaving a medium amorphization/defect risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KReCl₆ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is essentially zero, and comparable low-hull chlorides in CS/TS are experimentally synthesized. The best structural support comes from TS-5, where K₂MoCl₆ contains KCl₁₂ cuboctahedra and MCl₆ octahedra, although the target differs in K:Re ratio and oxidation state. The strongest enabling factor is chloride chemistry: volatile/reactive metal chlorides and molten chloride media can form complex chlorometallates at moderate temperatures, as shown in CS-4/5, SS-5, and TS-5. The main limiting factor is phase and oxidation-state control for Re(V), especially under sealed conditions where Re chlorides may volatilize or form competing binary/alkali chlorides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with molten chloride/self-flux growth the most forgiving route.

| 1 | All | Moisture/air sensitivity of high-valent metal chlorides and products | High | Load and grind in glovebox; use evacuated sealed ampoules, as in SS-1, SS-5, CS-4/5, and TS-5. |

| 3 | All | Competing phases such as K₂ReCl₆, ReCl₄/ReCl₅, or KCl-rich products | High | Screen K:Re ratio and chlorine activity; phase-control sensitivity is documented for CS-3, CS-5, SS-1, and TS-1. |
