### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.018322143 eV/atom, which places it effectively on the convex hull and within the highest stability scoring bin. Thermodynamic analogues in TS include several near-hull or on-hull compounds, but TS examples are chemically unrelated to Ba–Re–O and provide insufficient evidence from TS for BaReO4-specific phase stability. CS is more useful because BaWO4 is on or near hull and is experimentally synthesized in the same BaMO4 structure type (CS-2, CS-3), while CsReO4 perrhenates are also near-hull/on-hull Re oxyanion salts (CS-4, CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits moderate-to-high ceramic temperatures from BaWO4, which is synthesized at 1473 K/1200 °C for 2 h (CS-2, CS-3). The target likely needs lower or comparable high-temperature treatment because Re oxides may be more volatile/reactive than WO3 [Reasonable Inference], while Re oxide sealed-tube chemistry in CS-1 uses 1000–1200 °C. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this Ba–Re–O ceramic route because their closest entries are chemically unrelated hydrides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so SS/TS are treated as neutral for this route-specific severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic support is structural: BaWO4 has the same I4₁/a BaMO4 topology, with BaO8 and tetrahedral WO4 units closely matching target BaO8/ReO4 (CS-2, CS-3). The limiting kinetic factor is solid-state diffusion from BaCO3/Re oxide powders, since the BaWO4 analogue requires 1473 K and the Re oxide analogue CS-1 requires long annealing. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for BaReO4 solid-state kinetics and are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because CS-1 directly uses evacuated sealed silica tubes, 1000–1200 °C, and 5–10 day anneals for rare-earth rhenium oxides. The sealed-tube requirement and Re redox precursor handling introduce added severity relative to open ceramic BaWO4 synthesis in CS-2/CS-3. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this specific Ba–Re perrhenate redox route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CS-1 supports the feasibility of metaperrhenate/metal redox chemistry, but also shows slow kinetics because long anneals of 5–10 days are used. The target structure is not represented in CS-1 because the Re sites there include octahedral and square-planar geometries rather than isolated tetrahedral ReO4 units, so structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable. SS and TS are insufficiently chemically relevant for this redox pathway.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from mild aqueous perrhenate chemistry in CS-4/CS-5 and precipitation/calcination precedent at 500 °C in SS-3/TS-3. The main severity deductions are the multistep precursor processing and Re/Ba salt toxicity, not high pressure or highly reactive atmospheres. The route remains partly extrapolative because none of CS/SS/TS directly reports solution synthesis of BaReO4 with Re(VI).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution or gel processing should give high precursor activity because soluble Ba and Re species can mix at molecular length scales [Reasonable Inference], and perrhenate salts crystallize readily from aqueous solution (CS-4, CS-5). SS-3/TS-3 support that oxyanion oxides can crystallize after precipitation and calcination at moderate temperature. The uncertainty is that Re(VII) perrhenate chemistry may preferentially form Ba(ReO4)2 rather than Re(VI) BaReO4 unless redox conditions are controlled [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple BaMO4 framework with isolated tetrahedral ReO4 units and BaO8 coordination, analogous to the simple BaWO4 topology in CS-2/CS-3. The main amorphization risk is the large Ba/Re cation-size and coordination contrast [Reasonable Inference], but the unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms and the target description does not show complex mixed edge/face-sharing networks. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for BaReO4 amorphization specifically, so they are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaReO4 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is slightly negative, effectively on the hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS: BaWO4 is an experimentally synthesized, isostructural BaMO4 zircon/scheelite analogue (CS-2, CS-3), and CsReO4 demonstrates that crystalline ReO4 oxyanion salts are accessible by mild aqueous chemistry (CS-4, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the simple ABO4 framework with isolated tetrahedral oxyanion units, which has strong precedent in BaWO4 (CS-2, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is Re oxidation-state control: BaReO4 requires formal Re(VI), while common perrhenate precursors in CS-4/CS-5 contain Re(VII), and CS-1 shows Re redox chemistry may require sealed high-temperature treatment. Overall, solution-derived precursor calcination is recommended because it maximizes mixing and lowers diffusion barriers while allowing post-synthesis redox tuning.
