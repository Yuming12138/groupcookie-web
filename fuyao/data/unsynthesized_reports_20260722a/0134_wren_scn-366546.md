### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.03532179 eV/atom, which is effectively on/below the convex hull and therefore receives the maximum stability score under the provided rubric. Several chemically related Ca/Sr–Pt intermetallics also have E_hull = 0.0, including Ca2MgPt2, Ca2Pt2Al, Ca2Pt2Ga, and SrMgPt, supporting the plausibility of stable alkaline-earth–Pt ternaries (CS1–CS4). SS and TS examples are thermodynamically stable or near-stable but chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient target-specific thermodynamic transfer signal and are treated neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 1000 °C heating, controlled slow cooling, and annealing in sealed Ta ampoules, which is directly supported by Ca2MgPt2 and related Ca–Pt analogues (CS1–CS3). It avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, but it requires Ar glovebox handling, sealed ampoules, evacuated silica protection, multiple thermal steps, and toxic/air-sensitive precursor precautions (CS1–CS3; [Reasonable Inference for Be toxicity and Ca air sensitivity]). The main uncertainty is whether Be incorporation requires a slightly different temperature or longer anneal than Mg, Al, or Ga analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is structural continuity: Ca2MgPt2 is isostructural with the target and has nearly identical Ca/Pt coordination topology (CS1; target structure). The kinetic limitation is that Pt is refractory and elemental powders/pieces react more slowly than salt/solution precursors [Reasonable Inference], but CS1–CS3 demonstrate that 1000 °C plus slow cooling/annealing is sufficient for related Ca–Pt frameworks. The Mg→Be substitution shortens the M–Pt bond from 2.66 Å to 2.36 Å, so local strain and ordering may require careful annealing [CS1 and target structure; Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is harsher because CS4 includes high-frequency or muffle-furnace synthesis of related intermetallics with temperatures up to 1800 K, and CS5 uses multi-step sealed Nb-tube annealing. The route requires controlled Ar atmosphere, refractory-metal tube welding, oxidation protection, and potentially repeated anneals or grinding, all of which increase operational severity (CS4, CS5). Be toxicity and Ca loss during melt processing are additional concerns [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Arc/high-frequency treatment can improve homogenization and overcome slow Pt diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS4 demonstrates this style of synthesis for alkaline-earth–Mg–Pt intermetallics. CS5 also shows that repeated annealing can convert initial phase mixtures into purer intermetallic products, supporting a kinetic mitigation strategy. The uncertainty is that melt processing may aggravate Ca volatility and composition drift relative to the gentler Ta-ampoule route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux/slow-cooling variant inherits sealed Ta ampoule and inert-atmosphere requirements from CS1–CS3, and may require temperatures slightly above 1000 °C if a liquid flux is sought [Reasonable Inference]. It also adds possible decanting/recovery complexity beyond the directly reported stoichiometric reactions in CS1–CS3. Because no explicit flux synthesis is given in CS/SS/TS for Ca2BePt2 analogues, uncertainty is higher and SS/TS are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A liquid/self-flux environment should improve mass transport and crystal growth relative to dry elemental reaction [Reasonable Inference]. The slow-cooling protocols in CS1–CS3 provide partial support, but they do not prove a true flux mechanism. Phase-width and phase-mixture issues noted for related Ca–Pt compounds in CS1 and CS3 make composition control the key kinetic and thermodynamic risk.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small-cell ordered intermetallic with simple Ca–Pt and Be–Pt coordination, so large-cell and mixed-polyhedral amorphization triggers are not strongly indicated by the target structure. The main trigger is the large Ca/Be cation size mismatch, which can increase local strain and disordering tendency [Reasonable Inference]. However, the existence of an ordered isostructural Ca2MgPt2 analogue strongly lowers concern that the framework is intrinsically glass-forming (CS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca2BePt2 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is negative, and the closest Ca–Pt analogues are reported at E_hull = 0.0 (target data; CS1–CS4). The synthesis case is strongly supported by the isostructural Ca2MgPt2 analogue, which was made by direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction under conditions directly transferable to Ca2BePt2 (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural and chemical match between Ca2MgPt2 and Ca2BePt2, especially the conserved Immm topology and Ca/Pt coordination (CS1; target structure). The most critical limiting factor is safe and stoichiometric incorporation of Be while preventing Ca oxidation/loss and competing Ca–Pt or Be–Pt phase formation [Reasonable Inference; sealed ampoule and phase-control concerns supported by CS1–CS3]. SS and TS do not materially improve the route recommendation because their top examples are chemically unrelated and therefore are neutral for this target.
