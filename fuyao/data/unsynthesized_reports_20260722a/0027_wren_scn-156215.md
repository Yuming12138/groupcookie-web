### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.045187587 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin by the provided rubric. (Target thermodynamic data) Chemically similar alkali tetraoxo salts Rb2WO4, Cs2MoO4, K2WO4, and Cs2CrO4 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0, supporting the general thermodynamic accessibility of A2MO4 oxo-salt frameworks. (CS-1 to CS-4) SS and TS are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient evidence for Rb–Re–O stability, so they are neutral at 50. (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5)

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperature around 900 K/627 °C and ambient air, directly matching Rb2WO4 synthesis. (CS-1) The main deductions are for multiple powder-processing steps and rhenium oxide toxicity/possible volatility, which are [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly stated in CS. No high pressure, H2/NH3/vacuum, or >1000 °C conditions are required by the analogue. (CS-1)

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable structurally because target Rb2ReO4 is essentially the Re analogue of C2/m Rb2WO4, with similar Rb coordination and tetrahedral MO4 units. (CS-1; target structural data) Solid-state diffusion may still be moderate because carbonate/oxide reactions rely on interdiffusion, although Rb2WO4 formation at 900 K indicates the oxyanion salt pathway is not prohibitively slow. (CS-1) The remaining uncertainty is Re oxide redox/volatility behavior under the same conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 remains below 1000 °C and uses ambient pressure, consistent with the Rb2WO4/K2WO4 solid-state analogues. (CS-1, CS-3) It is penalized more than Route 1 because ReO2 requires oxidation-state control to reach Re(VI), adding controlled-atmosphere sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Re oxide toxicity/volatility and repeated grinding are additional risks [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The carbonate/oxide framework is supported by CS-1 and CS-3, but ReO2 must both oxidize and reconstruct into isolated tetrahedral ReO4^2− units. (CS-1, CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]) This makes precursor activity and structural reconstruction less favorable than direct Re(VI) oxide use. The route is plausible but has greater risk of ReO2 residue, perrhenate side products, or mixed-valent phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 benefits from mild aqueous or hydrothermal conditions, and CS-4 demonstrates that alkali tetraoxo salts such as Cs2CrO4 can be grown by slow evaporation from aqueous solution. (CS-4) However, the target-specific Re(VI) solution chemistry is not shown in the data, so inert atmosphere/redox control is a likely requirement [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral. (SS-1 to SS-5; TS-1 to TS-5)

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Solution crystallization offers high precursor mixing and bypasses solid-state diffusion, consistent with the Cs2CrO4 evaporation analogue. (CS-4) The limitation is structural selectivity: CS-4 is Pnma Cs2CrO4, not C2/m Rb2ReO4, and no provided source confirms aqueous Rb2ReO4 crystallization. (CS-4) Thus kinetics may be good, but phase selectivity is uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a simple ionic oxo-salt with isolated ReO4 tetrahedra and Rb–O coordination, so extensive mixed polyhedral network frustration is not indicated. (Target structural data) One amorphization trigger is assigned for the large cation-size mismatch between Rb+ and Re6+ coordination environments [Reasonable Inference]. The structurally matched crystalline Rb2WO4 analogue supports low amorphization risk for the same framework. (CS-1)
