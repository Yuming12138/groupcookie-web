### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.017815 eV/atom from the target thermodynamic data, which is effectively a convex-hull-stable prediction. Chemically related pnictide analogues Sr(MgAs)₂, NaBeAs, KMgAs, and NaMgAs are all listed at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5), supporting the plausibility of stable alkali/alkaline-earth–tetrel/pnictide frameworks. TS entries are stable or near-stable, but they are chemically unrelated, so TS gives insufficient specific K–Be–As stability evidence and is neutral 50 for chemical transfer. Remaining uncertainty is computational: small negative hull values can reflect numerical tolerance rather than experimentally guaranteed ground-state formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate-to-high temperatures comparable to 660 °C for NaBeAs (CS2), ~902 °C for NaMgAs (CS5), and below or near the 950–1000 °C used for Sr(MgAs)₂ (CS1). It avoids external high pressure and H₂/NH₃/vacuum chemistry, but it requires sealed containment because chemically relevant analogues use Ta/Nb or sealed ampoule assemblies (CS2, CS4, CS5). The main deductions are for volatile sealed precursors and toxic/air-sensitive handling, especially K, As, and Be [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient useful severity guidance for this chemistry and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct elemental routes are validated for NaBeAs and KMgAs (CS2, CS4), but Be metal is diffusion-limited relative to alkali/arsenic components [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s BeAs₄ tetrahedral network is chemically supported by NaBeAs (CS2), and the P-3m1 A(BAs)₂ topology is supported by Sr(MgAs)₂ (CS1). However, complete ordering of K, Be, and As may require prolonged annealing, consistent with the multi-temperature treatment for KMgAs (CS4). SS/TS provide insufficient transferable kinetic evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The binary arsenide route is based mainly on Na + NaAs + Mg in sealed Nb at 1175 K for NaMgAs (CS5), transferred by analogy to K–As and Be precursors [Reasonable Inference]. It lowers uncontrolled elemental reactivity but adds precursor preparation steps, so it receives a multistep penalty. The route still requires sealed containment and inert handling, as supported by CS2, CS4, and CS5. SS/TS provide insufficient K–Be–As precursor evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Using K–As or Be–As binaries should increase precursor activity and reduce the need to form every Be–As contact from elemental powders [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 directly supports alkali arsenide precursor use, while CS2 supports Be–As ternary formation. The P-3m1 structural target is strongly suggested by Sr(MgAs)₂ (CS1), reducing structural-reconstruction risk. Uncertainty remains because K–As binaries may introduce competing K-rich arsenides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The flux route uses the same sealed-container logic as CS2, CS4, and CS5 but adds flux handling and separation, giving a multistep penalty. It avoids >1000 °C operation in the proposed schedule, using the 660–902 °C chemically related window from CS2 and CS5 and the 800 °C annealing precedent from CS4. The largest hazards are K–As flux reactivity, As volatility, and Be toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient specific evidence for flux synthesis and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A K–As-rich flux should improve mass transport and crystallization relative to dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS5’s use of NaAs supports alkali arsenide activity, while CS2 demonstrates Be–As phase formation at relatively moderate temperature. The structural target is not directly flux-grown in CS/SS/TS, but the existence of a P-3m1 Sr(MgAs)₂ analogue (CS1) supports a realistic crystallographic endpoint. The score is high but not maximal because flux composition windows may be narrow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains highly dissimilar K and Be cations in very different coordination environments, which triggers the ionic-radius/coordination mismatch check [Reasonable Inference; target structural data]. The target unit cell is inferred to be small from the P-3m1 formula/site description, so the >40 atom check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target has mixed edge- and corner-sharing BeAs₄ tetrahedra, but no face sharing is described, so the strict vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. CS1 and CS4 contain related edge/corner-sharing pnictide tetrahedral networks, indicating such connectivity is not inherently prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(BeAs)₂ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is −0.017815 eV/atom, and multiple chemically similar arsenides are on the hull (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). The strongest analogue support comes from CS1, which has the same trigonal P-3m1 A(BAs)₂ structural family, and from CS2, which confirms Be–As ternary arsenide chemistry. The most critical enabling factor is sealed Ta/Nb ampoule synthesis, which preserves volatile K/As components and is repeatedly used for related alkali/alkaline-earth arsenides (CS2, CS4, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is practical handling and stoichiometry control involving K volatility, As toxicity/volatility, and Be toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS entries are not chemically transferable enough to guide specific synthesis, so they are treated as neutral evidence rather than contradictory evidence.
