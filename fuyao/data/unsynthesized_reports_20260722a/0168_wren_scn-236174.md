### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has exact reported E_hull = −0.054459161 eV/atom, which is effectively on or below the computed convex hull and falls in the highest stability category. This strongly favors synthesizability because stable or near-stable intermetallics are usually accessible by equilibrium or near-equilibrium high-temperature methods [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides mixed thermodynamic evidence: some chemically similar compounds are on hull, including Ta₃Ru₅B₂ and Nb₃Ru₅B₂ (CS-2, CS-5), while U₂Cr₃Si and Np(CrGe)₂ have positive E_hull values in the data (CS-1, CS-3). SS/TS are insufficiently chemically specific for Ta₂Cr₃Ge thermodynamics and are treated as neutral except for showing that low-hull materials can have diverse synthesis routes.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 requires arc melting under Ar, which is well supported by Ta-, Nb-, and Cr/Ge-containing chemically similar intermetallics (CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). The severity score is reduced because arc melting reaches very high temperatures and because repeated remelting plus post-annealing make it a multi-step process [Reasonable Inference; CS-2, CS-5]. The route does not require high pressure, and Ar melting is standard for intermetallic synthesis rather than a reactive atmosphere (CS-2, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly Ge loss during melting, which is inferred from Ge volatility at high temperature rather than explicitly reported in CS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate because elemental Ta and Cr are refractory and slow to homogenize in the solid state [Reasonable Inference]. Arc melting mitigates diffusion limitations by producing a liquid or partially liquid alloy, consistent with CS-2 and CS-5 where short arc-melting/remelting cycles form refractory intermetallics. The exact U₂Cr₃Si structure analogue supports the target ordering topology (CS-1), but ordering Ta, Cr, and Ge into the P6₃/mmc phase may still need annealing [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F3 is below average despite good thermodynamic stability.

#### F2 — Route 2

The sealed-tube route is milder in peak temperature than arc melting if limited to 850–950 °C, consistent with the 750–850 °C sealed-tube heating reported for the closest analogue U₂Cr₃Si (CS-1). The score is reduced because sealed/vacuum ampoules and long multi-step grinding/annealing are needed [Reasonable Inference; CS-1, CS-4]. No high pressure is supported by CS, and no reactive gas is required. Uncertainty is high because Ta and Cr may not diffuse sufficiently at these temperatures [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid-state kinetics are the main weakness: Ta and Cr are refractory, and CS-1 reports 700 h heating for the closest structural analogue, indicating slow equilibration. The structural reconstruction score is helped by the U₂Cr₃Si analogue having the same P6₃/mmc topology and similar Cr-centered cuboctahedra (CS-1). However, forming Ta₂Cr₃Ge directly from elemental powders requires long-range interdiffusion and ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore Route 2 is plausible but less attractive than melt-assisted processing.

#### F2 — Route 3

Melt growth/Czochralski-type processing is supported by SS-2/TS-2 for refractory metallic intermetallic crystals, but it is more equipment-intensive than a normal arc-melted polycrystalline synthesis. The score is reduced for very high temperature, multi-step processing, and possible Ge/Cr loss during growth [Reasonable Inference]. It does not require high pressure, and Ar atmosphere is supported by SS-2/TS-2. SS/TS are less chemically relevant than CS, so the route is plausible but not the primary recommendation.

#### F3 — Route 3

Melt growth improves diffusion relative to solid-state sintering because the feed is molten or partially molten [Reasonable Inference]. However, composition control and segregation during crystal pulling may be more difficult than in a simple arc-melted button [Reasonable Inference]. SS-2/TS-2 support melt growth of metallic intermetallics but do not match the Ta–Cr–Ge chemistry or P6₃/mmc structure. The kinetic score is therefore similar to Route 1 but with greater uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains atoms of different sizes and has mixed cuboctahedral connectivity with corner-, edge-, and face-sharing motifs, which triggers two amorphization/ordering-risk checks [Reasonable Inference; CS-1 and target description]. The unit cell is not large, and the close-packed intermetallic topology argues against a severe glass-forming tendency [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 supports that the same topology can crystallize as an ordered P6₃/mmc phase. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ta₂Cr₃Ge competes with binary Ta–Ge, Cr–Ge, or Ta–Cr phases during cooling [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ta₂Cr₃Ge is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and therefore effectively on the convex hull. The strongest support comes from CS-1, which gives an exact P6₃/mmc U₂Cr₃Si structural analogue with the same Cr-centered cuboctahedral framework, and from CS-2/CS-3/CS-5, which show that related refractory intermetallics can be synthesized by arc melting under Ar. SS/TS evidence is weaker for chemistry but supports melt/anneal processing of metallic intermetallics in at least one relevant case (SS-2, TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the combination of strong computed thermodynamic stability and a known same-structure analogue. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/stoichiometric control during high-temperature processing, especially homogenizing refractory Ta/Cr while preventing Ge loss [Reasonable Inference].
