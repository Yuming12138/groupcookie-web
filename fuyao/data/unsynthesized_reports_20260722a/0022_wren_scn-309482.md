### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.136897 eV/atom, which falls in the most favorable stability bin and motivates the high F1 score. Chemically similar SrCu₂O₃ analogues have low hull energies of 0.0169–0.0271 eV/atom, supporting the broader stability of Sr–Cu–O frameworks near this composition (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The negative hull value may reflect computational/reference-set inconsistency, but it is still a strong thermodynamic signal [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS entries are chemically unrelated to Sr–Cu–O, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS beyond neutral thermodynamic analogy.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-pressure oxidizing solid-state synthesis

The condition severity is substantial because the closest Sr–Cu–O analogues require 3–5 GPa and about 1050 °C or even melt-growth temperatures up to 1773 K (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Under the requested F2 rubric, 4–5 GPa does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty, but the >1000 °C and sealed oxidizer requirements reduce the score [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are moderately favorable because CS-1 and CS-3 obtained SrCu₂O₃ in only 30 min at 1050 °C/4 GPa, but forming SrCu₂O₄ requires oxygen insertion and likely Cu oxidation beyond the analogue [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Oxidizing thin-film deposition

CS-5 directly supports thin-film stabilization of a metastable alkaline-earth copper oxide, BaCu₃O₄, at 800–835 °C under controlled oxygen partial pressure with oxygen annealing. This avoids bulk multi-GPa conditions, giving relatively good F2, but metalorganic delivery or PLD target preparation introduces equipment and precursor complexity [Reasonable Inference; CS-5]. Kinetics are favorable for epitaxial crystallization because CS-5 demonstrates direct film growth, but the target’s Sr:Cu:O stoichiometry is likely narrow and oxygen-sensitive [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-oxygen-pressure solid-state synthesis

CS-4 shows that CaCu₂O₃ can be prepared by conventional solid-state reaction at 1100 °C with repeated heating and grinding in oxidizing atmosphere. This supports alkaline-earth copper oxide formation from simple oxides, but it does not demonstrate the more oxidized SrCu₂O₄ composition [Reasonable Inference]. The route is penalized for high temperature, controlled oxygen, sealed oxidizer risk, and multistep ceramic processing, while kinetics are limited by solid-state diffusion and likely competition with SrCu₂O₃/SrCuO₂/CuO [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines large 8-coordinate Sr with small square-planar Cu, which triggers the ionic-radius-mismatch check [Reasonable Inference; target structure]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing OSr₂Cu₂ tetrahedra but does not report simultaneous face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered. No CS/SS/TS source provides a reliable target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as not triggered with residual uncertainty.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically favorable or at least very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative, although the unusual negative value should be interpreted cautiously [Reasonable Inference]. Chemically similar Sr–Cu–O phases are well supported by CS data, especially high-pressure SrCu₂O₃ syntheses at 3–5 GPa and 1050 °C or melt conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The strongest enabling factor is the existence of closely related square-planar Sr–Cu–O frameworks that can be synthesized and quenched from high pressure (CS-1, CS-3). The strongest limiting factor is oxygen stoichiometry: SrCu₂O₄ requires a more oxidized Cu/O state than SrCu₂O₃, so oxygen chemical potential and competing reduced phases are critical [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical path is likely epitaxial thin-film stabilization under high oxygen activity, based on the BaCu₃O₄ MOCVD precedent (CS-5).
