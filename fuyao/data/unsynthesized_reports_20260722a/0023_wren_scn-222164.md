### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.128278747 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and giving F1 = 100. CS analogues include oxides with E_hull values from 0 to ~0.10 eV/atom, showing that chemically similar oxide families can be close to the convex hull (CS-1–CS-5). TS entries are also near-hull or on-hull, but they are chemically heterogeneous, so TS supports general thermodynamic plausibility more than Ba–Ag–O specificity (TS-1–TS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS for target-specific Ba–Ag–O thermodynamic stability; SS is treated as neutral for this subfactor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 benefits from CS ceramic precedent: Nd2CuO4/La2CuO4 analogues are made by heating oxide mixtures in air around 950–1100 °C with grinding (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The target route must be lowered to 350–550 °C and made more oxidizing because Ag oxides are less thermally robust than Cu oxides [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is penalized for controlled O2/sealed variants and sensitive oxidizing precursors [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is moderate because oxide diffusion and structural reconstruction are likely rate-limiting, consistent with repeated grinding in CS ceramic methods (CS-1, CS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by nitrate-based wet chemistry in CS-4 and AgNO3 precipitation/calcination in SS-3/TS-3. F2 is moderate because the route involves multiple processing steps and controlled oxidative calcination but avoids high temperature, high pressure, H2, NH3, and vacuum [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is relatively strong because solution precursors reduce diffusion length, a benefit inferred from nitrate/hydrothermal and precipitation routes (CS-4, SS-3/TS-3). The uncertainty is whether calcination will produce crystalline Ba(AgO2)2 instead of Ag metal, Ag2O, BaO2, or mixed Ba–Ag oxide impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 receives the best F2 because CS-4 demonstrates hydrothermal oxide synthesis at 60 °C and 35 bar using nitrate precursors and H2O2, avoiding severe temperature or pressure. The proposed Ba–Ag–O hydrothermal route is an extrapolation, but it directly addresses Ag–O thermal instability by crystallizing at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is high because solution-mediated transport and oxidizing aqueous chemistry should provide high precursor activity and fast ion transport [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the tetragonal Ba(AgO2)2 topology nucleates under hydrothermal conditions rather than forming simpler Ag oxides or Ba peroxide phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is motivated by CS-5, where La2CuO4+δ is oxidized in KMnO4 solution at 50 °C for 7 d, showing that low-temperature oxidation can change oxide oxygen content. High-pressure O2 annealing is more demanding than the CS-5 aqueous oxidation and thus receives a lower F2 [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is moderate because oxygen insertion may be feasible, but cation rearrangement at 250–450 °C could be slow unless a preorganized Ba–Ag–O precursor is used [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 also warns that oxidation can cause phase separation and sensitivity to conditions, which lowers confidence in phase control.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Ba/Ag size and coordination contrast, so one amorphization/complexity trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference]. The supplied target description reports edge- and corner-sharing OBa2Ag2 tetrahedra, but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity trigger as defined is not fully met. The formula is small, and there is no evidence that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference]. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, giving F4 = 80.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba(AgO2)2 is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable because the supplied E_hull is negative, so it should be treated as an on-hull or near-ground-state candidate. The CS evidence supports oxide synthesis in related square-planar transition-metal oxide chemistries, especially ceramic rare-earth cuprates (CS-1–CS-4), but the support is indirect because none of the references is a Ba–Ag–O phase. SS/TS provide useful wet-chemical AgNO3 precipitation/calcination precedent through AgCoPO4 (SS-3/TS-3), but otherwise several SS/TS analogues are chemically irrelevant and should be considered neutral where not directly applicable. The most critical enabling factor is the ability to maintain high oxygen chemical potential at low temperature to stabilize square-planar Ag–O units [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is competing reduction/decomposition of Ag oxides or formation of simpler Ba peroxide/silver oxide phases rather than the ordered tetragonal Ba(AgO2)2 network [Reasonable Inference].
