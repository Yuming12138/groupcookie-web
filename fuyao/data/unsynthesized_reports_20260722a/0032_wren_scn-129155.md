### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has a reported E_hull of −0.0863 eV/atom, so it receives 95/100 under the required stability rubric. Chemically similar KCrF₆, CaCrF₆, and SrCrF₆ are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, indicating that related high-fluoride MF₆ frameworks can be thermodynamically stable (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). However, SS/TS entries are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for KNF₆-specific thermodynamic validation; those sources are neutral at 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and GPa pressure because KCrF₆ is made at ambient temperature (CS-1). The score is reduced because CS-1 requires anhydrous HF, F₂ atmosphere, UV irradiation, FEP reaction vessels, PTFE valves, and 10–20 d reaction time. Toxicity, volatility, and air/moisture sensitivity are significant because HF/F₂ chemistry is explicitly used in CS-1, while nitrogen fluoride volatility is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Kinetically, Route 1 is favorable relative to solid-state ceramic synthesis because the KCrF₆ analogue forms photochemically in anhydrous HF at ambient temperature (CS-1). The same R-3 K–MF₆ topology strongly supports structural transfer from KCrF₆ to the target (CS-1). The uncertainty is the central-atom replacement: CrF₆ octahedra are chemically credible in CS-1, whereas NF₆ octahedra are not directly supported by CS/SS/TS and therefore penalized by [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 does not require >1000 °C or >5 GPa because CaCrF₆/SrCrF₆ analogues are synthesized at up to 300 °C and up to 300 atm (CS-2, CS-3). It remains severe because CS-2/CS-3 use Ar-diluted F₂ under pressure in Monell autoclaves, which is hazardous and specialized. Phase control difficulties and moisture sensitivity are explicitly noted for related chromium fluoride products in CS-2/CS-3.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-pressure F₂ provides strong fluorinating activity, and CaCrF₆/SrCrF₆ formation at 300 °C supports moderately favorable kinetics for high-fluoride framework formation (CS-2, CS-3). However, Route 2 is less directly matched to the target than Route 1 because the exact KCrF₆ analogue was not reported by this autoclave method in the provided data (CS-1 vs CS-2/CS-3). The nitrogen source and possible decomposition pathways remain unvalidated by CS/SS/TS and are treated as [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is designed as a low-temperature route, so it avoids high-temperature and GPa penalties by [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires reactive fluorinating conditions analogous to the F₂ chemistry in CS-1/CS-2/CS-3. Its severity remains high because the likely nitrogen fluoride precursors are volatile/reactive by [Reasonable Inference], and no CS/SS/TS source demonstrates a benign precursor set.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

A preformed nitrogen-fluoride intermediate could reduce diffusion barriers by [Reasonable Inference], but there is no direct CS/SS/TS evidence that such an intermediate can assemble into the R-3 KNF₆ network. The structural-reconstruction score is therefore low despite potentially high precursor activity. This route is best viewed as a speculative rescue path if Route 1 fails.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed corner/edge/face polyhedral connectivity, and this connectivity is also present in KCrF₆ (CS-1), so one amorphization trigger is clearly active. The K versus N cation size mismatch is likely very large by [Reasonable Inference], giving a second trigger. The unit-cell atom count is not shown to exceed 40 by CS/SS/TS, so that trigger is not applied; residual uncertainty remains because the exact crystallographic cell contents were not provided.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically stable by the supplied negative E_hull, but chemically it may behave as a metastable or nonclassical fluoride because no CS/SS/TS source validates an octahedral NF₆ unit. The strongest synthesis support is the exact structural and chemical analogue KCrF₆, which is R-3 and has nearly identical K coordination and polyhedral connectivity (CS-1). The most critical enabling factor is aggressive low-temperature fluorination in anhydrous HF/F₂ under UV, because this exact strategy forms KCrF₆ without high-temperature diffusion (CS-1). The most critical limiting factor is the uncertain nitrogen fluoride chemistry, especially whether an extended NF₆ octahedral framework can exist without decomposition or formation of simpler KF/NFₓ phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues do not meaningfully support KNF₆-specific synthesis and are treated as neutral evidence.
