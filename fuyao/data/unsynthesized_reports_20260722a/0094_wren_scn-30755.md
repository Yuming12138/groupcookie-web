### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.043788821 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin. CS analogues Rb₂TeCl₆, K₂TeCl₆, Rb₂SeCl₆, Rb₂PdCl₆, and Cs₂GeCl₆ all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing that related alkali chlorometallates can be stable crystalline compounds (CS-1 to CS-5). SS/TS provide stable or near-stable examples, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so their thermodynamic transferability to Rb₃Sb₂Cl₉ is insufficient and neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild because CS analogues form by precipitation from aqueous HCl or concentrated aqueous chloride solutions at room temperature to modest drying temperatures (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). No high pressure, furnace temperature, NH₃/H₂ atmosphere, or sealed ampoule is required in the CS-supported precipitation routes (CS-1 to CS-5). The main deduction is for toxic/corrosive SbCl₃/HCl handling, which is target-specific [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics are favorable because solution mixing provides high precursor activity and bypasses solid-state diffusion, consistent with CS precipitation routes (CS-1 to CS-5). Structural assembly is plausible because the target and CS analogues share large alkali–chloride cuboctahedra and main-group chloride octahedra (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). Uncertainty remains because CS analogues are mostly A₂MCl₆ with tetravalent M, whereas Rb₃Sb₂Cl₉ is an Sb(III) A₃B₂Cl₉ phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature because binary halides can react at low temperature relative to oxides [Reasonable Inference], and CS analogues do not require high-temperature synthesis (CS-1 to CS-5). The route is penalized for sealed/vacuum or inert handling and volatile SbCl₃ retention, which are not required in the CS solution routes but are likely necessary for dry solid-state stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS high-pressure or high-temperature routes are not chemically relevant and do not directly justify this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Kinetics should be moderate-to-good because halides are softer and more mobile than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and CS analogues demonstrate ready chlorometallate formation from chloride precursors (CS-1 to CS-5). The probable presence of molten/sublimed SbCl₃ at 180–250 °C could accelerate transport [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that a dry solid-state route is not directly demonstrated by CS, and formation of the layered/trigonal Rb₃Sb₂Cl₉ arrangement may require ordering beyond simple salt metathesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses moderate solvothermal temperatures and autogenous pressure rather than the extreme high-pressure ammonia conditions seen in SS-1/TS-1. The chemistry is still closer to CS because it uses HCl/chloride solution media (CS-1, CS-5). Deductions are applied for sealed volatile/corrosive HCl/SbCl₃ handling and toxic precursor risk [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solvothermal transport should maintain high precursor activity and rapid ion transport, extending the favorable solution chemistry shown in CS precipitation routes (CS-1 to CS-5). Slow cooling in a sealed HCl-rich medium may improve crystal growth relative to open precipitation [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that neither CS nor SS/TS provides a direct Rb₃Sb₂Cl₉ solvothermal example.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation coordination environments, with Rb in 12-coordinate RbCl₁₂ cuboctahedra and Sb in SbCl₆ octahedra, indicating a large cation-size/coordination mismatch that triggers C1. The target formula has only 14 atoms per formula unit, and the provided description does not show a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered. The target has corner- and face-sharing motifs but not explicit edge-sharing coexistence, so the strict C3 criterion is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₃Sb₂Cl₉ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is negative/within numerical hull tolerance. The CS analogues strongly support synthesis in the broad sense because multiple alkali chlorometallates with alkali–Cl cuboctahedra and MCl₆ octahedra form readily by mild chloride/HCl precipitation routes (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues do not provide useful chemical transfer to Rb–Sb–Cl, so they are treated as neutral rather than negative evidence. The most critical enabling factor is the high solubility/reactivity of chloride precursors in strongly acidic chloride media, which supports rapid solution assembly of the chlorometallate lattice (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Sb(III) chloride speciation and stoichiometry against hydrolysis or volatile SbCl₃ loss [Reasonable Inference].
