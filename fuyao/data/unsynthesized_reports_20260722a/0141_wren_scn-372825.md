### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.035215448 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and therefore merits a very high thermodynamic score. Several TS examples are also near-hull or hull-stable, but the TS set is chemically unrelated to Na–Ga–Pt and provides insufficient composition-specific signal for this target. The remaining uncertainty is whether the negative hull value reflects true ground-state ordering or small computational/phase-diagram uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state annealing

CS-4 supports sealed Ar ampoule synthesis at 650–900 °C for Li–Pt–In/Ga phases, and CS-3 supports Ta crucibles, quartz ampoules, slow cooling, and 700 °C tempering. The route avoids extreme pressure and avoids H₂/NH₃/vacuum conditions, but Na volatility and air sensitivity impose sealed handling penalties [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct signal for Na–Ga–Pt intermetallic conditions and are treated as neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube solid-state annealing

The kinetic limitation is Pt diffusion from dense metallic precursors, because CS-3/CS-4 use elemental metals rather than reactive solution precursors. CS-4 demonstrates related Pt-containing intermetallics can form below Pt’s melting point, which partly offsets the diffusion penalty. However, CS-5’s 4-week reaction time for LiPd₂Tl indicates slow ordering may be relevant in alkali–noble-metal–p-block intermetallics.

#### F2 — Route 2: Arc melting + annealing

CS-1 and CS-2 strongly support arc melting under Ar as a viable method for chemically similar metallic intermetallics, and SS-2/TS-2 also report arc melting plus ~1000 °C annealing. The high temperature of arc/induction melting and the need for secondary sealed Na incorporation make this route more severe [Reasonable Inference]. Direct arc melting with Na is not favored because Na loss is likely [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Arc melting + annealing

Arc melting improves homogenization of Pt–Ga mixtures, as supported by CS-1/CS-2 for complex metal systems. The main kinetic advantage is bypassing solid-state Pt diffusion by forming a prealloy [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is that the final Na-containing ternary phase must still form during annealing, and CS-1/CS-2 note phase-control issues and incongruent melting in some related intermetallics.

#### F2 — Route 3: Na/Ga-rich flux

CS-4 supports sealed 650–900 °C synthesis of related Li–Pt–Ga/In intermetallics, and CS-5 supports long-duration 700 °C alkali–noble-metal–p-block intermetallic synthesis. The flux route remains moderate in severity because it requires sealed Na handling, flux separation, and air-sensitive precursor manipulation [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids >1000 °C bulk melting and high pressure, which improves its F2 score relative to Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 3: Na/Ga-rich flux

The flux route scores best kinetically because a liquid Na/Ga-rich medium should enhance mass transport and Pt contact compared with dry powders [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4’s successful Li–Pt–Ga/In formation at 650–900 °C supports the feasibility of forming Pt–p-block intermetallic frameworks at moderate temperatures. The uncertainty is whether Pt dissolves sufficiently in Ga/Na flux and whether the P6/mmm NaGa₂Pt₃ ordering window is narrow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a simple reported P6/mmm structure and only one Na, one Pt, and one Ga crystallographic environment, which lowers amorphization risk. The large size mismatch between Na and the Pt/Ga sublattice triggers one amorphization check, consistent with the long Na–Pt/Na–Ga contacts versus shorter Pt–Ga contacts in the target structure. SS/TS do not provide useful target-specific amorphization evidence, so they are neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaGa₂Pt₃ is likely close to a ground-state phase because the target has a reported E_hull of −0.035215448 eV/atom. The chemical analogues support synthesis more strongly than the structural or thermodynamic analogues, because CS-3/CS-4/CS-5 include alkali–noble-metal–p-block intermetallics made under Ar, whereas SS/TS are mostly unrelated chemical classes. The most critical enabling factor is the strong thermodynamic stability combined with known sealed-ampoule formation of Li–Pt–Ga/In-type intermetallics. The most critical limiting factor is control of Na activity and volatility during high-temperature reaction. A Na/Ga-rich sealed flux route best balances transport, stoichiometry control, and moderate temperature.
