### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.0153 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull and therefore strongly favorable for synthesis. Several chemically similar Mn intermetallics are reported at E_hull = 0.0, including Mn₂VAl, ZrMn₆Sn₆, YMn₆Sn₆, and ScMn₆Sn₆ (CS). SS and TS contain near-hull examples but are chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient evidence for Zr–Mn–Al thermodynamic transferability and are neutral at 50.

**F2 — Route 1, arc melting.**  
The main severity comes from very high arc-melting temperature and the need for inert/vacuum conditions; Mn₂VAl was arc melted in purified Ar and then vacuum annealed (CS). No high pressure is required, and the equipment is standard for intermetallic synthesis (CS). The uncertainty is Mn loss during melting and whether the target orders cleanly during annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1, arc melting.**  
Arc melting improves kinetics by creating a liquid alloy, which reduces the slow solid-state diffusion expected for Zr-rich systems [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS notes that phase control in related Mn–Al Heusler systems can be difficult, with some compositions failing to form single phase (CS). The score is therefore moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule solid-state annealing.**  
CS solid-state analogues are made at moderate temperatures of 900–973 K in sealed Ar or vacuum ampoules, so thermal severity is lower than arc melting (CS). The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, multiple processing steps, and air-sensitive fine metal powders [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether Zr₂Mn₃Al forms before competing binaries consume the reactants [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2, sealed-ampoule solid-state annealing.**  
Elemental metal powders have lower precursor activity than salts or molecular precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues require grinding, recompacting, and long annealing for homogenization, implying slow kinetics (CS). Al may provide some transient liquid assistance above 660 °C, but this is an extrapolation and not directly shown in CS for Zr₂Mn₃Al [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3, arc-melt plus ordering anneal.**  
This route retains the high-temperature and vacuum/inert-atmosphere penalties of arc melting and adds a third ordering step [Reasonable Inference]. Its severity is therefore slightly worse than Route 1. CS supports both arc melting plus annealing and long annealing for Mn intermetallics (CS).

**F3 — Route 3, arc-melt plus ordering anneal.**  
The kinetics are better than pure solid-state synthesis because the precursor is already melt-homogenized [Reasonable Inference]. If the target forms by ordering from a disordered Laves-like parent, structural reconstruction is only partial [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether such a disordered parent phase actually exists for Zr₂Mn₃Al under accessible annealing conditions [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has mixed corner/edge/face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, which triggers one amorphization/complex-ordering risk from the target structural description. A second trigger comes from the large Zr–Mn metallic size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. However, the cell is not described as very large, and Laves-type intermetallics are generally compatible with size mismatch rather than necessarily amorphous [Reasonable Inference], so the score is medium rather than low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Zr₂Mn₃Al is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0153 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS: Mn₂VAl demonstrates arc melting plus annealing for Mn–Al ternary intermetallics, while ZrMn₆Sn₆ demonstrates that Zr–Mn ternary intermetallics can be obtained from elemental mixtures under sealed inert conditions. SS and TS analogues are not chemically matched to the target and therefore give insufficient useful synthesis evidence beyond neutral thermodynamic/process context. The key enabling factor is the metallic, near-hull nature of the phase, which makes arc melting and annealing plausible. The key limiting factor is phase control: Mn loss, competing Zr–Mn/Zr–Al/Mn–Al binaries, and slow Laves-derived ordering may prevent single-phase material without careful annealing and composition adjustment.
