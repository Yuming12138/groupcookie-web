### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.04098 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and indicating a likely ground-state or effectively on-hull phase [Reasonable Inference]. Chemically similar pnictides in CS #1–#5 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that related electropositive-metal pnictides can be stable crystalline phases. TS entries are also mostly low-hull, but because they are chemically unrelated to Ba–Sb–Br, they are only neutral evidence for this target’s actual chemistry. Remaining uncertainty is that computed negative hull values may reflect database/reference-state errors rather than experimentally demonstrated stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses sealed Nb/Ta ampoules and moderate-to-high temperatures, directly consistent with sealed Nb syntheses at 870 K for Cs₆GaSb₃, Cs₆InAs₃, and Rb₆AlSb₃ (CS #1/#2/#4). It is penalized for sealed handling of potentially volatile Br/Sb-containing mixtures and for air-sensitive/toxic precursors [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids >1000 °C, high pressure, H₂/NH₃/vacuum, and complex multi-step processing, so severity remains moderate. SS and TS provide insufficient useful condition signal for Ba–Sb–Br and are neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Direct reaction from elements is supported by CS #1/#2/#4 for alkali pnictides and by CS #3 for Ba–Sb chemistry, suggesting the kinetic barrier is not prohibitive. The target requires formation of a new Ba–Sb–Br ordered phase, so structural reconstruction is not trivial [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed heating and slow cooling should help equilibration and Br retention [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty remains because no CS analogue contains both Ba and Br in the target structural motif.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 follows the dry Ar alumina-crucible approach used for Ba₅In₂Sb₆ at 1270 K, 1 h, with controlled heating/cooling (CS #3). Its formal condition severity is low because it uses no high pressure, no specified H₂/NH₃/vacuum, and a temperature just below 1000 °C. The main unscored practical concern is Br retention in an unsealed system, which is a significant extrapolated risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS offer insufficient support for this specific route and are neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Ba–Sb formation from elements is directly supported by CS #3, so cation/anion diffusion at ~1000 °C is plausible. However, incorporating a stoichiometric small Br content into a Ba-rich antimonide lattice may be kinetically and thermodynamically sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. Compared with sealed Route 1, open Ar conditions may allow Br chemical potential drift, impairing phase selection [Reasonable Inference]. The lack of a Br-containing CS analogue keeps the kinetics score moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 likely operates at lower temperature than Route 2 because a BaBr₂-rich flux can enhance transport [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for sealed-system need, air-sensitive/toxic precursors, and extra flux-growth/separation steps. Sealed ampoule practice is supported by CS #1/#2/#4, but flux use itself is not directly evidenced by CS/SS/TS. Thus, condition severity is moderate despite the potentially lower thermal burden.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Flux growth should improve mass transport, nucleation, and crystal quality relative to powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. This is attractive because the target has ordered Ba/Sb/Br sublattices and may require slow equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. However, flux chemistry can bias toward BaBr₂-rich impurities or dissolve/react with container walls [Reasonable Inference]. Since no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates a BaBr₂ flux route, the kinetic score is favorable but uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s provided structure is a relatively ordered tetragonal I-4 phase with two Ba sites, two Sb sites, and one Br site, and the formula Ba₈Sb₅Br contains only 14 atoms per formula unit. No mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network is reported in the target description, and only one metal cation type is present, so none of the three amorphization triggers is clearly activated. CS #1–#4 show crystalline electropositive-metal pnictides can be obtained by direct thermal synthesis. SS/TS entries are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so they are neutral, 50, for this factor.
