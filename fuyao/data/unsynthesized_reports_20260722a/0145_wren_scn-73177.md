### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported `E_hull = −0.022427739 eV/atom` places it effectively on the convex hull, giving a high stability score [target data]. Chemically related Ca(Au₂Cd)₂, CaAu₅, Ce(Au₂Zn)₂, and CaIrIn₄ all have `E_hull = 0.0`, supporting the stability of Ca–Au and Ca–p-block intermetallic phases (CS1–CS4). SS/TS entries include stable phases but are chemically unrelated to Ca–In–Au, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for target-specific thermodynamics; neutral 50 for SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is mainly computational: negative hull values can reflect numerical tolerance rather than true exothermicity below the hull [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because CS1 uses sealed Ta tubes, Ar, high-frequency heating to 1473 K, annealing, and quenching/slow cooling. The rubric deductions are driven by >1000 °C temperature, sealed-system need, multi-step processing, and Ca air sensitivity [CS1, Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and reactive gases such as H₂/NH₃, which keeps the score from becoming low (CS1). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this intermetallic route and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable relative to subsolidus diffusion because CS1 uses a molten or near-molten metallic mixture at 1473 K and successfully forms a highly similar Ca–Au–p-block structure. The close structural match—CaAu₁₂ cuboctahedra and similar Au–Au distances—supports a high structural-reconstruction score (CS1). The main kinetic limitation is homogenizing dense Au with reactive Ca and low-melting In [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not improve or contradict this assessment.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 has slightly lower temperature severity because CS2 reports ~1000 °C rather than clearly above 1000 °C, but it uses evacuated sealed tubes or arc melting and annealing. Vacuum, sealed handling, multi-step processing, and Ca air sensitivity produce substantial deductions (CS2, Reasonable Inference). The route is equipment-accessible relative to Ta-tube induction, but quartz/alumina compatibility with Ca/In at high T is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS are insufficiently relevant and neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct elemental CaAu₅ formation demonstrates that Ca–Au intermetallics can form from pressed pellets or arc melting (CS2). However, the target requires incorporation and ordering of In into a tetragonal structure, whereas CS2 CaAu₅ is cubic, so structural reconstruction is only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Arc melting improves diffusion, but a purely pelletized anneal may leave unreacted Au/In segregation if not remelted [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide no useful Ca–In–Au kinetic signal.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses sealed Ta-tube melt growth comparable to CS1 and CS3, with high temperature, slow cooling, and annealing. It receives deductions for >1000 °C operation, sealed volatile/reactive components, multi-step processing, and Ca air sensitivity [CS1, CS3, Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high pressure and reactive gas atmospheres, which makes it moderate rather than highly difficult (CS1, CS3). SS/TS are unrelated and neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A Au–In-rich flux should provide excellent mass transport because In is low-melting and Au-rich metallic melts are likely at the proposed temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 and CS3 show that related Ca–Au–p-block intermetallics can be crystallized from sealed high-temperature melts with slow cooling and annealing. The kinetic risk is compositional: excess flux can stabilize competing CaAu₅ or In-rich phases, so the composition window may be narrower than in stoichiometric melting [CS2, Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient target-relevant kinetic evidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size contrast between Ca and Au/In, which triggers the radius-mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target and CS1 both feature edge-/face-sharing CaAu₁₂ cuboctahedra, but no clear vertex+edge+face coexistence is established, so the mixed-connectivity check is not fully triggered (CS1 and target description). The target unit-cell atom count is not provided in CS/SS/TS, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for triggering the >40 atom criterion. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium because metallic melts plus annealing often crystallize the related compounds (CS1–CS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaInAu₅ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very-near-ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is negative within numerical tolerance [target data]. The strongest support comes from CS1 and CS2: CS1 gives an extremely close Ca–Au–p-block tetragonal analogue with CaAu₁₂ cuboctahedra, while CS2 confirms stable CaAu₅ chemistry (CS1, CS2). CS3 and CS4 further support that Ca–Au–p-block or Ca–In intermetallics can be obtained from elemental melts under Ar using specialized furnaces and annealing (CS3, CS4). The most critical enabling factor is liquid-phase reaction/slow cooling in sealed Ta, which should overcome Au/Ca/In diffusion and ordering barriers [CS1, CS3, Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control, especially Ca loss/oxidation and possible competition with CaAu₅ or other Au/In-rich phases [CS2, Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not materially support or refute synthesis because their top analogues are chemically unrelated; they are treated as neutral 50.
