**Evidence-use note:** The CS set is the most relevant because it contains multiple alkali–selenide solid-state/fusion syntheses using Rb-containing precursors and Se at 800–850 °C or comparable temperatures, including Rb–M–Se phases that are on/near the hull (CS1–CS5). The SS and TS sets are chemically unrelated to Rb–Ga–Se and their similarity fields are NaN, so they provide insufficient route-transfer evidence for this target and are treated as neutral signals, score 50, for route selection; they mainly show generic high-temperature/controlled-atmosphere feasibility but not chemistry-specific guidance (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.018908902 eV/atom, placing it effectively on the hull and within the highest rubric bin. The CS analogues include several stable Rb/alkali selenides with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, such as Rb₂Ni₃Se₄, Rb₂Pd₃Se₄, K₂Pd₃Se₄, and Rb₂MnSe₂, supporting that alkali selenides can be thermodynamically accessible (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). The remaining uncertainty is that the target’s negative hull value is computational and no experimental Rb₂Ga₂Se₃ synthesis is provided.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses 800–850 °C H₂-assisted fusion, which is directly analogous to Rb₂Ni₃Se₄ at 850 °C in H₂ and other alkali chalcogenides in H₂/chalcogen streams (CS1, CS2, CS5). The main severity penalty is the reactive H₂ atmosphere and Se handling, while the temperature is below 1000 °C and no high pressure is required (CS1, CS2, CS5). Uncertainty comes from whether Ga chemistry under H₂/carbonate conditions forms clean selenide or side oxide/carbonate residues [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
CS1 shows that carbonate + metal + Se mixtures can react in only 4 h at 850 °C for an Rb selenide, which supports reasonable bulk kinetics. However, the target’s Ga–Ga/Ga–Se tetrahedral framework differs from the transition-metal selenide motifs in CS1–CS5, so structural reconstruction may be substantial [Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically plausible but less ideal than binary-selenide or flux routes.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 relies on vacuum or inert sealed-tube synthesis, which is supported by CS3 for selenides prepared in quartz tubes under 10⁻⁴ torr. The main penalties are controlled vacuum/sealed conditions, volatile chalcogen pressure, and likely air-sensitive Rb₂Se handling [Reasonable Inference; sensitivity supported by CS4 and CS5]. The advantage is that the proposed temperature is moderate and external high pressure is unnecessary.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Using Rb₂Se and GaSe should improve precursor matching because both Rb–Se and Ga–Se bonds are already present [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 supports slow heating, long holds, regrinding, and annealing as workable kinetic tools for selenides. The remaining uncertainty is whether Ga–Ga bonding in the target forms readily from GaSe without competing Ga₂Se₃ or elemental Ga phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 uses an Ar or sealed-flux environment at 750–850 °C, consistent with alkali chalcogenide fusion/crystal growth reported in CS2 and CS4. It avoids H₂ if Ar is used, but still has penalties for volatile Se/Rb–Se flux, multistep flux separation, and air-sensitive/toxic components [Reasonable Inference; CS4, CS5]. The severity is moderate rather than easy.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Flux/fusion chemistry is strongly supported by CS2 and CS4, where alkali chalcogenides are obtained as well-shaped crystals or by fusion reactions with excess alkali carbonate and chalcogen. A Rb–Se/Se-rich melt should enhance mass transport and allow slow ordering of the monoclinic C2/m framework [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that no CS analogue contains Ga–Ga tetrahedra, so the exact crystallization window for Rb₂Ga₂Se₃ is unknown.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains large Rb–Se polyhedra and smaller Ga-centered tetrahedra, so cation-size mismatch is significant [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description reports corner and edge sharing but no face sharing, and the exact unit-cell atom count is not provided, so only one amorphization trigger is counted. This gives low–medium amorphization risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂Ga₂Se₃ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is −0.018908902 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS analogues showing that Rb/alkali selenides can be synthesized by high-temperature fusion, H₂-assisted carbonate reactions, sealed-tube selenide reactions, or flux-like methods at roughly 750–850 °C (CS1–CS5). SS and TS provide insufficient chemistry-specific guidance because their top examples are unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides with NaN similarity fields, so they are neutral for route choice (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5). The key enabling factor is the combination of favorable thermodynamics and demonstrated Rb–Se high-temperature synthetic accessibility (target E_hull; CS1, CS4, CS5). The key limiting factor is phase-field control in a volatile, air-sensitive Rb–Ga–Se system with possible competing Rb–Se and Ga–Se binaries [Reasonable Inference; sensitivity and excess chalcogen/alkali control supported by CS4 and CS5].
