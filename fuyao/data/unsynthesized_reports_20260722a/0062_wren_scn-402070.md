### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.005221 eV/atom, which is effectively 0 within numerical uncertainty and therefore strongly supports thermodynamic accessibility. The thermodynamic-similarity set contains several stable fluoride analogues, including Cs2SnF6, Tl2SnF6, Cs2NaTlF6, K2NaTlF6, and Cs3Fe2F9 with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS). Chemically similar CsRuF6, CsSbF6, RbRuF6, BaGeF6, and RbBiF6 are also listed as E_hull = 0.0, supporting the broader stability of A–MF6 fluoride frameworks (CS). Remaining uncertainty is that thermodynamic stability does not guarantee that Sn can be trapped in the exact CsSnF6 stoichiometry under laboratory conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids extreme temperatures and high pressure because closest F2 fluorination analogues form at 150–500 °C and 350 °C/2 h (CS). The major severity penalty is the use of elemental F2, which is directly required in CsRuF6/RbRuF6 and RbBiF6 preparations and is a highly reactive controlled atmosphere (CS, [Reasonable Inference]). The route also receives a toxicity/sensitivity penalty due to F2 and moisture-sensitive fluoride products [Reasonable Inference supported by CS phase/moisture-control notes]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for F2-based CsSnF6 synthesis specifically is treated as neutral rather than negative.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The kinetics should be favorable because gas-phase fluorination of halide mixtures produces CsRuF6/RbRuF6 in only ~2 h at 350 °C (CS). The target structure is strongly matched by CsSbF6, which has the same R-3 family, CsF12 cuboctahedra, MF6 octahedra, and almost identical tilt angle (39° vs target 40°) (CS). This minimizes structural reconstruction relative to attempting to build the phase from unrelated oxide precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Sn fluorination forms CsSnF6 rather than Cs2SnF6/SnF4/SnF2-related products [Reasonable Inference; SS/TS show stable Sn fluoride analogues but different stoichiometry].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures because Cs2SnF6-related sealed reactions occur at 873 K and Tl2SnF6 forms at 300 °C for 4 d (SS, TS). However, it requires drybox/vacuum handling, sealed noble-metal tubes, and volatile/moisture-sensitive SnF4-containing mixtures (SS, TS, [Reasonable Inference]). It also likely needs an oxidizing fluoride additive to reach the target stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference], which increases chemical risk beyond the directly reported Cs2SnF6/Tl2SnF6 routes. Insufficient evidence from CS for sealed Sn fluoride chemistry is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Binary fluorides such as CsF and SnF4 are appropriate reactive precursors because they are directly used in Sn fluoride analogues (SS, TS). Ion diffusion is manageable at 300–600 °C, but the sealed-tube route may depend on vapor/transport processes involving SnF4 [Reasonable Inference]. The structural reconstruction score is reduced because Cs2SnF6/Tl2SnF6 contain SnF6 units but not the same CsSnF6 framework, and Cs2SnF6-related work reports incomplete phase purity with desired phase only at least 75 wt% (SS, TS). This makes phase selection a more serious uncertainty than in Route 1.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is mild thermally because CsSbF6 crystals are grown by slow evaporation from HF-acidified aqueous solution at non-elevated temperature (CS). The severity comes from HF toxicity/corrosion, possible HF volatility, and the likely need to preform CsSnF6 before crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. No high pressure or high temperature is required, and insufficient evidence from SS/TS for solution routes is neutral. The route is safer thermally but not chemically simple.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution growth avoids solid-state diffusion barriers, giving high intrinsic transport and precursor activity [Reasonable Inference]. CsSbF6 provides a strong structural analogue for crystallization of an R-3 CsMF6 phase from HF solution (CS). However, the CsSbF6 crystals reportedly required slow controlled evaporation and in some cases almost two years, so nucleation and crystal growth may be very slow (CS). The largest uncertainty is whether CsSnF6 remains stable in HF solution rather than hydrolyzing or changing oxidation state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination mismatch between Cs in 12-coordinate CsF12 cuboctahedra and Sn in SnF6 octahedra, triggering the cation-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target explicitly contains corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, triggering mixed-connectivity complexity. The formula is simple and there is no direct evidence that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so the unit-cell-size trigger is not applied [Reasonable Inference]. The resulting F4 = 60 reflects medium risk: crystallization should be possible, but phase ordering and avoidance of disordered/amorphous fluorides require controlled conditions.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsSnF6 is likely a near-ground-state or ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is slightly negative, effectively on the hull, and many thermodynamically similar fluorides are also stable (TS). The synthesis evidence is strongest structurally and chemically from CsSbF6/CsRuF6-type A–MF6 analogues, which share the CsF12/MF6 framework and are experimentally synthesized (CS). The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, because F2 routes successfully produce closely related CsMF6 and RbMF6 compounds at moderate temperature (CS). The most critical limiting factor is Sn redox/stoichiometry control: the provided Sn analogues are Cs2SnF6 and Tl2SnF6 rather than CsSnF6, so the target may compete with known Sn(IV) hexafluorostannates (SS, TS, [Reasonable Inference]). Overall, the target is feasible but should be pursued first by carefully controlled F2 fluorination rather than low-fluorine-potential sealed Ar synthesis.
