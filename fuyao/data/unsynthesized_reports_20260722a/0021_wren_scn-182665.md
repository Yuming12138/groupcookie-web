### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.138822274 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable rubric category and gives F1 = 100. CS contains stable or near-stable oxides such as LaCuO3 and La2CuO4 with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS), supporting that similar oxide chemistries can be thermodynamically viable. SS/TS include stable or near-stable materials, but they are chemically diverse and provide only generic support; for Sr–Ag–O specifically, evidence from SS/TS is insufficient and treated as neutral 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-Oxygen-Pressure Solid-State Oxidation

Route 1 is strongly supported for oxidation potential by LaCuO3, which required KO2 and 40 kbar oxygen pressure at 1500 °C (CS). The target likely contains formal Ag(III), so high oxygen fugacity is chemically appropriate [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is that Ag oxides are expected to be thermally labile, so the LaCuO3 temperature cannot be transferred directly; this uncertainty lowers the kinetic score [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct signal for this route and are scored neutral where not applicable.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-Temperature Oxidative Hydrothermal / Alkaline Precipitation

Route 2 receives the best condition score because CS La2CuO4 was made hydrothermally at only 60 °C and 35 bar using nitrate precursors and H2O2 (CS), while SS/TS AgCoPO4 used AgNO3 precipitation followed by moderate calcination (SS, TS). The solution route has excellent precursor activity and minimizes diffusion length, which raises F3. The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction into the exact tetragonal SrAg2O4 network and stabilization of formal Ag(III), neither of which is directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Oxidizing Flux Growth / Molten Salt Route

Flux growth is supported by CS YInO3, which crystallized from oxide precursors with PbF2 flux at ~950 °C, though repeated cycles were needed for single phase (CS). The flux should improve ion mobility and crystallinity relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target requires a lower-temperature oxidizing flux because Ag oxide stability is likely more limited than In³⁺ oxide stability [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS AgCoPO4 supports Ag-containing oxide-salt crystallization from solution/calcination, but not directly a Sr–Ag–O flux route (SS, TS).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Reactive Oxide Thin-Film Deposition

No CS/SS/TS source directly supports thin-film deposition, so the evidentiary basis is weak and neutral 50 from CS/SS/TS where direct evidence is absent. The route is included because nonequilibrium deposition under ozone/plasma oxygen can stabilize high-valent or metastable oxides [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetic limitations include composition control, Ag reduction/loss, and uncertain direct crystallization pathway [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, F2 is moderate but F3 is lower than hydrothermal/flux routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large Sr–O versus Ag–O bond-length contrast, consistent with significant cation size/coordination mismatch and triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing OSr2Ag2 tetrahedra, but not face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not specified; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for this factor, so it is treated neutrally. Overall amorphization risk is Low–Medium, giving F4 = 78.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(AgO2)2 is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or even below the computed hull, so it is likely a ground-state candidate by the provided thermodynamic metric. The strongest supporting analogue is high-pressure LaCuO3, which shows that high-valent transition-metal oxides may need high oxygen fugacity (CS), while hydrothermal La2CuO4 and AgCoPO4 show that square-planar oxide motifs and Ag-containing solids can form under relatively mild solution conditions (CS, SS, TS). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high oxygen chemical potential to stabilize formal Ag(III) [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is avoiding reduction/decomposition to Ag metal, Ag2O, SrO/SrO2, or other competing Sr–Ag–O phases during crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is promising but likely requires low-temperature oxidizing chemistry rather than conventional high-temperature air firing.
