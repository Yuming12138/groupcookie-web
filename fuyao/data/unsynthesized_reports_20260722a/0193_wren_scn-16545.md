### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.006987849 eV/atom, which is effectively 0 within computational uncertainty and falls in the most favorable thermodynamic bin. Thermodynamically similar silicides CeIr₃Si₂, Th(IrSi)₂, LaIr₂Si₂, IrSi, and NdIr₂Si₂ are all listed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, reinforcing that nearby Ir–Si intermetallic chemistry is stable (TS). The remaining uncertainty is whether the predicted P4₂/mnm YIr₄Si₂ phase is experimentally accessible rather than replaced by a nearby competing Y–Ir–Si phase such as YIr₂Si₂, for which SS reports narrow homogeneity/phase-control challenges.

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

The route uses standard intermetallic conditions: purified Ar arc melting and vacuum/inert annealing, directly supported by YIr₂Si₂ synthesis (SS). Deductions are mainly for controlled atmosphere/vacuum, multi-step remelting/annealing, and Y air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids high pressure and does not require an explicitly volatile precursor, so no deductions were applied for those factors; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports sealed volatile precursor control.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + annealing

The kinetics are limited by dense elemental precursors, especially refractory Ir [Reasonable Inference], and similar systems use elemental charges rather than reactive precursors (SS, TS). Arc melting improves mixing, but long anneals of 8 days are repeatedly reported for CeIr₃Si₂-like and RIr₂Si₂ analogues, indicating nontrivial ordering and diffusion requirements (CS, SS, TS). Structural similarity to YIr₂Si₂ helps, but the target’s P4₂/mnm structure is not identical to the P4/nmm analogue, so some reconstructive ordering is expected [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Melt growth / Bridgman-like growth

This route is more severe because IrSi and LaIr₂Si₂ analogues involve high-temperature melt processing, with LaIr₂Si₂ reported at up to 1750 °C and IrSi requiring Bridgman-like growth/annealing (TS). It also requires controlled inert atmospheres and specialized growth equipment, though no high pressure is needed (TS). The main uncertainty is the target’s melting behavior and whether it melts congruently; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes the liquidus or primary crystallization field for YIr₄Si₂.

#### F3 — Route 2: Melt growth / Bridgman-like growth

Melt growth can reduce solid-state diffusion barriers and directly crystallize silicides, as shown for IrSi Bridgman-like processing (TS). However, Ir-rich compositions remain challenging due to high melting temperatures and possible segregation [Reasonable Inference]. TS also reports polymorphism and phase-control difficulty in IrSi and LaIr₂Si₂-type systems, so the crystallization pathway is not risk-free.

#### F2 — Route 3: Metallic flux growth

Flux growth has the least severe thermal profile among the plausible routes because CeAu₄Si₂ was grown at 1050 °C and slowly cooled, avoiding >1400 °C sustained processing (CS). Deductions still apply for T >1000 °C, multi-step flux handling, and Y air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence from CS/SS/TS that high pressure or reactive H₂/NH₃/vacuum atmospheres are required for the flux route.

#### F3 — Route 3: Metallic flux growth

A liquid flux can improve kinetics by enhancing transport and lowering the effective reaction temperature [Reasonable Inference], and CeAu₄Si₂ demonstrates successful rare-earth/metal/silicon flux growth using Au–Si flux (CS). The major uncertainty is that the evidence is chemically adjacent but not direct: insufficient evidence from SS/TS confirms Y–Ir–Si solubility or nucleation behavior in Au–Si flux. Flux incorporation is also possible, since the CeAu₄Si₂ report notes flux-related Au observation/contamination in diffraction (CS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with relatively simple coordination descriptions and no reported large unit cell, so amorphization risk is not high [Reasonable Inference]. A size/coordination mismatch exists between large 14-coordinate Y and low-coordinate Ir environments in the target, so one amorphization-related trigger is assigned [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS supports triggering mixed polyhedral connectivity or >40 atom unit-cell criteria.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.006987849 eV/atom, and several thermodynamically similar Ir–Si silicides are at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS). The strongest synthesis support comes from the structurally similar and compositionally exact-element YIr₂Si₂ analogue, which is made from Y, Ir, and Si by tri-arc melting under purified Ar with sealed-tube annealing (SS). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ir–Si framework chemistry, reflected by similar Ir–Si bond lengths across the target and analogues (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: Y–Ir–Si analogues have narrow homogeneity ranges and annealing/quenching sensitivity, so YIr₂Si₂, IrSi, or other silicides may compete (SS, TS). Overall, the target is moderately likely to be synthesizable, but phase-pure bulk synthesis will probably require careful stoichiometry and long homogenization.
