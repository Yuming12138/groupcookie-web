### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as **−0.024875651 eV/atom**, so it is effectively hull-stable by the provided thermodynamic criterion and deserves a very high F1 score. Several chemically similar tellurides in CS are also on the hull, including KCr5Te8, CsCr5Te8, RbSc5Te8, and BaSc2Te4, supporting that alkali/metal telluride frameworks can be thermodynamically stable (CS). SS and TS lists contain mostly chemically unrelated compounds; therefore, they provide insufficient K–Ti–Te-specific stability transfer and are neutral, score 50, for chemistry-specific interpretation.

#### F2 — Route 1: Ion Exchange

CS ion-exchange analogues used evacuated silica tubes and molten alkali halides for 1 week, which is moderately demanding but not extreme in temperature or pressure (CS). The severity penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile/air-sensitive reduced telluride chemistry, multi-step parent-frame preparation, and possible Tl toxicity if a Tl-based precursor is used (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for condition severity in K–Ti–Te ion exchange and are treated as neutral, score 50.

#### F3 — Route 1: Ion Exchange

The kinetic advantage of Route 1 is that alkali exchange can occur through a pre-existing chalcogenide framework, as demonstrated for KCr5Te8 and CsCr5Te8 from Tl-based frameworks with alkali halides (CS). The molten halide medium lowers effective diffusion barriers for K transport compared with direct elemental diffusion (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). The main uncertainty is whether a structurally compatible Ti–Te parent phase can be made and whether K insertion preserves the desired trigonal framework [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

CS shows direct sealed-tube telluride synthesis is feasible for SrSc2Te4 and BaSc2Te4 at 800–900 °C under vacuum with long anneals (CS). For KTi6Te8, the conditions become more severe because K and Te are volatile/reactive and require careful sealed-ampoule management [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral, score 50.

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct Solid-State

Direct synthesis from K/Ti/Te requires complete nucleation and growth of the TiTe6 network, giving slower kinetics than topotactic exchange [Reasonable Inference]. CS direct telluride examples required extended anneals and controlled cooling, indicating that diffusion and phase ordering can be limiting even for related tellurides (CS). The high melting point and passivated surface of Ti powder further reduce precursor activity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Halide Flux

RbSc5Te8 synthesis in excess RbBr flux required 900 °C for 14 days in evacuated fused-silica ampoules loaded under Ar (CS). Translating this to KBr/KI flux gives similar sealed-tube and controlled-atmosphere penalties, plus volatility and air-sensitivity concerns for K/Te species [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS offer insufficient flux-specific K–Ti–Te evidence and are neutral, score 50.

#### F3 — Route 3: Halide Flux

Flux growth improves transport relative to direct solid-state reaction because molten alkali halides can dissolve or mobilize alkali/chalcogenide species, as implied by the RbSc5Te8 RbBr flux synthesis (CS). However, the CS flux example also reports phase-selection difficulty: RbSc5Te8 was obtained unexpectedly rather than the intended phase, so nucleation control may be challenging (CS). This route is therefore kinetically better than direct reaction but less structurally controlled than ion exchange.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(Ti3Te4)2 is likely a **thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase**, because its reported E_hull is slightly negative and effectively on the convex hull. The chemical evidence is supportive: CS contains multiple stable alkali/metal tellurides, and especially KCr5Te8/CsCr5Te8 show that alkali transition-metal telluride frameworks can be synthesized by ion exchange. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a molten alkali halide route that can deliver K into a preformed telluride framework while preserving MTe6 octahedral connectivity, as demonstrated in CS. The main limiting factor is not thermodynamics but **phase-selective synthesis**: Ti–Te frameworks, K volatility, and competing K–Te/Ti–Te binaries may make direct formation difficult [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not substantially strengthen the synthesis case because their nearest examples are chemically unrelated and therefore provide insufficient K–Ti–Te-specific signal.
