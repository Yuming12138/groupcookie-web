### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.0515 eV/atom, which places it effectively on the convex hull and warrants a very high thermodynamic score under the provided rubric. Chemically similar phosphides in CS1–CS5 are all listed with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that neighboring alkaline-earth/rare-earth/transition-metal phosphide chemistries contain stable ground-state compounds (CS). SS and TS entries are mostly chemically unrelated amides, borides, oxides, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient evidence for Sc–Ni–P stability and are neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures of about 800–950 °C, supported by direct phosphide syntheses in CS1, CS3, and CS4. The main severity deductions arise from volatile phosphorus requiring sealed/inert handling, air-sensitive/reactive Sc/P handling [Reasonable Inference], and multi-step grinding/reannealing supported by CS1 and CS4. There is no high-pressure requirement and no need for strongly reactive H₂/NH₃ atmospheres, unlike some SS/TS examples, which are chemically irrelevant and treated as insufficient evidence from SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct elemental reactions have only moderate precursor activity because Sc and Ni are dense metals and solid-state diffusion can be slow [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS3 shows the exact Zr(Ni₂P)₂ structure type can form by sintering at 1070 K, and CS1/CS4 show related phosphides form after heating and grinding, supporting feasible kinetics. The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: the target is isostructural with CS3, so reconstruction into the desired topology should be less demanding than for a novel framework.

#### F2 — Route 2

The Sn-flux route is conducted at moderate temperatures, consistent with CS2 at 1070 K and CS5 at 900 °C. It is penalized more heavily than Route 1 because CS2 uses evacuated ampoules, CS2/CS5 require flux removal, and red P volatility still requires sealed reaction control. The route is experimentally routine for experienced solid-state chemists but has extra steps and possible Sn contamination [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth improves mass transport and can enable better crystallinity than direct solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], supported by successful Sn-flux synthesis of Y–Ni–P and Li–Co–P phosphides (CS2, CS5). The kinetics score is higher than Route 1 because diffusion occurs partly through a melt, but transferability is not perfect because no CS source reports Sn-flux growth of the exact Sc(Ni₂P)₂ or Zr(Ni₂P)₂ structure type. SS and TS give insufficient chemically relevant evidence for fluxing this target and are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 3

Binary phosphide precursor annealing reduces free elemental phosphorus volatility [Reasonable Inference], but it likely requires evacuated/sealed-tube annealing and multiple grinding/annealing cycles. The annealing temperature remains moderate, based on CS1, CS3, and CS4. The need to prepare or source ScP and Ni₂P adds practical complexity not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 3

Preformed Sc–P and Ni–P bonds may help chemical homogeneity [Reasonable Inference], but the route still requires solid-state rearrangement from binary phosphides into the ternary P4₂/mnm network. CS3 supports that the final structure type is accessible by sintering, but no provided source directly demonstrates binary phosphide precursor conversion to this phase. Therefore, the kinetics score is moderate and slightly below the flux route.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is a compact intermetallic phosphide with a small formula unit, and CS3 shows an isostructural Zr analogue exists as an ordered crystalline phase. The main amorphization risk is the size/electronic mismatch between Sc and Ni [Reasonable Inference], which triggers C1, but there is no evidence for a >40-atom cell or complex mixed polyhedral sharing from the target description. SS/TS examples with complex polyhedral sharing are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient evidence for amorphization of Sc–Ni–P.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc(Ni₂P)₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state crystalline phase because its reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar phosphides are stable on the hull (CS). The best support comes from CS3, the exact Zr(Ni₂P)₂ P4₂/mnm structural analogue, with nearly identical coordination motifs and Ni–P distances; CS1 further supports the same P4₂/mnm phosphide topology in a related metal–transition-metal–P system. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of an isostructural, stable, synthesized analogue in Zr(Ni₂P)₂ (CS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control under phosphorus volatility, since chemically similar phosphide syntheses require sealed or inert handling, repeated grinding, and sometimes composition tuning (CS1, CS2, CS4). Overall, the target appears quite synthesizable by conventional sealed-tube solid-state methods, with Sn flux as a useful backup for crystal growth.
