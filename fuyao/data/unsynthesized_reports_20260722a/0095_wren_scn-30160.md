### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has reported E_hull = −0.041084782 eV/atom, which is within the highest stability bin and suggests a ground-state or near-ground-state phase within computational uncertainty (target data). Several TS examples are on or near the hull, but their chemistries are amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides rather than Rb–Bi–Cl, so TS gives insufficient chemically specific synthesis evidence and is treated as neutral for route selection. The remaining uncertainty is that negative calculated hull energy does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or hydration stability in solution [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives a high condition score because CS-1, CS-2, and CS-4 demonstrate alkali/metal chloride crystallization from hot aqueous or HCl solution under mild, ambient-pressure conditions. No high temperature, high pressure, vacuum, H₂, NH₃, or sealed tube is required by those CS examples. The only significant deduction is corrosive/moisture-sensitive chloride chemistry, especially BiCl₃/HCl handling [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because solution crystallization avoids long-range solid-state diffusion; this is directly supported by CS-1 slow cooling, CS-2 evaporation, and CS-4 cooling crystallization. Precursor activity is high because RbCl and BiCl₃ can form dissolved chloride complexes [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity among possible Rb–Bi–Cl stoichiometries and hydrolysis products, which is not resolved by SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because sealed ampoule handling, drying, and BiCl₃ volatility control are required [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 and CS-5 show that alkali chloride + metal chloride syntheses can require sealed/high-chlorine conditions, although their high Cl₂ pressure is mainly due to Pu redox chemistry and should not be fully transferred to Bi³⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide useful evidence for this chloride sealed-tube route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetics are still reasonably good because BiCl₃ is a low-melting/reactive chloride and can mediate transport at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3/CS-5 demonstrate chloride reactions at 350 °C, supporting feasibility of low-temperature chloride assembly, although those examples are more hazardous due to Pu. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the ordered trigonal Rb₃Bi₂Cl₉ polymorph forms rather than other Rb–Bi chloride phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate condition severity because it uses low temperature but requires a sealed acidic autoclave with corrosive HCl/BiCl₃ [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1/TS-1 show solvothermal synthesis can be high-pressure and equipment-intensive, but their supercritical NH₃ chemistry is not directly applicable to Rb–Bi–Cl and is not counted as required. CS-1/CS-4 better justify the chloride solution component.

#### F3 — Route 3

The hydrothermal route should have favorable kinetics because transport and nucleation occur in solution, similar in spirit to CS-1/CS-4 hot chloride solution crystallization. SS-1/TS-1 provide only broad evidence that solvothermal crystallization can yield well-crystallized ionic phases, not direct Rb–Bi–Cl evidence. The uncertainty is whether elevated temperature and pressure stabilize the desired anhydrous chloride or instead favor hydrolysis/oxychloride impurities [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger from the large difference between Rb and Bi coordination/bonding environments, supported by the much longer Rb–Cl distances than Bi–Cl distances in the target description. The target does not show mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity; it specifically contains corner-sharing BiCl₆ octahedra, so C3 is not triggered. Unit-cell size above 40 atoms is not established from the supplied data, so C2 is not triggered; SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₃Bi₂Cl₉ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is −0.0411 eV/atom (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS analogues: multiple alkali/metal chloride salts crystallize from hot chloride-rich aqueous or HCl solutions under mild conditions (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is solution-mediated chloride complexation, which should allow Rb⁺ and Bi–Cl units to assemble without slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Bi³⁺ hydrolysis and competing chloride stoichiometries during crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated, so they provide limited direct guidance and are treated as neutral except for broad thermodynamic/solvothermal context.
