### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The exact target E_hull is −0.019958207 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull and supports a high F1 score. CS gives strong corroboration because KHgF3 is a chemically close K–Hg–F phase with E_hull = 0 and experimentally reported synthesis (CS-1). TS analogues are near-hull, but chemically unrelated, so they provide only generic support; insufficient evidence from SS and limited useful evidence from TS for K–Hg–F-specific stability.

#### F2/F3 Route 1: Direct ambient-pressure fluorination

Route 1 is supported most strongly by CS-1, where KHgF3 is synthesized from HgCl2·2NH3 + KCl at 300–350 °C in F2 with slow cooling. The condition severity is dominated by F2 handling and Hg toxicity, while temperature and pressure are comparatively moderate (CS-1, CS-3). Kinetics are favorable because the closest chemical analogue forms in only 3–4 h, but KHgF4 likely requires higher fluorine chemical potential than KHgF3 [Reasonable Inference from target stoichiometry vs CS-1].

#### F2/F3 Route 2: High-pressure fluorination

Route 2 is justified by CS-4, where CsCuF4 is obtained by high-pressure fluorination of CsCuCl3 at 400 °C and pF2 ≈ 350–360 bar. This route is more severe than Route 1 because it requires a sealed Monel autoclave and high-pressure F2 (CS-4). Kinetically, high F2 pressure may help drive formation of the more fluorine-rich KHgF4 target, but precursor design and phase selectivity are less directly evidenced than for KHgF3 [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and CS-4].

#### F2/F3 Route 3: Sealed solid-oxidant fluorination

Route 3 has no direct CS/SS/TS precedent for KHgF4, so its score is lower and uncertainty is higher. It is proposed because CS-3 and CS-4 demonstrate that alkali MF4 fluorides require strong fluorinating/oxidizing conditions, which solid oxidants could mimic [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic weakness is that binary fluorides and solid oxidants may require complete structural reconstruction and may leave byproducts such as AgF/Ag [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed polyhedral connectivity: KF8 polyhedra share corners, edges, and faces, and HgF6 octahedra share corners with both K and Hg polyhedra, triggering a structural-complexity penalty from the target description. The large difference between K coordination and Hg octahedral coordination also suggests a substantial cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, the formula is simple and likely has fewer than 40 atoms per cell, so the amorphization risk is medium rather than high [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KHgF4 is predicted to be a near-ground-state or ground-state phase because its exact E_hull is slightly negative/effectively zero. The chemical evidence is fairly strong because KHgF3 is already known in the same K–Hg–F system and is made by F2 fluorination under accessible 300–350 °C conditions (CS-1). The best enabling factor is the demonstrated reactivity of K/Hg chloride precursors under F2 to form HgF6-containing alkali mercury fluorides (CS-1). The main limiting factor is the need to reach and retain the more fluorine-rich KHgF4 composition without decomposing or reverting to KHgF3 or binary fluorides [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and target stoichiometry]. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated, so they do not materially reduce the uncertainty in route selection.
