### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.011589394 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within computational tolerance and strongly favors synthesizability. (Target) The closest chemically similar R-3 Ln3Pd4 analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the stability of this structure family. (CS) SS and TS include low-hull examples, but they are chemically unrelated to Y–Pt, so they provide insufficient direct evidence for Y3Pt4 stability and are neutral signals. (SS/TS)

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly because CS analogue arc melting occurs near 1500 °C, triggering both >1000 °C and >1400 °C deductions. (CS) It avoids high pressure, reactive gas, and volatile sealed-ampoule requirements because the CS route is ambient-pressure Ar arc melting and Y was not among the rare-earths flagged as requiring sealed Mo crucibles for volatility. (CS) The remaining uncertainty is Pt homogenization because Pt is more refractory than Pd, which is a [Reasonable Inference] rather than directly shown in CS.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Y and Pt are dense metals, so precursor activity is low compared with nitrate/sol-gel routes. ([Reasonable Inference]) Arc melting mitigates slow diffusion by forming a molten or near-molten alloy, and CS analogues used repeated remelting to ensure homogeneity. (CS) Structural kinetics are favorable because the CS analogues share the same R-3 3:4 structure motif. (CS)

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe because CS induction examples require 1500–1550 °C and refractory crucible/sealed Ar handling. (CS) The added annealing step is inferred from SS/TS arc-melt/anneal examples and from general intermetallic ordering practice, so it is labeled [Reasonable Inference]. (SS/TS; [Reasonable Inference]) SS/TS are not chemically close to Y3Pt4, so they are weak/neutral evidence for the target-specific route. (SS/TS)

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The sealed induction route again starts from dense elemental metals, so precursor activity is low. ([Reasonable Inference]) CS induction examples show rare-earth–Pd alloys can form at 1500–1550 °C, but they also report sensitivity to container material and homogeneity/contact time in related binaries, which lowers kinetic confidence. (CS) The structural target is still supported by R-3 Ln3Pd4 CS analogues, but Route 2 itself is less directly demonstrated for the 3:4 phase than Route 1. (CS)

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is an intermetallic with a significant Y/Pt metallic radius mismatch, so one amorphization/size-mismatch check is triggered. ([Reasonable Inference]) The isostructural CS cells/formula representations are small, not >40 atoms, and the target description does not indicate oxide-like mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity. (CS; Target) Remaining uncertainty is that intermetallics can sometimes form competing crystalline phases rather than amorphous products, so the main risk is phase competition rather than true glass formation. ([Reasonable Inference])

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y3Pt4 is likely a ground-state or essentially ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is slightly negative within numerical tolerance. (Target) The strongest support comes from CS: multiple Ln3Pd4 analogues with the same trigonal R-3 structure and same 3:4 stoichiometry are known and were synthesized by arc melting. (CS) SS and TS are mostly chemically unrelated and therefore provide insufficient direct support for the Y–Pt target, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory. (SS/TS) The most critical enabling factor is the close isostructural chemical analogy to rare-earth–Pd 3:4 phases. (CS) The most critical limiting factor is high-temperature homogenization of a Y–Pt alloy containing refractory Pt, especially avoiding Y oxidation and incomplete mixing. ([Reasonable Inference])
