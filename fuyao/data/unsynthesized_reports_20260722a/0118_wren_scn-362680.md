### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0493 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin and suggesting it is predicted to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase [target data]. Chemically close Cs3BS3 and Cs4Ga2Se5 are reported with E_hull = 0.0, supporting that Cs chalcogenide networks of this type can be stable (CS-1, CS-4). SS and TS analogues are thermodynamically stable or near-stable but chemically unrelated; therefore they provide insufficient target-specific signal and are treated as neutral for chemical feasibility.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the closest Cs3BS3 analogue is synthesized at 720 °C and Cs–Ga–Se analogues at 600–700 °C (CS-1, CS-4). The main severity penalties arise from sealed-tube/vacuum or Ar handling, volatile Se, and air-sensitive alkali selenide chemistry, which are consistent with sealed ampoule and protective-gas procedures in CS-1 and CS-4. SS/TS give insufficient route-specific evidence for Cs–B–Se and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The direct Cs3BS3 analogue strongly supports the kinetic plausibility of forming trigonal planar boron chalcogenide units from amorphous B and alkali chalcogenide precursors (CS-1). However, amorphous B is refractory and solid-state diffusion may be slow [Reasonable Inference], and Cs–Ga–Se analogues required long annealing/intermediate grinding (CS-4). Thus kinetics are feasible but not rapid.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses similar temperature and atmosphere severity as Route 1, with the same sealed-tube and volatile Se penalties (CS-1, CS-4). Its additional flux-like character is an extrapolation rather than directly demonstrated for Cs3BSe3 [Reasonable Inference], but it does not introduce high pressure or >1000 °C processing. Safety and containment remain moderate concerns because Se vapor and Cs2Se are reactive [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The self-flux variant is scored higher kinetically because a molten or partially molten Cs2Se/Se phase should improve mass transport relative to a dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling is supported by Cs3BS3 procedures (CS-1), and long sealed-ampoule anneals are established for Cs–Ga–Se phases (CS-4). The uncertainty is that excess Cs2Se or Se may shift equilibria toward binary Cs–Se or B–Se impurity phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The azide route benefits from lower annealing temperatures near 500 °C, as used for Cs-containing chalcogenides (CS-2). However, it still requires vacuum/sealed-tube control, gas evolution, hazardous CsN3 handling, and multi-step preparation/decomposition (CS-2). No SS/TS analogue validates azide chemistry for Cs–B–Se, so the route remains more hazardous and less certain.

#### F3 — Route 3

CsN3 can introduce highly reactive Cs species without handling elemental Cs, which is a clear kinetic advantage demonstrated for Cs-containing chalcogenides (CS-2). The limiting kinetic uncertainty is boron incorporation, since CS-2 does not include boron and no provided analogue demonstrates B–Se framework formation from azide-derived Cs. This route is therefore plausible but less structurally directed than Routes 1–2 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains very dissimilar cation environments: large, high-coordinate Cs and small trigonal-planar B, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [target structure; Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network, so those checks are not triggered; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests otherwise. The amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs3BSe3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0493 eV/atom [target data]. The strongest synthesis support comes from the direct chemical analogue Cs3BS3, which was synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state reaction of Cs2S/amorphous B/S at 720 °C (CS-1), and from established Cs–Se ampoule chemistry using Cs2Se at 600–700 °C (CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly analogous Cs3BS3 trigonal boron chalcogenide phase (CS-1). The main limiting factor is kinetic and chemical control of volatile, air-sensitive Cs–Se/B–Se mixtures in sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference from CS-1 and CS-4]. SS and TS analogues do not provide strong chemical guidance for Cs–B–Se and are therefore treated as neutral background evidence.
