### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0076 eV/atom, which is effectively a ground-state prediction within normal computational tolerance (target). Chemically similar Ba₂ZnSb₂, Ba₂CdAs₂, Sr₃AlSb₃, Ba₂ZnAs₂, and Ba₃InAs₃ all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, indicating that nearby alkaline-earth pnictides are thermodynamically stable (CS1–CS5). Structurally and thermodynamically similar phases are also mostly on hull, with one structurally similar Ba–Cd–Sb compound at only 0.0395 eV/atom above hull, still commonly synthesizable (SS4; TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that the target is unsynthesized in the dataset and Ca substitution changes local coordination relative to Ba₂ZnSb₂ (target; CS1).

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 requires sealed, inert or vacuum handling and Pb flux, as demonstrated for Ba₂ZnSb₂ and Ba–Cd–Sb flux growth (CS1; SS4). The method avoids >1000 °C if operated near 900–950 °C, which reduces thermal severity relative to 1200 °C direct solid-state synthesis (SS4; CS3). Deductions arise from sealed/vacuum operation, flux decanting, volatile Sb, toxic Pb/Sb, and air-sensitive Ba/Ca handling (CS1; SS4; Reasonable Inference for Ca). CS1 gives insufficient temperature/time detail for Ba₂ZnSb₂, so the detailed thermal program is inferred mainly from SS4.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Flux growth should improve mass transport compared with dry solid-state reaction because Ba–Cd–Sb analogues are grown from molten Pb with slow cooling (SS4), and Ba₂ZnSb₂ was obtained in Pb flux (CS1). The target elements are not refractory [Reasonable Inference], so diffusion should be manageable in a molten-flux environment. The main kinetic penalty is the need to reorganize from the Ba₂ZnSb₂-like Ibam framework to CaSb₄ tetrahedral local coordination in the target (target; CS1). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS exists for actual Ca ordering kinetics in Ba₂CaSb₂, so that part is uncertain and effectively neutral-to-moderate.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Stoichiometric sealed-tube synthesis is supported by the report that Ba₂ZnSb₂ was repeated from elements in sealed Nb tubes (CS1; TS2). The route likely requires ~950–1050 °C based on sealed Nb pnictide analogues, including Ba₃InAs₃ at 1325 K (CS5). Severity is moderate because sealed Nb/Ta tubes, vacuum/Ar control, and air-sensitive Ba/Ca are required (CS1; CS5; Reasonable Inference for Ca). The evidence does not require high pressure or temperatures above 1400 °C, so those penalties are absent (CS1; CS5).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Direct reaction from dense elemental metals is less kinetically favorable than flux because there is no external transport medium [Reasonable Inference]. However, Ba₂ZnSb₂ has been made by stoichiometric sealed-tube reaction, which supports feasibility for an isostructural Ba-rich antimonide family (CS1; SS2). The target’s low hull energy should help thermodynamic driving force, but cation ordering between Ba and Ca and formation of distorted CaSb₄ tetrahedra may require annealing or repeated reaction [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for Ba₂CaSb₂ diffusion times, so the score remains moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

The high-temperature Ar route is based on Sr₃AlSb₃ synthesis at 1200 °C under Ar in an alumina/corundum crucible (CS3; TS5). It avoids vacuum penalties if truly performed under Ar, but it crosses the >1000 °C threshold and likely requires precautions against Sb volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Related Ba/Sb antimonides are air-sensitive and often need controlled atmosphere handling (SS1; TS1). The weakness is that the closest same-structure Ba₂ZnSb₂ precedent uses flux or sealed Nb rather than open high-temperature Ar synthesis (CS1; SS2).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High-temperature melt/solid-state reaction can be fast; Sr₃AlSb₃ was held only 0.5 h at 1200 °C (CS3; TS5). The kinetics benefit from partial melting and short diffusion lengths in reactive alkaline-earth/pnictogen melts [Reasonable Inference]. However, Route 3 lacks a direct same-Ibam Ba₂CaSb₂ analogue, so structural reconstruction and phase selection are less certain than for flux/sealed-tube routes (CS1; SS2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for pure-phase Ba₂CaSb₂ formation under open Ar, which limits confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains Ba and Ca, whose ionic-size mismatch is large enough to trigger the radius-difference check [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing SbBa₅Ca₂ polyhedra, triggering the mixed-connectivity check (target). The unit-cell atom count is not established as >40 by CS/SS/TS, so that check is not triggered and the source score for that subfactor is neutral, 50. Overall, the amorphization/structural-disorder risk is medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₂CaSb₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is −0.0076 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull (target). The closest support is strong: Ba₂ZnSb₂ has the same orthorhombic Ibam structure type and is both chemically and structurally similar, with successful Pb-flux and sealed-Nb syntheses reported (CS1; SS2; TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the established Ba₂MSb₂-type synthetic precedent in Pb flux/sealed Nb tubes, which should allow low-barrier exploration of Ba–Ca–Sb compositions (CS1; SS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase control: related Ba/Sb and Ba–Cd–Sb systems are air-sensitive and can form secondary phases or require narrow synthesis windows (CS1; SS1; SS5). Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires sealed inert handling and careful stoichiometry/cooling control.
