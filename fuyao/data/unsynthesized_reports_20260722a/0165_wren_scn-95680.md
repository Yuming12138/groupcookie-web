### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.028422701 eV/atom, which is effectively stable within calculation uncertainty and falls in the highest stability bin [Target data]. CS analogues with closely related Rb/K–Au–In and Ae–Pt–Cd chemistries are also computed on the hull and have reported synthesis, supporting high thermodynamic feasibility (CS1–CS5). SS and TS examples are mostly unrelated hydrides, borides, oxides, halides, or nitrides, so they provide insufficient evidence for Rb–Cd–Au thermodynamics and are neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 600 °C, which are directly supported by the Rb(Au₂In₃)₂ and K(Au₂In₃)₂ syntheses (CS1, CS2). The score is reduced because sealed Ta/silica handling, glovebox loading, slow cooling, and long annealing are required (CS1, CS2). Additional penalties are assigned for Rb air sensitivity and Cd toxicity/volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The exact structure-type match to Rb(Au₂In₃)₂ strongly supports feasible nucleation and ordering (CS1). Kinetics are not ideal because Au is dense and not molten at 600 °C, but successful formation of Au–In frameworks at similar temperatures indicates that intermetallic diffusion/alloying is adequate with long annealing (CS1, CS2, CS5). The 160 h anneal and controlled cooling in CS1/CS2 justify a moderate kinetic penalty.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 remains below 1000 °C and does not require external high pressure, matching the general severity of sealed Ta intermetallic syntheses in CS1/CS5. The score is reduced for sealed volatile Cd/Rb handling, air-sensitive Rb, toxic Cd, and extra flux-removal steps [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not provide useful route-specific evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

A Cd-rich melt should improve mass transport and Au dissolution compared with a purely solid-state route [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling and long holds are strongly supported by Rb–Au–In syntheses, where ordered intermetallic phases were obtained after controlled cooling/annealing (CS1, CS5). The main uncertainty is whether excess Cd stabilizes competing Au–Cd binaries rather than the target RbCd₆Au₄ phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is inspired by Cd–Pt intermetallics synthesized at 950 °C in sealed Ta containers followed by quenching and annealing (CS3, CS4). For Rb–Cd–Au, a lower 750–850 °C ceiling is recommended because Rb and Cd are more volatile than Sr/Ba/Pt systems [Reasonable Inference]. Severity remains moderate-to-high due to sealed reactive metal handling, quenching, and annealing.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Higher temperature should improve Au diffusion and homogenization [Reasonable Inference], and CS3/CS4 show Cd–noble-metal intermetallics can form under high-temperature sealed conditions. However, this route lacks the exact P-6m2 Rb(Au₂M₃)₂ structural precedent that Route 1/2 have from CS1/CS2. The high-temperature route may increase competing phase formation or volatile-component loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small-cell crystalline intermetallic with a reported ordered P-6m2 structure and only one clear amorphization trigger, the large size mismatch between Rb and Cd/Au [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. CS1/CS2/CS5 demonstrate that closely related Rb/K–Au–In intermetallics crystallize under slow-cooling sealed-tube conditions. Remaining uncertainty is whether Cd substitution changes ordering kinetics or promotes site disorder relative to In [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative, effectively placing it on the convex hull within computational uncertainty [Target data]. The chemical-similarity evidence is strong because Rb(Au₂In₃)₂ is isostructural with the target and was experimentally synthesized by sealed-tube elemental reaction (CS1). The structurally and thermodynamically similar SS/TS lists are not chemically relevant to Rb–Cd–Au intermetallic chemistry, so they provide insufficient useful transferability and are treated as neutral 50. The key enabling factor is the direct Rb(Au₂M₃)₂ structural analogue in CS1, plus established sealed-Ta synthesis for Rb–Au intermetallics (CS1, CS5). The key limiting factor is maintaining Rb/Cd stoichiometry and suppressing competing Au–Cd/Rb–Cd phases during slow cooling [Reasonable Inference].
