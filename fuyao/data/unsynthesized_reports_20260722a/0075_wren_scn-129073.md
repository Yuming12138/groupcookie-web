### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has a reported E_hull of −0.1248 eV/atom, which places it deep in the “≤0.05 eV/atom” high-stability category. Chemically similar Cs-rich fluorides in CS are mostly on or near the hull: Cs₃CrF₇, Cs₂MnF₅, Cs₂NbF₆, and Cs₃TiF₇ are reported at E_hull = 0, while Cs₃MnF₇ is only 0.0036 eV/atom above hull (CS-1–5). SS and TS entries are chemically unrelated and have NaN similarity fields, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for Cs–Mo–F-specific thermodynamics; both were treated as neutral supporting signals. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the negative hull value reflects a true missing ground-state correction or a computational artifact [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is strongly supported by chemically similar Cs₃MF₇ fluorides made at 300–500 °C for 1–5 days using A₂MF₆ + AF mixtures under dried Ar and diluted F₂ (CS-1/2/5). Its F2 score is penalized by the use of F₂, strict dry atmosphere, multiple handling steps, and toxic/reactive precursors (CS-1/2/5). Kinetically, preformed fluorides and low reaction temperatures help, but the target stoichiometry Cs₃MoF₆ likely requires Mo³⁺, and oxidizing F₂ could push Mo to higher oxidation states [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 1 is feasible but risky for oxidation-state control.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is based on sealed-tube alkali fluoride + metal fluoride annealing at 520 °C, directly demonstrated for Cs₂NbF₆-related fluoride chemistry in CS-4, with dry fluoride handling supported by CS-1/2/5. Its F2 score is reduced by sealed/inert handling, possible Cs/F volatility, and air-sensitive fluoride precursors [Reasonable Inference; CS-4]. Its F3 score is better than Route 1 because CsF-rich mixtures can provide a flux-like transport medium and because avoiding F₂ better preserves Mo³⁺ [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Cs₃MoF₆ competes with other Cs–Mo fluorides such as Cs₂MoF₆- or CsMoF₄-type phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by CS-3, where Cs₂MnF₅ was crystallized from Mn(III) precursor, HF, and excess Cs₂CO₃ at mild conditions. F2 benefits from low temperature and ambient pressure but is penalized for HF toxicity, likely inert handling for Mo³⁺, and additional dehydration/isolation steps [Reasonable Inference; CS-3]. F3 has excellent solution kinetics but poor structural certainty because CS-3 explicitly reports hydrated Cs₂MnF₅·H₂O precipitation, while the target is anhydrous cubic Cs₃MoF₆. The main uncertainty is whether solution chemistry yields the desired anhydrous perovskite-like phase rather than hydrated, oxyfluoride, or lower-Cs phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Cs/Mo size contrast, reflected by Cs–F distances of 2.80 and 3.49 Å versus Mo–F = 2.10 Å in the provided target structure, triggering the cation-radius mismatch criterion. The structure is nevertheless simple and highly ionic, with ordered CsF₆, CsF₁₂, and MoF₆ polyhedra, which should reduce amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. No edge-sharing is described, and there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so only one F4 trigger is counted. The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃MoF₆ is likely thermodynamically stable or at least very close to a ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is −0.1248 eV/atom and chemically similar Cs-rich fluorides are on or near the hull (target data; CS-1–5). The CS evidence is substantially more useful than SS or TS because CS contains actual Cs–transition-metal fluorides synthesized by dry solid-state fluorination, sealed fluoride annealing, and HF crystallization, while SS/TS entries are chemically unrelated and therefore neutral for practical synthesis transfer. The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for low-temperature formation of Cs-rich MF₆-based fluorides at 300–520 °C using alkali fluoride/metal fluoride chemistry (CS-1/2/4/5). The main limiting factor is Mo oxidation-state and phase control: Cs₃MoF₆ charge balance suggests Mo³⁺, whereas oxidizing fluorination or aqueous HF conditions could form higher-valent Mo fluorides, oxyfluorides, or hydrates [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, an anhydrous sealed-tube CsF + MoF₃ route is the most balanced synthesis strategy.
