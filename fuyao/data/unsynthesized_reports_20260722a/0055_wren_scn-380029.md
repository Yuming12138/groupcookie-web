### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.065242397 eV/atom, placing it within the highest stability category by the required rubric (Target). Chemically similar fluoride analogues KTeF5, RbCrF6, RbRuF6, KTe2F9, and BaGeF6 all have E_hull = 0.0, which supports the general viability of related alkali/metal fluoride phases (CS-1–CS-5). Insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for fluoride-specific stability transfer because their examples are chemically unrelated; SS/TS are neutral, 50, for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 is conducted at ambient temperature, which is favorable, but it requires anhydrous HF, elemental F2, UV irradiation, FEP/PTFE vessels, and a PTFE vacuum line in the RbCrF6 analogue (CS-2). The main penalties are reactive atmosphere, toxic/air-sensitive reagents, multiple operational steps, and sealed/contained volatile fluorinating media (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for this route; those sources are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are favorable because the RbCrF6 analogue forms in an anhydrous HF medium at ambient temperature under UV, avoiding long-range solid-state diffusion (CS-2). The target is essentially the same RbMF6 topology as RbCrF6, with RbF12 cuboctahedra, MF6 octahedra, and nearly identical octahedral tilt angles, so structural reconstruction should be minimal (Target; CS-2). The main uncertainty is whether Se can be held in the required Se(V) fluoride state rather than forming other selenium fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 benefits from the RbRuF6 precedent of 350 °C fluorination for only 2 h, avoiding very high temperature or GPa pressure (CS-3). However, hot F2 fluorination is inherently severe and requires specialized gas-handling equipment, and selenium fluorides introduce volatility/toxicity concerns [Reasonable Inference from CS-3]. Insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for hot F2 fluoride synthesis transfer; those sources are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The RbRuF6 analogue indicates that direct fluorination can produce an RbMF6 framework rapidly at 350 °C, suggesting acceptable kinetics (CS-3). The target’s RbF12/SeF6 framework is closely related to RbRuF6, though RbRuF6 is R-3m while the target is R-3, so some symmetry/ordering difference may be involved (CS-3; Target). Uncertainty remains high for gas/solid fluorination of selenium because volatile Se fluorides may escape or form competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses mild slow-evaporation conditions supported by KTeF5 and BaGeF6-type fluoride crystallization examples (CS-1; CS-5). The severity comes not from temperature but from HF, possible oxidants such as F2, and volatile selenium fluoride precursors [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-2/CS-4]. Insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for HF solution crystallization of RbSeF6; those sources are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution crystallization in HF should give high precursor mobility, as KTeF5 crystallizes directly by slow evaporation from KF/TeF4/HF (CS-1). This route avoids cation diffusion limitations and is promising for single-crystal growth, but the known Te analogues have different TeF5/Te–F coordination motifs rather than the target’s RbMF6 octahedral framework (CS-1; CS-4; Target). The central kinetic uncertainty is nucleating RbSeF6 instead of other Rb–Se–F salts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation environments, with large 12-coordinate Rb and much smaller octahedral Se, which triggers the ionic-size-mismatch criterion by reasonable ionic-radius interpretation (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). The target also has mixed corner, edge, and face sharing in the RbF12/SeF6 network, triggering the mixed-connectivity criterion (Target). The unit-cell atom-count criterion is not triggered because no provided evidence indicates >40 atoms per cell; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for a larger target cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbSeF6 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar fluorides are on the hull (Target; CS-1–CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from RbCrF6, which is isostoichiometric and essentially isostructural with the target and is synthesized by photochemical oxidative fluorination in anhydrous HF/F2 (CS-2). HF slow crystallization is also plausible because KTeF5 and related fluorides crystallize from alkali fluoride/chalcogen fluoride/HF media (CS-1; CS-5), though this is less structurally direct. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable RbMF6 frameworks with RbF12 cuboctahedra and MF6 octahedra under fluorinating conditions (CS-2; CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is redox/volatility control of selenium fluoride chemistry, especially maintaining Se in a nominal Se(V) [SeF6]− environment rather than losing volatile SeF4/SeF6 or forming competing salts [Reasonable Inference].
