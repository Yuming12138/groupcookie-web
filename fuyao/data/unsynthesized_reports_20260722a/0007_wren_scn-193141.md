### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.096636479 eV/atom, which places it effectively on or below the convex hull and therefore receives the maximum thermodynamic score. Several TS entries are also near-hull or on-hull, but they are chemically unrelated hydrides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so TS offers only weak generic evidence and is otherwise insufficient for Rb–Ir oxide stability. The main uncertainty is that a negative hull energy may reflect database/reference artifacts rather than experimentally guaranteed stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because CS-1 and CS-4 require sealed noble-metal tubes, reactive alkali oxides/peroxides/superoxides, controlled dry environments, and long staged heating. The peak temperature is not extreme because CS-4 succeeds at 800 °C, but operational severity remains high due to sealed-tube oxidizing alkali chemistry. SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific support, so they are neutral here.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are only moderate-to-poor because IrO₂ is expected to be refractory and the target structure is not the same as the tetrahedral Cs₂FeO₄/Cs₂RuO₄ analogues [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4’s 31 d dwell at 800 °C suggests slow cation ordering or slow diffusion. The reactive alkali oxide medium improves activity relative to a dry oxide route, but not enough to remove the reconstruction penalty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has simpler equipment than sealed-tube or flux methods, with ceramic oxide synthesis supported by CS-5 and high-temperature oxide flux/solid synthesis supported by CS-3. It is penalized for likely >1000 °C firing, controlled O₂ atmosphere, and repeated grinding/firing [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that CS-5 involves Ba–Zr oxide chemistry, not volatile Rb–Ir oxide chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are the weakest for the ceramic route because IrO₂ is refractory and solid-state diffusion without flux is expected slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 shows direct oxide formation at 1200 °C for Ba₂ZrO₄, but that evidence does not solve Rb volatility or high Ir oxidation-state control. SS and TS provide insufficient direct kinetic evidence for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 requires sealed or dry controlled conditions because Rb-rich oxidizing fluxes are moisture-sensitive and volatile [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the sealed alkali oxide handling in CS-1 and CS-4. It avoids the >1000 °C penalty if low-melting Rb halide/oxide fluxes are used [Reasonable Inference], unlike the Ba₂TiO₄ flux example at 1340 °C in CS-3. Multi-step flux handling and possible air-sensitive oxidants keep the severity score moderate.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because molten flux can improve transport, dissolve/reprecipitate refractory oxide species, and allow slow crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 directly demonstrates successful flux growth of Ba₂TiO₄ from BaCl₂/BaCO₃/TiO₂ melt, while CS-1/CS-4 support alkali-rich oxidizing environments. The remaining uncertainty is whether Ir dissolves sufficiently and whether Rb₃IrO₆ is stable during flux removal [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Rb and Ir coordination environments, triggering one amorphization risk check [Reasonable Inference from ionic radii and target coordination description]. The target description includes corner and face sharing, but no edge sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check requiring vertex + edge + face coexist is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not shown to exceed 40, so that check remains untriggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₃IrO₆ is predicted to be thermodynamically favorable because its reported E_hull is negative, so it should be considered a likely ground-state or near-ground-state phase rather than a strongly metastable one. The chemical analogues support the feasibility of sealed alkali-rich oxidative synthesis for high-valent transition-metal oxides, especially Cs₂FeO₄ and Cs₂RuO₄ (CS-1, CS-4), but no provided analogue directly matches Rb₃IrO₆ with IrO₆ octahedra. The strongest enabling factor is the very favorable computed thermodynamic stability combined with known alkali peroxide/superoxide synthetic routes. The strongest limiting factor is simultaneous control of Rb volatility, oxygen chemical potential, and Ir oxidation state [Reasonable Inference]. A reactive Rb-rich flux is the best compromise because it improves kinetics while preserving high alkali activity.
