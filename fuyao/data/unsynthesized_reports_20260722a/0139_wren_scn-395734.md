### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.05397 eV/atom, which is effectively stable and receives a high score of 98 under the provided ≤0.05 eV/atom criterion (Target data). Close chemical/structural analogues ScNi₄Au and ZrNi₄In are computed at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and LuNi₄Sn is only 0.00148 eV/atom above hull, supporting stability of this AB₄C F-43m family (CS2, CS4, CS5). SS provides insufficient useful signal for Zr–Ni–Pt thermodynamics because the structurally listed materials are chemically unrelated and have NaN similarity fields; neutral 50 from SS. TS also provides insufficient useful signal for this target because the thermodynamic entries are unrelated chemistries despite being near-hull; neutral 50 from TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + anneal

Condition severity is moderate because CS4 and CS5 show analogous phases prepared by arc-melting under Ar and homogenization/annealing at 800 °C, which avoids high pressure and exotic atmospheres (CS4, CS5). The penalty comes mainly from likely high transient melting temperature and possible evacuated/sealed annealing, since CS5 uses evacuated vials and long annealing (CS5; Reasonable Inference). Kinetically, arc-melting strongly improves mixing, but final ordering may remain slow because CS5 required 700 h annealing and slow cooling (CS5). The route is strongly supported by the same F-43m structure in ScNi₄Au and ZrNi₄In (CS4, CS5), leaving the main uncertainty as Pt-specific phase competition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct solid-state sintering

The conditions are not extreme in temperature or pressure, but the need for sealed inert/vacuum handling and repeated grinding lowers F2 (CS2, CS5; Reasonable Inference). CS2 proves that a same-structure LuNi₄Sn analogue can form by a long solid-phase reaction at 670 K for 720 h, but the target’s Zr/Pt-containing composition is likely slower because Zr and Pt are refractory and less mobile than Sn-containing systems [Reasonable Inference]. The F3 score is therefore lower because dense metal powders have limited activity and atomic diffusion is the rate-limiting step. Remaining uncertainty is whether a low-temperature ordering window exists for ZrNi₄Pt comparable to LuNi₄Sn (CS2).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Melt growth / Czochralski-type growth

F2 is lower than Route 1 because full melt growth likely requires >1400 °C and multi-step operation, as inferred from high-melting constituents and the specialized Czochralski/pulling setup described for CeCu₅Au and URu₄B₄-type materials (CS1, SS2/TS2; Reasonable Inference). F3 is highest because liquid-state diffusion and slow solidification provide favorable kinetic access to ordered intermetallics, and related noble-metal/Ni-rich phases are melt-processable (CS1, CS3–CS5). The uncertainty is crucible compatibility and segregation during solidification, especially due to reactive Zr and high-melting Pt [Reasonable Inference]. SS2/TS2 support the generic feasibility of tri-arc Czochralski growth but are chemically less relevant than CS1–CS5, so they are weighted lightly.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a significant metal-size mismatch, especially between Zr and Ni, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target and close F-43m analogues are compact intermetallic structures rather than large >40-atom unit cells, so C2 is not triggered (CS2, CS4, CS5). C3 is triggered because the target explicitly contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing cuboctahedra, and the same type of mixed connectivity is present in the F-43m analogues ScNi₄Au and ZrNi₄In (CS4, CS5). The resulting F4 = 55 reflects medium amorphization/ordering risk rather than a fundamental barrier.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ZrNi₄Pt is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and close F-43m analogues are on or nearly on the hull (Target data; CS2, CS4, CS5). The CS evidence is strong: ScNi₄Au and ZrNi₄In share the same cubic F-43m Laves-derived structure and are prepared by conventional intermetallic routes (CS4, CS5). SS and TS evidence is weak for chemistry transfer because those entries are mostly unrelated compounds with NaN similarity fields, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory. The key enabling factor is the existence of multiple same-structure AB₄C Ni-rich intermetallic analogues accessible by arc-melting/annealing (CS2, CS4, CS5). The key limiting factor is likely slow long-range ordering and possible competing Zr–Ni, Ni–Pt, or Zr–Pt binary/ternary phases during annealing [Reasonable Inference].
