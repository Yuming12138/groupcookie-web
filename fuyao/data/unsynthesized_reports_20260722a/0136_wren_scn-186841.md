### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s exact E_hull is −0.004823208 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable and clearly within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-score range. Thermodynamic analogues include Sr₂(Au₂Zn)₃, CaAuCd, SrAuCd, CaPdCd₂, and SrPdCd₂, all with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee a broad experimental phase field or easy kinetic access [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses conditions directly supported by Sr–Au–Zn induction melting in sealed Ta tubes at 1273 K and controlled cooling/quenching (SS1/TS1). Deductions arise from high-temperature operation near or above 1000 °C, sealed-system need for Zn volatility [Reasonable Inference], multiple thermal steps, and Ca air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS evidence supports high pressure or reactive gas use, so those penalties are not applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

The closest analogue Sr₂(Au₂Zn)₃ forms by a short high-temperature induction melt followed by annealing, indicating favorable liquid-assisted kinetics for Au–Zn alkaline-earth intermetallics (SS1/TS1). The target has Au–Zn distances of 2.65 Å, close to the analogue’s Au–Zn distances of 2.66–2.84 Å, supporting structural transferability (target description; SS1/TS1). Uncertainty remains because the target is Ca-based and I4/mmm, while the analogue is Sr-based and R-3c.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is based on CaAuCd melt reactions in Ta crucibles at 1100 °C under Ar with controlled heating/cooling (CS5/SS2/TS2). The penalties are similar to Route 1: >1000 °C temperature, sealed containment for Zn [Reasonable Inference], multi-step handling, and Ca air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. No high-pressure or H₂/NH₃/vacuum requirement is supported by CS/SS/TS.

#### F3 — Route 2

CaAuCd has Ca–Au bonding and CdAu₄ tetrahedra analogous to target ZnAu₄ tetrahedra, suggesting similar local ordering kinetics (CS5/SS2/TS2; target description). Slow cooling from a melt should help overcome diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference], and the analogue was obtained by a straightforward tube-furnace melt reaction (CS5/SS2/TS2). The limiting uncertainty is that Cd→Zn substitution may change volatility and phase stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is supported by Sr(AuGa)₂ melt growth at 1425 K with 50 K/h cooling (CS1). The temperature exceeds 1000 °C but not 1400 °C, and no high-pressure route is indicated by CS/SS/TS. Deductions also include Zn volatility requiring sealed ampoules [Reasonable Inference], multi-step controlled solidification, and Ca air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Melt growth gives the best kinetic access because full melting reduces solid-state diffusion limitations, as shown for Sr(AuGa)₂ and Ca–Au–In/Cd intermetallic analogues (CS1, CS3, CS5). The target shares the alkaline-earth Au–p-block 1:2:2 composition pattern with Sr(AuGa)₂, supporting transferability (CS1; target description). The main uncertainty is structural: CS1 is P4/nmm while the target is I4/mmm.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 uses lower annealing temperatures, reducing thermal severity relative to Routes 1–3, but CS2 explicitly uses vacuum annealing, which carries a controlled-atmosphere penalty. Long anneal and possible premelt/regrind/reseal steps make the process multi-step (CS2; [Reasonable Inference]). Zn volatility and Ca air sensitivity remain limiting factors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Long lower-temperature annealing can equilibrate related intermetallics, as CS2 reports about one week at 750–800 °C and SS/TS examples use annealing after melting (CS2; SS5/TS5). However, elemental Au and intermetallic alloy diffusion can be slow at 600–800 °C [Reasonable Inference]. This route is most useful as a post-treatment after premelting rather than as a standalone direct synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed edge- and corner-sharing ZnAu₄ tetrahedra, directly triggering the mixed-connectivity criterion (target description). A large Ca/Zn/Au size mismatch is likely based on metallic radii [Reasonable Inference], while the target formula and I4/mmm description provide no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell. The resulting F4 = 60 reflects moderate disorder/amorphization risk rather than a severe glass-forming tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca(ZnAu)₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is −0.004823208 eV/atom, and multiple thermodynamic analogues are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS1–TS5). The strongest support comes from Sr₂(Au₂Zn)₃, which is both structurally and thermodynamically similar and was synthesized by sealed-tube induction melting from elements (SS1/TS1). The most critical enabling factor is liquid-assisted intermetallic formation from elemental Ca/Sr–Au–Zn/Cd/Ga systems under Ar in refractory-metal containers, which is repeatedly supported by CS/SS/TS analogues (CS1, CS3, CS5, SS1/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Zn stoichiometry during high-temperature synthesis because Zn is volatile at the proposed temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limitation is phase competition and ordering control, as CaAuCd analogues report multiple competing phases and narrow homogeneity ranges (CS5/SS2/TS2).
