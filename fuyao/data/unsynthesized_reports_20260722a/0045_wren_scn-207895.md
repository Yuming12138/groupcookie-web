### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.117 eV/atom, which places it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability scoring band. CS gives strong corroboration that related ACrF₆ phases, including KCrF₆, are computed on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS-1 to CS-4). TS examples are low-hull synthesized materials but chemically unrelated, so TS provides only generic support for the idea that low-hull phases are synthesizable and otherwise insufficient target-specific evidence from TS. The main uncertainty is that a negative hull may reflect database/reference-energy issues rather than a guaranteed experimentally known ground state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high mechanical pressure because ACrF₆ photochemical syntheses occur at ambient temperature and low F₂ pressure or F₂ atmosphere (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). The severity score is reduced substantially because the route uses F₂, anhydrous HF, UV irradiation, FEP/PTFE equipment, and moisture-sensitive fluoride handling (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). SS and TS do not provide relevant fluoride-condition evidence, so insufficient evidence from SS/TS and neutral scoring are appropriate. The remaining uncertainty is whether Mo fluorides will undergo clean oxidation without volatile MoF₆ or lower fluoride byproducts [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The HF/F₂ photochemical medium should provide high precursor activity and low diffusion limitation because chemically analogous ACrF₆ phases form at ambient temperature over 10–20 d (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). KCrF₆ is especially valuable because it matches the K–M–F₆ formula class and trigonal R-3 symmetry (CS-3). The structural score is not maximal because target KMoF₆ has six-coordinate K in KF₆ octahedra, while KCrF₆ has 12-coordinate K in KF₁₂ cuboctahedra (CS-3; target comparison is [Reasonable Inference]). The main kinetic uncertainty is crystallization of the predicted “Upper Bainite-derived” corner-sharing network rather than a more common alkali hexafluorometallate packing [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses only moderate temperature, since SrCrF₆/BaCrF₆ autoclave syntheses are reported at up to 300 °C (CS-2, CS-4). It is nevertheless severe because 300 atm Ar/F₂ in Monell autoclaves, fluorine handling, and careful pressure operations are required (CS-2, CS-4). SS/TS do not add fluoride autoclave evidence, so insufficient evidence from SS/TS and neutral signal are assigned. The key uncertainty is whether K/Mo precursors will fluorinate to the desired KMoF₆ rather than volatile Mo fluorides or competing potassium molybdenum fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Gas-phase F₂ is highly reactive, but gas-solid fluorination of oxides/chlorides is less kinetically intimate than HF solution photochemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-4]. CS-2/CS-4 report phase-control problems including volatile chromium fluoride species and hard-to-obtain pure samples, which lowers the expected kinetics/phase score for an analogous Mo system. The structural match is weaker than Route 1 because Sr/BaCrF₆ are alkaline-earth chromates in R-3m-type structures rather than K-based R-3 KMoF₆ (CS-2, CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS source directly reports molybdenum autoclave fluorination to KMoF₆.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 may operate at low temperature because it uses the pre-oxidized Mo(V) fluoride MoF₅ and KF as a fluoride donor [Reasonable Inference]. The severity remains significant because MoF₅ and HF-type fluoride chemistry require dry, sealed, corrosion-resistant handling, consistent with the FEP/PTFE and anhydrous HF practices in CS (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). SS/TS provide insufficient relevant evidence for this direct complexation method. The uncertainty is that MoF₅ volatility and possible disproportionation or conversion to other Mo fluorides are not addressed by the provided data [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Kinetically, KF + MoF₅ is attractive because it directly supplies K⁺, Mo(V), and the sixth fluoride needed for [MoF₆]⁻ [Reasonable Inference]. Use of anhydrous HF could improve contact and crystallization, consistent with CS photochemical fluoride media (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). However, this route lacks direct CS evidence for MoF₅ + KF producing the target R-3 network, so structural reconstruction and polymorph control are uncertain. SS/TS are chemically irrelevant for this route and provide neutral evidence only.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large K/Mo cation size contrast, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target description only reports corner-sharing KF₆/MoF₆ octahedra and does not report mixed corner/edge/face connectivity, so C3 is not triggered. The target formula has only eight atoms per formula unit and no provided evidence for >40 atoms per unit cell, so C2 is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the unusual six-coordinate K octahedron will crystallize cleanly or relax toward the 12-coordinate K environment seen in KCrF₆ (CS-3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KMoF₆ is likely thermodynamically stable or at least very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.117 eV/atom, and chemically similar ACrF₆ phases are on the hull and experimentally synthesized (CS-1 to CS-4). The strongest synthesis support comes from KCrF₆, which is a direct K–transition-metal hexafluoride analogue prepared by photochemical AF/CrF₃/F₂ chemistry in anhydrous HF (CS-3). The most important enabling factor is the availability of highly reactive fluorinating media—F₂/HF/UV or high-pressure F₂—that can stabilize high-valent metal fluorides at low-to-moderate temperature (CS-1 to CS-5). The most important limiting factor is phase and volatility control, because related high-valent chromium fluorides show moisture sensitivity, thermal instability, volatile fluoride losses, and difficulty obtaining pure phases (CS-2 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues do not materially improve route selection because they are chemically unrelated to fluoromolybdates, so their route-specific evidence is insufficient.
