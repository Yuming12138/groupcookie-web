### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an E_hull of −0.020435 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable thermodynamic scoring bin from the prompt. Close Sr–Mg/Ba–Mg/Ca–Mg hydride analogues are all reported at or very near the hull, including SrMgH4 at 0.0 eV/atom and Sr₂MgH₆ at 0.0062 eV/atom (CS1, CS4). This supports that alkaline-earth/light-metal hydrides can be thermodynamically viable, but the exact Sr–Be–H phase diagram is not experimentally validated in the provided data [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS are chemically unrelated to Sr–Be hydrides, so they provide insufficient evidence and are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 Condition Severity

Route 1 inherits demanding H₂ autoclave requirements from SrMgH4 and related Sr/Ba/Ca–Mg hydrides, which use 53–96 bar H₂/D₂, ~700–760 K, and glovebox/inert handling (CS1–CS3, CS5). The temperature is moderate, but the reactive H₂ atmosphere, sealed high-pressure vessel, multistep alloying/hydrogenation sequence, and air-sensitive handling lower the F2 score. Be toxicity is a target-specific penalty [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS offer no relevant mitigation for hydride autoclave synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 1 Reaction Kinetics

The closest structural/chemical analogue SrMgH4 is directly synthesized by alloy hydrogenation and shares Cmc2₁ symmetry with the target (CS1), which is favorable for transferability. However, the reaction requires full conversion of a metallic alloy into a complex hydride framework and takes 6–8 days in analogues (CS1, CS2), indicating slow kinetics. Be is smaller, more refractory, and oxide-passivated relative to Mg, likely slowing incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. This motivates a moderate-low F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2 Condition Severity

Route 2 uses very specialized multi-anvil equipment but, under the prompt’s deduction table, 35 kbar / 3.5 GPa does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty (CS4). It avoids flowing H₂ if SrH₂ + BeH₂ are used, but still requires glovebox handling and multiple high-pressure preparation steps (CS4). The largest severity not fully captured by the table is BeH₂/Be toxicity and hydride air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Thus F2 is numerically better than Route 1 despite being less accessible experimentally.

#### F3 — Route 2 Reaction Kinetics

Binary hydride precursors are chemically closer to SrBeH4 than a metallic alloy, and SrH₂ + MgH₂ successfully produced Sr₂MgH₆ under multi-anvil conditions (CS4). High pressure and hydride starting materials should help maintain H content and reduce complete structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether BeH₂ can be obtained, handled, and reacted without decomposition or amorphous Be–H byproducts [Reasonable Inference]. Impurities in Sr₂MgH₆ synthesis show phase purity may still be challenging (CS4).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The large Sr²⁺/Be²⁺ size mismatch triggers one amorphization risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure itself is not reported as a very large-cell material, and the description includes corner and edge sharing but no face-sharing coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered by the provided target description. Hydride frameworks can crystallize well in close analogues under sufficiently high H₂ pressure or high-pressure solid-state conditions (CS1, CS4). The remaining uncertainty is whether BeHₓ units form crystalline BeH5-like coordination rather than disordered polymeric Be–H environments [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrBeH4 is predicted to be a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is negative within numerical tolerance, and close alkaline-earth/light-metal hydrides are also on or near the hull (Target, CS1–CS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1, where SrMgH4 shares both composition type and Cmc2₁ symmetry with the target. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high hydrogen chemical potential, achieved either by H₂ autoclave conditions in Route 1 or hydride-rich multi-anvil conditions in Route 2 (CS1–CS4). The most critical limiting factor is Be chemistry: Be is toxic, refractory/passivated as a metal, and BeH₂ availability/reactivity is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material appears synthetically plausible but best attempted in specialized hydride/high-pressure laboratories.
