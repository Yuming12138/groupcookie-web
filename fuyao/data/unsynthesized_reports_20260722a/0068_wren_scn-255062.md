### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0986 eV/atom, which is well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability band and therefore receives a high F1 score [Target data]. CS supports the plausibility of stable alkali fluorides because Rb₂GeF₆, RbCrF₆, and KFeF₄ are all on or very near the hull (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS entries are mostly unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient chemical evidence for K–Se–F stability and are treated as neutral 50 for source-specific signal.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Sealed-tube solid-state / in-situ fluorination

The condition severity is moderate because CS-1 used 660 °C for 2 d in a sealed Cu tube under Ar, which is not extreme temperature or pressure but does require sealed fluoride handling. The kinetic score is decent because CS-1 directly forms an A₂MF₆ compound with alkali-F cuboctahedra and MF₆ octahedra, but elemental Se fluorination and K-specific trigonal distortion require extrapolation [Reasonable Inference from CS-1]. Remaining uncertainty is the possible formation of competing K–Se–O–F, K₂SeO₃, or volatile selenium fluoride species [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — Hydrothermal HF / fluoride-mineralizer route

The condition severity is favorable because CS-3 and CS-4 demonstrate alkali fluorides can crystallize hydrothermally at ~200–230 °C, avoiding high-temperature sealed-tube severity. The kinetic score is the best because HF/H₂O or HF-rich media provide rapid ion transport and high fluoride activity, as shown by RbFe₂F₆ and KFeF₄ syntheses (CS-3, CS-4). The main uncertainty is that CS-3/CS-4 involve Fe fluorides rather than Se fluoroselenates, so selenium redox/speciation control is inferred rather than directly proven [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — Anhydrous HF / F₂ photochemical route

The kinetic environment is highly activating because ACrF₆ compounds were prepared at ambient temperature in anhydrous HF/F₂ under UV irradiation (CS-2, CS-5). However, condition severity is high because CS-2/CS-5 require F₂, anhydrous HF, FEP/PTFE equipment, UV irradiation, and long 10–20 d reaction times. The largest uncertainty is selectivity: F₂ may over-oxidize Se relative to the Se(IV) state required by K₂SeF₆ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large K–Se cation size mismatch based on their stated coordination environments, so one amorphization-risk trigger is assigned [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The small formula and simple crystallographic description suggest the unit cell is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms, and the described connectivity includes corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the other two checks are not triggered [Target data]. The resulting risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂SeF₆ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very low-energy phase because the reported hull energy is negative, which strongly favors synthesizability despite possible computational reference uncertainty [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support comes from CS, especially Rb₂GeF₆ as a chemically close A₂MF₆ analogue with alkali-F cuboctahedra and MF₆ octahedra (CS-1), plus hydrothermal potassium fluoride formation from KF/HF media (CS-4). SS and TS analogues do not give meaningful chemical or route-specific support because they are compositionally unrelated, so their contribution is neutral. The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride activity in a liquid or sealed fluoride medium, supported by HF hydrothermal and sealed-fluoride precedents (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is selenium speciation/volatility, especially avoiding oxyfluoride impurities or over-oxidation in strongly fluorinating media [Reasonable Inference].
