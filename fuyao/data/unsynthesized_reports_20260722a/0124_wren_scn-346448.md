### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.047946612 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and giving a high F1 score [Target data]. Chemically similar FeP4 analogues with E_hull = 0–0.028 eV/atom are experimentally synthesized by high pressure, CVT, or flux methods, supporting that near-hull anion-rich phases can be accessible (CS2–CS4). SS/TS also contain stable or near-stable entries, but they are chemically unrelated to Ti–S and therefore provide only neutral general thermodynamic support; insufficient Ti–S-specific evidence from SS/TS. Remaining uncertainty is that a negative computed hull value does not guarantee finite-temperature or sulfur-vapor stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state anneal

Route 1 avoids external high pressure and >1000 °C conditions, which is favorable relative to high-pressure FeP4/CrP4 routes (CS2, CS5). It still requires evacuated sealed ampoules and volatile sulfur handling, analogous in operational severity to sealed FeP4 CVT/flux routes (CS3, CS4) [Reasonable Inference]. The multi-step staged heating is needed to avoid sulfur pressure excursions [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not provide Ti–S-specific severity evidence, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state anneal

Solid-state kinetics are limited by refractory Ti diffusion and the need to form unusual isolated S clusters [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals are plausible because FeP4 CVT and flux routes required 30 d and 3 weeks, respectively (CS3, CS4). The target is structurally unlike simple TiS₂ or elemental precursors, so reconstruction is substantial [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. This justifies a relatively low F3 score.

#### F2 — Route 2: halogen-assisted vapor transport

Route 2 inherits the controlled vacuum/ampoule and toxic halogen penalties directly from Br₂ CVT and I₂-assisted FeP4 syntheses (CS3, CS4). It avoids external high pressure and likely avoids >1000 °C because sulfur transport can occur at lower temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. The route remains challenging due to volatile sulfur and halogen pressure control [Reasonable Inference]. SS4/TS4 support that halogen compounds can require long low-temperature vacuum/dry-N₂ treatments, but they are not Ti–S-specific.

#### F3 — Route 2: halogen-assisted vapor transport

CVT improves kinetics by moving material through vapor-phase species, as shown by FeP4 crystal growth using Br₂ in a temperature gradient (CS3). This partially bypasses refractory-metal solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. However, FeP4 CVT also shows phase-control difficulty among FeP2 and multiple FeP4 polymorphs (CS3), so the structural-reconstruction score is not high. Route 2 receives the best F3 because it most directly addresses mass transport and cluster assembly.

#### F2 — Route 3: high-pressure sulfur-rich synthesis

High-pressure synthesis receives the largest severity penalty because FeP4 and CrP4 analogues require 60 kbar or 15–65 kbar conditions (CS2, CS5). The target route would also require sulfur containment in a capsule, which adds volatile-component risk [Reasonable Inference]. Although high-pressure assembly avoids evacuated quartz ampoules, it requires specialized multianvil equipment like the cubic/tetrahedral anvil devices used in CS2 and CS5. SS/TS provide insufficient Ti–S high-pressure evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3: high-pressure sulfur-rich synthesis

High pressure can enable short reaction times for anion-rich phases, as FeP4 was made in 30 min at 60 kbar and 1100 °C and CrP4/MoP4 in 2 h at 15–65 kbar (CS2, CS5). For TiS14, lower proposed temperature may slow Ti diffusion, but pressure should suppress sulfur loss and improve contact [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s 0D cluster character is not matched by the extended FeP4/CrP4 structures, limiting confidence in structural transferability [Target data; CS2, CS5]. Thus F3 is moderate rather than high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ti, so there is no cation-radius mismatch trigger [Target data]. The likely large cluster-containing unit cell triggers the atom-count criterion [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The structure is zero-dimensional with isolated S and TiS2 clusters, so mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing is not indicated [Target data]. The amorphization risk is therefore low-to-medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a computed ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is −0.047946612 eV/atom [Target data]. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar anion-rich FeP4/CrP4 analogues, which are accessible by CVT, flux, and high-pressure routes despite phase-control challenges (CS2–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high sulfur chemical potential while enabling mass transport, best addressed by sealed sulfur/halogen vapor transport [Reasonable Inference; CS3, CS4]. The most critical limiting factor is the unusual TiS14 stoichiometry with isolated S clusters and TiS2 clusters, for which no direct Ti–S analogue is provided [Target data; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS]. SS/TS mainly show that structurally/thermodynamically near-hull materials can be synthesized by diverse methods, but they are chemically too dissimilar to strongly validate TiS14 synthesis.
