### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The supplied target E_hull is −0.0748 eV/atom, which is in the most favorable rubric bin and therefore receives 100/100. Chemically similar fluorides in CS have low E_hull values from 0.0 to ~0.048 eV/atom, supporting the broader idea that fluoride phases in this chemical family can be near the convex hull (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS are chemically unrelated for Cs–Sr–F synthesis, so they provide insufficient evidence for this factor and are treated as neutral, 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly because CS-1 and CS-2 use reactive F2 at ~350 bar in specialized autoclaves. The temperature itself is moderate, around 400 °C, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS-1, CS-2). The remaining uncertainty is whether such strongly fluorinating conditions stabilize the target or instead over-fluorinate/decompose by side reactions [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
High-pressure F2 improves reaction kinetics through aggressive halogen exchange, as shown by CsCuF4 from CsCuCl3 and Cs2CuF6 from CsF/CuF3 (CS-1, CS-2). However, binary fluoride precursors are relatively stable and the target structure lacks a close CS structural match, so structural reconstruction is a limiting kinetic factor [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 reports difficulty obtaining pure samples, which reduces the kinetics/phase-control score.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 avoids autoclave pressure but still requires toxic, corrosive F2 gas, so it receives deductions for reactive atmosphere and hazardous precursor handling (CS-3, CS-5). Direct fluorination at 270–500 °C is well supported for other complex fluorides, so no high-temperature penalty is applied (CS-3, CS-5). The main uncertainty is whether ambient F2 chemical potential is sufficient for the unusual target stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Direct F2 gas reactions can form complex fluorides in short to overnight times, as shown by Rb2PdF6 and K2TbF6 analogues (CS-3, CS-5). Kinetics are still limited by the low activity of SrF2 and the need to form an ordered Cs/Sr fluoride framework from binary precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The route is scored higher than sealed solid-state annealing because F2 gas provides a reactive transport/fluorination pathway (CS-3, CS-5).

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 uses no deliberate high pressure and stays below 1000 °C, but it requires sealed, dry, fluorine-compatible containment [Reasonable Inference]. The BaFeF4 analogue required a sealed Fe tube in quartz at 750 °C for 60 days, showing that such fluoride syntheses can be demanding even without F2 pressure (CS-4). The route is less hazardous than direct F2 if no strong fluorine donor is used, but less chemically forceful.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The solid-state fluoride route is kinetically disadvantaged because stable binary fluorides must interdiffuse and reconstruct into the target lattice [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports this concern because BaFeF4 required very long annealing and gave inhomogeneous products with competing phases. The score is therefore the lowest among the routes.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Cs/Sr size contrast, evident from Cs–F = 3.03 Å versus Sr–F = 2.52 Å in the supplied target structure, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The formula unit is small and the target description does not report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS and TS do not provide useful amorphization evidence for this fluoride target and are neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable by the supplied negative E_hull, but its formal CsSrF6 stoichiometry is chemically unusual for Cs⁺ and Sr²⁺ fluorides, so experimental realization may depend on whether the computed structure/formula is chemically consistent [Reasonable Inference]. The CS analogues strongly support the feasibility of making Cs-containing and complex fluorides under F2, especially at 270–500 °C or under ~350 bar F2 (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct evidence for Cs–Sr–F synthesis. The most critical enabling factor is access to a dry, strongly fluorinating environment that can overcome the stability of binary fluorides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against CsF, SrF2, and possible lower-fluoride or mis-stoichiometric Cs–Sr–F phases [Reasonable Inference].
