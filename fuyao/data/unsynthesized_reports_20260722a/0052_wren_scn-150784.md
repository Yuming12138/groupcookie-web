### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.0753 eV/atom, which is in the highest stability category by the requested rubric. Chemically similar fluoride references are stable or nearly stable, with E_hull values from 0.0 to ~0.028 eV/atom (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). TS entries also show near-hull synthesizable materials, but they are chemically unrelated to LiReF6, so they provide only weak generic thermodynamic support; SS gives insufficient fluoride-specific thermodynamic signal and is treated as neutral.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, consistent with sealed-tube fluoride syntheses at 550–1000 °C in CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5. The deductions arise from sealed dry atmosphere, likely volatile ReF5, and moisture-sensitive/toxic fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this fluoride route and are scored neutral for condition transferability.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are moderate because binary fluorides can be directly reactive in sealed tubes, as shown in CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5. However, LiF is comparatively inert and forming an ordered LiF6/ReF6 corner-sharing network requires cation ordering [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports similar corner-sharing MF6 octahedral motifs, but with different chemistry and difficult phase behavior.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is more severe because F2 at hundreds of atm to kbar is required in the closest high-valent fluoride analogues (CS-2, CS-3). Although the temperature is modest, F2 toxicity/corrosivity, pressure hardware, and multistep handling justify a lower score. SS/TS are not useful for F2 autoclave chemistry and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics benefit from reactive fluorination, since CS-2 and CS-3 form complex fluorides from oxide/carbonate precursors at 300–400 °C. The main kinetic penalty is that oxide-to-fluoride conversion requires complete anion exchange and oxidation-state control [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 and CS-3 both warn of decomposition, volatility, and phase-purity challenges in high-valent fluoride systems.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has the same sealed/reactive-handling deductions as Route 1 but may operate at lower effective reaction temperature due to flux-mediated transport [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 supports sealed Au-tube fluoride synthesis at 550 °C, while CS-4 and CS-5 show sealed noble-metal tubes are broadly applicable to fluoride synthesis. The route avoids external F2 pressure, so it is less severe than Route 2.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The flux/self-flux concept improves kinetics by increasing mass transport and crystallinity [Reasonable Inference]. This is consistent with the fact that sealed fluoride systems can form crystalline products under moderate-to-high temperatures in CS-1, CS-4, and CS-5. Remaining uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS entry explicitly demonstrates LiReF6 or ReF5 flux growth.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a simple corner-sharing octahedral framework, and the description does not include edge/face-sharing coexistence, which lowers amorphization risk. One risk trigger is the significant size/chemistry mismatch between Li⁺ and Re⁵⁺ in octahedral sites [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 shows that corner-sharing MF6/M′F6 networks with similar tilt angles can crystallize, but its phase chemistry is not identical.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiReF6 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is negative and well within the most favorable stability bin. The strongest support comes from chemically similar fluoride references showing sealed-tube and F2-pressure synthesis of complex fluorides with MF6 octahedra (CS-1 to CS-5). The most enabling factor is the chemically natural Li⁺/Re⁵⁺/F⁻ charge balance and the strong thermodynamic stability [Reasonable Inference supported by target E_hull and CS near-hull fluorides]. The main limiting factor is controlling Re(V) fluoride volatility and redox state during synthesis [Reasonable Inference], especially because high-valent fluoride analogues show decomposition, hydrolysis sensitivity, or impurity issues (CS-2, CS-3). SS and TS analogues are mostly non-fluoride and therefore provide insufficient direct synthesis-transfer evidence; they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory.
