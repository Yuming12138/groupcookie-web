### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.008641262 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable and falls in the highest score bin. This is reinforced by Li2SnSe3 being on the hull and sharing the enargite-like Cc structure with Li2GeSe3 (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). Na2GeSe3 and Na4Ge2Se5 are also hull phases in the Na–Ge–Se space, indicating that Ge–Se alkali chalcogenide frameworks are thermodynamically credible (SS #2, #5; TS #2–3). Remaining uncertainty is that a negative hull value is computational and does not by itself prove kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses the exact Li2SnSe3 precedent at 650 °C in high-vacuum sealed silica ampoules with controlled heating/cooling (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). The temperature is moderate, but vacuum sealing, volatile Se retention, Li2Se air sensitivity, and multiple handling/heating steps lower the condition-severity score [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS all provide useful signal for this route because the same analogue appears in all three datasets (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). The main uncertainty is whether Ge reacts as readily as Sn under identical conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic score is high because Li2SnSe3 already forms the same Cc corner-sharing tetrahedral network from Li2Se + Sn + Se (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). Ge substitution should require only modest framework adjustment rather than wholesale structural invention [Reasonable Inference]. Ge–Se tetrahedral chemistry is supported by Na2GeSe3 and Na4Ge2Se5 analogues (SS #2, #5; TS #2–3). The remaining uncertainty is possible competition with other Li–Ge–Se phases not listed in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is less operationally complex because it uses commercially available elements and borrows the Li2TeSe3 elemental sealed-ampoule protocol at 460 °C for 48 h (CS #2). It still requires a sealed system because Se is volatile and Li is air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference], consistent with glovebox and evacuated ampoule handling in Li2TeSe3 (CS #2). Ge–Se elemental ampoule synthesis is also supported by Na–Ge–Se analogues (SS #5; TS #3). The uncertainty is that the elemental Li + Se reaction can be exothermic and may need careful staged heating [Reasonable Inference], as slow heating was used in related ampoule reactions (CS #2; SS #2; TS #2).

#### F3 — Route 2

The kinetic score is slightly lower than Route 1 because Ge metal and direct elemental reaction may produce competing binaries before the ternary forms [Reasonable Inference]. However, elemental routes successfully produce Li2TeSe3 and Na–Ge–Se phases, supporting adequate precursor reactivity under sealed ampoule conditions (CS #2; SS #5; TS #3). The target structure is supported by Li2SnSe3, so the desired topology is not exotic (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). The main uncertainty is whether Li2GeSe3 nucleates directly or requires a Li2Se/GeSe2 intermediate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized because Bridgman-type growth requires high temperature, controlled ampoule translation, annealing, and strict stoichiometry control, as reported for LiGaSe2 (TS #4). It also retains the common alkali selenide issues of Se volatility and Li-containing charge sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides useful evidence for this route through LiGaSe2, but CS and SS provide only indirect support through Li2SnSe3 slow-cooling crystal synthesis rather than true Bridgman growth (CS #3; SS #1). The key uncertainty is whether Li2GeSe3 melts congruently [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetically, a melt-growth route could overcome solid-state diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and LiGaSe2 was grown from high-temperature ampoules by Bridgman–Stockbarger technique (TS #4). The enargite-like tetrahedral framework is supported by LiGaSe2 and Li2SnSe3 (TS #4; CS #3; SS #1). The score is limited by the lack of direct evidence that Li2GeSe3 can be grown from a stable melt without decomposition or volatilization [Reasonable Inference]. If incongruent melting occurs, this route would become substantially less feasible [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger: a large size/charge contrast between Li and Ge cations [Reasonable Inference]. The target and Li2SnSe3 analogue are dominated by corner-sharing tetrahedra rather than mixed edge/face-sharing polyhedra, which lowers amorphization risk (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). The unit cell is expected to be modest rather than >40 atoms based on the Li2SnSe3 analogue formula setting Li4Sn2Se6 (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). Remaining uncertainty is that Li/Ge ordering in a non-centrosymmetric Cc framework may require slow cooling to avoid disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li2GeSe3 is likely a ground-state or very-near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is −0.008641262 eV/atom, and several close analogues are stable hull phases (CS #3; SS #1–2; TS #1–3). The strongest synthesis support is the exact-structure Li2SnSe3 analogue, which has the same Li2MSe3 stoichiometry, same enargite-like Cc symmetry, and same corner-sharing LiSe4/MSe4 tetrahedral framework (CS #3; SS #1; TS #1). The most critical enabling factor is the established sealed-ampoule alkali selenide chemistry using either Li2Se + M + Se or elemental alkali/Ge/Se charges (CS #2–3; SS #2, #5; TS #1–3). The most critical limiting factor is handling and stoichiometry control for Li/Se-containing sealed reactions, especially Se volatility and air/moisture sensitivity of Li or Li2Se [Reasonable Inference], consistent with glovebox and ampoule use in all close analogues (CS #2–3; SS #1–2; TS #1–3). Overall, the target should be experimentally accessible, with Route 2 best for first-pass powder synthesis and Route 1 best if elemental reactions give impurities [Reasonable Inference].
