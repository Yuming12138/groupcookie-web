### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.017530545 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability bin and is effectively at or below the convex hull [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues with similar intermetallic chemistry and **Immm** 1:6:6 structure have E_hull = 0.0 and are reported synthesized, supporting high thermodynamic feasibility (CS). TS examples are also near-hull and synthesized, but they are chemically dissimilar hydrides/borides/oxides/halides/nitrides, so TS gives only generic near-hull support and insufficient route-specific signal.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting/annealing is strongly supported by CS because YFe6Ga6, Dy(FeGa)6, and TbFe6Ga6 are **Immm** 1:6:6 intermetallics made by arc melting and annealing at 800 °C for 120 h under Ar/evacuated conditions. The main severity penalties are controlled atmosphere, multistep remelting/annealing, and Ca air sensitivity/volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable because liquid-state mixing overcomes sluggish solid diffusion, but the long anneal reported for CS analogues indicates ordering/phase equilibration is still needed.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Induction melting is supported by CS, where chemically similar rare-earth/transition-metal/Ga intermetallics were induction melted in a cold crucible under Ar and annealed at 1000 °C for 2 days. Severity is similar to arc melting because controlled inert atmosphere, high temperature, and Ca handling remain necessary [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are good due to melt homogenization, but the structural match is less direct than Route 1 because the strongest induction example is not the exact target **Immm** 1:6:6 framework.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt growth is plausible because CS reports ScFe4Al8-type Al-rich intermetallic single crystals obtained from stoichiometric elements by repeated remelting in Czochralski equipment, and SS/TS also include Czochralski/arc-melt intermetallic examples. However, SS/TS are chemically dissimilar to Ca–Cu–Al, so their route evidence is secondary and mostly neutral for target-specific chemistry. Kinetics are favorable in the liquid, but composition control and Ca volatility make crystal growth more challenging [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
A sealed-tube annealing route is inferred from CS evidence that annealing is essential for related intermetallics, but CS does not demonstrate direct low-temperature solid-state formation of Ca(AlCu)6. The route has lower thermal severity than melt routes, but kinetics are less favorable because diffusion in dense metallic powders/prealloys is slower than in a melt [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this Ca–Cu–Al solid-state pathway and are treated as neutral.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large size mismatch between Ca and the Cu/Al sublattice, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target description explicitly includes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, triggering the mixed-connectivity criterion. The unit-cell atom count criterion is not triggered because the formula and described crystallographic complexity do not establish >40 atoms per cell; this remains somewhat uncertain from the provided text.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca(AlCu)6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state intermetallic because its reported E_hull is negative within computational tolerance. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS: several experimentally synthesized **Immm** 1:6:6 rare-earth/transition-metal/Ga analogues use arc melting plus long annealing, closely matching the target’s structure type and dense metallic bonding. SS and TS are much less chemically relevant and provide insufficient target-specific processing signal, so they are weighted mainly as neutral except for generic evidence that near-hull phases can be synthesized. The key enabling factor is the close structural/chemical analogy to known melt-synthesized **Immm** 1:6:6 intermetallics. The key limiting factor is practical control of Ca reactivity/volatility and stoichiometry during high-temperature melting/annealing.
