### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.021798338 eV/atom, which places it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and supports a high thermodynamic score. TS examples are synthesized near-hull compounds with E_hull values of 0–0.026 eV/atom, providing generic support that near-hull phases are accessible, although they are chemically unrelated to Y–Ni–Ge (TS1–TS5). Insufficient evidence from SS for Y–Ni–Ge-specific thermodynamic stability; SS is scored neutral 50 for this factor. The remaining uncertainty is that computed negative hull values can reflect numerical/reference errors rather than a guaranteed experimentally known ground state [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses standard intermetallic arc-melting, Ar annealing at 1070 K for 200 h, and quenching, directly matching YNi4Si synthesis (CS1). The severity penalty mainly comes from the high-temperature arc-melt step, multi-step anneal/quench procedure, and air sensitivity of Y [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. SS and TS provide insufficient useful signal for this Y–Ni–Ge route, so they are neutral 50 for condition transfer.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetically, elemental Y/Ni/Ge are dense precursors, but arc-melting bypasses much of the initial diffusion barrier by forming an alloyed button [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. The exact isostructural YNi4Si analogue strongly lowers the structural reconstruction penalty (CS1). The long 200 h anneal in CS1 indicates ordering is not instantaneous, which keeps F3 moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is supported by Ni-rich intermetallic syntheses using arc-melting followed by sealed long annealing and slow cooling, especially ZrNi4Zn at 800 °C for 700 h (CS2). It has similar high-temperature arc-melting penalties to Route 1 and a multi-step penalty, but it does not require high pressure or reactive gas (CS2). The sealed-ampoule anneal is standard for intermetallic homogenization but increases procedural burden [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS4, CS5].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Arc-melting helps precursor mixing, but CS2’s 700 h anneal shows that ordering/homogeneity can be slow. Structural support is weaker because CS2/CS4/CS5 are Ni-rich intermetallics but mostly cubic Laves-derived rather than the exact Cmmm YNi4Si-type target. Thus Route 2 is kinetically plausible but less targeted than Route 1.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 avoids arc-melting above 1400 °C, so the condition severity score is somewhat better than Routes 1–2. However, CS3 requires pre-reaction, grinding, and two high-temperature tempering cycles at 1000–1100 °C, so multi-step processing is still required. The route uses Ar/quartz ampoule conditions rather than high pressure or reactive gas (CS3).

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
The solid-state route has poorer kinetics because Y and Ni are dense metallic precursors and the reaction temperature is below their melting points [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 explicitly reports initially inhomogeneous products that needed repeated grinding and tempering, supporting a low-to-moderate kinetics score. The route is chemically plausible for germanides but lacks direct structural support for the target Cmmm YNi4X phase.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has significant size contrast between large Y and smaller Ni/Ge sites, and its polyhedral network includes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedra, which triggers two amorphization/ordering-risk checks [Reasonable Inference; target structure; CS1]. The compact intermetallic formula type gives no evidence for a >40-atom unit cell, so that check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. Remaining uncertainty is that amorphization is less commonly a dominant issue in arc-melted crystalline intermetallics than in low-temperature oxide or thin-film routes [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YNi4Ge is likely to be thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0218 eV/atom. The strongest support is the chemically and structurally near-identical YNi4Si analogue, which crystallizes in the same Cmmm RNi4X structure and is made by arc-melting, annealing at 1070 K for 200 h, and quenching (CS1). The most important enabling factor is that only a tetrel substitution, Si → Ge, is needed relative to a demonstrated YNi4Si synthesis route (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The main limiting factor is phase-pure ordering in a Ni-rich intermetallic, because analogues often require long anneals, repeated melting, slow cooling, or quenching to obtain homogeneous phases (CS1–CS5). SS and TS provide little chemistry-specific synthesis guidance, so the evaluation is dominated by CS evidence and target thermodynamics.
