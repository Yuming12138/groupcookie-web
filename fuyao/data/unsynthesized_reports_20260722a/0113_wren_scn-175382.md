### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.079791 eV/atom, which is treated as on-hull/thermodynamically stable because negative hull values usually reflect numerical or reference-set artifacts [Reasonable Inference from target data]. The K3MSe3 chemical family is strongly supported by on-hull analogues K3AsSe3, K3GeSe3, K3SbSe3, K3AlSe3, and K3GaSe3, each listed with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS5). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for target-specific thermodynamics because the SS/TS entries are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides; they are neutral for this factor.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
The direct route has moderate condition severity because it uses vacuum-sealed ampoules and air-sensitive/volatile components, but the temperature is only ~650–750 °C, consistent with K3GeSe3, K3GaSe3, and K3AlSe3 syntheses (CS2, CS4, CS5). Kinetics are favorable because K/Se can form reactive molten selenide species and K3GeSe3 is structurally almost identical to the target (CS2), although Si itself may react more slowly than Ge due to passivation/refractoriness [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Route 1 kinetics; neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
The H2/Se route has moderate condition severity due to flammable/reducing H2 saturated with volatile Se, directly matching K3AsSe3 and K3SbSe3 syntheses at 750–800 °C (CS1, CS3). Kinetics are less favorable for K3SiSe3 because the oxide-to-selenide transformation is extrapolated from As2O3/Sb2O3, while SiO2 is substantially harder to reduce and selenidize [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for oxide-to-K–Si–Se transferability; neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
The polyselenide flux route has higher procedural severity because it may require pre-synthesizing K2Se/K2Sex and separating air-sensitive flux [Reasonable Inference], but it is supported by flux/melt chemistry for K3AsSe3 and sealed chalcogenide ampoule chemistry for K3GeSe3 (CS1, CS2). Kinetically, a molten alkali polyselenide medium should improve Si transport and crystal growth relative to dry powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient chemically relevant flux evidence and are scored neutral.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The principal amorphization risk is the large cation size/coordination contrast between K and Si, as the target combines 6-coordinate K–Se environments with trigonal SiSe3 units [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. However, the same general separation of large alkali cations and trigonal MSe3 units crystallizes in K3GeSe3 and related K3AsSe3/K3SbSe3 phases (CS1–CS3), reducing concern. The target description does not show a >40-atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity, so only one trigger is counted; insufficient SS/TS evidence is neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K3SiSe3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and closely related K3MSe3 selenides are on the convex hull (CS1–CS5). The strongest synthesis support is K3GeSe3, which is chemically adjacent, isostructural C2/m, and directly synthesized from elements at 650 °C in sealed ampoules (CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a nearly exact K3GeSe3 structural analogue, implying the K3SiSe3 framework is chemically reasonable (CS2). The main limiting factor is kinetic incorporation of Si into selenide units, because Si is less reactive than K/Se and may be surface-oxidized [Reasonable Inference]. Air/moisture sensitivity and Se/K volatility also require careful sealed-ampoule and glovebox practice by analogy to related potassium selenides (CS1–CS5).
