### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.005040519 eV/atom, so it is effectively predicted to lie on the thermodynamic hull. This directly supports a high stability score. The CS/SS/TS sets also contain many synthesizable analogues with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom or near-hull values, including Ti5CuSb3, Ti4NiBi2, Ti4CoBi2, Ti3SnH, and Ti3Sn (CS3, SS1, SS4, SS5, TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty comes from possible finite-temperature phase competition and DFT error [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc-melting + evacuated-tube annealing

The closest I4/mcm Ti5MSb2 analogues use arc-melting under purified/high-purity Ar followed by evacuated silica/quartz annealing (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3). This is a standard intermetallic route, but the severity score is penalized because arc-melting reaches very high transient temperatures, the anneal uses vacuum sealing, and Sb/Sn volatility requires careful sealed-system handling [Reasonable Inference]. The source data provide strong route evidence but do not directly report Ti5SnSb2 synthesis, so stoichiometry robustness is uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + evacuated-tube annealing

Kinetically, arc-melting is favorable because it homogenizes refractory Ti with p-block elements through a liquid alloy state [Reasonable Inference], and close analogues were successfully homogenized after 800 °C/10 d or 870–1070 K/350 h anneals (CS1, CS2). Structural reconstruction is relatively favorable because Ti5RhSb2 and Ti5FeSb2 share the target I4/mcm structure type (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3). The long anneals indicate ordering and phase equilibration are not instantaneous.

#### F2 — Route 2: Direct solid-state sintering

The solid-state route avoids arc-melting’s extreme local temperature and is supported by Ti/Zr/Hf antimonide sintering at 800 °C in evacuated silica tubes (CS3, SS1, TS1). However, it still requires vacuum sealing, long dwell times, and handling of volatile/toxic Sb [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not show direct solid-state synthesis of the exact I4/mcm Ti5MSb2 analogue, so this route is less directly supported than arc-melting.

#### F3 — Route 2: Direct solid-state sintering

Kinetics are the main weakness of direct sintering because elemental Ti is refractory and slow-diffusing [Reasonable Inference]. The reported “up to eight weeks” sintering time for related Ti/Zr/Hf antimonides supports slow reaction kinetics (CS3, SS1, TS1). Structural formation from elements requires full intermetallic ordering, and the closest sintered analogue is structurally similar but not the same I4/mcm Ti5SnSb2-type structure (CS3, SS1, TS1).

#### F2 — Route 3: Sn-rich or Sn/Sb self-flux growth

Flux-assisted synthesis operates at 900–1000 °C in sealed crucible/jacket conditions for related early-transition-metal antimonides (CS5). It avoids arc-melting temperature but still requires sealed inert/vacuum handling and adds flux removal/decanting complexity (CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). The source evidence is indirect because the reported flux compounds are Zr antimonides, not Ti5SnSb2.

#### F3 — Route 3: Sn-rich or Sn/Sb self-flux growth

A liquid flux should improve Ti/Sn/Sb transport and reduce diffusion limitations compared with dry powder sintering [Reasonable Inference]. CS5 demonstrates that early-transition-metal antimonides can be formed in metal flux at 900–1000 °C over 6–7 days. The uncertainty is structural selectivity: flux growth may favor Ti–Sn binaries such as Ti3Sn or other Sn/Sb-rich phases, since Ti3Sn is a known stable/synthesizable Ti–Sn compound (SS5, TS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with simple metallic/pnictide coordination, and no target evidence indicates a large radius mismatch, very large cell, or highly frustrated mixed polyhedral network. CS/SS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for target unit-cell atom count and polyhedral sharing, so those checks are treated conservatively as not triggered. The low amorphization risk is consistent with many crystalline Ti–pnictide and Ti–Sn analogues being obtained by bulk synthesis (CS1–CS3, SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ti5SnSb2 is likely a thermodynamic or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is slightly negative, effectively on the hull. The analogue support is strong for bulk intermetallic synthesis: chemically and structurally close Ti5RhSb2 and Ti5FeSb2 are tetragonal I4/mcm phases made by arc-melting plus annealing (CS1, CS2, SS2, SS3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of closely related Ti5MSb2 I4/mcm analogues with similar Ti–Sb coordination environments (CS1, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry/phase control involving volatile Sb/Sn and competing Ti–Sn or Ti–Sb phases [Reasonable Inference], with Ti3Sn specifically supported as a stable/synthesizable competing Ti–Sn phase (SS5, TS5). Overall, synthesis is plausible, with flux-assisted or arc-melt/anneal routes being the most promising.
