### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.0020 eV/atom, which is effectively 0 eV/atom and therefore strongly suggests a ground-state or near-ground-state phase. Chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar phosphides listed in CS/SS/TS are also mostly at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting a high score. Remaining uncertainty is that calculated hull stability does not guarantee experimental accessibility if Li/P volatility or competing phases dominate [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
The severity is moderate because related phosphides are synthesized under standard high-temperature solid-state conditions near 850–1000 °C in Ar/sealed ampoules, without high pressure (CS, SS, TS). Deductions come from sealed-system need for volatile P/Li [Reasonable Inference], multi-step grinding/reannealing reported for analogues (CS, SS, TS), and air-sensitive Li [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no evidence for extreme pressure or >1400 °C requirements, so those penalties are not applied.

**F3 — Route 1 direct solid-state.**  
Kinetics are moderate because Ni metal diffusion is slow, and analogue syntheses often require grinding and reannealing to homogenize (CS, SS, TS). The score is helped by the existence of exact or near-exact structural motifs, especially P4₂/mnm CaCu4P2 and Zr(Ni2P)2 (CS), but cation substitution to Li/Ni is still an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Li stabilizes the same framework without forming binary Li–P or Ni–P impurities [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 Sn flux.**  
The severity remains moderate because LiCo6P4 is made at 900 °C in Sn melt with a simple corundum crucible and acid cleanup (CS), and Y–Ni–P flux syntheses use evacuated ampoules and slow cooling without high pressure (CS). The route incurs deductions for flux-removal/multi-step handling, sealed containment for volatile/reactive components, and air-sensitive Li [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide useful evidence for Sn flux but insufficient direct evidence that LiNi4P2 itself is stable during HCl washing, so that step must be verified experimentally [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 Sn flux.**  
Flux improves kinetics by providing liquid-phase transport; this is directly supported by LiCo6P4 and Y–Ni–P Sn-flux growth (CS) and by SrNi9P5 single-crystal growth from Sn flux (SS, TS). Ni diffusion limitations are therefore less severe than in a dry solid-state route [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is phase selectivity: Y–Ni–P flux syntheses note by-products (CS), and Sn incorporation or competing Ni–P/Sn phases may occur [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 two-step Ni–P precursor plus lithiation.**  
The conditions are not extreme because the proposed temperatures fall within the range used for related phosphides, and the route remains ambient-pressure sealed-tube chemistry (CS, SS, TS). The route is still multi-step by design, and Li/P handling imposes sealed/inert requirements [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly report this exact precursor-lithiation route, so its severity estimate is extrapolated.

**F3 — Route 3 two-step Ni–P precursor plus lithiation.**  
Using a Ni–P precursor can reduce phosphorus loss and improve local Ni–P connectivity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the dominance of Ni–P networks in SS/TS analogues. However, lithiation and reorganization into the P4₂/mnm ternary phase may be slower than crystallization from flux [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore below the Sn-flux route despite similar thermal severity.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The main amorphization risk is the large mismatch between Li and Ni cation size/coordination, with Li in high coordination and Ni in low-coordinate Ni–P units in the target description [Reasonable Inference]. The target is a small, ordered intermetallic phosphide framework rather than a large complex cell, and the target description does not indicate mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity. CS includes some structurally complex Ni phosphides, but insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates that the target itself has high amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li(Ni2P)2 is likely a ground-state or extremely near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0020 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull. The analogue support is strong: CS contains the exact P4₂/mnm structure type in CaCu4P2 and Zr(Ni2P)2, while SS/TS contain many stable Ni–P phosphide networks made by conventional high-temperature routes. The most critical enabling factor is the well-established accessibility of ternary metal phosphides from elemental mixtures or Sn flux at 850–1100 °C under inert/sealed conditions (CS, SS, TS). The most critical limiting factor is Li/P volatility and Li reactivity, which can perturb stoichiometry or attack containers [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears experimentally plausible, with Sn flux favored for kinetics and crystallinity.
