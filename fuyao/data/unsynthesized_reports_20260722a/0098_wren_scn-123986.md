### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.136838813 eV/atom, so it falls in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin (target data). Several close chemical analogues in CS are on the hull, including Sn₂P₂Cl₁₈, Sb₂S₁₆Cl₆, Ru₂S₆Cl₁₆, and AsS₂₄I₃ with E_hull = 0.0, supporting the plausibility of stable halide/sulfur-rich phases (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS provide generic examples of low-hull materials but little In–S–Cl chemical relevance, so their factor-specific signal is limited; insufficient chemical evidence from SS/TS beyond general stability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has mild conditions because the closest sulfur-halide adducts crystallize at 0–5 °C or about 4 °C in CS₂ without high pressure (CS-2, CS-5). The main severity penalty is chemical handling: anhydrous chlorides and CS₂ require dry, careful handling, consistent with drybox/dry conditions in chloride chemistry (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetically, solution crystallization should avoid solid-state diffusion limits and is strongly supported by the low-temperature crystallization of SbCl₃·S₈ and AsI₃·S₈ analogues (CS-2, CS-5). The uncertainty is that the target is an extended 1D InS₄Cl/S ribbon structure, not merely a molecular halide·S₈ adduct, so the structural reconstruction score is reduced [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because the Ru–S–Cl analogue uses sealed silica ampoules, an autoclave, N₂ atmosphere, SCl₂, 140 °C, and 7 days (CS-4). The sealed volatile sulfur-chloride reagent and dry/inert handling requirements lower F2 substantially (CS-4). Kinetically, however, the route is attractive because liquid SCl₂/S₂Cl₂ provides high chemical activity and because the Ru analogue forms edge-sharing metal sulfur/chloride octahedra similar to the target’s edge-sharing InS₄Cl₂ octahedra (CS-4; target structure). The major uncertainty is phase selectivity, since CS-4 reports multiple Ru–S–Cl compounds crystallizing from related solutions and requiring careful separation.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate severity because it uses sealed evacuated/dry tubes and volatile chloride/sulfur species but avoids high temperatures and high pressure [Reasonable Inference]. SS-4/TS-4 show that a 1D halide ribbon phase, AuBr, can form under dry N₂ or vacuum at 40–55 °C over weeks, but this is only a weak structural analogue because it lacks sulfur and indium. Kinetics may benefit from vapor transport and volatile InCl₃/chlorosulfur species [Reasonable Inference], but there is no direct CS/SS/TS evidence for In–S–Cl vapor-transport formation. Therefore, F3 is lower than Routes 1–2 due to uncertain structural reconstruction and composition control.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, In, so the cation-radius mismatch criterion does not apply (target structure). The target description reports edge-sharing InS₄Cl₂ octahedra and sulfur ribbons, but not simultaneous vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedral networks, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization trigger is not met (target structure). The full unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided in CS/SS/TS or the target description; because the formula is small and no >40-atom cell evidence is supplied, this check is not triggered but remains uncertain. Overall amorphization risk is low, especially for solution/ampoule crystallization rather than rapid quenching [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically stable or at least strongly favored in the reported phase diagram because its E_hull is negative relative to the supplied hull data (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar sulfur/halide systems that crystallize under mild solution conditions or sealed sulfur-chloride conditions (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). The most enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature, high-activity sulfur/halide media, which can assemble sulfur-rich halide phases without requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: the target requires an extended InS₄Cl ribbon plus sulfur ribbon arrangement, whereas many CS analogues are zero-dimensional molecular adducts or clusters (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5; target structure). The best practical strategy is therefore low-temperature solution screening followed by sealed sulfur-chloride ampoule optimization if the solution route gives only molecular adducts [Reasonable Inference].
