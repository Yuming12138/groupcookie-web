### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0585 eV/atom, which is treated as ground-state-stable within numerical uncertainty and receives the maximum thermodynamic score from the provided rubric. TS analogues include multiple hull or near-hull compounds, such as E_hull = 0.0 for Li3Na(NH2)4 and AuBr and 0.0034 eV/atom for W3Ni2N, indicating that the thermodynamic-neighbor set is not dominated by highly unstable phases (TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The main uncertainty is that TS analogues are chemically dissimilar to KWF6, so they support the stability metric but not the fluoride synthesis chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-tube fluoride solid-state reaction

This route is supported by chemically similar fluoride syntheses using binary fluorides in sealed Pt/Ni tubes at 750–1150 °C (CS-2, CS-4). F2 is reduced by sealed/vacuum handling, volatile or moisture-sensitive W fluoride precursors, and likely multi-step W(V) precursor preparation [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is limited by W diffusion and octahedral ordering, although fluoride media and long sealed holds used in BaCrF5/SmZrF7 make the route plausible (CS-2, CS-4). SS and TS provide insufficient direct support for this fluoride route and are treated as neutral evidence, score 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct fluorination / oxidative fluorination

BaCrF6 demonstrates that high-fluoride ternary phases can be formed by F2/Ar at 300 °C and up to 300 atm in Monel autoclaves (CS-1). F2 is penalized for reactive/toxic fluorine gas, pressure-rated corrosion-resistant equipment, and complex post-reaction handling (CS-1). F3 benefits from high chemical activity of F2 but is penalized because the structure must reconstruct from oxide/carbonate/chloride precursors and CS-1 reports phase-purity difficulties and volatile chromium fluoride losses. For KWF6, an added uncertainty is redox control of W(V) rather than over-fluorinated W(VI) phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: HF/fluoride solution crystallization

CsReF6, a chemically similar A–MF6 trigonal fluoride, was obtained by slow evaporation from dilute acid solution after HF-containing chemistry (CS-3), supporting a mild crystallization route. F2 is favorable because temperatures are low and no high-pressure furnace is required, but HF toxicity and likely multi-step redox/complexation lower the score (CS-3 plus [Reasonable Inference]). F3 is high because solution species bypass solid-state diffusion, but W(V) stability in acidic fluoride solution is not established by CS-3 and remains the main uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide useful solution-fluoride evidence and are neutral, score 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KWF6 is likely thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative within numerical uncertainty, and TS analogues include several hull-stable or near-hull materials (TS-1, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest chemical support comes from CS: BaCrF6 shows high-fluoride ternary formation under F2/Ar (CS-1), BaCrF5/SmZrF7 show sealed fluoride solid-state synthesis (CS-2, CS-4), and CsReF6 shows mild solution crystallization of an A–MF6-type fluoride (CS-3). The most enabling factor is the strong stability of octahedral MF6 fluoride chemistry and the availability of multiple fluoride synthesis paradigms from CS. The most limiting factor is W(V) redox control, because over-fluorination or disproportionation could lead to W(VI)/W(IV) impurity phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with the best initial attempt being HF/fluoride crystallization if W(V) can be stabilized, followed by sealed-tube fluoride reaction as a more robust but harsher backup.
