### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.040002366 eV/atom, placing it in the best ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. Chemically similar intermetallic references are also computed on-hull with E_hull = 0.0, including ErAl8Cu4, UCu6Al5, CeNi2Al3, UFe6Ga6, and Lu(FeGa)6 (CS1–CS5). SS/TS contain stable examples, but their chemistries are unrelated to Hf–Cu–Al, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for target-specific thermodynamic transferability. Remaining uncertainty is mainly computational: a negative hull value should be interpreted as stable within numerical/reference uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for very high melting temperature, controlled Ar/vacuum atmosphere, and multi-step processing, consistent with arc melting plus vacuum annealing in CS1–CS3. It avoids high pressure, and the bulk elemental precursors are manageable, as chemically similar references use pure elements (CS1–CS3). The uncertainty is whether Hf incorporation requires more severe melting or longer annealing than Er/U/Ce analogues [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Hf/Cu/Al pieces are low-activity precursors compared with salts or sol–gel precursors [Reasonable Inference], and CS references use repeated melting/annealing to achieve homogeneity (CS1, CS2, CS4). Liquid arc melting helps bypass purely solid-state diffusion, but Hf ordering into the 1:6:6 phase may still be slow [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is helped by close Immm analogues UFe6Ga6 and Lu(FeGa)6 (CS4, CS5).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar condition severity to Route 1 because induction/levitation melting still requires high temperature and Ar atmosphere, as in UFe6Ga6 preparation (CS4). It adds specialized equipment, though equipment is not explicitly included in the F2 deduction table; this is reflected qualitatively in difficulty rather than the numeric F2. No high pressure is required, consistent with CS4.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are slightly better than Route 1 because levitation/induction melting and repeated remelting can improve liquid homogenization, as reported for UFe6Ga6 (CS4). The same Immm 1:6:6 structure in UFe6Ga6 gives strong structural-pathway support (CS4), and Lu(FeGa)6 adds another Immm analogue (CS5). The main uncertainty is whether the Hf–Cu–Al liquid solidifies into the desired ordered phase rather than binary Cu–Al or Hf–Al/Hf–Cu competitors [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids >1000 °C melting but requires controlled atmosphere, sealed ampoule, multiple steps, and potentially air-sensitive fine powders [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing at 600–850 °C is supported by CS1, CS2, and CS5, but those examples generally follow melting rather than replace it. Therefore the route is not thermally severe but is operationally less robust.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state/powder metallurgy is kinetically limited by refractory Hf diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues emphasize arc/induction melting and repeated remelting for homogeneity (CS1–CS4), which implies that a no-melt route may struggle. Mechanical alloying improves precursor contact [Reasonable Inference], but complete ordering into the Immm 1:6:6 structure is still expected to need long anneals.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The Hf/Cu/Al size mismatch triggers the radius-difference criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula unit is not large, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom cell, so C2 is not triggered. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is inferred from the closest Immm analogues, where UFe6Ga6 and Lu(FeGa)6 have mixed corner/edge/face-sharing environments (CS4, CS5), giving a medium amorphization tendency.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf(AlCu)6 is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state intermetallic because the reported E_hull is −0.0400 eV/atom, and chemically similar intermetallics are on-hull in the references (CS1–CS5). The synthesis evidence is strongest from CS, especially the Immm 1:6:6 analogue UFe6Ga6 and Immm Lu(FeGa)6, both made by melt-based methods (CS4, CS5). SS and TS provide little chemically transferable guidance because their nearest entries are mostly amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides rather than Hf–Cu–Al metallic phases. The key enabling factor is that melt processing plus long annealing is a well-established route for related Al/Cu-containing intermetallics (CS1–CS3) and Immm 1:6:6 analogues (CS4, CS5). The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially possible Al loss and competition with binary Hf–Al, Hf–Cu, or Cu–Al phases [Reasonable Inference].
