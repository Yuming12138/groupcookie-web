### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.009219 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull and therefore earns a high score under the prompt rubric. This is consistent with several stable or near-stable Cs fluoride analogues: Cs₂CaF₄ has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS), Cs₂AgF₄ has E_hull = 0.0 or 0.0214 eV/atom depending on polymorph/source (CS/SS/TS), and Cs₂KMnF₆ has E_hull = 0.0–0.045 eV/atom (SS/TS). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability may not capture fluorine chemical potential, pressure stabilization, or unusual Cs oxidation/polyfluoride chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for controlled/reactive fluorine conditions, sealed containment, multistep handling, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents, all of which are supported by F₂/Ar fluorination and glovebox/Au ampule practices in Cs fluoride analogues (SS/TS). It is not penalized for >1000 °C or >5 GPa because the relevant analogues operate at 270–700 °C and 2.5–15 kbar, below the rubric’s high-pressure threshold (CS/SS/TS). The main uncertainty is whether pressure is truly required or merely helpful for stabilizing the short Cs–F octahedral site [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory oxides because Cs fluoride analogues crystallize at modest temperatures: Cs₂AgF₄ at 270–330 °C (CS/SS/TS), Cs₂KMnF₆ at 520 °C (SS/TS), and Cs₂CaF₄ at 700 °C (CS). The target shares layered La/BaCuO₄-derived motifs with Cs₂CaF₄ and Cs₂AgF₄ (CS/SS), supporting structural transferability. The uncertainty is that replacing the octahedral B-site cation by Cs gives very short Cs–F distances and may require unusual reconstruction or pressure [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 uses less external pressure than Route 1 but still requires sealed noble-metal containment, anhydrous handling, and high fluorine chemical potential, as indicated by Au tube/gold reaction tube Cs₂AgF₄ syntheses and F₂ fluorination analogues (CS/SS/TS). The route avoids high temperature and avoids >5 GPa pressure, so the main deductions arise from atmosphere, toxicity, volatility/pressure, and multistep workflow. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for direct ambient-pressure Cs₃F₄ formation, so the feasibility of avoiding pressure is uncertain.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
The closest structural/chemical analogues support reasonable kinetics because Cs₂AgF₄ forms in 12–24 h at 270–330 °C and Cs₂CaF₄ forms in 2 days at 700 °C (CS/SS/TS). The target’s I4/mmm layered topology is directly analogous to Cs₂CaF₄ and tetragonal Cs₂AgF₄ (CS/SS). The limiting kinetic issue is phase selection among CsF, possible polyfluoride phases, and the unusual Cs₃F₄ layered product [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 inherits the same atmosphere and sealed-system penalties because prefluorinated Cs–F species or XeF₂/F₂ donors are likely oxidizing, moisture-sensitive, and pressure-generating [Reasonable Inference]. Multi-step synthesis is supported by analogues requiring precursor fluorination or intermediate fluoride preparation before annealing, such as Cs₂KFeF₆, Cs₃CrF₇-related phases, and AgF₂-based Cs₂AgF₄ syntheses (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for isolable CsF₃/Cs-polyfluoride precursors in this exact target route, which increases uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
A prefluorinated Cs–F reagent should improve fluorine activity and reduce cation-mixing complexity because only Cs and F are introduced [Reasonable Inference]. The low-temperature formation of Cs₂AgF₄ and moderate-temperature fluorination of Cs-containing fluorides support crystallization in the 150–500 °C range (CS/SS/TS). However, no CS/SS/TS example demonstrates binary Cs₃F₄ layered phase formation from a cesium polyfluoride, so structural nucleation remains uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation type, Cs, so the cation-radius-mismatch amorphization trigger is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target and closest I4/mmm K₂NiF₄-type analogues are small-cell crystalline fluorides rather than large >40 atom/unit-cell frameworks (CS; target description). The structure has mixed corner/edge sharing in F-centered polyhedra but no reported coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the strict C3 criterion is not triggered (target description).

Cs₃F₄ is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or essentially ground-state because its E_hull is −0.009219 eV/atom, well within the high-stability range specified by the prompt. The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support synthesis of Cs-containing layered and perovskite-derived fluorides under anhydrous solid-state, sealed-tube, fluorination, and moderate-pressure conditions. The most important enabling factor is the existence of very close structural analogues, especially Cs₂CaF₄ and Cs₂AgF₄, that share La/BaCuO₄-derived layered motifs and Cs coordination environments (CS/SS). The main limiting factor is the unusual binary Cs₃F₄ stoichiometry, which is F-rich relative to normal CsF and may require high fluorine chemical potential, pressure, or polyfluoride chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but likely not a routine open-tube ceramic synthesis.
