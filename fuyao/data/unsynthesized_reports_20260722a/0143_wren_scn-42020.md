### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is −0.004724653 eV/atom, which is effectively a ground-state prediction and falls in the highest scoring thermodynamic category. This is strongly reinforced by stable same-family analogues ScNi4Au, ScNi4Cd, and ScCu4In with reported \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV/atom (SS1/TS1, CS2, CS1). Remaining uncertainty is that computed hull stability does not guarantee an experimentally accessible low-temperature ordering path [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Arc-melting under Ar with homogenization is well supported by ScCu4In, ScNi4Cd, and ScNi4Au analogues (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1). The main severity penalties are the high local temperatures needed to melt Ni/Pd-rich alloys and the handling of reactive Sc/Ni powders [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that Pd requires a sealed volatile-component strategy, so no volatile-precursor deduction was applied.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental metals are not highly reactive in the solid state, but arc-melting creates a liquid alloy, which is why many related phases are accessible despite refractory components (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1). The same F-43m Laves-derived structure in ScNi4Au and ScNi4Cd lowers reconstruction difficulty (SS1/TS1, CS2). Uncertainty remains in Pd/Ni site ordering and possible competing Sc–Ni binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The binary-precursor/sealed-anneal route is directly inspired by ScNi4Cd synthesis, which used a Sc–Ni precursor, grinding, sealing, and annealing (CS2). The route is more severe than direct arc-melting because it adds vacuum/sealed handling and ≥3 steps, similar to long-anneal approaches in ZrNi4Zn and Ni5Zr analogues (CS3, SS5/TS5). Pd itself is not expected to be volatile like Cd, so the sealed system is for homogenization/control rather than volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Grinding a pre-alloyed Sc–Ni precursor should improve contact with Pd compared with dense buttons [Reasonable Inference], and CS2 demonstrates precursor-based incorporation for ScNi4Cd. However, long anneals in CS3 and SS5/TS5 imply slow ordering or diffusion can be a limiting kinetic factor. The route is therefore reliable but not kinetically fast.

#### F2 — Route 3

Melt growth is supported by CaCu4In growth from elements at 1450 K and by melt-derived related Laves phases (CS5, CS1, SS1/TS1). For ScNi4Pd, the higher melting Ni/Pd-rich composition likely requires >1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], but no high pressure or reactive gases are needed. This keeps severity moderate rather than high.

#### F3 — Route 3

The liquid state provides the best diffusion and equilibration pathway among the proposed routes [Reasonable Inference]. Same-family crystallization is strongly supported by F-43m analogues including CaCu4In and ScNi4Au (CS5, SS1/TS1). The main uncertainty is whether the target crystallizes congruently or instead requires post-growth annealing to remove secondary phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Mechanochemistry avoids high-temperature melting and is supported for MgYNi4, a cubic F-43m Laves-derived compound, using 80 h milling and 773 K heating (CS4). Severity remains moderate because it requires controlled Ar/vacuum handling, long milling, and often pre-synthesized intermetallic precursors (CS4). There is insufficient direct SS/TS evidence for a Sc–Ni–Pd mechanochemical analogue.

#### F3 — Route 4

Ball milling can shorten diffusion distances and introduce defects, improving low-temperature reaction kinetics [Reasonable Inference from CS4]. However, Sc–Ni–Pd ordering into the target phase may require more extensive reconstruction than the YNi2/MgNi2-to-MgYNi4 case, because no direct Sc–Ni–Pd milled analogue is provided. The route is plausible but less certain than melt-based routes.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has mixed edge/corner/face-sharing Ni-centered cuboctahedra, which triggers the connectivity criterion and is also seen in several analogues (target description; CS2, SS1/TS1). The large Sc versus Ni/Pd metallic size mismatch triggers the radius-difference criterion [Reasonable Inference]. However, the structure is a compact, known intermetallic prototype family with multiple crystalline analogues, so amorphization risk is medium rather than prohibitive (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScNi4Pd is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state intermetallic because its reported \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is slightly negative and multiple closely related ANi4B phases are on the hull (target data; CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1). The analogue support is strong: ScNi4Au is nearly isostructural and experimentally made by arc-melting/homogenization, while ScNi4Cd and ScCu4In show that Sc–Ni/Cu–B Laves-derived variants are synthetically accessible (SS1/TS1, CS2, CS1). The most important enabling factor is the robust F-43m Laves-derived structure type across chemically similar systems (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1). The main limiting factor is achieving homogeneous Pd/Ni/Sc ordering without competing Sc–Ni or Pd–Ni-rich phases, which may require repeated melting and annealing [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS3, SS2, SS5/TS5]. Overall, the material should be experimentally accessible in a standard intermetallic synthesis laboratory.
