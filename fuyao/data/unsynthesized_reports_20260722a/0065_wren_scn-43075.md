### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported \(E_{\mathrm{hull}}\) is −0.259777526 eV/atom, so the rubric assigns the maximum stability score. This suggests the calculated structure is at least predicted to be strongly stable relative to the reference phase set [Reasonable Inference]. However, negative hull values can arise from reference inconsistencies or computational artifacts, so this should be interpreted as “very favorable calculated thermodynamics,” not guaranteed experimental stability [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues include both near-hull and on-hull fluorides, such as NiF₃/SrNiF₆ at 0 eV/atom (CS-4, CS-5), but none directly validates LiF₈.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and GPa pressure because CS-1 demonstrates room-temperature aHF/F₂ fluorination and CS-4 demonstrates below-0 °C aHF fluoride precipitation. The severity penalty is still large because F₂ and anhydrous HF are toxic, volatile, corrosive, and require specialized fluoropolymer equipment (CS-1, CS-4). The multi-step nature is inferred from dissolution/fluorination/crystallization/HF removal [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient useful signal for these chemical hazards and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The liquid aHF medium should improve transport relative to dry solid-state LiF fluorination [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 shows successful fluorination chemistry in aHF at room temperature. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: the target has ordered F₂ clusters and a LiF₆ sheet, which likely requires F₂ incorporation and anion ordering beyond simple LiF formation [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 and CS-5 show that high-fluorine materials often require careful phase control and can be metastable/sensitive. SS/TS analogues are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient specific kinetic evidence.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is not thermally extreme, since CS-5 used 280 °C rather than >1000 °C, and it is not >5 GPa. It is still severe because F₂/N₂ is used under high pressure in a Monell-Autoklav, and CS-5 explicitly reports pressure fluorination at pF ≈ 130–140 atm. Toxic/reactive gas handling, sealed pressure equipment, and multistep loading/heating/cooling justify the deductions. SS/TS do not provide fluoride pressure-chemistry evidence and remain neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solid LiF reacting with F₂ is likely surface-limited and less kinetically favorable than aHF solution chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 supports the feasibility of high-pressure fluorination for highly fluorinated phases, but also notes that Guinier-pure samples are extremely difficult and products can be air/moisture sensitive. Structural reconstruction from LiF to LiF₈ remains the largest kinetic barrier [Reasonable Inference]. Thus Route 2 scores lower than Route 1 despite its strong fluorine chemical potential.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from low nominal temperature, consistent with room-temperature ball milling in CS-2. However, the route requires sealed F₂ during mechanical activation, which is substantially hazardous [Reasonable Inference], and F₂ handling risks are supported by CS-1 and CS-5. The method is multistep because cryogenic loading, milling, and post-annealing are separate operations [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give insufficient mechanochemical Li–F₂ evidence.

#### F3 — Route 3

Mechanochemistry can increase precursor activity, and CS-2 describes a facile high-energy ball-milling process for a fluoride-containing FeF₃ composite. The transferability is weak because CS-2 does not synthesize a new F₂-rich fluoride phase and does not involve molecular F₂ uptake. The structural reconstruction score is therefore low: ordered molecular F₂/polyfluoride incorporation into LiF₈ is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. This route is exploratory rather than a primary recommendation.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains only one cation, Li, so a cation-radius mismatch trigger is absent [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described motif is simple—LiF₆ sheets plus F₂ clusters—and does not show mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity in the target description. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, but the formula and C2 layered description do not indicate a large >40 atom cell [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for LiF₈ and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable because its reported hull energy is negative, but experimentally it should still be treated as a potentially fragile F₂-rich or polyfluoride phase [Reasonable Inference]. The chemically similar data support the general feasibility of making high-fluorine solids using anhydrous HF/F₂ chemistry and high-pressure fluorination (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5), whereas the SS and TS sets are mostly chemically unrelated and provide insufficient route-specific support. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high fluorine chemical potential at low temperature, because the target contains discrete F–F bonds and excess fluorine [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The most critical limiting factor is phase retention: F₂-rich solids may lose fluorine, transform, or fail to crystallize unless pressure, temperature, and drying are tightly controlled [Reasonable Inference; phase-control concerns supported by CS-4 and CS-5]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires specialized fluorine chemistry rather than routine solid-state processing.
