### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.03008 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom bin (target data). Several chemically similar Pt-rich pnictides/arsenides are on or very near the hull: Ce2Pt8P and Pt5InAs have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and CePt5Sb has E_hull ≈0.015 eV/atom (CS1, CS4, CS5). SS and TS materials are largely unrelated in chemistry, so they provide insufficient evidence for Y–Pt–As stability transfer and are scored neutral 50 for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C processing because Pt5InAs was made at 750 °C followed by 600 °C annealing (CS4). The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile/toxic As, multiple grinding/annealing cycles, and air-sensitive Y handling [Reasonable Inference; CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is whether Y substitution requires higher temperature than In because Y is less fusible/reactive than In [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are moderate-to-slow because the route uses elemental Pt powder and solid-state diffusion, and the isostructural Pt5InAs analogue required 90–120 days for platinum-containing samples (CS4). The favorable kinetic feature is that the target shares the P4/mmm MAsPt5 structure with Pt5InAs, reducing structural reconstruction uncertainty (CS4; target data). The main uncertainty is whether YPt12 cuboctahedral ordering forms at the same low temperature as InPt12 cuboctahedra [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has higher condition severity because arc-melting involves very high local temperatures and controlled Ar atmosphere, followed by evacuated annealing (CS2, CS3, CS5). The As component adds an additional sealed-system and toxicity burden compared with Ge analogues (CS2, CS3; CS4). The route is experimentally standard for rare-earth Pt intermetallics, but direct As arc-melting is risky due to As volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc-melting improves homogeneity and diffusion, as shown by RE–Pt–Ge and CePt5Sb analogues that were remelted and annealed to achieve crystallinity/homogeneity (CS2, CS3, CS5). However, the structurally closest analogue is the ampoule-made Pt5InAs rather than the arc-melted materials, so reconstruction into P4/mmm YAsPt5 is not directly demonstrated (CS4 vs CS2/CS3/CS5). The main uncertainty is composition retention of As during melting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses demanding sealed Ta-ampoule high-temperature processing analogous to Ce2Pt8P, including 950–1250 °C anneals and multiple processing steps (CS1). It carries penalties for >1000 °C, vacuum/inert containment, volatile/toxic As, and multi-step handling [Reasonable Inference; CS1, CS4]. The severity is justified if lower-temperature Route 1 fails to overcome diffusion limitations.

#### F3 — Route 3

High temperature helps diffusion, but CS1 explicitly reports complex reactions and difficulty obtaining single-phase Pt-rich pnictides, even with multiple annealing cycles. Because CS1 is chemically similar but not structurally identical to YAsPt5, structural reconstruction confidence is only moderate-to-low (CS1; target data). The remaining uncertainty is whether high temperature stabilizes the desired P4/mmm phase or instead promotes competing binaries/ternaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains mixed face-, corner-, and edge-sharing motifs, which triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion directly from the target structural description. The Y/Pt size mismatch plausibly triggers the cation radius criterion [Reasonable Inference], while the simple P4/mmm formula description gives no evidence for a >40 atom cell (target data). SS/TS do not provide useful amorphization evidence for this Y–Pt–As intermetallic and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YPt5As is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative/near-zero and chemically similar Pt-rich pnictides/arsenides are stable or near-stable (target data; CS1, CS4, CS5). The strongest synthesis support is the isostructural Pt5InAs analogue in P4/mmm, made by long evacuated-ampoule annealing from elements (CS4). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly analogous MAsPt5 structure with very similar Pt–As bonding, which makes the target chemically and structurally credible (CS4; target data). The most critical limiting factor is slow Pt-rich solid-state ordering combined with As volatility/toxicity, as indicated by the 90–120 day anneals and impurity challenges in platinum arsenides (CS4). SS and TS analogues are not chemically relevant enough to override the CS evidence and are treated as neutral.
