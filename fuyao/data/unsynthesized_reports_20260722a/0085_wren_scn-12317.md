### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.043095472 eV/atom, which places it in the highest thermodynamic-stability scoring bin and suggests it is at or slightly below the computed convex hull within numerical uncertainty (target). Several TS references are also near-hull or on-hull, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so TS gives only generic support for synthesizability of near-hull phases and no specific Cs–Se–F route signal (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is whether the negative hull value reflects a robust Cs–Se–F phase field or a computational artifact in an unusual fluoride/selenium chemistry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild in temperature and pressure because CS-1 demonstrates Cs fluoride crystallization from 2 N HF and Cs₂CO₃ by standing for several days without high temperature or high pressure (CS-1). The deductions arise from HF toxicity and possible use of volatile/moisture-sensitive SeF₄, neither of which is eliminated by the CS evidence [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient useful condition-severity signal for this Cs–Se–F route and are treated as neutral 50 for that evidence channel.

#### F3 — Route 1

Reaction kinetics are favorable because HF solution chemistry gives high precursor activity and liquid-phase transport, as demonstrated by crystal growth of a chemically similar Cs fluoride from HF solution (CS-1). The main kinetic uncertainty is not cation diffusion but whether Se(IV) fluoride species crystallize into the exact C2/m Cs₃(SeF₄)₂ arrangement rather than forming soluble complexes or oxofluorides [Reasonable Inference from target]. SS and TS do not give chemically relevant kinetic information for Cs–Se–F and are neutral evidence sources.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses moderate temperature because ASnF₃ analogues were synthesized at 150 °C for 24 h in Teflon-lined autoclaves with slow cooling (CS-2, CS-4). The route is penalized for sealed-vessel use, volatile SeF₄, multiple operational steps, and toxic/moisture-sensitive fluoride chemistry [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-4 and target chemistry]. SS and TS do not provide useful signal for solvothermal Cs–Se–F feasibility and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

Solvothermal kinetics are good because CS-2 and CS-4 show crystallization of alkali main-group fluorides from fluoride precursors in methanol at moderate temperature. The target is not isostructural with the Sn fluoride analogues, so structural reconstruction/speciation control is less certain than in direct analogue synthesis [Reasonable Inference from target; CS-2, CS-4]. The biggest kinetic risk is precipitation of simpler CsF/selenium fluoride salts before the target ordering develops [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is the most severe because CS-3 and CS-5 require carefully dried Ar and diluted fluorine at 300–500 °C for Cs₃MF₇ analogues. Although the target may not need 500 °C, the need for anhydrous fluorination, sealed volatile SeF₄ retention, and possible intermediate synthesis justifies substantial deductions [Reasonable Inference from CS-3, CS-5]. SS and TS again provide no chemically useful route signal and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state kinetics are acceptable but weaker than solution routes because CsF-rich fluoride analogues can form at 300–500 °C, but they still require dry mixing/tempering and phase monitoring (CS-3, CS-5). Use of a preformed Cs–Se–F intermediate would improve structural reconstruction, but that intermediate is not given in the data and remains an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. Volatile SeF₄ transport may help diffusion but can also cause stoichiometry gradients [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size/coordination mismatch between highly coordinated Cs and square-coplanar SeF₄ units, triggering one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference from target]. The target description does not show a complex mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing polyhedral network, and the formula unit contains only 13 atoms, so there is insufficient evidence for high unit-cell complexity or mixed-connectivity amorphization risk (target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for larger cell count). The resulting amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃(SeF₄)₂ is likely to be a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.043095472 eV/atom (target). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Cs fluorides, especially HF solution crystallization of Cs₂MnF₅-related material and low-temperature solvothermal growth of CsSnF₃/RbSnF₃ analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is the high fluoride activity and high mobility available in HF or sealed solvothermal media, which can form Cs–F and Se–F coordination without high-temperature diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS-1, CS-2, CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is controlling Se(IV) fluoride speciation, volatility, and hydrolysis, since none of the CS analogues directly contain SeF₄ units [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues are too chemically dissimilar to provide strong route guidance and are treated as neutral for synthesis-method transferability.
