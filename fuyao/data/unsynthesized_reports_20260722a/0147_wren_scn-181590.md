### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.008416 eV/atom, which is effectively a ground-state prediction and merits a high F1 score. TS is strongly supportive because the thermodynamic neighbors include Pd5As, BePd3, Pd5HgSe, TlPd3, and Pd3In with E_hull = 0 or near 0 (TS1–TS5). SS is also supportive because the top structural analogues are stable Pd-rich phases, including Pd5As and Pd5HgSe (SS1, SS3). CS is less directly compositional because several top chemical analogues are Fe/rare-earth intermetallics rather than Li–Pd–As, but CaPd5 and LuNi5Sn being stable supports Pd-rich intermetallic accessibility (CS3, CS5).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because Pd5As and CaPd5 analogues form at 600–700 °C in sealed tubes (SS1, TS1, CS3). The severity deductions arise from vacuum/sealed-ampoule processing, volatile/toxic As, air-sensitive Li, and multiple handling steps [Reasonable Inference; SS1, CS3]. Insufficient evidence from CS for direct Li–As volatility management is noted, so that uncertainty is handled by conservative deductions rather than assuming easy synthesis. SS/TS provide strong sealed-ampoule evidence but no direct Li-containing analogue, leaving stoichiometry retention uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 adds a prealloying step, which increases process complexity but should reduce uncontrolled Pd/As/Li reaction mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. Pd5As prealloy preparation is directly supported by SS1/TS1, and sealed annealing of Pd-rich intermetallics is supported by CS3 and TS4. The same vacuum, volatile-element, and air-sensitive precursor penalties apply because Li and As must still be handled in a sealed system [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides useful signal for Pd-rich intermetallic processing through CaPd5 but insufficient evidence for Li-containing Pd arsenides specifically.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses controlled Ar/vacuum processing analogous to mechanochemical intermetallic synthesis in CS2, which explicitly addresses volatile-element stoichiometry control. It is penalized for multi-step milling/annealing, air-sensitive Li, toxic As, and sealed processing [Reasonable Inference; CS2, SS1]. It avoids high-temperature and high-pressure deductions because the proposed anneal is below 1000 °C and analogues use ambient-pressure milling/annealing (CS2). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for mechanochemical LiAsPd5 specifically, so the route remains moderately uncertain.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 benefits from relatively low CVT temperatures demonstrated for Be–Pd at 500–550 °C and In–Pd iodine-assisted synthesis at 750–1100 K (SS2, TS2, SS4, TS5). It is still penalized for evacuated ampoule use, volatile I2/As, air-sensitive Li, and multi-step handling [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that iodine may sequester Li as LiI, which is not addressed by the provided CS/SS/TS data and is therefore an extrapolated risk [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct signal for iodine-assisted Li–Pd–As synthesis and is scored neutral for that specific issue.

#### F3 — Route 1

Direct solid-state reaction has moderate kinetics because Pd is refractory and long-range diffusion through Pd-rich intermetallics is likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. However, Pd5As forms single-phase after 4 days at 973 K (SS1, TS1), and CaPd5 forms with extended annealing at 600–700 °C (CS3), which supports feasibility. Structural reconstruction is not excessive because the target and Pd5As both feature Pd–As bonding near 2.5 Å and 8-coordinate As environments (SS1, TS1), but Li ordering into P4/mmm is unproven. Remaining uncertainty is competition with binary Pd5As and Li loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Prealloying Pd–As should improve kinetics by starting from a chemically relevant Pd–As framework rather than from separate refractory Pd and volatile As [Reasonable Inference]. This is strongly supported by the existence and direct synthesis of Pd5As from elements (SS1, TS1). The main kinetic challenge is Li diffusion/order into the Pd–As framework, which may require several days of annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not directly show topotactic lithiation of Pd5As, so this remains an extrapolated but plausible strategy.

#### F3 — Route 3

Mechanical alloying improves precursor activity and shortens diffusion length; CS2 explicitly says mechanical alloying is well adapted to volatile elements and stoichiometry control. A post-milling anneal remains necessary because CS2 used annealing after milling to develop the intermetallic phase. The route may help avoid very long high-temperature exposure of Li/As [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty comes from possible amorphous/nanocrystalline intermediates and milling contamination [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

CVT/iodine assistance receives the best kinetic score because iodine is documented to enhance reactivity in Pd-rich Be–Pd and In–Pd systems (SS2, TS2, SS4, TS5). Vapor or halide-mediated transport can reduce the diffusion burden of refractory Pd [Reasonable Inference]. The drawback is phase-control sensitivity: In–Pd analogues sometimes required regrinding/repeated treatment, and one modification could not be obtained purely (SS4, TS5). For LiAsPd5, LiI formation may reduce effective Li activity, creating an added uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small-cell ordered intermetallic with no evidence of >40 atoms per unit cell and no reported simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity in the target description. The closest P4/mmm structural analogue Pd5HgSe also has simple cuboctahedral motifs with corner/face sharing (SS3, TS3), supporting low amorphization risk. Cation radius mismatch is not strongly applicable because this is not an ionic oxide/halide lattice; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for ionic-radius-driven amorphization in Li–Pd–As. The main structural uncertainty is not amorphization but competing crystalline Pd–As/Pd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiAsPd5 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is −0.008416 eV/atom and TS analogues are stable or nearly stable Pd-rich intermetallics (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is strongest for the Pd–As backbone: Pd5As is experimentally synthesized as single-phase by sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction, and the target’s Pd–As bond length is very close to Pd5As values (SS1, TS1). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated accessibility of Pd-rich arsenide/intermetallic frameworks under sealed moderate-temperature annealing (SS1, TS1, CS3). The most critical limiting factor is simultaneous retention and ordering of Li with volatile/toxic As in a sealed system, for which CS/SS/TS provide no direct Li–Pd–As example and thus require [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the phase is promising but should be approached with careful sealed-tube stoichiometry control and phase-screening around 500–750 °C.
