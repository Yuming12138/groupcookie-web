### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.016848461 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull and therefore receives a very high thermodynamic score [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull]. Chemically similar Na–In–Se/Na–In–S and alkali selenide analogues are reported with E_hull = 0.0, including NaInSe₂ and NaInS₂ (CS1, CS3). TS analogues also have low E_hull values, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so TS is useful only as a weak thermodynamic similarity signal and not as a route-specific chemical signal (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the computed Na₂In₂Se₃ phase is stable against experimentally accessible redox/stoichiometric competing phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Vacuum solid-state reaction

Route 1 is chemically well supported because NaInSe₂ is synthesized under vacuum from Na/In chalcogenide precursors at 300 °C (CS1). The condition score is reduced by the need for vacuum/sealed handling, volatile Se/Na–Se components, and air-sensitive/toxic selenides [Reasonable Inference; CS1 uses vacuum and dry N₂]. Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory oxide synthesis because the Na–In–Se analogue forms at low temperature in CS1, but structural reconstruction from NaInSe₂-like chemistry to monoclinic Na₂In₂Se₃ is uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific support for this Na–In–Se solid-state path and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct elemental sealed-ampoule synthesis

Direct elemental synthesis is supported broadly by alkali/metal/chalcogen sealed-tube reactions for alkali selenides (CS2, CS5). The main condition penalties arise from volatile Na and Se, sealed-vessel operation, and air-sensitive Na handling [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, molten/volatile components can improve mixing, but direct synthesis requires complete nucleation and ordering of the target and can easily form binary or NaInSe₂-type competing phases [Reasonable Inference; phase-control difficulty is noted in CS2]. SS/TS analogues do not provide chemically specific support and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Flux-assisted synthesis

Flux synthesis receives the best kinetic score because molten alkali halide or polyselenide media should enhance ion diffusion and crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 provides a direct precedent for flux methods in NaInS₂-related chemistry, while CS2 provides precedent for alkali halide media in Na₂M₂Se₃-related selenide synthesis. The severity score remains moderate because sealed/inert handling, volatile selenium, air-sensitive selenides, and flux-removal steps are still required [Reasonable Inference]. The key uncertainty is whether flux conditions stabilize the desired Na₂In₂Se₃ rather than the more familiar NaInSe₂/In₂Se₃ chemistry [Reasonable Inference based on CS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Cation-exchange / topochemical route

Cation exchange is plausible because CS2 reports cation-exchange techniques for Na₂Mn₂Se₃-related selenides and CS1 establishes NaInSe₂ as an accessible Na–In–Se precursor. However, converting NaInSe₂ to Na₂In₂Se₃ requires both stoichiometric and coordination changes, so the reaction may not be truly topotactic [Reasonable Inference]. Conditions are not extreme but require multiple steps, sealed/inert atmosphere, and air-sensitive reagents. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific support and are neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different Na and In local bonding environments, with Na–Se distances around 3.10–3.43 Å and In–Se distances around 2.66–2.67 Å, indicating cation-size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing connectivity but not face-sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided; CS/SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this check, so it is not triggered but remains uncertain. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium, not high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂In₂Se₃ is likely thermodynamically stable or very near ground-state because its reported E_hull is negative within computational tolerance [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull]. The strongest support comes from CS data: NaInSe₂ is a same-system Na–In–Se phase synthesized under mild vacuum solid-state conditions (CS1), and Na₂Mn₂Se₃ shows that Na₂M₂Se₃-type selenides can be accessed under sealed high-temperature or exchange conditions (CS2). The most critical enabling factor is the excellent thermodynamic stability combined with chemically close Na–In–Se and Na₂M₂Se₃ precedents (CS1, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry/redox control, because the target is Se-poor relative to NaInSe₂ and may compete with NaInSe₂, In₂Se₃, Na₂Se, or metallic In [Reasonable Inference]. Flux-assisted synthesis is recommended because it improves diffusion and crystallization while allowing control of Na and Se chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference based on CS2, CS3].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Se volatility and ampoule pressure | High | Use staged low-temperature prereaction before high-temperature anneal [Reasonable Inference]; sealed evacuated tube handling is supported by CS1 and CS2. |
| 2 | All | Na or Na₂Seₓ air/moisture sensitivity | High | Load and grind under dry Ar/N₂; CS1 explicitly used dry N₂ mixing, and CS5 used argon/dry-box handling. |
| 3 | All | Competing NaInSe₂ formation | High | Tune Se chemical potential lower and include In metal buffer [Reasonable Inference]; NaInSe₂ is a known stable same-system phase (CS1). |
| 4 | All | Competing In₂Se₃ or residual In | Medium | Use repeated grinding/annealing and small stoichiometry adjustments [Reasonable Inference]; repeated grinding/annealing is supported in alkali selenide synthesis by CS5. |
| 5 | Route 1 | Slow ordering or incomplete reconstruction from binary precursors | Medium | Extend annealing to several days and perform intermediate regrinding [Reasonable Inference]; long anneals are used in CS2 and CS5. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Exothermic Na–Se reaction during direct elemental synthesis | High | Prereact at 200–250 °C before higher-temperature annealing [Reasonable Inference]; gradual heating is used in CS5. |
| 7 | Route 3 | Flux contamination or product decomposition during washing | Medium | Choose removable halide flux and test dry/inert washing before water exposure [Reasonable Inference]; CS2 reports washing after salt-mediated synthesis, while CS3 reports flux dissolution. |
| 8 | Route 3 | Narrow Na/In/Se chemical-potential window | High | Use Na-rich flux with In/Se buffers and slow cooling to improve crystallization [Reasonable Inference]; alkali halide/cation-exchange media are supported by CS2 and flux methods by CS3. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Topochemical route may not preserve framework | High | Treat Route 4 as exploratory; verify precursor and product by powder XRD after short exchange trials [Reasonable Inference]; CS2 supports cation exchange but not specifically for Na–In–Se. |
| 10 | All | Crystal growth difficulty for single crystals | Medium | Use slow-cooling flux route; flux/single-crystal growth precedent exists in chemically similar NaInS₂-related systems (CS3), though not directly for Na₂In₂Se₃. |
