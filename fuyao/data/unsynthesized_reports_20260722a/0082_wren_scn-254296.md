### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has a calculated E_hull of −0.024344 eV/atom, so it is assigned a near-maximum stability score under the provided rubric (target). The closest Ba analogue BaF3/BaF(HF2) is also on the hull, and the Sr analogue is only ~0.049 eV/atom above hull, supporting accessibility of nearby alkaline-earth fluoride/HF-derived phases (CS-1; CS-3). SS and TS do not add chemically specific Ba–F evidence, so insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS; both are neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature above 1000 °C and uses only ~573 K in the analogue, which is favorable (CS-1; CS-3). The limiting condition is the 3 GPa HF hydrothermal environment, which is specialized but does not trigger the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty (CS-1; CS-3). HF toxicity and volatility require sealed, corrosion-resistant high-pressure handling, producing the main deductions (CS-1; CS-3; Reasonable Inference).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The closest Ba analogue forms rapidly, in 30 min, in HF at 3 GPa/573 K, indicating favorable solution-mediated kinetics (CS-3). The target Ba–F distances overlap the BaF3/BaF(HF2) analogue range, supporting structural transfer, although the target has more F sites and an F–F contact not fully proven by the analogue (target; CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is whether the nominal BaF5 structure is a true binary fluoride, a missing-H bifluoride/polyfluoride model, or a computationally stabilized phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses 450–600 °C, which avoids the high-temperature penalties (CS-2). However, pF2 > 3500 atm and F2 gas in a Monel autoclave impose a reactive atmosphere, volatile sealed precursor, and toxic precursor penalties (CS-2). The route is technically severe even though the rubric does not penalize pressure below 5 GPa.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High-pressure F2 gives a strong fluorination driving force, and BaF2 is demonstrated as a precursor in a Ba–F-containing high-pressure fluorination reaction (CS-2). The 10 d duration and unexpected formation in the Ba–Ni–F analogue imply a narrow kinetic/phase window and slower solid/gas reaction compared with HF solution synthesis (CS-2). Structural reconstruction from BaF2 to nominal BaF5 is large, so kinetics are less favorable [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 benefits from room-temperature solvolysis and fluorination conditions in the analogue (CS-5). The severe penalties come from aHF/F2 handling, sealed fluoropolymer/vacuum-line equipment, and the multi-operation nature of solvolysis plus fluorination plus crystallization (CS-5). SS and TS give insufficient evidence for this low-temperature BaF5 route and remain neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Solution transport in anhydrous HF/F2 should greatly reduce diffusion barriers, as the CS-5 route proceeds over 72 h at room temperature (CS-5). This may be favorable for assembling F-rich or molecular fluoride motifs, which is relevant because the target has an F–F contact (target; Reasonable Inference). The major uncertainty is BaF2 solubility and whether Ba-containing F-rich crystals nucleate rather than simply forming BaF2 or BaF(HF2)-type products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ba, so cation-size disorder does not contribute to amorphization risk (target). The target description does not provide total unit-cell atom count, and CS/SS/TS do not determine the target’s Z value, so there is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for the atom-count check. The reported target connectivity does not show vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the amorphization risk is scored low, with uncertainty tied to missing full crystallographic cell-size information (target).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is slightly negative, and nearby Ba/Sr fluoride–HF-derived analogues are also on or close to the hull (target; CS-1; CS-3). The best synthesis support comes from CS, especially BaF3/BaF(HF2) made from BaF2 in 43% HF at 3 GPa and 573 K; SS and TS provide insufficient chemically specific evidence and are treated as neutral. The most critical enabling factor is the HF-rich high-pressure medium, which can stabilize unusual alkaline-earth fluoride/bifluoride-like environments and accelerate transport (CS-1; CS-3; Reasonable Inference). The most critical limiting factor is chemical identity/stoichiometry control: nominal BaF5 is formally unusual for Ba2+ unless F–F/polyfluoride or missing-H chemistry is involved [Reasonable Inference from target]. A high-pressure HF route is therefore plausible, but product validation by single-crystal XRD, vibrational spectroscopy, and hydrogen analysis would be essential [Reasonable Inference].
