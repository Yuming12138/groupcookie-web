### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.06736 eV/atom, so by the provided rubric the phase is thermodynamically favorable and receives 100/100. The negative hull value is interpreted as “on hull” within numerical/reference uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. TS entries are mostly chemically unrelated Na–Li amide, U–Co–B, Co–Ag phosphate, AuBr, and W–Ni nitride examples; therefore TS provides insufficient direct Rb–In–Se thermodynamic transfer signal and is neutral 50 for chemistry-specific inference.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is moderately severe because it uses 750–800 °C, which is not extreme, but it requires reactive H₂ saturated with Se (CS1). The same H₂/chalcogen strategy appears in related alkali chalcogenide synthesis (CS2), supporting feasibility but also indicating atmosphere-control demands. SS and TS provide insufficient useful signal for this Rb–In–Se condition choice and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetic advantage of Route 1 is that CS1 shows a direct melt/flux-like carbonate-to-selenide route can form Rb3SbSe3 in only 9 h. The strongest structural/chemical support is the shared Rb3MSe3 stoichiometry and SeRb6M polyhedral environment between Rb3SbSe3 and the target (CS1). The uncertainty is the required change from SbSe3 trigonal coordination in CS1 to InSe4 tetrahedral coordination in the target, which may alter the phase field [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids H₂ gas but requires sealed evacuated or Ar-backfilled ampoules, as used for K3SnSe3 and K3AlSe3 (CS3, CS5). The main severity deductions come from volatile Se/Rb in a sealed system and air-sensitive Rb metal [Reasonable Inference]. Its temperature of 800–900 °C is comparable to the successful 900 °C and 1075 K syntheses in CS3 and CS5, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied.

#### F3 — Route 2

Elemental solid-state synthesis is kinetically plausible because K–Sn–Se and K–Al–Se analogues form directly from elements at high temperature (CS3, CS5). Low-melting Rb, In, and Se should promote liquid-assisted mixing [Reasonable Inference]. However, the route requires complete structural assembly from elements, and CS3 reports a 20 h hold, so ordering kinetics are not as favorable as a true flux route.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more operationally severe because it uses sealed Se-rich flux, air-sensitive Rb2Se/Rb2Sex species, and slow-cooling/separation steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports flux/melt-style Rb–Se chemistry, but no direct Rb–In–Se polyselenide flux recipe is provided. SS and TS are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient route-specific evidence, so they remain neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

A molten alkali-polyselenide flux should give the best diffusion and crystal-growth kinetics [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports the ability of Rb–Se-rich melt chemistry to form Rb3MSe3 phases, and CS4 supports compatibility of RbSe6 polyhedra with tetrahedral main-group selenide units. The uncertainty is phase selectivity, because excess Rb/Se may stabilize binary or alternative ternary Rb–In–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has large cation size/charge contrast between Rb+ and In3+, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly contains mixed edge/corner/face-sharing environments, triggering C3; similar mixed sharing is present in Rb3SbSe3 (CS1). No explicit unit-cell atom count above 40 is given, and the closest CS1 conventional formula contains 28 atoms, so C2 is not triggered; remaining uncertainty comes from the unknown crystallographic Z of the target cell.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3InSe3 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative, effectively on the convex hull within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1, where Rb3SbSe3 with the same Rb3MSe3 stoichiometry and similar Rb–Se/SeRb6M environments was synthesized by a selenium-loaded H₂ melt route. CS3 and CS5 further support that alkali-main-group selenides can be made directly from elements in sealed ampoules. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high Se chemical potential while allowing Rb-containing melts or reactive intermediates to form, as indicated by Se-loaded H₂ and sealed-tube analogues (CS1, CS3, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially avoiding Rb loss, Se loss, oxide residue, or competing Rb–Se/In–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].
