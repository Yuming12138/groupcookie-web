### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.0626 eV/atom, which is strongly favorable by the provided rubric and justifies a high F1 score of 98 (target data). CS evidence is mixed: BaCu₃O₄ is metastable and high above hull (CS-1), whereas BaNiO₃ and SrCu₂O₃ analogues include stable or near-stable phases (CS-2 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient chemistry-specific evidence and are treated neutrally for Ba–Cu–O stability transfer.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, MOCVD.**  
MOCVD severity is high because BaCu₃O₄ required specialized MOCVD equipment, controlled O₂ pressure, cooling under O₂, and post-annealing (CS-1). Kinetics are favorable for thin films because direct epitaxial crystallization of a Ba–Cu–O phase was demonstrated, but target stoichiometry and oxygen content require tighter composition control than the reported BaCu₃O₄ analogue [CS-1; Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the I4₁/a BaCu₂O₄ polymorph can be selected by epitaxy rather than forming BaCu₃O₄ or other Ba–Cu–O phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, high-pressure oxidizing solid state.**  
The route inherits the 4 GPa/1050 °C severity from SrCu₂O₃ synthesis (CS-4, CS-5), but the formal F2 penalty is limited because the scoring table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa. Kinetics are moderate because oxide precursors react under pressure over short times in SrCu₂O₃, yet BaCu₂O₄ requires additional oxygen insertion and likely stronger oxidation [CS-4/CS-5; Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether pressure favors the tetragonal BaCu₂O₄ topology or instead stabilizes a different dense Ba–Cu–O phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3, high-O₂-pressure flux.**  
This route is severe because BaNiO₃ flux growth required 2000 bar O₂ in a gold-tube capsule for 48 h (CS-3). Its kinetic score is relatively good because flux transport can bypass slow solid-state diffusion at only 600 °C, but structural transfer from BaNiO₃ to BaCu₂O₄ is incomplete (CS-3; Reasonable Inference). The uncertainty is Cu solubility/speciation in Ba hydroxide flux and whether the high oxygen pressure stabilizes the desired Cu–O square-planar network [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 4, reactive sol-gel / nitrate plus O₂ anneal.**  
This route has the mildest condition severity because it avoids MOCVD and high-pressure equipment, using only controlled oxygen at 750–900 °C [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1 oxygen sensitivity and CS-2 solid-state oxide processing]. Kinetics benefit from molecular-scale mixing, as generic precipitation/calcination can form crystalline oxides at moderate temperature in the unrelated CoAgPO₄ example (SS-3/TS-3), but no direct BaCu₂O₄ ambient-pressure precedent is provided. The low structural-reconstruction subscore reflects the risk of forming more common Ba–Cu–O competing phases rather than the target [Reasonable Inference; CS-1].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Ba/Cu cation size mismatch, which triggers C1 and creates some amorphization or segregation risk [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The target description does not show a very large unit cell or mixed edge/face/vertex polyhedral coexistence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered from the provided target data. SS/TS do not provide chemically relevant amorphization evidence for Ba–Cu–O, so their signal is insufficient and treated neutrally.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaCu₂O₄ appears thermodynamically favorable from the reported negative E_hull, so it may be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase in the calculation (target data). However, the closest Ba–Cu–O analogue BaCu₃O₄ is explicitly metastable and required epitaxial MOCVD stabilization, which warns that Ba–Cu–O phase selection may be kinetically delicate (CS-1). The strongest enabling factor is the combination of very favorable computed stability and known square-planar Cu–O chemistry in Ba/Sr cuprate analogues (target data; CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). The strongest limiting factor is oxygen chemical potential and Cu oxidation-state control, because high-O₂ pressure is needed in some Ba–transition-metal oxide growth and controlled O₂ is required for Ba–Cu–O film synthesis (CS-1, CS-3). Overall, synthesis is plausible but likely requires either highly oxidizing bulk conditions or epitaxial/flux stabilization rather than a simple unconstrained ceramic firing [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Oxygen stoichiometry and Cu oxidation-state control | High | Use controlled O₂ during deposition/annealing as in BaCu₃O₄ MOCVD (CS-1), or high-O₂ pressure as in BaNiO₃ flux growth (CS-3). |
| 2 | All | Competing Ba–Cu–O phase formation, especially BaCu₃O₄-, BaCuO₂-, or CuO-containing mixtures | High | Use phase-purity monitoring and strict conditions; CS-2 reports high purity only with careful pressure/temperature control, and CS-1 shows BaCu₃O₄ can be stabilized under specific epitaxial conditions. |
| 3 | Route 1 | Narrow cation-ratio control in vapor deposition | Medium–High | Calibrate Ba/Cu precursor delivery because MOCVD BaCu₃O₄ used specialized single-source flash evaporation and controlled growth conditions (CS-1). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Need for epitaxial stabilization and substrate selection | Medium | Use SrTiO₃ or LaAlO₃ substrates, which supported BaCu₃O₄ and related film growth (CS-1). |
| 5 | Route 2 | High-pressure apparatus availability and capsule compatibility | High | Use cubic-anvil conditions analogous to SrCu₂O₃ synthesis at 4 GPa and 1050 °C (CS-4, CS-5), with inert/noble-metal capsules [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Internal oxidizer hazards in sealed high-pressure capsules | High | Use minimal oxidizer excess and compatible capsule materials; high-O₂-pressure BaNiO₃ work used gold-tube capsules, supporting noble-metal containment (CS-3). |
| 7 | Route 3 | High-pressure oxygen safety and flux corrosion | High | Follow BaNiO₃ precedent using gold-tube capsule, 600 °C, 2000 bar O₂, and controlled thermal gradient/hold time (CS-3). |
| 8 | Route 3 | Crystal growth difficulty and possible low yield | Medium | Use slow cooling/thermal gradients because BaNiO₃ flux growth used a thermal gradient and 48 h dwell to obtain crystals (CS-3). |
| 9 | Route 4 | Ambient-pressure route may not stabilize the target polymorph | High | Escalate to high-O₂ flux or epitaxial MOCVD if ambient O₂/O₃ anneals yield impurities; this mitigation is supported by CS-1 and CS-3. |
| 10 | Route 4 | Slow ordering or incomplete reaction in bulk powders | Medium | Use sol-gel/coprecipitation for intimate mixing [Reasonable Inference], plus repeated grinding/annealing; careful solid-state control was needed for high-purity Ba–Ni oxide synthesis (CS-2). |
