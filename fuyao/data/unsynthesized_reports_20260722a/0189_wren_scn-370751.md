### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s exact \(E_\mathrm{hull}=-0.017477134\) eV/atom places it effectively on the convex hull, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the supplied ≤0.05 eV/atom rubric. Chemically similar phosphides and Ca–Pd intermetallic analogues are also computed on-hull, including Ca(RuP)₂, CaRh₂P₂, and Ca₂Pd₂Ga (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS include near-hull materials, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, oxides, halides, and nitrides, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for Ca–Pd–P-specific thermodynamic stabilization.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderately severe because the closest Ca/Rh/P and Ca/Ru/P solid-state analogues require 1000–1200 °C, Ar, sealed ampoules, long treatments, and repeated grinding (CS-1, CS-4). P volatility and Ca air sensitivity require sealed/inert handling [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-4]. There is no evidence for high pressure from CS-1 or CS-4, and insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this route, so SS/TS are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are limited by use of elemental Pd and Ca/P powders, because chemically similar solid-state routes require long annealing and repeated grinding to achieve homogeneous phosphides (CS-1, CS-4). The target’s monoclinic Pd-rich structure is not the same as the tetragonal CaRu₂P₂/CaRh₂P₂ analogues, so complete reconstruction and cation ordering are expected [Reasonable Inference from target vs CS-1/CS-4]. The uncertainty is whether Ca–Pd–P has a low-melting transient phase that would accelerate reaction; CS does not prove this.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is the most severe because arc-melting/induction annealing of phosphides uses very high local temperatures and controlled Ar/vacuum/Ta-tube environments (CS-2), and Ca–Pd intermetallic work used Ta ampoules sealed by arc-melting and glovebox handling (CS-5). Binary phosphide use reduces P loss but adds a precursor-preparation step [Reasonable Inference from CS-2]. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this Ca–Pd–P melt route and are scored neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt homogenization should improve cation mixing relative to powder sintering, as CS-2 explicitly used repeated arc-melting with sample inversion. The subsequent anneal is still needed because the target is an ordered ternary phosphide and may require slow Ca/Pd/P sublattice ordering [Reasonable Inference from CS-2/CS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether phosphorus loss during melting shifts the composition away from Ca₃Pd₇P₄.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has the best severity score because CS-3 shows alkaline-earth transition-metal phosphides can be grown in Sn flux at ~880 °C in sealed silica tubes, avoiding >1000–1400 °C bulk diffusion conditions. It still requires sealed handling because P is volatile and Ca is air-sensitive [Reasonable Inference from CS-3/CS-4]. SS and TS offer no useful Ca–Pd–P flux-synthesis signal and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth improves reaction kinetics by providing liquid transport, and CS-3 directly demonstrates tin-flux preparation of related alkaline-earth transition-metal phosphides. However, the target is Pd-rich and monoclinic rather than a direct ThCr₂Si₂-type MRu₂P₂ analogue, so structural nucleation selectivity is uncertain [Reasonable Inference from target vs CS-3]. The remaining risk is formation of Sn-containing phases or binary Ca–P/Pd–P impurities.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The Ca/Pd cation size and bonding contrast is large, triggering the ionic-radius/chemical-size mismatch check [Reasonable Inference from the target composition]. The target formula unit has 14 atoms, and the supplied target description does not provide a unit-cell atom count exceeding 40, so C2 is not triggered. The target description lists many high-coordinate environments but does not explicitly establish mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, so C3 has insufficient evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₃Pd₇P₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed \(E_\mathrm{hull}\) is slightly negative/effectively zero. The chemical-similarity evidence is reasonably strong: Ca–transition-metal phosphides are synthesizable by sealed solid-state and flux methods, and Ca–Pd intermetallic bonding is experimentally accessible near 1000 °C (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of sealed-tube/flux routes that maintain P activity while improving diffusion, especially the Sn-flux precedent for alkaline-earth transition-metal phosphides (CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity for the Pd-rich monoclinic Ca₃Pd₇P₄ stoichiometry, because the closest phosphide analogues are mostly 122-type tetragonal phases rather than the target structure [Reasonable Inference from target vs CS-1/CS-3/CS-4]. Overall, the target is synthesizable with moderate effort, with flux growth or flux-assisted annealing preferred over direct elemental sintering.
