### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.0061 eV/atom, so it is effectively a ground-state prediction in the supplied thermodynamic model. TS analogues include several stable E_hull = 0 chlorine-rich compounds, supporting the possibility of stable chloride frameworks or clusters (TS1–TS4). However, CS1 shows NaCl3 has positive E_hull at ambient conditions while still being synthesized under pressure, so pressure-dependent stability remains a key uncertainty.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, DAC high-pressure synthesis.**  
CS1 directly supports the use of Na + Cl in a laser-heated DAC at 10–80 GPa, so the route is chemically well matched but conditionally severe. The severe F2 score comes from high pressure, reactive Cl2, sealed loading, and air-sensitive Na/Cl handling (CS1). Kinetics are moderately favorable because elemental Na and Cl2 are reactive, but forming NaCl5 with Cl–Cl bonded units requires major structural reconstruction beyond the NaCl3 analogue [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2, large-volume high-pressure synthesis.**  
This route extrapolates CS1 from DAC to multi-anvil or other bulk high-pressure apparatus [Reasonable Inference]. The same pressure and chlorine-handling penalties apply, but reaction uniformity and capsule compatibility are more uncertain than in DAC [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics may be slower if NaCl is used instead of Na metal, and no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates bulk NaCl5 synthesis.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, sealed-ampoule vapor/chlorination route.**  
SS1, SS4, TS1, and TS4 show that chlorine-rich molecular chlorides can crystallize in sealed ampoules at 140–155 °C under vacuum gradients. SS2 and TS2 show that reactive chlorinating liquids such as SOCl2/SCl2 can drive chloride cluster chemistry at ~140–150 °C. The limitation is chemical: CS1 is the only binary Na–Cl analogue and points to high pressure, so ambient-pressure NaCl5 formation remains weakly supported.

**F2/F3 — Route 4, Lewis-acid chloride flux.**  
CS3 supports molten GaCl3/AlCl3 flux growth at 100–125 °C under Ar, and CS3/CS4 show Na–Cl coordination environments in Na chlorometallates. This route has mild temperatures and good ion mobility, which improves F2/F3. The major uncertainty is that Al/Ga fluxes may preferentially form NaAlCl4/NaGaCl4-type phases instead of binary NaCl5, so the raw score is expert-penalized.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-size disorder is not expected. The described connectivity is mainly edge-sharing NaCl6 octahedra plus Cl–Cl bonding, without evidence for simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity. SS/TS analogues include many molecular/cluster chlorides that crystallize as ordered phases rather than amorphous products, but they are chemically different, so F4 remains favorable but not definitive (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaCl5 is likely a pressure-stabilized or at least strongly condition-dependent phase rather than a straightforward ambient-pressure salt, because the closest binary Na–Cl analogue NaCl3 required 10–80 GPa laser-heated DAC synthesis (CS1). The computed hull value is highly favorable, but CS1 shows that non-1:1 Na–Cl phases may depend on pressure even when thermodynamic descriptors are promising. SS and TS analogues support the broader idea that chlorine-rich cluster or polyhalide-like structures can crystallize, but they do not directly prove ambient-pressure binary NaCl5 synthesis (SS1–SS4, TS1–TS4). The key enabling factor is high chlorine chemical potential under pressure, which CS1 explicitly used. The main limiting factor is phase control against NaCl, NaCl3, and possible chlorine loss during heating [Reasonable Inference from CS1].
