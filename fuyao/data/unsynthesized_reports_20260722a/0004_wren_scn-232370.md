### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported hull energy is −0.013753 eV/atom, which places it effectively on the convex hull and gives strong thermodynamic support for synthesizability. Several CS examples also have low hull energies, including Sm₃RuO₇ at 0.0049 eV/atom (CS-1), BaRuO₃ 4H at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-3), and Pr₅Ru₂O₁₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS-4), showing that low-hull complex Ru/Ir-like oxides can correspond to reported synthesized phases. SS and TS include low-hull materials, but they are chemically unrelated, so they provide thermodynamic similarity only in a generic sense and insufficient direct chemical synthesis signal for Cs₂IrO₆ (SS, TS).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate F2 score because ceramic oxide analogues use standard furnaces, air, grinding, pelletizing, and repeated firing rather than exotic apparatus (CS-1, CS-5). The main penalty is the need for multiple firings and likely Cs volatility control [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient direct signal for this solid-state Cs–Ir–O route and are treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Route 1 has weak kinetics because carbonate/oxide solid-state reactions involving refractory IrO₂ are expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-5 both report repeated grinding/refiring to complete analogous oxide reactions, supporting a low structural-reconstruction and diffusion score. The uncertainty is whether the target structure forms directly from Cs-rich intermediates at lower temperature, which is not demonstrated by CS/SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized strongly for high-pressure equipment, following CS-2 where BaRuO₃ high-pressure synthesis uses 1.5–5.0 GPa and specialized cubic-anvil hardware. It is also penalized for capsule/sealed handling and multi-step preparation [Reasonable Inference]. SS-1 and TS-1 show high-pressure autoclave synthesis only for amides, so they do not directly validate high-pressure Cs–Ir–O oxide synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

High pressure can help stabilize dense or metastable oxide polymorphs, as suggested by CS-2 for BaRuO₃, so Route 2 has better structural-phase leverage than ambient dry ceramic synthesis. However, the same route still uses solid precursors and refractory Ir chemistry, so diffusion is not intrinsically fast [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Cs₂IrO₆ needs pressure for topology stabilization or instead simply needs high oxygen chemical potential.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has relatively favorable severity because CS-4 demonstrates oxide flux growth at 600 °C in sealed silver tubes, well below the 1000 °C penalty threshold. The route is still penalized for sealed volatile/corrosive Cs-rich flux and multiple operations including sealing, controlled cooling, and washing [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 additionally shows flux methods can involve long thermal programs and leaching.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 has the best kinetics because molten flux can mediate dissolution and reprecipitation, reducing the need for slow bulk diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 provides strong support that hydroxide fluxes can crystallize complex Ru oxides at 600 °C, while CS-3 shows halide flux can grow BaRuO₃ crystals at ambient pressure. The uncertainty is phase selectivity, because CS-3 reports the desired BaRuO₃ polymorph can appear as a minor product mixed with a stable competing phase.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

F4 is moderately favorable because the target appears to have a small, ordered crystallographic description with one Cs, one Ir, and one O site, and no reported coexistence of vertex-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra. The major amorphization risk is the large size/coordination mismatch between 12-coordinate Cs and octahedral Ir [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS structural analogues are chemically unrelated and do not provide useful direct amorphization evidence for this oxide, so they are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₂IrO₆ is likely thermodynamically viable, because its reported hull energy is effectively zero, but it may still behave as a metastable or narrow-window phase if the required Ir oxidation state is unusually high [Reasonable Inference]. The CS data support oxide synthesis broadly: ceramic methods work for Sm–Ru–O and Ba–W–O analogues (CS-1, CS-5), high pressure stabilizes BaRuO₃ polymorphs (CS-2), and flux growth forms Ru oxides at lower temperature (CS-3, CS-4). The SS and TS sets do not chemically match Cs–Ir–O and therefore provide insufficient direct synthesis guidance beyond generic low-hull/metastability context. The most critical enabling factor is a Cs-rich, oxidizing flux that lowers kinetic barriers while preserving Cs stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is redox/stoichiometry control, especially preventing Cs loss and achieving the oxygen chemical potential needed for short Ir–O bonds [Reasonable Inference].
