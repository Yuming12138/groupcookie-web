### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.113865 eV/atom, so it is scored in the highest thermodynamic-stability bin by the requested rubric. The chemical analogue LiIrB and exact-structure analogue EuIr₂B₂ are both hull-stable with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, which supports the plausibility of stable Ir–B framework borides (CS-2, CS-5). TS examples include several hull-stable or near-hull phases, but they are chemically unrelated to Na–Ir–B, so there is insufficient evidence from TS for target-specific thermodynamic transfer and TS is neutral, 50, for this source-specific signal.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for T >1000 °C because EuIr₂B₂ used 1150 °C for 40 h and related borides used ~1100 °C conditions (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). The sealed system and air-sensitive/volatile precursor penalties arise from Na handling [Reasonable Inference], with LiIrB and Ca/Eu–Os–B analogues showing the need for glovebox/sealed-tube/excess-metal/distillation control (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). SS and TS do not provide useful target-chemistry severity evidence because their top hits include amides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides rather than Na–Ir–B borides; insufficient evidence from SS and TS, neutral 50 if considered independently.

#### F3 — Route 1

The main kinetic limitation is solid-state reaction among refractory Ir and B powders [Reasonable Inference], even though direct reaction works for LiIrB and EuIr₂B₂ (CS-2, CS-5). Structural reconstruction is favorable because EuIr₂B₂ has the same Fddd alpha-Pu-derived structure and LiIrB has the same Fddd Ir–B framework motif (CS-2, CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is whether Na can occupy the 14-coordinate site without forming a different Na–Ir–B stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Arc melting is established for REIr₂B₂ and YbOsB₂-related borides, but these methods require specialized arc-melting setups and high-temperature processing under gettered Ar (CS-4, CS-5). The main additional severity for NaIr₂B₂ is that Na cannot be retained easily in an open arc-melting environment, so a sealed post-anneal is required [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS route analogues such as phosphate precipitation or ammonolysis are not transferable to this metallic boride, so they provide insufficient evidence from SS/TS and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Arc melting improves Ir–B homogenization and is proven for rare-earth Ir–B borides (CS-5), which improves diffusion relative to cold powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, Na incorporation likely must occur in a separate sealed anneal, creating partial reconstruction and composition-control risk [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore kinetically feasible but less cleanly transferable than a Na-rich sealed flux route.

#### F2 — Route 3

The Na-flux route avoids the highest temperatures by using molten Na as a reactive medium [Reasonable Inference], while still relying on sealed refractory-metal ampoules analogous to Ta-tube boride syntheses (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). It is penalized for volatile/air-sensitive Na and for multi-step flux removal, which is consistent with excess-metal distillation used in related Ca/Eu/Li borides (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). It avoids high pressure, H₂, and NH₃, unlike irrelevant hydrothermal/ammonolysis SS/TS entries, so those SS/TS examples are insufficient for this route.

#### F3 — Route 3

The Na flux is expected to increase precursor activity and lower effective diffusion distances [Reasonable Inference], and this is supported indirectly by the use of excess electropositive metal and distillation in Ca/Eu–Os–B and LiIrB-related syntheses (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). The structure-transfer score is high because the target matches EuIr₂B₂ in Fddd topology and has very similar Ir–B distances to LiIrB/EuIr₂B₂ (CS-2, CS-5). The key uncertainty is whether Na flux stabilizes the desired Na:Ir:B = 1:2:2 phase rather than dissolving/precipitating binary Ir borides or other ternaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large site/bonding contrast between Na in a high-coordinate cavity and Ir in a compact Ir–B framework, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference from target structural description]. The available CS analogue EuIr₂B₂ does not establish a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network, and SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific structural evidence, so C2 and C3 are not triggered (CS-5; insufficient evidence from SS/TS). Thus amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(IrB)₂ is likely to be thermodynamically stable or at least very strongly stabilizable, because its reported E_hull is negative and the closest Ir–B analogues LiIrB and EuIr₂B₂ are hull-stable (CS-2, CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS, especially EuIr₂B₂, which has the same alpha-Pu-derived Fddd structure, and LiIrB, which confirms alkali–Ir–B Fddd chemistry (CS-2, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the robust Ir–B framework motif with Ir–B distances around 2.06–2.16 Å, closely matching LiIrB/EuIr₂B₂ (CS-2, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is retention and stoichiometric control of Na under high-temperature boride synthesis conditions [Reasonable Inference], since analogous syntheses require sealed ampoules, excess electropositive metals, homogenization, and sometimes distillation (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). SS and TS do not add strong target-specific support because their top hits are chemically dissimilar, so their useful signal is insufficient and neutral.
