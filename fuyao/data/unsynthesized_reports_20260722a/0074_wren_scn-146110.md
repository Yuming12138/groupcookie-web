### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.142217 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum stability score under the provided rubric. CS gives mixed thermodynamic context: conventional complex chlorides such as NaGaCl₄ and NaAlCl₄ are on the hull, while the chemically closest chlorine-rich NaCl₃ analogue has E_hull ≈ 0.240 eV/atom and required high pressure (CS). SS and TS contain low-hull materials but not chemically relevant Li–Cl chloride analogues, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for the specific thermodynamic reliability of LiCl₄; both are neutral. Remaining uncertainty is high because a negative hull for such an unusual binary stoichiometry may reflect missing competing phases or pressure-dependent stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because the NaCl₃ analogue required 10–80 GPa and laser heating in the presence of excess chlorine or sodium (CS). The target LiCl₄ is even more chlorine-rich than NaCl₃, so requiring high chlorine chemical potential and high pressure is a reasonable extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for high-pressure Li–Cl synthesis specifically, so they are neutral. The main uncertainty is the exact temperature required because CS states high temperatures but does not give a specific laser-heating temperature.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Li and Cl₂ should be highly reactive, and CS confirms that elemental alkali/halogen high-pressure reaction was experimentally used for unusual Na–Cl phases (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). High-pressure laser heating can overcome kinetic barriers, as explicitly noted for NaCl₃ synthesis in CS. However, the target requires formation of a 1D LiCl₆-octahedral ribbon from elements, which is a substantial structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore moderate-high rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from very mild temperatures because chloride flux chemistry in molten GaCl₃/AlCl₃ occurs at 100–125 °C (CS). The severity arises from moisture-sensitive Lewis-acid chlorides, sealed ampoules, possible Cl₂ or SbCl₃ additives, and long processing times (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS AuBr also shows dry N₂/vacuum low-temperature halide processing but does not reduce the air-sensitivity issue (SS/TS). The main uncertainty is whether ambient-pressure flux conditions can stabilize binary LiCl₄ at all.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because molten chloride media provide high ion mobility at low temperature [Reasonable Inference], and CS reports crystal growth in viscous GaCl₃/AlCl₃ melts over 5–69 days. The route avoids long-range solid-state diffusion through dense solids, which is favorable for Li-containing chloride assembly [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is structural reconstruction: NaGaCl₄/NaAlCl₄ are complex chloride salts with tetrahedral GaCl₄/AlCl₄ units, whereas LiCl₄ is a binary 1D LiCl₆-octahedral ribbon. Therefore, the route is kinetically plausible but chemically speculative.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CVT/sealed-tube chlorination has moderate severity because FeCl₃ CVT uses dry Cl₂ at 133 Pa and a 593 K to 373 K gradient, requiring controlled chlorine handling but not GPa pressure (CS). AuBr decomposition likewise uses dry nitrogen or vacuum over weeks, supporting dry low-temperature halide processing (SS/TS). The route is penalized for Cl₂ volatility/toxicity, sealed-tube handling, and Li air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. TS has insufficient Li–Cl-specific evidence and is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The vapor route has weak kinetic confidence because Cl₂ is volatile but no reference demonstrates transport of Li-rich or Li–Cl polyhalide vapor species to form LiCl₄. CS supports FeCl₃ CVT, but FeCl₃ has a known volatile chloride chemistry that ordinary LiCl lacks [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS AuBr warns that low-temperature halide phase formation can be slow and non-reproducible. The need to control a 1:4 Li:Cl composition makes crystallization and composition control difficult [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The reported structural motif is simple 1D edge-sharing LiCl₆ octahedra, so mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity is not triggered by the target description. CS and SS/TS contain several crystalline halides, including 1D AuBr ribbons, supporting a low intrinsic amorphization tendency when moisture and stoichiometry are controlled (CS; SS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is the exact unit-cell atom count, which is not provided by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted as thermodynamically stable by the supplied hull value, but the unusual LiCl₄ stoichiometry and chlorine-rich composition make it prudent to treat it as potentially pressure-stabilized or metastable at ambient conditions [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest chemical support is the high-pressure Na–Cl work, where unusual chlorine-rich alkali chlorides were accessed only under 10–80 GPa and excess chlorine (CS). The most enabling factor is the high predicted thermodynamic stability combined with highly mobile chloride flux or high-pressure chlorine-rich synthesis conditions (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). The most limiting factor is phase selectivity against ordinary LiCl plus molecular Cl₂, because no CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates binary LiCl₄ formation. The recommended practical strategy is to screen low-temperature Lewis-acid chloride fluxes first for kinetic accessibility, while regarding high-pressure Cl₂ synthesis as the most chemically defensible route if ambient-pressure attempts fail.
