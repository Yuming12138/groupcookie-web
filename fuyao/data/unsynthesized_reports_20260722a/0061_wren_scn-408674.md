### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.00677 eV/atom, which is effectively hull-stable by the rubric and supports a high F1 score. Thermodynamic analogues RbBiF₆, MgPbF₆, RbCrF₅-related fluoride, CsSbF₆, and NaBiO₃ are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom in TS-1 to TS-5, reinforcing that dense high-valent fluoride/oxide frameworks can be thermodynamically stable. CS evidence is somewhat less uniformly favorable because SrPbF₆ is listed at E_hull = 0.162 eV/atom while RbBiF₆ is on hull (CS-1; CS-4). The largest uncertainty is that computed stability may not capture the chemical difficulty of accessing formal Pb(V), which is inferred from charge balance rather than directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 1 F2 — Condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly for using elemental F₂, which is explicitly required in SrPbF₆, MgPbF₆, and RbBiF₆-related syntheses (CS-1; CS-4; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). The temperature is not severe by ceramic standards because close analogues form at 150–500 °C or 400–500 °C (CS-1; CS-4; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). Multi-step handling is likely because SrPbF₆ synthesis may involve precursor preparation, fluorination, and tempering/annealing (CS-1; SS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure or sealed volatile precursors, so those subconditions are scored neutral.

#### Route 1 F3 — Reaction kinetics

Kinetics are favorable relative to refractory solid-state synthesis because MgPbF₆ forms in 4–5 h at 500 °C and SrPbF₆ forms in several hours at 400–500 °C (CS-1; SS-3; SS-5; TS-2). Oxide/salt fluorination is a chemically aggressive conversion route, and RbBiF₆/NaBiF₆-related fluorides also form from oxides/sulfides/carbonates in F₂ at 150–500 °C (CS-4; TS-1). The structural reconstruction penalty is moderate because converting an oxide precursor to the fluoride framework is nontrivial, but using a Na–Pb oxide precursor could reduce cation rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is whether the cubic untilted NaPbF₆ aristotype is kinetically preferred over tilted trigonal/tetragonal analogues such as MgPbF₆/RbBiF₆/SrPbF₆ (SS-1; SS-3; SS-5).

#### Route 2 F2 — Condition severity

Route 2 also requires pure F₂ flow, directly supported by the MF₂ + TbF₄ fluoride syntheses (CS-3; CS-5). Its temperatures, 450–750 °C or 723–923 K in analogues, remain below the severe >1000 °C threshold (CS-3; CS-5). The route is penalized for repeated heating/grinding/annealing because CaTbF₆/CdTbF₆ used overnight heating followed by grinding and 12 h annealing (CS-5). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure or sealed volatile precursors in this route.

#### Route 2 F3 — Reaction kinetics

Binary fluoride precursors are compositionally close to the product, and CS-3/CS-5 show that stoichiometric fluoride mixtures can form complex fluorides under F₂. However, NaF + PbF₄/PbO₂ would need to assemble the three-dimensional corner-sharing NaF₆/PbF₆ network from separate solids, which is a larger structural reconstruction than topochemical fluorination [Reasonable Inference]. The need for grinding and annealing to improve crystallinity in CaTbF₆/CdTbF₆ suggests cation ordering and crystallization may be rate-limiting (CS-5). The route is therefore kinetically feasible but less attractive than oxide/oxy-salt fluorination.

#### Route 3 F2 — Condition severity

Route 3 avoids high temperature because TS-3 uses ambient-temperature photochemical fluorination. It is nevertheless severe because it requires F₂, anhydrous HF, UV irradiation, FEP vessels, and 10–20 days of reaction time (TS-3). Volatile/corrosive HF/F₂ handling justifies the sealed/contained-system penalty (TS-3). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates any need for high pressure above 5 GPa, so that subcondition is neutral.

#### Route 3 F3 — Reaction kinetics

The HF/F₂ medium and UV irradiation provide high chemical activity, as shown by ambient-temperature synthesis of ACrF₆ phases from AF/CrF₃ mixtures (TS-3). Diffusion barriers should be lower than in solid-state ceramic routes because the reactive medium can dissolve/transport fluoride species [Reasonable Inference from TS-3]. The major kinetic uncertainty is crystallization of NaPbF₆ rather than amorphous or microcrystalline Pb fluoride byproducts, because no Pb-based photochemical analogue is reported in CS/SS/TS. TS-3 also notes phase-control difficulties, thermal instability, polymorphism, and impurities for related chromium fluorides, reducing confidence.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s simple cubic Fm-3m ReO₃-derived framework with corner-sharing octahedra only suggests low amorphization tendency from connectivity, unlike RbBiF₆ where Rb coordination includes corners/edges/faces (SS-1; TS-1). The atom count is small for NaPbF₆, so the large-unit-cell trigger is not activated; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS suggests any large-cell complexity. A radius/coordination mismatch trigger is assigned because octahedral Na–F and Pb–F distances differ and formal Na⁺/high-valent Pb radii likely differ by >15% [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that real products may tilt or distort into lower-symmetry structures, as seen for MgPbF₆ and RbBiF₆ (SS-1; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaPbF₆ is likely thermodynamically viable according to its reported near-zero/negative hull energy and the hull-stable thermodynamic analogues in TS-1 to TS-5. The synthesis is supported by strong analogue precedent for PbF₆ and BiF₆ fluoride frameworks made in F₂ streams at 150–500 °C or 400–500 °C (CS-1; CS-4; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, because every close Pb/Bi/high-valent fluoride solid-state analogue uses F₂ or purified F₂ flow (CS-1; CS-4; SS-3; TS-1; TS-2). The most critical limiting factor is the formal oxidation-state mismatch: MgPbF₆ and SrPbF₆ correspond to Pb(IV) chemistry, while NaPbF₆ charge balance implies unusually high-valent Pb, so the target may require unusually oxidizing conditions or may relax to competing Na–Pb(IV) fluoride phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausible but high-risk experimentally, with Route 1 the best first attempt.
