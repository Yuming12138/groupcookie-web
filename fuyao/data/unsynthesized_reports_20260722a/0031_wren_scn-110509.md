### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.018900199 eV/atom, which is effectively a stable/on-hull prediction and warrants a very high F1 score under the provided rubric (target data). Chemically similar alkali oxides K3SbO4, Rb6Ge2O7, Rb6Si2O7, K3SbO3, and Rb2RuO4 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0, supporting the general stability of alkali-rich oxide phases (CS-1–CS-5). SS and TS examples are mostly chemically unrelated nitrides, borides, phosphates, and halides, so they give insufficient evidence for the Rb–Sb–O thermodynamic landscape and are treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

The K3SbO4 analogue was synthesized at 975 K, about 702 °C, under Ar, so no >1000 °C or high-pressure penalties are justified (CS-3). Deductions arise from Rb metal/RbO2 air sensitivity and likely Rb volatility near the proposed temperature [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the need for dry-box/inert handling in the K antimonate report (CS-3) and sealed/controlled Rb oxide syntheses (CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is whether Rb3SbO4 can be made in an open corundum crucible like K3SbO4 or requires sealed containment because Rb is more volatile/reactive than K [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

K/KO2/Sb2O3 directly gives isostructural K3SbO4, so the Rb analogue should have favorable reaction topology and high structural transferability (CS-3). Reactive alkali metal/superoxide precursors should provide high chemical activity and short diffusion lengths through molten/reactive intermediates [Reasonable Inference], and the required temperature is moderate. The main kinetic uncertainty is oxygen-activity tuning: too oxidizing or reducing a mixture could produce alternative Rb antimonates or lower-valent Sb oxides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The flux route is based on Rb-rich melts at 500–800 °C under Ar, which avoids very high temperatures and high pressures (CS-2). It is penalized for multiple operations—glovebox loading, flux reaction, slow cooling, and flux removal—because CS-2 explicitly discusses controlled heating/cooling and removal of excess flux. It is also penalized for air-sensitive/volatile Rb-rich reagents [Reasonable Inference], consistent with reactive alkali-metal flux chemistry in CS-2 and sealed Rb oxide syntheses in CS-5.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux/liquid transport should make kinetics favorable, and CS-2 shows that high-alkali Rb oxides can crystallize from A-rich melts at 650 °C. The route is less structurally direct than Route 1 because the CS-2 phases are Rb germanates with GeO4 tetrahedra rather than SbO6 octahedra; the SbO6 framework evidence comes from CS-3. The main uncertainty is phase selectivity under flux conditions, because CS-2 notes that stoichiometry and oxygen content must be controlled to avoid side phases.

#### F2 — Route 3

The carbonate/Sb(V) oxide route is milder in reagent handling than Rb metal routes and would not require high pressure or >1000 °C treatment [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for multiple firing/regrinding/decarbonation steps and toxic Sb(V) oxide handling [Reasonable Inference]. There is no direct CS/SS/TS report of Rb3SbO4 formation from carbonates, so this route has weaker evidence than Routes 1 and 2.

#### F3 — Route 3

Carbonate/Sb2O5 mixtures should be chemically plausible for an oxide with formal Sb(V), but they are less reactive than alkali metal/superoxide mixtures and may require complete solid-state reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s low E_hull and the existence of K3SbO4 support eventual crystallization of the framework (target data; CS-3). The major uncertainty is whether CO2 evolution, Rb carbonate stability, and Sb2O5 decomposition allow a clean path to Rb3SbO4 without competing Rb antimonates [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/charge contrast between Rb⁺ and Sb⁵⁺, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. However, the structurally analogous K3SbO4 has a compact P2/c cell description and the target contains a simple edge-sharing SbO6 framework rather than a large, mixed-connectivity network (CS-3; target data). The residual amorphization risk is therefore low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3SbO4 is likely a true ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is reported as −0.018900199 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull (target data). The strongest synthesis evidence is the direct K3SbO4 analogue: same A3SbO4 stoichiometry, same monoclinic P2/c symmetry, and same edge-sharing SbO6 octahedral motif, synthesized by a straightforward alkali metal/superoxide solid-state route (CS-3). Rb-containing oxide analogues also show that Rb-rich oxide phases can be synthesized using Rb metal/Rb oxide fluxes or controlled oxide reactions (CS-2, CS-5). The key enabling factor is the combination of high computed thermodynamic stability and a direct isostructural K analogue. The key limiting factor is practical control of Rb volatility, air/moisture sensitivity, and oxygen activity during formation of Sb(V) oxide [Reasonable Inference from CS-3/CS-5].
