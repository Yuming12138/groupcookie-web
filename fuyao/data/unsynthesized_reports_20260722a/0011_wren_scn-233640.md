### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.2041 eV/atom, which falls in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom category and gives F1 = 100. [Target thermodynamic data] Chemically similar Re/W oxides include VRe₂O₉ at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and ScRe₂O₆ near the hull at 0.0066 eV/atom, supporting that crystalline Re–O phases can be stable. [CS3, CS4] SS and TS examples are not chemically relevant to Sr–Re–O perrhenates, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS and their source contribution is treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses low-temperature solution chemistry and ambient pressure, so it avoids the high-temperature, high-pressure, sealed-vacuum penalties seen in CS1, CS3, and CS4. [CS1, CS3, CS4; Reasonable Inference] The only applied severity penalty is for toxic/oxidizing Re(VII) reagents. [Reasonable Inference] SS/TS do not provide direct evidence for aqueous Sr(ReO₄)₂ synthesis, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solution reagents such as Sr²⁺ and ReO₄⁻ should have high precursor activity and do not require refractory cation diffusion. [Reasonable Inference] The structural motif is simple ionic assembly of Sr coordination and ReO₄ tetrahedra; CS4 supports experimentally accessible ReO₄ tetrahedra with very similar Re–O bond lengths. [CS4] Remaining uncertainty is whether the isolated predicted P-3 layered anhydrous phase crystallizes preferentially over hydrates or other Sr perrhenate polymorphs. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 inherits sealed-tube and controlled-atmosphere penalties because CS4 uses a vacuum-sealed quartz tube at 700 °C and CS1 uses sealed quartz tubes under vacuum at 900 °C. [CS1, CS4] Additional penalties are assigned for volatile Re(VII) oxide/perrhenate species and multi-step grind/anneal processing. [Reasonable Inference] This route is feasible but less attractive than solution synthesis because high-valent Re species can be volatile and redox-sensitive. [Reasonable Inference; CS4]

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The solid-state route has moderate precursor activity if NH₄ReO₄/Re₂O₇ is used, but SrCO₃/SrO still requires solid-state reaction. [Reasonable Inference] Refractory-element diffusion would normally be slow, although CS4 shows ReO₄-containing crystalline material can form at 700 °C, implying reactive/volatile Re–O transport can help. [CS4; Reasonable Inference] Phase control remains uncertain because CS1 reports difficult phase control in chemically similar W oxide sealed-tube chemistry and CS4 involves redox-sensitive Re chemistry. [CS1, CS4]

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Hydrothermal crystallization avoids the >1000 °C and multi-GPa penalties; SS1/TS1 demonstrate autoclave synthesis but at NH₃ pressures of 1.7–3.4 kbar, which is not directly required for aqueous perrhenate chemistry. [SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference] The main penalty is again Re(VII) reagent toxicity/oxidizing behavior. [Reasonable Inference] Evidence from SS/TS supports autoclave crystallization generally, but not Sr–Re–O specifically, so the source signal is weak/neutral chemically.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Hydrothermal solution transport gives high precursor activity and minimal bulk diffusion, analogous in process logic to SS1/TS1 where mobile solvothermal media yielded well-crystallized products. [SS1, TS1; Reasonable Inference] CS4 supports the stability of ReO₄ tetrahedral units in crystalline oxides. [CS4] The main uncertainty is phase selection: hydrothermal conditions may favor hydrates, solvates, or different layered packings rather than the anhydrous P-3 target. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization trigger: a large cation size mismatch between Sr²⁺ and Re⁷⁺. [Reasonable Inference] The target structure uses corner-sharing SrO₆–ReO₄ connectivity and does not report edge/face-sharing coexistence, reducing topological frustration. [Target structure] The formula/unit description does not indicate a >40-atom cell, so the cell-size trigger is not activated. [Target structure; Reasonable Inference]

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(ReO₄)₂ is likely thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is strongly below the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold. [Target thermodynamic data] The strongest chemical support comes from CS4, which demonstrates experimentally synthesized ReO₄-tetrahedral rhenium oxide chemistry with Re–O distances closely matching the target. [CS4] CS1 and CS4 also warn that sealed high-valent oxide chemistry can require careful atmosphere and phase control. [CS1, CS4] The most critical enabling factor is the simple Sr²⁺/ReO₄⁻ salt chemistry, which should allow low-temperature solution synthesis rather than refractory oxide diffusion. [Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is selecting the desired anhydrous trigonal P-3 layered polymorph and avoiding hydrate/solvate formation or Re volatility during any thermal treatment. [Reasonable Inference; moisture/handling concern supported by CS4]
