### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.049613466 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and suggesting a ground-state or essentially stable phase. CS1–CS5 include several rare-earth/transition-metal borides with E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that this chemical family commonly forms stable crystalline phases. SS/TS entries are thermodynamically stable in several cases, but they are chemically unrelated to Y–Mo–B, so their target-specific thermodynamic transferability is insufficient and neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 1, arc melting + annealing.**  
Condition severity is moderate-to-high because CS1 uses 1600 °C and vacuum heat treatment, while CS1–CS3/CS5 require arc melting under Ar. Kinetics are helped by melt homogenization and by the existence of isostructural ErMoB3 in CS4, but Mo/B refractory behavior and boride phase competition remain limiting [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in CS2 and CS5 indicate that even after melting, equilibration may require extended treatment.

**F2/F3 — Route 2, direct solid-state.**  
The route has milder nominal temperature because CS4 reports direct solid-state preparation at 1070 K, but sealed inert/vacuum handling is still advisable based on CS1/CS2/CS5 and Y air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the main weakness because constructing a Y–Mo–B boride network from refractory elemental Mo and B at <1000 °C is likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. The isostructural CS4 ErMoB3 analogue improves the structural-reconstruction score.

**F2/F3 — Route 3, melt growth.**  
CS3 and SS2/TS2 support that crystalline borides can be produced by melt/arc/Czochralski-type methods, but they do not prove congruent melting for YMoB3. Kinetics may be favorable in the liquid/near-liquid state, yet phase selection during crystallization can be difficult, as CS5 and SS2/TS2 note phase-control issues in related borides. This route is better for crystal growth after Route 1 establishes phase purity.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one clear amorphization trigger: large Y–Mo size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is likely small because the target formula is YMoB3 and the isostructural CS4 ErMoB3 description corresponds to Er2Mo2B6, well below 40 atoms. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity in the target, so that check is neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YMoB3 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0496 eV/atom and multiple chemically related borides in CS1–CS5 are stable. The strongest support comes from CS4, where ErMoB3 has the same monoclinic P2₁/m structure type and similar Mo–B/B–B bonding, making Y-for-Er substitution plausible [Reasonable Inference]. The most important enabling factor is the strong precedent for rare-earth/transition-metal borides synthesized from elemental metals and boron by arc melting/annealing in CS1–CS5. The main limiting factor is phase control and slow homogenization in refractory boride systems, highlighted by long anneals in CS2/CS5 and phase-complexity comments in CS5. SS/TS analogues mostly do not resemble Y–Mo–B chemically, so they add little beyond weak support for melt/crystal-growth methods.
