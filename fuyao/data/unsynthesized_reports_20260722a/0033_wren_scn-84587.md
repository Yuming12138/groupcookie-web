### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.071472484 eV/atom, which is in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and gives F1 = 100 (Target). Several chemically similar fluorides are on or very near the hull, including CaCrF₆ and MgCrF₆ at E_hull = 0.0 and CaAlF₅ polymorphs at ~0.009–0.011 eV/atom, supporting that related fluoride networks can be stable (CS-1 to CS-4). However, SS and TS analogues are chemically remote from NaNF₆, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for the specific Na–N–F phase chemistry; both are neutral, 50, for this sub-factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized for reactive F₂ atmosphere, pressure-rated sealed/autoclave operation, multi-step handling, and toxic/corrosive reagents, all directly suggested by the CS fluorination of CaCrF₆ and MgCrF₆ in Monell autoclaves under Ar-diluted F₂ (CS-1, CS-4). It is not penalized for >1000 °C or >5 GPa because the CS analogues used only up to 300 °C and up to 300 atm, far below GPa pressure (CS-1, CS-4). Remaining uncertainty is high because the target requires NF₆ octahedral chemistry, for which CS gives insufficient direct evidence.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Gas-phase F₂ can provide high fluorination activity, and CS-1/CS-4 show ABF₆-like octahedral fluoride formation at only 300 °C, supporting moderate kinetics. The target shares corner-sharing octahedral connectivity with CaCrF₆ and MgCrF₆, but the target is cubic and untilted whereas CS analogues are tilted lower-symmetry structures, so structural reconstruction is only partially supported (CS-1, CS-4, Target). The major uncertainty is whether nitrogen can be oxidized and retained as a six-coordinate fluoride unit [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is modeled on sealed platinum fluoride reactions at 850–860 °C, which are below the >1000 °C penalty threshold but still require specialized sealed containers (CS-2, CS-5). It receives deductions for sealed volatile/reactive chemistry and toxic fluorinating/nitrogen reagents because the target cannot be made from conventional metal fluorides alone without an N–F-forming step [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this sealed-tube Na–N–F chemistry and are neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CS-2 and CS-5 indicate that sealed fluoride solid-state reactions can form complex fluorides, but they required long treatments of 96 h and 12 d, respectively, so diffusion and ordering are not fast. The target’s cubic NaF₆/NF₆ framework differs substantially from CaAlF₅/CaFeF₅ structures that contain distorted CaF₇ units and edge/corner connectivity, so reconstruction is significant (CS-2, CS-5, Target). The kinetic score is therefore lower than the flux route.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses CS-3 as the closest processing analogue, where CaAlF₅ was grown from a NaF/CaF₂/AlF₃/chloride flux in Pt under Ar at 998 K with slow cooling. The target adaptation adds penalties for reactive fluorination and volatile nitrogen/fluorine precursors, because CS-3 itself does not solve the N–F formation problem [Reasonable Inference]. No >1000 °C or >5 GPa penalty applies because CS-3 operates near 725 °C and ambient pressure.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth receives the highest kinetic score because CS-3 demonstrates liquid-mediated fluoride crystal growth, which should improve transport and allow slow ordering. The slow 1 K h⁻¹ cooling in CS-3 supports crystal-growth feasibility for related fluoride compositions. The uncertainty is that the CS flux product is CaAlF₅, not NaNF₆, so nitrogen-fluoride stabilization remains an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one triggered amorphization check: a large effective cation-size mismatch between Na⁺ and a high-valent N center in octahedral fluoride coordination [Reasonable Inference], while the target description explicitly gives NaF₆ and NF₆ octahedra (Target). It does not trigger the large-cell criterion because the formula is small and the reported space group is cubic Fm-3m (Target). It also does not trigger mixed-connectivity risk because only corner-sharing octahedra are reported, with no edge or face sharing (Target).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaNF₆ appears thermodynamically favorable by the supplied calculation, with E_hull = −0.071472484 eV/atom, so it would be classified as ground-state-like by the provided thermodynamic metric (Target). The CS set supports synthesis of related fluoride frameworks by high-pressure oxidative fluorination, sealed-tube fluoride reactions, and flux growth, but none directly validates octahedral nitrogen fluoride chemistry (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues are not chemically close to NaNF₆, so they provide insufficient direct synthesis signal and are treated as neutral. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining a high fluorine chemical potential while allowing enough ionic mobility, best addressed by flux-assisted oxidative fluorination using CS-3 plus CS-1/CS-4 logic. The most critical limiting factor is stabilization and retention of N–F coordination without nitrogen loss or decomposition [Reasonable Inference].
