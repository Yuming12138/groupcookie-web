### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has an explicitly provided E_hull of −0.0608 eV/atom, so it falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and receives a high thermodynamic score. The negative hull value suggests the phase is predicted to be stable against known competing phases within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues Sr₂NbN₃, Sr₂TaN₃, Na₃WN₃, Sr₂VN₃, and LiSr₂ReN₄ all have E_hull = 0.0, showing that related nitrides can be true ground-state phases (CS1–CS5). SS/TS provide mixed but generally stable/near-stable examples; however, most are chemically dissimilar, so there is insufficient direct thermodynamic evidence from SS/TS for Ca–W–N beyond neutral support.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Binary-nitride solid-state synthesis is conditionally severe because close analogues require 900–1030 °C, sealed or flowing inert/N₂ atmospheres, glovebox handling, and multi-day reactions (CS1, CS2, CS4). The deduction is driven mainly by air-sensitive alkaline-earth nitride handling and sealed high-temperature processing (CS2, CS4). SS/TS provide insufficient useful signal for this exact binary-nitride route, so they are treated as neutral for Route 1.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
The kinetics are moderate-to-poor because W-containing nitride formation likely has slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and Sr₂TaN₃/Sr₂VN₃ required 5 days at 1000–1030 °C (CS2, CS4). The route benefits from reactive nitride precursors, but the target WN₆ octahedral framework differs from MN₄ tetrahedral motifs in several CS analogues (CS1, CS2, CS4). SS/TS do not provide a close structural analogue for Ca₃WN₄, so structural-kinetic confidence from SS/TS is insufficient and neutral.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Direct nitridation avoids high-pressure and NH₃ penalties and is supported by nitridorhenate synthesis at 850–900 °C under N₂ after Ar preheating (CS5). The main severity penalties are Ca air sensitivity and likely need for containment due to Ca volatility [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 supports W powder pretreatment, but SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for direct Ca–W metal nitridation.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are limited by refractory W and the need to construct the full Ca–W–N framework from elements [Reasonable Inference]. Ca metal should be highly reactive and may enhance local transport near its melting point [Reasonable Inference], while CS5 shows direct metal nitridation can work for related nitridometallates. The uncertainty is high because CS5 involves Re rather than W, and no SS/TS analogue directly demonstrates Ca₃WN₄-type direct nitridation.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Ammonolysis requires flowing NH₃ and multi-step precursor preparation/regrinding, as shown for W₃Ni₂N synthesis from oxide precursors at 725–950 °C (SS5, TS5). It avoids sealed Ca metal handling, but NH₃ gas control and oxygen exclusion are significant operational burdens (SS5, TS5). CS provides insufficient direct evidence for Ca–W oxide ammonolysis to Ca₃WN₄, so CS is neutral-to-weak for this route.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
A mixed oxide precursor can provide excellent Ca/W homogeneity [Reasonable Inference], and SS5/TS5 confirm W nitride formation by ammonolysis. The major kinetic penalty is the reconstructive oxide-to-nitride transformation and likely persistence of CaO because alkaline-earth oxides are stable [Reasonable Inference]. SS5/TS5 also indicate narrow phase windows and decomposition/impurity risks in W nitride ammonolysis.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
The amide/autoclave route operates at lower temperature, but it incurs penalties for sealed reactive media, pressure generation, air-sensitive NaNH₂, and post-reaction washing (CS3). CS3 specifically reports autoclaves, excess NaNH₂, Ar glovebox handling, and air-sensitive products. SS/TS provide insufficient direct signal for amide-mediated Ca₃WN₄ synthesis and are neutral for this route.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
NaNH₂ provides high nitrogen activity and enabled Na₃WN₃ formation from W powder at 450–600 °C (CS3), so precursor activity is favorable. The limiting issue is chemical transferability: Na₃WN₃ formation does not guarantee Ca₃WN₄ formation, and alkali incorporation/contamination is plausible [Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically attractive but phase-selectivity uncertain.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Only the cation-size mismatch check is triggered because Ca²⁺ and W⁶⁺ differ substantially in ionic radius [Reasonable Inference]. The target description contains corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing network, so the mixed-connectivity criterion as written is not fully triggered. The cell size appears modest from the Ca₃WN₄ formula and limited inequivalent sites, but the exact conventional-cell atom count is not provided, so uncertainty remains.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₃WN₄ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the provided E_hull is −0.0608 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar alkaline-earth transition-metal nitrides synthesized by high-temperature solid-state routes and from the known W nitridometallate Na₃WN₃ (CS1–CS4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high nitrogen activity while excluding oxygen and moisture, as multiple analogues require glovebox handling, sealed crucibles, N₂/NH₃ atmospheres, or autoclaves (CS1–CS4, SS5/TS5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic/phase control: W is refractory, Ca is reactive/volatile, and analogous nitrides often require multi-day reactions with impurity issues (CS2, CS3, CS4, SS5/TS5). Overall, synthesis appears plausible but not routine, with direct nitridation or binary-nitride solid-state routes being the most credible starting points.
