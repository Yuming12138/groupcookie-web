### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0611 eV/atom, which is within the highest stability bin and suggests it is at least computationally ground-state-like. Several chemically similar references are computed stable, including Eu(OsB)₄, U₂Os₃Si₅, UIr₃B₂, and CeOs₄B₄ with E_hull = 0 (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5), while synthesized UPt₂Si₂ is still accessible at 0.062 eV/atom above hull (CS-2). TS examples also include low-hull compounds, but most TS materials are chemically unrelated to Na–Pt–Si, so TS is treated as neutral for chemistry-specific stability transfer.

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + anneal

Arc melting and annealing are directly supported for Pt–Si intermetallic UPt₂Si₂ in Ar with post-anneals in evacuated quartz tubes (CS-2), and similar arc/anneal processing is seen for U(CoB)₄-type analogues (SS-2, TS-2). The severity score is reduced because arc melting exceeds 1000–1400 °C and because Na requires sealed handling due to volatility and air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Remaining uncertainty is high because CS-2 contains U–Pt–Si but not Na–Pt–Si, so Na retention during arc processing is not directly proven.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + anneal

Kinetically, melting improves homogenization, and CS-2 shows that Pt–Si intermetallics can be formed by arc melting and annealing. However, Pt is refractory and elemental Pt/Si starting materials are less reactive than solution-derived precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Structural ordering into Na(PtSi)₄ after melting/annealing remains uncertain because no CS/SS/TS reference directly reports this Na-filled Pt–Si framework.

#### F2 — Route 2: Sealed solid-state sintering

Sealed, vapor-tight solid-state synthesis is supported by CS-1 and CS-5, where volatile rare-earth losses are mitigated using BN/Ta containers at 1400 °C, and by CS-3, where ternary silicides are annealed in evacuated quartz tubes at 800 °C for one week. This route is penalized for sealed controlled atmosphere, Na volatility, multi-step precursor preparation, and air-sensitive Na [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not add useful Na–Pt–Si solid-state evidence, so they are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2: Sealed solid-state sintering

Solid-state kinetics are weaker than melt/flux routes because Pt and Pt–Si binaries are refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1, CS-3, and CS-5 show that repeated grinding, recompaction, and long annealing are often needed for related hard intermetallic/boride phases. The main uncertainty is whether Na can diffuse into and stabilize the Pt–Si framework before loss or formation of competing sodium silicides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Na-rich flux

Direct flux evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so the method is proposed as [Reasonable Inference]. Its condition severity is lower than arc melting because the proposed synthesis temperature is below 1000 °C, but it still requires sealed handling, controlled inert loading, and management of volatile, air-sensitive Na [Reasonable Inference]. The sealed-containment logic is supported indirectly by CS-1 and CS-5 for volatile elements.

#### F3 — Route 3: Na-rich flux

Flux growth is kinetically favored because molten Na/Na–Si can enhance Pt transport and reduce reliance on solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Melt-based formation is supported generally by CS-2 and SS-2/TS-2, where arc melting and annealing produce refractory intermetallic/boride phases. The uncertainty is compositional: Na-rich flux could stabilize competing Na–Si or Pt–Si binaries unless the cooling profile and starting ratio are optimized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with a simple reported tetragonal I4 structure and short Pt–Si contacts of 2.44–2.50 Å, suggesting a strongly bonded framework (target description; CS-2 comparison for Pt–Si bonding). One amorphization trigger is assigned because Na is much larger and more electropositive than Pt/Si [Reasonable Inference]. Evidence for a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity is insufficient from CS/SS/TS, so those checks are not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na(PtSi)₄ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is negative, −0.0611 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable thermodynamic category. The strongest analogue support comes from chemically similar UPt₂Si₂, which shares Pt–Si bonding near 2.49 Å and is synthesized by arc melting plus annealing (CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the strong Pt–Si framework similarity and favorable thermodynamic stability (CS-2; target E_hull). The most critical limiting factor is Na volatility and air sensitivity during high-temperature treatment, requiring sealed Ta/Nb containment [Reasonable Inference supported indirectly by volatile-element containment in CS-1 and CS-5]. Overall, synthesis appears plausible, with Na-rich flux growth the best balance between reduced temperature and improved kinetics.
