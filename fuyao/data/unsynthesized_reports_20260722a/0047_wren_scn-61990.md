### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an exact supplied E_hull of −0.100093786 eV/atom, which places it at or below the convex hull by the given scoring rule and justifies a thermodynamic score of 100 (target data). Chemically similar fluorides CdPdF₆, ZnPdF₆, CuSnF₆, and PtNiF₆ are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the idea that ordered A/B hexafluoride frameworks can be true ground-state phases (CS1, CS2, CS3, CS5). SS and TS provide insufficient specific evidence for Ag–Ru fluoride stability because their closest examples are chemically unrelated; they are therefore treated as neutral 50 signals for this factor. The remaining uncertainty is that computed negative hull values can reflect database/reference inconsistencies, so experimental stability still depends on maintaining the correct Ag/Ru oxidation states [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized for reactive F₂ atmosphere, toxic/air-sensitive chemistry, and multistep fluorination, all of which are explicitly reflected in the direct fluorination protocols for PdCdF₆/ZnPdF₆ (CS1, CS2). Its kinetic score is moderate because F₂ fluorination is chemically activating and has produced same-family R-3 compounds, but diffusion/order formation may still require regrinding and refluorination as reported for related Pd fluorides (CS1, CS2). The strongest positive evidence is structural transferability: the target and CdPdF₆ share the same Upper Bainite-derived R-3 corner-sharing octahedral architecture, with similar short high-valent cation–F distances (CS1; target data). The largest uncertainty is whether Ag(II) and Ru(IV) remain mutually compatible without disproportionation or formation of binary fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because CS5 uses 350–400 atm F₂, pressure autoclaves, and multi-day fluorination at 390–450 °C for related noble-metal fluoride chemistry (CS5). The rubric does not deduct for 350–400 atm as >5 GPa, but the route is still operationally hazardous because F₂ pressure fluorination is explicitly demanding and explosion-sensitive in CS5. Kinetically, high F₂ pressure should improve oxidation/fluorination and phase completion, and PtNiF₆ shares the target’s Upper Bainite-derived motif (CS5; target data). The uncertainty is that high-pressure F₂ may over-fluorinate Ru or destabilize Ag-containing phases if the redox window is narrow [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is penalized for sealed reactive fluoride handling, possible volatile fluorides, air-sensitive precursors, and multiple preparation/annealing steps, consistent with sealed copper-tube fluoride syntheses in CS3. Its kinetic score is lower than Routes 1–2 because CS3 reports long reaction times, slow cooling, and phase sensitivity for ternary fluorides, suggesting diffusion/order formation can be slow (CS3). The advantage is that AgF₂ and RuF₄ would already contain the nominal Ag²⁺/Ru⁴⁺ oxidation states and fluoride anions [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is substantial because CS3 is not the same R-3 Upper Bainite-derived structure type, so structural transferability is weaker than CS1/CS2/CS5.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has one major amorphization-risk trigger: the Ag–F bond length is 2.42 Å whereas Ru–F is 1.91 Å, implying a large cation-size mismatch in the alternating octahedral framework (target data; [Reasonable Inference]). The structure is otherwise favorable for crystallization because the target description contains only corner-sharing octahedra and a simple bent F bridge, not mixed vertex/edge/face connectivity (target data). The atom-count criterion is not triggered because the formula and site description are simple, although the exact conventional cell multiplicity is not provided; therefore this remains a minor uncertainty (target data). The resulting F4 score of 78 reflects low-to-medium amorphization risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgRuF₆ is likely to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is negative and chemically similar M²⁺M⁴⁺F₆ fluorides have E_hull = 0 eV/atom (target data; CS1, CS2, CS5). The best synthesis support comes from CS, especially CdPdF₆/ZnPdF₆ direct F₂ fluorination and PtNiF₆ high-pressure F₂ fluorination, because these analogues share the Upper Bainite-derived corner-sharing octahedral fluoride framework (CS1, CS2, CS5). SS and TS provide little useful chemistry-specific guidance for this target because their examples are not comparable fluorides; they are treated as neutral evidence. The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, which should stabilize octahedral Ag/Ru fluoride bonding and the desired F-rich stoichiometry (CS1, CS2, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is redox/phase control involving Ag(II), Ru fluorides, and possible competing binary fluoride phases under highly reactive F₂ conditions [Reasonable Inference].
