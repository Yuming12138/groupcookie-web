### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

BaSiAu3 has a reported E_hull of −0.057747626 eV/atom, so it scores at the top of the provided stability scale. (target) Negative hull values should be interpreted as effectively on-hull within computational uncertainty rather than literally below the convex hull. [Reasonable Inference] TS analogues include many low-hull synthesized materials, but because they are chemically unlike Ba–Au–Si, TS provides insufficient target-specific evidence and is neutral = 50 for route applicability. (TS)

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct fusion

Condition severity is moderate because the closest analogue SrAu3Ge was made at only 700 °C under Ar with Ta/SiO2 containment, avoiding high pressure. (CS) The main penalties are the multi-step thermal program and air-sensitive Ba precursor; Ba sensitivity is extrapolated from general alkaline-earth handling and the target’s Ba content. (target; [Reasonable Inference]) Kinetics are favorable because the SrAu3Ge analogue has nearly matching AAu3T chemistry and forms by direct fusion, while the target Au–Si bonds of 2.49–2.54 Å are comparable to Au–Ge/Au–Si bonding in CS analogues. (CS; target)

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-tube solid-state sintering

This route has moderate severity because Rb–Au–Ga compounds were made at 500 °C in Ta tubes inside evacuated silica ampules, and SrAu3Ge at 700 °C under Ar. (CS) It is kinetically weaker than direct fusion because dense Au and Si may react slowly without a fully molten/flux pathway. [Reasonable Inference] Phase purity is uncertain because Rb/Cs AAu3Ga2 compositions showed impurity levels and sensitivity to composition, so BaSiAu3 may require annealing optimization. (CS)

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Au–Si flux

Flux synthesis is more severe because CeAu4Si2 flux routes used 1050–1150 °C, slow cooling, sealed ampoules, and flux decanting. (CS) Kinetics are strong because liquid Au–Si-rich flux should enhance mass transport and crystal growth. (CS; [Reasonable Inference]) The uncertainty is phase selectivity: CeAu4Si2 has different stoichiometry and rare-earth chemistry, so the method is transferable mainly as a crystallization strategy rather than a direct structure analogue. (CS; target)

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Arc melting plus anneal

Arc melting is broadly supported for intermetallics in CS/SS/TS, including Eu–Pt–B and U–Co–B systems prepared by arc melting and annealing under Ar. (CS; SS; TS) It is kinetically favorable because melting homogenizes refractory/dense elements, but Ba volatility and air sensitivity introduce composition risk. [Reasonable Inference] Because BaSiAu3 appears to require ordered tetragonal coordination, a post-anneal is likely necessary to avoid quenched disorder or competing Au–Si/Ba–Au phases. (target; [Reasonable Inference])

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple intermetallic formula and a described ordered tetragonal I4mm structure with high-coordinate Ba and Au–Si bonding, so amorphization risk is not high. (target) The only triggered risk is strong size mismatch between Ba and the Au/Si framework, inferred from Ba’s 17-coordinate environment and long Ba–Au/Ba–Si distances relative to Au–Si bonds. (target; [Reasonable Inference]) SS/TS do not provide useful amorphization evidence for Ba–Au–Si because the listed structures are chemically dissimilar; neutral source score = 50 for that source-specific factor. (SS; TS)

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaSiAu3 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.057747626 eV/atom. (target) The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar SrAu3Ge, an AAu3T analogue synthesized by direct fusion under moderate temperature and inert atmosphere. (CS) Additional CS examples show that Au-rich intermetallics with alkaline/rare-earth or p-block elements can be made in Ta tubes or fluxes, supporting practical access to Ba–Au–Si chemistry. (CS) The most important enabling factor is the apparent stability of the Au–Si framework with high-coordinate electropositive cation stabilization, analogous to SrAu3Ge and Ce–Au–Si phases. (CS; target; [Reasonable Inference]) The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving air-sensitive Ba and possible competing Ba–Au, Ba–Si, or Au–Si phases during sealed-tube annealing. [Reasonable Inference]
