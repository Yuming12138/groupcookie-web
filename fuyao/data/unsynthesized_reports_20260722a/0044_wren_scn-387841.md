### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.12273884 eV/atom, which is strongly favorable by the provided scoring table and gives F1 = 98. Chemically similar fluorides in CS-1, CS-2, and CS-3 are also reported at E_hull = 0, supporting that dense/ionic metal fluorides in this family can be thermodynamic ground states. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient evidence for fluoride thermodynamics and are neutral 50 for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits moderate conditions from CS-2: 400 °C, dry Ar, sealed Pt tubes, and slow cooling. The severity penalties arise from controlled atmosphere/sealed handling, expected NbF5 volatility, and Tl toxicity [Reasonable Inference], not from high temperature or high pressure. SS and TS give insufficient evidence for this fluoride-specific route and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderately favorable because binary fluorides should be more reactive than oxides, and CS-2 demonstrates TlF + ZrF4 reacting at 400 °C in 12 h. Diffusion remains a concern because Nb is refractory by elemental melting-point proxy, but fluoride volatility/transport may assist reaction [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is partial because the target is a salt-like assembly of NbF6 units and Tl, rather than an extended mixed-polyhedron framework [Reasonable Inference from target structure].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is clearly harsher because CS-1 and CS-3 use reactive F2 atmospheres and specialized autoclaves. Although the listed temperatures are only 300–400 °C, CS-3 requires p(F2) ≈ 3–4 kbar and long reaction times, making the route operationally challenging. The formal F2 deduction table does not include kbar F2 below 5 GPa, so the numerical F2 = 55 may underrepresent practical safety difficulty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are poor because oxide fluorination requires full anion exchange and structural reconstruction. CS-3 reports 5–6 weeks at 380–400 °C for Nb/Ta fluoride crystal formation and notes decomposition/phase-control issues. This supports a low F3 score despite strong thermodynamic driving force.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 avoids high temperature and high external pressure but uses hazardous HF/NbF5/Tl chemistry, so the condition-severity score is limited. No CS/SS/TS source directly validates HF solution growth for this target, so the route is an extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports the need for anhydrous Tl fluoride handling, but not the solution method itself.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics are expected to be best for Route 3 because solution chemistry avoids long-range solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s isolated NbF6 clusters suggest that preformed [NbF6]- units could crystallize with Tl+ more readily than a reconstructive oxide route [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The uncertainty is nucleation of the specific R-3 polymorph rather than a hydrate, solvate, or competing Tl–Nb fluoride.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one amorphization trigger from the large cation size/charge mismatch between Tl+ and Nb5+ [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids the other two triggers because the described structure is zero-dimensional, contains isolated NbF6 clusters, and does not show mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing connectivity. Therefore amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlNbF6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar fluorides in CS are stable at E_hull = 0. The strongest direct experimental support comes from CS-2, where a Tl–transition-metal fluoride was synthesized from TlF and a metal fluoride in sealed Pt tubes at 400 °C. CS-1 and CS-3 show that more aggressive F2 fluorination can access high-valent fluoride phases, including Nb/Ta systems, but with much greater difficulty. The most important enabling factor is the salt-like 0D NbF6/Tl structural motif, which should reduce structural reconstruction barriers [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is hazardous and volatile fluoride chemistry, especially Tl toxicity, NbF5 volatility/moisture sensitivity, and possible competing Tl–Nb–F phases [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-3 phase-control difficulty].
