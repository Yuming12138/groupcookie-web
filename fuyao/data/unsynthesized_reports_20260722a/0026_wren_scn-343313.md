### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The supplied target energy above hull is −0.053352403 eV/atom, so the phase is scored at the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. CS analogues include stable Rb₂Pb₄O₇ and BaBiO₃ polymorphs near the hull, supporting that related heavy-element oxides can be thermodynamically accessible (CS1–CS4). SS/TS provide only weak additional thermodynamic support because they include chemically unrelated stable phases; insufficient chemically specific evidence from SS/TS, so their factor contribution is treated as neutral-to-weak rather than controlling (SS1–SS5; TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or survival under oxidizing flux conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C processing because the closest Rb oxide analogue was synthesized at 450 °C (CS4). It is penalized for controlled/sealed atmosphere and likely Rb volatility because Rb₂Pb₄O₇ used Ag cylinders sealed under vacuum in glass ampoules (CS4). It is also penalized for air-sensitive Rb oxide/peroxide handling, which is an extrapolation from alkali oxide chemistry rather than directly stated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient direct oxide-route condition evidence for this target beyond AuBr low-temperature/vacuum sensitivity (SS4/TS4).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Dry solid-state reaction is kinetically plausible but slow because Rb₂Pb₄O₇ required 30 d at 450 °C and BaBiO₃ analogues required repeated regrinding over 48–480 h (CS1–CS4). The target structure is not a direct perovskite or Rb–Pb oxide analogue, so substantial reconstruction from binary oxides is expected [Reasonable Inference]. The AuBr analogue warns that Au-containing phases can have sluggish, nonreproducible transformations (SS4/TS4). Insufficient direct SS/TS evidence exists for Rb–Au–O oxide diffusion kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is chemically softer but operationally more complex because it uses a controlled oxidizing flux and post-growth flux removal [Reasonable Inference]. Flux growth is supported for BaIrO₃, but that example used 1000 °C BaCl₂ flux, whereas the target route is extrapolated to lower temperature because of Rb volatility and Au oxide lability (CS5; SS4/TS4). Sealed/controlled conditions are supported by Rb₂Pb₄O₇ sealed synthesis and AuBr dry/vacuum synthesis (CS4; SS4/TS4). SS3/TS3 support low-temperature reactive salts but not Rb–Au oxide chemistry specifically.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The flux route receives the best kinetic score because molten salts can improve mass transport, and BaIrO₃ was grown from flux under controlled cooling (CS5). Nitrate/salt precursor activity is supported by AgCoPO₄ precipitation/calcination from nitrates at 500 °C (SS3/TS3), though applying RbNO₃/Rb₂O₂ flux to RbAu₂O₄ is [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction remains a risk because no direct Rb–Au–O analogue is provided, and AuBr shows phase-transition sluggishness and reproducibility issues (SS4/TS4). Thus, flux improves kinetics but does not eliminate phase-selection uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is penalized for controlled high-pO₂ operation, sealed containment, and multi-step capsule handling [Reasonable Inference]. SS1/TS1 show that high-pressure autoclave synthesis can be feasible, but their NH₃ amide chemistry is not chemically transferable to an oxide target, so the evidence is equipment-level only (SS1/TS1). Rb₂Pb₄O₇ sealed synthesis supports the need for containment in Rb oxide chemistry (CS4). There is insufficient direct CS/SS/TS evidence for high-pO₂ Rb–Au–O synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

High pO₂ may help stabilize high-valent Au–O units, but this is inferred from charge-balance and noble-metal oxide chemistry rather than directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. Rb₂Pb₄O₇ shows Rb oxide phases can form at 450 °C but over long times, implying diffusion limitations (CS4). SS1/TS1 indicate high-pressure synthesis can yield well-crystallized tetragonal products, but the chemistry is unrelated and therefore only weakly informative. The route is plausible but less attractive than flux because mass transport is not clearly improved.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between 8-coordinate Rb and square-planar Au, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference from target coordination]. The target description reports edge- and corner-sharing ORb₂Au₂ tetrahedra, but not face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered by the supplied structural description. The unit-cell atom count is not directly supplied; based on the 7-atom formula and common I4₁/a multiplicities it is treated as likely ≤40, but this remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence, so they are neutral for this factor.
