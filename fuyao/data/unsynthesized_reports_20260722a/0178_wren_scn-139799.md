### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.05856167 eV/atom, which indicates it is predicted at or below the computed convex hull and therefore highly thermodynamically favorable by the provided criterion (target data). Chemically close A4Au7T2 analogues K4Au7Ge2 and Rb4Au7Sn2 both have E_hull = 0.0, supporting stability of this alkali–Au–tetrel structural family (CS5, CS1). SS and TS examples are chemically unrelated to Cs–Au–Ge and have missing similarity values, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for additional target-specific thermodynamic interpretation; neutral score 50 for those sources.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures of about 500–550 °C, consistent with Rb4Au7Sn2 and K4Au7Ge2 syntheses (CS1, CS5). The main severity penalties are Cs volatility/air sensitivity and the need for sealed ampoule containment, supported by the Cs intermetallic synthesis note that ampoules were sealed to avoid Cs evaporation losses (CS4). No high pressure or >1000 °C treatment is needed, so the route is relatively mild aside from air-free metal handling (CS1, CS4).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Elemental Au and Ge powders are not highly activated precursors, so precursor activity is only moderate-low [Reasonable Inference]. However, close analogues form at 500–550 °C despite Au/Ge density, implying alkali-assisted transport and favorable Au–tetrel framework formation (CS1, CS5). The exact K4Au7Ge2 analogue and Rb/Sn analogue strongly reduce structural-reconstruction uncertainty, although multi-day anneals indicate ordering is not instantaneous (CS1, CS5).

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

The CsN3 route inherits the 550 °C moderate-temperature window from the KN3 synthesis of K4Au7Ge2 (CS5). Its severity is higher because it requires evacuated/sealed containment, azide decomposition, gas generation, and careful air-free handling [Reasonable Inference from CS5]. CsN3 is an added safety risk relative to elemental Cs because alkali azides can be energetic [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The KN3 route produced K4Au7Ge2 directly from KN3, Ge, and Au at 550 °C, indicating a reactive alkali source can assist formation of the Au–Ge framework (CS5). Substituting CsN3 should improve alkali dispersion compared with chunks of Cs metal, but it adds gas evolution and containment constraints [Reasonable Inference from CS5]. The structural reconstruction score is high because the K analogue is the same A4Au7Ge2 composition type, but K→Cs size expansion introduces some uncertainty (CS5; target structure).

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

The flux/slow-cooling route remains below 1000 °C, consistent with 650 °C alkali–Au trielide synthesis and 700 °C alkali plumbide fusion (CS2, CS3). It is penalized for sealed Cs handling, multi-step thermal schedules, and air-sensitive Cs [Reasonable Inference from CS2–CS4]. It does not require high pressure, reactive gas, or ultra-high temperature (CS2, CS3).

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux/slow cooling should improve diffusion and crystal growth relative to dry elemental sintering [Reasonable Inference from slow-cooling/equilibration protocols in CS2 and flux use in CS3]. However, the route is less directly demonstrated for Cs–Au–Ge, and flux routes in related alkali plumbides showed phase-purity difficulties (CS3). The known K4Au7Ge2 and Rb4Au7Sn2 structures still support that the desired framework is accessible if composition is controlled (CS5, CS1).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different coordination environments: large Cs in 9–12 coordination and Ge in 3-coordinate Au environments, implying a large effective cation-size mismatch and triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The formula unit has only 13 atoms and the description reports edge-sharing cuboctahedra without simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity, so C2 and C3 are not triggered (target structure; [Reasonable Inference]). The successful crystalline analogues K4Au7Ge2 and Rb4Au7Sn2 further suggest the amorphization tendency is manageable (CS5, CS1).

Cs4Au7Ge2 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the provided E_hull is negative/on-hull and close analogues K4Au7Ge2 and Rb4Au7Sn2 are calculated stable at E_hull = 0.0 (target data; CS5, CS1). The chemical-similarity evidence strongly supports synthesis: K4Au7Ge2 has the same Ge–Au stoichiometric framework and R-3m structure family, while Rb4Au7Sn2 shows the same alkali–Au–tetrel motif in R-3m (CS5, CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated accessibility of alkali–Au–tetrel R-3m phases by moderate-temperature sealed solid-state synthesis (CS1, CS5). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Cs stoichiometry because Cs is volatile/reactive and Cs intermetallic syntheses explicitly require sealed ampoules to prevent Cs evaporation losses (CS4). SS/TS evidence is not directly useful for this chemistry, so route confidence is driven mainly by CS rather than SS/TS.
