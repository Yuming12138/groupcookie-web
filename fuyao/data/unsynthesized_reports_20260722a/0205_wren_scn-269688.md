### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported \(E_\mathrm{hull} = -0.010204434\) eV/atom places it effectively on the convex hull, so it receives a high thermodynamic score by the supplied rubric. Chemically similar Ca–P phases Ca3InP3, Ca5P3H, and Ca3PN are also reported as hull-stable with \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0.0\), supporting the broader feasibility of stable Ca–P frameworks (CS1, CS2, CS3). The negative hull value should be interpreted as numerical tolerance rather than literally “below all phases” [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS low-hull entries are chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Ca4CuP3-specific phase stability.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses direct high-temperature sealed synthesis, matching Ca3InP3 at 1000 °C under dry Ar and Ca5P3H at 1200 °C in welded Ta (CS1, CS2). Deductions come from T potentially exceeding 1000 °C, volatile P requiring a sealed system, and air-sensitive Ca/Ca–P handling, the latter supported by Ca5P3H air/moisture sensitivity (CS2). The route avoids >1400 °C, >5 GPa pressure, and reactive gases such as H2/NH3/vacuum under the scoring rubric. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ca4CuP3 has a narrow phase field against Ca phosphides and Cu phosphides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Direct elemental synthesis is chemically straightforward but kinetically limited by solid-state diffusion and complete structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Ca–P analogues form by direct reaction at 1000–1200 °C, so the route is not kinetically implausible (CS1, CS2). However, the target’s ordered Ca/Cu/P hexagonal structure has no direct structural analogue in SS/TS, and no CS entry demonstrates Cu incorporation, so reconstruction kinetics are uncertain. Dense Cu metal may further slow homogenization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 borrows the binary-phosphide strategy from Na3BP2, which can be prepared from Na3P and BP in sealed Nb/steel ampoules at 1000 K (CS4). Its severity is reduced relative to elemental P routes if the P activity is buffered by binaries [Reasonable Inference], but it becomes multi-step if Ca3P2/Cu3P must be prepared first. Air/moisture sensitivity remains important, as Ca5P3H is air/moisture sensitive and sealed Ta synthesis is used for Ca–P materials (CS2). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for binary-precursor transferability to Ca4CuP3.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary phosphides should increase precursor activity and provide pre-existing Ca–P/Cu–P bonding, reducing the reconstructive burden compared with elements [Reasonable Inference]. The idea of ternary phosphide formation from binary phosphides is directly supported by Na3BP2 from Na3P/BP (CS4). Diffusion remains a moderate issue because Cu-containing solids must homogenize with Ca-rich phosphide phases [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of a known same-structure precursor limits the structural reconstruction score.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is scored similarly in condition severity because it still requires sealed P containment and inert handling, as seen for P-rich/Ca–P analogues (CS2, CS4). Flux/fusion synthesis at sub-1000 °C is supported generally by Na6FeS4 synthesis at 925 K in H2 (CS5), although using H2 is not recommended for Ca–Cu–P without evidence [Reasonable Inference]. The multi-step nature of flux growth—loading, dwell, slow cooling, separation—is an extrapolated difficulty [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this exact route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A flux or self-flux route receives the best kinetic score because liquid-assisted transport can overcome slow solid diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The flux/fusion precedent in CS5 shows ternary alkali/transition-metal chalcogenide phases can form at lower temperatures than many direct sintering routes. For Ca4CuP3, a Cu–P or Ca–Cu–P melt could improve homogenization and crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly demonstrates a Ca–Cu–P flux window.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

Only the cation-size mismatch criterion is triggered because Ca and Cu differ substantially in size [Reasonable Inference]. The provided target description does not give enough unit-cell atom-count information to prove >40 atoms, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this target’s exact cell size. The target description lists coordination environments but not vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered due to insufficient evidence. Therefore the amorphization risk is Low–Medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca4CuP3 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported energy above hull is −0.010204434 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Ca–P phosphides that form by sealed/inert high-temperature solid-state reaction at 1000–1200 °C (CS1, CS2), while SS/TS analogues are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient target-specific synthesis evidence. The most critical enabling factor is the favorable thermodynamic stability combined with chemically reasonable Ca–P bond lengths comparable to known Ca phosphides (CS1, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry and phase control under sealed conditions because phosphorus volatility and Ca/Ca–P air sensitivity are documented concerns in related syntheses (CS2, CS4). A binary-phosphide or flux-assisted route should improve kinetics and reduce phosphorus-loss problems relative to direct elemental reaction [Reasonable Inference].
