### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has reported E_hull = −0.099 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin and justifies a high F1 score. CS gives indirect support because several related fluorides are calculated on the hull, including SrCrF6, CaCrF6, KCrF6, and RbCrF6 (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5), although SrCrF4 is 0.142 eV/atom above hull and still experimentally accessible (CS-2). SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant evidence for Na–Mo–F stability because their top entries are amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides rather than fluorides.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The high-pressure fluorination route is penalized mainly for F2 atmosphere, sealed autoclave operation, multistep handling, and toxic/corrosive reagents, all of which are directly supported by CS-1 and CS-3. The temperature itself is mild at ~300 °C and the pressure is high in engineering terms but not above the >5 GPa scoring threshold (CS-1, CS-3). The major uncertainty is whether Mo can be held at Mo(IV) instead of being over-fluorinated [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Gas-phase fluorination gives high chemical activity and can overcome diffusion barriers, as Cr fluorides form at 300 °C in CS-1 and CS-3. Structural reconstruction is only moderately favorable because the target contains NaF7 and MoF6 units with corner/edge sharing, whereas CS-1 and CS-3 have different A-site coordination and CrF6 network details. Therefore kinetics are plausible but not low-risk.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The photochemical route avoids high temperature and high pressure, as CS-4 and CS-5 operate at ambient temperature and only a few bar F2. However, it uses anhydrous HF, elemental F2, UV irradiation, FEP/PTFE vessels, and long reaction times, so the severity score remains only moderate (CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for HF/F2 route transferability.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are the best among the routes because HF/F2 photochemistry gives high precursor mobility and high fluorinating activity at ambient temperature in alkali chromium fluoride analogues (CS-4, CS-5). The main kinetic uncertainty is not diffusion but selective crystallization of NaMoF5 rather than soluble complexes, MoF5/MoF6-derived species, or amorphous fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. This route receives the best F3 score because it minimizes bulk diffusion.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The sealed binary fluoride route is less hazardous than F2/HF routes but still requires dry sealed handling and air-sensitive Mo fluoride precursors [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports moderate-temperature binary fluoride reaction at 700 °C for SrCrF4, so no high-temperature deductions apply. The uncertainty is vessel compatibility and MoF4 volatility/reactivity [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Direct fluoride reaction has reasonable precursor chemical matching, supported by SrF2 + CrF2 → SrCrF4 in CS-2. However, solid-state diffusion and reconstruction into the monoclinic NaMoF5 network may be slower than in solution or flux, especially if NaF and MoF4 remain as separate crystalline solids [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetic score is moderate.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
The flux route inherits the moderate-temperature advantage of fluoride melts and slow-cooling crystallization from CS-2. It is penalized for sealed fluoride-compatible equipment, potential volatility, multistep flux separation, and toxic/air-sensitive Mo fluoride chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient direct evidence from SS/TS for flux growth of NaMoF5.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Flux/melt transport should improve diffusion substantially relative to dry solid-state synthesis, and CS-2 directly shows single crystals can be obtained by slow cooling from a molten fluoride phase. The structural reconstruction score is moderate because there is no exact Na–Mo–F analogue; nucleation of the desired C2/c polymorph may require a narrow composition/redox window [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, this is likely second-best after HF/F2 photochemistry.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The main amorphization trigger is the large Na/Mo cation-size contrast [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s described connectivity includes corner and edge sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not fully triggered, even though some CS analogues have face-sharing motifs (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, so SS/CS/TS give insufficient direct evidence for C2, and it is treated as not triggered but uncertain.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaMoF5 is likely thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar fluorides include several on-hull examples (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The CS evidence supports synthesis of ternary fluorides under F2/HF, autoclave fluorination, and binary fluoride reaction conditions, while SS and TS provide insufficient chemically specific support and are treated as neutral. The most important enabling factor is the high fluoride chemical potential available from anhydrous HF/F2 or Ar-diluted F2, which is proven effective for alkali/alkaline-earth transition-metal fluorides (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). The main limiting factor is Mo redox/volatility control, because NaMoF5 formally requires Mo(IV) and fluorinating media can push Mo toward higher fluoride states [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but requires specialized fluorine chemistry rather than routine ceramic processing.
