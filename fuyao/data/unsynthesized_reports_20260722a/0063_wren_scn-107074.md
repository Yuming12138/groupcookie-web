### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.000233797 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull, so the assigned F1 score is 98. This is reinforced by thermodynamic analogues Na2GeF6, CuSnF6, BaGeF6, Tl2SnF6, and MgPbF6 with E_hull = 0 or near-zero values (TS-1, TS-2, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). Chemical analogues CaPdF6, BaSiF6, RbRuF6, and BaGeF6 are also stable on the hull, supporting robust fluoride phase thermodynamics (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that KGeF6 has an unusual 1:1:6 stoichiometry for K/Ge/F, whereas the directly evidenced Ge analogues are BaGeF6 and Na2GeF6-type charge-balanced compounds (CS-5, SS-2, TS-1, TS-3).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is mild in temperature and pressure because BaSiF6 and BaGeF6 are obtained by slow evaporation from acidic solutions rather than high-temperature sintering (CS-3, CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). The main severity penalties are HF toxicity and the practical multi-step sequence of dissolution, acidity adjustment, crystallization, and drying [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not indicate high pressure, high temperature, or sealed ampoules for this route, so those penalties are not applied (CS-3, CS-5, SS-4, TS-3). The uncertainty is whether a simple solution route can stabilize the exact KGeF6 stoichiometry rather than a conventional K2GeF6 phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solution chemistry should give high precursor activity because GeF6 units can form in HF medium and soluble K salts provide rapid ion mixing [Reasonable Inference]. BaGeF6 crystallization by slow evaporation indicates that the relevant cuboctahedral/GeF6 structure can assemble without high-temperature diffusion (CS-5, SS-4, TS-3). The structural reconstruction score is slightly reduced because BaGeF6 is not charge-identical to KGeF6, so oxidation/defect ordering may be required [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists on direct KGeF6 solution crystallization, so that point remains the largest kinetic uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids extreme temperature and pressure because analogous F2 fluorinations occur at 100 °C for CaPdF6, 350 °C for RbRuF6, and 400–500 °C for MgPbF6-type phases (CS-1, CS-4, TS-5). It is nevertheless severe because F2 is highly reactive/toxic and requires controlled fluorination infrastructure, which the analogues explicitly use (CS-1, CS-4, TS-5). Multi-step staged fluorination and slow cooling under F2 are supported by CaPdF6 and MgPbF6 procedures (CS-1, TS-5). SS provides insufficient evidence for F2-gas fluorination of the target and is neutral for this route-severity assessment.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Direct fluorination provides very high chemical driving force and can form complex fluorides at moderate temperatures, as shown for CaPdF6, RbRuF6, and MgPbF6 (CS-1, CS-4, TS-5). The RbRuF6 structure is especially relevant because it has an alkali cation in 12-coordinate cuboctahedra and MF6 octahedra in a trigonal framework similar to the target (CS-4). The kinetic risk is heterogeneous conversion of K/Ge precursors and possible over-fluorination/volatilization of Ge fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. The score is therefore good but lower than solution synthesis.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is supported by Na2GeF6 synthesis in sealed copper tubes under Ar at 620 °C with long heating and slow cooling (SS-2, TS-1). This route receives penalties for controlled inert atmosphere, sealed volatile-fluoride environment, multi-step ampoule preparation, and toxic/moisture-sensitive fluoride precursors such as CuF2 [Reasonable Inference]. Similar sealed-ampoule requirements appear in CuSnF6-related and Tl2SnF6/Tl2TiF6-related syntheses (SS-3, TS-2, TS-4). CS provides insufficient evidence for sealed-tube K–Ge fluoride synthesis specifically, so CS is neutral for this route-severity factor.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Na2GeF6 provides a strong GeF6 kinetic analogue with Ge–F = 1.81 Å, nearly identical to target Ge–F = 1.82 Å (SS-2, TS-1). However, the reported Na2GeF6 synthesis uses 620 °C heating, multi-day dwell, and slow cooling, and the products can contain metallic Cu, Cu–Ge intermetallic phases, and unknown fluorides, indicating difficult phase control and sluggish ordering (SS-2, TS-1). The sealed-tube route may benefit from CuF2-mediated fluorination/transport [Reasonable Inference], but it is still slower and less controllable than solution assembly. The F3 score is therefore moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains a large A-site cation polyhedron and a small GeF6 octahedron, a size/coordination contrast mirrored in BaGeF6, BaSiF6, and RbRuF6 analogues (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). The framework also contains mixed corner, edge, and face sharing between K/Ge polyhedra, which increases structural complexity and possible disorder during rapid precipitation or quenching, and this connectivity is directly present in BaGeF6/BaSiF6/RbRuF6 analogues (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-4; TS-3). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell atom count exceeds 40, so that trigger is not applied. The resulting F4 = 60 reflects medium amorphization/disorder risk, especially if crystallization is too fast.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KGeF6 is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is essentially zero, and many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar fluorides are on the hull (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; SS-2, SS-4; TS-1, TS-3, TS-4, TS-5). The strongest direct structural support comes from BaGeF6, which has the same GeF6 octahedra, similar Ge–F bond length, and the same cuboctahedral A-site framework motif (CS-5, SS-4, TS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the robustness of isolated/connected MF6 octahedral fluoride frameworks under mild solution conditions and oxidative fluorination conditions (CS-3, CS-4, CS-5; TS-5). The most critical limiting factor is the unusual KGeF6 charge balance relative to common K2GeF6/BaGeF6-type germanium(IV) fluorides, which may require high fluoride activity, oxidation control, or defect/valence stabilization [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with solution crystallization recommended first and F2 fluorination as the more aggressive backup.
