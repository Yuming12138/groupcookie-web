### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.205451 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin by the supplied rubric (Target). TS analogues include several stable or near-stable materials with E_hull = 0–0.026 eV/atom, showing that the thermodynamic-neighbor set contains experimentally accessible low-hull phases (TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that a negative hull is physically unusual and may reflect reference-state or database issues, but the score remains high under the stated scoring rule [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-pressure elemental Fe/S synthesis

The condition score is low because CS-1 required 60 kbar pressure for chemically similar FeP4, and the proposed FeS13 adaptation would also require sealed capsule handling of volatile sulfur [Reasonable Inference; CS-1]. The kinetic score is also modest because elemental Fe is diffusion-limited and the target requires full reconstruction into FeS6 ribbons and S–S clusters [Reasonable Inference from Target]. The route is credible because pressure can stabilize unusual anion-rich Fe phases, but it is experimentally demanding (CS-1).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-ampoule sulfur vapor transport / CVT

The condition score is moderate because CS-2 demonstrates FeP4 CVT in evacuated sealed ampoules with Br2 for 30 days, avoiding GPa pressures but requiring toxic volatile transport chemistry. The kinetic score is favorable because vapor transport bypasses bulk Fe diffusion and supports crystal growth, as shown by the FeP4 CVT analogue (CS-2). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity, since CS-2 reports competing FeP2 and multiple FeP4 polymorphs, implying a narrow growth window.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Sulfur-rich flux growth

The flux route has moderate condition severity because CS-3 used evacuated silica tubes, 900 K, slow cooling, and I2/Sn flux to grow FeP4. Its kinetics are fairly favorable because liquid flux improves mass transport, but target ordering into FeS6 ribbons plus discrete S–S clusters still requires substantial structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference; Target]. The route is limited by likely competing FeS2/FeS phases and by removal or avoidance of flux incorporation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Fe, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern (Target). The reported structure contains corner-sharing FeS6 octahedra and S–S units but does not report mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing coexistence, so the structural connectivity does not strongly favor amorphization (Target). Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly given, and CS/SS/TS do not provide direct useful evidence for this target-specific check, so that factor remains uncertain but not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically favorable or at least very low-energy according to the reported negative E_hull, although the negative value should be treated cautiously as a possible computational/reference artifact (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). The strongest chemical support comes from FeP4 analogues, where Fe-centered octahedra coexist with covalently bonded pnictogen networks and can be synthesized by high pressure, CVT, or flux growth (CS-1/CS-2/CS-3). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high sulfur chemical potential so that sulfur-rich S–S clusters and FeS6 ribbons can form rather than Fe-rich sulfides [Reasonable Inference from Target]. The most critical limiting factor is phase competition and stoichiometry control, because even FeP4 analogues show multiple polymorphs and competing Fe–P phases under CVT/flux conditions (CS-2/CS-3). Overall, the best path is sealed-ampoule sulfur CVT because it balances high sulfur activity, improved transport, and feasible laboratory equipment (CS-2/CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]).
