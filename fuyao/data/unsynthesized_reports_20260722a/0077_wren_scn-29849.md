### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.024070159 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. TS analogues are experimentally reported near-hull materials with E_hull values from 0.0 to 0.026 eV/atom, which supports synthesizability of thermodynamically stable or nearly stable phases (TS-1 to TS-5). CS includes ScF₃ examples with E_hull ≈0–0.002 eV/atom and reported high-confidence synthesis (CS-2 to CS-5). The main uncertainty is not the computed hull but the unusual CaF₄ formal stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because CS-1 uses anhydrous HF, F₂ gas, fluoropolymer reactors, Kel-F valves, and a vacuum line. The actual temperature is mild, since CS-1 reports room-temperature fluorination for 72 h, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The score is reduced mainly for reactive/toxic gases, volatile corrosive HF, and multistep handling. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for HF/F₂ chemistry and are treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, the HF/F₂ route benefits from very reactive fluorination chemistry, as demonstrated by CS-1. However, converting a CaF₂-like precursor into a 2D CaF₄ sheet is a major structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore chemically enabling but kinetically uncertain. The absence of a direct CaF₄ analogue in CS/SS/TS is the dominant uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has mild temperatures because CS-2/CS-5 used 150–200 °C solvothermal conditions. Deductions arise from sealed autoclave operation, multiple workup steps, and toxic fluoride reagents such as NH₄F, which is directly used in CS-2/CS-5. No external reactive atmosphere or extreme pressure is reported in the CS solvothermal examples. SS and TS provide insufficient fluoride-solvothermal signal and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

The nitrate/NH₄F solvothermal route has excellent precursor activity and low diffusion barriers, consistent with ScF₃ synthesis from soluble precursors in CS-2/CS-5. However, the lack of oxidizing F₂/HF chemistry makes it more likely to yield CaF₂ than CaF₄ [Reasonable Inference]. Thus its kinetic score is good for crystallizing a calcium fluoride phase, but its phase-selective transferability to CaF₄ is weak. The principal uncertainty is whether any solvothermal fluoride complex can template the reported 2D sheet [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 inherits the harshness of CS-4, where fluorides are synthesized at 550–600 °C for days under a fluorine stream or in sealed gold ampoules. The temperature is below 1000 °C, so high-temperature penalties are absent. The penalties come from F₂ atmosphere, sealed-system volatility/containment, multistep preparation, and toxic/reactive precursors. CS-3 gives only generic solid-state synthesis and insufficient condition detail, so it contributes little beyond confirming solid-state fluoride preparation is possible.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state fluorination has lower precursor activity than solution methods because CaF₂/CaO powders require diffusion through solid fluoride layers [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4’s 2–10 day heating windows support a slower kinetic picture. Structural reconstruction to the 2D CaF₄ target remains difficult, so the structural score is low. The route may improve fluorine chemical potential but not necessarily overcome the preference for CaF₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-size mismatch is not a concern [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description is simple—Ca in an eight-coordinate environment and F bridging two Ca—without mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing complexity. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct amorphization data for CaF₄, so their signal is insufficient and neutral. The small binary composition and simple bonding topology suggest low amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or at least near-ground-state because its reported E_hull is negative and lies well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom favorable window. The CS evidence supports general fluoride synthesis, especially aggressive HF/F₂ fluorination (CS-1), solvothermal fluoride crystallization (CS-2/CS-5), and high-temperature F₂ fluorination (CS-4), but none directly validates CaF₄. SS and TS provide weak route-specific support because their nearest examples are chemically unrelated, although TS shows that near-hull materials can be synthesized. The most critical enabling factor is access to very high fluorine chemical potential, best provided by HF/F₂ or F₂-stream chemistry. The most critical limiting factor is the unusual CaF₄ stoichiometry, which may compete strongly with ordinary CaF₂ formation [Reasonable Inference].
