### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.018961495 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and justifying F1 = 98 (Target). CS analogues are also low-energy intermetallics, with E_hull values around 0.021–0.060 eV/atom, supporting the idea that nearby rare-earth gallides can be thermodynamically accessible (CS). TS has low-hull examples, but they are chemically unrelated to Y–Ga–Os, so TS gives insufficient useful target-specific evidence and is treated as neutral 50. Remaining uncertainty is that DFT hull stability does not guarantee absence of low-temperature kinetic trapping or off-stoichiometric competing gallides (CS; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses arc/HF melting plus annealing, matching the most direct CS evidence for rare-earth gallides synthesized from elements and annealed at 600 °C (CS). The severity score is reduced because Os is refractory and likely requires very high local melting/dissolution temperatures, and because Y/Os handling requires air/toxicity precautions ([Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient useful condition evidence for this metallic target because their top examples involve unrelated hydrothermal, precipitation, decomposition, ammonolysis, or boride-growth routes; neutral 50 for SS/TS. The route is not penalized for high pressure or reactive gas because CS does not indicate those requirements (CS).

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

The elemental precursors are relatively inactive compared with salts or sol-gel precursors, and CS confirms elemental melt synthesis rather than chemically activated precursors (CS). Os makes homogenization difficult due to refractory character, while arc melting partly mitigates this by forming a high-temperature melt ([Reasonable Inference]). The target’s cubic Pm-3m structure differs from the orthorhombic Immm CS analogues, so ordering into the exact target structure is uncertain rather than directly template-supported (CS; Target).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The Ga-flux route is not explicitly reported in CS but is a reasonable adaptation of CS melt-growth/arc-HF gallide synthesis for a Ga-rich target ([Reasonable Inference]; CS). It avoids the most extreme need to fully melt Os but still likely needs >1000 °C and sealed inert/vacuum handling ([Reasonable Inference]). The route is penalized for controlled atmosphere, multiple operational steps, and air/toxicity-sensitive precursors ([Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS provide insufficient useful support for this route and are neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Ga-rich flux should improve transport and precursor activity relative to direct solid diffusion, which is consistent with the melt-based nature of the CS gallide syntheses but remains an extrapolation (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). Os dissolution remains the main kinetic bottleneck, so the diffusion score is moderate rather than high ([Reasonable Inference]). Structural assembly from a melt is plausible for a small-cell intermetallic, but CS does not provide a same-structure YGa₃T analogue, so crystallization pathway uncertainty remains (CS; Target).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small Pm-3m cell, so the >40-atom amorphization check is not triggered (Target). However, the target contains significantly size-mismatched metal species and mixed corner/edge/face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, triggering two of three checks (Target; [Reasonable Inference]). This gives a medium amorphization/disorder risk and F4 = 60. SS does not provide useful target-specific amorphization evidence because the structurally similar examples are chemically unrelated; neutral 50 for SS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YGa₃Os is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported hull energy is slightly negative, −0.018961495 eV/atom (Target). The chemical-similarity evidence supports synthesis most strongly: related rare-earth gallides are made from elements by arc/HF melting followed by 600 °C heat treatment (CS). The structural and thermodynamic-neighbor lists are much less useful for practical synthesis because their top entries are chemically unrelated to Y–Ga–Os, so SS/TS are treated as neutral evidence. The most important enabling factor is Ga-rich intermetallic melt chemistry, which should allow either direct arc-melting/anneal or Ga-flux crystallization (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). The main limiting factor is Os incorporation and homogenization, because Os is refractory and no direct Os-containing CS analogue is supplied ([Reasonable Inference]). Overall, the target appears moderately synthesizable, with the Ga-flux route slightly preferred for improved kinetics.
