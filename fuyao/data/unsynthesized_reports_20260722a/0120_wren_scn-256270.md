### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.020496384 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifying a high stability score. CS contains several chemically related alkali selenides with E_hull = 0, including K₂Ni₃Se₄, Rb₂Ni₃Se₄, K₂Pd₃Se₄, and K₂MnSe₂, which supports the plausibility of stable alkali–selenide phases (CS). SS and TS entries include stable phases but are chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient direct signal for K–Ga–Se stability and are treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 receives deductions for controlled atmosphere, Se volatility, and toxicity/air sensitivity. CS directly shows related alkali selenides require Ar or H₂ at 800–850 °C and sometimes use excess chalcogen, supporting both the moderate temperature and the need for atmosphere/stoichiometry control (CS). The main uncertainty is whether carbonate chemistry is compatible with Ga without forming oxide or carbonate side products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are moderate because carbonate + Ga + Se requires significant chemical reconstruction, although CS demonstrates that analogous alkali–metal selenides can form at 800–850 °C (CS). Low-melting Ga and mobile K-containing intermediates should help diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s Ga–Ga bond and monoclinic ordered tetrahedral network are not directly represented in CS, SS, or TS, so structural reconstruction remains the key uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 has similar severity to Route 1 because it still requires sealed or inert-atmosphere handling and volatile/toxic Se-bearing reagents [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports the need for controlled atmospheres in alkali selenides (CS). The route avoids carbonate chemistry, but K₂Se/K₂Seₓ introduces stronger air/moisture sensitivity [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Binary selenide precursors improve precursor activity because they already contain K–Se and Ga–Se bonding motifs [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports alkali selenide formation at 800–850 °C, indicating that the necessary temperature range is experimentally reasonable (CS). However, the exact target topology still requires ordering into KSe₆ and GaGaSe₃ units, so kinetics are not rated as easy.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has the harshest operational severity because K–Se flux is reactive, sealed containment is likely required, and workup/decanting adds steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports use of excess alkali/chalcogen in related flux/fusion syntheses and controlled Ar atmospheres at 800–850 °C (CS). SS and TS do not provide useful chemical evidence for this flux system, so uncertainty remains in flux compatibility.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because liquid-mediated transport should reduce diffusion limitations and allow slow ordering/crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports high-temperature alkali/chalcogen excess routes at 800–850 °C, which is consistent with a molten or partially molten reactive medium (CS). The major uncertainty is whether K₂Ga₂Se₃ has a sufficiently wide crystallization window or competes with other K–Ga–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization trigger: large cation size/coordination mismatch between K in KSe₆ octahedra and Ga in GaGaSe₃ tetrahedra [Reasonable Inference based on target structure]. The provided target description does not establish a >40 atom unit cell, and it reports corner/edge sharing but not face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered. SS and TS provide insufficient K–Ga–Se-specific evidence for amorphization tendency and are treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₂Ga₂Se₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is slightly negative, well within the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability window. The strongest support comes from CS: multiple alkali selenides are experimentally synthesized at 800–850 °C under Ar or H₂, often using excess alkali/chalcogen components. SS and TS do not strongly support the chemistry because their analogues are chemically unrelated, so they mainly provide neutral thermodynamic/process context. The most critical enabling factor is the high thermodynamic stability combined with likely high mobility in K–Se/Ga–Se flux or liquid-assisted environments. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control under volatile, air-sensitive K–Se–Se conditions, especially while forming the ordered Ga–Ga-containing monoclinic phase.
