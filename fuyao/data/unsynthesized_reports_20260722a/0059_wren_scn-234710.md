### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.013474 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and giving a high thermodynamic score. Several chemically similar chlorides in CS are also on or near the hull, including E_hull = 0.0 for NaGaCl4, SrZnCl4, Zr6CoCl15, and LuNb6Cl18 (CS2–CS5). The negative hull value should be interpreted as numerical tolerance rather than physically negative decomposition energy [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS entries are mostly chemically unrelated hydrides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient direct chemical thermodynamic signal for LiRuCl6 and are neutral, 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule solid-state chloride reaction

The severity is moderate-to-high because sealed evacuated/inert tubes are required, as in the R-3 chloride synthesis of LuNb6Cl18 and the phase-pure SrZnCl4/BaZnCl4 chloride syntheses (CS5, CS3). Temperatures below 1000 °C avoid the largest thermal penalty, but air-sensitive/volatile chloride reagents and multi-step ampoule handling impose deductions (CS3, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically, chloride precursors are favorable compared with oxides, but forming an ordered Li/Ru octahedral network and controlling Ru(V) remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The structural analogy to CS5 is useful but not exact because CS5 contains Nb chloride cluster/square-pyramidal units rather than only alternating LiCl6/RuCl6 octahedra.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Molten chloride flux growth

The condition severity is penalized mainly for sealed ampoule, vacuum/Ar handling, volatile Lewis-acid chlorides, and air-sensitive products, all supported by CS2. The route avoids high temperature and external high pressure, which helps F2 (CS2). Kinetically, this is the best route because molten GaCl3/AlCl3 flux enables ion mobility and crystal growth at low temperature in chloride systems (CS2). The uncertainty is that CS2 does not involve Ru or the LiRuCl6 structure, so oxidation-state and phase-selectivity transfer are extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: High-temperature sealed metal-container gradient reaction

CS1 and CS4 demonstrate that sealed Ta-tube chloride reactions at 750–900 °C over weeks can produce complex metal chlorides, including reactions involving LiCl. This route is severe because it uses sealed metal containers, long heat treatments, volatile chlorides, and air-sensitive handling (CS1, CS4). Kinetically, temperature gradients can aid transport and crystal growth, but elemental Ru or mixed Ru/RuClx precursors would likely react slowly and may create redox mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. Structural transfer is weaker because CS1/CS4 are Zr6 cluster chloride frameworks, not the target’s alternating LiCl6/RuCl6 octahedral framework.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target’s reported connectivity is simple corner-sharing LiCl6/RuCl6 octahedra with no edge or face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low from the target description. The likely >15% cation radius mismatch between octahedral Li+ and high-valent Ru triggers one amorphization/ordering-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is not indicated to contain >40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered. SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific amorphization evidence and are neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiRuCl6 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is −0.013474 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull within numerical tolerance [Reasonable Inference]. The best support comes from CS5, which demonstrates an R-3 chloride phase made in sealed silica at 750–800 °C, and from CS2, which demonstrates chloride flux growth under rigorously anhydrous sealed conditions. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and high chloride chemical potential available in molten Lewis-acid chloride fluxes, which should help form an ordered Li/Ru chloride network [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is oxidation-state control: LiRuCl6 requires high-valent Ru(V), and none of CS/SS/TS directly proves stabilization of Ru(V) in this exact composition. Overall, synthesis appears plausible but will likely require careful sealed-tube/flux handling and redox control.
