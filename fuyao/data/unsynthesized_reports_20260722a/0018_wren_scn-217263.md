### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.020682839 eV/atom, placing it effectively on the convex hull and therefore in the highest thermodynamic stability bin [Reasonable Inference from target data]. CS supports this favorable assessment because the chemically/structurally relevant CaWO₄-like analogue has E_hull ≈ 0.0023 eV/atom and the Re oxide La₃Re₂O₁₀ has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS contain mostly unrelated materials and provide insufficient evidence for Sr–Re–O stability, so they are neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 inherits demanding sealed high-temperature oxide conditions from Tc oxide analogues, where reactions are performed in sealed Pt capsules at 1030–1250 °C with preheating and quenching (CS-1–CS-3). The target route likely requires controlled atmosphere and volatile Re oxide retention [Reasonable Inference], analogous to the sealed systems used to prevent Tc oxide loss (CS-1–CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient directly relevant evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solid-state diffusion is the main limitation because the route starts from oxide/carbonate powders and requires reconstructing into a zircon-like ABO₄ phase [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1–CS-3 show multiday high-temperature treatments for related sealed oxide syntheses, supporting slow kinetics. CS-4 supports the structural plausibility of the final ABO₄ framework but does not remove the dry solid-state diffusion barrier. SS and TS are insufficient for Sr–Re–O kinetics and are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 avoids >1000 °C heating because the Re oxide hydrothermal analogue was synthesized at 700 °C (CS-5). It does require kbar hydrothermal equipment and sealed Au/Pt capsules, but the formal scoring table only penalizes pressure above 5 GPa, which does not apply to the 3 kbar CS-5 condition. Re oxide containment and capsule handling remain important severity factors [Reasonable Inference; CS-5]. SS-1 uses high-pressure ammonia chemistry, but that is chemically unrelated to oxide hydrothermal synthesis, so SS is insufficient and neutral; TS is also insufficient.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Hydrothermal transport should accelerate reaction kinetics relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 directly supports this because La₃Re₂O₁₀ formed from ReO₃ and La₂O₃ in water at 700 °C and 3 kbar after only 8 h. The remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS-5 reports both dark violet crystals and pale green powder, indicating possible multiphase products.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 is based on slow-cooling growth near 1150 °C, as in the CaWO₄-type analogue prepared from a melt at 1423 K in air (CS-4). For SrReO₄, the atmosphere likely must be sealed or oxygen-controlled because Re oxides may volatilize or change oxidation state [Reasonable Inference], unlike the simpler Ca–W–O air route. Sealed volatile-oxide handling is supported by CS-1–CS-3. SS and TS give insufficient direct evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux or melt conditions should substantially improve mass transport, giving better kinetics than dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports slow-cooling crystallization of an ABO₄-type oxide with similar 8-coordinate alkaline-earth/tetrahedral high-valent-cation geometry. The uncertainty is whether a Re-containing melt/flux can preserve Re(VI) and avoid volatilization or competing perrhenate/rhenate phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ABO₄-type framework with one Sr site, one tetrahedral Re site, and one O site, which argues against high amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The only triggered risk is the large cation size/coordination mismatch between 8-coordinate Sr and tetrahedral Re [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 shows that analogous alkaline-earth/tetrahedral transition-metal oxide frameworks can crystallize, reducing concern. SS and TS give insufficient directly relevant amorphization evidence and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrReO₄ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is slightly negative, effectively on-hull within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference from target data]. The strongest supporting analogue is CaWO₄, which shares the key ABO₄ motif with 8-coordinate alkaline-earth cation and tetrahedral high-valent transition-metal cation (CS-4). The strongest chemical synthesis precedent is the hydrothermal La–Re oxide route using ReO₃ in a sealed gold tube at 700 °C and 3 kbar (CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is use of a sealed hydrothermal or flux environment that improves transport while retaining volatile Re species [Reasonable Inference; CS-1–CS-5]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Re oxidation state and volatility, because sealed systems were required for volatile Tc oxide analogues and Re oxide hydrothermal synthesis produced multiphase material (CS-1–CS-3, CS-5).
