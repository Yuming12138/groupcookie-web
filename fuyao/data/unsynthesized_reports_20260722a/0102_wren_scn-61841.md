### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.005875884 eV/atom, so it falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and receives a high score. Thermodynamic analogues include Cs₂SnF₆ and Tl₂SnF₆ at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2), while EuSnF₇ and YSnF₇ are also near the hull at about 0.008 eV/atom (SS1, SS3). Chemically similar fluorides RbRuF₆, BaGeF₆, BaSiF₆, and CaPdF₆ are also stable at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS4). The remaining uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee the proposed P4₂/mcm KSnF₆ phase is the experimentally preferred K–Sn–F phase under accessible chemical potentials [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 direct oxidative fluorination

Route 1 is penalized because F₂ is explicitly required in close chemical analogues RbRuF₆ and CaPdF₆ and is highly reactive/toxic (CS1, CS4). The temperature is not severe because CS1 reports 350 °C/2 h and CS4 reports fluorination near 100 °C with further tempering. No high pressure is indicated in CS1/CS4. Uncertainty remains because Sn-specific direct F₂ synthesis of KSnF₆ is not directly shown in SS/TS, so oxidizing conditions may require optimization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 direct oxidative fluorination

The kinetics are favorable because gas-phase F₂ provides high fluoride chemical potential and can fluorinate mixed halide/oxide precursors at relatively low temperatures in CS1 and CS4. SnF₆ octahedra are structurally well supported by Cs₂SnF₆, Tl₂SnF₆, EuSnF₇, and YSnF₇ analogues (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2, SS1, SS3). However, the exact KSnF₆ arrangement is not directly represented among the nearest Sn analogues, so structural reconstruction and phase selection remain moderate risks [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures supported by Cs₂SnF₆ at 873 K/12 h and Tl₂SnF₆ at 300 °C/4 d (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). It is penalized for sealed-tube use with volatile/moisture-sensitive fluoride reagents, because SnF₄-containing reactions are performed in gold or Pt ampoules under Ar (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). Multi-step dry handling is supported by the dry box/vacuum procedures for Tl₂SnF₆ (SS4/TS2). Insufficient evidence from CS suggests this exact route for KSnF₆, so CS is neutral for this route’s detailed condition severity.

#### F3 — Route 2 sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetics are moderate because the route uses crystalline binary fluorides rather than highly active solution or nitrate precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The SnF₆ motif is strongly supported by Cs₂SnF₆ and Tl₂SnF₆ analogues (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). Phase purity is the main kinetic/thermodynamic coupling issue because SS2/TS1 reports that pure Cs₂SnF₆-related samples were not fully realized, and TS3 reports impurities in sealed-tube Ge fluoride synthesis. The target’s 1:1 K:Sn ratio is not directly proven by SS/TS analogues, increasing uncertainty [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 HF/aqueous crystallization

The route is mild because BaGeF₆ and BaSiF₆ analogues crystallize by slow evaporation from dilute acid fluoride solutions, with BaSiF₆ involving HF and drying at 150 °C (CS2, CS3). It is penalized for HF toxicity and multi-step solution processing. There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS that Sn-containing KSnF₆ forms by this route, so SS/TS are neutral for route-specific condition severity. The route is experimentally attractive but chemically less proven for this target.

#### F3 — Route 3 HF/aqueous crystallization

Precursor activity and diffusion are favorable because solution crystallization avoids bulk diffusion and has produced crystalline hexafluorides in CS2/CS3. The major deduction is structural/chemical uncertainty: the Sn-containing analogues in SS/TS are solid-state or sealed-tube SnF₄ routes, not HF evaporation routes (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). There is also a plausible competing tendency toward known A₂SnF₆-type chemistry rather than KSnF₆ [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, despite mild kinetics, the pathway has uncertain selectivity.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination contrast between 8-coordinate K and octahedral Sn, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference], and analogous alkali/tin fluorides show large A-site coordination versus SnF₆ octahedra (SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). The unit-cell-size trigger is not applied because the target formula is small and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence that this exact phase has >40 atoms per cell. The mixed-connectivity trigger is also not applied because the target description does not explicitly show simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing; some analogues have mixed sharing, but that cannot be transferred with confidence to KSnF₆ (SS2/TS1). Overall amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KSnF₆ is likely thermodynamically accessible, because its reported E_hull is effectively zero and many chemically/structurally/thermodynamically related fluorides are on or very near the hull (CS1–CS4, SS2/TS1, SS4/TS2). The analogues support the robustness of SnF₆ octahedra especially well, with Sn–F distances near 1.97–1.99 Å in Cs₂SnF₆, EuSnF₇, YSnF₇, and Tl₂SnF₆-related compounds (SS1–SS4/TS1–TS2). The most critical enabling factor is high fluoride chemical potential, obtainable either by HF solution chemistry as in BaGeF₆/BaSiF₆ (CS2, CS3) or by F₂/sealed-fluoride methods as in RbRuF₆/CaPdF₆/Cs₂SnF₆ (CS1, CS4, SS2/TS1). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity, because the direct Sn analogues are mostly A₂SnF₆ or LnSnF₇ rather than KSnF₆, and one close Cs₂SnF₆ synthesis reports incomplete phase purity (SS2/TS1). Overall, the target is plausible but should be approached as a phase-selectivity-sensitive fluoride rather than a routine binary-fluoride product.
