### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.070990762 eV/atom, so it is scored near the top of the ≤ 0.05 eV/atom bin using the required rubric. Several chemically similar synthesized chloride phases are on or near the hull, including BaZnCl4 at E_hull = 0.0 and BaPdCl4 at 0.019 eV/atom (CS-2, CS-4, CS-5), supporting the accessibility of near-hull halides. SS and TS contain thermodynamically stable synthesized materials but are chemically unrelated to K–Os–Cl, so they provide insufficient direct signal and are treated neutral = 50. The remaining uncertainty is whether the negative hull reflects a robust experimental ground state or DFT/correction artifacts for a high-valent osmium chloride [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures around 450–550 °C, consistent with phase-pure BaZnCl4 synthesis at 550 °C in evacuated silica ampoules (CS-4). The main severity penalties are vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, volatile Os chloride, and toxic/moisture-sensitive Os precursor handling [Reasonable Inference], while no >1000 °C or high-pressure step is required. SS and TS give insufficient condition-specific evidence for K–Os–Cl, so they are neutral = 50. The score is moderate because the thermal profile is simple, but Os redox/volatility makes the ampoule chemistry nontrivial.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Binary chloride precursors are kinetically favorable compared with refractory oxides/metals, as shown by BaZnCl4, BaPdCl4, and CsTi2Cl7 formation from chloride precursors (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). However, the target requires assembling a mixed KCl12/OsCl6 network from separate chlorides, and Os is a refractory cation under the formal melting-point metric [Reasonable Inference]. CsTi2Cl7 required melt/quench/anneal and about one week to obtain X-ray-pure samples, showing that ternary chloride ordering can be slow or condition-sensitive (CS-3). Thus Route 1 receives a moderate F3 rather than a high F3.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is based directly on chloride melt/Bridgman syntheses: BaPdCl4 at 770 °C for 6 h with 1 °C/h cooling and BaZnCl4 at 480–600 °C for 10 h with slow cooling (CS-2, CS-5). It avoids extreme pressure and >1000 °C temperatures but requires evacuated sealed ampoules, slow cooling, and handling of volatile/toxic Os chloride [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give insufficient route-specific evidence for chloride melt growth and are neutral = 50. The extra procedural control gives a lower F2 than Route 1.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Melt growth improves ion mobility and reduces diffusion limitations, as evidenced by BaPdCl4 and BaZnCl4 crystallizing from molten chloride mixtures (CS-2, CS-5). Direct crystallization from a chloride melt should help assemble K–Cl and OsCl6 motifs without requiring long-range solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is maintaining Os(V) and the exact K:Os:Cl stoichiometry during a high-temperature melt, because this issue is not addressed by the Ba/Pd/Zn analogues (CS-2, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Therefore F3 is moderately high but not excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits sealed/vacuum and slow-cooling requirements from alkali flux growth and chloride melt analogues (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The temperature is moderate, but the process has multiple steps: flux loading, heating, slow cooling, and flux separation, analogous to CS-1. Toxic/volatile Os chloride and possible air/moisture sensitivity add penalties [Reasonable Inference], and air/moisture sensitivity is explicitly observed in the CsTi2Cl7 chloride analogue (CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral = 50.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux growth offers the best kinetic pathway because excess molten alkali chloride can dissolve and transport Os chloride species, analogous to alkali flux transport in CS-1 and chloride melt crystallization in CS-2/CS-5. The method should reduce reconstructive barriers and favor ordered crystallization of the KCl12/OsCl6 framework [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that the exact solubility and redox stability of OsCl5 in KCl-rich flux are not established by CS/SS/TS, so the F3 is capped below 75. This route is nevertheless the best balance of transport and moderate temperature.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has strongly size-mismatched cations in KCl12 and OsCl6 environments, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch check [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The target also has corner, edge, and face-sharing polyhedral connectivity, directly triggering mixed-connectivity risk from the target structure. Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and CS/SS/TS do not provide useful signal for that check, so it is not triggered. The resulting F4 = 55 reflects medium risk of kinetic trapping or disorder, especially if quenched too quickly.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KOsCl6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative, −0.070990762 eV/atom, in the provided target data. The strongest support comes from chemically similar ternary chloride syntheses in sealed ampoules and melts, especially BaZnCl4, BaPdCl4, and CsTi2Cl7 (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5), while SS and TS are largely chemically unrelated and provide insufficient route-specific signal. The most critical enabling factor is the use of mobile chloride media—either a chloride melt or KCl-rich flux—to overcome Os diffusion limitations and crystallize OsCl6 octahedra [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Os(V) chloride chemistry, because Os chlorides are likely volatile, moisture-sensitive, and redox-sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but should be approached as sealed-tube halide chemistry rather than conventional open solid-state synthesis.
