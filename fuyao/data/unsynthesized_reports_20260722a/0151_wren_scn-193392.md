### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.00445 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable within numerical tolerance. TS strongly supports this because the closest thermodynamic analogues Pt₈V, Pt₃Pd, MnPt₃, and CaPt₅ are on the hull, and Pt₃V is only 0.0111 eV/atom above hull (TS-1 to TS-5). CS also supports low-energy Pt-rich V/Pt chemistry through Pt₃V and Pt₈V (CS-1, CS-5), while SS supports structural compatibility but gives insufficient direct hull evidence beyond analogue stability, so SS is less decisive.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting is a severe high-temperature method, and Pt–V analogues were prepared by arc melting and annealing under Ar/vacuum-like conditions (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). The main deductions are high local melting temperature, controlled atmosphere, and multi-step processing. No high pressure or volatile precursor is indicated by CS/SS/TS, so those penalties are not applied.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are moderate because elemental Pt/V/Ni metals are dense and refractory [Reasonable Inference], but arc melting provides rapid homogenization, as demonstrated for Pt₈V and Pt₃V (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target and Pt₃V/Pt₈V share cuboctahedral Pt-rich Uranium Silicide-derived motifs (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). The uncertainty is ternary V/Ni ordering, since Pt₈V showed incomplete transformation and metastable/disordered behavior (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Sealed-tube annealing avoids arc-melting temperatures if starting from powders or pre-alloyed material, so its nominal condition severity is lower. However, it still requires vacuum ampoules and repeated grinding/annealing, as shown by Pt₃V and Pt-rich P4/mmm ampoule syntheses (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4; SS-2; TS-3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for volatile V/Ni/Pt precursor loss, so no volatility penalty is applied.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The kinetic score is low because solid-state diffusion among Pt-rich refractory metals is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. Pt-rich P4/mmm ampoule analogues required long annealing and repeated grinding to reduce impurities (CS-3, CS-4), supporting a significant kinetic limitation. Structural similarity helps, but the lack of a liquid alloying stage makes this route less reliable than arc melting.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Mechanical working inherits the severe arc-melting and controlled-atmosphere requirements of Pt₈V processing (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). It also adds rolling and repeated annealing, so it remains multi-step and labor-intensive. No high pressure above 5 GPa is involved, because cold rolling is mechanical deformation rather than high-pressure synthesis (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Cold rolling can improve kinetics by introducing defects and shortening diffusion paths [Reasonable Inference], and this strategy was explicitly used for Pt₈V (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). The target is extremely close structurally to Pt₈V (SS-1), which supports ordering into a related Pt-rich framework. The limitation is that the Pt₈V analogue showed metastable cubic/disordered phases and incomplete ordering, so the target may also require careful anneal optimization (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Only one amorphization check is clearly triggered: mixed corner/edge/face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, which appears in the target description and in Pt-rich analogues (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). The compact P4/mmm/I4/mmm intermetallic analogues suggest a small ordered cell rather than a >40-atom complex cell (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). CS/SS/TS provide insufficient quantitative radius data, but metallic V/Ni/Pt size mismatch is not expected to exceed the 15% threshold [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

VNiPt₆ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state ordered intermetallic because its calculated E_hull is slightly negative within numerical precision and related Pt-rich analogues are hull-stable or nearly hull-stable (TS-1 to TS-5). The synthesis evidence is strong for the Pt–V/Pt-rich structural family: Pt₃V and Pt₈V are chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically close and were made by arc melting/annealing routes (CS-1, CS-5; SS-1, SS-2; TS-1, TS-3). The most important enabling factor is the robust Pt-rich cuboctahedral/Uranium Silicide-derived framework, which tolerates multiple minority metals in related analogues such as MnPt₃ and PdPt₃ (CS-2; SS-3, SS-5; TS-2, TS-4). The most important limiting factor is slow ordering and possible competition with disordered or binary Pt–V/Pt–Ni phases, especially because Pt₈V reports incomplete transformation and metastability issues (CS-5; SS-1; TS-1). Overall, the target is quite plausible but will likely require metallurgical synthesis plus annealing rather than a simple low-temperature solid-state route.
