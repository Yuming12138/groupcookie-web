### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.1160 eV/atom, so it falls in the most favorable stability bin and receives 98/100 based on the required rubric and target data. Chemically similar fluorides include several stable or nearly stable phases, including BaF₃, BaNiF₆, and EuF₃ at E_hull = 0.0 and SrF₃ near hull at 0.049 eV/atom (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). The negative hull value should not be overinterpreted as “more than stable,” because negative hulls usually indicate reference/calculation inconsistency [Reasonable Inference]. TS analogues are thermodynamically near-hull but chemically unrelated, so they provide only neutral support.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because CS-1 and CS-2 require specialized high-pressure equipment at 3 GPa, even though this is below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty. HF/F₂ handling imposes reactive-medium, sealed-system, and toxicity penalties by [Reasonable Inference] from HF use in CS-1/CS-2 and F₂/aHF use in CS-4. The moderate temperature of 573 K / 300 °C avoids high-temperature deductions (CS-1, CS-2). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for fluorochemical severity and are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

CS-1 and CS-2 show that fluoride–HF phases can crystallize rapidly, within 30 min at 573 K under pressure, supporting high mass transport in HF media. The target’s F–F bonded substructure and five inequivalent F sites imply more structural reorganization than simple CsF crystallization [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. Therefore kinetics are favorable for transport but only moderate for structural assembly. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for Cs–F/F–F reconstruction kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high temperature and GPa pressure because CS-4 used room-temperature aHF/F₂ chemistry. However, CS-4 explicitly requires FEP reactor/Kel-F valve/vacuum-line handling, and the use of aHF/F₂ triggers reactive atmosphere, sealed volatile reagent, multi-step handling, and toxic-reagent penalties. The route is operationally safer than GPa HF synthesis only in pressure/temperature terms, not in chemical hazard terms [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this fluorochemical route.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

CS-4 supports room-temperature fluorination in anhydrous HF over 72 h, so precursor activation and diffusion are scored high. Solution-mediated fluorination should reduce solid-state diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic uncertainty is whether CsF₅ nucleates as the ordered Immm phase rather than a disordered polyfluoride or CsF/F₂ adduct [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS-4]. SS/TS are chemically unrelated and neutral for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses the high-pF₂ strategy from CS-3, which involved pF₂ >3500 atm and 450–600 °C for 10 days. This pressure is severe experimentally but below the rubric’s >5 GPa cutoff, so no high-pressure deduction is applied under the specified rules. Reactive F₂ atmosphere, sealed volatile gas, and toxicity are penalized based on CS-3 and [Reasonable Inference]. The long duration is captured mainly in kinetics rather than F2 because the rubric does not directly penalize time.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

CS-3 indicates high-pressure fluorination can form unusual fluorides but also reports difficult phase control, instability, and long reaction times. Gas–solid fluorination of CsF should provide high fluorine chemical potential but may suffer from surface passivation or slow fluorine insertion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction into CsF₅ with F–F motifs is likely more difficult than forming a simple ionic fluoride [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for gas–solid Cs–F kinetics.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Cs, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described target structure has edge and corner sharing but no stated face sharing, so the mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not triggered (target structure). The formula is small, and no evidence indicates a >40-atom unit cell [Reasonable Inference from CsF₅ formula and Immm symmetry]. SS/TS structural analogues do not provide useful target-specific amorphization evidence and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsF₅ is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable by the reported E_hull of −0.1160 eV/atom, although the negative value should be treated as a strong “near/at hull” signal rather than literal extra stability [Reasonable Inference from target data]. The CS set supports fluoride synthesis under HF, aHF/F₂, and high-pF₂ conditions, but none of the references directly demonstrates CsF₅, so transferability is credible but not proven (CS-1–CS-4). The most important enabling factor is access to very high fluorine chemical potential through aHF/F₂ or high-pF₂ chemistry, as shown by CS-3 and CS-4. The most important limiting factor is phase control: the target requires ordered F–F-containing motifs, while CS-3 reports difficult phase control for unusual high-fluorine materials. SS/TS analogues provide insufficient chemical guidance because they are mostly non-fluoride or chemically unrelated phases, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory.
