### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.007392158 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable thermodynamic scoring bin and suggests a ground-state or numerically stable phase by the supplied criterion. Thermodynamic analogues CaZn5, SrZn5, CaZn11, EuZn5, and Nd3Zn11 are reported at E_hull = 0, supporting the broader stability of zinc-rich intermetallic frameworks (CS1, CS2, CS5, TS1–TS5). The remaining uncertainty is polymorphism: the known CaZn11 analogue in CS5/TS4 is monoclinic C2/m, while the target is orthorhombic Immm, so stability of the exact polymorph still requires experimental validation.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids high pressure and extreme temperature because Ca/Sr/Ba–Zn melt syntheses use ~800–850 °C sealed Ta ampoules (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). Deductions are mainly due to Zn volatility requiring sealing, multistep thermal processing, and Ca air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference from sealed-ampoule protocols in CS1/CS2 and Ca precursor use in CS5]. This route is therefore moderate rather than severe.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Melt growth gives favorable kinetics because the references explicitly synthesize zinc-rich alkaline-earth phases from melts, which greatly improves atomic transport (CS1, CS2). The structural framework is related to CaZn5/SrZn5 and other Zn cuboctahedral networks, but narrow phase ranges and reported impurities reduce the structural-reconstruction score (CS1, CS2, SS1, SS2). The main uncertainty is whether slow cooling selects the target Immm Ca3Zn11 or a competing Ca–Zn phase.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Direct solid-state reaction uses moderate temperatures of 400–600 °C and ambient pressure, which is favorable (CS5, TS4). However, it requires vacuum/Ar sealed vessels, Zn retention, pellet pressing, and handling air-sensitive Ca [Reasonable Inference from CS5/TS4]. These requirements lower F2 relative to simple open-atmosphere ceramic synthesis.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Metal filings and powders have moderate activity, and CaZn11-type phases can form after prolonged heating at 400–600 °C (CS5, TS4). Diffusion should be manageable because Ca/Zn are not refractory [Reasonable Inference], but solid-state ordering is slower than in the melt route. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target Immm 3:11 arrangement forms instead of the known monoclinic CaZn11.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The RE3Zn11-type route uses a preliminary 400 °C reaction and 600–700 °C sealed-tube sintering/annealing, avoiding high pressure and >1000 °C conditions (TS5). It still requires vacuum or reduced-pressure Ar, sealed Ta/quartz handling, and multiple steps (TS5). Ca air sensitivity and Zn volatility introduce additional practical severity [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
This route benefits from the same Immm 3:11 structural archetype shown by Nd3Zn11 (TS5). The use of metal filings and multistep sintering implies moderate precursor activity and ordering kinetics rather than rapid solution-like reaction (TS5). The uncertainty is chemical transferability from Nd–Zn to Ca–Zn, because Ca has different bonding/size behavior [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 4 condition severity.**  
High-pressure synthesis is penalized strongly because structurally related Ca/Sr–Zn phases require 7–10 GPa in multi-anvil assemblies (SS3, SS5). The route also involves encapsulation, quenching, decompression, and prior precursor preparation (SS3, SS5). It is therefore challenging despite short hold times.

**F3 — Route 4 kinetics.**  
High pressure and ~1000 °C can drive rapid reconstruction of related intermetallics within ~1 h (SS3, SS5). However, SS5 reports SrZn11 formation from high-pressure decomposition of SrZn2 and unidentified by-products, indicating difficult phase control and substantial reconstruction. This lowers the kinetic/structural pathway score.

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a mixed Ca/Zn size environment and mixed edge/corner/face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, both of which increase the risk of complex local ordering or glassy/defective products [Reasonable Inference; target description; CS1, CS5, TS5]. There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is treated as neutral. The resulting score reflects medium risk rather than high risk because intermetallic analogues with similar motifs are experimentally known (CS1, CS5, TS5).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca3Zn11 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because the target E_hull is negative and zinc-rich Ca/Sr/RE analogues are reported at or near the convex hull (target E_hull; CS1, CS5, TS1–TS5). The CS and TS evidence strongly supports synthesis of Ca–Zn zinc-rich intermetallics from sealed elemental reactions, while TS5 gives especially strong structural support through an Immm Nd3Zn11 analogue. The most critical enabling factor is liquid-assisted homogenization in sealed Ta ampoules, which addresses diffusion and Zn retention simultaneously (CS1, CS2). The most critical limiting factor is polymorph and stoichiometry control, because known CaZn11 in the references is monoclinic C2/m and zinc-rich systems show impurity/phase-window issues (CS1, CS5, TS4). High pressure is plausible but not preferred because the target is already predicted stable and ambient-pressure Ca–Zn chemistry is well supported (target E_hull; CS1, CS5).
