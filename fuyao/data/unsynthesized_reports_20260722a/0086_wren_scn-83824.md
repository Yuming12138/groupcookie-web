### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is reported as −0.021514967 eV/atom, so it is scored as effectively on the convex hull and assigned 95/100. CS supports the general idea that low-hull fluorides are synthesizable, with CaF₂ at E_hull = 0 in CS-2 and NaNiF₃ at E_hull = 0–0.0055 in CS-4/CS-5. SS and TS contain low-hull examples, but their chemistries are unrelated to Li–F, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Li–F-specific thermodynamic behavior and that signal is treated as neutral 50. Remaining uncertainty is high because Li₅F₈ is fluorine-rich and not charge-balanced by simple Li⁺/F⁻ valence counting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because CS-1 uses 8.6 GPa and 1100 °C, and CS-5 uses 12 GPa and 1273 K for fluoride high-pressure synthesis. The route also requires reactive fluorinating conditions because the target has more fluorine than LiF [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 and CS-5 show that multianvil/DAC equipment and phase control are major obstacles. SS/TS give insufficient direct route-severity evidence for Li–F high-pressure fluorination, so their source-specific contribution is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are partly favorable because Li is a small mobile cation and flux/high-pressure routes can enhance transport; flux growth is explicitly used for CaF₂ in CS-1 and fluoride flux melting is used in CS-2. However, the target beta-In₂S₃-like LiF₆ framework is structurally far from simple LiF, so structural reconstruction is difficult [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 also notes difficult phase control for high-pressure fluoride polymorphs, supporting a kinetic penalty. SS/TS lack Li–F kinetic analogues, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS and a neutral 50 sub-signal.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids >5 GPa pressure and >1000 °C temperature, so it is less severe than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 supports moderate-temperature binary fluoride reaction at 350–800 °C under N₂/high vacuum, but the proposed fluorinating agent adds toxicity, volatility, and sealed-system hazards [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports quench-assisted fluoride synthesis, but at higher 900–1150 °C. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for sealed Li–F oxidative fluorination, so their source-specific signal is neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

LiF and solid fluorinating agents provide moderate precursor activity, but LiF may be kinetically inert compared with nitrate/solution precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The diffusion burden is relatively low because only Li cations must order, and CS-4 demonstrates fluoride reaction at 350–800 °C. The main kinetic risk is nucleating the beta-In₂S₃-like framework without pressure, because CS-1/CS-5 imply dense fluoride polymorphs often need high-pressure stabilization. SS/TS offer insufficient Li–F kinetic evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has mild thermal severity and no bulk high-pressure requirement [Reasonable Inference]. However, it requires vacuum/plasma equipment and reactive fluorine chemistry, which is not supported directly by CS/SS/TS and is therefore speculative. Toxic fluorine plasma and possible Li volatility impose condition penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for thin-film synthesis is treated as neutral 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

Thin-film deposition could kinetically trap a fluorine-rich phase, but no CS/SS/TS analogue confirms such a pathway. Precursor vapor availability is only moderate because Li/F stoichiometry and fluorine reactivity are difficult [Reasonable Inference]. Crystallization and composition control are expected to be limiting because the target composition is far from LiF and likely has a narrow stability window [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore Route 3 has the lowest F3 despite a better F2.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation species, Li, so the cation-radius-mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The formula unit has 13 atoms, and no provided source establishes a >40-atom target cell, so the atom-count check is not triggered. The target description mentions edge-sharing LiF₆ octahedra but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is low. CS/SS/TS do not provide a direct amorphization analogue for Li₅F₈, so uncertainty remains neutral 50 for source-specific amorphization evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li₅F₈ is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or essentially on-hull because the reported E_hull is −0.0215 eV/atom. The strongest supporting analogues are chemically similar fluorides in CS: simple binary or multinary fluorides are synthesized by solid-state, flux, and high-pressure methods in CS-1 through CS-5. SS and TS provide little chemically specific support because their nearest examples are not Li–F fluorides, so they are treated mostly as neutral evidence. The most critical enabling factor is the very favorable computed thermodynamic stability combined with the availability of simple LiF-based precursor chemistry. The most critical limiting factor is the chemically unusual fluorine-rich, charge-imbalanced Li₅F₈ stoichiometry, which likely requires high fluorine chemical potential or non-equilibrium stabilization [Reasonable Inference].
