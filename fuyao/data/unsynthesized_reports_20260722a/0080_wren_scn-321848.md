### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.088742389 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom top bin of the supplied rubric and therefore receives 98/100 (target data). CS provides supportive but mixed precedent because GaCl₃, CdAl₂Cl₈, and GeCl₄ are on the hull, while CdCl₄ and FeCl₃ are metastable in the provided entries (CS). SS and TS contain stable/near-stable materials, but they are mostly nitrides, amides, phosphates, borides, or bromides rather than Ag–Cl hyperchlorides, so they provide insufficient chemical evidence and are effectively neutral for AgCl₅ chemistry (SS/TS).

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Sealed-ampoule chlorination / CVT

Condition severity is significant because the route requires dry Cl₂, sealed ampoules, and likely multistep loading/transport/collection, analogous to FeCl₃ CVT and GaCl₃ sealed sublimation (CS). Kinetics are favorable relative to solid-state synthesis because chlorine transport and vapor-phase mobility can bypass bulk diffusion, as shown for FeCl₃ and GaCl₃ crystal growth (CS). The main uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS source proves AgCl₅ volatility or stability under the proposed chlorine pressures, so this is a [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Low-temperature sublimation / in situ crystallization

This route has similar severity to Route 1 because it requires vacuum or sealed chlorine-containing systems and volatile, moisture-sensitive halide handling, as seen for GaCl₃ and GeCl₄ analogues (CS). Kinetically it is attractive because molecular halides can crystallize directly from vapor or liquid without solid-state diffusion, and both GaCl₃ and GeCl₄ are zero-dimensional chloride analogues (CS). The uncertainty is the unknown vapor pressure and decomposition behavior of AgCl₅; SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this factor and are scored neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Lewis-acid chloride flux / sealed melt growth

This route has the best balance of severity and kinetics because CdAl₂Cl₈ is made at 250 °C in sealed ampoules under Ar with slow cooling over five days (CS). A chloride flux such as AlCl₃ or GaCl₃ should enhance ion mobility and allow low-temperature crystallization [Reasonable Inference], while maintaining chlorine activity with Cl₂ addresses the chlorine-rich target stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is phase competition with AgCl or mixed Ag–Al–Cl/Ga–Cl complexes, since the CS analogue is Cd/Al chloride rather than AgCl₅.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Oxidative solution crystallization

Solution crystallization is kinetically mild, and chloride complex crystals can form by slow evaporation from aqueous solution in the CdCl₄-related analogue (CS). However, AgCl₅ likely requires a strongly oxidizing, chlorine-rich environment that may be incompatible with water or ordinary chloride solution chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS precipitation analogues show soluble-salt routes can work for Ag-containing compounds, but they are oxophosphates rather than chloride clusters and therefore give insufficient direct chemical evidence for AgCl₅.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is zero-dimensional and consists of small AgCl₅ clusters, so it lacks the extended mixed polyhedral connectivity that often drives glassy or poorly ordered products (target structure). The cation-radius-difference check is not triggered because there is only one cation, Ag, and the unit-cell content described as two clusters is not >40 atoms (target structure). CS molecular chloride analogues GaCl₃ and GeCl₄ also crystallize as zero-dimensional molecular solids, which supports low amorphization risk for molecular halide crystallization (CS), though AgCl₅’s unusual oxidation/chlorine content remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgCl₅ appears thermodynamically favorable according to the reported negative hull energy, so it is best treated computationally as a likely ground-state or strongly stabilized phase, not merely a high-energy metastable phase (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar molecular and chloride-rich halides: GaCl₃ and GeCl₄ crystallize as zero-dimensional molecular chlorides, FeCl₃ demonstrates dry chlorine vapor transport, and CdAl₂Cl₈ demonstrates sealed chloride melt growth at moderate temperature (CS). SS and TS mostly support only the general idea that near-hull phases can be synthesized; they provide insufficient direct Ag–Cl chemical guidance. The most critical enabling factor is use of a sealed, anhydrous, chlorine-rich environment that prevents Cl₂ loss and stabilizes the AgCl₅ cluster [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is the unusual chlorine-rich Ag:Cl = 1:5 stoichiometry with Cl–Cl bonding, which may compete with AgCl + Cl₂ separation or other silver chlorides [Reasonable Inference].
