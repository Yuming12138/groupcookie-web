### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.04777084 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin and suggests a ground-state or near-ground-state compound [Reasonable Inference based on target data]. Chemically similar Ca₂MgPd₂, Ca₂Pd₂Al, Ca₂AgPd₂, and Li₂PdGa all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting stability of nearby Ca–Pd and Li–Pd intermetallic chemistries (**CS1–CS5**). SS/TS contain stable or near-stable compounds, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient transfer signal for Li–Ca–Pd thermodynamics and are neutral evidence (**SS1–SS5**, **TS1–TS5**). Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether the negative hull value reflects a true ordered ground state or a computational artifact around the convex hull [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 requires 950–1000 °C, slow cooling, and 48–96 h annealing in sealed Ta, which closely matches Ca₂MgPd₂ and Ca₂AgPd₂ synthesis conditions (**CS1**, **CS3**). The severity score is reduced because Li and Ca require air-free handling [Reasonable Inference] and sealed ampoules are needed, as demonstrated by Ca–Pd and Li–Pd analogues (**CS1**, **CS5**). There is no evidence that high pressure, H₂, NH₃, or >1400 °C conditions are required for the closest analogues (**CS1**, **CS3**). SS/TS high-pressure or reactive-gas examples are not chemically relevant, so they are not used to penalize this route (**SS1**, **SS5**, **TS1**, **TS5**).

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The strongest kinetic advantage is structural: Ca₂MgPd₂ has the same Immm motif and nearly identical Ca–Pd/small-cation–Pd coordination, so only Li/Mg-like site substitution and ordering are required (**CS1**; target data). The limiting kinetic factor is Pd’s dense metallic nature and high melting point [Reasonable Inference], but CS analogues show that 1000 °C plus long annealing is sufficient for Ca–Pd intermetallic formation (**CS1**, **CS3**). The 48–72 h anneals in CS analogues justify a moderate kinetic penalty for slow ordering/diffusion (**CS1**, **CS3**). Remaining uncertainty is whether Li occupies the intended site cleanly or promotes competing Li–Pd binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 lowers the maximum temperature to ≤900 °C, consistent with Li₂PdGa formation at 650–900 °C and Ca₂Pd₂Al annealing near 900 °C (**CS5**, **CS2**). Lower temperature reduces thermal severity but still requires sealed Ar ampoules and inert handling because of Li/Ca sensitivity [Reasonable Inference], consistent with sealed Li–Pd and Ca–Pd syntheses (**CS5**, **CS1**). No high pressure or reactive gas is indicated by the relevant CS examples (**CS2**, **CS5**). SS/TS routes involving supercritical ammonia or flowing NH₃ are chemically irrelevant and treated neutral for this route (**SS1**, **SS5**, **TS1**, **TS5**).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

The lower-temperature route may better preserve Li stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference], but it also slows diffusion relative to the 1000 °C route used for the closest Ca₂MgPd₂ analogue (**CS1**). Li₂PdGa demonstrates that Li–Pd intermetallic bonding is accessible in sealed ampoules, but its cubic structure differs from the target Immm Ca₂MgPd₂-type structure (**CS5**, **CS1**). Therefore, Route 2 is kinetically less certain than Route 1 despite milder temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the target phase nucleates directly or requires higher-temperature equilibration [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Arc-melting/pre-alloying is supported only generically by SS/TS examples of arc melting plus annealing around 1000 °C in Ar (**SS2**, **TS2**), not by the closest Li–Ca–Pd chemistry. The route is penalized for likely local temperatures above 1000–1400 °C [Reasonable Inference], multi-step handling, and Li/Ca volatility/air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Closest CS analogues use sealed Ta ampoule reactions rather than open melting of volatile Li-containing samples (**CS1**, **CS3**, **CS5**). Thus, this route is feasible but more severe and less directly evidenced.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Pre-alloying can improve contact between Ca and Pd and reduce diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference], and arc/anneal methods are known for some intermetallic-like systems (**SS2**, **TS2**). However, the target-relevant structural evidence comes from Ca₂MgPd₂ in sealed solid-state annealing, not from melt growth (**CS1**). Li loss or segregation during melting could undermine composition control [Reasonable Inference]. Long sealed annealing is still needed to reach the ordered Immm phase [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the annealing requirement in Ca–Pd analogues (**CS1**, **CS3**).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a small ordered intermetallic with simple reported Li–Pd, Ca–Pd, and Pd-centered coordination, so unit-cell complexity and mixed polyhedral connectivity do not appear severe from the target description. The main amorphization/ordering risk is the large size difference between Li and Ca [Reasonable Inference], even though the target assigns them to distinct crystallographic environments (target data). CS analogues such as Ca₂MgPd₂ and Li₂PdGa crystallize as ordered intermetallics rather than amorphous products, supporting low-to-medium amorphization risk (**CS1**, **CS5**). SS/TS provide insufficient useful signal because their structures are chemically unrelated and include ionic amides/phosphates/halides/nitrides rather than Li–Ca–Pd intermetallics (**SS1–SS5**, **TS1–TS5**).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Li(CaPd)₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is −0.04777084 eV/atom, and chemically similar Ca–Pd/Li–Pd analogues are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (target data; **CS1–CS5**). The strongest synthesis support is the isostructural Ca₂MgPd₂ analogue, which has the same Immm framework and was made by direct elemental reaction in sealed Ta ampoules (**CS1**). The most important enabling factor is the excellent structural and bonding match between Li(CaPd)₂ and Ca₂MgPd₂, including comparable Ca–Pd and small-cation–Pd distances (**CS1**; target data). The main limiting factor is maintaining Li and Ca stoichiometry during high-temperature annealing, because both require inert handling and Li is volatile at the proposed synthesis temperature [Reasonable Inference], while CS analogues rely on sealed ampoules to manage reactive metals (**CS1**, **CS5**). SS/TS analogues do not materially change the recommendation because they are chemically dissimilar and provide insufficient specific signal for Li–Ca–Pd synthesis (**SS1–SS5**, **TS1–TS5**).
