### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E\(_\text{hull}\) is −0.06848733 eV/atom, which is effectively on/below the hull and receives a high stability score under the requested rubric (target data). The exact Cs analogue Cs4IrO4 is reported at E\(_\text{hull}\) = 0.0 and is experimentally synthesized, supporting stability of the A4IrO4 structure type (CS-4). Other chemically similar alkali oxides are also on the hull, but they contain Bi, In, Al, or Fe rather than Ir, so they provide secondary chemical support only (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS provide insufficient Rb–Ir–O-specific evidence and are treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the Cs4IrO4 analogue was made at 740 °C after a 900 °C stage, not above 1000 °C or at GPa pressures (CS-4). The major penalties are air-sensitive/volatile alkali oxide precursors, the need for sealed Ag-bomb containment, and multi-step handling/sealing/heating (CS-4; CS-1 to CS-5). The uncertainty is whether RbO\(_x\) behaves identically to CsO1.8 in the Ag-bomb environment [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The exact structural match to Cs4IrO4 strongly lowers the reconstruction barrier because the target has the same C2/m A4IrO4 framework and nearly identical Ir–O geometry (CS-4 and target). However, CS-4 required 86 d at 740 °C, indicating slow transport or narrow nucleation/growth kinetics. Rb oxides should be reactive and flux-like, but IrO2 is refractory, so the kinetic score is moderate rather than high (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses the same general sealed alkali oxide strategy as Rb3BiO3, Cs6Al2O6, and oxoferrate syntheses, all under Ar and moderate temperatures (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). It receives the same severity deductions as Route 1 for sealed volatile/air-sensitive alkali oxides and multi-step handling. The uncertainty is redox suitability, because the exact Cs4IrO4 route used CsO1.8 rather than Cs2O (CS-4).

#### F3 — Route 2

Rb2O is a documented reactive precursor for Rb-rich oxometallates such as Rb3BiO3 (CS-1), but IrO2 is more refractory than Bi2O3 and may react slowly [Reasonable Inference]. Long anneals in related alkali-rich oxide chemistry—6 months for Cs6Al2O6 and ~4 months for oxoferrates—show that bulk diffusion/ordering can be very slow (CS-3, CS-5). The structural match to Cs4IrO4 is favorable, but redox mismatch relative to CsO1.8 lowers confidence (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has moderate nominal temperature and no GPa pressure, but the route is extrapolated and would require sealed handling of oxidizing Rb nitrate/peroxide/superoxide mixtures [Reasonable Inference]. It is penalized for volatile/reactive sealed precursors, multi-step flux activation/reaction/recovery, and air-sensitive oxidizers [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports only the general need for an alkali-rich oxidizing environment via CsO1.8 in Cs4IrO4, not the nitrate/peroxide flux implementation (CS-4).

#### F3 — Route 3

A nitrate/peroxide flux could improve precursor activity and cation transport relative to oxide-only powder reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS entry demonstrates this flux for A4IrO4, and the exact Cs4IrO4 analogue still required a long reaction even with reactive CsO1.8 (CS-4). Therefore kinetics may improve, but phase selectivity and redox control remain uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large Rb–Ir cation size contrast, which triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description is relatively simple, with one O site, two Rb sites, and square-coplanar IrO4 units, and does not show the mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing complexity associated with high amorphization tendency (target). The exact crystalline Cs analogue is experimentally known, which further argues that this structure family can crystallize rather than vitrify under appropriate alkali-oxide conditions (CS-4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb4IrO4 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E\(_\text{hull}\) is negative within calculation tolerance and the isostructural Cs4IrO4 analogue is on the hull and experimentally synthesized (target; CS-4). The chemical similarity evidence is strong, especially CS-4, because it matches the A4IrO4 formula, C2/m symmetry, square-planar IrO4 unit, and alkali coordination environment. The most critical enabling factor is use of a sealed, alkali-rich RbO\(_x\) environment that can act simultaneously as Rb reservoir, oxygen buffer, and flux, as shown by CsO1.8 in the Cs4IrO4 synthesis (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is practical phase control: alkali oxide volatility/air sensitivity, redox control, and very slow annealing were all problematic or demanding in related alkali-rich oxide syntheses (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS do not materially strengthen the synthesis prediction because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated and are therefore treated as neutral evidence.
