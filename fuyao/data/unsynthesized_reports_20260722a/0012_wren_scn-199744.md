### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.0095 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within numerical tolerance and therefore receives a high thermodynamic score (target data). Thermodynamic-neighbor analogues Zn(ReO4)2, AgReO4, VRe2O9, and ZrMo2O8 are reported at or very near E_hull = 0, supporting the chemical plausibility of stable perrhenate/oxide frameworks (CS1, CS3, CS5, SS4, TS1, TS3, TS4, TS5). Remaining uncertainty is not the hull value but whether the predicted Ag(II) phase is kinetically accessible instead of disproportionating to Ag(I) AgReO4 plus oxidized/reduced byproducts [Reasonable Inference from CS5/TS5].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses mild solution temperatures because close analogues crystallize after 50–60 °C reaction and room-temperature aging, and AgReO4 forms by mixing aqueous AgNO3/NaReO4 and slow cooling from 50 °C (CS1, CS5, SS1, TS5). The score is reduced for multi-step solution handling and likely use of strong oxidants or Ag(II) precursors, which are not directly demonstrated for the target [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a direct Ag(II) perrhenate precipitation protocol, so that uncertainty limits the score.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Aqueous perrhenate chemistry has high precursor activity because HReO4 is generated in solution and AgReO4 precipitates from soluble salts (CS1, CS5, TS5). Structural reconstruction should be favorable because Zn(ReO4)2 is nearly isostructural with the target P-3 layered tetrahedral–octahedral framework (CS1, SS1, TS1). The principal kinetic uncertainty is not diffusion but nucleation of the Ag(II) sheet instead of Ag(I) AgReO4 [Reasonable Inference from CS5/TS5].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The sealed-tube route is more severe because a Re oxide analogue requires vacuum-sealed quartz at 700 °C with controlled heating/cooling, and structurally similar oxide synthesis can require long anneals and regrinding (CS3, SS4, SS5, TS3, TS4). Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, sealed volatile Re species, multi-step processing, and oxidizing/toxic precursors [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for a sealed-tube Ag(II) perrhenate target specifically, so the route is viable but riskier.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Solid-state kinetics are poorer than solution kinetics because cation rearrangement from AgReO4/Re oxides to Ag(ReO4)2 requires changing Ag coordination and stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference; CS5, SS1]. The VRe2O9 sealed-tube example shows Re oxide frameworks can form at 700 °C, supporting moderate kinetic feasibility (CS3, SS5, TS3). The ZrMo2O8 analogue requiring 71 h and repeated regrinding signals that oxide sheet phases may need extended diffusion/ordering (SS4, TS4).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Hydrothermal conditions are not directly reported for the analogues, so this route is extrapolated from low-temperature aqueous perrhenate crystallization [Reasonable Inference; CS1, CS5]. The temperature and pressure are not severe compared with solid-state routes, but multi-step autoclave handling and strong oxidants lower the score [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for hydrothermal perrhenate synthesis specifically is treated as a neutral direct-evidence signal.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Hydrothermal transport should retain the high activity of soluble perrhenate and silver species while improving crystal growth relative to room-temperature precipitation [Reasonable Inference; solution reactivity supported by CS1, CS5]. The close P-3 Zn analogue supports structural assembly of a tetrahedral–octahedral sheet (SS1). The main uncertainty is whether elevated temperature destabilizes Ag(II) or promotes AgReO4, which is a directly known Ag–Re–O competing phase (CS5, TS5; [Reasonable Inference]).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is a relatively simple corner-sharing ReO4–AgO6 sheet, and the structurally closest Zn analogue uses the same corner-sharing tetrahedral–octahedral motif without edge/face-sharing complexity (target data; SS1). One amorphization trigger is assigned because Ag and Re have very different cation sizes and coordination preferences, which can frustrate homogeneous nucleation [Reasonable Inference]. CS and TS provide insufficient direct amorphization data, so their source-specific signal is neutral for this factor.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Re2AgO8 is likely thermodynamically stable or very near ground-state because its computed E_hull is −0.0095 eV/atom, and multiple chemical/structural/thermodynamic analogues are stable or near-stable (target data; CS1, CS5, SS1, TS1, TS5). The analogue support is strong structurally because Zn(ReO4)2 has essentially the same P-3 layered ReO4–MO6 topology, but the chemical support is mixed because known Ag–Re–O evidence points to AgReO4 with Ag(I), not Ag(ReO4)2 with Ag(II) [CS1, SS1, CS5, TS5; Reasonable Inference]. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature solution mobility, since perrhenate analogues crystallize under mild aqueous conditions and avoid refractory solid diffusion (CS1, CS5, SS1). The most critical limiting factor is maintaining Ag in the divalent state while suppressing Ag(I) AgReO4 precipitation [Reasonable Inference from target charge balance and CS5/TS5]. Overall, the material is promising but requires redox-controlled synthesis rather than a simple AgNO3/NaReO4 precipitation.
