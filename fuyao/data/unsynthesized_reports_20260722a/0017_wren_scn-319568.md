### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.040425 eV/atom, so it is predicted to be on or slightly below the computed hull and receives 98/100. CS analogues Na₄GeO₄, Na₅NbO₅, Na₄TiO₄ and related Na–Ru oxide examples are reported at or near the hull, supporting the general feasibility of Na-rich oxides (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS examples are mostly chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient target-specific thermodynamic evidence and are treated as neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for high-pressure controlled O₂, sealed autoclave operation, multi-step loading/reaction/cooling, and moisture-sensitive alkali reagents/products, all supported by CS-5. It avoids >1000 °C and avoids >5 GPa pressure, so the severity is not as high as true multi-anvil synthesis. Remaining uncertainty is whether Na₅RuO₆ needs even higher oxygen chemical potential than the CS-5 Na–Ru oxide.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because Na₂O₂/NaOH are reactive and CS-5 already crystallized a Na–Ru–O octahedral Caswellsilverite-like phase. RuO₂ is still a refractory oxide, which lowers precursor activity [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is relatively favorable because CS-5 and the target both involve NaO₆/RuO₆ octahedral networks.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 benefits from avoiding external high pressure and can use temperatures comparable to CS-2/CS-4. It is penalized for sealed alkali oxide handling, air-sensitive Na₂O/Li₂O, and very long multi-step anneals, all indicated by CS-2/CS-3/CS-4. Its biggest uncertainty is whether inert sealed conditions can stabilize the high Ru oxidation state required by Na₅RuO₆ [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Kinetics are slower because CS-2/CS-3/CS-4 Na-rich oxide syntheses require 25–75 days, showing diffusion and ordering are limiting. The chemistry supports Na-rich oxide formation, but not specifically Na–Ru–O or Caswellsilverite-like ordering. Therefore Route 2 receives a lower F3 despite acceptable F2.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has moderate temperature but uses reactive peroxide/hydroxide flux and likely high-pressure O₂, as inferred by combining CS-1 and CS-5. It is penalized for controlled oxidizing conditions, sealed flux operation, multi-step flux removal, and air/moisture-sensitive precursors. It is not penalized for >1000 °C or >5 GPa because the relevant CS conditions are 450–650 °C and 220 MPa O₂.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Flux/mineralizer chemistry should improve ion mobility and crystallization, supported by CS-1 alkaline melt chemistry, CS-3 flux growth, and CS-5 NaOH mineraliser. The route is kinetically strongest because it combines high Na activity with high oxygen chemical potential. The main uncertainty is exact phase selection among possible sodium ruthenates [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size/charge contrast between Na⁺ and high-valent Ru, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target description contains corner- and edge-sharing octahedra but no face-sharing, so C3 is not triggered. CS-5 demonstrates that related Na–Ru–O octahedral compounds can crystallize rather than amorphize, but SS/TS provide insufficient chemically relevant evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₅RuO₆ is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is −0.040425 eV/atom, placing it effectively on the hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-5, which directly demonstrates Na–Ru–O Caswellsilverite-like octahedral chemistry under high O₂ pressure with Na₂O₂/RuO₂/NaOH. CS-2 to CS-4 further support that highly Na-rich oxides can be crystallized in sealed alkali-rich environments, although these are not Ru systems. The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential combined with high Na activity, best achieved by Na₂O₂/NaOH flux under O₂ pressure. The most critical limiting factor is stabilization of the very high formal Ru oxidation state and suppression of lower-valent sodium ruthenate competing phases [Reasonable Inference].
