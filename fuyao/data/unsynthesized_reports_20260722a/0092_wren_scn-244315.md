### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.059071714 eV/atom, so the structure is predicted to lie on or slightly below the computed convex hull; this justifies a near-maximum F1 score. Negative hull values are usually numerical/database artifacts rather than physically negative instability, but they still indicate strong thermodynamic favorability [Reasonable Inference]. TS entries include stable or near-stable materials, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, AuBr, and nitrides, so TS gives insufficient specific evidence for Cs₃As₂Br₉ beyond the general concept that low-hull phases can be synthesized; TS is neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS-1 shows bromometallate precipitation from concentrated HBr without reported high temperature, pressure, vacuum, or specialized equipment. The principal severity deduction is toxic/corrosive chemistry: As₂O₃ and HBr are hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because solution precipitation bypasses bulk solid-state diffusion, as shown by Rb₂TeBr₆ crystallizing from concentrated HBr in CS-1. The target framework must self-assemble from solution, but bromide octahedral complex formation is chemically plausible in high-HBr media [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is As(III) speciation and possible hydrolysis, which are not addressed by CS-1.

#### F2 — Route 2

Slow evaporation is also mild because CS-3 reports Rb₃Os₂Br₉ crystal growth from aqueous solution at 23 °C. The score is reduced for toxic arsenic and corrosive hydrobromic acid handling [Reasonable Inference]. If isolated AsBr₃ is used, practical severity increases because it is volatile and moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are favorable because CS-3 demonstrates room-temperature crystallization of an A₃M₂Br₉ bromometallate from solution. The target shares A₃M₂Br₉ stoichiometry and octahedral bromide motifs with CS-3, supporting good structural transferability. The remaining uncertainty is that the CS-3 precursor is a preformed Os bromometallate, whereas As(III) bromide species must be generated or stabilized [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The sealed halide melt route is more severe because CS-2 requires glovebox loading, evacuated quartz ampoules, Bridgman equipment, and long slow cooling for Cs₃Cr₂X₉. For Cs₃As₂Br₉, AsBr₃ volatility and arsenic toxicity add further constraints [Reasonable Inference]. The proposed temperature is lower than the 790 °C bromide case in CS-2 because AsBr₃ can act as a low-temperature flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetic prospects are good because molten/flux halides provide fast transport, and CS-2 demonstrates formation of the closely related Cs₃Cr₂Br₉ framework. Structural reconstruction is especially favorable because the target and CS-2 analogue both contain CsBr₁₂ cuboctahedra and MBr₆ octahedra in A₃M₂Br₉-type chemistry. The major kinetic uncertainty is maintaining As:Br stoichiometry under volatile AsBr₃/Br₂ pressure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

Hydrothermal growth has moderate severity because CS-4 uses sealed autoclaves at 523 K for 66 h, which is routine in materials synthesis but more demanding than ambient precipitation. The target route would require corrosive HBr-rich media and toxic As compounds [Reasonable Inference]. SS hydrothermal evidence involves supercritical ammonia amide chemistry and is not chemically useful for this bromide target, so SS is neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 4

Hydrothermal kinetics should be favorable because transport occurs through solution, and CS-4 reports single-crystal CsNiBr₃ formation under sealed acidic conditions. However, the CS-4 structure is CsNiBr₃ rather than Cs₃M₂Br₉, so structural transferability is weaker than for CS-2 or CS-3. The main uncertainty is whether hydrothermal HBr stabilizes the desired As(III) bromide complex rather than hydrolyzed arsenite phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Cs⁺ and As³⁺, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The prompt does not establish a unit cell atom count above 40, and the reported connectivity clearly includes corner and face sharing but not the full vertex + edge + face coexistence required by C3. Therefore, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃As₂Br₉ is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very low-energy phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0591 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS: CS-2 provides a very close Cs₃M₂Br₉ halide framework analogue, CS-3 provides A₃M₂Br₉ slow evaporation growth, and CS-1 provides concentrated-HBr bromometallate precipitation. SS and TS are largely chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct support, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory. The most critical enabling factor is the high mobility and complexation chemistry of bromide-rich acidic solution or halide flux media, which should allow Cs⁺ and As(III)-Br units to assemble without high-temperature diffusion. The most critical limiting factor is arsenic bromide chemistry: As(III) toxicity, hydrolysis, and possible volatility can complicate stoichiometry and phase purity.
