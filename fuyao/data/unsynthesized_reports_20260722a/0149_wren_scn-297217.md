### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.00646 eV/atom, which is effectively a ground-state value and directly places it in the highest scoring range. TS analogues NaSrP, Na3Ca3AlSb4, Na3Ca3AlAs4, NaBaP, and Ca2CdP2 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic-neighbor set supports synthesizable low-energy pnictide phases (TS1–TS5). CS evidence is also generally favorable because CS1, CS2, and CS5 have E_hull = 0.0, although CS3 is a contradictory high-E_hull outlier and is weighted less because it is a nitride perovskite rather than a Ca–Hg–P phosphide.

#### F2 — Route 1

The direct sealed ampoule route avoids >1000 °C conditions, which is favorable relative to high-temperature Nb ampoule examples such as SS3/TS3 and SS4/TS4. However, it requires controlled atmosphere/sealed handling, consistent with CS1, CS2, and SS2/TS2, and Hg/P volatility makes sealed containment mandatory [Reasonable Inference]. The biggest uncertainty is the internal pressure and whether Hg remains in the desired ternary phase rather than segregating or volatilizing.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, the route benefits from reactive Ca, volatile/mobile P, and liquid/vapor Hg transport [Reasonable Inference], but formation from elements still requires substantial solid-state reconstruction. Analogues often need long holds, slow cooling, or sealed tubes, e.g., 120 h for NaSrP-related flux products (SS1/TS1) and 20–24 h plus slow cooling for Ca2CdP2 flux crystals (SS5/TS5). The route is therefore kinetically plausible but not trivial.

#### F2 — Route 2

Flux growth uses moderate temperatures similar to Cd/Sn/Pb flux windows of 700–960 °C reported for Ca2CdP2-related phases (SS5/TS5), avoiding extreme temperatures and high pressure. The penalties come from sealed ampoule handling, volatile Hg/P, toxic flux components, and decanting/multi-step workflow; these are directly analogous to controlled flux procedures in SS5/TS5 and SS1/TS1. The remaining uncertainty is whether Hg can act as a practical flux at useful temperatures without excessive vapor pressure [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth has the best kinetic score because molten Cd/Sn/Pb fluxes successfully produce Ca–group-12 pnictide crystals in SS5/TS5, and Na/Sn flux yields NaSrP crystals in SS1/TS1. A liquid flux should accelerate diffusion and enable crystallization at lower effective temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. The structural mismatch between Ca2CdP2 and trigonal Ca4HgP2 means nucleation of the target phase is not guaranteed.

#### F2 — Route 3

The binary precursor route is more operationally complex because it includes precursor preparation or alloying before final annealing, similar in spirit to Ca3N2 preparation in CS3 and binary arsenide use in SS3/TS3. It still requires sealed inert/vacuum conditions and must manage Hg vapor pressure [Reasonable Inference]. Its severity score is therefore lower than Route 1 even though it may improve chemical homogeneity.

#### F3 — Route 3

Binary precursors can improve kinetics by pre-forming Ca–P or Ca–Hg contacts, and binary precursor strategies are documented for pnictide syntheses in SS3/TS3 and nitride syntheses in CS3. The approach should reduce elemental P volatility and shorten the reaction pathway [Reasonable Inference]. However, Ca3P2 and Ca–Hg alloy particles still require interdiffusion and structural rearrangement into the R-3m ternary phase.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a substantial cation mismatch between electropositive Ca and softer Hg, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate >40 atoms per cell, and it reports edge/corner sharing but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so C2 and C3 are not triggered based on available target information. SS5/TS5 and SS2/TS2 demonstrate that complex mixed-cation pnictides can crystallize, but phase-control issues in SS5/TS5 keep the amorphization/poor-crystallinity risk from being negligible.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca4HgP2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is essentially zero, and TS analogues are predominantly hull-stable pnictides (TS1–TS5). The CS/SS/TS analogues strongly support the general feasibility of sealed-tube and flux synthesis for reactive alkaline-earth pnictides, especially through Nb/Ta ampoules and controlled slow cooling (CS1, CS2, SS1/TS1, SS5/TS5). The most important enabling factor is the availability of well-established sealed refractory-metal ampoule chemistry for air-sensitive, volatile pnictide systems (CS1, CS2, SS2/TS2, SS4/TS4). The most important limiting factor is Hg volatility/toxicity and the lack of a direct Ca–Hg–P experimental analogue in the provided data [Reasonable Inference]. The best initial experiment is a conservative sealed Ta/Nb solid-state reaction with staged heating and slight Hg control, followed by flux screening if phase purity is poor.
