### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The reported E_hull of Cs₃HgF₆ is −0.052337637 eV/atom, so the target is predicted to be at or below the convex hull within computational tolerance (target data). Chemically similar Cs fluorides include several stable or near-stable phases, such as Cs₂AgF₄ and Cs₃NiF₇ with E_hull = 0.0 and CsEuF₃ near the hull (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). SS and TS give insufficient chemically specific evidence because their nearest examples are unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so they are treated as neutral 50 for source utility (SS, TS).

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is penalized because it requires diluted F₂/Ar, which is supported by A₃MF₇ fluoride synthesis but is a reactive atmosphere (CS-3). It also uses toxic Hg fluoride and likely sealed or corrosion-resistant equipment because CS fluoride examples require Au tubes, dried Ar, or specialized fluoride-compatible vessels (CS-1–CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). The temperature itself is mild relative to refractory solid-state synthesis because Cs fluoride analogues form at 270–500 °C (CS-1–CS-3).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluoride precursors have moderate activity, as shown by direct CsF + AgF₂ reactions at 270–330 °C (CS-1, CS-2). Diffusion should be favorable for alkali fluorides at these temperatures, but the target’s cubic perovskite-like structure differs from the layered Cs₂AgF₄ analogues and tetragonal Cs₃NiF₇, so structural reconstruction remains uncertain (CS-1–CS-3; target structure). The main uncertainty is whether Hg can be oxidized and retained without forming competing Hg fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 avoids flowing F₂ during the main anneal and therefore scores better on condition severity than Routes 1 and 3 (CS-1, CS-2). However, it likely requires prior preparation of a high-fluorine Hg precursor, and the Hg precursor would be toxic and possibly unstable [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient useful evidence for this fluoride route and are neutral 50 for route-transfer assessment (SS, TS).

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The low-temperature sealed Au-tube approach is kinetically supported by Cs₂AgF₄ synthesis from CsF and AgF₂ (CS-1, CS-2). The limiting kinetic factor is not diffusion but phase selectivity, because the closest demonstrated route gives layered Cs₂AgF₄ rather than the cubic Cs₃HgF₆ perovskite-like phase (CS-1, CS-2; target structure). Precursor uncertainty for high-valent Hg further reduces confidence [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 is severe because it combines a reactive diluted F₂ atmosphere, toxic Hg fluoride, sealed/compatible vessels, and multiple operational steps (CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). Its temperature range is still relatively mild because analogous Cs-rich fluoride syntheses occur at 300–500 °C and Cs–Ag fluorides at 270–330 °C (CS-1–CS-3). The need for strict dry handling is supported by Cs fluoride synthesis protocols using drybox/dried Ar and <5 ppm H₂O control (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The CsF-rich flux should improve mass transport and lower the diffusion barrier compared with a dry pellet reaction [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 supports low-temperature fluoride synthesis from AF-rich mixtures under diluted F₂, and CS-5 supports perovskite-type CsEuF₃ formation under alkali-rich conditions. The main uncertainty is whether flux conditions stabilize the desired Cs/Hg ordering rather than dissolving or decomposing Hg fluoride species [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation-size contrast, as reflected in Cs–F distances of 2.75 and 3.53 Å versus Hg–F = 2.22 Å (target structure). The structure is nevertheless highly ordered and simple, with no reported edge-sharing and a perovskite-like topology, lowering amorphization risk (target structure). SS and TS do not provide useful amorphization-transfer evidence for this fluoride chemistry and are neutral 50 for this factor (SS, TS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₃HgF₆ is likely thermodynamically stable or very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative within computational tolerance (target data). Chemically similar Cs fluorides strongly support the general feasibility of making Cs-rich ternary fluorides by low-temperature solid-state or flux-like routes under dry Ar, Au-tube, or diluted F₂ conditions (CS-1–CS-5). Structurally, the strongest relevant analogue is CsEuF₃, which is a cubic perovskite fluoride with CsF₁₂ and MF₆ coordination motifs comparable to the target (CS-5; target structure). The most important enabling factor is the high fluoride/alkali mobility and CsF-rich chemistry that allow ordered fluorides to form at only a few hundred °C (CS-1–CS-3). The most important limiting factor is the inferred need to stabilize unusual Hg–F oxidation chemistry while suppressing Hg volatility/toxicity and competing Hg fluoride phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated, so they do not materially increase confidence beyond neutral source utility (SS, TS).
