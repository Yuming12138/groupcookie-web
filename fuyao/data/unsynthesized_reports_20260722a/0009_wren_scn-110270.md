### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is **−0.0196 eV/atom**, placing it effectively on the convex hull and therefore in the highest stability bin. This supports a high thermodynamic feasibility score, although the slightly negative hull value is likely within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. TS entries are near-hull but chemically unrelated, so there is insufficient Li–Ir–O-specific evidence from TS and it is treated as neutral 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: High-pressure oxidizing solid-state

The route is strongly supported by the NaIrO₃ analogue, which has essentially the same Cmcm post-perovskite framework and was made at 4.5 GPa/800 °C with Na₂O₂, Ir, and NaClO₃ (CS1). F2 is low because high pressure, reactive oxidizers, Ar glove-box handling, sealed capsules, washing, and air/moisture-sensitive Li oxidizers are all severe or operationally demanding (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). F3 is moderate-high because peroxide/chlorate oxidizers are reactive and the target framework is already proven for NaIrO₃, but Ir metal oxidation and Li ordering remain kinetic barriers [Reasonable Inference; CS1].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure reactive-oxide route

CaIrO₃ analogues show that oxide mixtures can form post-perovskite iridates under high pressure, using Ca₂IrO₄ + IrO₂ at 2.0 GPa and 1150 K for 48 h (CS4, CS5). F2 remains low because HP processing and multistep precursor preparation are required, and Li volatility/oxidation control add additional severity [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is lower than Route 1 because IrO₂ and preformed oxides are less reactive than peroxide/chlorate mixtures, and the route requires substantial reconstruction into the target Cmcm framework (CS4, CS5).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Topochemical Li-for-Na exchange

This route receives the best overall score because NaIrO₃ already provides the correct Cmcm IrO₆ framework (CS1), so the exchange step could avoid full reconstructive synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is moderate because the exchange itself is ambient pressure and low temperature, but the route still requires a high-pressure NaIrO₃ precursor and repeated salt-exchange/washing steps (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). F3 is favorable for structural reconstruction but uncertain for ion transport, since a dense 8-coordinate alkali site may not allow complete Na→Li exchange [Reasonable Inference from target/CS1 structures].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Thin-film route

Thin-film synthesis has the least direct evidence: no CS/SS/TS entry reports PLD, sputtering, or MBE of a Li–Ir–O post-perovskite, so direct support is insufficient from CS/SS/TS and neutral 50. F2 is comparatively high because it avoids bulk GPa pressure, but F3 is limited by uncertain crystallization, Li volatility, and narrow composition control [Reasonable Inference]. The route is plausible only as non-equilibrium stabilization inspired by the fact that bulk NaIrO₃/CaIrO₃ analogues require high pressure (CS1, CS4, CS5).

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains a large cation size/coordination mismatch between Li and Ir and has mixed edge/corner-sharing polyhedral connectivity, both of which increase kinetic frustration and amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference; target description]. The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms for this Cmcm ABO₃-like framework, so only two checks trigger [Reasonable Inference from target description and CS1 analogy]. The resulting F4 = 55 reflects medium risk: crystallinity is feasible, but likely needs pressure, epitaxy, or topochemical templating.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiIrO₃ in the specified Cmcm structure is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is essentially zero, but it may still behave experimentally as a pressure-stabilized or kinetically selected phase [Reasonable Inference from target hull and HP analogues CS1/CS4/CS5]. The strongest support comes from NaIrO₃, which is nearly identical structurally and chemically and has an established high-pressure synthesis (CS1). CaIrO₃ and LiOsO₃ analogues further support the broader idea that 5d oxides and post-perovskite iridates often need high-pressure synthesis (CS2–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the pre-existing Cmcm NaIrO₃ framework, making topochemical Li-for-Na exchange attractive (CS1; [Reasonable Inference]). The main limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control, especially maintaining Li content and Ir⁵⁺ while avoiding competing Li–Ir–O phases or residual Na [Reasonable Inference].
