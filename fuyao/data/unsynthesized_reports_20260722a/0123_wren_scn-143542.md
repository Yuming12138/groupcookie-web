### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s provided E_hull is −0.354321244 eV/atom, which is below the ≤0.05 eV/atom threshold and therefore receives 100/100 by the requested rubric. CS contains a very close sulfur-rich 0D analogue, AsI3(S)24, with E_hull = 0.0, supporting that sulfur-rich molecular co-crystals can be thermodynamic ground-state entries in the reference database (CS-1). TS analogues are also stable or near-stable, but they are chemically unrelated to Sn–S and therefore give insufficient evidence for actual Sn–S decomposition products; TS is neutral 50 for Sn–S phase-competition interpretation. The main uncertainty is that negative hull values are not normally expected in a completed phase diagram, so the value may reflect database/reference inconsistency [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS-1 uses slow evaporation at about 4 °C in simple round-bottom tubes. It still receives deductions because the method involves several manipulations and CS2 is a hazardous volatile solvent, while optional Sn halides would require dry handling analogous to CS-2 [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-2]. SS and TS do not improve or contradict this condition assessment because they lack Sn–S solution analogues; insufficient evidence from SS/TS, neutral 50. The remaining uncertainty is whether SnS2 can be mobilized sufficiently in such a solvent system [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because solution evaporation avoids long-range solid-state diffusion and CS-1 demonstrates assembly of sulfur-rich 0D molecular crystals from solution. Structural reconstruction is only moderate-to-favorable because the target contains SnS2 clusters whereas CS-1 contains arsenic triiodide molecules plus octathiocane, so the sulfur-hosting motif transfers better than the metal-sulfide motif [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 supports that 0D Sn-containing halide clusters can form by solution mixing under dry conditions, but it does not prove Sn–S cluster formation. SS/TS provide insufficient kinetic signal for this molecular Sn–S route and are neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because it requires a sealed evacuated ampoule and sulfur is volatile at the proposed reaction temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. Vacuum/sealed-tube methods are supported by CS-5, while SS/TS-4 also shows vacuum/dry-nitrogen decomposition approaches, but those are not Sn–S-specific. The route avoids >1000 °C and >5 GPa conditions, so the severity remains moderate rather than extreme [Reasonable Inference]. Uncertainty is dominated by sulfur vapor pressure and ampoule safety [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are moderate because sulfur melt/vapor can assist mass transport, but SnS2 powder may be poorly soluble/reactive and may not readily reorganize into discrete molecular SnS2 clusters [Reasonable Inference]. Flux/liquid transport is conceptually supported by CS-4/CS-5, although those examples are phosphides, not sulfides. Structural reconstruction is the largest kinetic penalty because a crystalline binary sulfide plus sulfur must reorganize into a 0D co-crystal [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient direct kinetic evidence for Sn–S cluster formation and are neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses sealed flux processing and sulfur excess, so it is penalized for controlled atmosphere, volatile sulfur, and multiple processing steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4 and CS-5 support tin flux growth in sealed ampoules and slow cooling, but their temperatures are much higher than desirable for the target molecular co-crystal. No >1000 °C penalty is applied because the proposed Sn–S adaptation should be kept at 250–400 °C [Reasonable Inference]. The major uncertainty is whether the flux can be operated at low enough temperature to avoid dense Sn–S phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Tin flux can strongly improve transport and crystallinity, as shown for RuP4/RuP4 polymorphs grown in liquid tin or tin flux (CS-4, CS-5). However, those CS examples also indicate phase-control challenges and polymorphism in flux-grown systems (CS-5), which lowers the structural-reconstruction score for this target. The target is a 0D sulfur-rich co-crystal, so higher-temperature flux conditions may favor SnS2/SnS instead of SnS2(S)12 [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct Sn–S flux evidence and are neutral 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, Sn, so the cation-size mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The large formula/cluster description likely corresponds to more than 40 atoms per cell, so C2 is triggered [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The target is explicitly zero-dimensional with discrete clusters rather than an extended mixed-connectivity polyhedral framework, so C3 is not triggered. The resulting amorphization risk is low-to-medium, with the main concern being molecular disorder rather than glass-forming mixed-network topology [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely predicted as a ground-state or very strongly stable phase because its reported E_hull is negative and well below the ≤0.05 eV/atom criterion, although the negative value should be treated cautiously [Reasonable Inference from target E_hull]. The best analogue support comes from CS-1, where a sulfur-rich 0D molecular co-crystal, AsI3(S)24, was synthesized by low-temperature slow evaporation from sulfur-containing CS2 solution. CS-2 adds indirect support for solution formation of 0D Sn-containing clusters under dry conditions, but it is chloride chemistry rather than sulfide chemistry. The critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature molecular assembly routes that can preserve discrete sulfur clusters, supported by CS-1. The critical limiting factor is controlling SnS2 cluster solubility/speciation and avoiding separation into ordinary SnS2 plus elemental sulfur [Reasonable Inference].
