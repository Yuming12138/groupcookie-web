### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.1674 eV/atom, placing it in the highest thermodynamic score bin; the negative value is treated as a strong predicted stability signal rather than a literal negative decomposition energy [Reasonable Inference from target data]. Synthesized low-hull analogues occur in CS, including WCl6 near hull and MoCl5 on hull (CS-1, CS-3), and in TS, where all listed analogues are low-hull and reported synthesized (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that hull stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility, especially for a sulfur-rich Re phase with S–S bonds [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 1 direct sealed ampoule.**  
Direct synthesis has relatively moderate condition severity because it avoids high pressure and temperatures above 1000 °C, but it requires vacuum/sealed ampoule conditions and volatile sulfur management [Reasonable Inference]; sealed direct WCl6 synthesis supports the concept (CS-1). Kinetics are weaker because Re metal is refractory and formation from elements requires complete reconstruction into ReS6 units [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 suggests Re chalcogen/halide phases can require long times and have sensitive phase windows, which lowers confidence for direct powder reaction.

**F2/F3 — Route 2 CVT / sulfur-rich transport.**  
This route has moderate condition severity because it requires evacuated sealed ampoules and possibly toxic/volatile halogen transport agents, as seen in chalcogen/halide CVT chemistry (CS-4) and Re ampoule chemistry (CS-2). Kinetics are better than direct reaction because vapor transport can bypass some Re solid-state diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and CS-4 demonstrates crystalline products from temperature gradients. The largest uncertainty is sulfur stoichiometry control for ReS6, since CS-2 shows competing phases when Re/chalcogen/halide compositions and temperatures vary.

**F2/F3 — Route 3 sulfurization of Re halides.**  
This route uses more reactive Re precursors, which improves precursor activity, and ReCl4 is directly evidenced as a Re precursor in chalcogen/halide ampoule reactions (CS-2). However, it is penalized for air-sensitive/toxic precursors, possible H2S or halide handling, chloride removal, and multiple processing steps [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the complexity of Mo chloride sealed reactions in CS-3. Kinetic uncertainty remains high because eliminating chloride while forming a binary ReS6 phase with S–S bonds may require major structural reconstruction [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a concern from the target formula; CS/SS/TS provide insufficient additional evidence for this check and are neutral 50. The described structure is simple—one Re site, one S site, ReS6 octahedra, and S–S bonds—so unit-cell complexity and mixed polyhedral connectivity appear low [Reasonable Inference from target description]. The remaining uncertainty is whether the crystallographic cell contains more atoms than implied by the formula, since the full CIF cell count is not provided.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ReS6 is likely predicted as a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin [Reasonable Inference from target data]. The chemical analogues support sealed-tube and sealed-ampoule synthesis of high-valent Re/W/Mo halide or chalcogen/halide compounds, especially through volatile-anion and transport chemistry (CS-1 to CS-4). The structural and thermodynamic analogues are less chemically relevant, so SS and TS mainly support general low-hull synthesizability rather than a specific Re–S synthesis pathway. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high sulfur chemical potential in a sealed or transport environment to stabilize sulfur-rich ReS6 with S–S bonds [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity, because Re chalcogen/halide analogues show slow formation, competing phases, and narrow reaction windows (CS-2).
