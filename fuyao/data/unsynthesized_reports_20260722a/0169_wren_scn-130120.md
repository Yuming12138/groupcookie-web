### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.043069703 eV/atom, which falls in the highest stability bin and is treated as a numerically stable/ground-state prediction from the target data. Chemically similar intermetallics Co₂HfAl, TaCo₂Al, Ti₄FeBi₂, and Ti₄CoBi₂ are all listed with E_hull = 0.0, supporting that related metallic ternaries can be thermodynamic phases (CS-1 to CS-4). SS and TS are not chemically matched to Hf–Al–Fe and therefore provide insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Hf–Al–Fe thermodynamics; they are neutral source signals.

#### F2 — Route 1: Arc melting + anneal

Route 1 requires very high local temperature because arc melting is used, and the Hf/Fe components are refractory [Reasonable Inference]; chemically similar Hf/Ta aluminides are synthesized by arc melting in purified Ar (CS-1, CS-2). The controlled atmosphere/vacuum penalty is justified because CS-1 and CS-2 explicitly use purified Ar and vacuum annealing. The remaining uncertainty is whether the target can be obtained directly after arc melting or requires extensive annealing, since CS-1 notes annealing was applied when samples were not single phase.

#### F3 — Route 1: Arc melting + anneal

The starting metals are low-activity dense precursors by solid-state standards [Reasonable Inference], but arc melting provides liquid-state mixing, which is strongly supported by CS-1 to CS-4. Diffusion after melting is mainly needed for ordering/homogenization, so kinetics are moderate rather than prohibitive [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is not directly matched to the Heusler CS structures, so the score is limited by uncertainty in ordering into the target P6₃/mmc phase.

#### F2 — Route 2: Powder metallurgy / sealed-tube sintering

Route 2 avoids the highest melting step but requires sealed/inert annealing, as CS-5 reports cold-pressed powders heated in inert atmosphere or sealed quartz tubes for 700 h at 850 °C. It also introduces powder-handling risks because Hf and Al powders are more air-sensitive/flammable than bulk pieces [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether 850–950 °C is sufficient for Hf–Fe–Al equilibration, because CS-5 demonstrates long annealing but not specifically Hf₂AlFe₃.

#### F3 — Route 2: Powder metallurgy / sealed-tube sintering

Solid-state kinetics are poor because elemental Hf and Fe have high melting points and low diffusion rates at 850–950 °C [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5’s 700 h at 850 °C supports that sealed powder sintering can work for related aluminide chemistry but also signals slow kinetics. Without a liquid phase or same-structure precursor, the reaction requires complete reconstruction from metals, making this the least favorable route kinetically.

#### F2 — Route 3: Arc melting + quench/anneal optimization

Route 3 has the same high-temperature and controlled-atmosphere demands as Route 1, with additional step complexity because quenching and anneal screening are added. CS-1 explicitly notes water quenching in some samples and vacuum annealing when arc-melted samples were not single phase. The uncertainty is whether quenching helps or hinders the target, because the target’s favorable E_hull suggests equilibrium annealing may be sufficient [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3: Arc melting + quench/anneal optimization

Liquid-state mixing keeps diffusion difficulty moderate, consistent with the arc-melted CS analogues (CS-1 to CS-4). Quench/anneal screening can improve phase selection if competing phases form, which is motivated by CS-1/CS-2 reports of phase-purity issues. However, quenching may trap disorder or metastable products, so the structural-ordering score remains moderate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with significantly different metallic sizes, triggering the radius-mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description explicitly includes corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral units, triggering the mixed-connectivity check from the target data. There is insufficient evidence that the unit cell contains >40 atoms; the single inequivalent Hf/Fe/Al sites and formula Hf₂AlFe₃ suggest a relatively compact intermetallic cell [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify amorphization tendency for this target; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for this subfactor beyond general route behavior.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Hf₂AlFe₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state intermetallic because the provided target E_hull is −0.043 eV/atom. The best synthesis support comes from CS, especially Hf–Al–transition-metal and refractory-metal aluminides made by arc melting under Ar followed by vacuum annealing (CS-1, CS-2); SS and TS are largely chemically unrelated and provide insufficient transferable route evidence. The most critical enabling factor is that metallic ternary aluminides with Hf/Ta/Zr-family elements are routinely accessible by melt processing and annealing (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). The main limiting factor is phase purity/order control, because CS-1/CS-2 note that annealing or quenching may be needed and some related compositions fail to form single-phase target structures. Overall, the target is reasonably synthesizable, with arc melting plus homogenization anneal as the preferred route.
