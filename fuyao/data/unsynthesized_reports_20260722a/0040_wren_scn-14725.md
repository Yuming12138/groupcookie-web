### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.07553 eV/atom, so it scores in the highest stability bin. Low-hull chemically similar fluorides are experimentally synthesized, including BaF₃ at 0.0 eV/atom, SrF₃ at 0.049 eV/atom, CsCuF₄ at 0.048 eV/atom, and Cs₂CuF₆ at 0.0086 eV/atom (CS-1 to CS-4). TS analogues also show synthesized low-hull phases, but they are chemically dissimilar, so TS provides only weak generic thermodynamic support (TS-1 to TS-5). The remaining uncertainty is that a negative computed hull does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or correct treatment of molecular/polyfluoride bonding [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 inherits harsh but precedented high-pressure HF conditions from CS-1/CS-2, which used 3 GPa and 573 K. The rubric does not penalize 3 GPa because the threshold is >5 GPa, but HF/F₂ toxicity and volatility significantly reduce the score. CS-1/CS-2 provide strong evidence for HF hydrothermal fluoride crystallization, while CS-3/CS-4 provide the separate need for high fluorine chemical potential in Cs fluorides. SS and TS provide insufficient direct chemical evidence for HF/F₂ hydrothermal synthesis of CsF₄.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable because solution/hydrothermal transport can bypass slow solid-state diffusion, and CS-1/CS-2 achieved crystals after only 30 min at 573 K. The structural transfer is only partial because the target has 1D edge-sharing CsF₆ octahedral ribbons, while CS-1/CS-2 have Sr/Ba 9-coordinate environments but do include water-like F sites. The main uncertainty is whether CsF₄ requires discrete polyfluoride bonding or a fluorine pressure not achieved by HF alone [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is strongly supported chemically by CsCuF₄ and Cs₂CuF₆ syntheses under high-pressure F₂ at 400 °C and ~350 bar (CS-3, CS-4). The temperature is moderate, but F₂ gas, sealed pressure, toxicity, and corrosion impose large practical penalties. CS-4 also reports long annealing and difficulty obtaining pure samples, which adds practical severity even if not fully captured by the rubric. SS/TS are insufficient for this specific fluorine-autoclave chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Gas-phase F₂ can strongly fluorinate CsF, and CS-3/CS-4 show Cs fluorides can be produced at 400 °C under F₂. However, conversion from CsF to a CsF₄ ribbon phase requires large anion uptake and structural reorganization [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4’s five-week anneal and reported difficulty preparing pure samples imply slow ordering and narrow phase stability. Thus the kinetics are moderate rather than excellent.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 reduces external pressure relative to Route 2 but still uses hazardous, volatile, moisture-sensitive fluorinating agents [Reasonable Inference]. The score is limited by sealed-system operation, reactive fluorinating atmosphere, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. No CS/SS/TS source directly demonstrates XeF₂ synthesis of CsF₄, so the route is an extrapolation from high-F₂-potential Cs fluoride synthesis in CS-3/CS-4. SS and TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this chemical route.

#### F3 — Route 3

The fluorinating-agent route should have high precursor activity because XeF₂/interhalogens can deliver fluorine at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. Low-temperature reaction may help trap a metastable or polyfluoride-rich phase [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic uncertainty is structural: no analogue confirms that CsF can reorganize into the Cmmm CsF₄ ribbon framework under these conditions. Therefore the score is good but not as high as the HF high-pressure route.

#### F2 — Route 4

Route 4 is mild in nominal temperature because SS-4/TS-4 show AuBr can be made by decomposition at 40–55 °C. However, for CsF₄ it would require prior synthesis of a fluorine-rich precursor, controlled decomposition, and management of volatile fluorine species [Reasonable Inference]. This triggers multi-step, controlled-atmosphere, volatile, and toxic-reagent deductions. The SS/TS support is structural but chemically weak.

#### F3 — Route 4

A topochemical route could be kinetically efficient if a compositionally close Cs polyfluoride precursor exists [Reasonable Inference]. SS-4/TS-4 show a 1D halide ribbon phase can be produced by controlled low-temperature decomposition, but AuBr is not chemically similar to CsF₄. The uncertainty is high because preformed CsF₄-like polyfluoride precursors are not identified in CS/SS/TS. Thus the route is plausible but less reliable than Routes 1–3.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation radius mismatch criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The target description lists one Cs site and two F sites and does not indicate a >40-atom cell, so the atom-count criterion is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is described as edge-sharing CsF₆ octahedra without simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so mixed connectivity is not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is that ribbon phases may still show stacking disorder, but the formal amorphization checks indicate low amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsF₄ is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or at least very close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.07553 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar high-fluorine systems: Sr/Ba fluoride phases crystallized in HF at 3 GPa/573 K, and Cs-containing fluorides were synthesized under high-pressure F₂ at 400 °C (CS-1 to CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high fluorine chemical potential, likely through HF plus F₂/XeF₂ or direct F₂ pressure [Reasonable Inference based on CS-3/CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is phase control, because CsF₄ is an unusual F-rich binary cesium fluoride and CS-4 reports that pure Cs fluoride-related samples can be difficult to obtain. Overall, the target is plausible but should be treated as a high-risk fluorine-chemistry synthesis rather than a routine solid-state reaction.
