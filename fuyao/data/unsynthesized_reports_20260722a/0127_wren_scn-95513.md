### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.0559 eV/atom, which places it in the highest stability bin and suggests a likely ground-state or overbound-near-ground-state phase within computational uncertainty (Target). The closest chemical analogue K₂S₅ is also on the hull, E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing that alkali polysulfides can be thermodynamically stable crystalline materials (CS-2). The Na–Cl analogue is high-pressure and above hull, and NaN₃ entries are chemically different; therefore CS-1 and CS-3/4/5 provide insufficient direct Na–S thermodynamic evidence and are treated as neutral-to-weak. SS/TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated but have low E_hull values, so they give only generic confidence that low-hull calculated phases can be accessible, not direct NaS₃ evidence (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is chemically well supported by K₂S₅, which used Schlenk-tube dry N₂ handling, H₂S saturation, sulfur addition, and ethanol recrystallization (CS-2). The score is reduced because the closest analogue requires controlled atmosphere, toxic H₂S if following the direct route, and multiple solution steps (CS-2). The uncertainty is that NaS₃ may not crystallize from ethanol with the same solubility behavior as K₂S₅ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because solution-phase polysulfide formation avoids solid-state diffusion, and K₂S₅ was formed through solution sulfur addition and recrystallization rather than prolonged high-temperature treatment (CS-2). The S–S bond lengths in target NaS₃, 1.97–2.05 Å, are close to those in K₂S₅, 2.05–2.08 Å, supporting transfer of polysulfide chain chemistry (CS-2; Target). The limiting kinetic issue is nucleating the exact monoclinic NaS₃ framework rather than a different sodium polysulfide stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The sealed-ampoule route avoids H₂S and extreme pressure, so its severity is moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Deductions come from the need for vacuum/Ar sealing, sulfur volatility, and air-sensitive Na₂S/polysulfides; the air-sensitivity concern is supported by K₂S₅ rapid air decomposition (CS-2). No CS/SS/TS source directly reports sealed-ampoule NaS₃ synthesis, so this route relies on [Reasonable Inference] from alkali polysulfide chemistry.

#### F3 — Route 2

Reaction kinetics are expected to be reasonably good because molten/soft sulfur-rich media can promote Na–S redistribution at modest temperature [Reasonable Inference]. K₂S₅ confirms that sulfur addition to alkali sulfide/hydrosulfide chemistry can produce crystalline polysulfides (CS-2). The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction and phase competition among Na₂Sₓ compounds, requiring slow cooling and stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The solvothermal/liquid-ammonia route is penalized for NH₃ pressure, autoclave equipment, Ar/glovebox loading, long reaction times, and hazardous reagents, all reflected in the Li₃Na(NH₂)₄ analogue conditions of 430–470 K, 1.7–3.4 kbar NH₃, and 4–20 days (SS-1/TS-1). It is less severe than GPa high-pressure synthesis but more specialized than solution or sealed-ampoule work (SS-1/TS-1). The SS/TS analogue is not a sulfide, so route transferability is uncertain.

#### F3 — Route 3

Kinetics may be favorable because ammonia solvates/reacts with alkali metals and produced well-crystallized alkali compounds in 4–20 days (SS-1/TS-1). However, SS-1/TS-1 gives insufficient direct evidence for sulfur-chain assembly or NaS₃ phase selectivity, so the structural reconstruction component is not high. This route is best viewed as a crystal-growth or exploratory route rather than the primary bulk synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

High-pressure synthesis is heavily penalized because the Na–Cl analogue required 10–80 GPa and laser heating in a DAC (CS-1). Such equipment and conditions are much more severe than those needed for the K₂S₅ solution analogue (CS-2). Because the target is already calculated below the hull, high pressure is not thermodynamically motivated except as a fallback for kinetic or polymorph access [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 4

Elemental Na and S are reactive, but the route must assemble the full NaS₃ framework under confined high-pressure conditions [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 explicitly notes high-pressure experiments and high temperatures to overcome kinetic barriers for unusual Na–Cl stoichiometries, suggesting phase control can be nontrivial (CS-1). The lack of direct Na–S high-pressure analogue evidence keeps this F3 modest.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Na, so cation-radius mismatch does not contribute to amorphization (Target). The reported structure contains edge- and corner-sharing motifs, but no face-sharing connectivity is stated, so the specified mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered (Target). Remaining uncertainty is the actual CIF unit-cell atom count, but formula and P2₁/c description suggest it is unlikely to exceed 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaS₃ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is negative and falls in the highest stability bin (Target). The strongest analogue support comes from K₂S₅, an experimentally synthesized alkali polysulfide with similar S–S bonding and air-sensitive behavior (CS-2). The most important enabling factor is the high chemical activity of sulfur-rich alkali polysulfide chemistry, which should allow formation at low to moderate temperature rather than requiring refractory solid-state diffusion (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity: sodium and sulfur can plausibly form multiple Na₂Sₓ/NaSₓ compositions, and the exact monoclinic NaS₃ framework is not directly demonstrated in the provided references [Reasonable Inference]. High-pressure data from Na–Cl shows unusual alkali stoichiometries can be made under extreme conditions, but it is a weak analogue for Na–S and should not be the first route (CS-1).
