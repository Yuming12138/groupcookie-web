### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.10248 eV/atom, so by the provided rubric it falls in the highest thermodynamic-stability category (target data). Chemically similar Pb–noble-metal oxides include PbPt₂O₄ and Pb₂Ir₂O₇ with E_hull = 0.0, supporting that Pb–late-transition-metal oxides can be ground-state or near-ground-state phases (CS-2, CS-4). SS and TS lists are largely chemically unrelated to Pb–Au–O, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for chemistry-specific stability transfer; they are treated as neutral 50 where chemistry matters.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses 550–680 °C and ambient air/O₂, which is comparable to Pb–Pt oxide syntheses at 600–720 °C in air (CS-1, CS-2). The deductions come from repeated heating/regrinding and Pb toxicity, since CS-1 and CS-2 both require regrinding and PbO is a toxic Pb precursor (CS-1, CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). There is no evidence from SS/TS that harsher conditions are needed for Pb–Au–O, so SS/TS are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solid-state reaction from PbO and noble-metal powder is credible because Pb₂PtO₄ and PbPt₂O₄ were made from PbO and Pt metal in air (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetics are penalized because noble-metal oxidation and cation ordering require repeated grinding and long heating, as shown in CS-1/CS-2. The square-planar PtO₄ environments in CS-2 support structural transfer to square-planar AuO₄, but the target topology is not identical, so structural reconstruction remains moderate-to-large (CS-2; target data).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 operates near 500–550 °C, matching the low-temperature Bi₂O₃ flux preparation of PdBi₂O₄ at 500 °C (CS-3). Its condition score is limited mainly by multi-step flux processing and toxic Pb handling, not by high temperature or high pressure (CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). There is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for flux synthesis of Pb–Au oxides, so SS/TS are neutral for this route.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Flux growth improves kinetics because CS-3 states that excess Bi₂O₃ flux was selected to avoid thermal decomposition of PdO and to grow single crystals. The target’s Au–O bond length of 2.05 Å is very close to the Pd–O bond length of 2.04 Å in CS-3, supporting local structural plausibility (CS-3; target data). The uncertainty is that CS-3 is Bi–Pd–O rather than Pb–Au–O, so phase selectivity and flux incorporation remain extrapolated [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 has mild temperature severity because CS-5 hydrothermal Bi₂Ir₂O₇ forms at 240 °C rather than high ceramic temperatures. It uses a sealed autoclave but not >5 GPa pressure, and CS-5 describes the route as one-step, so the main deduction is toxic/reactive Pb and oxidant handling (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS hydrothermal examples include chemically unrelated materials, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for Pb–Au–O hydrothermal transfer; they are neutral for chemistry-specific scoring.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Hydrothermal synthesis scores highly in precursor activity because CS-5 uses soluble/reactive precursors in concentrated NaOH with Na₂O₂. It also avoids the slow solid-state diffusion implied by CS-1/CS-2 repeated regrinding. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: CS-5 forms a pyrochlore iridate, not tetragonal Pb(AuO₂)₂, so the target framework must nucleate directly from solution [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines large 8-coordinate Pb and square-planar Au in a mixed edge/corner-sharing oxygen framework, which triggers ionic-size and connectivity checks (target data; [Reasonable Inference]). The target description explicitly reports edge- and corner-sharing OPb₂Au₂ tetrahedra, supporting a medium amorphization risk (target data). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS on amorphization behavior for this exact Pb–Au oxide, so the residual uncertainty is significant.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Pb(AuO₂)₂ is likely thermodynamically favorable or at least not strongly metastable because its reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar Pb–Pt/Pb–Ir oxides include ground-state examples (target data; CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS, especially Pb–Pt oxides made by air solid-state reaction and a flux-grown noble-metal oxide with similar M–O bond lengths (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). SS and TS provide little chemically specific support because the listed structurally/thermodynamically similar examples are mostly unrelated chemistries, so they are treated as neutral signals. The key enabling factor is the strong local plausibility of square-planar AuO₄ with Au–O ≈ 2.05 Å, close to Pt/Pd oxide analogues (CS-2, CS-3; target data). The key limiting factor is oxidation-state and phase control for Au in an oxide lattice, which is not directly demonstrated by the Pb–Au–O references and is therefore a [Reasonable Inference] risk.
