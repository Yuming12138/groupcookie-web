### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.0757 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable thermodynamic scoring range (Target). Alkali amide analogues NaNH₂ and Li₃Na(NH₂)₄ are also on the hull with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS2, TS1), supporting that related alkali pnictogen hydride frameworks can be stable. The remaining uncertainty is that no CS/SS/TS entry directly reports NaPH₂, so P-for-N substitution remains partly extrapolative **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids extreme temperature and high pressure because the closest analogue NaNH₂ forms by direct Na + NH₃ reaction at 300 °C (CS1). Deductions are driven by controlled atmosphere and hazardous precursors: CS1 required inert handling, and PH₃/Na hazards are inferred for the target route **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for direct PH₃ handling conditions, so CS1 is weighted most heavily.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable because CS1 demonstrates direct formation of the same Fddd NaXH₂ structural motif at moderate temperature (CS1, Target). Molten Na plus a gaseous hydride source should provide high contact reactivity **[Reasonable Inference]**. The key uncertainty is whether PH₃ reacts cleanly like NH₃ or instead forms Na phosphides or disproportionation products **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
High-pressure NH₃ analogues require kbar autoclaves, long times, and inert handling: Li/Na/NH₃ used 1.7–3.4 kbar for 4–20 days (CS2, SS1, TS1), and Rb/Ca/NH₃ used 5000 bar for 17 days (CS5). These are below the >5 GPa scoring penalty but still practically demanding (CS2, CS5). PH₃ substitution adds toxicity and volatility risk **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The solvothermal/high-pressure medium should reduce diffusion limitations because analogous amides crystallized well under 430–573 K conditions (CS2, CS5, SS1, TS1). However, the closest high-pressure structural analogue is Li₃Na(NH₂)₄ rather than exact Fddd NaNH₂, so structural transfer is less direct than Route 1 (CS2, SS1, TS1). Insufficient evidence from TS2–TS5 for chemical transferability to Na–P–H.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Sealed-ampoule hydride synthesis is supported by Ca₂H₃Br formation in silica-jacketed Nb ampoules under Ar (CS4). The proposed NaH/P route avoids very high temperature but still requires sealed/inert handling and manages volatile/reactive components **[Reasonable Inference]**. Insufficient direct evidence from CS/SS/TS for NaH + P + H₂ forming NaPH₂, so the route is scored more cautiously.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The solid-state route has weaker kinetics because NaH and P must reconstruct into P–H anions and the Fddd Na–P framework **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS4 shows sealed hydride reactions are feasible, but its Ca–H–Br framework is structurally unlike NaPH₂, so the analogy is limited (CS4, Target). The main uncertainty is competing Na–P binary formation and incomplete hydrogenation **[Reasonable Inference]**.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Na, so there is no cation size mismatch trigger (Target). The structure is simple compared with large-cell complex frameworks, and the closest NaNH₂ Fddd analogue has a 16-atom described cell (CS1), while no >40 atom target cell is indicated (Target). The target has edge- and corner-sharing NaP₄ tetrahedra but no face-sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not fully triggered (Target).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaPH₂ is predicted to be a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative, −0.0757 eV/atom (Target). The synthesis case is strongly supported by the Fddd NaNH₂ analogue, which has nearly identical connectivity and was synthesized directly from molten Na and NH₃ at 300 °C (CS1). Additional support comes from high-pressure alkali amide syntheses that produce well-crystallized products from alkali metals and NH₃ under controlled gas conditions (CS2, CS5, SS1, TS1). The most important enabling factor is the close structural match between NaPH₂ and NaNH₂ (CS1, Target). The main limiting factor is that all practical P–H routes require extrapolation from NH₃ to PH₃, and PH₃ is highly toxic/volatile while competing sodium phosphides may form **[Reasonable Inference]**.
