### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.08696489 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability bin. Chemical analogues Sr₂RuO₄, Na₃OsO₅, and Rb₂RuO₄ are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the plausibility of stable alkali/alkaline-earth late-transition-metal oxides (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). Thermodynamic references also include hull-stable or near-hull materials, but they are chemically unrelated in several cases, so TS gives stability-context support but limited chemical guidance (TS-1 to TS-5). The main uncertainty is that a negative hull value may reflect calculation/reference artifacts rather than an experimentally known ground state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because Na₃OsO₅ forms at 400 °C under O₂ flow and Rb/K ruthenates form at 625–780 °C in sealed/noble-metal environments, avoiding >1000 °C but requiring reactive alkali oxidants and multistep handling (CS-3, CS-4). Kinetics are moderate-to-poor because peroxide reagents are reactive, but IrO₂/Ir diffusion and oxidation-state equilibration are likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. CS-4’s 45–62 day syntheses indicate that related alkali ruthenates can require very long equilibration. The uncertainty is whether Ir can be oxidized into the target IrO₆ environment without forming lower iridates or IrO₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 has acceptable operational simplicity because ceramic oxide routes in air are well established for Sr₂RuO₄ and Ca₃WO₆ using standard furnaces, grinding, and reheating (CS-2, CS-5). Its condition score is penalized for high temperatures and repeated heating, consistent with 1200–1300 °C for Sr₂RuO₄ and 800–1350 °C for Ca₃WO₆ (CS-2, CS-5). Kinetics are poor because K₂CO₃/IrO₂ are less reactive than peroxide or solution precursors, and K loss is expected at high temperature [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether high oxygen chemical potential is sufficient in air/O₂ to stabilize the implied high Ir oxidation state [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 scores best on condition severity because K₃TaO₈ is crystallized simply from KOH/H₂O₂ by cooling to 0 °C, with no high pressure, furnace, or special atmosphere reported (CS-1). Kinetically, solution chemistry provides excellent mixing and avoids solid-state diffusion, as demonstrated in CS-1. The main penalty is structural transferability: CS-1 forms K₃[Ta(O₂)₄] with TaO₈/peroxide coordination, whereas the target has cubic K/Ir octahedral/cuboctahedral oxide connectivity. Thus Route 3 is a good discovery route for potassium iridate/peroxo chemistry, but exact K₃IrO₆ formation remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 balances flux-enhanced kinetics with high oxygen and K activity, drawing from alkali ruthenate synthesis in sealed ampoules with alkali peroxide/superoxide reagents (CS-4). Its condition severity is penalized for sealed oxidizing alkali media, air-sensitive oxidants, and multistep flux handling, but it avoids >1000 °C [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. Kinetically, the flux should improve cation transport relative to Route 2 [Reasonable Inference], and CS-4 confirms that alkali ruthenates can crystallize at 625–780 °C. The uncertainty is the likely narrow stability window and possible need for very long dwell times, as CS-4 reports 45–62 day reactions.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between K and Ir sites, and the structure explicitly separates large K cuboctahedral sites from compact IrO₆ octahedra [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target contains both corner-sharing and face-sharing motifs, but no edge-sharing is described, so the strict mixed-connectivity amorphization criterion is not fully triggered. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for the exact unit-cell atom count, so C2 is scored neutral/not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low-to-medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₃IrO₆ appears thermodynamically favorable by the reported negative E_hull, so it is more likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase than a highly metastable phase, although the negative hull value should be interpreted cautiously [Reasonable Inference]. The chemical analogues support synthesis of alkali-rich oxidized 5d oxides using peroxide/superoxide or oxidizing alkali media, especially Na₃OsO₅ and Rb₂RuO₄/K₂RuO₄ (CS-3, CS-4). The strongest enabling factor is the availability of highly oxidizing K-rich reagents/fluxes that can simultaneously supply potassium and oxygen chemical potential (CS-3, CS-4). The strongest limiting factor is controlling the Ir oxidation state and K stoichiometry without forming IrO₂, Ir metal, lower potassium iridates, or hydrated/peroxo side phases [Reasonable Inference]. Route 3 is experimentally mild and attractive for initial screening, but Route 4 may be more realistic for crystallizing the exact anhydrous cubic oxide.
