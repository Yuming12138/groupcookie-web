### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.08493 eV/atom, so it is scored as a ground-state-level phase under the provided thermodynamic criterion. Chemically similar fluorides CdPtF6, NiPbF6, NiPtF6, Ni(AuF4)2, and Zn(AuF4)2 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, reinforcing that related noble/late-transition-metal fluorides can be thermodynamically stable (CS1–CS5). SS and TS are mostly non-fluoride materials and therefore provide insufficient evidence for AgIrF6 chemical stability; they are treated as neutral rather than negative.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids ultra-high temperature and multi-GPa pressure, since the best direct analogue NiPbF6 forms at 400–500 °C in F2 stream (CS2). The major severity penalties are the reactive/toxic F2 atmosphere and moisture-sensitive fluoride precursors [Reasonable Inference from CS2, CS4]. Uncertainty remains because AgIrF6 may require a higher fluorine chemical potential than NiPbF6 [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are moderately favorable because the target has the same R-3 Upper-Bainite-derived octahedral framework as the direct-fluorination NiPbF6 analogue (CS2). The use of fluoride precursors and F2 should be more reactive than starting from oxides [Reasonable Inference], but cation ordering involving Ir may still be slow due to refractory Ir chemistry [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the score is moderate-high but not excellent.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is more severe because CS4 uses 350–400 atm F2, prefluorination, and pressure-autoclave processing for Upper-Bainite Pt fluoride analogues. Although this is not >5 GPa, high-pressure F2 is an extreme safety and equipment burden (CS4). Sealed noble-metal tube annealing further adds process complexity (CS1, CS4).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The high F2 pressure and longer treatment should improve conversion and oxidation control, as shown for PtNiF6-related compounds (CS4). However, the need for prefluorination and annealing in analogues suggests that phase ordering and crystal growth are not rapid (CS1, CS4). The same structure type gives strong structural-transfer support, but kinetic complexity remains.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 operates at relatively low temperature, around 300 °C, as in the AHF solvothermal Ni[AuF4]2 synthesis (CS3). The penalties arise from anhydrous HF volatility/toxicity, sealed Monel autoclave requirements, glovebox loading, and air-sensitive fluorides (CS3). It is conditionally attractive but hazardous.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
AHF should improve mass transport and can support crystal growth of transition-metal fluorides at low temperature, as demonstrated by CS3. The weakness is that CS3 does not have the same R-3 Upper-Bainite framework, so nucleation of AgIrF6 is not structurally guaranteed. This route receives the best kinetic score but poorer transferability than Route 1.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only corner-sharing octahedra, matching simple Upper-Bainite-derived analogues and avoiding mixed edge/face/vertex connectivity (CS1, CS2, CS4). The major amorphization risk is the large Ag–F versus Ir–F bond-length difference, which implies a significant cation-size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The small formula and simple single-site description reduce concern for glassy disorder [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

AgIrF6 is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the provided E_hull is negative and chemically related fluoride analogues are reported on the hull (CS1–CS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1, CS2, and CS4, which contain the same Upper-Bainite-derived R-3 corner-sharing octahedral framework as the target. The most important enabling factor is the structural match to known MF6 fluorides that can be made under F2-rich conditions (CS1, CS2, CS4). The most important limiting factor is redox/phase control involving Ag fluoride and Ir fluoride chemistry under highly oxidizing fluorination conditions [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide chemically relevant fluoride synthesis evidence, so they neither strongly help nor strongly harm the assessment.
