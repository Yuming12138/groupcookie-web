### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.040280777 eV/atom, placing it within the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin and justifying a high F1 score from the target data. CS analogues include stable or near-stable arsenides, e.g., Ca₂CdAs₂ polymorphs at 0.0 and 0.0215 eV/atom and Na₂LiGaAs₂ at 0.0 eV/atom (CS1–CS2, CS4). TS entries also show experimentally reported low-hull compounds in the 0–0.026 eV/atom range, supporting the general idea that low-hull phases are synthesizable, but TS chemistry is not directly relevant to Na–Pd–As and is therefore only weak supporting evidence (TS1–TS5). Remaining uncertainty comes from possible DFT convex-hull errors and missing competing Na–Pd–As phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Flux growth uses moderate high temperatures, 700–960 °C in chemically similar arsenides and 760 °C in Na flux (CS1–CS3), so no >1000 °C deduction was applied. Deductions arise from sealed/inert handling, volatile Na/As, multi-step flux cooling/decanting, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors, all consistent with CS arsenide flux protocols and Na-flux Nb containment (CS1–CS3). SS/TS do not provide chemically useful severity evidence for this route and are neutral 50 (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Flux improves kinetics because molten Cd/Sn/Pb or Na enables transport and crystal growth in analogous arsenides/intermetallics (CS1–CS3). Pd’s high melting point would normally hinder diffusion, but a liquid flux should reduce the need for long-range solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural transfer is partial because CS4 has Na–As/M–As coordination similarities but not the target Pd–Pd/As–As network, leaving moderate uncertainty (CS4).

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The sealed Nb ampoule route follows CS4, where Na₂LiGaAs₂ was synthesized from Na₃As/LiAl/As at 1275 K, approximately 1002 °C (CS4). This triggers the >1000 °C penalty, and the use of Na₃As/As in a sealed system triggers controlled-atmosphere, volatility, and toxic/air-sensitive deductions (CS4). **F3 — Route 2.**  
Na₃As/NaAs should be reactive toward Na–As framework formation because Na₃As is used successfully in CS4, but Pd metal remains kinetically sluggish [Reasonable Inference]. The reaction lacks a flux transport medium, so diffusion through solid Pd/Pd–As intermediates may limit completion [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction is significant because the target’s Pd-centered cuboctahedral connectivity differs from the Li/Ga arsenide analogue in CS4.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The binary-arsenide solid-state route avoids temperatures above 1000 °C by targeting 650–850 °C [Reasonable Inference from CS1–CS3], but still requires sealed inert/vacuum containment due to Na/As volatility. It is multi-step because binaries must be made or handled, ground, pelletized, annealed, and likely reground [Reasonable Inference]. CS and SS examples show that repeated thermal processing is common for difficult solid-state/flux materials, but direct evidence for this exact route is insufficient from SS/TS and treated as neutral 50 (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Using binary arsenides should improve precursor activity over elements because Pd–As and Na–As bonds are preformed [Reasonable Inference]. However, solid-state diffusion remains difficult for Pd-containing intermediates, and no CS entry demonstrates direct low-temperature solid-state synthesis of Na₂PdAs₂. The target’s mixed edge/corner/face-sharing cuboctahedra imply substantial reconstruction from binaries, lowering the kinetics score [Reasonable Inference from target structure].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂PdAs₂ is likely to be a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0403 eV/atom, and chemically similar arsenides in CS are experimentally known at low hull energies (CS1–CS2, CS4–CS5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS rather than SS/TS: CS1–CS3 establish flux growth of arsenides and Na-containing intermetallics, while CS4 establishes sealed Nb ampoule chemistry for Na arsenides. The most critical enabling factor is the use of a molten flux or sealed Nb/Ta ampoule to control Na/As volatility and enhance Pd dissolution/transport (CS1–CS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase control among competing Na–As, Pd–As, and possible Na–Pd–As binaries, especially because CS1–CS2 report polymorphism and flux/temperature-sensitive arsenide phase formation. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with flux growth being the most promising route for first crystals and sealed binary reactions useful for powder confirmation.
