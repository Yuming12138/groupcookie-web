### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.00234 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull, giving a very high thermodynamic score. The closest thermodynamic analogue CaAu5 has E_hull = 0.0 and very high TS similarity, while EuAu5, KAu5, RbAu5, and Ca(Au2Cd)2 are also listed as stable in TS. CS and SS also contain multiple stable Au-rich intermetallics, especially CaAu5, giving consistent support. Remaining uncertainty is that computational hull stability does not guarantee the specific monoclinic C2/c polymorph is the experimentally accessible low-temperature form.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is experimentally grounded in CaAu5 synthesis from pressed Ca/Au pellets heated near 1000 °C in evacuated quartz tubes. The main penalties are the evacuated sealed environment, multiple processing steps, and Ca air sensitivity/volatility [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide no high-pressure requirement, so high pressure is not penalized. The uncertainty is whether CaAu6 requires slightly above 1000 °C or a narrower annealing window than CaAu5.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Pressed elemental metals have modest precursor activity because Au is dense and diffusion can be slow [Reasonable Inference]. However, CaAu5 synthesis near 1000 °C shows that Ca–Au intermetallic formation is kinetically feasible in the binary system. The target’s Ca–Au and Au–Au bond distances are close to those in CaAu5, supporting structural transferability, but monoclinic ordering may require longer annealing or controlled cooling.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses higher-temperature melt or induction processing, supported by CaAu5 arc melting and Ca(Au2Cd)2 sealed Ta-tube high-frequency heating. It is penalized for >1000 °C operation, sealed-system use due to Ca volatility [Reasonable Inference], multiple steps, and air-sensitive Ca. It avoids high-pressure synthesis and avoids H₂/NH₃/vacuum under the strict scoring table. The main uncertainty is whether the CaAu6 phase survives melt cooling or requires low-temperature ordering.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

Melt/induction processing improves homogenization and diffusion relative to solid-state sintering; this is supported by Ca(Au2Cd)2 synthesis, where short high-temperature treatment plus annealing produced Ca–Au-containing intermetallics. CaAu5 arc-melting evidence further supports melt access to Ca–Au phases. Structural reconstruction is not minimal because Ca(Au2Cd)2 is ternary/tetragonal and the target is binary/monoclinic, but CaAu5 provides strong supplemental similarity. The remaining uncertainty is competing formation of CaAu5 or other Ca–Au phases during cooling.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is based on EuAu5 direct melt synthesis in sealed Mo crucibles followed by annealing, with additional support from CaAu5 and Ca(Au2Cd)2. It receives similar severity penalties to Route 2 because it uses >1000 °C melting, sealed containment for volatile/reactive Ca [Reasonable Inference], multiple thermal steps, and air-sensitive Ca. It does not require high pressure. The route is somewhat more operationally demanding because EuAu5 evidence included thermal cycling and long annealing.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Melt homogenization should strongly reduce diffusion barriers, as shown by EuAu5 synthesis using heating to the melting point and shaking. However, EuAu5 also required long annealing and showed phase-control difficulty, so structural ordering kinetics are scored lower than for Route 2. The target’s closest analogue remains CaAu5, so the EuAu5 route is a transferable but less direct precedent. The uncertainty is whether the monoclinic CaAu6 structure forms by equilibrium annealing or needs a kinetic quench.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a dense binary intermetallic with only Ca and Au, and the provided target structure shows well-defined Ca–Au and Au–Au bonding, which argues against strong glass-forming disorder. One amorphization check is triggered by the large Ca/Au size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not provide the unit-cell atom count or explicit mixed polyhedral sharing; although several CS/SS/TS analogues show edge/corner/face-sharing polyhedra, that cannot be directly assigned to the target. Thus F4 is favorable but not maximal.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CaAu6 is likely to be thermodynamically stable or very near ground-state, because its reported E_hull is −0.00234 eV/atom and the nearest TS analogues are stable. The CS/SS/TS support is strong, especially because CaAu5 is simultaneously the closest chemical, structural, and thermodynamic analogue and has an established elemental synthesis route. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable Au-rich Ca–Au intermetallic chemistry accessible by direct elemental reactions. The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity: CaAu6 is close in composition to CaAu5, so small Ca loss or off-stoichiometry could favor the known CaAu5 phase instead [Reasonable Inference]. A sealed Ta-tube melt/anneal route is recommended because it balances homogenization, Ca retention, and post-melt ordering.
