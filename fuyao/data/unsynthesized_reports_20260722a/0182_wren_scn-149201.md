### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has an energy above hull of **−0.035062346 eV/atom**, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability scoring bin. A negative hull value is treated as effectively on the convex hull within numerical uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues YFe6Ga6, TbFe6Ga6, DyFe6Ga6, and ErFe6Ga6 are reported with **E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom**, supporting that this structure family can be thermodynamically stable (CS1, CS3–CS5). SS and TS provide insufficient chemically specific stability evidence for Y–Cu–Ga beyond listing several near-hull or stable unrelated compounds, so their direct signal is neutral: insufficient evidence from SS; insufficient evidence from TS.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate annealing temperature, **800 °C**, and no high pressure, consistent with CS1 and CS3–CS5. The severity penalties come from evacuated sealed-tube processing, possible Ga volatility, multiple operations, and air-sensitive Y handling [Reasonable Inference supported by CS sealed Ar/vacuum procedures]. The route is not extreme, but it is more demanding than a simple ceramic calcination because arc melting and sealed ampoule annealing are required (CS1, CS3–CS5). SS and TS give insufficient evidence for this exact Y–Cu–Ga arc-melt route and are treated as neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by arc melting, which gives a chemically mixed alloy button before annealing [Reasonable Inference], and CS analogues use remelting/annealing to achieve homogeneity (CS1, CS5). The target has the same **Immm** structure type as YFe6Ga6 and several REFe6Ga6 analogues, so structural reconstruction is favorable (CS1, CS3–CS5). The main kinetic concern is the long **120 h** anneal, implying slow ordering or homogenization (CS1, CS3–CS5). SS and TS do not add relevant kinetic guidance for this target chemistry.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 benefits from no vacuum requirement in the cited induction-melting step and an anneal at **1000 °C**, which is not above the scoring threshold (CS2). Deductions are assigned for multi-step melting/annealing, Ga retention risk, and Y air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. CS2 confirms induction melting in Ar is viable for Y–M–Ga intermetallics, but the route is less structurally specific than Route 1. SS and TS are neutral because their examples do not describe related Y–Cu–Ga induction-melted gallides.

#### F3 — Route 2

Induction melting improves atomic mixing and diffusion relative to purely solid-state reaction, as shown for Y–Ni–Ga samples in CS2. However, CS2’s **YNi3Ga2** is hexagonal **P6/mmm**, not target **Immm**, so structural selectivity is less certain. The route may form competing Y–Cu–Ga phases unless annealing and composition are tuned [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is higher than Route 1 because no same-structure Cu analogue is provided.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is extrapolated and therefore carries higher uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. It likely requires sealed or inert processing and handling of large excess molten Ga, which adds volatility/containment and decanting complexity [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS source directly reports Ga-flux growth for this family, so the direct evidence is insufficient from CS for flux specifically, insufficient from SS, and insufficient from TS. Its operational severity is higher than Route 2 because of flux decanting and possible >1000 °C dissolution.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth is kinetically attractive because a liquid Ga medium should enhance diffusion and promote crystal growth [Reasonable Inference]. CS evidence that related RE/TM/Ga intermetallics form after melt processing supports the chemical plausibility of liquid-assisted synthesis, though not flux growth specifically (CS1–CS5). The main kinetic uncertainty is whether the desired **Y(CuGa)6 Immm** stoichiometry crystallizes from Ga-rich solution rather than Cu/Ga-rich competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide useful route-specific kinetic signal.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains large Y with smaller Cu/Ga atoms, implying significant size mismatch [Reasonable Inference], and the target structure contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity. These features raise ordering and glass/amorphous trapping risk relative to a simple binary alloy [Reasonable Inference]. However, chemically similar **Immm** REFe6Ga6 phases are crystalline and reported stable/synthesized, which reduces concern (CS1, CS3–CS5). The unit-cell atom count trigger cannot be confidently applied because the provided text gives the formula but not the total cell atom count; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state intermetallic because its reported hull energy is negative, **−0.035 eV/atom** [Reasonable Inference from target thermodynamics]. The strongest support comes from CS: **YFe6Ga6** and multiple **REFe6Ga6** analogues are same-space-group **Immm** rare-earth transition-metal gallides and are reported as stable, arc-melt/annealed phases (CS1, CS3–CS5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of a directly transferable **RE(TM,Ga)6 Immm** framework that tolerates rare-earth and transition-metal substitution [Reasonable Inference from CS1, CS3–CS5]. The most critical limiting factor is phase/stoichiometry control in the Y–Cu–Ga ternary, especially Ga loss and possible competing Cu–Ga or Y–Ga intermetallics [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS offer little direct chemical guidance because their nearest examples are chemically unrelated, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory.
