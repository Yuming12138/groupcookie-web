### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.075652944 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin. Chemically similar fluorides in CS all have E_hull = 0.0, including same-topology CsReF6 and CsSbF6, supporting stability of this CsMF6 family (CS-1, CS-5). TS materials are also near-hull, but they are chemically unrelated; therefore TS gives insufficient chemistry-specific evidence and is treated as neutral for fluorometallate transferability.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild because CS-1 and CS-5 use slow evaporation rather than high temperature, high pressure, or exotic equipment. The only major severity deduction is HF/W-fluoride toxicity and likely moisture sensitivity, which is a [Reasonable Inference] based on the required fluoride chemistry and the HF use in CS-1/CS-3/CS-5. SS/TS do not provide useful evidence for this route because their chemistries are unrelated, so they are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

The reaction kinetics should be favorable because CS-1 and CS-5 directly crystallize same R-3 CsMF6 frameworks from solution. Solution transport removes the refractory diffusion limitation that would be expected for tungsten in a solid-state route [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether W maintains the needed oxidation state without forming oxyfluoride or other W fluoride complexes.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because CS-2 uses sealed Pt tubes under Ar at 600 °C for four weeks, and CS-4 uses welded Pt ampoules/high vacuum at 750–1150 °C. Although the recommended target route can likely stay below 1000 °C [Reasonable Inference], sealed volatile W fluoride chemistry, inert/vacuum handling, and possible precursor preparation justify strong deductions. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific fluoride evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2

Fluoride solid-state reactions can work, as demonstrated by RbHfF5 and BaCrF5 formation from binary fluorides (CS-2, CS-4). However, CS-2 reports by-product/side-product formation and a four-week anneal, indicating slow ordering or narrow phase windows. The target’s close structural match to CS-1/CS-5 helps, but Route 2 lacks a direct same-structure solid-state precedent.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is less severe than Route 2 because it uses low-temperature HF solution chemistry, consistent with CS-3 and CS-5, but more severe than open evaporation because sealed HF introduces pressure and containment hazards [Reasonable Inference]. No GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required, so those penalties do not apply. Toxic/corrosive HF and possible W fluoride volatility remain the main severity penalties.

#### F3 — Route 3

HF solvothermal crystallization should have good kinetics because CS-3 demonstrates fluorometallate crystallization from 5 M/48% HF over days, and CS-1/CS-5 show CsMF6-type crystallization from acidic fluoride solution. The high fluoride activity should favor WF6 octahedral formation [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is redox/speciation of tungsten under sealed HF conditions.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between 12-coordinate Cs and octahedral W, and it contains mixed corner/edge/face-sharing connectivity in the described CsF12/WF6 framework. These two triggered checks give a medium amorphization risk score. However, same-topology crystalline CsReF6 and CsSbF6 exist (CS-1, CS-5), so the amorphization risk is not prohibitive.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsWF6 is likely thermodynamically stable or very near a ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is −0.075652944 eV/atom, and chemically similar fluorides in CS are on the hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from CsReF6 and CsSbF6, which share the same R-3 CsMF6 structure and are made by slow evaporation in acidic fluoride media (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of nearly isostructural CsMF6 analogues with very similar Cs–F and M–F coordination environments (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is tungsten fluoride redox/speciation control, since CS does not directly demonstrate CsWF6 and W can form multiple fluoride/oxyfluoride species [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not add useful chemistry-specific support, but they do not contradict the favorable thermodynamic assessment.
