### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Li₂SiSe₃ has E_hull = −0.012448988 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable within computational precision and merits the maximum score under the provided rubric. The chemically similar Li₂TeSe₃ and Li₂TeS₃ analogues are also near/on the hull, supporting the stability of Li-rich ternary chalcogenides with A₂MQ₃ stoichiometry. (CS-1, CS-4) SS and TS analogues are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient useful signal for Li–Si–Se thermodynamics and are treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is not thermally severe because the closest Li₂TeSe₃/Li₂TeS₃ analogues form at 460 °C, far below 1000 °C. (CS-1, CS-4) The score is reduced because CS requires Ar glovebox work, evacuated ampoules, sealed handling of volatile chalcogen, and air-sensitive Li. (CS-1, CS-4) The uncertainty is whether Si substitution requires a higher temperature or longer anneal than Te analogues. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The direct elemental route benefits from reactive Li and Se, but elemental Si is less reactive than preformed Si–Se reagents, lowering precursor activity. [Reasonable Inference] The success of Li₂TeSe₃ at 460 °C indicates that chalcogenide formation can proceed at moderate temperature in a sealed ampoule, but the target requires full construction of a tetrahedral Li/Si/Se network from elements. (CS-4) Uncertainty remains because no CS entry directly demonstrates Li₂SiSe₃ or a Si-containing Li selenide.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses the same sealed-ampoule and inert-handling framework as CS-5 and therefore avoids extreme temperature or pressure. (CS-5) Deductions arise from moisture-sensitive binary selenides, volatile selenium chemistry, evacuated ampoule use, and multiple preparative steps. (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]) The main uncertainty is whether SiSe₂ is available in suitable purity or must be synthesized separately. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary selenides should improve kinetics by providing preformed Li–Se and Si–Se bonding, analogous to Li₂S/Sb₂S₃ use in lithium thio-compound synthesis. (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]) Diffusion difficulty should be lower than in elemental synthesis because the chemical reaction no longer depends on direct conversion of Si metal to Si–Se units. [Reasonable Inference] Structural reconstruction is still nontrivial because the target Cc enargite-like network must be assembled. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits sealed evacuated ampoule and glovebox requirements from LiI-flux Li₃AsS₃ synthesis. (CS-5) It avoids high temperature and high pressure but still incurs deductions for vacuum/sealed handling, volatile selenium chemistry, multi-step flux processing, and air-sensitive/toxic materials. (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]) The added flux separation step makes the route operationally more complex despite favorable temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The LiI flux precedent for Li₃AsS₃ supports improved crystallization and ion transport in lithium chalcogenides. (CS-5) Flux should enhance Li mobility, promote local dissolution/reprecipitation, and improve ordering of the tetrahedral Li₂SiSe₃ framework. [Reasonable Inference] The main uncertainty is whether Li₂SiSe₃ is stable against LiI incorporation or flux-induced formation of competing iodide/selenide phases. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure consists of corner-sharing LiSe₄ and SiSe₄ tetrahedra without described edge- or face-sharing, which lowers amorphization risk. The main triggered risk is the large Li⁺/Si⁴⁺ cation size/charge mismatch in tetrahedral sites. [Reasonable Inference] SS and TS offer insufficient useful signal for amorphization of Li–Si–Se because their structures and chemistries are unrelated; they are neutral for this factor.
