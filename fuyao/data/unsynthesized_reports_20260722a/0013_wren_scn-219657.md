### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.144368067 eV/atom, so it is scored in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin with F1 = 98. The negative hull value is interpreted as a strong ground-state prediction, although negative values usually reflect reference/DFT correction artifacts rather than physical energy below the true convex hull [Reasonable Inference]. CS supports plausibility because the closest chemical/structural analogue Na5OsO6 has E_hull = 0.0 and Na4IrO4 also has E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1, CS-4). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for target-specific thermodynamic stability, because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is moderate because Na5OsO6 was synthesized at only 750 °C for 12 h under O2, not at ultrahigh temperature or pressure (CS-1). Deductions are applied for dry O2/peroxide oxidizing conditions, multi-step pre-reaction plus tempering, and air/moisture-sensitive Na peroxide chemistry (CS-1). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is warranted because CS-1 used ambient O2 and 750 °C. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ir requires more oxidizing or longer conditions than Os to stabilize Na5IrO6 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Kinetics are helped by Na2O2, which is chemically reactive and directly successful for isostructural Na5OsO6 (CS-1). Kinetics are limited by Ir being a refractory noble metal if Ir metal is used [Reasonable Inference]. The structural-reconstruction score is high because the target and Na5OsO6 share the same A5MO6 Caswellsilverite-like C2/m framework with alkali and transition-metal octahedra (CS-1 and target structure). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this Na–Ir–O kinetic pathway, so SS/TS are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because sealed Au/Ag flux growth adds volatile alkali containment, pressure buildup, and longer processing [Reasonable Inference]. The use of sealed gold tubes for Na5OsO6 crystal work and sealed Ag/quartz systems for Na4IrO4 supports the need for specialized containment in alkali-rich Os/Ir oxides (CS-1, CS-4). The temperature remains moderate, near 725–800 °C, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied (CS-1, CS-4). The main uncertainty is the compatibility of peroxide-rich flux with Ag/Au liners under strongly oxidizing conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux-assisted Na2O/Na2O2 chemistry should improve mass transport relative to dry diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and related Na-rich Os/Ir oxides were made in alkali oxide/peroxide environments (CS-1, CS-4). Ir diffusion remains intrinsically slow, but sealed long annealing and flux transport partly compensate [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. Structural access is good because Route 2 deliberately combines the isostructural Na5OsO6 chemistry with known Na–Ir–O alkali oxide exchange chemistry (CS-1, CS-4). SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this route.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 avoids very high temperature and GPa pressure because the Na4IrO4 exchange analogue used 800 °C under Ar (CS-4). It is penalized for sealed containment, multiple precursor/reaction steps, and air-sensitive Na2O/Na-rich oxide handling (CS-4). The long 71-day anneal in CS-4 indicates practical severity, although duration is not directly listed as an F2 deduction. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for Na–Ir–O ion exchange.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Route 3 has the weakest kinetics because Na4IrO4 required 71 days at 800 °C, showing slow exchange and ordering kinetics in Na–Ir–O systems (CS-4). The route also requires substantial stoichiometric and redox transformation from Li2IrO3/Na2O-type chemistry to Na5IrO6 [Reasonable Inference]. It remains plausible because Na4IrO4 proves that Na–Ir–O phases can form by exchange using Li2IrO3 and Na2O (CS-4). The uncertainty is high because no CS/SS/TS source reports direct exchange to Na5IrO6.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Na and high-valent Ir, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure contains corner- and edge-sharing NaO6/IrO6 octahedra, but no face-sharing connectivity is reported, so C3 is not triggered by the provided target structure. The likely cell size is not above the >40 atom threshold based on the A5MO6 formula and its close Na5OsO6 analogue [Reasonable Inference from CS-1], so C2 is not triggered. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for amorphization behavior in this chemical family.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na5IrO6 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.144368067 eV/atom, and the closest Na5OsO6 analogue is also thermodynamically stable with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1). The strongest support comes from CS, especially isostructural Na5OsO6 synthesized by direct Na2O2/O2 oxidation and Na4IrO4 synthesized in Na-rich iridate chemistry (CS-1, CS-4). SS and TS provide insufficient chemical guidance because their nearest entries are unrelated nitrides, amides, borides, phosphates, and bromides. The key enabling factor is the very close Os analogue showing that the Caswellsilverite-like A5MO6 framework can be made under moderate-temperature peroxide/O2 conditions (CS-1). The main limiting factor is redox/phase control: Na5IrO6 formally requires very high-valent Ir and a Na-rich, moisture-sensitive oxide environment [Reasonable Inference from charge balance and CS-1/CS-4].
