### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.159406 eV/atom, so it is scored at the top of the provided ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin. CS1–CS5 all have E_hull = 0.0 for chemically similar rare-earth/transition-metal carbides, which supports that comparable carbide compositions can be true ground-state phases. SS and TS entries are mostly unrelated hydrides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient carbide-specific thermodynamic evidence and are scored neutral, 50. Remaining uncertainty is that a negative hull value can reflect database/reference inconsistencies, so experimental phase competition with ScC, TaC, and graphite must still be checked [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses the same general arc-melting/annealing conditions reported for CS1–CS4, including Ar arc melting and evacuated silica-tube annealing. The temperature severity is moderate because the anneal is 780–1000 °C in CS1–CS4, but the controlled atmosphere, sealing, and multi-step workflow reduce the score. The main uncertainty is whether Ta-containing ScTaC₂ requires higher annealing temperature than Mo/Re/Cr analogues because Ta is more refractory [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Elemental Sc/Ta plus graphite are kinetically sluggish compared with nitrate, acetate, or sol–gel precursors [Reasonable Inference], even though CS1–CS4 demonstrate that elemental carbide reactions can succeed after arc melting. Diffusion is the limiting kinetic factor because Ta is refractory and carbon ordering may require long anneals [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is not identical to the CS analogues, so structural reconstruction and Sc/Ta ordering add uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because CS5 uses 1623 K, ~1350 °C, sealed refractory crucibles, Ar flushing, and glovebox-level dry handling. It does not require >5 GPa pressure, and it avoids reactive gases such as NH₃/H₂, but high-temperature containment and multi-step processing make it challenging. The uncertainty is whether the higher temperature improves ordering or instead drives binary TaC/ScC equilibration [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The higher temperature in Route 2 should improve diffusion and cation ordering relative to Route 1 [Reasonable Inference], and CS5 shows high-temperature treatment is viable for related ternary carbides. However, the precursors remain refractory elemental solids/graphite, and the exact target structure is not represented among CS5 analogues. Kinetic risk remains moderate-to-high because high-temperature annealing may not be sufficient to order Sc and Ta on the Caswellsilverite sublattices [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is extrapolated and uses SPS/hot pressing at high temperature under vacuum or Ar, so deductions apply for T >1000 °C, controlled atmosphere, and multi-step processing [Reasonable Inference]. If temperatures above 1400 °C are required, severity increases further [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS entry directly validates this route for the target, so it is not recommended as the primary synthesis.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

SPS or hot pressing could improve reaction rates by reducing diffusion lengths and applying pressure-assisted densification [Reasonable Inference]. However, rapid densification may trap partially reacted TaC/ScC or prevent long-range Sc/Ta ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Since CS1–CS5 rely on arc melting and annealing rather than SPS, the kinetic score is only moderate and highly uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple crystalline description with ScC₆ and TaC₆ octahedra sharing corners and edges, and no face-sharing is reported in the target description. The expected cell size is small for a Caswellsilverite-type R-3m carbide [Reasonable Inference], and the Sc/Ta site-size mismatch is not expected to be large enough to trigger the >15% radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly quantify target amorphization risk, so their direct evidence is insufficient and neutral, 50. Overall amorphization tendency is low; phase separation is a larger concern than glass formation [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScTaC₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the provided E_hull is strongly negative, and chemically similar ternary carbides in CS1–CS5 are reported at E_hull = 0.0. The CS analogues support synthesis well at the chemical level, especially CS3, which uses Sc filings, a refractory transition-metal powder, and graphite to form a ternary Sc carbide by arc melting and annealing. SS and TS provide little direct chemical guidance because their nearest entries are not structurally or chemically close carbides, so they are treated as neutral evidence. The most critical enabling factor is the demonstrated success of arc melting plus long inert annealing for rare-earth/transition-metal carbides (CS1–CS5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase-control difficulty: Ta is refractory, graphite is low-reactivity, and competing binary carbides such as TaC or ScC are plausible [Reasonable Inference].
