### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = −0.09055 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and therefore receives 100/100 under the rubric. Chemically similar oxides in CS are also stable or nearly stable: Gd₃RuO₇ and Tb₃RuO₇ have E_hull = 0.0 (CS-1, CS-2), Sm₃OsO₇ has E_hull = 0.0 (CS-4), and Gd₃OsO₇ has E_hull ≈ 0.0008 eV/atom (CS-5). SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant evidence for Ba₂OsO₆ stability because their listed analogues are unrelated systems; each is neutral, score 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 is supported by CS ceramic oxide routes requiring 1000–1300 °C, 1200 °C, or 1473 K with repeated grindings (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3), so condition severity is moderate-high but not extreme. The F2 score is reduced for >1000 °C operation, multi-step regrinding, and Os toxicity [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are the major weakness because solid-state diffusion of Ba/Os/O from refractory or dense oxide precursors is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference], and CS analogues required long holds and repeated regrinding (CS-1 to CS-3). SS and TS are insufficient for this route and are neutral, 50.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is directly supported by Os-containing CS flux syntheses at 600 °C for 12 h in NaOH/H₂O flux using silver tubes (CS-4, CS-5). F2 remains limited by sealed-tube handling, caustic flux, and toxic Os chemistry [Reasonable Inference], but it avoids the >1000 °C penalty. F3 is higher than Route 1 because molten hydroxide flux provides mass transport and allowed crystallization of related Os oxides at low temperature (CS-4, CS-5). The uncertainty is that Ba₂OsO₆ requires a higher Os oxidation state than Ln₃OsO₇ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is an extrapolated oxidizing sealed-route variant based on the low-temperature Os flux conditions in CS-4/CS-5 and the high-valent Os requirement of Ba₂OsO₆ [Reasonable Inference]. F2 is worse than Route 2 because sealed oxidizing systems and possible volatile OsO₄ formation trigger additional safety and volatility penalties [Reasonable Inference]. F3 benefits from reactive peroxide/osmate precursors [Reasonable Inference], but no CS/SS/TS entry directly validates Ba₂OsO₆ formation by this chemistry. SS and TS remain insufficient and neutral, 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target has a large cation-size contrast between Ba in 12-coordinate BaO₁₂ cuboctahedra and Os in OsO₆ octahedra [Reasonable Inference], so C1 is triggered. The structure is simple cubic Fm-3m with one Ba site, one Os site, and one O site in the target description, so the structural complexity is not high [Reasonable Inference]. The target description includes corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the full mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not triggered. SS and TS are chemically irrelevant for amorphization behavior and are neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba₂OsO₆ is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable because its reported E_hull is negative, so it is likely a ground-state or at least strongly stable computed phase under the chosen reference set. The CS evidence is supportive because several chemically similar Ru/Os oxides are experimentally synthesized and are thermodynamically stable or nearly stable (CS-1 to CS-5). The strongest enabling factor is the availability of a demonstrated low-temperature Os oxide hydroxide-flux route using Os metal, NaOH, H₂O, and sealed silver tubes (CS-4, CS-5). The main limiting factor is the likely need to stabilize very high-valent Os in Ba₂OsO₆, with associated risk of volatile/toxic OsO₄ formation [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give little useful practical guidance because their top analogues are chemically unrelated, so they are treated as neutral evidence.
