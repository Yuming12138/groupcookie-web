### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s supplied E_hull is **−0.0485 eV/atom**, which places it in the most favorable stability bin and justifies a score of 98. Chemically similar intermetallics CS-1 through CS-5 are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that nearby rare-earth/scandium–transition-metal–p-block compositions can be stable. SS and TS include stable entries, but they are chemically unrelated to Sc–Cu–Al; therefore, **insufficient evidence from SS/TS**, scored neutral 50 for target-specific stability transfer.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Arc melting plus 800 °C / 120 h annealing and quenching is directly supported for Immm analogues (CS-1/4/5). The route is penalized for very high local melting temperature, evacuated sealed annealing, multi-step processing, and possible Al volatility [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether Sc(CuAl)₆ requires the same long anneal or can form directly from the melt.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetics are favorable after melting because the liquid alloy homogenizes rapidly [Reasonable Inference], and the Immm structure is directly analogous to CS-1/4/5. The 120 h annealing in CS-1/4/5 indicates that ordering and phase equilibration are not instantaneous. The score remains good because the structural template is the closest among all evidence sources.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Induction melting under Ar followed by annealing at 1000 °C for 2 days is supported by CS-2. This route avoids evacuated ampoules and quenching as mandatory operations, so its severity score is higher than Route 1. The uncertainty is that CS-2 is not the same structure type as the target.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
The liquid alloy stage should overcome slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS-2 demonstrates successful induction melting for rare-earth–transition-metal–Ga intermetallics. However, CS-2 is hexagonal P6/mmm rather than orthorhombic Immm, so the exact Sc(CuAl)₆ ordering pathway is less certain. The kinetics score is therefore moderate rather than high.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Czochralski/levitated melt processing is supported by CS-3 for Sc–Fe–Al intermetallic single crystals. This route is more equipment-intensive and likely requires high melt temperatures, with possible Al loss [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 lacks full atmosphere and temperature details, so those aspects carry uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Melt growth gives excellent mass transport and can produce high-quality crystals, as shown for ScFe₄Al₈ in CS-3. The structural mismatch between tetragonal CS-3 and orthorhombic target lowers confidence in direct phase selection. This route is attractive for crystal growth after phase existence is established, but less optimal for first synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains differently sized metallic species Sc, Cu, and Al, giving one amorphization/chemical-disorder trigger by cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The dense intermetallic structure likely has mixed face/edge/corner connectivity by analogy to Immm CS-1/4/5, giving another trigger. The atom-count trigger is not established from the supplied data, so it is not counted; remaining uncertainty is the degree of Cu/Al site disorder.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc(CuAl)₆ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state intermetallic because the supplied E_hull is negative and chemically similar CS intermetallics are stable on the hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-1/4/5, which share the same orthorhombic Immm dense intermetallic motif and are made by arc melting plus long annealing. The most critical enabling factor is access to a homogeneous liquid alloy, because melting bypasses slow Sc/Cu/Al solid-state diffusion and allows subsequent ordering [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-1/2/3]. The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially possible Al loss and Cu/Al ordering during annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS are not chemically aligned with the target, so they provide mostly neutral evidence rather than direct synthetic guidance.
