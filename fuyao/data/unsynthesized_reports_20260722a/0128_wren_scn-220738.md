### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.018347 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom class and supporting strong thermodynamic synthesizability. Rb₂S₅ and K₂S₅ analogues are both reported at E_hull = 0.0, showing that alkali polysulfide phases are not merely high-energy hypothetical structures (CS-1, CS-2). The negative hull value is interpreted as effectively zero within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS entries are structurally/thermodynamically near-stable but chemically unrelated to Rb–S, so their direct signal is insufficient and neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is penalized because Rb₂S₅ synthesis required NH₃, pressure, a dry box, NH₃ condensation, a custom autoclave, controlled gradients, and slow cooling (CS-1, CS-4). The temperature itself is mild, only ~430 K for Rb₂S₅, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS-1). The main severity comes from pressure/NH₃/air-sensitive Rb rather than thermal extremity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because ammonothermal synthesis produced well-crystallized Rb₂S₅ with quantitative yield and no detectable impurities, implying efficient solution-mediated transport (CS-1). The target Rb–S and S–S distances overlap closely with Rb₂S₅, so only moderate structural rearrangement is expected (CS-1). The uncertainty is whether the 1:3 stoichiometry will crystallize before sulfur-richer Rb₂S₅-like phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high pressure and high temperature, as K₂S₅ was prepared in a Schlenk tube with ethanol/H₂S and recrystallization under dry nitrogen (CS-2). However, it still requires dry inert handling, toxic H₂S, reactive Rb metal by analogy to K metal, and multiple chemical operations (CS-2, [Reasonable Inference]). Product air sensitivity is a significant concern because K₂S₅ decomposes rapidly in air (CS-2).

#### F3 — Route 2

Solution polysulfide chemistry should be kinetically fast because K₂S₅ forms through hydrosulfide + sulfur speciation in ethanol and can be recrystallized (CS-2). Ion diffusion is not expected to limit the route because transport occurs in solution [Reasonable Inference from CS-2]. The lower score versus Route 1 reflects weaker direct evidence for RbS₃ and greater uncertainty in selecting the exact polysulfide chain length.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses moderate temperatures comparable to the Cs₄S₁₂ sealed-ampoule anneal at 803 K, so no high-temperature penalty applies (CS-3). It is penalized for vacuum/sealed-tube operation, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive Rb sulfide/Rb metal precursors, and multiple handling/annealing steps (CS-3, [Reasonable Inference]). The method is less equipment-intensive than high-pressure synthesis but more hazardous than an ordinary solid-state reaction.

#### F3 — Route 3

Solid-state kinetics are less favorable than ammonothermal or solution synthesis because selecting the RbS₃ polysulfide chain/network likely requires rearrangement among several possible Rb–S stoichiometries [Reasonable Inference]. Cs₄S₁₂-related synthesis required several days of annealing and had residual impurity/phase-control issues, supporting only moderate kinetic confidence (CS-3). Molten/vapor sulfur and soft Rb chemistry partially mitigate diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Rb, so cation-size disorder does not trigger C1 [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The described P2₁/c structure has one Rb and three inequivalent S sites, suggesting a relatively simple crystalline basis rather than a very large amorphization-prone cell [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The connectivity includes edge and corner sharing but no reported face-sharing coexistence, so C3 is not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbS₃ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is −0.018347 eV/atom. The chemical analogue support is strong: Rb₂S₅ was experimentally synthesized as high-quality single crystals under ammonothermal conditions, and K₂S₅ shows that alkali polysulfides can also be accessed by solution polysulfide chemistry (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable Rb–S polysulfide bonding motifs with S–S distances close to the target, as shown by Rb₂S₅ (CS-1). The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among neighboring Rb polysulfide stoichiometries, especially sulfur-richer phases such as Rb₂S₅ [Reasonable Inference from CS-1]. SS and TS analogues provide little chemically specific guidance, so confidence is driven mainly by CS evidence.
