### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.007162909 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within numerical tolerance [Reasonable Inference]. Chemical and thermodynamic analogues are stable or near-stable: CePd₃As₂ is on-hull (CS-2, TS-4), Pd₃As is on-hull (SS-1, TS-1), and RE–Pd–P analogues are on/near hull (SS-2/SS-3/SS-5; TS-2/TS-3/TS-5). This strongly supports synthesizability, though computed stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate high temperatures comparable to CePd₃As₂ at 850/750 °C and Pd₃As-type evacuated-tube reactions (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1/TS-4). The main severity penalties come from vacuum/sealed ampoule operation, As volatility, and multistep prereaction/annealing; these are supported by evacuated-tube and multi-stage protocols in As/Pd analogues (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1/TS-4). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for needing high pressure, so that subfactor is neutral/no penalty.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

Solid-state reaction from Y/Pd/As elements likely suffers from slow diffusion because Y and Pd are metallic/refractory solids [Reasonable Inference]. Long annealing and repeated processing in CePd₃As₂ and RE–Pd–P analogues indicate that phase ordering and homogenization can be slow (CS-2; SS-2/SS-3/SS-5; TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). The route remains plausible because Pd–As and rare-earth–Pd–pnictide phases are demonstrably accessible from elements (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1/TS-4).

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 benefits from the Ce–Pd–Sb halide-flux precedent at only 900→700 °C, avoiding the >1000 °C penalty (CS-1, SS-4). It still requires an evacuated quartz tube and multistep flux handling, and the target’s As component imposes sealed-tube and toxicity concerns [Reasonable Inference based on As-containing CS-2/SS-1]. No CS/SS/TS evidence indicates high pressure or exotic atmosphere beyond vacuum, so those are not additional penalties.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

The flux route scores better kinetically because liquid/flux-assisted transport can bypass slow diffusion of dense metallic precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The Ce–Pd–Sb analogue used NaBr/KBr flux specifically to grow larger crystals and manage multiphase behavior (CS-1, SS-4). Remaining uncertainty is that Sb-to-As and Ce-to-Y substitution may shift phase stability and flux solubility, so the exact target phase window is not proven [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Bi-flux analogues require 1320 K, about 1047 °C, so Route 3 receives a >1000 °C penalty (CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). It also requires evacuated ampoules, slow cooling, and flux removal, all reported in RE–Rh–Ge Bi-flux syntheses (CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). The As-containing target adds volatility/toxicity penalties by inference from As chemistry and sealed As analogues (CS-2; SS-1; TS-1/TS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

Bi flux should improve crystallization kinetics relative to dry solid-state synthesis because RE–Rh–Ge single crystals were grown from Bi flux by slow cooling (CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). However, those analogues are less chemically matched than Pd–As or RE–Pd–pnictide examples, so solubility and phase selectivity for Y–Pd–As are uncertain [Reasonable Inference]. The higher temperature may also worsen As retention compared with the NaBr/KBr route [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing Pd-centered cuboctahedral motifs, directly triggering the mixed-connectivity criterion from the target description. Similar complex polyhedral sharing occurs in CePd₃As₂ and RE–Pd–P structural/thermodynamic analogues (CS-2; SS-2/SS-3/SS-5; TS-2/TS-4/TS-5). Unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS exists for that check and it is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

YPd₄As₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is −0.007162909 eV/atom, effectively on-hull within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support comes from stable Pd–As and Ce–Pd–As analogues, especially Pd₃As and CePd₃As₂, both synthesized from elements and on the hull (SS-1/TS-1; CS-2/TS-4). The most enabling factor is the robust Pd–As intermetallic chemistry and high-coordinate rare-earth/transition-metal pnictide structural motif shared across the analogues (CS-1/CS-2; SS-1/SS-4; TS-1/TS-4). The main limiting factor is phase control: analogous systems often require long annealing, flux growth, or repeated processing to overcome multiphase products and slow ordering (CS-1/CS-2; SS-2/SS-3/SS-5). The best practical route is halide-flux growth because it offers improved mass transport and crystal selection at moderate temperature while remaining close to the rare-earth–Pd–pnictide chemistry (CS-1, SS-4).
