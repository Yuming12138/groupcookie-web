## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.031452214 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifying a score of 100 [target data]. Negative hull values are best treated as indicating very strong calculated stability within numerical uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. TS examples are thermodynamically near-stable but chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient evidence from TS for K–Cd–Au synthesis transferability and are treated as neutral 50.

### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits 550–600 °C sealed-tube conditions from K–Au–In analogues, avoiding very high temperatures or applied high pressure (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The main deductions come from volatile/air-sensitive K, toxic/volatile Cd, and the multi-step sequence of glovebox loading, Ta sealing, heating, slow cooling, and annealing [Reasonable Inference; CS-1/CS-2]. SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant evidence for condition severity in K–Cd–Au and are neutral 50.

### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

K(Au₂In₃)₂ is an almost exact structural and chemical analogue, and its successful formation at 600 °C supports feasible kinetics for the target framework (CS-1). However, long annealing at 450 °C for 160 h in CS-1 indicates ordering and phase equilibration are not instantaneous. The Cd substitution may help by creating a low-melting reactive medium, but it may also increase volatility and competing Cd–Au phase formation [Reasonable Inference].

### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is penalized more heavily because CS-3 uses 1473 K, approximately 1200 °C, for Cd–Au intermetallic synthesis. It remains below the additional >1400 °C penalty and does not require GPa pressure, but sealed volatile Cd/K, air-sensitive K, toxic Cd, and multi-step quench/anneal processing reduce the score [Reasonable Inference; CS-3/CS-5]. This route is chemically relevant but harsher than the isostructural K–Au–In route (CS-1).

### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

High-temperature melt homogenization should dissolve Au more effectively and accelerate Cd–Au network formation [Reasonable Inference], consistent with CS-3’s short 1473 K reaction followed by annealing. The penalty is structural: Ca(Au₂Cd)₂ in CS-3 is tetragonal I4/mmm rather than the target hexagonal P-6m2, so additional reconstruction is required. This makes kinetics acceptable but less directly supported than Route 1.

### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 stays in the 500–650 °C range inferred from slow-cooled K–Au–In syntheses, so it avoids high-temperature penalties (CS-1, CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). It still requires sealed handling of volatile K/Cd, multi-step flux growth/annealing, and toxic/air-sensitive precursors [Reasonable Inference]. No SS/TS examples support Cd/K self-flux chemistry, so SS and TS remain neutral 50.

### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

A Cd/K-rich melt should improve precursor activity and diffusion relative to a dry elemental powder mixture [Reasonable Inference]. The slow-cooling protocols used to obtain K–Au–In intermetallics support the idea that controlled cooling can select ordered Au–p-block/metal frameworks (CS-1, CS-4). The main uncertainty is that no direct K–Cd–Au self-flux synthesis is listed, so composition control and flux removal remain unresolved [Reasonable Inference].

### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a simple metallic intermetallic with a small formula count KAu₄Cd₆, which reduces glass-forming/amorphization tendency [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. One trigger is assigned for the large size mismatch between K and the Au/Cd framework, consistent with long K–Au/Cd distances relative to Au–Cd distances in the target description. No evidence indicates a >40 atom cell or simultaneous vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network, so the amorphization risk is low–medium.

The target is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state intermetallic because its reported E_hull is −0.031452214 eV/atom [target data]. The chemical-similarity evidence is strong: K(Au₂In₃)₂ has the same formula topology, same P-6m2 space group, and very similar Au–M coordination environment, and it was synthesized by sealed-tube solid-state reaction (CS-1). The key enabling factor is the existence of a close isostructural K–Au–In analogue made under accessible 550–600 °C sealed-tube conditions (CS-1, CS-4). The key limiting factor is K/Cd volatility and air/toxicity handling, which demand excellent sealing, controlled stoichiometry, and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues are not chemically transferable, so they do not materially strengthen or weaken the synthesis case; they are neutral 50.
