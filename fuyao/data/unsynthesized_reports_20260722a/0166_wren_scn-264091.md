### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has an exact reported E_hull of −0.014516356 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within numerical tolerance and receives the maximum F1 score (target thermodynamic data). The closest CS analogues CeRh₆Ge₄, LaRh₆Ge₄, HoRh₆Ge₄, GdRh₆Ge₄, and PrRh₆Ge₄ all have E_hull = 0.0, reinforcing that this structure family is thermodynamically credible (CS). SS and TS contain stable or near-stable compounds, but they are chemically unrelated to Ca–Rh–Ge, so they give insufficient specific evidence and are scored neutral, 50, for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1: Bi-flux growth

The Bi-flux route inherits a high-temperature sealed-ampoule protocol from CS: the analogues were heated to 1320 K in evacuated silica ampoules and slow-cooled, with Bi flux later dissolved chemically (CS). Deductions are applied for T >1000 °C, vacuum/sealed operation, multi-step handling, and Ca air sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not provide useful signal for this exact route because their methods include hydrothermal, ammonolysis, precipitation, or unrelated melt growth; neutral score 50 for SS/TS route support.

#### F3 — Route 1: Bi-flux growth

The main kinetic advantage is that Bi flux provides liquid-mediated transport, which is exactly how multiple P-6m2 RERh₆Ge₄ analogues were grown (CS). Rh is refractory and would otherwise be slow to homogenize, but successful flux growth at 1047 °C suggests the diffusion bottleneck is mitigated (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). Structural reconstruction is favorable because the target is isostructural with the CS analogues, requiring mainly Ca substitution on the large-cation site (CS; target description).

#### F2 — Route 2: Arc-melting / annealing

Arc-melting and annealing are generically supported by the CS discussion of RERh₆Ge₄ processing and by SS/TS melt-growth examples, but the direct chemical evidence is weaker than for Bi flux (CS; SS; TS). Severity is moderate because the route avoids flux removal and external pressure but uses very high local arc temperatures, inert atmosphere, multiple steps, and air-sensitive Ca [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-purity concerns in CS analogues lower confidence for this route relative to flux growth (CS).

#### F3 — Route 2: Arc-melting / annealing

Kinetics are less favorable than in flux because Rh is refractory and the reaction relies on bulk homogenization and annealing [Reasonable Inference]. CS notes by-products and challenges obtaining phase-pure related samples, which indicates that solid-state or melt-derived phase control may be nontrivial (CS). The same structure family still supports eventual ordering into the target framework, but repeated remelting/annealing may be necessary [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Ca–Ge self-flux

This route is an extrapolation from the CS-supported flux concept, not a directly reported synthesis for the target or analogues [Reasonable Inference from CS]. It likely requires sealed reactive-metal containment, careful oxygen exclusion, and possibly temperatures near or above 1000 °C, producing a more severe handling profile than Bi flux [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient specific signal for Ca–Ge reactive flux, so they remain neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3: Ca–Ge self-flux

A Ca–Ge liquid flux could improve mass transport and partly overcome Rh diffusion limitations, analogous to the Bi-flux benefit observed in CS [Reasonable Inference from CS]. However, Rh solubility and product separation in Ca-rich flux are uncertain, so the kinetic score is lower-confidence than Route 1 [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction remains favorable because the target structure is supported by the same Rh₆Ge₄ framework chemistry in CS.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a crystalline intermetallic with a compact 11-atom formula and no evidence for a large complex unit cell, so the unit-cell-size amorphization check is not triggered (target description; [Reasonable Inference]). The large size/electropositivity mismatch between Ca and Rh/Ge triggers one risk point, but that mismatch is accommodated by the CaGe₆Rh₆ cuboctahedral site described for the target and by analogous large-cation cuboctahedra in CS. The target description reports face-sharing cuboctahedra but not simultaneous vertex/edge/face sharing, so mixed-connectivity amorphization risk is not triggered (target description).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca(Rh₃Ge₂)₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0145 eV/atom, effectively on the hull within computational tolerance (target thermodynamic data). The CS evidence is unusually strong: five top chemically similar RE(Rh₃Ge₂)₂ analogues crystallize in the same hexagonal P-6m2 structure and were experimentally grown by Bi flux (CS). The most critical enabling factor is the established Bi-flux pathway, which can dissolve refractory Rh/Ge components and promote slow crystal growth of the same Rh₆Ge₄ framework (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). The most critical limiting factor is Ca handling: Ca is more reactive and more air-sensitive than the rare-earth metals in the reported CS procedures, and it may complicate ampoule compatibility and stoichiometry control [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is quite plausible, with Bi flux strongly preferred over direct solid-state reaction.
