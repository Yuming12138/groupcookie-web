### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = −0.086271266 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable stability bin by the required rubric. Chemically close Mg₂OsH₆ and Mg₃ReH₇ are reported on the hull, supporting that Mg–late-5d ternary hydrides can be stable phases (CS-1, CS-2). SS and TS examples are not chemically comparable to Mg₂IrH₅; insufficient evidence from SS and TS, neutral 50. Remaining uncertainty is that a negative computed hull may reflect database/reference-state issues for hydrides, so experimental H₂ pressure may still be required to stabilize the phase [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because the closest analogue uses only 480 °C and 40 bar H₂, but H₂ pressure and air-sensitive hydride handling are required (CS-1). Kinetics are moderate because MgH₂ is an activated Mg/H source, but Ir is refractory and likely slower than MgH₂ decomposition or hydrogen diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The strong Mg–H and Os/Ir–H bond-length similarity supports transferability (CS-1; target data). Uncertainty remains because Mg₂OsH₆ is octahedral OsH₆, whereas the target contains square-pyramidal IrH₅ (CS-1; target data).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is more demanding kinetically because Mg₃ReH₇ required 2 weeks at 510–520 °C and 120–155 bar H₂/D₂, and Mg₂RuH₄ sometimes required up to 1050 K and 160 bar H₂ (CS-2, CS-3). The severity score is penalized for high-pressure H₂, Mg volatility, and air-sensitive handling (CS-2, CS-5). Elemental metal powders require complete formation of the ternary hydride, and unreacted Re/impurities were reported for Mg₃ReH₇ (CS-2). The route is plausible but less attractive than MgH₂-based or flux-assisted chemistry.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 benefits from LiH/LiD flux/mineralizer precedent in Ba–Mg–Os hydride synthesis at 500–530 °C and 100–150 bar H₂/D₂ (CS-4). The route is penalized for H₂ pressure, sealed-system hydride chemistry, and air-sensitive LiH/MgH₂ handling [Reasonable Inference supported by CS-4]. Kinetically, flux assistance may improve hydride mobility and contact with refractory Ir [Reasonable Inference], but CS-4 reports impurity phases such as BaLiD₃ and Mg₂OsD₆, indicating phase-control risk. Because the target lacks Ba, transferability is useful but not direct.

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is based on the EuMg₂H₆ strategy of preparing Mg-containing intermetallics by arc melting and then hydrogenating at 600 K and 5 MPa for 5 days (CS-5). The condition severity is high because arc-melting, Mg volatility control, multi-step handling, and high-pressure H₂ are all involved (CS-5). Kinetics could improve if a suitable Mg–Ir intermetallic precursor forms, because hydrogenation would be partly topochemical/insertion-like rather than complete reaction from separated powders [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is the absence of direct evidence for a Mg–Ir intermetallic precursor that maps onto Mg₂IrH₅.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is not expected to be highly amorphization-prone because the described P4/nmm structure is relatively simple and does not indicate a >40-atom unit cell (target data; [Reasonable Inference]). One risk is the large Mg–Ir size/electronic mismatch, which can slow ordering and increase disorder tendency [Reasonable Inference]. The target has edge- and corner-sharing hydride polyhedra but not explicitly simultaneous face sharing, so the strict C3 criterion is not fully triggered (target data). CS-1 shows a related Mg₂OsH₆ network with face/edge/corner-sharing polyhedra, suggesting a possible but not decisive amorphization/defect risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Mg₂IrH₅ is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is negative and chemically close Mg₂OsH₆ and Mg₃ReH₇ are reported on the hull (CS-1, CS-2; target data). The chemical-similarity evidence strongly supports synthesis under moderate-temperature, high-H₂-pressure conditions, especially from MgH₂ plus a late-5d metal powder (CS-1). The most critical enabling factor is high hydrogen chemical potential, since all closest Mg–late-transition-metal hydrides require H₂/D₂ pressure during synthesis (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). The most critical limiting factor is slow reaction/phase ordering involving refractory Ir, inferred from long reaction times and residual refractory-metal impurities in Mg–Re/Ru analogues (CS-2, CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS analogues provide little direct chemical guidance, so the assessment is dominated by CS evidence, with SS/TS treated as neutral for Mg–Ir hydride transferability.

### SECTION 7 — Synthesis Challenges

| # | Route | Challenge | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
|:--:|:--:|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Air/moisture sensitivity of hydride products and Mg/MgH₂-containing mixtures | High | Use Ar glove-box loading/transfer; Mg₃ReH₇ synthesis used glove-box handling and controlled evacuation/Ar/H₂ operations (CS-2), and Mg₂OsH₆ is reported reactive/ignites in air (CS-1). |
| 2 | All | Need for high H₂ chemical potential | High | Use sealed high-pressure autoclave; Mg₂OsH₆ used 40 bar H₂ (CS-1), Mg₃ReH₇ used 120–155 bar H₂/D₂ (CS-2), and BaMg₂OsH₈ used 100–150 bar H₂/D₂ (CS-4). |
| 3 | All | Slow incorporation of refractory Ir | Medium–High | Use fine Ir powder, pellet pressing, long holds, and possibly regrinding [Reasonable Inference]; long reaction times for Mg₃ReH₇ and Mg₂RuH₄ demonstrate this kinetic issue in related refractory-metal systems (CS-2, CS-3). |
| 4 | Route 1 | Incomplete reaction of Ir with MgH₂ | Medium | Use MgH₂ excess, intimate grinding, pelletization, and 4–7 day sintering; Mg₂OsH₆ was made by finely powdering, pressing into a tablet, and sintering for 4 days (CS-1). |
| 5 | Route 2 | Mg volatility and stoichiometry drift | Medium | Use slight Mg excess and sealed H₂ pressure; 25% Mg excess was used to compensate Mg evaporation in Eu/SrMg₂ precursor synthesis (CS-5). |
| 6 | Route 3 | Li-containing impurity phases | Medium–High | Use minimal LiH loading and screen products by powder XRD; BaLiD₃ and Mg₂OsD₆ impurity phases were reported in related LiH/LiD-assisted Ba–Mg–Os hydride synthesis (CS-4). |
| 7 | Route 4 | Difficulty forming homogeneous Mg–Ir intermetallic precursor | Medium–High | Use repeated melting/grinding and Mg excess [Reasonable Inference]; arc-melting and Mg excess were used for Eu/SrMg₂ intermetallic precursors (CS-5). |
| 8 | All | Competing Mg–Ir–H stoichiometries, especially hexahydride-like phases | Medium | Map H₂ pressure/temperature and Mg:Ir ratio; Mg₂OsH₆ and Mg₃ReH₇ show that multiple Mg–late-transition-metal hydride stoichiometries are stable (CS-1, CS-2). |
| 9 | All | Crystal growth/single-phase difficulty | Medium | Start with powder synthesis and optimize before attempting crystals; related Mg₃ReH₇ had unreacted Re/MgO impurities, and Mg₂RuH₄ samples sometimes contained educts (CS-2, CS-3). |
