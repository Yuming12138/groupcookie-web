### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported target E_hull is −0.227141937 eV/atom, so under the required rubric it receives the maximum stability score [Target data]. Several TS analogues are exactly or nearly on the hull, including Li3Na(NH2)4 at 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1), AuBr at 0.0 eV/atom (TS-4), and W3Ni2N at 0.0034 eV/atom (TS-5), supporting that nearby thermodynamic examples can be synthesizable. The main uncertainty is that a negative hull value is nonphysical as a strict convex-hull distance and likely reflects reference/model inconsistency [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and GPa pressure, which is favorable relative to high-temperature analogues like BaP10 at 1050 K for 4 weeks (CS-5). It still requires sealed, dry, controlled conditions because comparable liquid-media syntheses used sealed ampoules/autogenous atmospheres (CS-1) and glovebox/ampoule handling (CS-2). Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly specifies Li2S/S8 solvent pressure behavior, so the pressure and volatility penalties are based on [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Solution/solvothermal chemistry should reduce diffusion barriers by forming mobile lithium polysulfide species [Reasonable Inference]. This is supported indirectly by CS-1, where an anion-rich MgN8 phase crystallized from liquid ammonia at only 150 °C, and CS-2, where sealed solvothermal media enabled crystallization/polymorph control. The uncertainty is that SS gives only a generic 1D ribbon analogue, AuBr (SS-4), not a Li–S polysulfide framework.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 uses only moderate temperatures but requires sealed ampoules because sulfur is volatile and the target is sulfur-rich [Reasonable Inference]. Sealed ampoule synthesis is supported by BaP10 preparation (CS-5), and dry vacuum/N2 decomposition conditions are supported by AuBr synthesis (SS-4/TS-4). Insufficient evidence from TS specifies sulfur-rich ampoule safety, so the sealed-system severity is inferred.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Powder Li2S + sulfur has less precursor mobility than solution chemistry, so precursor activity is only moderate [Reasonable Inference]. Long dwell times in anion-rich BaP10 synthesis (CS-5) and low-dimensional AuBr formation (SS-4/TS-4) imply that ordering and phase development can be slow. Sulfur melt/vapor transport may partially compensate for solid-state diffusion limits [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Mechanochemistry avoids high temperature and high pressure, but it requires inert milling and post-anneal handling because Li2S and polysulfides are moisture-sensitive [Reasonable Inference]. Controlled-atmosphere handling is supported by CS-2 and SS-1/TS-1. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS directly supports mechanochemical LiS8 formation, so the score is not allowed to exceed the sealed-solution route.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Mechanical milling should improve contact and reduce diffusion length [Reasonable Inference]. Grinding/regrinding benefits are supported indirectly by intermittent grindings in ammonolysis synthesis (SS-5/TS-5) and repeated powdering for AuBr decomposition (SS-4/TS-4). The limiting kinetic uncertainty is whether the milled product crystallizes into the specific monoclinic C2/m LiS8 ribbon rather than remaining amorphous or forming another lithium polysulfide [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Li, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. No >40 atom unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing connectivity is evident from the target description, so F4 is high [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5 shows that complex mixed-connectivity materials can exist, but insufficient evidence from SS indicates such complexity for LiS8.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely thermodynamically stable or at least strongly predicted to be stable because its reported E_hull is −0.227 eV/atom, although the negative value should be interpreted cautiously as a computational/reference artifact [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The CS evidence supports synthesis of anion-rich materials in sealed solution or sealed ampoule environments, especially MgN8 from liquid ammonia (CS-1) and BaP10 from sealed solid-state reaction (CS-5). The SS/TS evidence supports that low-dimensional ribbon or near-hull phases can be synthesized, especially AuBr as a 1D ribbon material (SS-4/TS-4), but it does not directly validate Li–S polysulfide chemistry. The most important enabling factor is the availability of mobile polysulfide species in sulfur-rich solution or melt, which should help assemble S–S bonded motifs [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among many possible lithium polysulfides and sulfur-rich compositions, which is not directly resolved by CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference].
