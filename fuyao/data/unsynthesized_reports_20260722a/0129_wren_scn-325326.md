### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.0084 eV/atom, which is effectively stable within DFT numerical tolerance (Target). CS analogues are mostly on the hull or near it, including CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5 at 0 eV/atom and CS-4 at 0.0049 eV/atom, supporting that related hydride-rich ionic solids can be thermodynamically accessible. SS/TS provide a particularly relevant stable hydrosulfide analogue, NaHS at 0 eV/atom (SS-3, TS-1). Remaining uncertainty is that computed stability does not guarantee kinetic accessibility or retention of Se–H bonding during synthesis [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 has low thermal severity because NaHS-type hydrosulfides form at 50–150 °C (SS-3/TS-1), and the proposed H₂Se analogue uses similar low temperatures [Reasonable Inference]. The main deductions come from sealed toxic volatile gas handling, Ar/glovebox control, and multiple gas-loading/product-handling operations, all analogous to the autoclave/glovebox/liquid-N₂/tensiometer workflow for hydrosulfides (SS-3/TS-1). CS ammonia autoclave examples also show that hydride-rich salts often require sealed pressure vessels and inert handling (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5), but they do not directly validate Ba–Se–H chemistry; insufficient evidence from CS for this exact factor, neutral signal 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are favorable because gas-phase H₂Se should provide a high-activity Se–H reagent [Reasonable Inference], analogous to metal + H₂S/D₂S hydrosulfide formation at low temperature (SS-3/TS-1). Diffusion limitations should be reduced in an autoclave gas/condensed phase compared with dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], again supported by low-temperature hydrosulfide synthesis (SS-3/TS-1). Structural reconstruction is moderate because the target contains a 2D Ba–Se–H sheet (Target), while NaHS is simpler but still shares terminal chalcogen–H bonding and electropositive-cation coordination (SS-3/TS-1).

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 avoids high-pressure H₂ and extreme temperatures, but it requires sealed evacuated/inert ampoule conditions similar to reactive hydride halide syntheses in Ta/silica ampoules (CS-3, CS-4) and selenide ampoule synthesis (SS-5). Deductions arise from controlled atmosphere, volatile Se/H-containing species, and air-sensitive hydride precursors [Reasonable Inference; hydride sealed handling supported by CS-3/CS-4]. Insufficient evidence from TS for sealed Ba–Se–H ampoule synthesis specifically; neutral signal 50 for this route transfer.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are only moderate because solid BaH₂ and Se must form Se–H bonds and an ordered 2D Ba–Se framework [Reasonable Inference from Target]. Ampoule selenides can be made by long direct solid-state annealing (SS-5), and hydride halides can be made in sealed Ta ampoules (CS-3/CS-4), so the route is plausible. However, the target’s terminal Se–H units may decompose or equilibrate with H₂/H₂Se at elevated temperature [Reasonable Inference], limiting the kinetics/phase-retention score.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 has a relatively high rubric F2 score because the specified penalty only applies to >5 GPa, whereas TS hydride analogues use ~1500–1800 bar H₂, far below 5 GPa but still specialized (TS-3–TS-5). It still incurs controlled H₂ atmosphere and air-sensitive hydride/selenide precursor penalties (TS-3–TS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Insufficient evidence from CS and SS for high-pressure hydrogenation of Ba–Se frameworks; neutral signal 50 from those sources.

#### F3 — Route 3

High-pressure H₂ can stabilize hydride-rich phases, as shown by Cs₂PtH₆/Rb₂PtH₆/K₂PtH₆ syntheses (TS-3–TS-5). However, those analogues form PtH₆-centered complex hydrides rather than Se–H hydroselenide sheets, so structural transfer is weak (TS-3–TS-5 vs Target). A BaSe precursor would likely require significant reconstruction and hydrogen insertion [Reasonable Inference], making kinetics less favorable than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Ba, so cation-size disorder is not a concern (Target). The described structure is ordered, monoclinic P2₁, and contains defined Ba–Se coordination plus terminal Se–H bonds rather than a highly mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network (Target). SS-3/TS-1 NaHS also shows simple cation–chalcogen hydride connectivity, supporting low amorphization risk, although exact target unit-cell atom count is not given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target cell size, neutral signal 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaH₄Se₃ is likely close to a ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is −0.0084 eV/atom (Target), and many chemically or thermodynamically similar hydride/chalcogenide analogues are on or near the hull (CS-1–CS-5, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3–TS-5). The strongest synthesis analogy is NaHS made from metal + H₂S/D₂S in an autoclave at 50–150 °C (SS-3/TS-1), because the target also contains terminal chalcogen–H bonding (Target). The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature sealed-gas chemistry, which can preserve Se–H bonds while enabling reactive transport [Reasonable Inference from SS-3/TS-1]. The most critical limiting factor is safe, precise control of H₂Se/Se/H chemical potential, since H₂Se is volatile and toxic and Se–H stoichiometry may be narrow [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but experimentally demanding, with Route 1 favored over solid-state or high-pressure hydrogenation routes.
