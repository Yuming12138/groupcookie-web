### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.074388396 eV/atom, so it is scored in the most stable bin and treated as computationally stable within numerical uncertainty (target data). Chemically similar chloride materials at E_hull = 0.0 are experimentally known, including Zr(SeCl₆)₂, SnCl₅PCl₄, MoCl₅, and Hf(SeCl₆)₂ (CS #1, CS #2, CS #3, CS #5). SS and TS contain many unrelated chemistries, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for Rh–Cl thermodynamic stability; they are neutral at 50 for this factor.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because it avoids high temperature and GPa pressure, consistent with low-temperature ampoule chloride syntheses at 140–155 °C or 303–453 K (CS #1, CS #4, CS #5). However, it requires vacuum/sealed ampoules, volatile Cl₂, and toxic/air-sensitive chloride handling, which are supported by the ampoule/vacuum/dry handling requirements in CS #1, CS #4, and CS #5 and by [Reasonable Inference] for Cl₂. Kinetically, vapor transport should overcome solid-state diffusion limitations, as CS chloride analogues crystallize at low temperature, but ordered incorporation of five Cl₂ molecules per RhCl₃ remains uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is conditionally mild in temperature because CS #2 demonstrates chloride-rich products from dry solution mixing at room temperature or reflux. Its F2 remains reduced by drybox requirements, hydrolyzable reagents, volatile solvents, and possible Cl₂ dosing, all consistent with CS #2 and [Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetic score is highest because solution-phase mixing gives high precursor activity and minimal diffusion barriers (CS #2), but the transformation from solvated Rh chloride species into a neutral RhCl₃(Cl₂)₅ crystal is not directly demonstrated by CS, SS, or TS.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 is more operationally demanding because CS #3 reports Ar glovebox handling, sealed ampoules, controlled cooling, and sometimes autoclave/counterpressure precautions for chloride solvothermal synthesis. Kinetically it is favorable because a chloride-rich flux/solvent can dissolve and transport Rh chloride species [Reasonable Inference], analogous to MoCl₅ crystal growth in CCl₄/SbCl₅ media (CS #3). The main uncertainty is phase selectivity: CS #3 explicitly notes multiple MoCl₅ polymorphs and narrow conditions, suggesting similar halide polymorph/solvate competition could occur for the Rh–Cl₂ target.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so cation-radius mismatch is not a meaningful amorphization driver [Reasonable Inference] from target formula. The target description does not show mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing networks, and the reduced formula has only 14 atoms, so none of the rubric amorphization triggers is clearly activated (target description; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS for target-specific amorphization). The remaining risk is not classical amorphization but molecular Cl₂ disorder or loss, which is treated as a phase/stoichiometry problem rather than an F4 trigger [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is negative, −0.074388396 eV/atom, in the supplied thermodynamic data (target data). The CS set gives strong chemical support that low-temperature chloride-rich compounds can be synthesized in sealed ampoules or dry chlorinating media, especially CS #1, CS #2, CS #4, and CS #5. SS and TS are mostly chemically unrelated, but SS/TS #4 provides a useful caution that 1D halide phases such as AuBr can require dry/vacuum conditions and can suffer phase-control difficulty. The most critical enabling factor is high chloride/Cl₂ chemical potential at low temperature, which should help retain molecular Cl₂ units [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is maintaining RhCl₃(Cl₂)₅ stoichiometry without losing Cl₂ or forming simpler Rh chlorides, which is not directly solved by any CS/SS/TS analogue [Reasonable Inference].
