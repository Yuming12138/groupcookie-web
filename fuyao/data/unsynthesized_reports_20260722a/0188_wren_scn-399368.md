### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.029565401 eV/atom, which is inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin and is treated as effectively on the convex hull [Reasonable Inference]. Chemically similar reference phases SrAu₃In₃, Ba(AuIn)₂, Sr(AuIn)₂, and EuAu₃In₃ all have E_hull = 0.0, indicating this ternary Au–In alkaline-earth chemistry supports stable intermetallic phases (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). SS and TS entries are chemically unrelated and have NaN similarity values, so they provide insufficient evidence for Ba–Au–In thermodynamic transferability and are scored neutral as requested (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
Route 1 uses 1400 K/900 K processing in sealed Ta under Ar, which is high temperature but not ultrahigh pressure or a reactive gas process (CS1, CS4). The penalties come from T >1000 °C, multi-step sealing/melting/cooling/annealing/quench, and air-sensitive Ba handling [Reasonable Inference supported by glovebox use in CS2/CS5]. The route is demanding but well precedented for the closest Sr/Eu analogues (CS1, CS4).

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
Kinetics are favorable because CS1/CS4 demonstrate that AAu₃In₃ phases can be made by induction melting rather than long repeated solid-state grinding. The same Pmmn structure and same 1:3:3 stoichiometry in SrAu₃In₃/EuAu₃In₃ imply low structural-reconstruction penalty for Ba substitution [Reasonable Inference from CS1/CS4]. Remaining uncertainty is whether larger Ba stabilizes a competing Ba–Au–In stoichiometry such as Ba(AuIn)₂, which is experimentally known (CS2).

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
Route 2 uses 1000–1050 °C tube reactions, dry glovebox loading, welded Ta/Nb tubes, quenching, annealing, and slow cooling (CS2, CS5). It avoids high pressure and reactive gases, but the process complexity and air-sensitive Ba handling justify moderate deductions (CS2, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). It is less direct than Route 1 because the Ba precedent is Ba(AuIn)₂, not BaAu₃In₃ (CS2).

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Solid-state elemental reactions have slower homogenization than full induction melting because Au is dense and relatively refractory [Reasonable Inference]. CS2/CS5 show that Ba/Sr–Au–In phases can form under these conditions, but CS2 reports sensitivity to Au/In ratio and the need for annealing/cooling, lowering the phase-selection kinetics score. The target Pmmn phase may compete with Pnma Ba(AuIn)₂ under similar Ba–Au–In conditions (CS2).

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
The In-flux route is extrapolated and may operate at or below ~1000 °C, so its temperature severity can be slightly lower than direct 1127 °C induction melting [Reasonable Inference]. It still requires inert loading, sealed Ta ampoules, slow cooling, and decanting, giving multi-step and air-sensitive precursor penalties [Reasonable Inference from CS1–CS5]. Since no CS/SS/TS example directly reports In-flux growth of BaAu₃In₃, uncertainty is higher than for Route 1.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
A liquid In-rich medium should improve mass transport and help dissolve Au compared with powder diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. Melt-based formation of same-stoichiometry Sr/Eu AAu₃In₃ phases supports the idea that liquid processing is compatible with the desired Au–In framework (CS1, CS4). The limiting kinetic risk is that excess In may stabilize In-rich or off-stoichiometric competing phases rather than BaAu₃In₃ [Reasonable Inference; phase-ratio sensitivity noted in CS2].

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains large Ba together with Au/In framework atoms, giving a significant size mismatch by chemical-radius trends [Reasonable Inference]. The target description includes mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing InBa₂Au₄ tetrahedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion; similar Au–In polyhedral networks occur in Sr/Eu analogues (CS1, CS4). However, intermetallic melt/anneal routes in CS1/CS4 produced X-ray-pure crystalline products, so amorphization is a medium rather than prohibitive risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ba(InAu)₃ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is slightly negative, effectively on the hull within numerical uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. The chemical-similarity evidence is strong: SrAu₃In₃ and EuAu₃In₃ are same-stoichiometry Pmmn analogues made reproducibly by induction melting, and Ba–Au–In intermetallic chemistry is experimentally established through Ba(AuIn)₂ (CS1, CS2, CS4). The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated stability and melt accessibility of AAu₃In₃ Au–In frameworks in closely related alkaline-earth/rare-earth systems (CS1, CS4). The main limiting factor is phase competition within the Ba–Au–In system, especially possible formation of Ba(AuIn)₂ or other Au/In-ratio-sensitive phases (CS2). SS and TS data provide insufficient useful transferability because the listed analogues are chemically unrelated and have NaN similarity fields, so they are treated as neutral rather than contradictory (SS1–SS5, TS1–TS5).
