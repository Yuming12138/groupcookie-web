### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.010944789 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable within normal computational uncertainty and falls in the highest scoring bin. Several chemically similar chlorides are also reported with E_hull = 0 or near 0 and are experimentally synthesized, supporting the idea that low-hull chloride phases can be accessible. (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5) TS analogues are low-hull and synthesized but chemically unrelated, so they provide only generic support rather than direct Rb–Ru–Cl evidence. (TS1–TS5) The remaining uncertainty is that high-valent Ru chloride stability depends strongly on chlorine chemical potential. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and avoids GPa pressure, consistent with moderate-temperature sealed chloride syntheses at 480–600 °C. (CS3, CS4) The main severity deductions come from the need for controlled Cl2/vacuum/sealed conditions, volatile chloride handling, and air-sensitive/toxic chloride precursors. [Reasonable Inference] This is more demanding than the straightforward BaZnCl4 direct reaction but less demanding than the hydrogen-driven K2Zr7Cl18 synthesis requiring sealed tantalum tubes and 20 days under H2. (CS2, CS4)

#### F3 — Route 1

Binary chlorides are kinetically more reactive than dense oxides and are directly used in related chloride syntheses. (CS1, CS3, CS4) However, forming an ordered RbRuCl6 phase requires oxidation-state control and assembly of RbCl12/RuCl6 connectivity, and related Cs–Ti chloride synthesis reports phase reproducibility and moisture challenges. (CS1) The kinetics are therefore moderate rather than easy. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 adds process complexity because it involves flux loading, high-temperature melt/slow cooling, and post-growth flux removal under dry conditions. [Reasonable Inference] CsTi2Cl7 uses a melt at 900 °C followed by annealing at 550 °C, and BaZnCl4 uses slow cooling in sealed ampoules, so the general thermal program is supported. (CS1, CS3) The route remains below the >1000 °C deduction but still requires sealed volatile/chlorinating conditions. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 2

A molten RbCl-rich flux should improve diffusion and crystal growth compared with a dry powder reaction. [Reasonable Inference] This inference is supported by the melt/anneal route for CsTi2Cl7 and Bridgman slow-cooling behavior for BaZnCl4. (CS1, CS3) The limiting kinetic factor is phase selectivity during cooling, since related chloride systems can show polymorphism, metastability, and phase-purity sensitivity. (CS1, CS3)

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses lower temperatures but still requires a sealed reactive chlorine atmosphere and volatile chloride species. [Reasonable Inference] SS4/TS4 show that halide decomposition can occur under dry gas/vacuum over long times, but this is only weakly transferable because AuBr is not chemically similar to RbRuCl6. (SS4, TS4) Therefore, the route is not thermally severe but is operationally challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3

Vapor transport may provide good crystallinity, but RbCl and Ru chloride species likely have mismatched volatility. [Reasonable Inference] The AuBr analogue demonstrates kinetic control of halide formation over weeks but does not establish Rb–Ru–Cl transport chemistry. (SS4, TS4) The reaction kinetics and composition control are therefore less favorable than Route 1 or Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a large Rb+ site and a much smaller Ru-centered chloride octahedron, so the cation size mismatch check is triggered. [Reasonable Inference] The target description explicitly includes corner, edge, and face sharing among RbCl12 and RuCl6 polyhedra, triggering the mixed-connectivity check. The unit-cell atom-count check is not triggered because the provided target description does not indicate >40 atoms, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for such a large cell.

RbRuCl6 is likely to be a thermodynamically allowed or near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is slightly negative, effectively placing it on the hull. The most relevant analogues are the chemically similar chloride compounds made from binary chlorides in sealed ampoules or molten/annealed chloride environments. (CS1, CS3, CS4) SS and TS analogues provide little target-specific synthesis guidance because they are chemically unrelated to Rb–Ru–Cl, so they are best treated as neutral background evidence. [Reasonable Inference] The key limiting factor is maintaining Ru in the required chlorine-rich/high-valent environment without losing volatile Ru chlorides or forming competing Rb–Ru–Cl phases.
