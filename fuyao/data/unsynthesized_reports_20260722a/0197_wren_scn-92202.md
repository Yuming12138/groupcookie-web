### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The reported target E_hull is −0.0384 eV/atom, placing Sr₃Pd₂ in the highest stability bin. The closest chemical analogue Sr₂Pd has E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS-1), and Ba₂Pd is nearly stable with E_hull ≈ 0.002 eV/atom (CS-5), supporting a stable alkaline-earth–Pd phase field. SS and TS contain entries with low hull energies, but they are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct evidence for Sr–Pd thermodynamics, so their chemical contribution is neutral, score 50.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
The closest Sr₂Pd/Ba₂Pd analogues use moderate high temperatures of 710–770 °C, which avoids the >1000 °C penalty (CS-1, CS-5). The route requires H₂ pressure and air-free hydride handling, so controlled-atmosphere and air-sensitivity penalties are appropriate (CS-1, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). The major uncertainty is whether the binary Sr₃Pd₂ can be isolated directly or whether hydride intermediates require an additional dehydrogenation step [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
SrH₂ is a reactive Sr source, and the analogues show SrH₂ + Pd can react at 750–770 °C in 2 days (CS-1, CS-5). Pd remains a dense metallic reactant, so diffusion and homogenization are not trivial [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure and stoichiometry differ from cotunnite-type Sr₂Pd, so structural ordering may require additional annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Elemental melt synthesis of Sr-rich intermetallics used 1675 K in sealed Ta crucibles under Ar (CS-4), so high-temperature penalties are appropriate. Sr metal is air-sensitive and likely volatile at these temperatures, requiring sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. The route avoids H₂ pressure, but the higher temperature makes it more severe than Route 1.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Dense Sr/Pd elemental mixtures can suffer slow solid-state diffusion, especially because Pd is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. Melt or partial-melt processing should improve homogenization, consistent with Sr-rich intermetallic synthesis from elements in Ta crucibles (CS-4). Phase selection may be sensitive to cooling rate, as Sr₅In₃ required modified cooling conditions for improved phase purity (CS-4).

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Arc melting provides rapid alloying but imposes very high local temperatures and likely Sr volatility [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally/thermodynamically similar metallic analogues used arc melting and annealing in Ar, but they are chemically unrelated to Sr–Pd (SS-2, TS-2). Because SS/TS do not address volatile alkaline-earth behavior, they are neutral for Sr-specific risk, score 50.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Arc melting can overcome diffusion limitations by forming a molten alloy [Reasonable Inference], and arc/melt methods are reported for dense metallic materials in SS-2/TS-2. However, Sr loss can shift composition away from Sr₃Pd₂ and may promote competing Sr₂Pd or Pd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Phase-purity difficulty in related arc-melted materials suggests a long anneal and careful stoichiometry control are needed (SS-2, TS-2).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
Sr and Pd have a large size mismatch, which triggers the radius-difference check [Reasonable Inference]. The target is a simple binary intermetallic with only Sr and Pd and no explicit evidence for a >40-atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral network in the provided description; SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for those checks. Therefore, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr₃Pd₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is negative and chemically adjacent Sr₂Pd is on the hull (CS-1). The strongest support comes from CS, especially the Sr₂Pd analogue synthesized from SrH₂ and Pd under H₂, while SS and TS are mostly chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct Sr–Pd synthesis signal. The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated reactivity of SrH₂ with Pd at moderate temperatures to form Sr–Pd phases (CS-1, CS-5). The main limiting factor is isolating the binary Sr₃Pd₂ rather than hydride-containing or competing Sr₂Pd/Pd-rich phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target appears realistically synthesizable, with Route 1 being the most evidence-supported starting point.
