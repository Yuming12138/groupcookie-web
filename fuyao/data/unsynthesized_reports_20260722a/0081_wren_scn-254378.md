### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported E_hull of −0.06899 eV/atom places NaF₅ effectively on/under the convex hull, so the thermodynamic score is very high (Target data). Chemically similar fluorides include stable CaFeF₅ and SrNiF₆ at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom and a fluorine-rich K₂F₁₀ analogue at 0.102 eV/atom, showing that fluorides in this chemical neighborhood can be either stable or moderately metastable (CS-1–CS-3). The negative hull value may reflect numerical/reference-state uncertainty, so it should be interpreted as “strongly thermodynamically plausible” rather than literally below all competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient chemically specific thermodynamic evidence for Na–F polyfluoride synthesis and are treated as neutral signals (SS-1–SS-5, TS-1–TS-5).

#### F2 — Route 1 severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because CS-1 used room-temperature aHF/F₂ fluorination for 72 h. However, the use of anhydrous HF, F₂ gas, FEP/Kel-F hardware, and a vacuum line makes the severity high despite mild temperature (CS-1). The deductions are driven by reactive atmosphere, volatile sealed/corrosive medium, multistep handling, and toxic precursors (CS-1). The main uncertainty is whether NaF₅ requires higher F₂ pressure than K₂F₁₀ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The aHF medium should greatly improve fluoride mobility compared with dry solid-state fluorination [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 directly demonstrates room-temperature fluorination in aHF. Only Na is present as a cation, so cation interdiffusion is not a major barrier [Reasonable Inference]. The main kinetic penalty is structural reconstruction: NaF₅ requires incorporation of extra fluorine and formation of F–F bonding, and no CS/SS/TS entry provides a direct NaF-to-NaF₅ topotactic precedent. Overall, kinetics are favorable relative to dry solid routes but uncertain for phase ordering.

#### F2 — Route 2 severity

Route 2 uses moderate temperature but high F₂/N₂ gas pressure, analogous to SrNiF₆ synthesis at 280 °C and 130–140 atm in a Monell autoclave (CS-2). The pressure is not >5 GPa, so it does not trigger the GPa high-pressure penalty, but the F₂ atmosphere and sealed high-pressure apparatus are significant hazards (CS-2). Compared with Route 1, Route 2 avoids bulk HF solvent but increases mechanical pressure risk [Reasonable Inference]. The route is therefore severe but not as chemically corrosive as aHF solvolysis.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

High-pressure F₂ can drive highly fluorinated phase formation, as shown for SrNiF₆ (CS-2). Because the target contains only Na as a cation, cation diffusion and cation ordering are simple [Reasonable Inference]. However, the solid NaF precursor is less reactive than an aHF solution, and adding four extra F atoms per Na requires substantial anion rearrangement and possible F–F bond formation [Reasonable Inference based on Target]. This gives good but not excellent kinetic feasibility.

#### F2 — Route 3 severity

Route 3 uses low temperatures, inspired partly by the low-temperature halide decomposition route for AuBr under dry N₂/vacuum (SS-4/TS-4). It still requires F₂ or possibly HF, so it receives deductions for reactive atmosphere, volatile sealed components, and toxic precursors (CS-1, CS-2). It is operationally less severe than high-pressure fluorination if only modest F₂ pressure is used [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is that long low-temperature exposure may not reach equilibrium.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Low-temperature halide routes can be slow and kinetically sensitive; AuBr required about two weeks to one month and had reproducibility/phase-control issues (SS-4/TS-4). Dry NaF–F₂ contact is likely less reactive than aHF solution [Reasonable Inference]. The single-cation nature of NaF₅ is favorable for ordering, but formation of the target NaF₅ framework still requires anion rearrangement and F–F bonding [Reasonable Inference based on Target]. Thus, Route 3 is plausible but kinetically less reliable than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, Na, so there is no cation-radius mismatch criterion to trigger amorphization [Reasonable Inference based on Target]. The target description does not report a large unit cell or mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing, and the structure is described as a NaF₅ framework with Na–F coordination and F–F bonding (Target). CS/SS/TS do not provide useful direct atom-count or amorphization evidence for NaF₅, so those sources are neutral for this factor. The resulting amorphization risk is low, although local disorder in F–F/polyfluoride units remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

NaF₅ is likely thermodynamically accessible or very near a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.06899 eV/atom (Target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from the chemically similar K₂F₁₀ analogue, which was made using anhydrous HF and F₂ at room temperature, directly matching the need for a fluorine-rich alkali-fluoride environment (CS-1). High-pressure F₂/N₂ fluorination provides a second plausible route because SrNiF₆ was formed under 130–140 atm F₂/N₂ at 280 °C (CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining very high fluorine chemical potential while avoiding moisture, as shown by the use of aHF/F₂ or pressurized F₂ in the CS fluorination routes (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical limiting factor is phase control of an unusually fluorine-rich NaF₅ framework containing F–F bonding, for which no direct NaF₅ analogue is provided in CS/SS/TS; this is a [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure.
