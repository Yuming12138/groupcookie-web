### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The reported E_hull for CsTl3Se2 is −0.0543 eV/atom, which indicates a computed ground-state or very stable phase within numerical uncertainty [target data]. Chemically similar CsAu3Se2, RbAu3Se2, Rb2Hg3Se4, Cs2Pt4Se6, and Cs2Pt3Se4 are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the plausibility of stable alkali/late-metal selenide ternaries (CS-1–CS-5). SS/TS examples are not chemically close to Cs–Tl–Se, so they provide insufficient chemically specific evidence and are treated as neutral.

**F2/F3 — Route 1: sealed-tube solid-state.**  
The severity score is lowered by vacuum sealing, volatile Se/Tl containment, multi-step binary precursor preparation, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents; sealed high-vacuum week-long annealing is explicitly used in CS-1/CS-2. Kinetically, binary selenides should be more reactive than refractory oxides [Reasonable Inference], and low-temperature chalcogenide formation in CS-1/CS-2 supports manageable diffusion. The main uncertainty is structural reconstruction because the target is monoclinic and layered, while the closest CS Au analogues are trigonal (CS-1, CS-2).

**F2/F3 — Route 2: polyselenide/self-flux.**  
This route still requires sealed vacuum and toxic/air-sensitive precursors, but it avoids very high temperature and can use liquid/flux transport [Reasonable Inference]. Slow cooling in sealed tubes is supported by CS-1/CS-2, and excess chalcogen/alkali use is supported by CS-4/CS-5. The largest uncertainty is whether the flux window selects CsTl3Se2 rather than binaries such as Cs2Se/Tl2Se or other Cs–Tl–Se compositions [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 3: solvothermal.**  
CS-3 provides a strong precedent for alkali/late-metal selenide solvothermal synthesis in ethylenediamine at 160 °C using Rb2Se and HgCl2. The route has low thermal severity but still requires sealed tubes, Ar/vacuum handling, toxic Tl precursors, and air-sensitive alkali selenide [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are favorable due to solution transport, but structural selectivity is uncertain because CS-3 is Rb–Hg–Se rather than Cs–Tl–Se.

**F2/F3 — Route 4: high-temperature fusion.**  
CS-4/CS-5 show that Cs–Pt–Se chalcogenides can be prepared at 800–850 °C in Ar or H2 charged with selenium, so high-temperature fusion is plausible. However, for CsTl3Se2, Tl and Se volatility make this more difficult than for Pt analogues [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics improve at high temperature, but precursor reactivity and phase selectivity are weaker than in flux or solvothermal approaches [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size/coordination contrast: Cs is 7-coordinate to Se, whereas Tl is low-coordinate L-shaped or water-like (target data), which triggers the cation-radius/coordination mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description does not indicate a >40 atom cell, and it reports edge-sharing octahedra rather than simultaneous vertex/edge/face connectivity, so only one amorphization trigger is assigned (target data). CS/SS/TS do not provide additional useful amorphization evidence for this exact structure, so remaining uncertainty is moderate.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsTl3Se2 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative within computational uncertainty [target data]. Chemically similar alkali/late-metal selenides are experimentally accessible and often computed on the hull, especially Cs/Rb–Au–Se and Cs–Pt–Se analogues (CS-1–CS-5). The most enabling factor is the soft, low-temperature chalcogenide chemistry of Cs/Tl/Se, which should allow flux or sealed-tube reaction without refractory diffusion barriers [Reasonable Inference] and is consistent with low-temperature sealed chalcogenide syntheses in CS-1/CS-2. The most limiting factor is practical control of toxic Tl, volatile Se/Tl species, and air-sensitive alkali selenides [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues are not chemically representative, so their main role is neutral rather than strongly supportive.
