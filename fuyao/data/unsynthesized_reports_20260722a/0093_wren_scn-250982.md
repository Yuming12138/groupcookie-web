### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.047715726 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin. CS1, CS2, CS3, and CS4 are chemically similar chlorides with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and CS5 is only 0.0339 eV/atom above hull, showing that chlorine-rich halide compounds can be thermodynamically competitive. TS analogues also include E_hull values of 0.0–0.026 eV/atom, but they are chemically unrelated, so TS gives only a generic stability signal and is otherwise insufficient for chloride chemistry. The main uncertainty is that CoCl13 is highly chlorine-rich and may rely on high chlorine chemical potential not fully captured by a 0 K hull calculation [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 severity.**  
CS1 and CS5 use sealed evacuated ampoules, vacuum, temperature gradients, and volatile chlorinating components at 140–155 °C, so temperature severity is low but atmosphere/containment severity is significant. CS2 and CS4 indicate that highly chlorinated systems require dry or inert handling, supporting the air/moisture-sensitivity deduction. SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant severity evidence for this route and are treated as neutral 50 signals.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
CS1 and CS5 report crystal deposition over days, supporting a feasible vapor-transport crystallization pathway. The target’s Cl2 clusters and ribbons imply a narrow chlorine chemical-potential window, making composition control the main kinetic/thermodynamic coupling risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS are insufficient for direct kinetics of cobalt chlorides.

**F2 — Route 2 severity.**  
CS2 supports room-temperature or reflux solution synthesis under dry-box conditions, so there is no high-temperature or high-pressure penalty. The main severity deductions come from multiple handling steps and toxic/air-sensitive chlorinating reagents, both supported by CS2’s drybox and reflux/drying workflow. CS/SS/TS do not provide direct evidence for a cobalt version, so cobalt-specific hazards are [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
CS2 demonstrates that molecular chloride reagents in solution can react rapidly, from 15 min to several hours, giving high precursor activity and avoiding slow solid-state diffusion. The target structure is not isostructural with CS2, so nucleation of the specific CoCl13 arrangement is uncertain and the structural-reconstruction score is only moderate. SS and TS are insufficient for solution crystallization kinetics of CoCl13.

**F2 — Route 3 severity.**  
SS4/TS4 show low-temperature decomposition under dry N2 or vacuum, so temperature severity is low. However, long reaction times, controlled dry/vacuum atmosphere, and likely volatile halogen buffering justify moderate penalties. CS gives insufficient direct evidence for cobalt chloride decomposition to CoCl13.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
SS4/TS4 explicitly indicate sluggish halide phase transformations and poor reproducibility for AuBr, which lowers the structural ordering score. A cobalt chloride precursor may be reactive, but no supplied source demonstrates formation of CoCl13 by decomposition, so this route has high uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. TS provides the same AuBr evidence as SS4 but is chemically indirect.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has only one cation, Co, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The reduced formula contains 14 atoms, and the structural description does not establish a >40-atom unit cell or a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network. ### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely close to a ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0477 eV/atom, but the unusual CoCl13 stoichiometry and explicit Cl2 subunits suggest it may require a high-chlorine-chemical-potential environment to remain intact [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support comes from CS1 and CS5, which show that very chlorine-rich halide complexes can crystallize at low temperatures in sealed ampoules, and from CS2, which shows dry solution chemistry can isolate highly chlorinated molecular salts. SS and TS analogues provide little direct chemical support, except that TS confirms low-E_hull materials are often synthesizable and SS4/TS4 illustrate low-temperature halide decomposition behavior. The most critical enabling factor is low-temperature, moisture-free crystallization under high chlorine activity. The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control of volatile molecular chlorine, which may lead to decomposition or competing cobalt chloride phases [Reasonable Inference].
