### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has a reported E_hull of −0.09755269 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin by the provided rubric. Chemically similar fluorides in CS are also hull-stable, with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom for CdPtF₆, NiPbF₆, Pd(AuF₄)₂, Zn(AuF₄)₂, and Cd(AuF₄)₂ (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS are chemically mismatched to Tl–Ru–F, so they provide insufficient evidence for target-specific fluoride stability and are treated as neutral 50. The remaining uncertainty is whether the negative hull value reflects numerical correction issues or an actually stable low-temperature phase [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized mainly for requiring F₂ or F₂/Ar, since both NiPbF₆ and CdPtF₆-type fluorides require F₂-containing atmospheres (CS-1, CS-2). The temperature is moderate, because CS-2 reports 400–500 °C for ABF₆ formation, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Toxicity is significant because F₂ and Tl compounds are hazardous, and volatile Ru fluorides may require sealed or carefully controlled systems [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this fluoride fluorination severity and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
F₂ enhances precursor activity, and CS-2 shows ABF₆ fluorides can form within 4–5 h in F₂, which supports moderate kinetics. However, solid-state Tl/Ru cation ordering and oxidation-state control may be slow or incomplete [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s zero-dimensional RuF₆/Tl cluster structure differs from the corner-sharing ABF₆ frameworks in CS-1 and CS-2, lowering the structural reconstruction score. SS and TS do not provide chemically relevant kinetic guidance for TlRuF₆.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 avoids high temperature, since CS-3 demonstrates anhydrous HF solvothermal crystallization at 300 °C. It still receives strong penalties because anhydrous HF requires a sealed Monel autoclave and glovebox/inert handling (CS-3). Additional penalty is assigned for toxic/reactive media and likely precursor prefluorination [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS are insufficient for HF fluoride route severity and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
The HF solvothermal route scores highest kinetically because CS-3 shows single-crystal formation of a complex high-valent fluorometallate in anhydrous HF after one day. Solution-mediated fluoride transport should reduce the diffusion bottleneck relative to dry solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether TlRuF₆ nucleates the intended R-3m zero-dimensional phase rather than alternative Tl/Ru fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not supply useful target-specific kinetic evidence.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 has moderate temperature but severe operational constraints: CS-4 and CS-5 use sealed Au tubes, dry Ar, F₂/N₂ or F₂-related fluorination, and very long heating profiles. CS-1 also supports sealed Pt-tube annealing after F₂/Ar fluorination. The route is penalized for reactive atmosphere, sealed volatile fluoride chemistry, multi-step processing, and hazardous reagents. SS and TS are insufficient for this factor and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
CS-4 and CS-5 show that sealed noble-metal tube reactions can produce crystalline high-valent fluorides, but they require 30–40 days or similarly long treatments. This indicates slow equilibration and likely narrow vapor/fluorine chemical-potential windows. Vapor transport may help crystallization, but the target’s exact zero-dimensional structure is not represented in the CS examples [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide no useful fluoride-specific kinetic evidence.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target triggers only the cation-size-mismatch check, because Tl⁺ and high-valent octahedral Ru are very different in size [Reasonable Inference]. It does not trigger the large-cell criterion because the described cell contains three TlRuF₆ units, or 24 atoms, and it does not trigger mixed-connectivity because the target is described as zero-dimensional clusters. CS-1 and CS-2 suggest large cation contrast can still be accommodated in ABF₆ fluorides, reducing concern. SS and TS provide insufficient chemically specific evidence for amorphization and are treated as neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlRuF₆ is likely a thermodynamically accessible phase rather than a highly metastable one, because the reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar fluorides are hull-stable (target data; CS-1 to CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS, especially ABF₆ fluorination routes and high-valent fluorometallate HF/closed-tube methods (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS analogues are not chemically informative for Tl–Ru–F synthesis and are therefore treated as neutral rather than contradictory. The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high fluorine chemical potential to stabilize RuF₆ octahedra, as F₂/HF/high-valent fluoride environments are repeatedly used in CS routes. The most critical limiting factor is controlling volatile/reactive Ru and Tl fluoride chemistry while avoiding competing fluorides or decomposition [Reasonable Inference].
