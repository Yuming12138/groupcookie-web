## SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.128697958 eV/atom, which falls in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and is therefore scored 98/100. The negative value suggests the phase is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or at least within computational noise of a stable ground state [Reasonable Inference]. TS analogues are mostly near-hull/on-hull, but they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides, so there is insufficient evidence from TS for Rb–Os–O chemistry-specific stability.

### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate 625–700 °C conditions, matching the 550–700 °C range in CS1/CS2/CS5, so no high-temperature penalty is applied. The route does require sealed handling because CS1/CS5 use sealed Ag containers for alkali-rich oxides, and Rb oxides are air-sensitive as implied by Ar handling in CS2/CS5. The additional Os toxicity/volatility issue is a target-specific risk [Reasonable Inference], giving an F2 score of 80.

### F3 — Route 1

CS1/CS2/CS5 show alkali-rich oxides can form at moderate temperatures, supporting reasonable precursor activity and giving some confidence in the solid-state path. However, OsO₂ is likely refractory and Os diffusion is expected to be slow [Reasonable Inference]. The need to reconstruct from binary oxides into ordered monoclinic Rb₅OsO₆ and the multi-day anneals in CS1/CS5 lower the kinetic score to 47.

### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 remains below 1000 °C, following the 650–700 °C precedent of CS1. It is more severe than Route 1 because elemental Rb/RbO₂ mixtures are more reactive, require sealed loading, and may require ≥3 operational steps including precursor preparation and regrind/reseal, as suggested by CS1/CS5 workflows. Toxic/air-sensitive precursor penalties are applied, giving F2 = 70.

### F3 — Route 2

The reactive Rb/RbO₂ mixture should enhance precursor activity, and CS1 specifically supports elemental alkali + superoxide routes for alkali-rich oxides. Kinetics remain limited by the need to oxidize and incorporate Os into a high-valent OsO₆ environment without forming volatile OsO₄ [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, the route is chemically powerful but not kinetically simple, giving F3 = 50.

### F2 — Route 3

The flux route uses CS3/CS5-type sealed Pt/Ag-tube conditions at 650–680 °C, so the temperature severity is modest. The penalties arise from sealed volatile/air-sensitive alkali oxide flux, multiple processing steps, and Os toxicity/possible OsO₄ formation [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, F2 is 70 despite moderate temperature.

### F3 — Route 3

Flux/self-flux conditions should improve cation mobility and nucleation relative to dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference], and CS5 demonstrates single-crystal formation from alkali-rich oxide media at 650 °C for 14 d. The uncertainty is that CS3 required 6 months for a related Cs-rich oxide, showing that some alkali-rich oxide lattices have slow ordering kinetics. Balancing these signals gives the highest route-specific F3 score, 60.

### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The major amorphization trigger is the very large size/charge contrast between Rb⁺ and high-valent Os in OsO₆, consistent with the target’s long Rb–O and short Os–O bonds [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description does not show a large >40 atom cell or mixed vertex/edge/face sharing, so those triggers are not assigned. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient target-specific amorphization evidence, so F4 is set to 80, indicating low–medium risk.

## SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₅OsO₆ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is strongly negative. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS analogues, especially sealed Ag/Pt-tube alkali-rich oxides made at 550–700 °C under dry inert conditions or alkali oxide flux conditions. SS and TS analogues provide little chemistry-specific guidance because their compositions and anion chemistries are largely unrelated; they are treated as neutral/insufficient evidence where appropriate. The key enabling factor is the availability of highly reactive Rb oxide/peroxide/superoxide media that can provide both Rb activity and oxygen chemical potential. The key limiting factor is redox control of Os(VII), especially avoiding loss or hazard from volatile OsO₄ while still achieving sufficiently oxidizing conditions.
