### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is -0.043726412 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom category. Negative hull values are normally treated as numerical/on-hull stability within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS contains both near-ground-state silicides such as ZrSi2 with E_hull = 0.0 and NbSi2 with E_hull ≈0.0003 eV/atom (CS-2, CS-5), supporting that binary silicides can be thermodynamically robust. SS/TS examples include stable or near-stable materials, but they are chemically unrelated to Y–Si; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for binary Y–Si thermodynamic competition, so they are neutral beyond the general hull-stability signal.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melt / induction-melt

Condition severity is moderate because rare-earth silicide melt synthesis uses about 1600 °C under Ar (CS-3), triggering both high-temperature deductions. The route avoids high pressure and vacuum, but Y handling adds air-sensitivity risk [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetically, melting strongly improves homogenization compared with solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference from CS-3], but the target structure is not the same as known YSi2/YSi3 analogues (CS-1, CS-4), so post-annealing and phase selection remain uncertain.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed solid-state reaction

The solid-state route is conditionally severe because analogous silicide ampoule synthesis uses 1200 °C for 20–30 h (CS-5), and sealed/inert processing is likely needed to prevent oxidation or Si loss [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are relatively poor because dense elemental Y/Si powders require interdiffusion and structural ordering [Reasonable Inference], consistent with long sintering times in CS-5. The main uncertainty is whether Y3Si5 has a stable phase field wide enough to form before competing YSi2/YSi3 phases dominate [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Thin-film co-deposition

This route has favorable severity because direct Y–Si thin-film formation occurs at 423–773 K under vacuum (CS-1), avoiding bulk melting temperatures. The vacuum requirement and air-sensitive Y source reduce F2 (CS-1; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically, vapor-deposited multilayers have short diffusion lengths and CS-1 confirms post-deposition crystallization of YSi2, but exact Y3Si5 stoichiometry control may be narrow [Reasonable Inference from CS-1/CS-4].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: High-pressure high-temperature route

This route is penalized heavily for 12–15 GPa pressure and specialized multi-anvil/DAC equipment, as reported for YSi3 synthesis (CS-4). Its kinetic score is better than its severity score because small high-pressure capsules and pre-synthesized YSi2/Si precursors reduce diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. The uncertainty is whether pressure stabilizes Y3Si5 specifically rather than driving formation of known YSi3 or YSi2 phases (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2/F3 — Route 5: Combustion/metallothermic route

Combustion synthesis has simple equipment but multi-step post-treatment, as CS-2 reports ignition followed by acid/base washing and centrifuging. The route may be kinetically energetic due to exothermic local heating [Reasonable Inference from CS-2], but phase control is likely poor because CS-2 notes stoichiometry deviations and impurity-removal steps for disilicides. For Y3Si5, the uncertainty is larger because CS-2 demonstrates ZrSi2-type synthesis, not Y–Si combustion directly; insufficient evidence from SS/TS for this route.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency
