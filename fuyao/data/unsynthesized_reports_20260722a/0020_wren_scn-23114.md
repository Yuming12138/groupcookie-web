### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has reported E_hull = −0.1383 eV/atom, so it is scored at the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin. Several chemically similar K-rich oxides are computed on the hull, including K₄GeO₄, K₃SbO₄, K₃SbO₃, and K₆Ge₂O₇ (CS1–CS4), which supports stability of alkali-rich oxide compositions. TS analogues are low-hull but chemically unrelated, so TS provides insufficient chemistry-specific evidence and is treated as neutral. Remaining uncertainty is that a negative hull value may reflect database/reference inconsistency rather than a physically meaningful negative decomposition energy [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature because close K-rich oxide examples form around 600 °C to ~700 °C (CS1, CS2, CS3). It requires dry handling and air-sensitive K₂O, consistent with ultra-dry glovebox/sealed-tube practice in K₃XO₄ synthesis (CS5). The main uncertainty is whether TeO₃ remains stable and reactive at 550–650 °C without decomposition or Te loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

K₂O is chemically active, but a purely solid-state oxide route may be diffusion-limited, as K₄GeO₄ required 28 d at 600 °C (CS1). The target’s TeO₆ octahedron differs from tetrahedral GeO₄/CrO₄ analogues, so structural reconstruction is not trivial [Reasonable Inference using CS1/CS5]. The kinetics score is therefore moderate despite favorable cation mobility.

#### F2 — Route 2

The K + KO₂ route is supported by K₃SbO₄ and K₃SbO₃ syntheses under Ar at 973–975 K (CS2, CS3). Severity is increased by air-sensitive K metal and KO₂ and by controlled inert atmosphere handling (CS2, CS3). No >1000 °C or >5 GPa condition is required, so the penalty is mainly chemical hazard rather than thermal severity.

#### F3 — Route 2

K/KO₂ mixtures are highly reactive and can supply both K activity and oxygen activity, as demonstrated in alkali antimonate synthesis (CS2, CS3). This should improve kinetics relative to dense oxides [Reasonable Inference]. The limiting kinetic step is oxidation/coordination conversion of TeO₂ to Te(VI) TeO₆ and avoidance of side phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

The amide/autoclave route is based on K₆Ge₂O₇ synthesis in alkali amide melts at 530–600 °C for 6–8 d (CS4). It is penalized for NH₃/amide atmosphere, autoclave/sealed volatile medium, multi-step isolation, and air-sensitive reagents, all supported by CS4 and SS1/TS1. It avoids very high temperature and ultra-high pressure, so it remains challenging rather than prohibitive.

#### F3 — Route 3

Reactive amide melts should provide good transport and crystallization, as K₆Ge₂O₇ was obtained from GeO₂ in this medium (CS4). Supercritical ammonia systems can yield well-crystallized phases, but SS1/TS1 are chemically amides rather than oxides, so they are only weak kinetic support. The key uncertainty is tellurium redox stability in amide/NH₃ media [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4

The K-rich flux route uses moderate temperatures supported by K₄GeO₄ and K₆Ge₂O₇ precedents (CS1, CS4). It is less severe than the K/KO₂ route because K metal is not required, but still uses corrosive, hygroscopic, or oxidizing K-rich flux components [Reasonable Inference; drybox/sealed handling is supported by CS2/CS5]. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific oxide flux evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 4

The chemical-similarity evidence is strong for making K-rich oxides by sealed solid-state, superoxide, and flux-like routes, while SS/TS are mostly chemically unrelated and provide insufficient direct synthetic guidance. The most critical limiting factor is control of Te oxidation state and K volatility/moisture sensitivity, which must be inferred because no direct K–Te–O analogue is given [Reasonable Inference].
