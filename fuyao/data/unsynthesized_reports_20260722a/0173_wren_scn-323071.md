### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.016629 eV/atom, which places it clearly in the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability bin and justifies a high F1 score by the provided rubric. Chemically similar intermetallics Be15Ta6Cu8, TaCo2Al, and Be2W are reported with E_hull = 0 or near-stable values (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4), supporting the plausibility of stable refractory intermetallic formation. SS/TS entries also include stable or near-stable materials, but most are chemically unrelated to Be–Nb–Ir, so they are neutral-to-weak supporting evidence rather than decisive evidence.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Elemental powder sintering

The condition severity is high because chemically similar Be intermetallics require elevated temperatures and inert atmospheres: Be15Ta6Cu8 uses 1000–1200 °C under gettered Ar (CS-1), and Be2W requires 1650 °C for 32 h in Ar (CS-4). Kinetics are poor because the target contains refractory Nb/Ir metals [Reasonable Inference], and the Be2W analogue demonstrates that Be–refractory-metal intermetallic formation can require long high-temperature sintering (CS-4). The main uncertainty is whether Be3Nb2Ir forms at a lower temperature than Be2W because Ir/Nb may form lower-melting alloys with Be [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Arc melting followed by annealing

Arc melting has severe conditions but better kinetics because it bypasses long-range solid-state diffusion through transient/liquid mixing [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 directly supports arc melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing for a refractory intermetallic, and SS/TS-2 support arc melting plus ~1000 °C Ar annealing for another metallic system. The uncertainty is Be volatility under arc conditions, which is not directly tested in CS-3 but is strongly suggested by Be loss issues in Be2W synthesis (CS-4).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Thin-film sputtering

The thin-film route has lower thermal severity but weak evidentiary support because no CS/SS/TS entry reports Be–Nb–Ir or analogous Be-containing thin-film crystallization; therefore this route is mainly [Reasonable Inference]. Its kinetic advantage is atomic-scale mixing during deposition [Reasonable Inference], but crystallization into the ordered P6₃/mmc phase is uncertain. Composition control is likely difficult because Be loss/volatility is a known issue in high-temperature Be intermetallic synthesis (CS-4).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one strong amorphization-risk trigger because Be is much smaller than Nb and Ir [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is likely not large because the reported structure has only one crystallographic Be, Nb, and Ir site and a simple P6₃/mmc description [Reasonable Inference]. The target has edge- and face-sharing cuboctahedra, but not explicitly vertex + edge + face coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity criterion is only partially suggested and is not scored as triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Be3Nb2Ir is likely a ground-state or very-near-ground-state intermetallic because its E_hull is negative by the provided calculation. The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Be-containing refractory intermetallics made by solid-state sintering in inert Ar (CS-1, CS-4) and from arc-melted refractory intermetallic analogues followed by annealing (CS-3; SS-2; TS-2). The most critical enabling factor is the very favorable thermodynamic stability, which should drive phase formation if Be stoichiometry is maintained. The most critical limiting factor is Be management: Be is toxic and prone to loss at high temperature, and Be loss/phase-control problems are explicitly noted for Be2W (CS-4). Overall, synthesis is plausible but not routine, with arc melting plus annealing judged the best compromise between kinetics and phase control.
