### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.04943341 eV/atom, which strongly indicates predicted thermodynamic stability within the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin [Target data]. Chemically similar heavy-metal fluorides in CS1–CS5 are all reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting that this chemical family can host ground-state mixed fluorides. SS and TS examples include stable or near-stable phases, but their chemistries are mostly amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for TlIrF₆-specific chemistry; both are treated neutral for chemical stability transfer.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized mainly because CS1 requires high-pressure F₂ at 350–400 atm and 390–450 °C for 4 days, with pre-fluorination and specialized corrosion-resistant equipment. The temperature is not high by solid-state standards, but F₂ pressure, toxicity, and multi-step handling make the severity high (CS1). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for F₂ autoclave fluorination and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The highly reactive F₂ atmosphere should help overcome sluggish diffusion by producing mobile/reactive fluoride species, as implied by successful PtPbF₆ synthesis at only 390–450 °C (CS1). However, the target requires ordered TlF₆/IrF₆ octahedral corner sharing, while CS1 contains PtF₆ octahedra coupled to PbF₁₂ cuboctahedra, so structural transfer is incomplete. The 4-day dwell in CS1 indicates kinetics are not fast.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 avoids high external pressure and uses moderate temperatures, consistent with sealed-tube fluoride syntheses at ~400–550 °C (CS2, CS3, CS5). The penalties arise from sealed volatile/reactive fluorides, likely precursor pre-synthesis, toxic Tl compounds, and high-valent Ir fluoride handling [Reasonable Inference]. Compared with CS1, this is less severe because it avoids hundreds of atmospheres of F₂ during the final crystallization step.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Binary fluoride reactions are plausible because CS3 directly used ZrF₄ + PbF₂ in a sealed Pt tube, and CS2/CS5 used binary fluorides in Au tubes. The kinetic limitation is slow cation ordering/diffusion, especially for Ir-containing solids [Reasonable Inference], and CS2/CS5 required 30-day treatments. Structural reconstruction is moderate-to-large because none of the sealed-tube CS analogues exactly matches the target R-3 corner-sharing octahedral framework.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses lower temperature, around 300 °C, as shown for anhydrous HF solvothermal synthesis in CS4. The route remains severe because anhydrous HF, Monel autoclaves, glovebox handling, and reactive high-valent fluorides are required (CS4; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS give insufficient evidence for HF solvothermal fluoride chemistry and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

HF solvothermal conditions can improve kinetics by dissolving or transporting fluoride complexes, and CS4 reports single-crystal fluoride formation in anhydrous HF. This gives Route 3 the best kinetic score despite severe handling requirements. The uncertainty is whether TlF and Ir fluoride species have compatible solubility and redox stability under the same HF conditions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch, reflected by Tl–F = 2.67 Å versus Ir–F = 1.92 Å, which triggers the radius/bond-length mismatch criterion [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The target structure is otherwise simple: it is described as corner-sharing IrF₆ and TlF₆ octahedra with no reported edge/face sharing, and no large unit-cell complexity is indicated [Target data]. Therefore, amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlIrF₆ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.04943341 eV/atom [Target data]. The chemical-similarity evidence is supportive: multiple heavy-metal fluorides are known ground-state compounds and can be synthesized by pressure fluorination, sealed noble-metal tube reactions, or HF solvothermal methods (CS1–CS5). The strongest enabling factor is the high thermodynamic stability combined with precedent for noble-metal/high-valent fluoride synthesis in Au/Pt tubes and F₂/HF environments (CS1–CS5). The strongest limiting factor is redox/volatility control: Tl compounds are toxic and potentially volatile, while Ir must remain in a high fluoride oxidation state [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues provide little direct chemical transfer, so confidence is driven mostly by CS rather than by structural or thermodynamic-neighbor evidence.
