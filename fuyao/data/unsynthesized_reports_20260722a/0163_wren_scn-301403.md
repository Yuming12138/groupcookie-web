### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.034200691 eV/atom, which is within the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability range [Target data]. This strongly suggests a ground-state or near-ground-state phase, although negative hull values are interpreted as numerical tolerance rather than physically negative metastability [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues Sc₂Co₁₂P₇, Sc₂Fe₈P₄, LiCo₆P₄, and LaCo₈P₅ are also reported at or near E_hull = 0, showing that nearby phosphides often lie on stable convex-hull positions (CS1–CS5). SS/TS top examples include many E_hull ≈ 0 materials but are chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient material-specific thermodynamic transferability; neutral = 50 for SS/TS.

#### F2 — Route 1, tin-flux growth

The temperature severity is moderate because directly relevant phosphide flux syntheses use 850–900 °C rather than >1000 °C (CS1–CS4). The main penalties are sealed evacuated ampoules, volatile phosphorus, multiple operations, and handling reactive elements/acid flux removal (CS1–CS4; [Reasonable Inference]). This route is not extreme because no high pressure, ammonia, hydrogen, or ultra-high-temperature equipment is required (CS1–CS4). Remaining uncertainty is phase selectivity, because related flux syntheses produced multiple phosphides or side products such as CoP/Co₂P (CS1, CS5).

#### F3 — Route 1, tin-flux growth

Kinetics are helped by the Sn liquid flux, which is repeatedly successful for Sc–M–P and ACo₆P₄ phosphides (CS1–CS4). The exact P-6m2 LiCo₆P₄ analogue provides strong structural-template evidence for the target framework (CS3), and Sc–Co–P contains similar ScM₆P₆ cuboctahedral motifs (CS1). The main kinetic drawback is that Sc and Fe are dense metallic elements and the target requires ordered Sc/Fe/P assembly rather than precipitation from reactive salts [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient kinetic evidence for this route because their top examples rely on hydrothermal, oxide precipitation, decomposition, ammonolysis, or arc-melt methods in unrelated chemistries; neutral = 50.

#### F2 — Route 2, direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

The proposed annealing temperature of 850–950 °C follows the Sc–Fe–P sealed silica/tin-flux temperature window reported in CS2/CS4, so no >1000 °C penalty is applied. However, sealed vacuum conditions, volatile P, multistep prereaction/regrinding/annealing, and reactive Sc/P handling all add severity (CS2, CS4; [Reasonable Inference]). The route remains accessible in a conventional solid-state lab because evacuated silica tubes and furnaces are standard in the cited references (CS2, CS4). The uncertainty is higher than for flux growth because the provided CS evidence mostly uses Sn flux rather than flux-free solid-state synthesis for the closest Sc–Fe–P analogues (CS2, CS4).

#### F3 — Route 2, direct sealed-tube solid-state reaction

Kinetics are significantly worse than the flux route because Sc and Fe must interdiffuse as solids, and the target structure must nucleate from elements or binary phosphides [Reasonable Inference]. The long 850 °C/10-day heat treatment used for related Sc/Zr–Fe–P phosphides indicates that extended annealing is needed even with flux involvement (CS2, CS4). Without Sn flux, the system is more likely to retain FeP/Fe₂P or Sc–Fe–P competing phases [Reasonable Inference, supported by side-product issues in CS1/CS5]. SS/TS give insufficient route-specific evidence; neutral = 50.

#### F2 — Route 3, arc-melting/annealing

Arc or induction melting introduces high-temperature severity above 1000 °C and likely above 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS support arc melting plus ~1000 °C annealing for an intermetallic boride, but not for volatile phosphides (SS2, TS2). The route also requires prereacted phosphide feedstock, inert Ar, and post-annealing, which increases operational complexity [Reasonable Inference; SS2/TS2]. CS provides insufficient direct evidence for arc-melt synthesis of ScFe₆P₄; neutral = 50 for that route.

#### F3 — Route 3, arc-melting/annealing

Melting can improve macroscopic cation homogenization, as suggested by arc-melt preparation of U–Co–B intermetallics (SS2, TS2). However, phosphorus volatility can drive off-stoichiometry and Fe phosphide segregation during melting [Reasonable Inference]. The target P-6m2 ordered phosphide likely needs a post-melt anneal to form the correct framework [Reasonable Inference]. Because direct phosphide flux analogues are stronger than SS/TS arc-melt analogues, Route 3 is exploratory rather than preferred (CS1–CS4 versus SS2/TS2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains cations with substantially different sizes, Sc and Fe, which triggers the cation-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The unit cell is not expected to exceed 40 atoms based on the 11-atom ScFe₆P₄ formula and P-6m2 description [Target data; Reasonable Inference]. The structure explicitly includes edge-, face-, and corner-sharing polyhedral motifs, triggering mixed-connectivity risk [Target data]. Thus the risk is medium, mainly reflecting structural complexity and possible antisite/disorder rather than a strong expectation of amorphous product [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

ScFe₆P₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its computed E_hull is −0.034200691 eV/atom [Target data]. The strongest support comes from CS: Sc–Fe–P phosphides are already synthesized in Sn flux at 850 °C in evacuated silica tubes (CS2, CS4), and the exact P-6m2 ACo₆P₄ structure type is realized by LiCo₆P₄ under Sn-flux conditions (CS3). The most critical enabling factor is the Sn flux, which lowers kinetic barriers and supports crystal growth at 850–900 °C in multiple related phosphide systems (CS1–CS4). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among Fe-rich phosphides and related Sc–Fe–P compositions, because analogous flux systems report multiple phases and side products (CS1, CS5). SS and TS provide little direct additional support because their nearest examples are chemically dissimilar; they are treated as neutral evidence where route-specific transfer is unclear.
