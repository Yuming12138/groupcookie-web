### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The reported E_hull of Rb₂HgF₆ is −0.0485 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bracket by the required rubric. Chemically similar fluorides are also often stable or near stable, including Cs₂CuF₆ at 0.0086 eV/atom (CS-1), Cs₃NiF₇ at 0 eV/atom (CS-2), Cs₃Fe₂F₉ at 0 eV/atom (CS-4), and KHgF₃ at 0 eV/atom (CS-5). SS and TS do not provide chemically specific fluoride evidence; insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for Hg/Rb/F phase stability beyond generic near-hull behavior.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is severe because it transfers high-pressure F₂ autoclave conditions: Cs₂CuF₆ used F₂/Ar at ~350 bar and 400 °C for 5 weeks plus post-annealing (CS-1), and CsCuF₄ used pF₂ ≈ 350–360 bar at 400 °C in a Monel autoclave (CS-3). The temperature is not high by ceramic standards, but the fluorine pressure, corrosive atmosphere, toxic Hg precursor, and likely multistep preparation make the route challenging [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this F₂ autoclave severity and are scored neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The kinetics are moderately favorable because reactive fluorides under F₂ can form complex fluorides at 400 °C, as demonstrated for Cs₂CuF₆ and CsCuF₄ (CS-1, CS-3). However, Cs₂CuF₆ required 5 weeks and a post-anneal (CS-1), indicating slow phase ordering or a narrow phase window. The target’s simple A₂MF₆ stoichiometry helps, but Hg oxidation and phase selectivity remain uncertain [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is less severe than Route 1 because the closest Hg analogue synthesis used flowing F₂ at 300–350 °C for only 3–4 h, not high pressure (CS-5). The major penalties are still the F₂ atmosphere, toxic HgCl₂·2NH₃, dry handling, and possible volatility/reactivity of mercury fluoride species [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS/TS for Hg chloride fluorination conditions, so CS is weighted most strongly.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has favorable kinetics because HgCl₂·2NH₃ + MCl mixtures convert to MHgF₃ in 3–4 h at 300–350 °C under F₂ (CS-5). The uncertainty is that Rb₂HgF₆ is not the same stoichiometry as RbHgF₃ and likely needs stronger oxidation or higher fluoride chemical potential [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, precursor activity and diffusion are scored high, while structural/oxidation reconstruction is scored only moderate.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 inherits diluted-F₂ fluoride flux conditions at 300–500 °C for 1–5 days from A₃MF₇ preparations (CS-2). The temperature is moderate, but dried Ar handling, diluted F₂, flux processing, toxic Hg species, and post-reaction flux separation make it operationally challenging (CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for fluoride flux synthesis and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

The flux route receives the highest kinetic score because excess RbF should enhance fluoride activity and ion mobility [Reasonable Inference], consistent with A₂MF₆ + AF reacting under diluted F₂ at only 300–500 °C (CS-2). It is especially plausible for crystal growth or annealing after initial target formation [Reasonable Inference]. The limitation is that flux chemistry can shift equilibria toward Rb-rich competing phases, as phase control in alkali fluorides is reported to be difficult and moisture-sensitive (CS-2).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large size/coordination contrast between Rb–F distances of 2.99–3.14 Å and Hg–F distances of 2.12 Å, so the cation-radius-difference check is triggered by the structure description and ionic-size reasoning [Reasonable Inference]. The atom count is likely small, and the target description reports corner and face sharing but not edge sharing, so the unit-cell-size and full mixed-connectivity checks are not triggered. Remaining uncertainty is mainly whether local Hg–F distortion or off-stoichiometry could induce glassy/amorphous byproducts during fluorination [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb₂HgF₆ is likely thermodynamically accessible, possibly even a ground-state or slightly overbound computed phase, because its reported E_hull is −0.0485 eV/atom and chemically similar fluorides include multiple on-hull or near-hull examples (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4, CS-5). The strongest support comes from CS rather than SS/TS, because CS contains both A₂MF₆-type alkali fluorides and Hg-containing alkali fluorides, while SS/TS are chemically unrelated and give insufficient route-specific signal. The most critical enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential, supplied by flowing or pressurized F₂, as used for Cs₂CuF₆, CsCuF₄, and MHgF₃ analogues (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). The main limiting factor is stabilization of the required Hg–F octahedral chemistry at Rb₂HgF₆ stoichiometry without forming RbHgF₃, HgF₂, RbF, or other competing fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible but requires specialized fluorine and mercury handling.
