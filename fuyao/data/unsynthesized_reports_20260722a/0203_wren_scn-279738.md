### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported \(E_\mathrm{hull} = -0.0490\) eV/atom places it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom bin, so a high F1 score is justified from the target thermodynamic data. Chemically close Ti5RhSb2 and Ti5FeSb2 are experimentally synthesized even with reported positive hull energies of 0.0786 and 0.0224 eV/atom, respectively, which supports accessibility of this structure family (CS-1, CS-2). SS and TS do not provide Ti–Al–Sb-specific thermodynamic evidence; insufficient evidence from SS and TS, scored neutral 50 for source-specific thermodynamic transferability.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Arc-melting + annealing

Condition severity is high because arc-melting implies very high local temperature, followed by controlled Ar/vacuum processing and sealed-tube annealing (CS-1, CS-2; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically, the route is favorable because melting bypasses slow Ti solid-state diffusion, and the exact Ti5MSb2 I4/mcm analogues were synthesized by this method (CS-1, CS-2). The remaining uncertainty is Al/Sb volatility and whether Al orders cleanly on the Fe/Rh-like site rather than forming competing Ti–Al or Ti–Sb binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Direct sealed-tube sintering

This route avoids the extreme temperature of arc melting, but it still needs vacuum sealing, volatile Sb containment, and likely intermediate grinding (CS-3; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics are the main weakness because elemental Ti is refractory and CS-3 reports up to eight weeks at 800 °C for related Ti/Zr/Hf antimonides, implying slow diffusion (CS-3). The uncertainty is whether the I4/mcm target can nucleate directly under subsolidus conditions or whether it requires melt homogenization [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Metal-flux route

Flux synthesis has moderate condition severity because it needs sealed Ar ampoules, flux handling, decanting/removal, and volatile Sb/Zn or Al control (CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetically it is attractive because a liquid flux can enhance transport at 900–1000 °C, as seen in related Zr–Sb flux syntheses (CS-5). Its limitation is weaker structural transferability: the closest flux-supported compound is Zr5AlSb3-related and hexagonal rather than exact Ti5AlSb2 I4/mcm (CS-5).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a compact metallic intermetallic with simple Ti/Al/Sb coordination motifs and no reported mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network in the target description. The close I4/mcm analogues are crystalline ordered intermetallics prepared by conventional arc-melting and annealing, supporting low amorphization risk (CS-1, CS-2). SS/TS provide insufficient Ti–Al–Sb-specific amorphization evidence and are treated as neutral.
