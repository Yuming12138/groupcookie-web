### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s E_hull is −0.12008 eV/atom, placing it well inside the ≤0.05 eV/atom high-stability bin by the required rubric. Chemically similar fluorides in CS are all reported on the hull, including TlZrF5 and Tl2TiF6 (CS-1, CS-5), which supports the plausibility of stable Tl-containing fluoride salts. SS and TS provide insufficient direct chemical evidence for Tl–Ta–F, so their source-specific chemical signal is neutral, although TS/SS low-hull entries generically show that low-hull compounds are often synthesizable. Remaining uncertainty is the negative hull value itself, which may reflect reference-energy inconsistency, but it still indicates strong predicted stability.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The sealed solid-state route uses moderate temperatures of 300–400 °C, directly supported by Tl2TiF6 at 300 °C and TlZrF5 at 400 °C (CS-5, CS-1), so no high-temperature penalty is applied. Severity arises from dry Ar/dry-box handling, sealed Pt ampoules, toxic TlF, and likely volatile/moisture-sensitive TaF5 [Reasonable Inference; sealed/dry handling supported by CS-1 and CS-5]. The main uncertainty is whether TaF5 vapor transport improves reaction or instead causes stoichiometry gradients.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluoride precursors are relatively active because TlF + TiF4 and TlF + ZrF4 form ternary fluorides at only 300–400 °C (CS-5, CS-1). Kinetics should be favorable relative to oxide routes because the target consists of isolated TaF6 units, closely analogous to isolated TiF6 in Tl2TiF6 (CS-5). The remaining uncertainty is ordering of Tl positions and avoiding partial formation of TlF/TaF5-rich phases.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The fluorination route avoids very high temperature but uses highly reactive F2/Ar and pressure equipment; CS-4 reports F2 diluted with Ar in Monel autoclaves up to 300 atm and 300 °C. The rubric does not penalize 300 atm as “>5 GPa,” but fluorine toxicity and sealed pressure containment justify deductions for controlled atmosphere, sealed system, multi-step handling, and toxic precursors. The uncertainty is whether such aggressive fluorination will preserve Tl+ stoichiometry rather than form separate Tl fluorides or over-fluorinated species [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 2.**  
F2 is highly reactive and can form high-fluoride octahedral compounds at low temperature, as shown for CrHgF6 (CS-4). However, CS-4’s product is a 3D octahedral network, while TlTaF6 is zero-dimensional with isolated TaF6 clusters, so structural transfer is weaker. Kinetics are likely adequate, but selectivity is less certain than in Route 1 or Route 3 [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The HF route is low-temperature, so thermal severity is low [Reasonable Inference]. The deductions come from closed HF-compatible containment, multiple solution/crystallization/drying steps, and severe toxicity of HF and Tl salts [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS do not directly demonstrate TlTaF6 HF synthesis, so this route is chemically plausible but extrapolated.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
Solution crystallization should provide high precursor activity and minimal diffusion limitation because Ta and Tl are mixed as solvated/complex ions [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s isolated TaF6 cluster structure is especially compatible with crystallization from preformed fluoro-tantalate species [target; Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is hydrate/oxyfluoride formation during solvent removal, which could block crystallization of the anhydrous C2/c phase.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one amorphization trigger: a large cation size mismatch between Tl+ and Ta5+ [Reasonable Inference]. It does not trigger the atom-count criterion because the described conventional contents correspond to 32 atoms, and it does not trigger mixed-connectivity risk because the TaF6 octahedra are isolated with singly bonded F atoms. SS/TS structurally similar entries are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient useful signal for this specific amorphization factor, so the score is based mainly on the target structural description.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlTaF6 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or very strongly stable phase because its reported E_hull is −0.12008 eV/atom and the chemically similar CS fluorides are all on the hull (CS-1 to CS-5). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-5, where Tl2TiF6 contains isolated MF6 clusters and is made from TlF + TiF4 in a Pt ampoule under Ar, and from CS-1, where TlF + ZrF4 gives a Tl–metal fluoride under sealed dry Ar conditions. The most enabling factor is the target’s discrete [TaF6]− cluster chemistry, which should be accessible from either binary fluorides or fluoroacid solution [Reasonable Inference]. The main limiting factor is chemical handling: Tl toxicity, HF/TaF5 hazards, moisture sensitivity, and sealed-system requirements are significant [Reasonable Inference; dry sealed handling supported by CS-1 and CS-5]. Overall, synthesis appears feasible, with solution fluoroacid crystallization and sealed fluoride reaction as the two most credible routes.
