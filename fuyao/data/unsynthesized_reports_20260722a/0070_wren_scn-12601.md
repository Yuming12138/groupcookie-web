### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as −0.037818 eV/atom, which is within the highest stability bin and supports a high F1 score (target data). Chemically similar Cs fluorides in CS are often on or near the hull, including Cs₆Ni₂F₁₄, Cs₆Fe₄F₁₈, Cs₄Cu₃F₁₀, and CsAgF₃ at E_hull = 0.0, and Cs₂CuF₆ at ~0.0086 eV/atom (CS). The major uncertainty is chemical plausibility: Cs₂LiF₆ is formally difficult to charge-balance using ordinary Cs⁺, Li⁺, and F⁻ oxidation states **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS entries are thermodynamically similar but chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient evidence for Cs–Li–F stability and are neutral score 50 (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is severe because the closest Cs₂CuF₆ analogue used F₂/Ar in an autoclave at ~350 bar and 400 °C for 5 weeks, followed by lower-temperature post-annealing (CS). The temperature is moderate, but the reactive fluorine atmosphere, sealed pressure vessel, and long duration impose significant operational difficulty (CS). The score is not lower because no >5 GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperature is required by the analogue (CS). Remaining uncertainty is whether Li-containing Cs₂LiF₆ can be stabilized even under high fluorine chemical potential **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 1

The kinetics are favorable relative to conventional refractory solid-state reactions because the Cs₂CuF₆ analogue forms at 400 °C in a reactive fluorine medium (CS). The target and Cs₂CuF₆ share the CsF₁₂/MF₆ polyhedral motif, which reduces the expected structural reconstruction penalty (CS + target description). However, LiF is expected to be a less active fluoride precursor than high-valent CuF₃ **[Reasonable Inference]**. The major uncertainty is whether the reaction path can generate a chemically meaningful Li-centered F-rich octahedral phase **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 2

Sealed-tube fluoride synthesis is less severe than F₂ autoclave work because Cs₃Fe₂F₉ was made in a sealed Pt tube at 700 °C for 2 days, and Cs₄Cu₃F₁₀ was made in a closed Au tube under dried Ar at 630 °C (CS). The route still requires dry handling, sealed noble-metal containers, and possibly added fluorinating agents for the F-rich target **[Reasonable Inference]**. No evidence requires multi-GPa pressure or >1000 °C temperature (CS). The uncertainty is that sealed binary CsF/LiF reaction alone may not supply enough fluorine chemical potential for Cs₂LiF₆ **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F3 — Route 2

Kinetics are moderate because CS sealed-tube analogues crystallize Cs fluorides from binary fluorides at 630–700 °C (CS). CsF is a demonstrated precursor, but LiF is not validated in the CS set and is likely less reactive than transition-metal fluorides such as FeF₃ or CuF₂ **[Reasonable Inference]**. Full assembly of the target framework from binary salts likely requires more reconstruction than Route 1 or flux growth **[Reasonable Inference]**. Remaining uncertainty is high because no SS/TS entry provides useful Li–fluoride structural support, so SS/TS are neutral (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 3

Flux growth under fluorinating conditions inherits both the benefits and hazards of CS fluoride routes: Cs₃NiF₇ used fluoride-rich mixtures under dried Ar and diluted fluorine at 300–500 °C, while CsAgF₃ used sealed, dry conditions at 360–420 °C (CS). The route avoids extreme temperature and pressure, but F₂ or equivalent fluorinating conditions are severe and moisture-sensitive (CS). Multi-step handling and slow cooling are expected for crystal growth **[Reasonable Inference]**. The score is therefore limited mainly by atmosphere and containment, not by temperature.

#### F3 — Route 3

Flux growth receives the best kinetics score because molten or partially molten alkali fluorides should improve ion transport and nucleation **[Reasonable Inference]**. CS analogues show Cs fluoride phases can be made at 300–500 °C under fluorinating flux/tempering conditions and at 360–420 °C in sealed systems (CS). The structural target resembles the CsF₁₂/MF₆ framework of Cs₂CuF₆, supporting a plausible crystallization pathway (CS + target description). The remaining uncertainty is again the absence of a validated Li analogue and the formal charge-balance problem **[Reasonable Inference]**.

#### F2 — Route 4

Thin-film deposition is an extrapolated route because no CS/SS/TS entry reports vapor deposition of Cs–Li fluorides; thus direct source evidence is insufficient and neutral (CS/SS/TS). It avoids high bulk temperature and high pressure **[Reasonable Inference]**. However, it requires vacuum systems, post-fluorination, and air-sensitive fluoride handling **[Reasonable Inference]**. Its condition severity is comparable to Route 3 but with lower direct evidence support.

#### F3 — Route 4

The kinetic score is low-to-moderate because CsF and LiF film deposition plus post-fluorination is plausible but not supported by the provided analogues **[Reasonable Inference]**. Crystallization of the P-3m1 target from an amorphous or polycrystalline film would likely need post-annealing **[Reasonable Inference]**. Composition control is expected to be difficult because Cs-containing films can be volatile or moisture-sensitive **[Reasonable Inference]**. SS/TS entries provide no useful thin-film signal for this fluoride chemistry and are neutral (SS/TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The main amorphization risk is the very large size mismatch between Cs⁺ and Li⁺, triggering the cation-radius criterion **[Reasonable Inference]**. The target description indicates ordered CsF₁₂ cuboctahedra and LiF₆ octahedra, and only corner plus face sharing is reported, so the mixed vertex+edge+face connectivity criterion is not triggered (target description). The formula unit is small, and there is insufficient evidence that the crystallographic unit cell contains >40 atoms, so C2 is not triggered **[Reasonable Inference]**. The resulting F4 score is low–medium risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target appears thermodynamically favorable by the reported negative E_hull, so it should be treated as a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state phase by the provided calculation (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Cs fluorides, especially Cs₂CuF₆, which shares the CsF₁₂/MF₆ fluoride framework and was synthesized under high fluorine chemical potential (CS). SS and TS analogues are mostly unrelated in chemistry and therefore provide insufficient direct synthesis guidance for Cs₂LiF₆, so their signal is neutral (SS/TS). The most important enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature fluoride routes using CsF, sealed systems, fluxes, and F₂/Ar atmospheres demonstrated in CS (CS). The most important limiting factor is the unusual formal chemistry of Cs₂LiF₆, because ordinary Cs⁺/Li⁺/F⁻ valences do not charge-balance the formula **[Reasonable Inference]**. Therefore, the best experimental strategy is a high-fluorine-potential CsF-rich flux search with rigorous moisture exclusion and careful phase analysis (CS + [Reasonable Inference]).
