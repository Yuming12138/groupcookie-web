### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is reported as −0.027912375 eV/atom, so it receives the maximum score under the given rubric. Chemically similar Cs₂CaF₄ and Cs₂AgF₄ are reported as stable or near-stable and experimentally synthesized, supporting the idea that low-hull Cs ternary fluorides can be accessible (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). TS entries are synthesized near-hull phases, but they are chemically unrelated amides/borides/phosphates/bromides/nitrides, so TS provides only weak generic support and insufficient route-specific evidence.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, consistent with CS fluoride syntheses at 270–700 °C (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3). Deductions are driven by sealed dry atmosphere, moisture-sensitive fluoride handling, and use of an added fluorinating source, which is extrapolated from the target’s F-rich stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 explicitly required strict water control and sealed handling, supporting the severity penalty.

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Binary fluoride precursors are proven workable in CS analogues, including CsF + CaF₂ and CsF + AgF₂ (CS-1, CS-3). However, the target is triclinic P1 and structurally unlike the tetragonal K₂NiF₄-type CS-1/CS-2/CS-3 phases, so structural reconstruction/order is a kinetic concern [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 shows that complex low-symmetry Cs fluorides may require extremely long anneals, lowering the kinetics score.

**F2 — Route 2.**  
The flux route remains below 1000 °C but requires sealed, dry, chemically resistant containers, similar to the sealed Pt/Au setups used for CS fluorides (CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). It also requires multi-step preparation and likely a fluorinating additive because CsF + LiF alone cannot supply Cs₂LiF₄ stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. The condition severity is therefore moderate-to-high despite moderate temperatures.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux growth improves ion mobility compared with dry-powder sintering [Reasonable Inference], and CS examples demonstrate that Cs fluoride phases can form in the 270–700 °C range (CS-1, CS-3). The target’s many inequivalent sites and corner/edge-connected CsF₆/LiF₄ network imply ordering demands [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 supports the caution that low-symmetry Cs fluoride phases can require long thermal programs.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Thin-film synthesis has no direct support in CS/SS/TS, so the route is entirely extrapolated [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids high bulk temperatures and GPa pressure, but vacuum deposition, reactive fluorination, and volatile Cs/F management impose severity penalties [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS, SS, and TS is treated as neutral for direct thin-film feasibility.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The kinetics score is lower because vapor delivery and stoichiometry control for Cs–Li–F are expected to be difficult [Reasonable Inference]. No CS/SS/TS analogue demonstrates direct thin-film crystallization of this structure type, and the target likely requires post-deposition fluorination and ordering [Reasonable Inference]. The route may help access metastability but is not strongly evidenced.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large Cs/Li size mismatch and distinct CsF₆ vs LiF₄ coordination, triggering the cation-radius criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The described P1 cell contains 14 atoms, so it does not trigger the >40 atom criterion. The structure has corner and edge sharing, but no face sharing is reported, so the strict mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is formally predicted as a ground-state or very stable phase because its reported E_hull is negative, but the unusual Cs₂LiF₄ stoichiometry is chemically concerning under standard Cs⁺/Li⁺/F⁻ valence counting [Reasonable Inference]. The chemical-similarity set strongly supports the general feasibility of sealed dry synthesis of Cs-rich ternary fluorides, especially using CsF and binary fluorides in Pt/Au containers (CS-1, CS-2, CS-3, CS-5). Structural and thermodynamic similarity sets provide little chemically specific guidance for Cs–Li fluorides; SS and TS are mostly insufficient for route design and are treated as neutral where not directly applicable. The most important enabling factor is the known success of sealed, anhydrous fluoride chemistry for Cs₂MF₄-type compounds (CS-1, CS-3). The most important limiting factor is the target’s charge/stoichiometry anomaly and the need to control fluorine chemical potential without forming simpler CsF/LiF or other alkali fluorides [Reasonable Inference].
