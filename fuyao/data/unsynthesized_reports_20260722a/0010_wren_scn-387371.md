### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = −0.014464978 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable within numerical uncertainty, so it scores 98/100. CS supports the plausibility of stable anhydrous transition-metal/rare-earth phosphates: Ir phosphate and W phosphate analogues are on the hull, and Lu/Gd metaphosphates are near-hull (CS-1 to CS-4). SS and TS sets contain several stable materials but are mostly chemically unrelated to Os–P–O; therefore, insufficient chemistry-specific evidence from SS/TS, scored neutral = 50 for source signal. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by the possibility that DFT oxygen chemical potentials and Os redox energetics may not fully capture experimental OsO₄ volatility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: CVT

The CVT route has severe conditions because CS-1 uses evacuated silica ampoules and a 900 → 800 °C gradient with chloride transport, and the target adaptation would require volatile phosphate and toxic Os handling. Its kinetics are favorable relative to bulk solid-state synthesis because vapor transport bypasses long-range Os diffusion, and the Ir analogue has very similar MO₆–PO₄ corner-sharing topology and M–O bond lengths (CS-1). The main uncertainty is that Ir and Os chloride/oxide vapor equilibria are not identical [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for this specific route.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Sealed-Tube Solid-State

The sealed-tube route is supported by WPO₅ formation from WO₃, W metal, and P₂O₅ at 1250 °C for several days in fused silica (CS-3). It scores poorly in kinetics because OsO₂ and possible Os metal are refractory, and complete reconstruction to the OsP₂O₇ network would be needed [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 also reports phase-control difficulty, with WPO₅ initially obtained as a minor product before optimization. SS/TS do not provide useful Os–phosphate sealed-tube evidence and are neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Ammonium-Phosphate Solid State

This route has milder equipment and temperature requirements because Lu(PO₃)₃ was formed from Lu₂O₃ and (NH₄)₂HPO₄ after a 350 °C preheat and 600–1000 °C firing for 24 h (CS-2). The ammonium phosphate precursor is reactive, but OsO₂ diffusion and redox stability limit kinetics [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 noted trace LuPO₄ impurity, so competing phosphate phases remain plausible. SS/TS CoAgPO₄ precipitation/calcination gives only weak general support for phosphate crystallization at moderate temperature (SS-3, TS-3).

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: Phosphate Flux

Flux growth scores best overall because CS-4 demonstrates phosphate crystal growth from excess H₃PO₄ flux at relatively low temperature with controlled cooling. Liquid flux should reduce diffusion barriers and improve crystallization compared with dry oxide reaction [Reasonable Inference]. However, the chemical match is weaker than CVT because CS-4 is a redox-inert Gd phosphate, not an Os(IV) phosphate, and SS/TS provide insufficient Os-specific evidence. The expert adjustment penalizes this uncertainty.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains strongly size- and charge-contrasting cations, Os in octahedral coordination and P in tetrahedral coordination, which triggers the ionic-radius mismatch check [Reasonable Inference]. The structure description reports corner-sharing OsO₆–PO₄ and P–O–P connections but no coexistence of edge or face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided, and the formula has only 10 atoms, so evidence for >40 atoms is insufficient. The resulting low–medium amorphization risk is consistent with many crystalline phosphate analogues in CS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a near-ground-state or ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is slightly negative within numerical tolerance, and several chemically related phosphates in CS are stable or near-stable. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-1, where an Ir phosphate with comparable IrO₆–PO₄ corner-sharing topology was obtained by CVT, and from CS-4, where phosphate flux growth produced crystalline metaphosphate. The most important enabling factor is the robust phosphate framework chemistry: PO₄ tetrahedra readily form corner-sharing networks with high-valent metal–oxygen octahedra, as seen in CS-1, CS-2, and CS-3. The most important limiting factor is osmium redox/volatility control, especially avoiding formation of volatile and highly toxic OsO₄ under oxidizing conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, OsP₂O₇ appears synthesizeable but will require careful atmosphere, phosphate activity, and phase-purity control.
