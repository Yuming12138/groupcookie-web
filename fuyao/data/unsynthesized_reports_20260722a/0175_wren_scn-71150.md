### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The exact target E_hull is −0.0089 eV/atom, placing Ca₂Al₃Cu₇ in the ≤0.05 eV/atom category and justifying a very high F1 score from the supplied target thermodynamic data. Thermodynamic analogues are also favorable: TS1–TS5 all have E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, and several chemically/structurally similar compounds are stable or near-stable, including CaCu₄In at 0.0205 eV/atom (CS2, SS1). CS and SS do not by themselves prove the target phase is experimentally isolated, so they are supporting rather than decisive evidence; insufficient evidence from CS/SS for exact target phase purity is treated as uncertainty, not as a penalty to the hull-based score.

#### Route 1 — F2 condition severity

Route 1 uses high-temperature melting and long sealed annealing, which are common for analogues but still moderately severe: Ca–Cu–Sn analogues require induction melting at 1200–1250 °C plus 600–700 °C annealing (SS3, TS2), and CaCu₄In is grown from elements at 1450 K (CS2, SS1). The need for sealed/inert handling is supported by Ta crucibles and Ar atmospheres in SS3/TS2 and by sealed ampoules in SS2/TS1. The main uncertainty is whether Ca₂Al₃Cu₇ forms congruently or needs a narrower annealing window [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 1 — F3 kinetics

Elemental metals are relatively low-activity precursors by the rubric, but induction melting largely overcomes initial diffusion barriers, as shown for CaCu₄In and Ca–Cu–Sn analogues (CS2, SS1, SS3, TS2). The target’s monoclinic P2/m structure contains five inequivalent Cu sites and two inequivalent Al sites, so ordering is likely slower than in cubic CaCu₄In [Reasonable Inference from target structure and CS2/SS1]. Long anneals reported for RE–Mg–Cu and Ca–Cu–Sn analogues support a moderate kinetic penalty (SS2–SS4, TS1–TS3, TS5).

#### Route 2 — F2 condition severity

Arc melting plus annealing is not exotic but involves high temperature, air-sensitive Ca, and sealed post-annealing. CS5 reports arc melting Ca/Ni/In under purified Ar, sealing in evacuated quartz, annealing for three months, and quenching; CS4 reports arc melting followed by sealed Ta-tube heat treatment. No high-pressure or reactive gas chemistry is required in CS4/CS5, so the severity is moderate rather than extreme.

#### Route 2 — F3 kinetics

Route 2 has better kinetics than Route 1 because repeated arc melting can rapidly homogenize dense metallic elements, as explicitly used for Ca₃Ni₈In₄ (CS5). The long anneal remains important because the target has multiple Cu/Al sublattices and lower symmetry than many analogues [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. CS5 is especially relevant because it describes a complex Ca–transition metal–p-block intermetallic requiring long annealing, matching the expected ordering problem.

#### Route 3 — F2 condition severity

Flux/self-flux growth can reduce diffusion severity but introduces vacuum/sealed-tube requirements, slow cooling, and flux separation. CS3 demonstrates that Ca-containing intermetallic flux growth can require vacuum sealing, multiday thermal profiles, and post-synthesis etching/washing. No direct Ca–Al–Cu flux example is provided, so insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS makes this route more uncertain than Routes 1–2.

#### Route 3 — F3 kinetics

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because a liquid medium improves mass transport; CS3 explicitly uses a low-melting Ca–Zn eutectic solvent to grow new Ca-containing intermetallics. For Ca₂Al₃Cu₇, an Al-rich or Cu–Al self-flux is a [Reasonable Inference], not directly proven by CS/SS/TS. The main uncertainty is whether the desired 2:3:7 phase crystallizes before competing Ca–Al, Cu–Al, or Ca–Cu phases.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target contains a large electropositive Ca atom with smaller Cu/Al framework atoms, giving a significant size mismatch [Reasonable Inference from elemental radii]. The target structure also has mixed corner-, edge-, and face-sharing cuboctahedral connectivity, which is explicitly stated in the target description and is also common in CaCu₄In and Ca–Cu–Sn analogues (CS2, SS1, SS3, TS2). The unit-cell atom count cannot be confidently assigned from CS/SS/TS; therefore that check is not triggered, and the score reflects medium amorphization/ordering risk rather than high risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₂Al₃Cu₇ is likely to be a ground-state or very near-ground-state intermetallic because its computed E_hull is −0.0089 eV/atom, and the closest thermodynamic analogues are all reported on the hull (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is reasonably strong because chemically and structurally related Ca/Cu/p-block and Cu-rich intermetallics are repeatedly made from elemental melts followed by annealing (CS2, CS5, SS1, SS3, TS2). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of liquid-phase or fully molten processing, which overcomes slow diffusion among dense metallic precursors (CS2, CS5, SS3, TS2). The most critical limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control, especially Ca retention and Cu/Al ordering across several inequivalent sites [Reasonable Inference from target structure and Ca-excess practice in CS5]. Overall, the material is plausibly synthesizable in a well-equipped intermetallic synthesis lab but may require annealing optimization and careful phase analysis.
