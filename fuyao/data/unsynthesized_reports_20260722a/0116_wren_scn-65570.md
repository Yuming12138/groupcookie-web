### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is −0.067863 eV/atom, which places it effectively on the convex hull and therefore strongly favors synthesizability [Reasonable Inference from target data]. Chemically similar alkali tellurides K3SbTe3, K3AlTe3, Cs2MnTe2, K2ZrTe3, and Rb2PtTe2 all have E_hull = 0.0, showing that nearby chemical spaces contain stable phases (CS1–CS5). SS and TS do not provide useful Rb–Si–Te thermodynamic signal because their listed examples are unrelated chemistries; insufficient evidence from SS/TS, neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

The elemental sealed-ampoule route avoids extreme temperature and high pressure, matching CS1/CS2 syntheses at 973–1075 K in evacuated graphitized quartz ampoules. The main severity penalties are vacuum sealing, volatile Rb/Te components, and air-sensitive Rb handling [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS give insufficient route-specific evidence for alkali telluride ampoule synthesis and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are plausible because CS1/CS2 show direct elemental reactions can crystallize A3MTe3-type alkali tellurides. The limiting kinetic issue is Si, a covalent solid that may react more slowly than Sb or Al [Reasonable Inference]. The structure is chemically related to K3SbTe3 through A3MTe3 stoichiometry and Te–alkali–M coordination motifs (CS1), but the Si–Si bond in the target adds reconstruction uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 2

The Rb2Te precursor route is moderate in severity because it uses glovebox handling and evacuated ampoules, as in Rb2ZrTe3 synthesis (CS4). It does not require pressure above sealed-ampoule autogenous pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C if optimized [Reasonable Inference]. Its hazards arise from air-sensitive Rb2Te and volatile/toxic Te [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Rb2Te should improve Rb–Te preorganization and chemical activity compared with elemental Rb [Reasonable Inference], and CS4 directly supports Rb2Te as a precursor for Rb tellurides. Diffusion should be assisted by telluride/Te melt components [Reasonable Inference]. However, CS4 notes impurities for Rb2ZrTe3 and failure of a Cs analogue, indicating that phase selection can be sensitive.

#### F2 — Route 3

Carbonate tellurization avoids sealed ampoule pressure but requires a reactive H2/chalcogen atmosphere, as shown in CS3 and CS5. The temperature range is not extreme, but H2/Te handling creates safety and composition-control penalties [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS are insufficient for carbonate-to-Rb–Si–Te transferability and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3

Carbonate routes are proven for alkali transition-metal tellurides (CS3, CS5), but the Rb–Si–Te target requires oxygen removal and formation of Si–Te/Si–Si units from a carbonate-derived mixture [Reasonable Inference]. This gives lower precursor activity and greater structural reconstruction burden. The absence of a Si analogue in CS increases uncertainty.

#### F2 — Route 4

The flux route has similar severity to sealed ampoule synthesis because it requires vacuum sealing, volatile Te, and air-sensitive Rb/Rb2Te [Reasonable Inference based on CS1/CS2/CS4]. It does not require high pressure or temperatures above 1000 °C. Slow cooling adds time but not a formal severity penalty.

#### F3 — Route 4

Rb–Te/Te self-flux should improve mass transport and crystallization compared with dry solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. CS1/CS2/CS4 support high-temperature sealed telluride formation, and CS4 demonstrates controlled cooling for alkali telluride crystals. The main uncertainty is whether Te-rich flux stabilizes the desired Rb3SiTe3 rather than competing Te-rich or binary Rb–Te/Si–Te phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size/chemistry mismatch between Rb and Si, which can promote local structural frustration [Reasonable Inference]. The target description explicitly contains coexisting corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra, which increases topological complexity and amorphization/poor crystallinity risk; similar mixed sharing is also present in K3SbTe3 (CS1). There is insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS that the target unit cell exceeds 40 atoms, so that check is not triggered and SS/TS remain neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3SiTe3 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is negative and chemically related alkali tellurides are on the hull (CS1–CS5). The strongest support comes from CS, especially direct ampoule synthesis of A3MTe3 analogues K3SbTe3 and K3AlTe3 (CS1, CS2), while SS and TS are not chemically informative and are treated as neutral. The most important enabling factor is the well-precedented sealed-ampoule alkali telluride chemistry using graphitized quartz and high Te activity (CS1, CS2, CS4). The main limiting factor is phase and stoichiometry control involving volatile Rb/Te and the need to form Si–Te plus Si–Si bonding, which is not directly demonstrated in the references [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best odds from a Te-rich Rb–Te self-flux or carefully controlled direct sealed-ampoule reaction.
