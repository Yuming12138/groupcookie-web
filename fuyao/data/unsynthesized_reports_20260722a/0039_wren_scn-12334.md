### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.251959463 eV/atom, which strongly satisfies the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability criterion and justifies the maximum thermodynamic score (target data). Several chemically similar fluorides are reported at or near the hull, including BaF₂ at 0.0 eV/atom, CsAgF₃ at 0.0 eV/atom, and Cs₂AgF₄ polymorphs at 0.0–0.021 eV/atom (CS-1/CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). SS and TS entries are mostly chemically unrelated to Cs–F chemistry, so they provide insufficient specific validation and are neutral, 50. The remaining uncertainty is that a negative hull value may reflect reference-set limitations rather than guaranteed experimental stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure, which is favorable relative to the 270–420 °C Cs–Ag–F solid-state analogues (CS-3/CS-5). The penalties arise from reactive F₂, sealed fluorination, and toxic/corrosive handling, all inferred from the F₂ fluorination precedent used to make AgF₂ in CS-4/CS-5 and from the sealed/dry requirements in CS-3/CS-5. SS and TS provide insufficient condition-specific guidance for CsF₂ and are neutral, 50. The main uncertainty is the unknown F₂ pressure/temperature window needed to stabilize CsF₂ rather than CsF or other polyfluoride-like products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics should be favorable because CsF is a simple ionic precursor used in Cs fluoride syntheses (CS-2/CS-3/CS-4/CS-5), and F₂ is expected to provide high fluorinating activity [Reasonable Inference]. Cs–F bond lengths in the target, 2.99–3.18 Å, match the range observed in CsAgF₃ and Cs₂AgF₄, about 2.96–3.22 Å, supporting geometrical compatibility (CS-3/CS-5). The structural-reconstruction score is only moderate because transforming CsF into tetragonal CsF₂ requires fluorine uptake and likely a different fluorine arrangement [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS are insufficient for kinetics of this chemistry and are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 uses lower nominal temperatures than CS-3/CS-5 analogues, but sealed-tube fluorination and volatile XeF₂/gas evolution impose safety and containment penalties [Reasonable Inference]. The dry sealed handling is directly analogous to Cs–Ag–F syntheses under Ar and sealed Au/metal systems (CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). SS and TS provide no useful route-specific severity signal and are neutral, 50. The key uncertainty is whether XeF₂ transfers fluorine cleanly without forming trapped xenon-containing or fluorine-deficient products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

The active fluorinating reagent and simple one-cation target support relatively good kinetics [Reasonable Inference], while CsF is a proven precursor in multiple Cs-containing fluorides (CS-2/CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). The lower reaction temperature enabled by XeF₂ may reduce decomposition risk but could slow long-range ordering [Reasonable Inference]. Structural reconstruction remains a moderate barrier because the route does not start from an isostructural precursor [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS are insufficient for this kinetic assessment and are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 adds a mechanochemical step, so it is more operationally complex than Routes 1–2 [Reasonable Inference]. The dry-box grinding precedent in CS-5 supports the idea that intimate mixing of CsF and a fluorinating reagent is practical, but sealed milling/annealing with fluorinating agents increases hazard [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient mechanochemical route evidence and are neutral, 50. The route is penalized for multi-step processing and reactive/volatile fluorinating agents.

#### F3 — Route 3

Mechanochemical activation should shorten diffusion lengths and improve conversion of CsF to an F-rich phase [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 supports intimate dry-box grinding before low-temperature reaction as a successful tactic for Cs–fluoride synthesis. The limiting kinetic issue is not cation diffusion but controlled fluorine insertion/order, because the target is a distinct tetragonal CsF₂ phase rather than a simple mixture of binary fluorides [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS are insufficient for this chemistry and are neutral, 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation species, Cs, so the cation-radius-difference amorphization check is not triggered (target data). The reported structure is simple tetragonal I4/mmm with one Cs site and one F site, and no mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network is described (target data). Chemically similar Cs fluorides are crystalline under solid-state routes (CS-3/CS-4/CS-5), indirectly supporting low amorphization risk. SS and TS are chemically unrelated for this factor and are neutral, 50.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsF₂ is predicted to be strongly thermodynamically stable by the supplied hull value, so it is best treated as a likely ground-state or very strongly stabilized phase within the computational reference set (target data). Chemically similar evidence supports Cs–fluoride synthesis from CsF under dry, inert, sealed, low-to-moderate temperature conditions (CS-2/CS-3/CS-4/CS-5). The strongest enabling factor is the excellent thermodynamic score and the structural plausibility of the target Cs–F distances relative to Cs–Ag–F analogues (target data; CS-3/CS-5). The strongest limiting factor is the need to control fluorine chemical potential without decomposing, under-fluorinating to CsF, or forming other F-rich/polyfluoride-like species [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues do not materially support the specific Cs–F chemistry and should be treated as neutral evidence, 50.
