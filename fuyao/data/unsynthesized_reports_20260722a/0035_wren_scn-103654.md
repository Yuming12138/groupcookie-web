### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.008676837 eV/atom, which is effectively a computed ground-state value and falls in the best scoring bin. CS/SS/TS analogues such as RbRuF6, RbCrF6, Ge(BrF5)2, Ag2F5, and Au fluoride salts are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing that the reference set is dominated by thermodynamically stable fluorides (CS-1, CS-2, SS-1, SS-3, TS-1, TS-3). The remaining uncertainty is chemical rather than thermodynamic: the calculation predicts stability, but the CS/SS/TS evidence does not directly validate an NF6-centered rubidium fluoride.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 benefits from moderate temperature because RbRuF6 forms at 350 °C for 2 h in F2 (CS-1). However, elemental F2 is a reactive/toxic atmosphere, and use of volatile nitrogen fluorides or staged fluorination is an extrapolated complication [Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS for this exact RbNF6-type direct fluorination route, so SS/TS are neutral for Route 1 severity.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The close structural analogue RbRuF6 forms rapidly by direct fluorination, suggesting favorable kinetics for building the RbF12/MF6 framework under F2 (CS-1). RbCrF6 has the same R-3 family and similar RbF12/CrF6 network, supporting structural reconstruction plausibility (CS-2). The major uncertainty is whether nitrogen can occupy the octahedral M site without decomposing to volatile nitrogen fluorides or nitrogen gas [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 is low-temperature because ACrF6 analogues form at ambient temperature under UV irradiation in anhydrous HF/F2 (CS-2, CS-3). The severity remains high because anhydrous HF, F2 pressure, FEP/PTFE apparatus, and UV photochemistry are required (CS-2, CS-3). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this exact photochemical RbNF6 route, so their severity contribution is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Photochemical HF/F2 synthesis is kinetically favorable for ACrF6 because RbCrF6/KCrF6 form at ambient temperature over 10–20 d (CS-2, CS-3). The solvent-mediated route should reduce bulk diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference] and is the best match for a potentially fragile nitrogen fluoride. The uncertainty is again central-site chemistry: CS supports Cr(V)-fluoride formation, not N(V)-fluoride octahedra.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 uses well-precedented high-valent fluoride conditions but is operationally severe: Monel autoclaves, anhydrous HF, high-pressure F2/Ar, and glovebox handling are used in SrCrF6, Ag2F5, and Au fluoride analogues (CS-4, SS-3, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). Temperatures of 300–450 °C are not extreme by solid-state standards, but the reactive gas/solvent pressure dominates difficulty (CS-4, SS-3, TS-3). The route is a fallback rather than first choice because harsh conditions may decompose nitrogen fluoride species [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solvothermal HF/F2 conditions provide strong transport and crystallization pathways, as shown by single-crystal growth or recrystallization of Ag2F5 and Au fluoride analogues (SS-3, SS-4, TS-3, TS-4). However, several SS/TS analogues report byproduct formation, accidental crystallization, or difficult phase control, which lowers the kinetics/phase-purity score (SS-3, SS-5, TS-3, TS-5). Insufficient evidence from TS for direct RbNF6-type structural reconstruction, so TS mainly informs high-fluoride process feasibility rather than exact phase formation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cation sizes, Rb in 12-coordinate cuboctahedra and N in a very small octahedral NF6 site, triggering the ionic-radius mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target also contains mixed connectivity—corner-, edge-, and face-sharing polyhedra—which is explicitly described for the target and mirrored in RbRuF6/RbCrF6 analogues (CS-1, CS-2). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS to directly determine the target unit-cell atom count, so C2 is treated as non-triggering/neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

RbNF6 is predicted to be a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is −0.008676837 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from chemically similar RbRuF6 and RbCrF6, which have nearly identical RbF12/MF6 framework descriptions and zero hull energies (CS-1, CS-2). The most critical enabling factor is access to highly oxidizing fluorination chemistry, especially F2/HF photochemistry, which forms ACrF6 phases at ambient temperature (CS-2, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is the absence of direct evidence for a stable octahedral NF6⁻ solid; replacing Cr/Ru/Si-type centers with N is a major chemical extrapolation [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible enough to screen experimentally, but it should be treated as high-risk fluorine chemistry rather than a routine solid-state target.
