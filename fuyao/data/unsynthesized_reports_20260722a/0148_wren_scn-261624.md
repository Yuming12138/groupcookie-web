### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.0078 eV/atom, which is effectively a ground-state value and places it in the highest stability bin. The thermodynamic analogues in TS1–TS5 are all reported at E_hull = 0, and the chemically/structurally similar analogues are also mostly hull-stable (CS1–CS5, SS1–SS5). Remaining uncertainty is computational: a slightly negative hull is numerical rather than physically negative, and finite-temperature Li/P chemical potentials may shift phase competition [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1

Route 1 has moderate condition severity because CS1 and CS5 show Li-containing intermetallics can be made at 800–897 °C in welded Mo or sealed Ta ampoules under Ar, avoiding high pressure. The major deductions are sealed volatile components, multistep handling, and Li/P sensitivity [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics are less favorable because elemental Pt is refractory and the target requires ternary ordering, although CS1/CS5 demonstrate noble-metal lithiation/alloying at practical temperatures.

#### F2/F3 — Route 2

Route 2 is conditionally more severe because CS2/SS2/TS2 use 1100 °C chloride flux growth, triggering the >1000 °C penalty. However, the flux route improves F3 because molten NaCl/KCl enabled growth of DyPt₈P₂ from Dy/Pt/P, directly supporting Pt–P formation under transport-enhanced conditions (CS2, SS2, TS2). The key uncertainty is Li compatibility with the chloride flux and whether Li activity can be controlled without forming LiCl-rich side products [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3

Route 3 has extra processing complexity because it intentionally separates Pt–P prealloying from Li incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. The approach is supported indirectly by Pt-rich boride/phosphide/pnictide syntheses from elements (CS2–CS4, SS1/SS4, TS1/TS3) and Li intermetallic sealed-tube reactions (CS1, CS5). Kinetically, preforming Pt–P bonds should help, but stable Pt–P binaries could compete with LiPt₅P [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4

Route 4 is structurally well motivated because Pt₅InAs and Pt₈In₂As are P4/mmm Pt-rich pnictides closely related to the target topology (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). Its F3 is low because those analogues required very long 90–120 day anneals and still showed impurity or homologue coexistence for Pt-containing samples (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). For LiPt₅P, Li volatility and reactivity add further uncertainty beyond the In/As systems [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target combines a small, reactive Li sublattice with a dense Pt-rich framework and P-centered Pt coordination, suggesting notable local size/coordination mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure explicitly contains mixed face/corner-sharing cuboctahedra and edge/corner-sharing tetrahedra, which raises disorder or amorphization risk; similar Pt-rich structural analogues also show mixed cuboctahedral connectivity (SS1, SS3, SS4). The risk is not high because the cell appears simple and P4/mmm Pt-rich analogues are crystalline (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiPt₅P is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0078 eV/atom and thermodynamically similar Pt-rich compounds are hull-stable (TS1–TS5). The synthesis evidence is fairly strong: Li–noble-metal intermetallics are accessible in sealed refractory ampoules (CS1, CS5), Pt-rich phosphides are accessible by chloride flux (CS2, SS2, TS2), and P4/mmm Pt-rich pnictides closely resemble the target structure (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The most important enabling factor is the existence of multiple experimentally synthesized Pt-rich analogues with similar cuboctahedral Pt frameworks and pnictogen-centered coordination (SS1, SS4, TS1, TS3). The most important limiting factor is simultaneous control of Li and P volatility/reactivity during formation of a dense Pt-rich ordered phase [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is moderately promising, with flux-assisted synthesis judged the best first attempt.
