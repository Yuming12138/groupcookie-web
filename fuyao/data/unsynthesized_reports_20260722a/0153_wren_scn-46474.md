### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is −0.03142 eV/atom, which falls in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin and is treated as essentially ground-state within computational uncertainty. Chemically similar Ca/Sr/Eu–Ir–P and Sr/Eu–Os–P phosphides are reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting stable alkaline-earth/late-5d phosphide chemistry (CS). SS and TS examples are either chemically unrelated amides, oxides, halides, nitrides, or borides, so they provide insufficient specific thermodynamic signal for Ca–Pt–P and are neutral for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State

The direct route inherits high-temperature, long-duration, sealed-ampoule conditions from Sr/Eu(IrP)₂ syntheses at 900–1100 °C for up to weeks under Ar (CS). The main penalties are T >1000 °C, volatile P in a sealed system, multi-step grinding/annealing, and air-sensitive/reactive precursors [Reasonable Inference; CS]. No high-pressure apparatus is indicated by the chemically similar references (CS). Remaining uncertainty is whether Ca₂Pt₄P₃ forms cleanly or instead decomposes into Pt–P and Ca–P binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct Sealed-Ampoule Solid-State

Solid-state kinetics are expected to be slow because Pt powder is refractory and diffusion-limited [Reasonable Inference]. The CS direct reactions require weeks and repeated grinding, which supports a relatively low kinetics score (CS). The structure differs from the closest trigonal Ir analogues, so nucleation of the target tetragonal phase may require substantial reconstruction (CS; [Reasonable Inference]).

#### F2 — Route 2: Pb-Flux Growth

Pb flux at 1050 °C under Ar is directly supported for the closest Ca–Ir–P analogue (CS). The condition severity remains moderate because Pb is toxic, P is volatile, sealed ampoules are needed, and flux removal adds processing steps (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). The route avoids high pressure and avoids H₂/NH₃-type reactive gases, which improves F2 relative to more aggressive synthesis methods (CS). Uncertainty lies in Pt solubility and whether Pb stabilizes unwanted phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2: Pb-Flux Growth

Flux growth improves reaction kinetics by providing liquid-phase transport and lowering the need for bulk diffusion through Pt-rich solids [Reasonable Inference]. The direct success of Ca(IrP)₂ single crystals from Pb flux is strong kinetic and chemical evidence (CS). The target structure is not identical to Ca(IrP)₂, so the structural reconstruction score is not maximal (CS). Phase competition is possible, but flux growth offers a practical way to search composition/temperature space [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Sn-Flux Growth

Sn-flux phosphide synthesis is reported at ~880 °C for about two weeks in evacuated sealed silica tubes (CS), so it avoids the >1000 °C penalty. However, the use of evacuated sealed tubes, volatile P, multi-step flux processing, and air-sensitive Ca still impose moderate severity (CS; [Reasonable Inference]). This route has weaker chemical specificity because the CS Sn-flux examples are Ru/Os phosphides rather than Pt phosphides (CS).

#### F3 — Route 3: Sn-Flux Growth

Sn flux should improve mass transport and lower the effective reaction temperature compared with direct solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. CS shows that Sn flux can form alkaline-earth/late-transition-metal phosphides at 880 °C (CS). The limitation is that Pt may form Pt–Sn binaries and the target structure is not the same as the reported ThCr₂Si₂-type analogues [Reasonable Inference; CS]. Therefore, kinetics are favorable but phase selectivity is uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target combines a large electropositive Ca site with smaller, covalent/metallic Pt–P framework sites, triggering the radius/coordination contrast check [Reasonable Inference]. The target formula is small and the supplied structure description does not prove a >40-atom unit cell, so C2 is not triggered. Mixed polyhedral connectivity is explicitly reported for the Ca–Ir–P analogue but not for the target, so C3 is treated as insufficient evidence rather than triggered (CS). The successful crystallization of related Ca/Sr/Eu–Ir–P phosphides reduces the amorphization concern (CS).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Ca₂Pt₄P₃ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its supplied E_hull is −0.03142 eV/atom, and multiple chemically similar alkaline-earth/late-5d phosphides sit on the hull (CS). The strongest synthesis support comes from chemically similar Ca(IrP)₂ grown from elemental Ca/Ir/P in Pb flux and from Sr/Eu(IrP)₂ made by long direct reactions in sealed ampoules (CS). The most critical enabling factor is flux-assisted transport, especially Pb flux, because it addresses sluggish Pt diffusion and promotes crystalline product formation [Reasonable Inference; CS]. The main limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible Pt–P, Ca–P, Ca–Pt, and Ca–Pt–P phases, particularly because the target structure is not identical to the reported Ir/Os analogues [Reasonable Inference; CS]. SS and TS provide limited useful chemical guidance because their nearest examples are chemically unrelated despite some structural or thermodynamic similarity; they are treated as neutral evidence for synthesis transferability.
