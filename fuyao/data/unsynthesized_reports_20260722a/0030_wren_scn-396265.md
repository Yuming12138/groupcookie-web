### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The supplied target E_hull is −0.05139 eV/atom, placing SrAg₃O₄ in the highest stability bin. This strongly supports synthesizability as a predicted ground-state or near-ground-state oxide [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues include NaAg₃O₂ at E_hull = 0.0 (CS1), but SS/TS lists are chemically diverse and give insufficient direct Sr–Ag–O thermodynamic evidence; for this factor, SS and TS are treated as neutral signal sources with score 50.

**F2/F3 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 is strongly supported by CS1 because NaAg₃O₂ was synthesized from binary oxides under 350 atm O₂ at 400 °C for 3 d, closely matching the needed Ag–O chemical environment. The main severity penalty is high-pressure oxygen rather than temperature, because CS1 demonstrates only moderate thermal conditions. The kinetic score is favorable because the closest analogue forms at low temperature, but uncertainty remains because SrAg₃O₄ likely requires higher Ag oxidation than NaAg₃O₂ [Reasonable Inference].

**F2/F3 — Route 2.**  
Route 2 is supported by CS2 and CS3 for high-pressure oxide synthesis, with CS2 using 6 GPa and CS3 using 100–150 kbar plus KClO₃ oxygen control. The high-pressure requirement makes F2 low, but kinetics may be helped by pressure-assisted densification and short diffusion lengths [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is chemical transferability, because CS2/CS3 are Cu/rare-earth oxides rather than Ag–Sr oxides.

**F2/F3 — Route 3.**  
Route 3 has favorable precursor activity because SS3/TS3 use AgNO₃ in precipitation and CS5 uses hydrated nitrates plus H₂O₂ in mild oxide synthesis. Its F2 is better than Route 1 because it can start from soluble salts and moderate calcination temperatures, but high oxygen activity is still likely required by comparison to CS1 [Reasonable Inference]. The weak point is structural reconstruction: no supplied source directly shows precipitation of Sr–Ag–O into this cubic phase, so SS/TS evidence is indirect.

**F2/F3 — Route 4.**  
Route 4 is mostly extrapolative because no CS/SS/TS entry reports vapor deposition of a related Sr–Ag–O phase. F2 is moderate because deposition temperatures can be low, but ozone/plasma oxygen and target fabrication add hazards [Reasonable Inference]. F3 is limited by likely Ag volatility and narrow Sr:Ag composition control [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has a large cation size/coordination mismatch between eight-coordinate Sr and square-planar Ag, triggering C1 [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. The target description gives edge and corner sharing but not face sharing, so C3 is not triggered. The small formula unit and high-symmetry cubic Pm-3n structure suggest low unit-cell complexity [Reasonable Inference], but the exact conventional-cell atom count is not supplied by CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrAg₃O₄ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because the supplied E_hull is −0.05139 eV/atom. The best analogue support is chemical rather than structural: NaAg₃O₂ shares Ag–O chemistry and related O–A₂Ag₃ trigonal-bipyramidal coordination, and it was made directly under high O₂ pressure (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential, because the Sr²⁺ stoichiometry implies a more oxidized Ag–O framework than NaAg₃O₂ [Reasonable Inference]. The most critical limiting factor is control of Ag oxidation and oxygen content without decomposing Ag oxides or forming competing SrO/SrO₂/Ag₂O phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS analogues provide useful general route ideas, especially precipitation and high-pressure methods, but they are chemically less specific than CS1.
