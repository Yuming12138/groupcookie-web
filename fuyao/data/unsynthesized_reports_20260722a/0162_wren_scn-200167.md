### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.056863966 eV/atom, which places it in the most favorable stability bin and suggests a computed ground-state or near-ground-state phase (target data). Chemically similar P-6m2 Ho/Gd(Rh3Ge2)2 and HoRh3Si2 analogues have E_hull = 0.0, supporting the stability of related rare-earth/Rh/main-group intermetallics (CS-1, CS-4, CS-5). SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant stability evidence because their examples are not Sc–Rh–Si-like; they are treated as neutral 50 signals for this factor (SS, TS). The main uncertainty is that a negative computed hull can reflect database/reference limitations rather than experimentally verified stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Elemental solid-state melting + vacuum anneal

The severity score is reduced because CS-2/CS-4 require melting under purified Ar followed by vacuum annealing at 950 °C for four days, giving controlled atmosphere and multistep penalties (CS-2, CS-4). The kinetics score is modest because dense elemental Sc/Rh/Si precursors require interdiffusion and the closest silicide analogues have a different P6/mmm structure rather than the target P-6m2 ordering (CS-2, CS-4; target structure). This route is experimentally standard but may produce competing LaRh3Si2-type or binary silicide-like phases rather than ScRh6Si4 [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not improve confidence for this route because their listed materials are chemically unrelated, so their signal is neutral 50 (SS, TS).

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: Bi-flux growth

The condition severity is moderate because CS-1/CS-5 use 1320 K, evacuated silica ampoules, slow cooling, and chemical flux removal, but no high pressure or ultrahigh temperature (CS-1, CS-5). The kinetics score is the best among the routes because liquid Bi flux can enhance transport and because the P-6m2 RERh6Ge4 analogues have the same structural motif as the target ScRh6Si4 (CS-1, CS-5; target structure; [Reasonable Inference]). The main uncertainty is chemical: Ge analogues are directly proven, while the Si analogue with smaller Sc is inferred rather than reported (CS-1, CS-5; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS remain neutral because they do not provide chemically relevant flux-growth evidence for Sc–Rh–Si (SS, TS).

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: Arc/induction melting + extended anneal

The severity score is lowered because CS-3 uses high-frequency induction melting, protective Ar, evacuated quartz tubes, and a one-week anneal at 1050 °C, implying multistep high-temperature processing (CS-3). The kinetics score is modest because CS-3 demonstrates rare-earth transition-metal silicide formation but with Co and a tetragonal GdCo9Si4 structure rather than Rh and the target P-6m2 structure (CS-3; target structure). This method could yield homogeneous buttons quickly, but ordering into the desired ScRh6Si4 structure may require long annealing or may be bypassed by competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide only generic intermetallic high-temperature precedent and insufficient direct Sc–Rh–Si evidence, so they are neutral 50 (SS, TS).

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a compact intermetallic with a small formula count, ScRh6Si4, and the structure description does not indicate a large, complex >40 atom cell (target structure). The target description highlights face-sharing ScSi6Rh6 cuboctahedra rather than a mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing network, so only one amorphization trigger is assigned (target structure). A radius-mismatch trigger is assigned because Sc differs substantially in metallic size from Rh/Si, which can broaden phase fields or favor disorder [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is that amorphization criteria are more naturally suited to ionic/oxide networks than dense intermetallics [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state intermetallic because its reported E_hull is −0.056863966 eV/atom (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from CS-1 and CS-5, which are exact P-6m2 RE(Rh3Ge2)2 structure-type analogues grown by Bi flux, while CS-2 and CS-4 show that related Rh–Si rare-earth intermetallics can be made by elemental melting and annealing. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of stable P-6m2 Rh–main-group frameworks with RE-centered cuboctahedral coordination in the chemically similar Ge system (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is whether Sc and Si stabilize the same P-6m2 ordered ScRh6Si4 phase rather than the simpler P6/mmm RERh3Si2-type silicide or binary Sc–Si/Rh–Si phases (CS-2, CS-4; [Reasonable Inference]). SS and TS analogues are not chemically representative of the target and therefore do not materially increase confidence beyond a neutral signal (SS, TS).
