### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target is essentially on the hull, with E_hull = −0.002814721 eV/atom, so the thermodynamic score is very high by the required rubric (target data). Thermodynamic analogues include NaHS and KHS hydrogensulfides with E_hull = 0 eV/atom, which strongly supports stability of S–H-containing ionic solids near this chemistry (TS1, TS3). CS analogues are mostly amides rather than sulfides, so CS provides general evidence that H-rich ionic solids can be stable, but insufficient direct sulfur-specific signal from CS; CS is therefore not weighted as strongly as SS/TS.

#### Route 1 F2/F3 — Direct Ba + H₂S autoclave

Condition severity is moderate because hydrogensulfide analogues are synthesized at only 50–150 °C for 1–3 d, avoiding high-temperature solid-state processing (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The main penalties are sealed volatile H₂S, multiple glovebox/autoclave/gas-transfer operations, and toxic/air-sensitive reagent handling (SS1/SS3/TS1/TS3; [Reasonable Inference]). Kinetics are favorable because metal + H₂S is a highly reactive gas–solid pathway, and the target’s S–H distances match NaHS/KHS closely, but BaH₄S₃ requires divalent Ba stoichiometry and H₂S incorporation beyond simple MHS [Reasonable Inference].

#### Route 2 F2/F3 — BaS + H₂S protonation

Condition severity is slightly better than Route 1 because BaS avoids the most reactive Ba-metal step if commercial dry BaS is used [Reasonable Inference]. However, H₂S remains toxic/volatile and requires a sealed system, as in the hydrogensulfide analogues (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Kinetics are less certain than Route 1 because BaS must be protonated and reorganized into an H-rich layered BaH₄S₃ phase, which is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference; insufficient direct evidence from CS].

#### Route 3 F2/F3 — High-pressure H₂S

High-pressure fluid synthesis is supported by NH₃ analogues at 2000–5000 bar and 130–300 °C, including Sr amide and RbCa(NH₂)₃-type materials (CS2, CS5, SS4, TS4). Translating that concept to H₂S is plausible for retaining H-rich sulfide stoichiometry, but it is not directly demonstrated in the provided data [Reasonable Inference]. Kinetics should benefit from fluid-mediated transport and high H₂S chemical potential, but equipment severity, corrosion, and safety make this route more challenging [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Ba, so cation-size disorder is not expected to drive amorphization [Reasonable Inference from target formula]. The reported structure is ordered monoclinic P2₁/m with well-defined Ba–S and S–H distances, and similar NaHS/KHS P2₁/m hydrogensulfides are crystalline (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). Exact unit-cell atom count and polyhedral sharing topology are not fully provided, so C2/C3 have insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS; no trigger is assigned.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

BaH₄S₃ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its calculated E_hull is essentially zero, −0.0028 eV/atom (target data). The strongest synthesis support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar NaHS/KHS hydrogensulfides, which share monoclinic P2₁/m symmetry and nearly identical S–H bond lengths and are made directly from metals + H₂S/D₂S in autoclaves (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-temperature H₂S autoclave chemistry that can form S–H-containing crystalline salts without requiring refractory diffusion (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3). The most critical limiting factor is stoichiometry control for a divalent Ba compound that appears more H₂S-rich than simple alkali hydrogensulfides [Reasonable Inference]. A secondary limitation is the toxicity, volatility, and corrosion risk of H₂S, which necessitates specialized sealed equipment and inert handling (SS1, SS3, TS1, TS3).
