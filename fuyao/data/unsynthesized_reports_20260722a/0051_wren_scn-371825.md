### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.0755 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable thermodynamic category. Chemically related fluoride analogues SrNiF₆, CaPdF₆, and BaNiF₆ are reported at E_hull = 0.0, indicating that dense high-fluoride frameworks can be stable phases (CS-2, CS-3, CS-4). TS analogues are near-hull phases, but chemically unrelated, so there is insufficient evidence from TS for SrF₇-specific thermodynamics beyond generic stability.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 is penalized because the closest Sr fluoride analogue requires F₂/N₂ pressure fluorination at pF ≈ 140 atm and 280 °C in a Monell autoclave (CS-2). The more extreme Ba/Ni fluoride case requires pF₂ > 3500 atm, 450–600 °C, and 10 d in a Monel autoclave (CS-4). The temperature is not extreme by the rubric, but F₂ pressure, toxic gas handling, and sealed autoclave operation make the route challenging.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The structural match to SrNiF₆ is favorable because both have R-3m-type SrF₁₂ cuboctahedral motifs with similar Sr–F distances (CS-2; target structure). However, SrF₂ is a stable fluoride and likely kinetically resistant to further fluorine incorporation [Reasonable Inference]. The need for pressure fluorination and reports of difficult phase purity in SrNiF₆ lower the kinetic score (CS-2).

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 avoids high temperature and high pressure because CS-1 demonstrates room-temperature solvolysis/fluorination in anhydrous HF/F₂. It is still heavily penalized for anhydrous HF, F₂ gas, sealed fluoropolymer equipment, and multi-step workup (CS-1). The low thermal severity is offset by substantial chemical hazard.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Anhydrous HF should improve fluoride transport and nucleation relative to solid SrF₂ powder [Reasonable Inference], and CS-1 shows a chemically similar F₂-containing fluoride framework can form at room temperature over 72 h. The target’s F₂ clusters make this route especially plausible because CS-1 also contains F₂-like clusters in a fluoride framework. The main uncertainty is whether Sr²⁺ forms the desired R-3m SrF₁₂ framework during HF removal rather than crystallizing SrF₂ or solvated fluoride salts [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 uses mild temperatures comparable to direct fluorination of CaPdF₆ at 100 °C and pressure fluorination of SrNiF₆ at 280 °C (CS-3, CS-2). It remains hazardous because flowing F₂ is reactive and toxic, and the staged fluorination/tempering/cooling sequence is multi-step (CS-3). It is operationally less severe than high-pressure autoclave fluorination but likely less chemically powerful.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Direct low-temperature fluorination benefits from reactive F₂ gas, as shown by CaPdF₆ synthesis from intimate mixtures (CS-3). However, SrF₂ is already a stable binary fluoride, so incorporation of additional fluorine or F₂ clusters at ambient pressure is likely surface-limited [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of a directly matching ambient-pressure SrF₇ analogue makes the kinetic score modest.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target contains only one cation, Sr, so cation-radius mismatch does not apply. The target description reports edge-sharing SrF₁₂ cuboctahedra and F–F units, not mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral coexistence, so the structural frustration trigger is not activated. Exact unit-cell atom count is not given, and SS/TS do not provide useful SrF₇ cell-size evidence; therefore uncertainty remains but the amorphization risk is scored low.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The target is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or at least effectively ground-state-like because its reported E_hull is negative. The strongest support comes from chemically similar Sr/Ba/Ni high-fluoride phases made under F₂ pressure and from the aHF/F₂ route that produced an F₂-cluster fluoride framework (CS-1, CS-2, CS-4). The most important enabling factor is high fluorine chemical potential in a rigorously anhydrous medium, especially anhydrous HF/F₂, because the target contains F₂ clusters rather than only simple F⁻ ions (CS-1; target structure). The most important limiting factor is phase control: related SrNiF₆ preparation is reported to have extremely difficult Guinier-pure phase formation and moisture sensitivity (CS-2). SS and TS analogues are not chemically close, so they provide insufficient direct synthesis guidance for SrF₇.
