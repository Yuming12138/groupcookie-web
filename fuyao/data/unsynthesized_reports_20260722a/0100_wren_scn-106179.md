### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s E_hull is reported as −0.106 eV/atom, so by the required rubric it receives a high thermodynamic score. TS analogues are mostly on-hull or near-hull synthesized materials, including TS-1 and TS-4 at E_hull = 0.0 and TS-3/TS-5 near zero, which supports thermodynamic accessibility of similar-hull phases. CS fluoride analogues include both on-hull compounds and a synthesized F-rich compound at ~0.102 eV/atom, showing that fluoride phases do not need to be exactly on the hull to be experimentally accessible (CS-1 to CS-5). Remaining uncertainty is that the negative hull may reflect calculation/reference inconsistency, and thermodynamic stability does not resolve whether the proposed CsF₇ bonding is chemically realistic [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 avoids high temperature and high pressure because CS-1 used room-temperature aHF/F₂ chemistry. The severity comes from anhydrous HF, F₂, fluoropolymer reactor requirements, and vacuum-line handling, all explicitly present in CS-1. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for the chemical severity of Cs–F fluorination and are neutral. The route is therefore operationally challenging despite mild temperature.

#### F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics

CS-1 supports rapid chemical access through a liquid aHF solvolysis/fluorination medium rather than slow ceramic diffusion. This justifies high precursor activity and high diffusion scores. The main kinetic uncertainty is structural reconstruction: no CS/SS/TS analogue verifies formation of isolated CsF₇ clusters, and producing such a local environment from CsF requires major rearrangement [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are favorable for fluorination but uncertain for crystallizing the exact target phase.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

CS-2 and CS-4 show that highly fluorinated compounds may require pressurized F₂ in Monel autoclaves, ranging from 130–140 atm to >3500 atm and 280–600 °C. These conditions are below the rubric’s >5 GPa threshold but still severe due to reactive F₂ gas and specialized containment. The route receives deductions for reactive atmosphere, sealed pressurized gas handling, and toxic precursors. SS/TS do not provide chemically relevant F₂-pressure evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics

High F₂ pressure increases fluorine chemical potential and can drive formation of fluorine-rich solids, as shown in CS-2 and CS-4. However, a solid CsF precursor is less reactive than a solvated HF medium, and the transformation to a discrete CsF₇ cluster is not supported by those transition-metal fluoride structures. CS-2 also notes phase instability/decomposition and an unexpected synthesis window, while CS-4 reports difficulty obtaining Guinier-pure samples, indicating kinetic and phase-control risks. Therefore, Route 2 has moderate kinetics.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

CS-3 and CS-5 use 3 GPa and 573 K in HF, which is specialized but below the rubric’s >5 GPa penalty. The main deductions are sealed volatile HF, toxic/corrosive medium, and multi-step high-pressure loading/recovery. CS-3/CS-5 explicitly require modified belt/high-pressure cell apparatus, supporting the “challenging” classification. SS/TS provide insufficient chemical evidence for this HF route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics

CS-3 and CS-5 show fluoride/HF systems crystallize in only 30 min at 573 K under 3 GPa, which supports high mobility and favorable crystallization kinetics. High-pressure HF should reduce diffusion barriers relative to solid-state synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is chemical: without F₂, the medium may stabilize CsHF₂ or CsF·HF phases rather than a binary CsF₇ phase [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, kinetics are good for forming HF-rich fluoride crystals but only moderately convincing for exact CsF₇.

#### F2 — Route 4 condition severity

SS-4/TS-4 show low-temperature decomposition can occur at 40–55 °C under dry N₂ or vacuum, so thermal severity is low. The deductions arise from the need for controlled dry atmosphere/vacuum, a potentially volatile HF/polyfluoride precursor, and multiple operations such as precursor synthesis, long decomposition, and monitoring/powdering [Reasonable Inference]. CS provides insufficient direct support for a Cs polyfluoride decomposition route and is neutral. Overall severity is moderate-to-high because the chemistry is hazardous even at low temperature.

#### F3 — Route 4 reaction kinetics

SS-4/TS-4 support the concept of low-temperature halide decomposition but also report sluggish, nonreproducible phase transitions and difficulty obtaining single crystals. If a preorganized Cs polyfluoride precursor exists, local rearrangement could be kinetically accessible [Reasonable Inference]. However, no CS/SS/TS source identifies such a CsF₇ precursor, and volatile HF/F loss may outcompete crystallization [Reasonable Inference]. The kinetic score is therefore moderate.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has only one cation, so the cation-size-mismatch amorphization check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The described unit cell contains four CsF₇ clusters, or 32 atoms, below the >40 atom threshold [Reasonable Inference]. The target is zero-dimensional and lacks mixed vertex/edge/face polyhedral connectivity, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered [Reasonable Inference]. CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct amorphization evidence for CsF₇ and are neutral for F4.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

The reported negative hull suggests CsF₇ is predicted to be thermodynamically stable or at least not strongly metastable, but this should be treated cautiously because the local bonding description is chemically unusual for a Cs–F binary phase [Reasonable Inference]. The CS evidence is the most relevant: CS-1 supports anhydrous HF/F₂ alkali fluoride chemistry, while CS-2/CS-4 support high-fluorine-potential pressure routes and CS-3/CS-5 support HF-rich high-pressure fluoride crystallization. SS and TS mainly support generic feasibility of near-hull materials and some low-temperature decomposition concepts, but they are chemically weak for CsF₇ and are therefore mostly neutral. The most critical enabling factor is access to very high fluoride/HF or fluorine chemical potential under water-free conditions, supported by CS-1 through CS-5. The most critical limiting factor is whether the proposed CsF₇ cluster is a physically meaningful phase rather than a misassigned polyfluoride/HF-containing structure [Reasonable Inference].

|:--:|:--|:--|:--:|:--|
| 1 | All | Chemical plausibility of CsF₇ stoichiometry and isolated CsF₇ clusters is uncertain because terminal F atoms around Cs without F–F bonding are unusual [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Use Raman spectroscopy to identify F–F/polyfluoride or HF₂ units, because CS-1 relied on spectroscopic confirmation of fluorination products; verify structure by low-temperature single-crystal or neutron/XRD refinement [Reasonable Inference]. |
| 2 | Routes 1–2 | F₂ handling and oxidation hazard. | High | Use FEP/Kel-F/vacuum-line methods as in CS-1 or passivated Monel autoclave methods as in CS-2/CS-4; dilute F₂ with N₂ as in CS-4. |
| 3 | Routes 1, 3, 4 | HF volatility, corrosion, and toxicity. | High | Use sealed HF-compatible equipment; CS-1 used FEP reactor/Kel-F valve/vacuum line, while CS-3/CS-5 used high-pressure cells for HF media. |
| 4 | Routes 2–3 | Specialized pressure equipment requirement. | High | For gas fluorination, use Monel autoclave precedent from CS-2/CS-4; for HF hydrothermal synthesis, use modified belt/high-pressure cell conditions from CS-3/CS-5. |
| 5 | All | Competing phases such as CsF, CsHF₂, CsF·HF, or other polyfluorides may form [Reasonable Inference]. | High | Map CsF:HF:F₂ ratio and temperature systematically; CS-3/CS-5 show pressure can suppress normal-pressure secondary phases and improve crystal quality. |
| 6 | Route 2 | Phase-pure samples may be difficult under high-pressure F₂. | Medium–High | CS-4 reports Guinier-pure samples are extremely difficult for related high-fluorine phases; mitigate by using diluted F₂/N₂, lower temperature, and short holds before increasing severity. |
| 7 | Route 1 | Loss of volatile HF/F-rich species during drying may decompose the desired phase [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Remove HF slowly under low temperature and dynamic vacuum, adapting CS-1 vacuum-line handling. |
| 8 | Route 3 | HF route may stabilize HF₂-containing salts rather than binary CsF₇ [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium–High | Combine HF pressure with post-treatment under F₂/aHF if needed; Route 1 conditions from CS-1 provide the F₂ chemical potential absent from CS-3/CS-5. |
| 9 | Route 4 | Low-temperature decomposition can be sluggish and nonreproducible. | Medium | SS-4/TS-4 used long holds, dry N₂/vacuum, and frequent powdering; monitor phase evolution by powder XRD/Raman. |
| 10 | All | Crystal growth difficulty for a volatile/reactive fluoride phase [Reasonable Inference]. | Medium | Favor slow cooling under pressure or slow HF removal; CS-3/CS-5 obtained better crystals under high-pressure HF, while CS-1 used controlled solvolysis/fluorination in aHF. |
