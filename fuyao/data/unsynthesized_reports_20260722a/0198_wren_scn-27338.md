### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.024201355 eV/atom, which is effectively hull-stable within computational uncertainty and scores near the top of the ≤0.05 eV/atom bracket. K₂Au₃ and KAu₂ are also hull-stable K–Au compounds with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, showing the binary K–Au system contains stable intermetallics at nearby compositions (CS-1, CS-3). SS and TS examples are mostly unrelated hydrides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides; they provide insufficient chemistry-specific evidence for K₃Au₂ and are treated as neutral.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: Direct sealed-ampoule solid-state reaction

The direct elemental route is strongly supported by K₂Au₃, which was obtained from K and Au in a sealed Ta ampoule at 1000 °C followed by prolonged slow cooling (CS-1). Its severity score is lowered by the need for Ar/vacuum handling, sealed containment, air-sensitive K, and multi-step ampoule processing (CS-1). Kinetically, this route is limited by dense Au and the need for long-range ordering, as indicated by the slow-cooling schedule used for K₂Au₃ (CS-1). The remaining uncertainty is whether K₃Au₂ is an equilibrium ambient-pressure phase or whether competing K₂Au₃/KAu₂ dominate [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: High-pressure direct reaction/quench

The high-pressure route is based on KAu₂ synthesis from K and Au at 4.0 GPa and 1075 K with quenching (CS-3). By the provided F2 rubric, 4.0 GPa does not trigger the >5 GPa penalty, but expert judgment still views the route as experimentally challenging because CS-3 requires a modified Belt-type apparatus. Kinetically, pressure may improve K retention and contact, and KAu₂ forms in only 30 min at 1075 K (CS-3). The main uncertainty is whether the K-rich K₃Au₂ target is favored under the same pressure regime or whether pressure preferentially stabilizes Au-rich KAu₂ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: KN₃ + Au azide route

The azide route is inferred from Rb₂Au₃ synthesis using RbN₃ and Au powder at 450 °C in evacuated quartz ampoules (CS-4). Its condition severity is moderate because it avoids 1000 °C and GPa pressures, but it requires hazardous azide handling, evacuated ampoules, and inert conditions (CS-4). Kinetics may be better than direct metal diffusion because in situ alkali generation enabled Rb–Au intermetallic formation at only 450 °C (CS-4), but KN₃ use for K₃Au₂ is not directly demonstrated and is therefore [Reasonable Inference]. The largest uncertainty is whether nitrogen evolution, K activity, and target stoichiometry can be controlled without forming K₂Au₃, KAu₂, or elemental Au [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target is a binary intermetallic with simple K/Au composition and no reported mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing polyhedral network in the target description. The formula unit contains only five atoms, and no evidence indicates a large complex cell above 40 atoms [Reasonable Inference]. Similar K–Au compounds are crystalline intermetallics synthesized by solid-state or high-pressure methods rather than amorphous products (CS-1, CS-3). Remaining uncertainty is the exact crystallization domain of the trigonal R-3 K₃Au₂ phase, since no direct experimental synthesis is provided.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₃Au₂ is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is slightly negative, and nearby K–Au phases K₂Au₃ and KAu₂ are hull-stable and experimentally synthesized (target data; CS-1, CS-3). The chemical-similarity evidence is strong because two close K–Au binaries are known from K + Au routes, while SS and TS evidence is largely non-specific and therefore neutral. The most critical enabling factor is sealed or high-pressure containment of reactive K, which both prevents K loss and allows K–Au intermetallic formation (CS-1, CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition among K–Au intermetallics, because K₂Au₃, KAu₂, and Rb₂Au₃ analogues show that small changes in stoichiometry and temperature can select different alkali–Au phases (CS-1, CS-3, CS-4). Overall, the target appears synthesizable but will likely require careful K activity control and either high-pressure quenching or prolonged sealed-ampoule annealing.
