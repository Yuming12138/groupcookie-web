### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.017171 eV/atom, which is within the ground-state stability window and receives a high score. CS-2 strengthens this interpretation because isostructural LiCaBi in Pnma is reported with E_hull = 0.0 and the same Li–tetrahedral/Ca–5-coordinate topology. SS and TS examples are mostly chemically unrelated despite low hull values, so they provide insufficient evidence for LiCaPt-specific thermodynamics and are treated as neutral. The remaining uncertainty is computational: negative hull values generally imply numerical/reference-set uncertainty rather than literal negative metastability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids extreme temperature, high pressure, vacuum, NH3, or H2, consistent with the moderate 510 °C sealed Ar synthesis of LiMgIr in CS-1 and Ar ampoule syntheses in CS-3/4/5. Deductions are mainly for volatile/air-sensitive Li and Ca requiring sealed handling, with the Li/Ca sensitivity inferred from standard reactivity and the sealed Li precedents in CS-1 and CS-3/4/5 [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS give insufficient evidence for direct LiCaPt sealed-tube synthesis, so they are neutral. The uncertainty is whether Pt reacts sufficiently at 500–650 °C without a flux or pre-alloying step.

#### F3 — Route 1

The main kinetic penalty is refractory Pt, which likely limits diffusion in an elemental solid-state reaction [Reasonable Inference]. CS-1 is encouraging because a Li–Mg–Ir phase formed at only 510 °C over 4 days, showing that Li–light metal–5d intermetallics can form at moderate temperature. CS-3/4/5 also show Li ternary intermetallics can require multiday to multiweek anneals and may contain binary impurities, lowering the kinetics score. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this specific kinetic pathway.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe because CS-2’s flux protocol uses 900 °C, evacuated silica/Nb containment, slow cooling, annealing, quenching, and centrifugation. It does not require high external pressure or >1000 °C temperatures, but vacuum/sealed containment, flux handling, toxic Pb, and Li/Ca air sensitivity produce substantial deductions. SS and TS provide insufficient flux-specific LiCaPt evidence and are neutral. The uncertainty is whether Pt dissolves adequately in a Pb/Sn/Bi/Ca/Li flux [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives a higher kinetic score than direct solid state because a liquid medium can bypass slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and CS-2 demonstrates flux growth of structurally related Li/Ca-containing phases. The strongest support is structural: LiCaBi is Pnma with the same local coordination pattern as target LiCaPt (CS-2). However, CS-2 also reports complex processing and phase-control difficulty, which reduces the score. The main uncertainty is flux compatibility with Pt and avoidance of Pt–flux binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Arc/induction melting plus annealing is supported only generically by SS-2/TS-2, which describe arc melting and annealing around 1000 °C in Ar for intermetallic borides. Because Li and Ca are volatile/reactive, the route must be modified to pre-alloy Ca–Pt and then add Li in a sealed anneal [Reasonable Inference]. This adds multistep complexity and sealed volatile-component control. SS/TS are useful only for the melt/anneal concept, while CS-1 and CS-3/4/5 support the Li-containing sealed anneal.

#### F3 — Route 3

Pre-alloying can improve Pt reaction kinetics by avoiding direct reaction of dense Pt powder with Li/Ca at low temperature [Reasonable Inference]. However, structural reconstruction into ordered Pnma LiCaPt after Li addition may be slow, and SS-2/TS-2 note phase-purity and crystal-growth challenges for melt-grown analogues. CS-3/4/5 similarly show that Li ternary intermetallics can require long anneals and suffer from binary impurities. Therefore, kinetics are moderate-to-challenging despite improved Pt activation.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a small expected Pnma cell by analogy to LiCaBi Li4Ca4Bi4, which reduces amorphization risk (CS-2). The target has mixed edge/corner-sharing LiPt4 tetrahedra, but no face-sharing connectivity is reported, so the strict C3 criterion is not fully triggered. A size mismatch among Li, Ca, and Pt is a real disorder/amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for LiCaPt amorphization behavior and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

LiCaPt is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because the reported E_hull is −0.017 eV/atom, and the isostructural LiCaBi analogue is also hull-stable (CS-2). The best support comes from CS: CS-2 gives a very strong structural analogue in Pnma, while CS-1 and CS-3/4/5 demonstrate that Li-containing ternary intermetallics can be synthesized from elements under sealed inert conditions. SS and TS are much less chemically relevant and mainly provide generic evidence that intermetallics may be accessible by melt/anneal routes; otherwise they are neutral. The most critical enabling factor is the strong structural/thermodynamic match to LiCaBi and the favorable target hull stability. The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and compositional control: Pt is refractory, while Li/Ca are reactive and potentially volatile, making sealed stoichiometric reaction control essential.
