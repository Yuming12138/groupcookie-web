### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.107091918 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and suggesting a ground-state or numerically over-stabilized ground-state candidate from the supplied target data. Chemically related fluoride analogues BaCrF5, BaCrF6, CsReF6, CsRuF6, and RbCrF5 are all listed at E_hull = 0.0, supporting stable fluoride chemistry in related compositions (CS1–CS5). SS and TS analogues are stable or near-stable but chemically unrelated, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for fluoride-specific thermodynamics; neutral source score = 50 for each.

#### F2 — Route 1 direct gas fluorination

The severity score is reduced because CS4 and CS2 require elemental fluorine, and CS2 additionally uses pressure up to 300 atm. The route avoids very high temperature and avoids GPa pressure, since CS4 reports 350 °C fluorination and CS2 reports 300 °C fluorination. The main uncertainty is whether Mo forms volatile fluorides more readily than Ru/Cr under these conditions; this volatility concern is [Reasonable Inference] and is consistent with CS2 reporting volatile chromium fluoride losses.

#### F3 — Route 1 direct gas fluorination

Kinetics are favorable because CS4 obtained CsRuF6-type material after only ~2 h at 350 °C. Reactive F2 and chloride precursors should create active fluorinated intermediates [Reasonable Inference]. However, chloride-to-fluoride network conversion requires substantial structural reconstruction, and phase purity may be sensitive to Mo fluoride volatility [Reasonable Inference], lowering the structural-reconstruction component.

#### F2 — Route 2 photochemical HF fluorination

CS5 directly supports an ambient-temperature but chemically severe route using F2, anhydrous HF, FEP vessels, and UV irradiation for 10–20 d. The absence of high temperature and GPa pressure is beneficial, but the hazards of HF/F2, sealed fluoropolymer equipment, and long multistep handling impose large deductions. SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 photochemical HF fluorination

Kinetics are strong because CS5 uses anhydrous HF solvent and photochemical F2 oxidation, avoiding solid-state diffusion. CS5 is highly transferable because it reports ACrF6 for A = Li–Cs, which matches the alkali hexafluorometallate motif of CsMoF6. The remaining uncertainty is the Cr → Mo substitution and stabilization of Mo(V) rather than overoxidized/volatile Mo fluorides [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3 sealed solid-state fluoride synthesis

CS1 shows that sealed noble-metal ampoule sintering can produce phase-pure fluoride powders, but it required 750 °C for 65 h in high vacuum. The proposed target route stays below 1000 °C, so it avoids the high-temperature penalties, but it still requires sealed dry/vacuum handling and air-sensitive high-valent Mo fluoride precursors [Reasonable Inference]. The source signal from SS/TS is insufficient and neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 sealed solid-state fluoride synthesis

The kinetic score is moderate because binary fluorides can preserve local M–F bonding motifs, but cation diffusion and long-range ordering are slower than in HF solution. CS1’s 65 h sintering time indicates nontrivial solid-state kinetics even for chemically similar fluorides. Volatile Mo fluoride formation could help transport but also risks stoichiometry loss [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 4 HF solution crystallization

CS3 gives a close structural precedent, CsReF6 in R-3, obtained by slow evaporation, but the listed synthetic precursor details are not chemically persuasive for CsReF6. A realistic Mo route likely requires anhydrous HF and inert/fluorinating control [Reasonable Inference], which makes the route hazardous despite mild temperature. Therefore, the condition-severity score remains similar to Route 2.

#### F3 — Route 4 HF solution crystallization

Solution crystallization has excellent diffusion kinetics, and CS3 shows that a CsMF6 R-3 phase can crystallize by evaporation. The limitation is chemical: Mo(V) fluoro complexes may disproportionate, hydrolyze, or over-fluorinate unless anhydrous/redox conditions are controlled [Reasonable Inference]. Thus, the route is kinetically attractive but less securely evidenced than Route 2.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation size mismatch between Cs and Mo, so C1 is triggered [Reasonable Inference]. The target description gives a simple corner-sharing octahedral framework and does not report edge/face-sharing coexistence or a >40-atom cell, so C2 and C3 are not triggered by the supplied target data. CS3 and CS4 show crystalline CsMF6 analogues, which supports low-to-moderate amorphization risk; SS/TS are chemically insufficient and neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

CsMoF6 is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because the supplied E_hull is −0.107091918 eV/atom, and several chemically related fluorides in CS are listed on the hull. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS5, which demonstrates photochemical preparation of ACrF6 for A = Li–Cs in anhydrous HF/F2, and from CS4, which demonstrates CsRuF6 formation under direct fluorination. The most critical enabling factor is access to highly reactive fluoride chemistry—F2/HF or F2 gas—which can form MoF6 octahedra without requiring extreme temperature. The most critical limiting factor is control of Mo oxidation state and volatile/moisture-sensitive Mo fluoride species, inferred from high-valent fluoride chemistry and supported indirectly by CS2’s warning about volatile chromium fluoride losses. SS and TS analogues are not chemically close, so they do not materially strengthen the synthetic prediction beyond neutral thermodynamic context.
