### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is −0.0095 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull within computational tolerance, so the thermodynamic score is very high. Thermodynamic analogues Tm₂FeC₄, Dy₂FeC₄, Ho₂FeC₄, and Y₂FeC₄ are all reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (TS-1 to TS-4), supporting a stable rare-earth iron carbide chemical space. The remaining uncertainty is that the target stoichiometry Y₂Fe₂C₃ differs from the strongest analogue Y₂FeC₄, so phase competition may still occur despite favorable hull energy [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 1.**  
Route 1 uses moderate annealing temperature, 900 °C, no high pressure, and standard inert/vacuum tube processing, as directly reported for Y₂FeC₄ and related R₂FeC₄ phases (SS-4, TS-4). Deductions come from evacuated-tube annealing, multi-step pellet/arc/anneal processing, and oxidation-sensitive rare-earth metal handling (SS-4, TS-4, CS-1). The uncertainty is whether Y₂Fe₂C₃ needs a different annealing window than Y₂FeC₄ [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Kinetically, elemental Y/Fe/graphite are not highly reactive precursors, so precursor activity is modest [Reasonable Inference supported by SS-4 use of elements]. However, successful formation of R₂FeC₄ through arc melting plus 900 °C peritectic annealing shows that the Y/R–Fe–C network can equilibrate under similar conditions (SS-4, TS-4). The main kinetic uncertainty is structural: target C2/m Y₂Fe₂C₃ is not the same structure type as Ibam Y₂FeC₄, so ordering may require careful annealing [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
Li-flux synthesis is more severe than Route 1 because CS-2 uses high temperature near 1250 °C, sealed Nb ampoules, slow cooling, and high-vacuum Li removal. Reactive Li and air-sensitive handling add additional severity [Reasonable Inference based on CS-2]. Insufficient evidence from SS and TS supports Li-flux synthesis of structurally/thermodynamically similar Fe-containing carbides, so this route is penalized for uncertainty.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
Flux should improve transport relative to dry solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and Y–Al–C carbides can be crystallized from Li flux (CS-2, CS-4). However, CS-2 reports Y₅Al₃C₄ as a minority phase, indicating phase-selection difficulty. Because no Y–Fe–C flux analogue is provided, structural transferability is uncertain and the kinetic score remains moderate-low.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
Direct arc melting is operationally simpler and is supported for YCoC/Y₂C in Ar (CS-3). It still receives high-temperature deductions because arc melting transiently exceeds typical metal melting temperatures [Reasonable Inference], and Y handling is oxidation-sensitive [Reasonable Inference; CS-1]. It avoids vacuum annealing and multi-step processing, so condition severity is better than Route 1, but this does not imply better phase purity.

**F3 — Route 3.**  
The direct-melt route has good homogenization potential but poor evidence for forming the desired Fe-rich carbide structure directly [Reasonable Inference]. Structurally similar Fe-containing R₂FeC₄ analogues form through annealing/peritectic reaction rather than just as-cast arc products (SS-4, TS-4). Therefore, the direct route is best viewed as a screening step, not the preferred synthesis.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one clear amorphization trigger: large Y–Fe cation size mismatch [Reasonable Inference]. The structure contains edge- and corner-sharing motifs but no reported face-sharing coexistence, so the mixed-connectivity trigger is not fully met (target description; SS-4, TS-4). Unit-cell atom count is not given; insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS and target data for >40 atoms, so it is treated as neutral/not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Y₂Fe₂C₃ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is −0.0095 eV/atom and thermodynamic analogues in the rare-earth–Fe–C space lie on the hull (TS-1 to TS-4). The strongest support comes from structurally and thermodynamically similar R₂FeC₄ compounds, including Y₂FeC₄, synthesized from elemental rare-earth metal, Fe, and graphite by arc melting followed by 900 °C annealing (SS-4, TS-4). The most critical enabling factor is that Y–Fe–C carbide frameworks with FeC₄ units are experimentally accessible under standard arc-melting/annealing conditions (SS-4, TS-4). The most critical limiting factor is phase competition with nearby Y–Fe–C carbides such as Y₂FeC₄ or binary YC₂/Fe-containing products during peritectic equilibration [Reasonable Inference from SS-4/TS-4 peritectic formation]. Overall, synthesis is plausible, with best prospects via arc melting plus careful long annealing.
