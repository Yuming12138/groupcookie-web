### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has an explicitly reported E_hull of −0.010877294 eV/atom, so it is scored as effectively on-hull and thermodynamically favorable within calculation noise (target data). Chemically similar Cs₂PtF₆, Cs₂IrF₆, Cs₃NiF₇, and Cs₂SnF₆ are all at or near the hull, reinforcing that Cs fluoride salts with isolated MF₆ units can be stable (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS examples are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so they provide insufficient evidence for Cs₂TlF₆ stability and are treated as neutral.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 is penalized because Cs₂PtF₆ required diluted F₂/N₂ fluorination at 400 °C for 10 d and sealed-tube heating at 800 °C for 35 d under dry Ar (CS-1). The temperature is below 1000 °C, so no high-temperature penalty is applied, but reactive atmosphere, sealed processing, multi-step operation, and toxic/air-sensitive materials all apply (CS-1; [Reasonable Inference for Tl toxicity]). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for fluoride sealed-tube severity and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

The target and Cs₂PtF₆ share the same cubic Fm-3m polyhedral arrangement, so structural reconstruction should be favorable once the correct Cs₂MF₆ stoichiometry and oxidation state are achieved (CS-1; target data). However, CS-1 required 35 d at 800 °C, implying slow kinetics or a narrow crystallization window. The precursor activity is moderate because binary fluorides are less reactive than solution precursors [Reasonable Inference], although F₂ fluorination improves chemical activity as shown by CS-1.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

Route 2 follows the Cs₂CuF₆ precedent, which used CsF + CuF₃ in a F₂/Ar autoclave at 400 °C for 5 weeks and about 350 bar (CS-4). The pressure is significant but below the scoring threshold of >5 GPa, so no GPa penalty is assigned. Reactive F₂/Ar atmosphere, sealed volatile gas handling, multi-step synthesis, and toxic/air-sensitive materials justify the deductions (CS-4; [Reasonable Inference for Tl compounds]).

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Route 2 has strong kinetic plausibility because Cs₂CuF₆ is structurally identical to the target, with the same Fm-3m CsF₁₂/MF₆ framework (CS-4; target data). Pressurized F₂ should assist oxidation and fluoride transport [Reasonable Inference from CS-4], so the precursor activity and diffusion scores are higher than in ordinary solid-state synthesis. The main kinetic uncertainty is phase purity, because CS-4 explicitly notes difficulty obtaining pure Cs₂CuF₆ samples.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

Route 3 is mild in temperature because Cs₂IrF₆ was produced by reacting Cs₂CO₃ with aqueous H₂[IrF₆] followed by slow evaporation at room temperature (CS-2). It avoids high temperature, GPa pressure, and furnace-based sealed tubes, giving a high F2 score. The deductions arise from likely need to prepare H₂[TlF₆] and from toxic/corrosive Tl/HF chemistry [Reasonable Inference]; CS-2 itself does not provide evidence for Tl-specific hazards.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solution crystallization is kinetically favorable because soluble Cs and hexafluorometallate precursors can mix on the molecular scale, as shown for Cs₂IrF₆ (CS-2). This largely bypasses slow solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is structural and redox-related: the solution analogue Cs₂IrF₆ is trigonal P-3m1 rather than cubic Fm-3m, and Tl(IV) may be less robust in solution than Ir(IV) [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ordered Fm-3m Cs₂MF₆-type structure with only CsF₁₂ cuboctahedra, TlF₆ octahedra, and F sites described in the target data. The large Cs/Tl size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk criterion [Reasonable Inference], but the structure is simple and lacks reported edge-sharing coexistence, so the overall amorphization risk is low–medium. CS-1 and CS-4 show structurally identical cubic Cs₂MF₆ phases can crystallize rather than amorphize, supporting an F4 score of 80.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₂TlF₆ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0109 eV/atom, effectively on the convex hull within numerical uncertainty (target data). The strongest support comes from chemically similar and structurally identical cubic Cs₂PtF₆ and Cs₂CuF₆ analogues, both containing the same CsF₁₂ cuboctahedral / MF₆ octahedral framework (CS-1, CS-4). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining sufficiently high fluoride chemical potential to stabilize isolated [TlF₆]²⁻ octahedra and Tl(IV) [Reasonable Inference supported by F₂-based syntheses in CS-1 and CS-4]. The most critical limiting factor is the uncertain redox and hydrolytic stability of Tl(IV), especially for the solution route [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues provide little direct chemical guidance because they are not Cs–Tl fluorides, so they are treated as neutral rather than decisive.
