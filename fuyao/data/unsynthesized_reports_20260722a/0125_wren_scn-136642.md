### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is −0.025605 eV/atom, placing it in the ≤0.05 eV/atom bin and giving a high thermodynamic score. This suggests the phase is at least computationally stable or within numerical tolerance of the convex hull [Reasonable Inference based on target data]. CS analogues KCr5Te8, CsCr5Te8, and RbSc5Te8 are also reported with E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, supporting the general stability of alkali/transition-metal telluride frameworks (CS1–CS3). SS and TS provide insufficient useful chemical-family evidence for this target because their top entries are unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides; they are neutral signal sources for this factor.

#### F2 — Route 1 Severity

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C and high pressure because CS1/CS2 ion exchanges are performed only slightly above alkali halide melting points in evacuated silica tubes. The severity penalty comes mainly from vacuum sealing, volatile/molten halide containment, multiple processing steps, and possible toxic Tl-containing parent use (CS1, CS2; [Reasonable Inference]). SS/TS provide insufficient useful route-specific evidence, so their severity signal is neutral.

#### F3 — Route 1 Kinetics

Ion exchange is kinetically favorable relative to full solid-state reconstruction because CS1/CS2 demonstrate week-long alkali exchange in transition-metal tellurides using molten alkali halides. The key [Reasonable Inference] is that a preformed Ti–Te framework can be retained while Rb occupies the alkali site, giving a high structural-reconstruction score. The main uncertainty is whether a suitable Tl/K/Na Ti–Te parent with the same framework can be synthesized.

#### F2 — Route 2 Severity

Route 2 uses 900 °C sealed-ampoule RbBr flux conditions analogous to RbSc5Te8, so it is high-temperature but not above the 1000 °C scoring threshold (CS3). It still requires inert/vacuum handling, sealed containment, and long dwell/controlled cooling, which lower the F2 score (CS3). SS/TS are insufficient for route-specific severity because their synthesis chemistries are unrelated to Rb–Ti–Te.

#### F3 — Route 2 Kinetics

Flux improves transport and can dissolve/reprecipitate components, as demonstrated by the RbBr flux synthesis of RbSc5Te8 (CS3). However, starting from Ti and Te requires full framework assembly, and Ti diffusion/reactivity may be limiting [Reasonable Inference]. CS3 also reports unexpected phase formation, indicating that phase selection in Rb–M–Te fluxes can be difficult.

#### F2 — Route 3 Severity

Direct sealed-tube synthesis follows the general conditions of SrSc2Te4/BaSc2Te4, which use vacuum-sealed silica tubes at 800–900 °C plus long annealing (CS4, CS5). The penalties arise from vacuum sealing, volatile Rb/Te containment, multistep annealing/regrinding, and air-sensitive Rb/Rb–Te precursors [Reasonable Inference]. This route is operationally standard for tellurides but chemically less forgiving than the alkaline-earth examples.

#### F3 — Route 3 Kinetics

Direct reaction from solids has the poorest kinetics because dense Ti metal or Ti–Te intermediates require long-range diffusion [Reasonable Inference]. CS4/CS5 show that even related scandium tellurides require long annealing and controlled cooling for phase purity/crystals. Full reconstruction from binary/elemental precursors makes competing Ti–Te and Rb–Te phases likely [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization / Crystallization Tendency

The target has mixed edge-, corner-, and face-sharing TiTe6 octahedra, which triggers the mixed-connectivity criterion directly from the target structure. Rb and Ti have very different cation sizes, triggering the cation-size mismatch criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom count is not explicitly provided; the formula unit has 15 atoms, so there is insufficient direct evidence for a >40-atom cell and that check is not triggered. The resulting F4 score is medium risk rather than low risk.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb(Ti3Te4)2 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.025605 eV/atom. The strongest synthesis support comes from CS1/CS2, where chemically similar alkali transition-metal tellurides are obtained by molten-halide ion exchange, and from CS3, where an Rb–M–Te phase is grown using RbBr flux. The most critical enabling factor is the availability of a molten Rb halide medium that can deliver Rb while preserving or assembling a transition-metal telluride framework (CS1–CS3). The most critical limiting factor is phase control in the Rb–Ti–Te system, especially because Ti diffusion is slow and Rb/Te species are volatile/reactive [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not materially strengthen the synthesis recommendation because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated and have missing similarity values.
