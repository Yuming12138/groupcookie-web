### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target has E_hull = −0.000428 eV/atom, which is effectively zero within computational tolerance and indicates predicted ground-state stability [target; Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues RAg₂In and SS/TS analogues SrPdIn₂, Sr(AuIn)₂, Na₃AgIn₂, and Sr₄Au₉In₁₃ also include E_hull = 0 entries, supporting stable nearby intermetallic chemistry (CS/SS/TS). The remaining uncertainty is that the exact Pmmn Sr(AgIn)₃ phase is not directly reported in the references, so the hull prediction could compete with unlisted Sr–Ag–In phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is mild for an intermetallic because CS demonstrates induction or arc melting plus 700–800 °C annealing without high pressure or exotic reactive gases (CS). The main severity penalty is Sr air/moisture sensitivity, supported by SrPdIn₂ handling requirements in the SS/TS data (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from CS gives no explicit melting temperature, so the T >1000 °C penalty was not applied and that source signal is treated cautiously.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetics are helped by metallic melting and relatively low-melting constituents, and CS analogues show Ag–In–metal compounds homogenize after melting/annealing (CS; Reasonable Inference). However, CS analogues are mostly Heusler Fm-3m phases, whereas the target is orthorhombic Pmmn, so structural ordering may require annealing (CS; target). The uncertainty is whether Sr prefers the target SrAg₃In₃ ordering or diverts to other Sr–Ag–In compositions [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 receives a high F2 because SrPdIn₂ was made by one-step high-frequency melting under purified Ar at ambient pressure, with no reported prolonged high-temperature anneal or high-pressure requirement (SS/TS). The only deduction is for Sr air/moisture sensitivity, which is explicitly noted for Sr rods in the SrPdIn₂ synthesis description (SS/TS). Insufficient evidence from SS/TS gives no numerical peak temperature, so no sustained T >1000 °C deduction was applied.

#### F3 — Route 2

The molten-metal pathway gives good diffusion, and SrPdIn₂ was obtained as single-phase material in 1 g quantities with no decomposition by high-frequency melting (SS/TS). The target’s Pmmn Sr–Ag–In structure differs from Cmcm SrPdIn₂, but both contain high-coordinate Sr and 9-coordinate transition/noble-metal–In environments, supporting moderate structural transferability (SS/TS; target). The main uncertainty is Ag/In site ordering, which may be slower than gross alloy formation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is more severe because Sr–Au–In analogues used welded Ta/Nb tubes, evacuated silica jackets, dry gloveboxes, high temperatures up to 1050 °C, quenching, annealing, and controlled cooling (SS/TS). These steps trigger penalties for >1000 °C, vacuum/sealed containment, multi-step processing, and Sr sensitivity (SS/TS). The advantage is robustness against volatile or reactive component loss, but the operational burden is higher than direct melting [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

Sealed high-temperature reactions should allow full equilibration because SS/TS Sr–Au–In phases formed in sealed Ta/Nb tubes after high-temperature holds and annealing (SS/TS). Kinetics are still limited by starting from dense elemental metals and by possible formation of multiple nearby Sr–coinage–In phases (SS/TS; Reasonable Inference). Phase optimization reported for Sr–Au–In compositions lowers confidence in straightforward single-phase SrAg₃In₃ formation (SS/TS).

#### F2 — Route 4

Flux growth is operationally more complex because the EuAg₄In₈ analogue required vacuum sealing, staged heating/cooling, centrifugation, sonication, and flux removal (SS). With Sr replacing Eu, dry inert loading and possibly refractory containment become more important because Sr analogues require moisture control (SS/TS; Reasonable Inference). The route avoids high pressure but is not the simplest bulk synthesis method (SS).

#### F3 — Route 4

Flux growth has strong kinetic advantages because molten In-rich flux enhances diffusion and can yield single crystals, as shown for EuAg₄In₈ (SS). The structural similarity of EuAg₄In₈ to the target supports using Ag–In flux chemistry, but the target stoichiometry is less In-rich and may not crystallize from the same liquid composition (SS; target; Reasonable Inference). Remaining uncertainty is high because flux conditions can select different Sr–Ag–In phases rather than the target [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target is an intermetallic with high-coordinate Sr/Ag/In environments rather than a complex oxide framework, and SS/TS analogues are crystalline intermetallics made by melting or high-temperature reaction (target; SS/TS). The main amorphization-related risk is size mismatch between large Sr and smaller Ag/In, inferred from high Sr coordination and long Sr–Ag/Sr–In bonds in the target and Sr analogues [target; SS/TS; Reasonable Inference]. Insufficient evidence from CS/SS/TS is available for total unit-cell atom count and explicit mixed polyhedral sharing in the target, so those checks were not triggered.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sr(InAg)₃ is likely a ground-state or very-near-ground-state phase because its E_hull is essentially zero at −0.000428 eV/atom [target]. The analogue support is strong chemically for Ag–In intermetallic formation by melting and strong structurally/thermodynamically for Sr–In–metal phases made by high-frequency melting or sealed high-temperature reaction (CS/SS/TS). The most critical enabling factor is the availability of low-melting metallic elements and successful precedent for direct elemental alloying in related systems (CS/SS/TS; Reasonable Inference). The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity among possible Sr–Ag–In intermetallics, especially because the exact Pmmn SrAg₃In₃ structure is not directly demonstrated in CS/SS/TS [Reasonable Inference]. The best practical starting point is high-frequency melting under purified Ar with optional annealing for ordering (SS/TS; CS).
