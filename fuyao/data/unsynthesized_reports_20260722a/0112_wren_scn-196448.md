### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target’s reported E_hull is −0.0850 eV/atom, which is inside the best stability bin in the provided rubric. Several close chemical analogues are also reported at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom, including K3GaS3, K2TiS3, K3AlSe3, and K3AsSe3, supporting the plausibility of stable K-rich chalcogenides (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). SS/TS analogues are thermodynamically stable but chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient specific signal for K3SiS3 and are scored neutral 50.

**F2 — Route 1.**  
The route requires evacuated sealed ampoules like K3GaS3 and K3AlSe3 syntheses, so controlled atmosphere and sealed volatile-component deductions apply (CS1, CS4). The temperature is moderate, around 750–825 °C, based on 1025–1075 K analogues, so no >1000 °C penalty applies (CS1, CS4). The main uncertainty is ampoule safety/reactivity with K/S vapor [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 1.**  
Elemental precursors are proven for close analogues, but elemental Si is less reactive than Ga or Al, lowering precursor activity [Reasonable Inference; CS1, CS4]. The structural relation to K3GaS3/K3AlSe3 is chemically strong but not exact because target Si is 3-coordinate while Ga/Al analogues are tetrahedral (CS1, CS4 and target structure). Remaining uncertainty is whether Si forms the desired SiS3 motif rather than competing thiosilicate phases [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2.**  
K2S-based synthesis is directly supported by K2TiS3, which used K2S, S, vacuum sealing, long anneal, and slow cooling (CS2). Deductions arise from vacuum, volatile S, air-sensitive K2S, and possible prior K2S synthesis, which CS2 explicitly identifies as adding precursor complexity. No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty applies because CS2 used 1070 K, i.e., 797 °C.

**F3 — Route 2.**  
K2S improves precursor activity by providing preformed K–S bonding, as demonstrated for K2TiS3 (CS2). Kinetic transfer is only partial because K2TiS3 contains TiS5 trigonal bipyramids while target K3SiS3 contains trigonal SiS3 units (CS2 and target structure). Long annealing in CS2 indicates diffusion/order may be slow, which limits F3.

**F2 — Route 3.**  
The flux route inherits penalties for sealed/inert handling, volatile sulfur, air-sensitive sulfide flux, and multi-step crystal-growth operations [Reasonable Inference], while K3AsSe3 flux/melt synthesis supports similar 750 °C alkali chalcogenide crystallization (CS5). No high-pressure or >1000 °C penalty is required. The largest uncertainty is flux separation without decomposing an air-sensitive product, as air sensitivity is noted for K3AsSe3 (CS5).

**F3 — Route 3.**  
A molten K2S/K2Sx flux should enhance mass transport relative to solid-state diffusion [Reasonable Inference], and K3AsSe3 demonstrates crystalline alkali chalcogenide formation from a melt/flux at 750 °C (CS5). K2S precedent from K2TiS3 supports sulfide precursor compatibility (CS2). The structural match is still inferred rather than proven because no Si analogue is provided in CS/SS/TS.

**F2 — Route 4.**  
Reactive gas sulfurization is supported by hydrogen/chalcogen routes for K2CoS2 and K3AsSe3-type materials (CS3, CS5). It avoids sealed ampoule pressure but uses controlled H2/chalcogen or H2S-type atmospheres, causing severity deductions (CS3, CS5; [Reasonable Inference]). It is less preferred because the provided analogues use Co/As chemistry, not refractory SiO2 sulfurization.

**F3 — Route 4.**  
Carbonate/oxide routes can generate chalcogenides under reducing chalcogen atmospheres, as in CS3 and CS5. However, converting SiO2 to Si–S units is expected to be kinetically difficult and requires complete reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore, F3 is the lowest among the routes despite a relatively mild F2 score.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The large K/Si cation size mismatch triggers one amorphization-risk check [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge and corner sharing but not face sharing, so the mixed vertex/edge/face criterion is not fully met. No evidence from CS/SS/TS establishes a >40-atom unit cell, so that check is treated as insufficient evidence and neutral.

K3SiS3 is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0850 eV/atom, and several chemically related K-rich chalcogenides are stable at E_hull = 0.0 eV/atom (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). The chemical-similarity evidence is strong, especially K3GaS3 and K3AlSe3 from direct ampoule reactions and K2TiS3 from K2S/S sealed synthesis (CS1, CS2, CS4). The structural-similarity and thermodynamic-similarity lists are not chemically relevant to K–Si–S, so they provide insufficient useful transfer signal and are treated as neutral 50. The most important enabling factor is the demonstrated accessibility of K-rich chalcogenide phases under evacuated or flux/melt conditions near 750–800 °C (CS1, CS2, CS4, CS5). The most important limiting factor is controlling volatile/air-sensitive K–S chemistry while avoiding competing potassium thiosilicates or residual Si/SiS2 [Reasonable Inference].
