### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target E_hull is reported as −0.060000148 eV/atom, placing it effectively at or below the convex hull and giving a high thermodynamic score [Target data]. Chemically close references Rb4Au7Sn2, Rb2Au3Tl, K4Au7Ge2, and K3Au5In all have E_hull = 0.0, showing that nearby alkali–Au–p-block intermetallics are stable in computed databases (CS1–CS3, CS5). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for target-specific chemistry because their nearest entries are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, so they are neutral rather than negative evidence.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 500–550 °C, based directly on CS1 and CS3, with no high pressure. The main severity penalties are sealed handling of volatile/reactive Rb and air/moisture sensitivity, which is supported by CS1 reporting sealed Ta/steel ampoules and extreme air/moisture sensitivity. Remaining uncertainty is whether Ge substitution changes the phase field relative to Sn, but CS3 strongly reduces that concern.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The isostructural Rb4Au7Sn2 analogue forms in a single 500 °C anneal for 10 days (CS1), and K4Au7Ge2 forms at 550 °C (CS3), so the target should not require severe kinetic activation. Dense Au/Ge powders may slow interdiffusion [Reasonable Inference], but liquid/reactive Rb at synthesis temperature and the close structural match support acceptable kinetics. The long CS1 anneal justifies not assigning a near-perfect kinetics score.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The KN3 route for K4Au7Ge2 requires Ar grinding, evacuated ampoules, and sealed containment at 550 °C (CS3). Adapting it to RbN3 introduces azide safety and alkali volatility risks [Reasonable Inference], so this route receives larger severity deductions than direct elemental annealing. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for azide-assisted Rb–Au–Ge chemistry and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
CS3 shows that an alkali azide route efficiently forms K4Au7Ge2 at 550 °C in 3 days, suggesting reactive alkali generation can improve kinetics. For Rb4Au7Ge2, the same Au–Ge network is targeted, but substitution from K to Rb and the lack of direct RbN3 evidence add uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. The route is kinetically promising but operationally less clean than Route 1.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The controlled-cooling route uses higher temperatures, 650–700 °C, long equilibration, and sealed Ta/silica containment, consistent with CS2, CS4, and CS5. It still avoids high pressure and ultra-high temperature, but dry N2/vacuum jackets, slow cooling, and Rb handling justify moderate deductions. Its condition severity is therefore similar to Route 2 but for different reasons.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
CS2 and CS5 show that alkali–Au–trielide intermetallics can form from elements using controlled cooling and long equilibration. However, the route is less structurally specific than Route 1 because CS2/CS5 are not the exact Rb4Au7Ge2 structure, and reported unsuccessful analogue attempts indicate composition sensitivity (CS2, CS5). Thus, kinetics and phase selection are plausible but less favorable than the isostructural direct anneal.

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target is an ordered intermetallic layered phase, and CS1/CS3 demonstrate analogous crystalline phases rather than glasses. One amorphization-risk trigger is assigned because Rb is much larger than Au/Ge in metallic coordination [Reasonable Inference], but the structure does not show the mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing complexity criterion and no explicit >40-atom primitive cell evidence is provided. The resulting low–medium amorphization risk is consistent with crystalline analogues in CS1 and CS3.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb4Au7Ge2 is likely a thermodynamic ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0600 eV/atom [Target data]. The synthesis outlook is strongly supported by CS1, which gives an isostructural Rb4Au7Sn2 phase made by direct elemental annealing, and by CS3, which gives K4Au7Ge2 with the same Au–Ge stoichiometric framework. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of both a same-alkali/same-structure Sn analogue and a same-Ge/same-framework K analogue (CS1, CS3). The most critical limiting factor is handling and retaining Rb under sealed, air-free conditions, as related syntheses use Ta containers, steel or silica jackets, and glovebox/inert atmospheres (CS1–CS3, CS5). Phase purity may also depend on precise stoichiometry and annealing time because related alkali–Au–p-block systems show composition sensitivity and unsuccessful analogue attempts (CS2, CS5).
