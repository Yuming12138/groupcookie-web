### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.046868487 eV/atom, which is in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom range and suggests a ground-state or near-ground-state prediction. Chemically similar phases Rb₄Au₇Sn₂, Rb₂Au₃Tl, Cs₂PdBi₂, Rb(Au₂In)₂, and Cs₃NaPb₄ all have E_hull = 0.0, supporting the thermodynamic plausibility of alkali-heavy-metal intermetallics in nearby chemical spaces (CS-1–CS-5). SS gives insufficient chemically relevant evidence for Cs–Au–In stability and is treated as neutral 50. TS gives generic near-hull examples but is chemically unrelated, so it is not heavily weighted beyond confirming that near-hull phases are often synthesizable.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses moderate temperature, 500–550 °C, consistent with Rb₄Au₇Sn₂ at 500 °C and Rb(Au₂In)₂ at 550 °C (CS-1/CS-4). It avoids high pressure, hydrogen, ammonia, or extreme thermal conditions, but it requires sealed Ta containment because alkali metals are volatile/reactive and Cs loss is a known concern in Cs reactions (CS-3). The main deduction is for air-sensitive/volatile Cs and the sealed system, not for intrinsic temperature severity.

#### F3 — Route 1

Elemental Au is kinetically sluggish, but the success of direct elemental synthesis for Rb₄Au₇Sn₂ and Rb(Au₂In)₂ shows that the alkali/In-containing reaction medium can overcome diffusion barriers at 500–550 °C (CS-1/CS-4). The target’s R-3m layered structure is highly analogous to Rb₄Au₇Sn₂, so structural reconstruction demands are low (target description; CS-1). The remaining uncertainty is whether Cs/In substitution changes phase stability or produces competing Cs–Au or Au–In binaries [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is more severe than Route 1 because it uses a slow-cooling melt/flux-like protocol, multiple thermal stages, and possibly vacuum-jacketed containment as in alkali–Au–trielide syntheses (CS-2/CS-4). It still remains below 1000 °C and does not require high pressure, which keeps the severity moderate. The major penalties are sealed containment, air-sensitive Cs, and multi-step thermal processing.

#### F3 — Route 2

The melt/flux-like environment should improve reaction kinetics and crystallization by increasing contact among Cs, In, and Au [Reasonable Inference]. This inference is supported by slow-cooling synthesis of Rb(Au₂In)₂ and alkali gold trielides from elemental melts (CS-2/CS-4). However, flux growth may introduce composition-control problems and competing Au–In/Cs–Au phases, analogous to phase-purity difficulties reported in alkali plumbides (CS-5).

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 uses 650–750 °C, which is still moderate by solid-state standards and supported by 650 °C alkali–Au–trielide synthesis and 700 °C alkali plumbide fusion (CS-2/CS-5). Its severity is increased because higher temperature raises Cs vapor pressure and makes sealed containment more critical, consistent with explicit Cs evaporation precautions in Cs syntheses (CS-3). The route is therefore a useful screen but not the preferred first attempt.

#### F3 — Route 3

Higher temperature should accelerate diffusion through molten Cs/In-containing media [Reasonable Inference], and related alkali gold trielides can form at 650 °C (CS-2). The kinetic risk is that high-temperature fusion may stabilize competing phases or cause stoichiometry drift, especially because phase-purity difficulties occur in related alkali-heavy-element systems (CS-5). Thus kinetics are acceptable but less selective than Route 1.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has one clear amorphization trigger: the size/coordination mismatch between Cs and Au/In is large [Reasonable Inference from target composition and coordination description]. The unit cell appears to be around or below 40 atoms based on three Cs₄Au₇In₂ sheets, and the target description reports edge-sharing cuboctahedra but not mixed vertex/edge/face coexistence. Crystalline, layered alkali–Au–trielide analogues exist, especially Rb₄Au₇Sn₂ and Rb(Au₂In)₂, reducing amorphization risk (CS-1/CS-4).

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Cs₄Au₇In₂ is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative within computational uncertainty and chemically related alkali/intermetallic phases are on the hull (CS-1–CS-5). The synthesis outlook is strongly supported by the isostructural Rb₄Au₇Sn₂ analogue, which forms by direct elemental annealing at 500 °C in sealed Ta crucibles (CS-1). The most critical enabling factor is the apparent structural match to an experimentally synthesized R-3m layered alkali–Au–trielide, combined with moderate-temperature sealed-tube chemistry (CS-1/CS-4). The main limiting factor is Cs handling: Cs volatility, air sensitivity, and stoichiometry retention require rigorous glovebox and sealed-container practice (CS-3). A secondary limitation is possible competition from Au–In or Cs–Au phases, especially in flux or higher-temperature routes [Reasonable Inference supported by phase-control concerns in CS-2/CS-5].
