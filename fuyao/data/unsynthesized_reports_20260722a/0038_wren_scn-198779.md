### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has E_hull = −0.018173361 eV/atom, which is inside the highest stability bin and suggests it is at or slightly below the computed convex hull (Target data). Chemically similar SrHfN₂ and Sr₂TaN₃ are also near-hull or hull-stable, with E_hull = 0.0313 and 0.0 eV/atom respectively (CS-1, CS-2). SS and TS contain many chemically unrelated materials, so there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS for SrWN₂-specific thermodynamics; those sources are treated as neutral rather than negative.

#### F2/F3 — Route 1: solid-state nitride sintering

The severity score is reduced because direct analogues require 1000–1030 °C, glovebox handling, sealed crucibles, Mo foil, and 5-day reaction times (CS-1, CS-2). Kinetically, Sr₂N plus WNₓ should be more chemically compatible than oxide ammonolysis, but W diffusion and W oxidation-state selection are likely slow [Reasonable Inference]. The remaining uncertainty is whether W prefers the target WN₄ trigonal-pyramidal environment rather than forming binary W nitrides or alternative Sr–W–N phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 2: ammonolysis

The severity is moderate-to-high because flowing NH₃ is reactive/toxic and W₃Ni₂N analogues required 725–950 °C, 12–72 h, and intermittent grinding (SS-5, TS-5). Kinetics benefit from reactive NH₃ and oxide precursor mixing, but conversion of an oxide Sr–W precursor to an oxygen-free SrWN₂ framework is a major reconstruction [Reasonable Inference]. SS-5/TS-5 also warn that W nitride phase windows can be narrow and decomposition/metallic impurities can occur.

#### F2/F3 — Route 3: high-pressure ammonothermal synthesis

The severity score is limited by high-pressure NH₃, sealed autoclaves, air-sensitive amides, and multistep loading/recovery, as seen for NaTaN₂ and Li₂Ta₃N₅ (CS-3, CS-5). Kinetically, this route is attractive because amide/NH₃ media can dissolve, transport, and recrystallize nitride species, and pure Li₂Ta₃N₅ was obtained at 550 °C and 0.6 GPa NH₃ for 5 days (CS-5). The major uncertainty is that the closest successful examples involve alkali tantalates rather than alkaline-earth tungstates, so SrWN₂ transferability is not guaranteed [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2/F3 — Route 4: reactive flux

The severity is high because reactive Li/Sr flux requires glovebox loading, high-temperature reaction, possible volatile alkali/alkaline-earth components, and flux separation [Reasonable Inference from CS-4]. Kinetically, flux transport could help overcome W diffusion barriers, and CS-4 shows Ba₂TaN₃ single crystals from Li/Ba/N₂ at 1000 °C. The uncertainty is large because the target W is not supplied by a Ta crucible analogue and SrWN₂ may require a different W nitride activity than Ba₂TaN₃ [Reasonable Inference].

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target has mixed connectivity: WN₄ trigonal pyramids share edges and corners, and N-centered polyhedra share corners, edges, and faces (Target). The Sr/W size and coordination mismatch is large because Sr is 7-coordinate while W is 4-coordinate in the target [Target; Reasonable Inference]. The unit-cell atom-count trigger is not supported by the provided description, and SS/TS provide insufficient useful signal for this structural-complexity check.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrWN₂ is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is negative, placing it effectively on the convex hull within numerical uncertainty (Target data). The strongest synthetic support comes from chemically similar SrHfN₂ and Sr₂TaN₃, which show that Sr–refractory-transition-metal nitrides can be prepared by Sr₂N-based solid-state methods near 1000 °C (CS-1, CS-2). The best-scoring route is high-pressure ammonothermal synthesis because it may overcome refractory W diffusion and promote nitride crystallization in a reactive NH₃/amide medium, as demonstrated for NaTaN₂ and Li₂Ta₃N₅ analogues (CS-3, CS-5). The key enabling factor is strong thermodynamic stability plus access to nitrogen-rich synthesis environments (Target, CS-1, CS-3, CS-5). The key limiting factor is phase control: W nitride chemistry can be kinetically narrow, and Sr-containing nitrides are oxygen/moisture sensitive, with SrO impurities reported in related Sr nitride syntheses (CS-1, CS-2, SS-5/TS-5).
