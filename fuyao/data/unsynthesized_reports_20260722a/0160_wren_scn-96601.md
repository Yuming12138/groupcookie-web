### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1: Thermodynamic stability

The reported target \(E_\mathrm{hull}=-0.03204544\) eV/atom places K(IrB)₂ in the highest thermodynamic score bin. Chemically close borides and silicides, including Eu(IrB)₂, Nd(OsB)₂, Eu₂Os₃B₅, Ca₂Os₃B₅, and Ba(IrSi)₂, are all reported at \(E_\mathrm{hull}=0\) eV/atom, supporting that related Ir/Os–B frameworks can be stable (CS-1 to CS-5). SS and TS entries are mostly unrelated chemical systems, so there is insufficient evidence from SS/TS for K–Ir–B stability transferability; they are treated as neutral 50.

#### F2: Synthesis condition severity

Route 1 receives a moderate severity penalty because EuIr₂B₂ required 1150 °C for 40 h under Ar in a sealed Ta/steel assembly (CS-3), and K adds volatility/air sensitivity by [Reasonable Inference]. Route 2 is more severe because it combines arc melting, grinding, K addition, and sealed annealing, reflecting the multi-step nature seen in arc-melt/anneal boride analogues (CS-3, CS-4). Route 3 avoids sustained >1000 °C operation by using K-rich flux [Reasonable Inference], but still requires sealed-tube handling and excess flux management, analogous to excess Ca/Eu removal in CS-2/CS-5.

#### F3: Reaction kinetics

Route 1 has limited kinetics because Ir and B are refractory and elemental solid-state diffusion is slow by [Reasonable Inference], although EuIr₂B₂ proves that the same Fddd Ir–B framework can form after 40 h at 1150 °C (CS-3). Route 2 improves Ir–B contact by pre-alloying but then requires K incorporation into a refractory framework; long annealing for Nd(OsB)₂ at 1000 °C for 330 h shows that homogenization of related borides can be slow (CS-4). Route 3 scores best kinetically because a K-rich liquid/vapor medium should enhance transport [Reasonable Inference], and excess electropositive metals were beneficial in Ca/Eu–Os–B syntheses (CS-2, CS-5), though no direct K–Ir–B flux precedent is provided.

#### F4: Amorphization tendency

Only the cation-size mismatch check is triggered, because K is much larger than Ir by standard size trends [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the target placing K in high coordination and Ir in a compact Ir–B environment. The target description does not provide enough information to confirm unit-cell atom count above 40 or coexistence of vertex/edge/face polyhedral sharing, and CS/SS/TS provide insufficient direct evidence for those two checks. Therefore, the amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K(IrB)₂ is likely a thermodynamically stable or very near-ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is slightly negative, and several chemically close Ir/Os–B analogues are stable on the hull (CS-2 to CS-5). The strongest support is Eu(IrB)₂, which has the same Fddd structure type and very similar Ir–B bond lengths, and was synthesized directly from elements (CS-3). The most critical enabling factor is the existence of an already realized Fddd RE(IrB)₂ framework, showing that the Ir–B network is chemically plausible (CS-3). The most critical limiting factor is K volatility and air sensitivity, because none of the CS/SS/TS examples directly demonstrate K incorporation into this exact boride framework. The best practical route is therefore a sealed K-rich flux method that uses K chemical potential to drive incorporation while improving mass transport, but this is partly [Reasonable Inference] from excess Ca/Eu boride syntheses (CS-2, CS-5).
