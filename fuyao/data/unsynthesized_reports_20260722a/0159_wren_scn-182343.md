### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

Na₂SiAs₂ has an energy above hull of −0.0351 eV/atom, placing it in the highest stability bin and suggesting it is at least computationally ground-state stable. Chemically related arsenides in CS, including K₂GeAs₂ and Ca₂CdAs₂ polymorphs, are also stable or near-stable, which supports feasibility of ternary arsenide Zintl-type phases (CS). TS entries are thermodynamically stable/near-stable but chemically unrelated, so they provide insufficient evidence for Na–Si–As synthesis transferability and are treated as neutral. The main uncertainty is whether the negative hull reflects a robust experimentally accessible phase or DFT/reference-state numerical effects.

#### F2 — Route 1: Direct solid-state

The direct route avoids >1000 °C conditions because the closest alkali tetrel pnictide analogues were made at 950–1200 K, i.e., about 677–927 °C (CS). The severity is increased by Na air sensitivity and As toxicity/volatility, and sealed containment is likely needed [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS do not provide useful condition evidence for this Na–Si–As route, so they are neutral. The resulting severity is moderate rather than severe.

#### F3 — Route 1: Direct solid-state

The presence of an isostructural K₂GeAs₂ analogue and reported Cs₂SiAs₂ synthesis makes the structural reconstruction component favorable (CS). Kinetics are partly limited by solid Si, but molten Na should improve contact and transport [Reasonable Inference]. The uncertainty is whether Na₂SiAs₂ forms directly or whether binary Na–As, Na–Si, or Si–As intermediates compete [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2: Na-rich self-flux

Na self-flux uses moderate temperatures but adds process complexity because excess Na must be removed, analogous to excess alkali removal in related alkali tetrel pnictides (CS). The route also carries volatile/air-sensitive/toxic precursor penalties due to Na and As [Reasonable Inference]. It does not require high pressure >5 GPa or >1000 °C heating. Overall severity is lower than high-pressure or ammonolysis methods but higher than a simple sealed stoichiometric reaction.

#### F3 — Route 2: Na-rich self-flux

The Na-rich liquid medium should give the best kinetics because it can dissolve/transport As and activate Si [Reasonable Inference]. CS directly supports molten alkali-element mixtures for K₂GeAs₂/Cs₂SiAs₂ and notes excess alkali removal (CS). Slow cooling and flux practice are also supported by arsenide flux examples (CS). The main kinetic uncertainty is the solubility of Si in Na-rich melts and the possibility of forming Na–Si binaries before arsenide ordering [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3: Sn/Pb-assisted flux

Sn/Pb flux growth is supported for arsenide Zintl phases at 700–960 °C using sealed or inert conditions and decanting (CS). However, using a foreign flux introduces additional steps and possible vacuum/sealed-tube handling penalties, and Pb is toxic if selected [Reasonable Inference]. This route is therefore more severe than the Na self-flux route. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this specific Na–Si–As flux choice.

#### F3 — Route 3: Sn/Pb-assisted flux

Molten Sn/Pb should enhance diffusion and crystal growth, as demonstrated for arsenide flux syntheses (CS). The structural transferability is weaker because the flux-grown CS examples are Ca–Cd–As polymorphs, not alkali–Si–As **Ibam** phases (CS). Competing incorporation of Sn/Pb or formation of Sn–As/Pb–As phases is a likely risk [Reasonable Inference]. Thus kinetics are acceptable but phase selectivity is less favorable.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a simple ordered structure description with one Na, one Si, and one As site, and the closest K₂GeAs₂ analogue has a small conventional cell formula K₄Ge₂As₄ (CS), so large-cell glass-forming complexity is not indicated. The main amorphization trigger is the large chemical/radius contrast between Na and Si cationic roles [Reasonable Inference]. No evidence from CS/SS/TS indicates complex mixed vertex/edge/face-sharing topology for the target, so that trigger is not applied. The amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Na₂SiAs₂ is likely a ground-state or very near-ground-state phase because its reported hull energy is −0.0351 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from CS: K₂GeAs₂ is isostructural **Ibam**, and Cs₂SiAs₂ is explicitly reported as synthesized from molten elemental mixtures under Ar. SS and TS entries are largely chemically unrelated, so they provide little useful synthesis guidance beyond neutral background stability/route diversity. The most critical enabling factor is the existence of directly related alkali tetrel pnictide syntheses using molten alkali/elemental mixtures. The most critical limiting factor is controlling Na and As volatility/reactivity while avoiding competing Na–As, Na–Si, or Si–As phases [Reasonable Inference].
