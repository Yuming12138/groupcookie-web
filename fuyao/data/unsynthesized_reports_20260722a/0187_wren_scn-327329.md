### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic Stability

The target E_hull is reported as −0.046415189 eV/atom, which falls in the ≤0.05 eV/atom scoring bin and indicates very high thermodynamic viability. A negative hull is physically interpreted as effectively on-hull within computational/reference-state uncertainty [Reasonable Inference]. CS analogues include Sc5Re2C7 with E_hull = 0.0 and Sc3OsC4/Sc3IrC4 with E_hull near 0.003 eV/atom, supporting that nearby Sc–late-transition-metal carbides can be thermodynamically stable or nearly stable (CS1–CS3). SS/TS entries are chemically unrelated for this factor, so they provide insufficient target-specific evidence and are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 uses arc melting and sealed-tube annealing, directly supported by Sc5Re2C7 synthesis under purified Ar followed by 780 °C annealing for two weeks in evacuated silica tubes with Ta foil (CS1). The main severity penalties are the high-temperature arc-melting step, vacuum sealed annealing, and multi-step processing [Reasonable Inference based on CS1]. No high pressure is required, and the elemental precursors are standard for carbide arc melting (CS1). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this route and are neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 1

Kinetically, Route 1 is helped by the direct isostructural analogue Sc5Re2C7, which has the same Cmmm Sc5T2C7 framework and was successfully prepared by arc melting plus annealing (CS1). The limiting kinetic issue is that W and graphite are refractory/low-diffusivity reactants, so repeated remelting and long annealing are likely needed [Reasonable Inference]. The two-week anneal in CS1 suggests ordering/crystallization is not instantaneous. SS/TS give insufficient target-specific kinetic evidence and are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 is supported by Sc3OsC4 and Sc3IrC4 syntheses using arc melting and high-frequency annealing slightly below the melting point under purified Ar for about 10 h (CS2, CS3). It avoids vacuum sealed-tube annealing, making conditions somewhat less severe than Route 1, but it likely requires higher annealing temperatures for W-containing material [Reasonable Inference]. It remains a multi-step high-temperature route. SS/TS are insufficiently specific and neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 2

The Sc3TC4 analogues show that Sc–late-transition-metal carbides can crystallize well after arc melting and short high-temperature annealing (CS2, CS3). However, their Immm structure is not the same as the target Cmmm Sc5W2C7 framework, so structural transfer is weaker than for Route 1. W diffusion and carbide ordering remain kinetic bottlenecks [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS do not provide useful kinetic evidence for Sc5W2C7 and are neutral, 50.

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is an extrapolated solid-state ceramic route, not directly reported in CS/SS/TS for Sc5T2C7 [Reasonable Inference]. It avoids the most extreme arc-melt temperatures but still requires sealed inert/vacuum conditions and multiple grinding/annealing steps [Reasonable Inference]. CS1 supports the need for Ta protection and sealed annealing, but not the absence of melting. SS/TS are insufficient and neutral, 50.

#### F3 — Route 3

The kinetics of Route 3 are expected to be poor because W and graphite are refractory and diffusion-limited without a melt [Reasonable Inference]. No source reports successful low-temperature ceramic formation of the target structure; CS1 instead uses arc melting followed by long annealing. Structural reconstruction from elements would be substantial, so this route is less reliable. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence and are neutral, 50.

#### F4 — Amorphization Tendency

The target contains distinct Sc and W coordination environments, and the Sc/W cation size and bonding differences likely exceed the 15% radius-difference criterion [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure description reports corner- and edge-sharing polyhedra but does not report face-sharing, so the mixed vertex+edge+face criterion is not fully triggered. The unit-cell atom count is not provided, and CS/SS/TS do not resolve it; this check is treated as insufficient evidence and not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc5W2C7 is likely a ground-state or effectively ground-state carbide because its reported hull energy is negative, and chemically close Sc–T–C analogues are on or very near the hull (target data; CS1–CS3). The strongest synthesis support comes from Sc5Re2C7, which is essentially the same Sc5T2C7 Cmmm structure type and was synthesized by elemental arc melting plus 780 °C annealing (CS1). The most critical enabling factor is the direct isostructural chemical precedent, which makes W-for-Re substitution plausible [Reasonable Inference from CS1]. The main limiting factor is kinetic homogenization and ordering of refractory W with graphite and Sc, likely requiring arc melting, repeated remelting, and long annealing [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated and therefore provide insufficient target-specific synthesis guidance, so they are treated as neutral evidence.
