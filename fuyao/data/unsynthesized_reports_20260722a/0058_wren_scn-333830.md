### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.0270 eV/atom, which is within numerical uncertainty of a ground-state hull phase and scores in the highest stability bin (Target). CS analogues are all reported at E_hull = 0.0, showing that chemically related complex fluorides are often stable crystalline phases (CS-1–CS-5). TS analogues also have low E_hull values, but because they are chemically unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, bromides, and nitrides, their chemistry-specific signal is insufficient and neutral aside from general low-hull support (TS-1–TS-5).

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Route 1 avoids the high-pressure F2 conditions seen in CS-1 and CS-2, but it still requires sealed, dry handling because VF5 is volatile/reactive [Reasonable Inference]. The sealed-tube concept is supported by TlZrF5 synthesis in sealed Pt tubes under dry Ar (CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient evidence for this fluoride route and are neutral at 50 for route transferability.

**F3 — Route 1 reaction kinetics.**  
Using TlF and VF5 should minimize the need for oxidation-state changes and bulk reconstruction because the target is formally Tl+[VF6]− [Reasonable Inference; Target]. The kinetics should be better than oxide fluorination because the starting species already contain fluoride and V(V) [Reasonable Inference]. The main uncertainty is whether the R-3 polymorph nucleates cleanly rather than alternative Tl–V fluorides or solvated/defective salts [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
Route 2 is penalized because CS-1 and CS-2 show that oxidative fluorination of related high-valent fluorides requires F2, high-pressure autoclaves, and corrosion-resistant equipment. CS-1 used F2/Ar at up to 300 atm and 300 °C for 2–3 days, while CS-2 used 3–4 kbar F2 at 380–400 °C for 5–6 weeks. These conditions are not >5 GPa, so the formal high-pressure deduction in the rubric is not triggered, but the route remains practically severe.

**F3 — Route 2 reaction kinetics.**  
F2 is chemically powerful and should fluorinate vanadium precursors, but converting oxides/chlorides/carbonates to a dry, ordered TlVF6 phase requires major reconstruction [Reasonable Inference; CS-1, CS-2]. CS-1 reports phase-control complications from volatile/decomposing chromium fluorides, and analogous V fluorides are expected to present similar volatility/control issues [Reasonable Inference; CS-1]. The long reaction times in CS-1 and especially CS-2 indicate nontrivial kinetics and narrow phase windows.

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
Route 3 inherits the relatively mild sealed-tube conditions from TlZrF5, which used 400 °C, 12 h, sealed Pt tubes, and dry Ar (CS-3). It is less severe than F2 autoclave routes in CS-1 and CS-2 because it avoids externally pressurized F2 [Reasonable Inference]. The penalties come from volatile V fluoride species and toxic Tl-containing precursors [Reasonable Inference].

**F3 — Route 3 reaction kinetics.**  
The use of fluoride precursors and possible TlF-rich flux should improve mass transport compared with oxide-based fluorination [Reasonable Inference]. CS-3 demonstrates that TlF can react with a metal fluoride to form a crystalline Tl metal fluoride at 400 °C in 12 h, supporting feasible kinetics for this family. The uncertainty is that ZrF4 forms extended/higher-coordinate Zr fluoride networks, whereas the target requires isolated VF6 units, so the structural transfer is partial rather than exact (Target, CS-3).

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The target has one amorphization trigger: a large cation-size mismatch between Tl+ and V5+ [Reasonable Inference]. However, the target is a simple zero-dimensional salt-like arrangement of Tl clusters and VF6 clusters, and no mixed edge/face/vertex-sharing network is reported (Target). Therefore, crystallization should be more favorable than for complex mixed-connectivity frameworks, but local disorder or polymorphism remains possible [Reasonable Inference].

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

TlVF6 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0270 eV/atom, which is effectively on the convex hull (Target). The chemical analogues provide meaningful support: CS-3 directly demonstrates Tl fluoride synthesis from TlF + metal fluoride in sealed Pt tubes, while CS-1 and CS-2 show that high-valent hexafluorometallate chemistry is accessible under strong fluorinating conditions. SS and TS analogues are much less chemically relevant and are treated as neutral for route selection, although TS provides general evidence that low-hull phases are often synthesizable. The most critical enabling factor is the preformation or in situ generation of V(V) fluoride units that can form isolated [VF6]− octahedra [Reasonable Inference; Target]. The main limiting factor is practical: controlling volatile/reactive V fluorides and toxic Tl compounds in sealed or fluorinating environments [Reasonable Inference; CS-1, CS-2, CS-3].
