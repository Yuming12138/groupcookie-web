### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target has an energy above hull of −0.1226 eV/atom, placing it in the most favorable ≤0.05 eV/atom score range; this numerical value is from the target data. Thermodynamic analogues in TS include several materials at or near the hull, such as Li3Na(NH2)4 at 0.0 eV/atom and AuBr at 0.0 eV/atom, which are reported as synthesized, supporting the general idea that near-hull phases are experimentally accessible (TS-1, TS-4). However, TS materials are chemically dissimilar to SrCl6, so TS provides insufficient specific chemical signal for Sr–Cl stability and is treated as neutral for chemistry. The remaining uncertainty is whether the negative hull value reflects a true stable Sr–Cl polyhalide phase or a computational/reference-state artifact. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 uses moderate temperatures because FeCl3 CVT operated around 593 K/320 °C to 373 K/100 °C (CS-2), and Nb/Te chloride ampoules used 423–453 K gradients (CS-5). The main severity penalties are the controlled dry Cl2 atmosphere, volatile Cl2/sealed-system requirement, and toxicity/corrosivity, all supported by the dry chlorine CVT precedent (CS-2). SS and TS give insufficient route-specific severity evidence for Sr–Cl CVT and are neutral. The route avoids extreme temperature and GPa pressure, keeping F2 moderate rather than poor. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

FeCl3 CVT demonstrates that chloride phases can form and crystallize through vapor transport at low temperature in dry Cl2 (CS-2), which strongly improves diffusion and mass transport relative to solid-state synthesis. The target is not a simple trichloride but contains SrCl8 polyhedra and Cl–Cl units, so structural reconstruction from SrCl2 is likely nontrivial. [Reasonable Inference] Nb/Te chloride CVT/ampoule examples show sealed-gradient methods can crystallize complex chloride motifs (CS-5), but they also report phase-control challenges for metastable forms, which tempers the kinetics score. SS and TS provide insufficient kinetic evidence for Sr–Cl chemistry and are neutral.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The AlCl3 flux route is low temperature because ZrCl3 was synthesized in aluminum halide melts at 220–300 °C (CS-1). Severity arises from volatile AlCl3, sealed ampoule handling, dry/inert conditions, and optional strong chlorinating additives such as Cl2 or SbCl5; Al-halide melt handling is supported by CS-1, and SbCl5 chloride media are supported by CS-3. The route likely involves multiple operational steps—drying, loading, melting, slow cooling, and flux removal—which is [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient useful evidence for this flux route and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

Flux growth receives a favorable kinetics score because aluminum halide melts provide a liquid medium, and ZrCl3 crystallized at only 220–300 °C in such melts (CS-1). Ion diffusion is therefore expected to be much easier than in a dry solid-state reaction. [Reasonable Inference] The limiting kinetic factor is structural assembly of the target’s chlorine-rich SrCl8/Cl–Cl network, which is not directly demonstrated in CS-1. The route may also produce competing Sr–Al–Cl adducts, which is a target-specific uncertainty. [Reasonable Inference]

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

MoCl5 solvothermal preparations required Ar glove-box handling, sealed glass ampoules, reactive chlorinated solvents such as CCl4/SbCl5, 473–573 K heating, and controlled cooling (CS-3). Those conditions are not high-temperature or GPa-pressure, but they are hazardous because of sealed volatile chlorinating media and air-sensitive reagents. CS-5 also supports sealed ampoule chloride chemistry with temperature gradients and inert handling. SS and TS provide insufficient solvothermal chloride-specific evidence and are neutral.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Solvothermal chloride media should provide high precursor activity and short diffusion lengths, as shown by MoCl5 crystallization in CCl4/SbCl5 at 473–573 K over 2–5 days (CS-3). Controlled cooling in CS-3 suggests a feasible crystallization pathway, but CS-3 also reports multiple MoCl5 polymorphs and phase-control difficulty, lowering confidence. The target’s need for Cl–Cl bonded units under high chlorine activity is [Reasonable Inference] from the target structure. SS and TS are neutral for this route because they do not provide chemically relevant solvothermal Sr–Cl examples.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target contains only one cation, Sr, so the cation-radius mismatch check is not triggered; this follows directly from the target formula. The Fddd space group and 7-atom formula unit suggest a likely large conventional cell, so the unit-cell-size check is tentatively triggered, but the exact atom count is not provided and this is [Reasonable Inference]. The target description reports edge-sharing SrCl8 units and Cl–Cl molecular connectivity, but not simultaneous vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity check is not triggered. Overall amorphization risk is low–medium rather than high.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

SrCl6 is predicted to be thermodynamically very favorable based on the reported −0.1226 eV/atom hull value, so it should be treated as a likely ground-state or near-ground-state candidate, although the negative hull value may reflect reference-state uncertainty. The chemically similar chloride analogues support low-temperature halide synthesis most strongly: FeCl3 by dry-Cl2 CVT (CS-2), ZrCl3 by AlCl3 flux (CS-1), and MoCl5/Nb–Te chlorides by sealed solvothermal or ampoule methods (CS-3, CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is maintaining high chlorine chemical potential while allowing mass transport, because the target is far more chlorine-rich than ordinary SrCl2 and contains a 2.30 Å Cl–Cl bond. [Reasonable Inference] The most critical limiting factor is phase selectivity against SrCl2 and possible molecular/polyhalide decomposition products, since no provided CS/SS/TS example directly demonstrates SrCl6 or an alkaline-earth hexachloride. SS and TS analogues are mostly chemically unrelated, so they contribute mainly neutral or general synthesizability context rather than direct synthetic guidance.
