### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is reported as −0.0695 eV/atom, which is interpreted as effectively stable/ground-state within computational uncertainty [Reasonable Inference from target data]. CS analogues Rb3SbSe3, Rb2SnSe3, Rb2MnSe2, K3AsSe3, and K3GeSe3 are all listed at E_hull = 0.0, so chemically similar alkali selenides are thermodynamically accessible (**CS1–CS5**). SS and TS do not provide useful target-specific chemical signal because their top entries are unrelated amides, borides, phosphates, halides, and nitrides with NaN similarity fields; therefore there is insufficient evidence from SS and insufficient evidence from TS, each treated as neutral 50.

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 avoids >1000 °C heating because K3GeSe3 was made at 650 °C (**CS5**). The main severity deductions are controlled Ar/vacuum handling, sealed volatile Se chemistry, and air-sensitive Rb/product handling [Reasonable Inference from CS5 moisture-/oxygen-free sealed-ampoule procedure]. SS/TS give insufficient evidence for this Rb–Ge–Se sealed-ampoule route, so the F2 judgment is dominated by CS5.

#### F3 — Route 1

K3GeSe3 is a direct structural and chemical analogue to Rb3GeSe3, giving high structural-reconstruction confidence (**CS5**). Although elemental Ge can be kinetically sluggish, the 650 °C synthesis of K3GeSe3 indicates that alkali–selenide melt/vapor chemistry can activate the reaction (**CS5**, [Reasonable Inference]). Remaining uncertainty is whether the larger Rb cation changes competing phase stability or ampoule compatibility [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 2

The carbonate–oxide selenizing route operates at 750–800 °C for A3MSe3 analogues, below the severe >1000 °C threshold (**CS1**, **CS4**). It requires H2 saturated with Se, which is a reactive controlled atmosphere and introduces safety/stoichiometry penalties (**CS1**, **CS4**). The route is less directly validated for Ge than for Sb/As, so the score is not increased above Route 1 [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 2

Rb3SbSe3 and K3AsSe3 form in ~9 h by H2/Se-assisted melt reactions, indicating favorable kinetics for A3MSe3 frameworks (**CS1**, **CS4**). The carbonate/oxide starting materials are more reactive than dense metals, but Ge oxide reduction/selenization may have a different redox pathway than Sb/As oxides [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence, so the kinetic score is based mainly on CS1/CS4.

#### F2 — Route 3

The polyselenide-flux route is inferred rather than directly reported for Rb3GeSe3 [Reasonable Inference]. It likely stays below 650–800 °C by analogy to K3GeSe3 and A3MSe3 melt syntheses (**CS1**, **CS4**, **CS5**), but it adds multi-step precursor preparation, sealed volatile Se conditions, and highly air-sensitive polyselenides [Reasonable Inference]. Therefore its F2 is lower than Routes 1 and 2.

#### F3 — Route 3

A Rb–Se-rich flux should accelerate diffusion and crystallization by providing mobile alkali/selenide species [Reasonable Inference], consistent with the success of melt/flux-like A3MSe3 synthesis (**CS1**, **CS4**). The structural target is supported by the exact K3GeSe3 analogue (**CS5**), but flux chemistry can broaden the phase field and produce competing Rb–Ge–Se compositions [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this flux pathway and are neutral.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has very different cation sizes and mixed edge/corner/face-sharing connectivity, both of which increase crystallization complexity [Reasonable Inference; target structure]. However, K3GeSe3 crystallizes in the same C2/m A3GeSe3 framework with mixed SeK6Ge polyhedral connectivity, showing that this topology is experimentally crystallizable (**CS5**). The unit cell is not indicated to exceed 40 atoms, and SS/TS provide insufficient target-relevant amorphization evidence.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Rb3GeSe3 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and chemically similar alkali selenides are stable on the hull [Reasonable Inference from target data; **CS1–CS5**]. The strongest support comes from K3GeSe3, which has the same C2/m A3GeSe3 structure type and was synthesized directly from elements at 650 °C (**CS5**). The most critical enabling factor is the close alkali substitution relationship K → Rb in an already demonstrated germanium selenide framework [Reasonable Inference from **CS5**]. The main limiting factor is practical synthesis control: Rb metal, Se vapor pressure, and likely air/moisture sensitivity require sealed, inert handling [Reasonable Inference; **CS5**]. SS and TS do not materially strengthen the conclusion because their listed analogues are chemically unrelated and provide insufficient evidence for Rb–Ge–Se synthesis.
