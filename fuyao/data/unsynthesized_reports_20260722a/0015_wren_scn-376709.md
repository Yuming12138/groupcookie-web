### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target’s reported E_hull is −0.127384475 eV/atom, which is more favorable than the ≤0.05 eV/atom stability threshold and therefore receives 100/100. The exact sodium analogue Na₅OsO₆ is also reported with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-3), supporting the plausibility of a stable A₅OsO₆ osmate family. SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant stability transfer because their nearest examples are unrelated systems, so SS = neutral 50 and TS = neutral 50 for chemical stability interpretation.

#### F2 — Route 1 condition severity

Route 1 inherits moderate high-temperature O₂ synthesis from Na₅OsO₆: 150 °C initial reaction followed by 750 °C/12 h under O₂ (CS-3). The main penalties are dry oxidizing atmosphere, air-sensitive potassium peroxide, multi-step handling, and likely sealed corrosion-resistant containment [Reasonable Inference from CS-3 and CS-1]. No high pressure or >1000 °C condition is required, which keeps the score from falling into the very difficult range.

#### F3 — Route 1 kinetics

Na₅OsO₆ is the direct structural and chemical analogue, so structural reconstruction risk is low once K/Os/O chemistry is achieved (CS-3). The kinetic limitation is Os metal oxidation/diffusion, because Os is refractory [Reasonable Inference], but peroxide/O₂ chemistry enabled the Na analogue at only 750 °C (CS-3). This gives Route 1 the best F3 among the routes.

#### F2 — Route 2 condition severity

The AO₂ hyperoxide route is supported by K₃BiO₄ synthesis at 700 °C under Ar using alkali hyperoxides (CS-2). It avoids >1000 °C and high pressure, but KO₂ is strongly oxidizing and air-sensitive, and oxygen release makes sealed reactions hazardous [Reasonable Inference]. The score is similar to Route 1 because the atmosphere and precursor-handling penalties remain substantial.

#### F3 — Route 2 kinetics

KO₂ should be highly reactive and can oxidize metals into oxometallates, as shown for K₃BiO₄ from AO₂ + Bi (CS-2). However, no Os-containing hyperoxide analogue is provided, and the route may need to reconstruct from Os metal to ordered K₅OsO₆ with precise oxygen stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Thus the kinetics are plausible but less favorable than the direct Na₅OsO₆ route.

#### F2 — Route 3 condition severity

The K₂O/peroxide flux route is based on K₂ZrO₃ growth in excess K₂O from KNO₃ at 1000 °C under dry N₂ (CS-5) and K₄PbO₄ synthesis in sealed Ag ampoules under O₂ at 590 °C for 100 days (CS-1). It requires dry atmosphere, corrosive/hygroscopic K-rich flux, and likely sealed containment [Reasonable Inference]. The absence of high pressure helps, but flux volatility and prolonged heating make it challenging.

#### F3 — Route 3 kinetics

Flux chemistry improves precursor activity and cation transport, as shown by K₂ZrO₃ crystal growth from K₂O flux (CS-5). The downside is that Os incorporation and Os(VII) stabilization are not demonstrated in the flux analogue, and long K-rich oxide anneals in CS-1 and CS-4 indicate slow ordering can be a real issue. Therefore Route 3 is useful for crystal growth after phase discovery but is less attractive as the first synthesis route.

#### F4 — amorphization tendency

The target contains very different cations, K⁺ and high-valent Os, giving a large cation size/charge contrast [Reasonable Inference]. The target structure also contains both corner- and edge-sharing KO₆/OsO₆ connectivity, which increases the need for long-range ordering compared with a simple isolated-polyhedron salt (target description). The unit-cell size is not explicitly given, so that check is not triggered; uncertainty remains because the conventional cell atom count was not supplied.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

K₅OsO₆ is likely a thermodynamically stable or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is negative and the direct Na₅OsO₆ analogue is experimentally known with E_hull = 0.0 (CS-3). The CS data strongly support synthesis, especially through the Na₅OsO₆ peroxide/O₂ route; SS and TS are mostly unrelated chemically and therefore provide neutral rather than reinforcing evidence. The most critical enabling factor is high oxygen chemical potential from dry O₂ plus alkali peroxide/superoxide chemistry, which is needed to stabilize high-valent Os(VII) [Reasonable Inference from CS-3]. The most critical limiting factor is safe and precise control of potassium oxide/peroxide volatility, moisture sensitivity, and possible OsO₄ formation under oxidizing conditions [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the material is quite plausible synthetically, but the route should be treated as specialized high-valent alkali osmate chemistry rather than routine oxide sintering.
