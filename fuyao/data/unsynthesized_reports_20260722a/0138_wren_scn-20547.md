### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

**F1 — Thermodynamic stability.**  
The target has E_hull = −0.0745 eV/atom, which is within the most favorable stability bin and supports a high F1 score [target data]. CS also contains several stable chemically related intermetallics with E_hull = 0, including MgSc3Ru2, Sc5Rh2In4, and MgSc2Ru (CS-1, CS-2, CS-5). One Sc–In–Ni analogue has E_hull ≈ 0.115 eV/atom but is still reported as synthesized, indicating that modest metastability need not preclude synthesis (CS-3). SS and TS provide insufficient chemically relevant evidence for Sc–Ru–In stability and are treated as neutral 50 for source-specific thermodynamic inference.

**F2 — Route 1 condition severity.**  
Arc-melting and annealing are well supported by CS for Sc–In–Rh/Ni and Sc–Ru–Mg intermetallics (CS-1 to CS-5). The penalty is significant because arc-melting imposes very high transient temperatures, the process is multi-step, and sealed containment is prudent for In [Reasonable Inference]. There is no evidence requiring high pressure; CS uses Ar at roughly 600–700 mbar or sealed ampoules (CS-2, CS-5). SS/TS provide insufficient evidence for this intermetallic route and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 1 kinetics.**  
The main kinetic issue is Ru’s refractory nature and slow diffusion [Reasonable Inference], partly mitigated by arc-melting and repeated remelting used in the CS analogues (CS-2, CS-3). The structural reconstruction score is favorable because MgSc3Ru2 and MgSc2Ru share tetragonal P4/mmm Sc–Ru frameworks similar to the target (CS-1, CS-5). However, CS-5 reports phase mixtures requiring long annealing, so kinetics are not easy. SS/TS are not chemically transferable here.

**F2 — Route 2 condition severity.**  
The sealed Nb route avoids the extreme transient temperatures of arc-melting but still uses >1000 °C annealing, sealed ampoules, and multiple processing steps (CS-5). CS-5 directly supports sealed Nb tubes under Ar, quartz protection, grinding, pelletizing, and long second annealing. The score is higher than Route 1 because it can be performed below 1400 °C [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient evidence and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 2 kinetics.**  
Solid-state reaction from elemental Sc/Ru/In is expected to be diffusion-limited because Ru is refractory [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 shows that even chemically close MgSc2Ru required a two-step procedure and 96 h second anneal to approach phase purity, supporting a moderate-low kinetics score. The close P4/mmm structural relationship from CS-1/CS-5 improves confidence that the ordered phase can form if diffusion is sufficient. Remaining uncertainty is whether In substitution stabilizes the same ordering rather than forming competing Sc–In or Ru–In binaries [Reasonable Inference].

**F2 — Route 3 condition severity.**  
The In-flux route is extrapolated and uses sealed ampoule conditions similar to CS intermetallic anneals but adds flux removal/decanting [Reasonable Inference; ampoule basis from CS-2/CS-3/CS-5]. It avoids high-pressure and reactive gases, which are not needed in the closest CS routes. It still requires high temperature, sealed containment for In, and inert handling of Sc [Reasonable Inference]. SS/TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral.

**F3 — Route 3 kinetics.**  
Liquid In should enhance mass transport and improve Ru/Sc reaction compared with purely solid-state sintering [Reasonable Inference]. The target’s simple P4/mmm framework and close relation to MgSc3Ru2/MgSc2Ru support feasible structural ordering (CS-1, CS-5). The limitation is that no direct CS/SS/TS example demonstrates an In-flux synthesis of this exact structure, so this route receives an expert uncertainty penalty. Competing In-rich phases are a plausible risk [Reasonable Inference].

**F4 — Amorphization tendency.**  
The high-symmetry, small-cell P4/mmm intermetallic structure suggests low intrinsic amorphization risk [Reasonable Inference from target structure]. A radius/size mismatch trigger is assigned because Ru is significantly smaller than Sc/In in metallic-radius terms [Reasonable Inference]. CS shows related Sc–Ru and Sc–In intermetallics crystallize under conventional high-temperature methods rather than forming glasses (CS-1 to CS-5). SS/TS provide insufficient amorphization evidence for Sc–Ru–In and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Sc3InRu2 is likely thermodynamically stable or at least very near the ground state because the computed E_hull is strongly favorable [target data]. The best supporting evidence comes from CS: P4/mmm Sc–Ru analogues MgSc3Ru2 and MgSc2Ru exist and are synthesized by sealed high-temperature routes, while Sc–In–transition-metal indides are also synthesized from elements by arc-melting/annealing (CS-1 to CS-5). The most critical enabling factor is the close structural/chemical analogy to known Sc–Ru P4/mmm intermetallics (CS-1, CS-5). The most critical limiting factor is kinetic and phase-purity control involving refractory Ru, reactive Sc, and possible In loss or In-rich competing phases [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, synthesis is plausible but will likely require sealed inert processing, slow annealing, and careful stoichiometry control.
