### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.01674 eV/atom, which is effectively thermodynamically stable within computational uncertainty, so it receives 98/100 by the provided rubric. CS-2, CS-4, and CS-5 show several chemically similar oxides with E_hull near 0–0.03 eV/atom and successful synthesis, supporting the general link between low hull energy and accessibility. TS entries are low-hull but chemically unrelated, so they provide generic but not chemically specific support; insufficient chemically specific evidence from TS is scored neutral 50. Remaining uncertainty is mainly redox/phase competition rather than hull stability [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1

Route 1 is modeled directly on CS-2, where PbSb₂O₆ was synthesized in air at ~852 and ~952 °C with grinding and long reheating. This avoids high pressure and >1000 °C temperatures, but deductions are applied for toxic Tl/As precursors, likely volatile As/Tl oxide containment, and multistep heat–grind–reheat processing [Reasonable Inference]. SS and TS provide insufficient route-specific evidence and are neutral for this factor.

#### F3 — Route 1

CS-2 strongly supports favorable structural kinetics because PbSb₂O₆ has the same P-31m octahedral framework as the target. However, CS-2 also required a 100 h reheating step, indicating slow cation/oxygen ordering and justifying a moderate rather than high precursor-activity score. The non-refractory nature of Tl/As oxides and sub-1000 °C analogue synthesis support good diffusion kinetics [Reasonable Inference from CS-2].

#### F2 — Route 2

Route 2 follows CS-4, where an octahedral antimonate was grown from V₂O₅ flux in an evacuated quartz ampoule at 1170/970 K. The flux route avoids >1000 °C operation but requires sealed/vacuum conditions, multistep crystal growth, toxic reagents, and volatile-component management [Reasonable Inference]. CS-5 provides additional evidence that heavy p-block oxides can be grown from high-temperature oxide melts, though not for Tl arsenates specifically.

#### F3 — Route 2

Flux growth receives the best kinetic score because liquid-mediated transport should reduce diffusion limitations [Reasonable Inference], and CS-4 demonstrates this principle for CuSb₂O₆ using V₂O₅ flux. The structural pathway is plausible because CS-2 and CS-4 both involve octahedral group-15 oxide frameworks. Remaining uncertainty is flux compatibility and possible V/Mo/Bi incorporation [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 3

Route 3 is the most severe because it uses high pressure, sealed capsules, and toxic/volatile components. The high-pressure concept is not directly demonstrated by CS/SS/TS for Tl–As–O, although SS-1/TS-1 show high-pressure synthesis is technically possible in unrelated systems; this is insufficient chemically useful evidence and scored neutral. The route is therefore challenging and only recommended if ambient/flux methods fail [Reasonable Inference].

#### F3 — Route 3

High pressure may stabilize the dense AsO₆ topology relative to tetrahedral TlAsO₄-like arsenates [Reasonable Inference], especially since CS-3 shows TlAsO₄ with tetrahedral AsO₄ forms readily under mild conditions. Kinetics are only moderate because high-pressure capsules use short reaction times and limited sample size, and structural reconstruction from tetrahedral arsenate precursors could be substantial. No direct SS/TS chemical analogue is available, so uncertainty remains high.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target has a large cation-size mismatch between Tl and As and mixed corner/edge octahedral connectivity, both of which can frustrate crystallization and increase glass/amorphous byproduct risk [Reasonable Inference]. CS-2 partially mitigates this concern because the same topology crystallizes as PbSb₂O₆, but CS gives no explicit amorphization data. SS/TS provide insufficient useful amorphization evidence and are neutral.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

Tl(AsO₃)₂ is likely thermodynamically accessible because its computed E_hull is essentially zero, but experimentally it may behave as a redox-sensitive or metastable-access phase due the unusual Tl/As oxidation-state combination [Reasonable Inference]. The strongest support comes from CS-2: PbSb₂O₆ is an almost exact structural analogue in the same P-31m space group and is made by conventional solid-state sintering. Flux evidence from CS-4 provides a useful backup route for improving transport and crystal growth in octahedral group-15 oxides. The most important enabling factor is the exact PbSb₂O₆-type structural match, which strongly suggests the framework is chemically plausible. The main limiting factor is maintaining Tl and As stoichiometry/oxidation state at high temperature while avoiding volatile oxide loss and competing TlAsO₄-type tetrahedral arsenates [Reasonable Inference from CS-3].
