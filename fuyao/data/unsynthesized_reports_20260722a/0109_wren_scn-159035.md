### SECTION 5 — Scoring Rationale

#### F1 — Thermodynamic stability

The target E_hull is −0.009348172 eV/atom, which is effectively hull-stable and receives 100/100 under the provided rubric. This is consistent with many chemically, structurally, and thermodynamically similar references being hull-stable, including CS1–CS5, SS1, SS5, and TS1–TS5. The remaining uncertainty is computational: a slightly negative hull energy is numerical rather than physical, so experimental stability still depends on finite-temperature entropy and competing K–Tl–Se phases [Reasonable Inference].

#### F2 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state

Route 1 is penalized for requiring vacuum-sealed ampoules, staged processing, volatile chalcogen control, and toxic/air-sensitive reagents. Vacuum or sealed-tube processing is directly supported by CS1/CS3/CS4, SS1/TS1, and TS3, while toxicity/air sensitivity for Tl/K is [Reasonable Inference]. The temperature itself is not severe because analogous materials are made near 500–900 °C rather than above 1000 °C (SS1/TS1, TS3, TS5).

#### F3 — Route 1: sealed-ampoule solid-state

K, Tl, and Se are not refractory, so diffusion is expected to be easier than oxide ceramic synthesis [Reasonable Inference]. However, analogues often need one-week anneals or even months of annealing, indicating slow ordering or phase equilibration in some Tl/alkali chalcogenides (CS1/CS3/CS4, SS1/TS1). Because no exact KTl3Se2 structural analogue is provided, structural reconstruction is scored moderate rather than high.

#### F2 — Route 2: melt growth / slow cooling

Route 2 uses moderate temperatures of ~550–700 °C, directly supported by SS5/TS2 and SS2/SS3, so no high-temperature penalty applies. It still requires vacuum ampoules, possible volatile chalcogen containment, multiple steps if a Tl–Se binary precursor is made, and toxic/air-sensitive handling [Reasonable Inference]. The severity score is therefore limited by safety and containment rather than by thermal extremity.

#### F3 — Route 2: melt growth / slow cooling

Melt growth gives the best kinetic score because molten stoichiometric mixtures are explicitly used for Tl-rich chalcogenides in SS2/SS3 and SS5/TS2. Slow cooling at 1 °C/h in SS5/TS2 provides a direct strategy for ordering complex Tl-rich structures. The main uncertainty is phase selection, because SS5/TS2 reports peritectic/phase-control difficulty for AgTl3Te2 and no exact KTl3Se2 analogue is given.

#### F2 — Route 3: solvothermal

The solvothermal route has low thermal severity because CS2 uses only 160 °C for 24 h. It is still penalized for Ar/vacuum sealed-tube handling, multi-step operation, and toxic/air-sensitive K2Se/Tl reagents [Reasonable Inference]. There is insufficient evidence from SS and TS for solvothermal KTl3Se2-like Tl selenide formation, so these sources are neutral for this route’s condition-transferability assessment.

#### F3 — Route 3: solvothermal

CS2 provides useful evidence that alkali selenides can react with metal salts in ethylenediamine at low temperature, which supports good precursor activity and low diffusion barriers. However, CS2 concerns Rb–Hg–Se chemistry, not Tl-rich K–Tl–Se, and no structural analogue supports solvothermal crystallization of the target. Thus the kinetics for forming any selenide are favorable, but the kinetics for forming the specific C2/m 2D KTl3Se2 phase remain uncertain.

#### F4 — Amorphization tendency

The target structure has a relatively simple formula and no stated large unit cell, so the >40 atom check is not triggered. The target description mentions edge-sharing Se-centered polyhedra but not coexistence of vertex, edge, and face sharing, so the mixed-connectivity amorphization check is not triggered. Cation-radius mismatch is treated as low because K and Tl are both large cations in soft selenide environments [Reasonable Inference], but direct radius evidence is insufficient from CS/SS/TS.

### SECTION 6 — Synthesizability Interpretation

KTl3Se2 is likely a ground-state or near-ground-state phase because its reported E_hull is −0.0093 eV/atom. The strongest support comes from thermodynamically stable Tl–Se and alkali chalcogenide analogues, especially SS1/TS1, SS5/TS2, TS3, and TS5, plus chemically related alkali selenides in CS1–CS5. The most enabling factor is the low thermodynamic driving force against decomposition, combined with low-melting/reactive K–Tl–Se components that should allow melt-assisted equilibration [Reasonable Inference]. The most limiting factor is experimental control: Tl toxicity, K air sensitivity, Se/Tl volatility, and possible competing K–Se/Tl–Se binaries require sealed ampoule synthesis and careful stoichiometry [Reasonable Inference]. Overall, the target is plausibly synthesizable, with melt/slow-cooling route preferred over direct powder annealing or solvothermal exploration.
